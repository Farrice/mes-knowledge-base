## Case 1 — McDonald’s: IBM Automated Order Taker (voice AI) pilot — June 2024 termination

### Executive summary
McDonald’s tested an IBM‑built Automated Order Taker (AOT) voice‑AI system beginning in 2021 and deployed it at “over 100” U.S. drive‑thru locations as a pilot. Viral social‑media videos showing serious misorders and order‑mixing occurred in 2023 and 2024; McDonald’s confirmed the IBM pilot would be shut off in all test restaurants no later than July 26, 2024, and removed the technology from about 100 restaurants by the end of July 2024. Reported operational accuracy during tests was roughly in the mid‑80% range (McDonald’s/CEO statements and analyst reporting), below the ~95% threshold cited by analysts for viable broad adoption. Causes reported in press and analyst commentary include speech‑recognition accuracy limits (accents/dialects), integration/operational issues leading to order mixing across lanes, and high operating costs; McDonald’s and IBM stated they remain partners and will evaluate future voice solutions. Viral examples were widely covered by mainstream press citing social media videos. [1], [2], [4], [5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10]

## Cross‑case synthesis and high‑level observations (based only on the supplied findings)

- Pilot vs rollout: All seven cases involve pilots or limited rollouts rather than immediate system‑wide replacements. McDonald’s, Wendy’s, Sweetgreen, Chipotle, Domino’s, and Presto all tested voice or makeline automation in limited geographies or via selected restaurant counts before wider deployment decisions. [2], [23], [15], [17], [19], [29]

- Common reported challenges: Speech recognition accuracy (accents/noise), integration with legacy POS and lane management, high operating/integration costs, and viral social media incidents are recurring themes (McDonald’s and broader voice‑AI reporting). Voice systems frequently required human intervention when accuracy was insufficient (McDonald’s ~15–20% human assist; Presto ~70% human intervention in advanced pilot). [4], [29], [1]

- Vendor vs independent evidence: Several performance claims (e.g., Dynamic Yield uplift, Wendy’s 86% handled without human intervention, Sweetgreen throughput and labor‑savings in early reporting) derive primarily from vendor or company statements and trade press that echoes those vendor claims. Independent regulatory disclosure was available in the Presto case, and mainstream press corroborated McDonald’s operational challenges. [11], [23], [16], [29], [2], [4], [7]

- Viral reputational risk: McDonald’s pilot termination demonstrates the reputational sensitivity of public pilots, where a handful of viral misorders reported by mainstream outlets precipitated corporate action; this cautionary example is highlighted by multiple mainstream sources. [7], [8], [9], [10], [2]

- Measurable throughput/accuracy claims: Where provided, robotics/makeline pilots reported clear throughput and cycle‑time figures (Sweetgreen ~500 orders/hr capacity claim; Hyphen makeline up to 350 meals/hr and 99% accuracy; Autocado ~26s/avocado). These claims are mainly in trade or corporate reporting and appear internally consistent in the supplied findings. [16], [20], [22]
