### The owner archetype (calibrated to the Cherng family / PRG, not a generic multi-unit operator)

**The label that fits:** *"Founders / family stewards of an Asian-American hospitality company that happens to operate restaurants."* The restaurants are the artifact. The family's identity is the system, the people, and the cross-cultural mission.

#### What they think about every shift
The Cherng family does not run a shift directly. But the *brand* of each legacy concept does, and the GMs report into a corporate ops chain. The shift-level concerns at Yakiya, Wasabi, Panda Inn:
- **Cover counts** — especially against last year same-day-of-week. Wasabi at CityWalk lives or dies on Universal Studios attendance, which is itself a daily moving target driven by ride downtime, weather, school calendars, and marquee events.
- **Ticket times** — Wasabi at CityWalk has an OTD (out-the-door) clock that has to compete with the surrounding food-court psychology even though the price point is full-service. Theme park guests will not wait 25 minutes for a table.
- **Comp rate** — every comped meal has to be reviewed and coded. Comps over 2% of sales trigger a flag.
- **Callouts** — especially BoH on a Friday in Glendale or Pasadena when the kitchen is already running short.
- **Online review velocity** — particularly the negative ones that hit between 9pm and 11pm when the FoH manager is in the weeds and cannot respond until tomorrow.
- **Comp meals to VIPs / regs** — Panda Inn Glendale has 50-year regulars. Some are family friends of the Cherngs.

#### What they think about every week
- **P&L review** by location, by daypart, by category. PRG runs a corporate FP&A function. The legacy concepts get reviewed but with less frequency and granularity than the Express system.
- **Labor hours vs forecast** — California minimum wage compression (16 general, 20 fast-food) is squeezing prime cost on the legacy concepts harder than Express because they have full-service labor structures.
- **Food cost variance** — particularly the premium fish line at Yakiya and Wasabi (sushi-grade tuna, hamachi, uni). Spot prices for bluefin tuna moved 18% in 2024.
- **Manager 1:1s** — Chris (the store manager) is the bottleneck the Cherngs care about. Manager development is one of the four pillars of the "Panda Way."
- **Inventory variance** — what got 86'd, what got dead-plated, what got walked.
- **Yelp / Google review triage** — three locations means three review surfaces, three response queues, three sentiment trends.

#### Existential concerns
1. **The legacy brands feel orphaned next to Express.** Every quarter, capital allocation, marketing budget, technology investment, and best-talent rotation goes to Panda Express because the Express ROIC is enormous. The legacy brands (Panda Inn, Wasabi, Yakiya, Hibachi-San) are emotionally important but operationally a rounding error. The family knows it. There is a private pain about it.

3. **Universal CityWalk lease economics.** Theme park retail is not a normal lease — it is almost always a percentage rent structure (base rent + % of gross sales above breakpoint) plus marketing/CAM, plus lease terms that limit operator flexibility. Wasabi at CityWalk has been there for years; the lease is a long-tail commitment.

The question Andrew and Peggy quietly ask: *"Will the legacy concepts survive the transition, or will the next generation focus only on Express?"* This is unspoken at the executive level but it shapes every "should we invest in this small brand" decision.
5. **Loss of the family feel at scale.** When Panda Inn opened in 1973, Master Chef Ming-Tsai Cherng was on the line. The restaurant was the family. 2,600 stores later, the family has to manufacture culture instead of breathe it. This is the wound the "Panda Way" curriculum exists to scar over.
6. **Immigration enforcement risk on BoH.** California Asian restaurant kitchens depend on immigrant labor. Any federal escalation is a kitchen-staffing emergency.
7. **Yelp / Google sentiment as proxy for brand health.** Andrea Cherng's CBO function tracks brand sentiment as a leading indicator. A bad month at Yakiya Pasadena is a personal embarrassment in a way a bad month at any one Panda Express store is not.

## Deliverable 2 — The Operator Language Map

A native-fluency dictionary. Two columns per term: what it means, and how an operator actually uses it.

### Money / Margin language

| Term | What it means + how operators use it |
|---|---|
| **Prime cost** | Food + labor combined as a % of sales. *"Our prime cost was 64 last week, we need it back under 60 by month-end."* The single most-watched number. |
| **The 60% rule / 65% rule** | Industry rule of thumb: prime cost should be ≤ 60% of revenue (fine dining can stretch to 65% in California due to labor). Anything over 65% means the location is bleeding. |
| **Food cost %** | COGS / sales. Industry average 28-32%. **High-end sushi runs 30-35% because of premium fish; some operators see 33%+ on sushi-grade product alone.** Cooked items (teriyaki, donburi, tempura) come in lower at 28-29%, which lets sushi places blend down. (altametrics.com; financialmodelslab.com fine-dining KPIs) |
| **Labor cost %** | Total labor (wages + benefits + payroll tax) / sales. FSR target 30-35%. Upscale full-service in CA is now pushing 35-40% post-AB1228 wage compression. *"Labor is killing me — we're at 38 and the schedule is already cut to the bone."* (toasttab.com restaurant payroll percentage) |
| **COGS** | Cost of goods sold. Used interchangeably with food cost in casual conversation but technically includes paper goods, beverages, and consumables. |
| **PPA / check average / avg ticket** | Per Person Average. Total sales divided by covers. *"Our PPA is 42 at dinner, 28 at lunch."* The fastest read on whether the menu is doing its job. Upscale Asian targets 60-120 PPA. |
| **Comp / comping / comp rate** | A "comp" is a comped (free) item, given for service recovery, manager discretion, VIP courtesy, or training. Comp rate = comped sales / gross sales. *"Comps were 3.1% this week, that's too high — pull the report."* Anything over 2.5% triggers an audit. |
| **Comp report** | The weekly report listing every comp by server, by reason code, by manager. Manager performance gets judged on it. |
| **Contribution margin** | Sales minus variable costs (food, hourly labor) per dish. Used to decide what to push and what to cut from the menu. Menu engineering language. |
| **Daypart** | A defined service window — breakfast, lunch, happy hour, dinner, late night. *"Lunch daypart is soft. Dinner is carrying us."* PRG uses dayparts religiously because Panda Express invented dayparting in fast casual. |

### Service / FoH language

| Term | What it means + how operators use it |
|---|---|
| **Cover** | One guest. *"We did 280 covers last night."* A "cover count" is the number of butts in seats for a service period. |
| **Doing 200 covers** | Phrase used to convey volume as a brag or a complaint. *"Friday we did 312 covers and the kitchen barely held."* |
| **Two-top / four-top / six-top / deuce** | Table sized by number of seats. A "deuce" = a two-top. *"Sit them on the deuce by the window."* |
| **Server section** | The block of tables a server is responsible for. *"You're on section 4 tonight, that's tables 21-26."* |
| **Sidework** | Non-table tasks the server does on shift — rolling silverware, marrying ketchups, polishing glassware, restocking sugar caddies. Always assigned, always behind. |
| **Table turn / second turn** | How many times a table gets seated in a service period. Upscale = **1-1.5 turns** per service, fine dining stretches to 1.5-2; casual = 3-4. *"We need a second turn on those four-tops by 8:30 or we're toast."* (chowbus.com; getknowapp.com; financialmodelslab.com) |
| **Turn time** | Average minutes from seat to check drop. Fine dining 90-120 min, upscale casual 60-75 min, fast casual 25-40 min. |
| **The book** | The reservation system. Used as a synonym for OpenTable/Resy/Tock. *"Get me the book — what does Saturday look like?"* |
| **OpenTable / Resy / Tock** | The three reservation platforms. Resy is more aggressively used by hip independents, OpenTable is the legacy default for upscale, Tock is prepay omakase / tasting menu. Yakiya uses OpenTable. |
| **VIP / regs** | Repeat guests on a tracked list. Comped consideration, table preferences, allergies all flagged. Regs are the social spine of a neighborhood concept like Panda Inn Glendale. |
| **Attachment rate** | The % of orders that include a specific upsell category (apps, dessert, drinks). *"Our dessert attachment is 18%, kitchen says we can push it to 25% if servers actually offer it."* |
| **In the weeds / weeded** | Overwhelmed during service. *"I was so in the weeds at 8pm I literally couldn't remember table numbers."* |
| **On the fly** | Made-to-order rush, no prep buffer. *"I need a salmon nigiri on the fly for table 14."* |

### BoH / Kitchen language

| Term | What it means + how operators use it |
|---|---|
| **86 / 86'd** | "We're out of it." *"86 the bluefin, the fish guy didn't show."* Etymology debated (Chumley's bar, Soup Nazi era), but every kitchen on earth uses it. Also means "remove from the menu." |
| **All day** | The total count of an item across all open tickets. *"Five steaks all day, two med-rare, two med, one well."* The expediter lives in "all day" math. |
| **Fired / fire it** | Tells the kitchen to start cooking the item (vs just printing the ticket). *"Fire the omakase course at table 8."* |
| **Dead plate / refire** | A plate that came up wrong or sat too long under the heat lamp and has to be remade. Logged as waste. |
| **Dupes / dupe printer / KDS** | A "dupe" is a duplicate ticket — old usage referred to the carbon copy. Modern usage = the printed/digital order in the kitchen. KDS = Kitchen Display System (Toast KDS, Aloha KDS). *"Check the dupes — table 14 ordered 20 minutes ago."* |
| **The pass** | The physical bar/window where finished food gets handed from kitchen to FoH. The expediter ("expo") works the pass. *"Pickup at the pass!"* |
| **Expo / expediter** | The person who calls and reads tickets, organizes plates by table, and quality-checks before food leaves the pass. Often the chef de cuisine or sous chef on a busy night. |
| **On the line** | Working the active cooking stations during service. *"I was on the line for 14 hours yesterday."* |
| **Stations** | Saute, grill, garde manger (cold appetizers / salads), expo, fry, pantry. In a sushi-forward kitchen: sushi bar, hot line, fry station, robata. |
| **Mise en place / mise** | "Everything in its place." The prep work done before service so the line can move at speed. *"My mise wasn't ready and I got crushed."* |
| **Walk-in** | The walk-in cooler (NOT a walk-in customer). *"Check the walk-in for the hamachi."* |
| **Out the door / OTD / ticket time** | Minutes from order entry to food delivery to table. Watched obsessively. Upscale Asian target = 18-25 min for entree, 8-12 for app. Theme park casual (Wasabi at CityWalk) target = 12-18 min entree. |

### Tech stack language

| Term | What it means + how operators use it |
|---|---|
| **POS** | Point of Sale. Toast and Square dominate independents; Aloha (NCR Voyix) and Micros (Oracle Simphony) dominate enterprise / fine dining; Brink and Revel are niche. PRG runs custom systems on the Express side and likely Aloha or Micros on legacy concepts. |
| **Toast** | The fast-growing modern POS, cloud-native, opinionated. Most independent operators have an opinion about Toast (love or hate). |
| **Square for Restaurants** | Square's restaurant product, popular with smaller independents. |
| **Aloha** | The legacy fine-dining workhorse. Owned by NCR Voyix. Cranky but trusted. |
| **Micros / Oracle Simphony** | Enterprise-grade legacy. Hilton, big chains, hotel F&B. |
| **Restaurant365 (R365)** | Back-office accounting + inventory + scheduling, the dominant enterprise BoH platform. PRG-scale companies often use R365 or build their own. |
| **MarginEdge** | Invoice OCR + food cost tracking. Independents' favorite. |
| **MarketMan** | Inventory and supplier ordering. |
| **xtraCHEF / Compeat** | Older R365 competitors, now mostly absorbed. |
| **KDS** | Kitchen Display System — replaces paper tickets at the line. |

### Asian-restaurant specific

| Term | What it means + how operators use it |
|---|---|
| **Omakase** | Literally *"I leave it up to you."* Chef-curated tasting at a sushi counter, typically 10-20 courses, 80-400+ PPA. Pacing is part of the product — too fast feels assembly line, too slow feels neglected. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omakase) |
| **Kaiseki** | The older, more formal Japanese tasting tradition with strict seasonal/structural rules. Omakase is a casualized descendant. |
| **Izakaya** | A Japanese pub format — drinks plus shareable small plates. Casual, after-work, bar-forward economics. Yakiya is more steakhouse than izakaya, but the small-plate language carries over. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izakaya) |
| **Robata / robatayaki** | Charcoal grilling in an open hearth. The chef sits behind a long counter, items are cooked over glowing coals and passed directly to guests. Smoky, theatrical, premium. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robatayaki) |
| **Hibachi vs teppanyaki** | Often conflated. **Hibachi** = traditional small charcoal "fire bowl" grill, intimate, smoky. **Teppanyaki** = post-WWII flat iron griddle, gas/electric, large surface, the Benihana-style "show cooking" experience. Yakiya is closer to teppanyaki / robata with table-side preparation, not "hibachi" in the strict sense. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teppanyaki; benihana.com/blog) |
| **Sushi-grade fish** | Marketing term, not an FDA designation. The FDA only requires that fish served raw be frozen to specific temperatures to kill parasites. "Sushi-grade" really means "this purveyor knows how to handle fish for raw service" — which is a relationship, not a label. |
| **Banchan** | Korean side dishes brought before/during the meal, typically 4-12 small plates of pickled vegetables, kimchi, namul. Free-refill culture. Not relevant to Yakiya (Japanese) but operators in LA's Koreatown live and die on banchan economics — they are a hidden food cost line. |
| **Family-style service** | Plates brought to the center of the table for sharing. Panda Inn's traditional service model. Higher PPA when done well, slower turn times. |
| **Dim sum** | Traditional Cantonese tea-time small-plates service. Cart service is dying out (Hong Kong-style) in favor of order-from-the-menu (cleaner cost control, less waste, faster turn). |
| **Nigiri / sashimi / maki** | Nigiri = fish on rice. Sashimi = fish only. Maki = rolled. Order on a sushi menu by the count, not the dollar amount. Operators talk about "nigiri attachment rate" — how often diners order nigiri vs. cooked items. |

## Deliverable 4 — Objection Tree (PRG-calibrated)

The Cherngs are **not** the typical "scared of tech" mom-and-pop owner. They are the opposite — they may be the most data-mature Asian restaurant family in America. Generic objections will be replaced by **sophisticated buyer objections**. Here is the realistic objection tree.

### Objection 1: *"We've been investing in restaurant tech since 1975. What's a consultant going to tell me I don't already have on my CIO's roadmap?"*

- **Underneath:** Identity threat. Peggy invented this discipline. A consultant with no track record looks like a tourist in her field.
- **Reframe (Sutherland-style):** Do not claim to be smarter than the system. Claim to be unencumbered by it. The corporate roadmap optimizes for the 2,600-store system. Wasabi, Yakiya, and Panda Inn do not get the roadmap's attention because they are too small to move the corporate needle but too important to the family to neglect. An outside partner can run experiments on the legacy concepts that would never clear the corporate prioritization queue.
- **One-sentence pre-empt for the dossier:** *"Everything in this folder was built specifically for the legacy concepts — the brands the corporate roadmap can't justify the analyst hours on."*

### Objection 2: *"AI is hype. Show me one Asian restaurant that's actually moved prime cost two points with this stuff."*

- **Underneath:** Engineering rigor. Peggy will not accept handwaving. She built her reputation on measurable systems.
- **Reframe:** Do not over-claim. Lead with the smallest, most provable win (menu engineering on a single category at a single location, with a measurable hypothesis and a 30-day clock). The dossier should already contain ONE specific, falsifiable claim with a number attached.
- **One-sentence pre-empt:** *"This document doesn't claim AI saves restaurants. It identifies seven specific decisions where structured analysis can shift one number by one measurable amount in 30 days."*

### Objection 3: *"My team can barely use the POS, and you want to layer AI on top of it?"*

- **Underneath:** Real pain about unit-level adoption, especially at the legacy brands where staff are not all native English speakers and tenure varies.
- **Reframe:** AI does not have to live at the unit level. It can live in the analyst's seat — running daily/weekly reports the GMs already need but nobody has time to compile. The team never touches the AI. They get a one-page summary in their email at 6am.
- **One-sentence pre-empt:** *"Nothing in this proposal asks Chris's team to learn a new tool. The intelligence shows up the same way the manager P&L shows up — pre-built and ready to read."*

### Objection 4: *"We have an internal data team. Why outsource?"*

- **Underneath:** Loyalty signal. The internal team is good and protected.
- **Reframe:** Position as **augmentation, not replacement.** The internal team optimizes the 2,600-store engine. The legacy concepts are a side quest the internal team genuinely does not have bandwidth for. Be the side-quest specialist who hands work back to the internal team in formats they can absorb.
- **One-sentence pre-empt:** *"Anything in this engagement that proves valuable becomes a deliverable the corporate data team can absorb and scale across other concepts at zero marginal friction."*

### Objection 5: *"I had a consultant come in three years ago. They left a beautiful deck and we kept doing things the same way. Wasted my money."*

- **Underneath:** The "deck without delivery" wound. The PRG team has been pitched by McKinsey / Deloitte / boutique restaurant consultants their whole career. Most leave decks. Few leave systems.
- **Reframe:** Lead with shipped artifacts, not slides. This entire dossier IS the proof of work. The first deliverable in the engagement should be something installed, not something presented.
- **One-sentence pre-empt:** *"Nothing in this folder is a slide. Every artifact is a working tool — you can hand it to a manager today and they can use it tomorrow."*

### Objection 6: *"Too expensive right now. Maybe next quarter."*

- **Underneath:** Real budget reality and a polite way to defer. Often it is not about cost, it is about *prioritization fatigue.*
- **Reframe:** Make the first engagement small enough that no quarterly budget meeting is required. **Sub-5K pilot, single concept, single decision, 30-day clock.** Nothing to defer.
- **One-sentence pre-empt:** *"The first phase doesn't compete with capital priorities — it sits inside one location's existing operating budget for less than the cost of a single weekend's labor overage."*

### Objection 7: *"Send me a proposal."* (the polite no)

- **Underneath:** The client does not want to say no in the room. They are killing the deal with kindness, intending to ghost the proposal email.
- **Reframe (NEPQ-style):** Do not accept the brush-off. *"Happy to. So I send the right thing — what's the one decision we'd be helping you make in the first 30 days that would tell you whether we're worth a second meeting?"* This forces a specific commitment and lets you write a proposal that solves an actual stated problem.
- **One-sentence pre-empt for the dossier:** *"The next step isn't a proposal. It's a 30-minute working session to choose ONE decision worth testing. We come prepared with three candidates. You pick one or veto all three."*

### Bonus objection (PRG-specific)
**Objection 8: *"My daughter Andrea handles brand. Have you spoken with her team?"***
- **Underneath:** Real org chart reality. Andrea Cherng as CBO will gatekeep anything that touches brand or marketing across the legacy concepts.
- **Reframe:** Position the menu/operations work as **upstream of brand, not adjacent to it.** *"This work is about what the menu is doing operationally, not how it's marketed. Anything we find that touches brand expression we'd hand to Andrea's team to decide on."*
- **One-sentence pre-empt:** *"Nothing in this document modifies brand expression. It looks only at the operational layer — pricing, menu mix, throughput — and flags brand questions for the CBO function to handle."*

## Deliverable 5 — "What Keeps You Up at Night" Map (Multi-Unit, Cherng-Specific)

The standard multi-unit pain framework, calibrated to PRG legacy concepts.

### Pain 1: Cross-location staffing
**The reality:** Wasabi, Yakiya, and Panda Inn do not share a labor pool the way three Panda Express units in the same DMA do. A line cook who knows Yakiya's yakiniku station cannot cover Wasabi's fusion sushi line. Cross-coverage is nearly impossible, which means callouts are absorbed by overtime or by closing a station.
**AI angle (briefly):** Forecasting + scheduling models that predict callout-prone shifts using historical patterns + weather + Universal CityWalk attendance data. Not a "scheduling app" — a *labor risk score* that flags the next 14 days.

### Pain 2: Inventory across diverse concepts
**The reality:** Three concepts = three vendor lists, three food specs, three waste profiles. Sushi-grade fish at Yakiya cannot be re-purposed at Panda Inn. The corporate purchasing team optimizes for Express scale, not for legacy concept variety, which means the legacy brands get less negotiating leverage on niche items.
**AI angle:** Variance analysis on the legacy concept inventory data (already sitting in their POS / R365) to find the 3-5 line items where waste is concentrated. One-time analysis, repeatable monthly.

### Pain 3: Brand consistency vs concept differentiation
**The reality:** Each legacy concept needs a distinct identity that does not bleed into the others (Wasabi ≠ Hibachi-San ≠ Yakiya ≠ Panda Inn). At the same time, the family wants them to feel like part of one stewardship culture. Andrea Cherng's CBO function lives this tension.
**AI angle:** Sentiment + review topic analysis across all legacy concept reviews, mapped to brand position. Flags where guest perception is drifting from intended brand. Quiet, diagnostic, not prescriptive.

### Pain 4: Manager development (Chris is the bottleneck)
**The reality:** Chris is the store manager across three concepts. He is being asked to be three different operators. The corporate Panda Way curriculum was built for Express GMs and does not fit the legacy brands' realities. Manager bench depth at the legacy concepts is thin.
**AI angle:** Create a "manager intelligence brief" — a weekly digest pre-built for Chris that compresses his three concepts into a single decision-grade document. *Not* a dashboard. A two-page brief he can read with morning coffee.

### Pain 5: The "second-best location" problem
**The reality:** When you run three locations, one always gets the founder's attention and the others drift. At PRG corporate scale, the legacy concepts ARE the second-best locations relative to Express. Wasabi at CityWalk is itself the second-best Wasabi (the original Wasabi was elsewhere). Drift is inevitable without intentional intervention.
**AI angle:** Variance dashboards comparing each legacy concept's KPIs against its own historical baseline (not against Express). Catches drift early.

### Pain 6: POS data fragmentation
**The reality:** Express runs on custom corporate systems. The legacy concepts may run on different POSes — Aloha, Micros, or older systems — depending on when each was last refreshed. Cross-concept reporting requires manual aggregation in Excel, which means it does not happen monthly.
**AI angle:** A nightly extract that pulls from each legacy POS into a unified data view. Set-it-and-forget-it. Nobody has to learn anything.

### Pain 7: Reviews triage across three locations
**The reality:** Three brands × Yelp + Google + OpenTable + Tripadvisor = 12 review surfaces. Andrea Cherng's CBO team monitors brand-level sentiment but unit-level review response is GM-dependent, which means it is inconsistent. A 1-star review on Yakiya at 10pm Friday will not get a response until Monday.
**AI angle:** Auto-drafted response suggestions (manager approves, GM sends) cut response time from 60+ hours to 2 hours. Sentiment trend report flags categories of complaint before they cluster.

### Pain 8: Marketing budget allocation across very different brands
**The reality:** A dollar spent marketing Wasabi at CityWalk is a fundamentally different dollar than one spent on Yakiya Pasadena. Theme park marketing is calendar-driven (school breaks, marquee park events). Yakiya is occasion-driven (Mother's Day, Valentine's, anniversaries). Panda Inn is loyalty-driven (50-year regulars). The corporate marketing team optimizes for Panda Express national media. The legacy concepts need three different micro-strategies that do not get built.
**AI angle:** A monthly "where is each legacy concept's customer in the calendar" brief. Four pages, three concepts, one read. Hands the strategic decisions back to Chris and Andrea's team to action.
