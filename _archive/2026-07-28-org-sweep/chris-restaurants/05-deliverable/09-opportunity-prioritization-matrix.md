## The three-zone version (for the room that wants the simplest possible read)

### Zone A — Do this first (next 30 days, total cost under $7,000)

**The case here is mathematical, not strategic.** Three moves stack together inside the same month:

1. **Activate the Yakiya beverage program.** This is a beverage director meeting, a cellar restock, and a menu reprint. The lift, conservative, models to $120K–$250K in incremental annual revenue at the single Pasadena location. No new staff. No new tools. The 6% beverage attach number is from a verified Parkzer.com receipt; the 20–30% benchmark is from published industry data. **The single highest-dollar opportunity in the entire deliverable.**

2. **Activate the Panda Inn founding myth.** Five zero-cost moves (table card, plaque, Google Business update, host-stand script, menu heritage marks) plus one press pitch. Under $500 across all five activations. The asset already exists; the cost is the cost of saying it.

3. **Fix the Yakiya tableside grill SOP.** One training day. Photographic doneness checklist. Pre-portioned salt ramekin. The fix protects every $128 cover from the documented Parkzer failure mode and converts the wagyu execution from a vulnerability into a credibility signal.

**Total Zone A cost:** Under $7,000.
**Total Zone A timeline:** 30 days.
**Total Zone A revenue impact (low estimate):** $120K–$250K annual lift on Yakiya beverage alone, plus the Panda Inn rating lift, plus the protection of the Yakiya $128 tasting from continued execution failure.

### Zone B — Ship this in months 2 and 3 (cost: $2,500–$5,500/month for the AI tooling)

The 90-day roadmap. Six items across Phases 1, 2, and 3. The Zone B work is the operational infrastructure that lets Chris run all three (or all four, or all ten) legacy concepts as a portfolio instead of as three separate phone-call queues.

The marquee item in Zone B: **inventory variance tracking** (MarketMan or MarginEdge), modeled on the MarketMan / Dolar Shop case study at a 50+ location international Chinese hotpot chain. Conservative outcome: 1–3 percentage point food-cost reduction across the legacy concepts. **The bigger margin lever than any voice or marketing AI in the entire roadmap.**

The fast-trust item in Zone B: **the manager voice log → daily digest demo**. ~$20/month/location. Demoable in 5 minutes on a phone. The "show, don't tell" asset that turns this folder from a deliverable into a working prototype.

### Zone C — Defer or watch (these are the things to consider but not commit to in the first 90 days)

- **Voice AI for phone reservations** (Slang.ai, Hostie AI). Mature enough to deploy at Yakiya and Panda Inn, but not the highest priority. Add in months 4–6 after the Phase 1 wins compound.
- **Cross-location BI dashboard** (Tenzo, Lineup.ai). Phase 3. Deploy after the Phase 1 + 2 work has stabilized the data hygiene at each location.
- **Loyalty program with AI personalization** (Bikky, Thanx). Best fit for Yakiya and Panda Inn. Add in month 4.
- **Predictive equipment maintenance, kitchen automation, AI menu development.** Skip in V1. These are research projects at independent and four-unit-concept scale.

## Why this order

The ranking optimizes for three things, in order:

**1. Defensible dollar impact.** Every Zone A item has a documented number behind it. The Yakiya beverage program is anchored in a verified eyewitness receipt and published benchmarks; the Panda Inn founding myth is anchored in real reviews from real customers calling it out unprompted. **No item in Zone A relies on a vendor case study with bias.**

**2. Time to visible result.** Anything that ships in under 30 days outranks anything that takes 60. Anything that demos in a meeting outranks anything that requires a deployment cycle. Chris's first conversation with the owner is the moment everything depends on; the items that earn that conversation get to ship first.

**3. Dependency-light sequencing.** Zone A items don't depend on each other or on tools that need to be procured. Zone B items can ship in parallel with Zone A. Zone C items can wait until the cadence is established.

The single most important sequencing rule, applied across the matrix: **the Yakiya throughput diagnosis must precede any tool that depends on POS data hygiene at Yakiya.** If the Phase 2 inventory tools find a 12% variance gap on the wagyu line, that's data, not chaos. But the owner needs to be prepared for that conversation before the report lands. This is a sequencing decision Chris and the owner make together, not a technology decision.

---

## What this matrix is not

This matrix is not a quote. The dollar ranges above are conservative, defensible, and built from the public-data analysis in this folder. They are not commitments. The 90-day timeline assumes coordinated execution and access to basic operational data the locations already have internally (POS history, vendor invoices, GM bandwidth). Real engagement scoping would tighten every number on this page within a week of having actual access to the data.
