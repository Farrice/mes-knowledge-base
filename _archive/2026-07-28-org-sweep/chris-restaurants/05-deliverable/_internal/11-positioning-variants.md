# 11 — Positioning Variants

**Audience:** Farrice (for Checkpoint 2 decision)
**Purpose:** Four positioning options to choose from before the folder ships to Chris. Each variant carries the non-negotiable DNA from WS2 — the "side-quest specialist" premise, the unencumberance reframe, and the respect for Peggy Cherng's 1975 systems lineage. What varies is the wrapper.
**Decision needed:** Pick ONE primary positioning. Farrice can keep a second as a fallback for the room.

---

## The Non-Negotiable DNA (present in all four variants)

Every positioning below is built on the same foundation. Do not lose it in any variant:

1. **Farrice is unencumbered, not superior.** He works on what the PRG corporate roadmap can't justify this quarter — not because the internal team isn't good, because the arithmetic on 4 legacy concepts can't compete with the arithmetic on 1,650 Panda Express stores.
2. **The legacy portfolio is the arena.** Panda Inn, Yakiya, Wasabi, Hibachi-San — expanding to Ippudo, Just Salad, Pieology, Uncle Tetsu as trust compounds.
3. **The deliverable is the proof.** Everything Farrice shows is already built. Nothing is a slide.
4. **Peggy's lifework is honored, not challenged.** The 1975 POS analytics work is a foundation he's standing on, not a bar he's trying to clear.

What changes between variants: who the primary reader is, where the price ceiling sits, and how much of the enterprise payload shows through the wrapper on first read.

## Variant A — "AI Operations Partner for Legacy Concepts" (PRIMARY)

**One-sentence value proposition:**
*"I'm the operations partner for the legacy concepts your best people can't get to this quarter — and the proof point is the Digital Dining analytics bridge at Wasabi that the corporate roadmap will never reach, because the arithmetic on one location can't compete with the arithmetic on 1,650 Panda Express stores."*

**Primary audience:** Gigi Cheung (VP of Concept). Secondary: Tom Davin, David Landsberg.

**Price band it anchors:** Tier 2 → Tier 3. Lands in the $15K–$30K Phase 1 engagement. Opens the door to $25K–$50K/mo ongoing partnership once three wins are banked.

**What it gains:**
- Most defensible against the 1975 objection. The word "partner" signals peer respect, not vendor servitude.
- Specialist framing ("legacy concepts") makes the price feel cheap — Gigi is already paying for specialists in every other function.
- Highest retainer ceiling of the four variants.
- Pairs naturally with the Portfolio Partnership (Tier 3) upsell narrative.

**What it gives up:**
- Slightly formal. Less warmth on the Chris-level handoff than Variant C.
- Requires the reader to already buy that "legacy concepts" are a category worth having a partner for. If Gigi hasn't named this problem internally yet, the framing does half the work but isn't a warm entry.

**Sample 60-second verbal intro (what Farrice says in the room):**

> "Chris asked me a simple question about menu analysis for Wasabi, Yakiya, and Panda Inn. I did the thing he asked for — prices, competitive sets, efficiency moves. But the deeper I got, the more I noticed the same pattern across all three: the answers sit inside the data your team already owns. Your CIO knows this. Peggy knows this — she built the original Panda Inn analytics in '75. The real problem isn't knowledge. It's prioritization. The clearest example is sitting at Wasabi: that location runs Digital Dining, a legacy POS no cloud AI vendor has a plug-and-play connector for. Your corporate BI team can build the bridge — and your team is good enough that they could do it tomorrow. They just won't, because the ROI math on engineering hours for one Digital Dining location can't compete with the ROI math on 1,650 Panda Express stores already sitting on modern infrastructure. That's not a critique of the team. That's arithmetic. So I built something different. I work only on the legacy concepts. The four brands the arithmetic can't justify internally, but the family can't justify walking away from either. I call what I do an operations partner — not because I replace anyone on your team, but because I run the experiments the corporate queue will never get to. Gigi, I'd rather show you what that looks like than describe it. Give me 10 minutes on Chris's phone."

## Variant B — "Fractional AI Chief of Staff"

**One-sentence value proposition:**
*"Think of me as a fractional chief of staff for the brands your corporate engine can't staff — same fluency, no headcount, no roadmap queue."*

**Primary audience:** Tom Davin (CEO) or Andrea Cherng. Executive-adjacent positioning.

**Price band it anchors:** Tier 3 territory. Enterprise retainer, $25K–$50K/mo. Implies access to leadership and a reporting cadence that matches a real CoS role.

**What it gains:**
- Highest price ceiling of the four. "Chief of staff" carries executive gravity.
- Reframes Farrice as a strategic thinking partner, not a vendor running tools.
- Natural fit for cross-concept portfolio work — a CoS role assumes you're looking across the whole portfolio.

**What it gives up:**
- Highest bar to defend on first impression. If Farrice can't carry an executive conversation, the framing collapses.
- "Fractional chief of staff" is now a saturated LinkedIn category. Real CoS people may eye-roll.
- Slightly overclaims relative to the actual first deliverable. The audit-plus-voice-log demo doesn't look like CoS work until month 2 or 3.
- Risks triggering the "we already have one" response from Tom Davin.

**Sample 60-second verbal intro:**

> "The corporate chief of staff at a $5B company serves the flagship. That's right. It's also why the small concepts don't get a chief of staff at all — there's no one whose full-time job is looking across Panda Inn, Yakiya, Wasabi, and Hibachi-San as a single portfolio. I am that person, fractionally. I read the weekly numbers across all four concepts, flag the three decisions worth your time, and build the operational assets the corporate queue won't get to for 18 months. I'm not replacing your team — I'm sitting in the seat nobody on your org chart was ever funded to fill. Here's what my first month looks like…"

**Honest caution from WS5:** This variant has the highest ceiling but the highest collapse risk. Recommend only if Farrice is confident carrying a conversation with a PRG C-level executive cold.

## Variant C — "The Side-Quest Specialist"

**One-sentence value proposition:**
*"Your best people are on the main quest — 1,650 stores, 50,000 associates, the Express engine. I'm the team that runs the side quests your best people can't get to."*

**Primary audience:** Chris first, Gigi Cheung second. This is the warmest, most friend-forward wrapper of the four.

**Price band it anchors:** Tier 1 → Tier 2. Lands Chris-level engagement first ($2,500–$7,500 audit completion), expands to Phase 1 engagement ($15K–$30K) once Gigi is in the room.

**What it gains:**
- Highest land-rate of the four. The metaphor disarms. Nobody feels threatened by a "side-quest specialist."
- Most defensible across both the Chris level and the Gigi level simultaneously. Chris doesn't have to translate — he just forwards.
- Completely non-threatening to Peggy. It explicitly cedes the main quest to the internal team.
- The metaphor does the positioning work without requiring Farrice to prove enterprise credibility in the first 60 seconds.

**What it gives up:**
- Lower ceiling than Variants A and B. "Side quest" caps the perceived strategic weight.
- May need a second, more formal positioning once the relationship deepens past month 3.
- Some executives will hear "side quest" as "non-essential" — and essential things command higher fees. Worth a re-anchor by month 2.

**Sample 60-second verbal intro:**

> "Peggy built the POS analytics at Panda Inn in 1975. Your internal team inherited that lineage. They are good — I've looked at what's public and I wouldn't want to compete with them on the main quest. But the main quest is 1,650 Panda Express stores. That's where the ROI math lives. The four legacy concepts — Panda Inn, Yakiya, Wasabi, Hibachi-San — are a rounding error against that math, which means they get rounding-error attention from a very good internal team that has bigger fish to fry. I am the team that exists to work the side quests. Not because I'm smarter than your people. Because I'm unencumbered by the priority math they live inside. Here's what a side quest looks like shipped…"

**WS5's call on this variant:** This is the strongest single-variant choice if Farrice is optimizing for land rate over retainer ceiling on the first engagement. The problem is it caps later.

## Variant D — "The Unclaimed Margin Hunter"

*(WS5's own proposal — April Dunford × Daniel Priestley approach)*

**One-sentence value proposition:**
*"Your Yakiya beverage attach is 6% in a category where the floor is 20%. That's $300K–$700K a year sitting on the floor of one dining room. I find the unclaimed margin inside the legacy concepts — the kind your corporate prioritization queue will never get to — and I work only on those specific decisions until they ship."*

**Primary audience:** Gigi Cheung primarily, with David Landsberg (CFO) as the secondary reader. This variant targets the P&L directly.

**Price band it anchors:** Tier 2 with a clean Tier 3 expansion path. The "margin hunter" frame justifies $15K–$30K Phase 1 engagement because it's priced against found money, not against consulting hours.

**What it gains:**
- **Built entirely around a specific, dollar-attributed, defensible finding from WS1 Yakiya (the beverage attach gap).** Nobody else can show up with the same sentence. The positioning is already proven in the deliverable itself.
- Survives the CFO test. David Landsberg doesn't care about frameworks — he cares about found margin. This variant speaks his language.
- Priestley's credibility mechanic: the positioning itself is a demonstration of the capability. Farrice is showing his work the moment he introduces himself.
- Natural Tier 3 expansion: "We found $500K at Yakiya. Let's apply the same lens to Hibachi-San. Then Ippudo. Then the whole specialty portfolio."
- The frame is specific enough to survive the "we've had consultants" objection — consultants leave frameworks, margin hunters leave dollars.

**What it gives up:**
- Puts all the credibility weight on the first finding. If the beverage attach number doesn't hold up under audit, the positioning collapses.
- Less warm at the Chris level. Chris will still forward it, but the framing is clearly executive-focused.
- "Margin hunter" is less permanent than "operations partner." Feels like a campaign title more than a permanent identity.

**Sample 60-second verbal intro:**

> "I came in to do a menu analysis for Chris. Simple ask. But when I pulled Yakiya's beverage data against the Pasadena benchmark, I found something nobody at PRG has raised in a strategy meeting — your beverage attach rate at Yakiya is sitting at 6% in a category where the industry floor is 20%. At $128 a person, that's somewhere between $300,000 and $700,000 a year of margin that never leaves the dining room. Found money, at one location. I'm the person who hunts for that — specifically inside the legacy concepts, because your corporate prioritization queue is locked on Panda Express for the next 18 months and will never get to this. I don't do slides. I find the margin, then I build the thing that captures it. I can show you what I mean in ten minutes on Chris's phone. I brought the demo."

## WS5's Recommendation

**Primary: Variant A — "AI Operations Partner for Legacy Concepts."**

Rationale:
- Most defensible against the 1975 objection. "Partner" frames Farrice as a peer of Peggy's lineage, not a challenger.
- Highest retainer ceiling that's still grounded in the actual deliverable (unlike Variant B which overclaims).
- Scales cleanly across the Tier 1 → Tier 2 → Tier 3 arc without needing a mid-engagement rebrand.
- Survives both the Chris-level read and the Gigi-level read without losing either audience.

**Fallback for the room: Variant D — "Unclaimed Margin Hunter."**

If Farrice gets a moment directly with David Landsberg or a finance-first stakeholder, Variant D's found-money framing speaks CFO. Use Variant A as the permanent positioning and Variant D as the tactical anchor in any P&L-heavy room.

**Do not use Variant B (Fractional Chief of Staff) unless Farrice is confident in cold executive dialogue.** The collapse risk isn't worth the ceiling delta.

**Do not use Variant C (Side-Quest Specialist) as the primary positioning — use the metaphor inside Variant A.** The metaphor is valuable as a disarming line during the conversation, but it caps the strategic ceiling if it becomes the title on the one-pager.

## Final Note on the 1975 Reframe

Every variant above assumes the "unencumberance, not superiority" reframe is present in the deliverable itself. If the reader encounters ANY of these positioning statements before encountering the reframe, the positioning dies on arrival. Sequencing rule for the folder:

**The reader must hit "this work is for the four brands the corporate queue can't justify this quarter" BEFORE they hit any positioning language about what Farrice does.**

That sentence is the unlock. It's in the Executive Brief already (per WS4). Keep it there. Every artifact below assumes the reader has already encountered it.
