# Positioning Strike Brief — Authority Flywheel One-Liner
**Mission:** Stress-test and sharpen the one-line positioning for Farrice's Authority Flywheel offer ($2K-$5K, ghostwriting-repositioned), aimed at the Invisible Expert ICP (deep S&C/fitness expertise, can't articulate their value, LinkedIn-avoidant).
**Experts:** April Dunford (positioning) x Jeremy Haynes (cold-offer copy) | **Verdict on current line:** FAIL, independently, by both.

---

## THE CURRENT LINE (both experts kill it)

> "I architect Authority Flywheels for invisible experts — the system that turns what you know into who comes to you."

Dunford: fails the market-category component outright (invented category standing in for a ghostwriting rename — her own genius.md calls this "a disaster"); competitive alternatives absent. Haynes: fails cold-offer sequencing — opens "I architect" (self before buyer), buries the one strong line ("who comes to you") at the end, and "Authority Flywheel" is undecoded jargon sitting exactly where scanner-mode legibility is required.

---

## WINNING ONE-LINER

> **"For S&C coaches who know they're good but freeze at the blank page: Authority Flywheel turns your own voice memos into posts that sound like you — not a ghostwriter borrowing your name, not another quarter waiting on referrals."**
> — April Dunford

**Why it wins:** the only candidate that explicitly names and defuses *both* documented buyer defaults — do-nothing/referrals (60% of TAM, Pre-Contemplation) and hire-a-ghostwriter (feared as "fake/performative," risk of guru-lumping) — while stating the real mechanism (voice-memo extraction) in the buyer's own language. Structurally cannot be misread as commodity ghostwriting because it names ghostwriting and excludes itself from it.

**What it's built to survive:** "Isn't this just a ghostwriter with a better name?" — the line pre-empts the objection by naming the alternative and drawing the line inline.

---

## RUNNER-UP 1

> **"You already have the expertise — I build the system that gets clients finding you, without you writing a single post."**
> — Jeremy Haynes

**Dies on:** "Sounds like every other ghostwriter." It leads with buyer self-interest and removes the specific avoided task (writing/LinkedIn), which generic reach-promise copy doesn't do — but it never names a competitive alternative, so a skeptical scanner has nothing that distinguishes it from any other done-for-you content pitch. Strongest for cold-scan legibility; weakest on differentiation.

---

## RUNNER-UP 2

> **"I help invisible experts become visible without becoming a guru — voice-memo-to-content, not ghostwriting."**
> — April Dunford

**Dies on:** it kills alternative (b) — hire-a-ghostwriter/guru fear — cleanly, but never touches alternative (a), the do-nothing/referral status quo that 60% of the TAM actually defaults to. Leaves the largest segment of the buyer pool unaddressed.

*(The original/current line is demoted out of the runner-up slots entirely — both experts independently scored it a structural fail, not a near-miss; see above.)*

---

## PRESERVED DISSENT — Naming Alternatives vs. Cold-Scan Legibility

This is a genuine, load-bearing disagreement in method, not a wording quibble. It should decide which line ships, not be smoothed over.

**Tension:** Should a cold one-liner explicitly name and negate the competitive alternatives (Dunford's 5-component doctrine), even at the cost of length and one instance of a branded term ("Authority Flywheel")? Or should it lead purely with buyer self-interest/outcome, strip self-reference and jargon entirely, and trust the removed-friction promise to differentiate on its own (Haynes' Two-Cliffs cold-offer doctrine)?

| Expert | Position |
|---|---|
| April Dunford | The winning line **must** name the alternatives (referrals-as-default, ghostwriter-as-feared-option) inline, or the buyer's brain silently files the offer under "another agency with a fancier name" — this is the exact failure mode of the current line and of any rewrite that omits it. Category/alternative-naming is not optional decoration; it is the load-bearing component of positioning. |
| Jeremy Haynes | Cold audiences get a 30-second legibility test with zero prior context; self-reference ("I architect"), branded jargon ("Authority Flywheel"), and buried mechanism all cost decode time the line doesn't have. The winning cold-offer line leads with the buyer's own situation and desired outcome, sequences problem → circumstance → outcome → THEN mechanism, and only earns the right to use proprietary naming once trust is established — not in the opening hook. |

**Practical fork this creates:** Dunford's winner is right for a bio/profile/landing-page context where a reader has a few seconds of sustained attention and dwell time to parse a compound sentence. Haynes' runner-up is right for a true cold-stop context (DM opener, ad hook, first line of a cold outreach) where scan-speed dominates. Farrice should decide which surface this one-liner actually lives on before locking a single winner — the two experts are not disagreeing about craft quality, they're optimizing for different attention conditions.
