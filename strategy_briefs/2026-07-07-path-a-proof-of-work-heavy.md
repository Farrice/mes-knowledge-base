## 1. Answer

**Build the Named-Brand Claim Redline & Rescue.**

Six of eight lenses (McKay, Harding, Deya, Hiette, Sky Tan, and Herk in substance) converged independently — from risk, buyer-psychology, contrarian, evidence-audit, simplicity, and mechanism angles — on the same core artifact, and each explicitly rejected the same wrong answer: a mock portfolio / sample deck / fictional case study. A polished hypothetical is just the 40th agency saying the same thing prettier (Herk), an argument a compliance buyer is professionally trained to distrust (Harding), planning theater (Deya), and whistling on the steps of Carnegie Hall (Sky Tan).

**Corrected framing (verification changed this).** The aggregate's load-bearing premise — "compliance-first agencies show ZERO public proof of funded-brand clients" — was **REFUTED**. Independent verification found compliance-first healthcare agencies (Hedy & Hopp, 1nessAgency) publishing public case studies with named results (30% appointment lift, 394% lead increase, 88% conversion increase for Hospital Sisters Health System). The market is NOT proofless. The strategy therefore cannot rest on "we are the only proof in a proof-free category." What survives, and what the artifact still delivers, is a narrower and honestly defensible wedge: **generic case studies are retrospective and other-people's-results; a redline of the prospect's OWN live ad copy is proof-of-work about THEM, before any engagement exists.** No agency's case-study page can compete with a document that already did the anxious internal debate their legal team runs every week — on their actual creative. (Caveat: verification did not confirm whether those agencies' clients are venture-funded startups specifically; a Day-1 spot-check of the 3–5 nearest competitor agencies is still worth 30 minutes before writing any market-gap language into outreach. Write none until then.)

### The artifact

1. **Pick one real, verifiably funded health/wellness brand** in the sports-nutrition beachhead 🟢 GROUNDED (beachhead per path decision) — funding confirmed via Crunchbase/press, currently running live ad creative with visible claim exposure (Meta Ad Library / public IG). **UNCONFIRMED that a findable candidate exists today** — this is the build's first kill-switch: verify Day 1 before committing anything downstream. General Meta enforcement patterns (e.g., a collagen brand's ad disabled over "helps improve skin in just 7 days") make the premise plausible but no specific instance is verified.
2. **Redline their actual live copy**: flag the specific lines carrying FTC substantiation exposure or FDA structure/function boundary issues, each citation anchored to the real guidance clause (FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance / NAD precedent) — never opinion. The honesty spine is non-negotiable; one wrong flag converts the differentiator into the same unverified assertion the market is drowning in (McKay + Harding, independently).
3. **Rewrite side-by-side, in the brand's own voice** — equally persuasive, claim-safe, the exact line not a generic example (Deya's "in their voice" + Sky Tan's "fix without killing the persuasion").
4. **Conditionally add the AI-citation test** (Hiette/Herk/Deya): manually query ChatGPT/Perplexity/Google AI Overviews for the brand's category term, screenshot who gets cited, show whether claim-safe framing wins. **UNCONFIRMED and possibly backwards**: verification found no study supporting "claim-safe rewrites are more citable by AI search," and some AEO sources indicate AI Overviews favor direct, unhedged factual statements and deprioritize hedged language — which would cut AGAINST the claim. Include ONLY if the live Day-3 test shows real, screenshot-able lift for this brand's category; otherwise cut it and ship the pure redline (per Fork 4's resolver). Never assert citation lift in outreach without the screenshot in hand.

### Legal risk (named-brand flags without consent)

**UNCONFIRMED — no qualified legal opinion exists.** McKay names this the single failure point (defamation exposure if a flag is wrong). Consensus mitigations stand: private 1:1 delivery only, gift framing, every flag citation-anchored to real regulatory text, zero "gotcha" publication. Truth/accuracy is generally a defamation defense, which is why the citation-anchor discipline is load-bearing — but "manageable residual risk" is a projection, not a vetted judgment. Treat Wang's public-essay variant (Fork 1) as carrying materially higher unvetted legal exposure than the private route; do not execute the public variant without a real legal read.

### Packaging

- **One page** (before/after redline + named flags) **+ a 3–5 minute Loom walkthrough**. No agency bio, no capabilities deck, no pricing, no positioning vocabulary — the words "compliance-first," "trusted," "FTC-safe" never appear (Harding: received language reads as pre-fabricated to someone trained to detect pre-fabrication).
- The felt message, never stated: *this person has already run the anxious internal debate our legal team runs every week.*

### Delivery

- **Private, 1:1, cold or warm, by name** to that brand's founder/CMO (or legal-ops/marketing lead), gift-framed: "Saw this ad, thought one claim was a word away from a problem — here's the fix, no ask." Majority position (McKay, Harding, Hiette, Sky Tan, Deya); see Fork 1 for the public-essay dissent.
- **Escalation is a renamed nano-offer, not a call**: "Claim-Risk & Citation Audit," $500–1,500 beta price in exchange for testimonial/case-study rights — that testimonial becomes credible public proof (Deya + Hiette, independent convergence). 🔴 PROJECTED pricing.
- **Success metric**: treat >5% reply rate as a working heuristic, not a validated cutoff — **UNCONFIRMED**: published 2026 cold-email benchmarks range from 0.45% (Belkins, strict net-new B2B) to 5–8% ("good" per Apollo/Instantly), with contradictory C-suite figures (4.2% vs 6.4%) and no data for the funded-brand-executive ICP. Use the paired qualitative signal as the real validator: wording reciprocation — the buyer repeating the reframe back is the belief installed (Hiette). Opens, views, likability are vanity. If replies land between 1–5%, read it as signal-with-friction and iterate wording before declaring the channel dead.
- **Sequencing discipline**: publicize NOTHING until the first conversion (McKay: win the Target PO quietly before dictating distributor terms). If diagnostics land and nobody replies, the wording is wrong, not the mechanism — refine the message, don't rebuild the artifact (Herk).

### Build plan (target 5 days, solo, existing stack, $0 paid APIs)

**Timeline is UNCONFIRMED** — a self-labeled projection (takes ranged 3–6 days), subject to normal planning-fallacy inflation. The sequencing logic is the firm part: Day 1: source candidate brand(s), verify funding, pull live claims (Meta Ad Library + Crunchbase — kill-switch if no candidate; also run the 30-min competitor case-study spot-check). Day 2: compliance redline + real regulatory citations — the highest-risk, least-compressible step runs first (McKay); if it overruns, let the schedule slip rather than the citation quality. Day 3: claim-safe rewrites in brand voice + run the AI-citation test live (include/cut decision). Day 4: one-pager + Loom (record once, constraint-shipping beats polish — Bolton). Day 5: deliver by name; queue brands #2–3 (Hiette's 2–3-brand batch as the reply-rate denominator).

## 2. Forks (preserved verbatim from Aggregate)

### Fork 1 — Private 1:1 delivery vs. public essay-as-distribution
- **Private-first** (McKay, Harding, Hiette, Sky Tan; Deya mostly): the artifact is a 1:1 belief-shift weapon aimed at a named buyer; publishing generically recreates the "converged language, zero proof" trap and adds legal exposure. Publicize only after it converts.
- **Public-first** (Dan Wang, alone but coherent): the artifact IS the distribution — a "Compliance Theater Audit" essay tearing down 12–15 funded brands' claims travels inside a small networked niche precisely because of its named specificity; a private dossier has no reason to travel and a solo operator has no other channel. Anonymizing kills it.
- **Resolver**: empirical, two weeks. Send 3–5 private redlines Week 1; if reply rate >5% or one audit books, private wins and Wang's essay becomes the post-conversion amplifier (both camps accept this sequencing). If replies are ~0 after wording iteration, the private channel is falsified and the public essay is the tested next move, using the same research corpus.

### Fork 2 — Document vs. live tool (Infrastructure)
- **Document/dossier** (7 of 8): a specific redline of the prospect's own ad is the proof a first client buys.
- **Tool** (Bolton, alone): documents scale like agency labor (cost per prospect); build a narrow one-claim-category claim-safe content generator, demo'd via a 5–8 min screen recording — zero marginal cost per prospect, and "tools get credited as you."
- **Resolver**: unit economics matter at prospect #10, not prospect #1. If the redline converts within 2–3 weeks, the tool is deferred (build it as delivery infrastructure for the client, not as proof). If 10+ personalized sends produce zero conversions, the fixed-asset demo becomes the next format test. Note: Bolton's "neon-sign rule" (one asset, all channels route to it) survives the fork and applies to whichever artifact wins.

### Fork 3 — One brand deep vs. multiple brands
- **Exactly one** (McKay, Sky Tan): one clamp beats five loose ones; splitting a one-week build across brands reproduces the shallow-proof failure mode.
- **2–3 named brands** (Hiette; Herk scales to 5–10 after validating one): a batch gives the reply-rate metric a denominator; n=1 is unmeasurable.
- **Resolver**: depth-first sequencing settles it — build brand #1 to full McKay/Harding depth (Days 1–4), then replicate the now-proven template for #2–3 on Day 5+. If per-brand replication takes >1 day, depth was the constraint and one-brand purism was right.

### Fork 4 — AI-citation angle: differentiator or dilution
- **Include** (Herk, Hiette, Deya): the citation-lift test is the belief-shift payload and the only element no compliance consultancy offers — it reframes compliance from cost to growth asset.
- **Exclude/pure-compliance** (McKay, Harding, Sky Tan — by omission and by simplicity doctrine): every added element loosens the clamp; the redline alone is the recognition moment, and an unproven citation claim risks the honesty spine.
- **Resolver**: run the citation test live on Day 3 before deciding. Real lift on screenshot = include (it's checkable, satisfying Harding's spine). Ambiguous or no lift = cut it, ship the pure redline. The artifact decides, not the aggregator.

### Fork 5 — Direct cold vs. Trojan Horse partner channel
- **Direct to founder/CMO** (everyone but Herk).
- **Partner-first** (Herk): route white-label through a compliance-adjacent partner (RD network, FTC consultant, nutrition PR) who already has funded-brand trust, rev-share; fall back to direct cold with Honest Underdog framing if no partner bites in week one.
- **Resolver**: parallelize cheaply — Herk's own fallback concedes direct works; send direct Week 1 while making 2–3 partner asks in the same week. Whichever produces the first booked audit wins the channel.
