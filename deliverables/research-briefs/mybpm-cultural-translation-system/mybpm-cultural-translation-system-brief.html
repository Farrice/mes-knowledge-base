01

the verdict

WHAT CHANGED

Afterimage Uniform is no longer the assumed Capsule 001 concept. Afterimage can remain a campaign and photography language, but it has not earned the right to become the product idea.

The stronger strategic territory is not quieter festival merch. It is an attainable-premium wardrobe for people whose rave identity is part of ordinary life—built through a repeatable method that translates scene-specific cultural material into garments without erasing origin.

Capsule 001 should prove the parent worldview before a single genre takes over. This guide recommends ROOM 001 / COMMON GROUND as the lead territory, but holds the final lock for Farrice's felt verdict. Drop economics and the five genre lenses are executable now regardless of which territory wins.

what ABAKADA actually teaches

✓

Begin with a source community that can recognize itself.

ABAKADA's name, Baybayin, jeepneys, sampaguita, Philippine flag imagery, language, and +63 references come from Filipino cultural memory. The value is recognition without an explanatory caption—not decoration applied after a garment is chosen.

✓

Keep the source stable while the garment language evolves.

Published references show Filipino codes moving through graphic tees, jerseys, lounge sets, washed fleece, ultra-wide hoodies, shorts, accessories, outerwear, and a larger Penshoppe wardrobe. The visual source remains coherent while cut, material, and product role gain range.

VERIFIEDThe Gatepost · Filipino fashion profile ↗
✓

A cultural artifact becomes stronger when it changes the garment—not only the print.

ABAKADA's sports references become paneled jerseys and oversized forms; faded dye and proportion elevate familiar streetwear. The deeper move is medium selection: decide whether a source wants to become typography, construction, trim, material, proportion, or a full silhouette.

Community participation completes the meaning.

ABAKADA and collaborators frame the clothes as Filipino pride, return to roots, community events, and support for local artists. The garment is a carrier of participation and representation, not the endpoint of the cultural work.

07

the moat, if MyBPM earns it

NOT A TAGLINE

The moat is not knowing that house came from Chicago or that jungle used pirate radio. Those facts are available to everyone. The moat is repeatedly converting cultural fluency into products that insiders recognize, outsiders desire, and a small team can actually produce.

MyBPM needs five accumulating assets: a provenance ledger for every capsule; a living archive of artifacts and participant stories; a fixed translation grammar; a community recognition panel spanning identities and genres; and a product ladder that reinvests paid demand into better forms.

The primary purchase trigger is belonging. Individualism supports it, but superiority must not lead. The desired thought is not “I look richer than them.” It is “this was made by someone who knows what the room means.”

09

genre lens 01 · house

SANCTUARY + RELEASE

ROOT: Chicago's Black, Latino, and largely gay dance-floor communities; The Warehouse, Frankie Knuckles, Ron Hardy, radio mixes, extended records, and collective release.

SOURCE ARTIFACTS: membership and guest records, room addresses, handbills, mix logs, record labels, drum-machine buttons, wooden floors, sweat-darkened cotton, a hand raised when the track opens.

FORMAL DIALS: circular repetition inside a strong grid; generous movement; warm black, mineral white, oxblood or amber as the rare genre cue; soft structure, rib, mesh, satin piping, and relaxed drape.

GARMENT LANGUAGE: a draped heavyweight tee, roomy track trouser, or softly paneled jersey. Let rhythm appear through repeated seams, spacing, or trim—not a waveform.

REJECT: acid-smiley default, checkerboard nostalgia, disco-ball costume, generic “HOUSE MUSIC ALL NIGHT LONG,” and any history that erases the Black queer sanctuary at the source.

10

genre lens 02 · techno

MACHINE SOUL + FUTURE

ROOT: Black Detroit invention, the Belleville Three and wider Detroit network, Afrofuturist imagination, machine technology, automotive city logic, and global exchange—without rewriting the origin as Berlin monochrome.

SOURCE ARTIFACTS: equipment model plates, assembly marks, city infrastructure, technical manuals, radio transmissions, Exhibit 3000 objects, futuristic record sleeves, and city-code systems.

FORMAL DIALS: exact modular grids, interruption, matte hardware, dense black and graphite against Signal White, narrow reflective or cobalt cue, cropped and engineered proportion.

GARMENT LANGUAGE: paneled zip layer, precise heavyweight tee, straight or articulated trouser, hidden utility, serial labels, and construction that feels engineered rather than tactical cosplay.

REJECT: random chrome gothic type, fake security warnings, Berlin-only brutalism, gas masks, circuit-board wallpaper, and dystopia without Detroit's authorship or soul.

11

genre lens 03 · jungle / drum & bass

TRANSMISSION + VELOCITY

ROOT: Black British and Caribbean hybridity, Jamaican sound-system practice, UK hardcore mutation, breakbeats, pirate radio, dubplates, MCs, white labels, and the compressed communication network of early-1990s rave.

SOURCE ARTIFACTS: station frequencies, pager and phone listings, acetate labels, MC cue sheets, record-shop walls, flyer hierarchies, rewind marks, speaker cloth, and reflective roadwear.

FORMAL DIALS: dense information against large quiet fields; cut-and-reassemble rhythm; high-visibility green or transport orange as one cue; ripstop, mesh, nylon, wide track proportion, and zipped ventilation.

GARMENT LANGUAGE: paneled wind layer, wide track trouser, zip-neck jersey, or heavyweight tee where type behaves like broadcast information.

REJECT: lions and jungle animals as default, random graffiti, Union Jack shorthand, faux-ragga language, breakbeat diagrams without meaning, and treating Black British lineage as a decorative “90s rave” texture.

12

genre lens 04 · trance

DURATION + ASCENT

ROOT: multiple lineages rather than one origin story—early Frankfurt and German trance, Goa's transnational party culture, Balearic sunrise narratives, and later global scenes organized around extended buildup, immersion, release, and return.

SOURCE ARTIFACTS: route directions, gates, full-moon dates, set duration, tape and CD sequencing, horizon light, translucent scrim, star position, condensation, and dawn color shift.

FORMAL DIALS: elongated vertical movement; phased gradients used sparingly; translucent layers over dense cotton; midnight blue, mineral, silver, and a dawn-peach or spectral cue.

GARMENT LANGUAGE: long-sleeve layer, translucent shell, fluid trouser, adjustable hood or hem, and sequencing marks that reward close inspection.

REJECT: third eyes, sacred geometry, borrowed religious symbols, generic mandalas, rainbow fractals, and “spiritual” language that turns a complex transnational culture into exotic decoration.

13

genre lens 05 · bass

PRESSURE + CONSTRUCTION

ROOT: bass is not one lineage. For the first MyBPM lens, name the branch. A dubstep / sound-system branch begins with Jamaican sound-system institutions, dub, UK garage, Croydon record shops, dubplates, local testing, and physical sub pressure. An American festival-bass branch carries a different history and visual vocabulary. Never merge them silently.

SOURCE ARTIFACTS: speaker-cabinet plans, hand-painted sound names, flight cases, cable maps, record-shop stickers, dubplate stamps, load-in gloves, room-pressure notes, and barrier wear.

FORMAL DIALS: weight, negative space, stacked rectangles, reinforcement, low placement, matte black, wood brown used only when materially justified, and one electric cue.

GARMENT LANGUAGE: structured oversized tee, reinforced hoodie, quilted or paneled outer layer, utility bag, or heavy wide trouser. The form should feel load-bearing.

REJECT: monster faces, slime, teeth, random wobble graphics, speaker cones pasted on chest, “headbanger” caricature as the whole audience, and uncredited borrowing from Jamaican sound-system culture.

AFTERIMAGE IS NOT THE DEFAULT

three Capsule 001 territories

These are territories to react to, not production approvals. Each starts with a cultural truth, not a garment name.

01

01 · ROOM 001 / COMMON GROUND — recommended

Cultural truth: the room is temporary, but the belonging is real. Source artifacts: floor plan, room stamp, membership mark, address, capacity, meeting point, door time, and the people who make the floor possible. Product translation: an entry tee with a restrained room mark; a paneled jersey or hoodie whose seams map movement through the room; a carry object; and a future wide trouser or overshirt. It can introduce Night Society without choosing one genre, and later lenses can change the room's tempo without changing the parent identity.

02

02 · SIGNAL CHAIN / THE NIGHT IS BUILT

Cultural truth: a rave is labor and infrastructure before it is spectacle. Source artifacts: selector, operator, light, door, cable, case, speaker stack, load-in, soundcheck, and handoff. Product translation: construction lines, reinforcement, modular pockets, serial labeling, and one workwear-derived future silhouette. Strongest route toward technical garments; highest risk of techno-uniform cliché or uncredited sound-system borrowing.

03

03 · ARCHIVE OF MOTION / EVERY SCENE LEAVES A GRAMMAR

Cultural truth: scenes transmit through flyers, radio, records, routes, bodies, and repeated rituals. Source artifacts: broadcast frequencies, white labels, set-time grids, dance notation, ticket stock, and print wear. Product translation: collectible graphic chapters with a fixed MYBPM archive system, one genre-specific construction change per release, and a growing provenance ledger. Strongest long-term content system; risk is becoming museum merch unless silhouettes and community participation keep it alive.

15

the representation gate

NO FALSE FLUENCY

Every capsule needs a source ledger naming the people, place, artifact, and lineage used. If the source belongs to a community Farrice does not personally represent, move from extraction to collaboration: invite participants, pay creative contributors when production begins, and let them veto flattening or false claims.

A hero piece passes only when: one insider can identify the cultural truth without a caption; the garment is desirable after every symbol is removed; no sacred, protected, or community-specific mark is used as filler; the source materially changes form or construction; and the product page can name provenance without pretending MyBPM invented it.

Diversity is an advantage only when it creates more authorship, more specificity, and better products. A moodboard containing many cultures is not diversity.

the cash-safe drop

01

Sample one

Approve garment, print, fit, wash, color, placement, packaging, and photographed truth.

→
02

Preview the source

Show the cultural artifact, participant voice, and translation choices before asking for money.

→
03

Build a qualified waitlist

Capture email or SMS, product preference, size, price comfort, and purchase timing.

→
04

Open a bounded paid preorder

Use a clear close date, delivery estimate, refund path, and minimum paid-unit threshold.

→
05

Release production cash only after the gate

If the minimum is missed, refund or offer the pre-disclosed alternative. Do not fund hope with reserves.

→
06

Fulfill and publish the receipt

Report on-time rate, defects, refunds, retained contribution, and the next silhouette funded.

Interest is not demand. Money collected is not profit. Each step must earn the next one.
