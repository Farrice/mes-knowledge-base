# ABAKADA to MYBPM

> MYBPM · BRAND REFERENCE + DROP SYSTEM · window: 2018–Sep 1, 2026 · lens: brand · product · culture · pre-order economics · sources: 9 web sources · 9 visual references · 5 MyBPM canon files · compiled: sep 1, 2026

A source-grounded reference guide to how a small Chicago Filipino streetwear label turned cultural fluency into product depth, community demand, and larger collaborations. It shows how MyBPM can adapt the operating principles without borrowing the identity.

## the verdict
_Study the operating engine, not the clothes_
ABAKADA is a useful benchmark because its growth is legible. It began with a clear cultural premise, built a repeatable symbol vocabulary, expanded into more distinctive garment forms, and used bounded releases and collaborations to widen the world. The visible proof is longevity, repeated sell-through claims, a retail shop, and progressively larger partnerships. Revenue and profit remain unverified.

MyBPM should keep one permanent worldview: Raver as Identity Keeper / Night Society. Genres can act as seasonal lenses inside it. The first drop should stay ethos-led rather than genre-led. That preserves recognition, reaches more of the audience, and creates a clean baseline before house, techno, trance, bass, or drum & bass capsules change the texture.

Print-on-demand is the low-risk production bridge, not the final design ceiling. Waitlist interest chooses what deserves a paid pre-order. Paid orders fund production. Retained contribution after fulfillment pays for the next sample, custom silhouette, or tech pack.

## why ABAKADA qualifies as a benchmark
- Founded in Chicago: **2018** (Arvin Boyon + Calvin Calma)
- Disclosed pre-order window: **4–6 weeks** (official product page)
- Out-of-stock restock SKUs: **37** (snapshot; not proof of paid sell-through)
- Pieces in PENSHOPPE collaboration: **23** (announced for Oct 2026)

## what is actually supported
- **The premise was culturally specific from day one.** [VERIFIED] — ABAKADA was founded in Chicago in 2018 by Arvin Boyon and Calvin Calma. The name comes from the traditional Filipino alphabet; the brand repeatedly uses Baybayin, the Philippine eagle, +63, jeepneys, sampaguita, language, and diaspora experience as design material. (https://www.galingstudio.com/brands/abakada)
- **The brand built a translation system, not a single logo trick.** [VERIFIED] — Across published product and editorial references, cultural codes move through minimal graphics, illustrative tees, lounge sets, sports jerseys, washed fleece, patchwork outerwear, knits, bottoms, and accessories. The source stays stable while the garment language evolves. (https://www.fsugatepost.com/post/the-stitch-shining-a-light-on-filipino-fashion)
- **Pre-order and waitlist mechanics are part of the operating model.** [VERIFIED] — An official product page labels a distressed patch zip-up as PRE-ORDER and discloses a 4–6 week production/fulfillment window after the pre-order closes. An indexed password-gated storefront page asked visitors to join the waitlist while a collection was in production or fulfillment. (https://abkdco.com/products/distressed-patch-zip-up-black)
- **The growth path progressed from small-team fulfillment to broader cultural distribution.** [LIKELY] — Multiple current profiles describe apartment-complex beginnings, collections selling out within hours, and a physical retail shop. Those are credible, repeated secondary claims, but no audited sales or profit data was found. (https://lifestyleasia-onemega.com/style/penshoppe-designer-lab-and-abakada-launch-streetwear-collection/)
- **Collaborations compound the original story rather than replacing it.** [VERIFIED] — The 2023 DBTK collaboration connected a Chicago diaspora brand with a Philippine streetwear label. The 2024 Toyota/Complex/NTWRK project translated ABAKADA's visual language into anime and race-inspired merchandise. The output included a one-of-one letterman jacket. PENSHOPPE then announced a 23-piece Chicago-to-Manila collection for October 2026. (https://www.complex.com/pop-culture/a/kylie-mar/toyota-grip-anime-series-drops-abakada-merch)
- **Out-of-stock labels are not the same as sales evidence.** [VERIFIED] — The official RESTOCK page showed 37 products as out of stock when indexed. That proves the merchandising state of the page, not why the inventory is unavailable, how many units existed, or whether the drop was profitable. (https://abkdco.com/collections/restock)

## the visible growth arc
- 2018 · **Cultural premise locked** — Founded in Chicago around a name and mission that could keep generating product: the Filipino alphabet as foundation, heritage as active design material.
- 2020 · **Recognizable capsule vocabulary** — Coverage highlighted minimal graphics, pastel and light shades, Baybayin, Filipino sayings, and named UBE and Forest collections. These were early signs of collection worlds rather than logo-only merch.
- 2023 · **Diaspora-to-homeland collaboration** — DBTK x ABKD joined Chicago and Philippine streetwear through shared graphics, exclusive colorways, tees, hoodies, shorts, and full-look styling.
- 2024 · **Brand-world translation** — Toyota GRIP moved ABAKADA into anime, racing graphics, heavyweight hoodies, premium patchwork, embroidery, and an extremely limited letterman jacket.
- 2025 · **Product depth + pre-order discipline** — The catalog shows outerwear, washed denim, mohair, fleece, knits, pants, accessories, and pre-order products with explicit fulfillment windows.
- 2026 · **Scaled distribution without abandoning the premise** — PENSHOPPE announced a 23-piece limited collection and an event pre-order before the full October launch. This is current as of Sep 1; the public launch has not happened yet.

## ABAKADA's transferable engine
Lived source → Recognizable codes → Contemporary form → Bounded release → Community proof → World expansion
  - Lived source: Filipino language, memory, symbols, diaspora tension
  - Recognizable codes: Baybayin, +63, eagle, phrases, colors
  - Contemporary form: tees → knits → jerseys → outerwear → full looks
  - Bounded release: waitlist, pre-order, limited capsule, explicit window
  - Community proof: wearing, events, sell-through, recurring cast
  - World expansion: artists, brands, retailers, broader distribution

## what MyBPM should take
- **Borrow the principle**: One inexhaustible source (Raver identity, ritual, care, memory, transition), Capsule coherence (one story, several product roles, one release window), Demand-funded ambition (paid proof funds better forms)
- **Translate for MyBPM**: Cultural fluency (use insider rave rituals without explaining the joke), Genre lenses (change pacing and silhouette, not parent identity), Community portraits (same people across event, club, and ordinary life)
- **Do not copy**: Filipino symbols (Baybayin, +63, eagle, jeepney, flag codes belong to their story), Exact layouts or garments (no split ABKD lettering, duplicated jersey blocks, or traced graphics), Earth-tone palette (MyBPM's white/black/acid system is already more ownable)
- **Hold until proof**: Custom cut-and-sew (quote, tech pack, sample, MOQ, and paid demand first), Large collection (three to five pieces until buying patterns exist), Genre sub-brands (fragment recognition and inventory too early)

## the MyBPM brand architecture
One permanent world; rotating lenses; product ambition earned by paid behavior.
1. **Permanent worldview: Night Society** — The brand serves the person whose rave identity survives outside the event. PLUR beyond the dance floor, afterimage, found family, ritual, endurance, and return are the durable source material.
2. **Seasonal capsule lens: ethos first, genre second** — Capsule 001 should prove the broad identity. Later capsules may use house, techno, bass, drum & bass, or trance to alter motion, typography, material, and silhouette without creating separate brands.
3. **Product ladder: signal → uniform → silhouette → artifact** — Signal is an accessible tee or object. Uniform is the repeatable heavyweight core. Silhouette is the one new garment form. Artifact is the scarce, higher-craft piece that demonstrates where the brand is going.
4. **One cultural code per hero piece** — Use a coat-check number, wristband residue, transit time, water handoff, room stamp, set-time grid, or after-hours phrase as the single anchor. Layering every rave symbol at once becomes costume.
5. **Preserve the current visual lock** — MYBPM wordmark, Signal White, Sublevel Ink, Mineral White, one Frequency Acid cue, sharp geometry, near-blank fronts, raster-backed statements, and no generic lasers/equalizers remain the recognition system.

## how each genre changes the dial
The source is always the raver's lived experience. A genre only changes how that source is expressed.
1. **House: exchange + repetition** — Human proximity, shared motion, loops, warm flashes, relaxed knits or sport shapes, rounder typographic rhythm. Keep the direction away from predictable disco-ball imagery.
2. **Techno: endurance + architecture** — Functional pockets, modular placements, washed black, reflective micro-detail, narrow technical labeling, hard negative space. Keep these details functional instead of borrowing generic hazard tape.
3. **Drum & bass: velocity + information** — Cropped outerwear, panel lines, transit maps, compressed type, asymmetric rhythm, sharp image slicing. Use the information density to organize the composition instead of covering it with random glitch overlays.
4. **Trance: release + afterimage** — Translucent layers, edge light, optical trails, softer gradients, lifted proportions, white as atmosphere. Rainbow-neon nostalgia would flatten the idea.
5. **Bass: pressure + body memory** — Heavyweight fleece, low-center graphics, dense raster fields, oversized forms, sub-frequency diagrams, tactile decoration. Speaker and waveform icons are too literal.

## recommended first capsule: Afterimage Uniform
A three-piece paid test that can be fulfilled through the existing POD bridge while creating one clear step toward original product.
1. **Hero: heavyweight near-blank tee** — Small exact MYBPM front; one photographic/raster back built around the moment the room ends but the body is still carrying the beat. DTG is the first sample path for tonal detail and transparency.
2. **Uniform: heavyweight hoodie or zip-up** — Quiet chest or split-seam mark, one sleeve/hood locator, oversized or drop-shoulder fit. It should feel like the garment someone reaches for after the venue, not event merch.
3. **Signal object: cap, compact bag, or earplug case** — A lower-commitment object widens entry without adding another large graphic tee. Choose only what can be sampled and fulfilled reliably now.
4. **One experimental direction, shown but not produced** — Reveal a cropped technical shell, paneled jersey, or custom trouser as the future artifact. Let waitlist preference and paid orders decide which tech pack earns funding.
5. **Campaign: one identity, three environments** — The same cast and hero looks move through festival/open air, club/nightlife, and daily life/city. Afterimage is connective tissue: coat check, transit, food stop, and blue hour. It is not a separate gritty brand.

## cash-safe capsule sequence
Sample one → Preview the world → Build qualified waitlist → Open 7-day pre-order → Lock production cash → Fulfill + report → Fund the next rung
  - Sample one: fit, print, wash, photography; no premium claim before proof
  - Preview the world: story, hero look, price band, delivery expectation
  - Build qualified waitlist: email/SMS + size + preferred piece + price comfort
  - Open 7-day pre-order: full payment for proven POD pieces; clear close date
  - Lock production cash: COGS, fees, tax, refund/defect reserve are untouchable
  - Fulfill + report: weekly status, delay consent/refund process, receipts
  - Fund the next rung: retained contribution pays for one sample or tech pack

## the pre-order math
Do not define success by gross sales. Per paid order, first reserve landed garment cost, decoration, packaging, transaction fees, subsidized shipping, taxes, and a refund/defect buffer. What remains is contribution; it becomes profit only after the order is fulfilled and all obligations are covered.

Minimum paid units = ceiling((sample + photography + setup + contingency) ÷ contribution per unit). Example only: if fixed launch cost is $600 and true contribution is $24 per paid order, the drop needs 25 paid units to cover those costs. At a modeled 15% waitlist-to-order conversion, that implies roughly 167 qualified waitlist members. The conversion rate is UNTESTED until MyBPM runs its first launch.

For the current POD bridge, collect full payment only on products whose supplier, sample, price, and delivery basis are known. For a future cut-and-sew experiment, use a refundable deposit or reservation until the factory quote, tech pack, sample, MOQ, and delivery range are real. Never spend the production reserve on content or the next capsule.

## internal decision blocks
**Capsule generation brief**
```
Create one MyBPM Night Society capsule from a lived rave ritual, not a genre moodboard. Preserve the MYBPM wordmark, Signal White / Sublevel Ink / Mineral White / one Frequency Acid cue, near-blank fronts, and one strong back statement. Produce exactly three sellable product roles: hero, uniform, and signal object. Add one unproduced future silhouette. Use the selected genre only to change pacing, material, typography, and shape. Reject lasers, equalizers, generic waveform art, festival slogans, and copied streetwear layouts.
```
**Go / no-go check**
```
Do not open paid pre-orders until one sample passes, landed cost and contribution are known, the supplier supports the advertised ship window, product/size preference is collected, and the minimum paid-unit threshold is calculated. Waitlist joins are interest. Paid orders are demand. Retained contribution after fulfillment is profit evidence.
```
**Future silhouette gate**
```
Choose one custom silhouette only when retained contribution can cover the quoted tech pack and at least two sample rounds without touching production reserves, and when paid demand concentrates around a clear product/fit request. Build one new form, not a collection.
```

Secondary reports of fast sellouts and apartment beginnings are credible but not audited. The official out-of-stock page does not prove how many units sold.
