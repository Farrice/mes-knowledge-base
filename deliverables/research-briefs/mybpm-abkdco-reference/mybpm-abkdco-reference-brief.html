MYBPM · BRAND REFERENCE + DROP SYSTEM

ABAKADA to MYBPM

A source-grounded reference guide to how a small Chicago Filipino streetwear label turned cultural fluency into product depth, community demand, and larger collaborations. It shows how MyBPM can adapt the operating principles without borrowing the identity.

01

the verdict

Study the operating engine, not the clothes

ABAKADA is a useful benchmark because its growth is legible. It began with a clear cultural premise, built a repeatable symbol vocabulary, expanded into more distinctive garment forms, and used bounded releases and collaborations to widen the world. The visible proof is longevity, repeated sell-through claims, a retail shop, and progressively larger partnerships. Revenue and profit remain unverified.

MyBPM should keep one permanent worldview: Raver as Identity Keeper / Night Society. Genres can act as seasonal lenses inside it. The first drop should stay ethos-led rather than genre-led. That preserves recognition, reaches more of the audience, and creates a clean baseline before house, techno, trance, bass, or drum & bass capsules change the texture.

Print-on-demand is the low-risk production bridge, not the final design ceiling. Waitlist interest chooses what deserves a paid pre-order. Paid orders fund production. Retained contribution after fulfillment pays for the next sample, custom silhouette, or tech pack.

what is actually supported

Claim discipline
✓

The premise was culturally specific from day one.

ABAKADA was founded in Chicago in 2018 by Arvin Boyon and Calvin Calma. The name comes from the traditional Filipino alphabet; the brand repeatedly uses Baybayin, the Philippine eagle, +63, jeepneys, sampaguita, language, and diaspora experience as design material.

VERIFIEDGaling Studio brand profile ↗
✓

The brand built a translation system, not a single logo trick.

Across published product and editorial references, cultural codes move through minimal graphics, illustrative tees, lounge sets, sports jerseys, washed fleece, patchwork outerwear, knits, bottoms, and accessories. The source stays stable while the garment language evolves.

VERIFIEDThe Gatepost · product overview ↗
✓

Pre-order and waitlist mechanics are part of the operating model.

An official product page labels a distressed patch zip-up as PRE-ORDER and discloses a 4–6 week production/fulfillment window after the pre-order closes. An indexed password-gated storefront page asked visitors to join the waitlist while a collection was in production or fulfillment.

VERIFIEDABAKADA official product page ↗
✓

The growth path progressed from small-team fulfillment to broader cultural distribution.

Multiple current profiles describe apartment-complex beginnings, collections selling out within hours, and a physical retail shop. Those are credible, repeated secondary claims, but no audited sales or profit data was found.

the visible growth arc

Selected milestones · Not a complete company history
2018

Cultural premise locked

Founded in Chicago around a name and mission that could keep generating product: the Filipino alphabet as foundation, heritage as active design material.

2020

Recognizable capsule vocabulary

Coverage highlighted minimal graphics, pastel and light shades, Baybayin, Filipino sayings, and named UBE and Forest collections. These were early signs of collection worlds rather than logo-only merch.

2023

Diaspora-to-homeland collaboration

DBTK x ABKD joined Chicago and Philippine streetwear through shared graphics, exclusive colorways, tees, hoodies, shorts, and full-look styling.

2024

Brand-world translation

Toyota GRIP moved ABAKADA into anime, racing graphics, heavyweight hoodies, premium patchwork, embroidery, and an extremely limited letterman jacket.

2025

Product depth + pre-order discipline

The catalog shows outerwear, washed denim, mohair, fleece, knits, pants, accessories, and pre-order products with explicit fulfillment windows.

2026

Scaled distribution without abandoning the premise

PENSHOPPE announced a 23-piece limited collection and an event pre-order before the full October launch. This is current as of Sep 1; the public launch has not happened yet.

the MyBPM brand architecture

One permanent world; rotating lenses; product ambition earned by paid behavior.

01

Permanent worldview: Night Society

The brand serves the person whose rave identity survives outside the event. PLUR beyond the dance floor, afterimage, found family, ritual, endurance, and return are the durable source material.

02

Seasonal capsule lens: ethos first, genre second

Capsule 001 should prove the broad identity. Later capsules may use house, techno, bass, drum & bass, or trance to alter motion, typography, material, and silhouette without creating separate brands.

03

Product ladder: signal → uniform → silhouette → artifact

Signal is an accessible tee or object. Uniform is the repeatable heavyweight core. Silhouette is the one new garment form. Artifact is the scarce, higher-craft piece that demonstrates where the brand is going.

04

One cultural code per hero piece

Use a coat-check number, wristband residue, transit time, water handoff, room stamp, set-time grid, or after-hours phrase as the single anchor. Layering every rave symbol at once becomes costume.

05

Preserve the current visual lock

MYBPM wordmark, Signal White, Sublevel Ink, Mineral White, one Frequency Acid cue, sharp geometry, near-blank fronts, raster-backed statements, and no generic lasers/equalizers remain the recognition system.

Lenses, not new identities

how each genre changes the dial

The source is always the raver's lived experience. A genre only changes how that source is expressed.

01

House: exchange + repetition

Human proximity, shared motion, loops, warm flashes, relaxed knits or sport shapes, rounder typographic rhythm. Keep the direction away from predictable disco-ball imagery.

02

Techno: endurance + architecture

Functional pockets, modular placements, washed black, reflective micro-detail, narrow technical labeling, hard negative space. Keep these details functional instead of borrowing generic hazard tape.

03

Drum & bass: velocity + information

Cropped outerwear, panel lines, transit maps, compressed type, asymmetric rhythm, sharp image slicing. Use the information density to organize the composition instead of covering it with random glitch overlays.

04

Trance: release + afterimage

Translucent layers, edge light, optical trails, softer gradients, lifted proportions, white as atmosphere. Rainbow-neon nostalgia would flatten the idea.

05

Bass: pressure + body memory

Heavyweight fleece, low-center graphics, dense raster fields, oversized forms, sub-frequency diagrams, tactile decoration. Speaker and waveform icons are too literal.

Ethos-led baseline

recommended first capsule: Afterimage Uniform

A three-piece paid test that can be fulfilled through the existing POD bridge while creating one clear step toward original product.

01

Hero: heavyweight near-blank tee

Small exact MYBPM front; one photographic/raster back built around the moment the room ends but the body is still carrying the beat. DTG is the first sample path for tonal detail and transparency.

02

Uniform: heavyweight hoodie or zip-up

Quiet chest or split-seam mark, one sleeve/hood locator, oversized or drop-shoulder fit. It should feel like the garment someone reaches for after the venue, not event merch.

03

Signal object: cap, compact bag, or earplug case

A lower-commitment object widens entry without adding another large graphic tee. Choose only what can be sampled and fulfilled reliably now.

04

One experimental direction, shown but not produced

Reveal a cropped technical shell, paneled jersey, or custom trouser as the future artifact. Let waitlist preference and paid orders decide which tech pack earns funding.

05

Campaign: one identity, three environments

The same cast and hero looks move through festival/open air, club/nightlife, and daily life/city. Afterimage is connective tissue: coat check, transit, food stop, and blue hour. It is not a separate gritty brand.

12

the pre-order math

Modeled · Replace with live quotes

Do not define success by gross sales. Per paid order, first reserve landed garment cost, decoration, packaging, transaction fees, subsidized shipping, taxes, and a refund/defect buffer. What remains is contribution; it becomes profit only after the order is fulfilled and all obligations are covered.

Minimum paid units = ceiling((sample + photography + setup + contingency) ÷ contribution per unit). Example only: if fixed launch cost is $600 and true contribution is $24 per paid order, the drop needs 25 paid units to cover those costs. At a modeled 15% waitlist-to-order conversion, that implies roughly 167 qualified waitlist members. The conversion rate is UNTESTED until MyBPM runs its first launch.

For the current POD bridge, collect full payment only on products whose supplier, sample, price, and delivery basis are known. For a future cut-and-sew experiment, use a refundable deposit or reservation until the factory quote, tech pack, sample, MOQ, and delivery range are real. Never spend the production reserve on content or the next capsule.

what this guide cannot prove

ABAKADA's revenue, margins, team size, production contracts, exact launch funnel, customer acquisition cost, waitlist conversion rate, refund rate, and inventory financing are UNCONFIRMED. Secondary reports of fast sellouts and apartment beginnings are credible but not audited. The official out-of-stock page does not prove how many units sold.

The PENSHOPPE collection is announced for October 2026; as of Sep 1, 2026, the launch and its market outcome are future events. Visual references are used for internal analysis only. Rights remain with ABAKADA, PENSHOPPE, DBTK, the photographers, and their publishers; do not reuse them in MyBPM campaigns.

The FTC rule requires a reasonable basis for an advertised shipping time. If shipment is delayed, the customer must be offered a choice to consent to the delay or cancel for a prompt refund. Shopify also requires a pre-order app and supports full, partial, or no payment depending on the setup. This guide is operational research, not legal advice.
