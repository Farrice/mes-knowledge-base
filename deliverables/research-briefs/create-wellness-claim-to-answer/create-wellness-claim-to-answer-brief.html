the finding in 30 seconds

CLAIM-TO-ANSWER GAP

Create's public materials carry strong product-quality signals. One support answer viewed for this review says stacking the gummies and electrolyte product gives more than 5mg of creatine, while the product pages use grams. That is likely a unit typo.

The higher-value issue is consistency: dose, expected benefits, and certification scope need to point to the right evidence every time a customer, retailer, creator, or search system asks the question.

what is supported and what needs review

THE EVIDENCE
✓

Three Watermelon gummies provide 4.5 g of creatine monohydrate.

The current product page publishes the serving amount; NSF's database lists the Watermelon product with a three-gummy serving.

VERIFIEDCreate product page ↗
✓

The support article's 5mg language is likely a unit error.

The text is public and verified. The intended unit and total stacked dose require Create's internal confirmation before correction.

LIKELYCreate support center ↗
✓

NSF Certified for Sport is strong quality proof for the listed product.

NSF describes checks for label contents, contaminants, prohibited substances, and manufacturing controls. The certification scope does not establish benefit efficacy.

Sharper focus and a 2-to-3-week benefit window need an exact evidence match.

the smallest useful repair

Begin with the Watermelon gummies. Keep the work small enough to verify and useful enough to improve the next creative push.

01

Correct the answer layer

Confirm the intended stacked dose, unit, products, and serving assumptions before changing the support article.

02

Map four phrases to proof

Review sharper focus, clean energy, benefits in 2-3 weeks, and cognitive performance against the exact product, dose, delivery form, and population.

03

Separate quality proof from benefit proof

Use NSF certification for the quality questions it answers. Give benefit language its own evidence trail and review owner.

04

Build three approved-proof creative routes

Once the evidence ceiling is known, turn the strongest approved facts into campaign ideas without widening the claims.
