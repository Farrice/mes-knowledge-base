# Rello: creatine has a Tuesday problem

> Proof specimen · public-source only · window: Public sources checked September 3, 2026 · lens: Meta creative strategy · sports nutrition · claim-safe spec work · sources: Rello site + Meta Ad Library + NSF + launch release · compiled: September 3, 2026

A buyer-inspectable Creative Test Autopsy showing how product truth becomes a human tension, a sharper point of view, and a controlled test without inventing account performance or health claims.

## the diagnosis
_Decision, not decoration_
Rello is qualified for a public-source specimen. The brand has a live DTC product, nationwide Sprouts availability, current NSF Certified for Sport listings for both bar flavors, and active Meta advertising.

Four active Rello ads found in the Meta Ad Library use the same primary text and near-identical lo-fi creator pattern. The visible work makes a rational case by combining protein, creatine, clean ingredients, strength, recovery, energy, and everyday performance. Rello's origin story contains the more human opening: nutrition should not feel like a second job.

INTERPRETATION: buyers may believe in creatine and still abandon the tub because the routine breaks before the ingredient does. The missed emotion is the quiet self-blame of another half-finished habit. That makes adherence friction worth testing against the current product-led control. A discipline-first explanation remains plausible, so this is a falsifiable creative hypothesis rather than an account diagnosis.

## two concepts worth testing
Concept A isolates the strongest owned mechanism. Concept B preserves the product's performance breadth but gives it a cleaner entry point.
1. **A · The tub you meant to finish** — Target: creatine-aware active professionals with a half-used powder and a routine that keeps losing to real mornings. Tension: every missed scoop feels like a discipline failure. Emotional release: fix the format instead of blaming the person. Proof: current label facts, portable format, and NSF listing. Best first vessel: lo-fi creator scene built around the abandoned tub.
2. **B · Make the snack earn its place** — Target: practical optimizers who resent carrying several single-job products. Tension: a gym-bag snack should do more than occupy space. Promise: 20g protein plus 1.2g creatine in one bar. Proof: product panel and ingredient list. Best first vessel: headliner static, then a matched creator video if the message earns a second test.

## six hooks
**Concept A · private recognition**
```
A1  Your half-used creatine tub may have a format problem.

A2  The best supplement routine is the one that survives a late Tuesday.

A3  You remembered the gym bag. The creatine is still on the counter.
```
**Concept B · useful ambition**
```
B1  If a snack takes up room in your gym bag, make it earn the space.

B2  This bar brought 20 grams of protein. It also brought creatine.

B3  A protein bar for people who hate carrying the rest of the routine.
```

## one creator script
**Lo-fi product demonstration**
```
OPEN · 6:42 a.m. Close on a half-used creatine tub. A hand grabs keys and a gym bag, then leaves the scoop behind.

VOICE: The creatine tub is still half-full. Your calendar is not.

TENSION · Follow the rushed kitchen exit. Keep the tub framed on the counter.

VOICE: By the time you leave for work, the routine has already asked for a scoop, a shaker, water, timing, and a memory your morning did not have.

TURN · The hand drops a Rello bar into the side pocket of the bag.

VOICE: Rello puts 20 grams of protein and 1.2 grams of creatine into a bar that can leave the house with you.

PROOF · Tight shots of the current wrapper and nutrition panel.

VOICE: The current bar also lists 9 grams of fiber. Both flavors appear in NSF's Certified for Sport directory.

BOUNDARY · Creator holds the bar; no transformation imagery or outcome promise.

VOICE: It does not replace a complete supplement plan, and one amount will not fit everyone. It gives one part of the routine a better chance of surviving the day.

CTA · If the tub keeps winning the counter, read the label and see whether Rello fits the day.

PRODUCTION NOTE · First-person experience is allowed only when true and documented. Final spoken claims and NSF mark usage require brand approval.
```

## one static brief
**1:1 and 4:5 production specification**
```
GOAL · Make a familiar failure visible before presenting the product.

HEADLINE · Creatine, for people who keep forgetting creatine.

SUPPORT · 20g protein · 1.2g creatine · 9g fiber

PROOF CHIP · NSF Certified for Sport, only after the exact mark usage is approved.

VISUAL HIERARCHY · Headline first. A sharp wrapper and broken-open bar sit inside an open gym bag. The half-used tub remains behind on the kitchen counter, small but recognizable.

CTA · Meet Rello

DESIGN · High contrast, restrained package palette, generous empty space, no athlete collage, and no benefit-icon cloud.

ONE-SECOND TEST · A stranger should understand that Rello puts creatine into a protein bar for an easier-to-carry routine.

HOLD BACK · strength gains, cognition, longevity, skin, gut, energy, recovery, cost savings, and replacement claims. Those would turn one tension back into a product inventory.
```

## the test sequence
0 · Claim lock → 1 · Hook test → 2 · Read the signal → 3 · Format swap → 4 · Concept challenger → 5 · Learning record
  - 0 · Claim lock: Brand approves the exact label facts, NSF mark usage, creator language, offer, and landing-page match before production.
  - 1 · Hook test: Keep the Concept A body, creator, edit, CTA, audience, and offer constant. Change only A1, A2, or A3.
  - 2 · Read the signal: The media buyer defines spend sufficiency and evaluates hold, click, conversion, and downstream quality. Public spec work supplies no threshold.
  - 3 · Format swap: Move the winning Concept A wording into the headliner static. This tests video versus static without changing the core message.
  - 4 · Concept challenger: Only after a usable vessel signal, introduce Concept B in the same winning format. Do not change concept and format together.
  - 5 · Learning record: Log what ran, spend, audience, dates, hook definition, conversion result, fatigue state, and the next decision.

## strict proof boundaries
_What this specimen cannot know_
VERIFIED: current product facts, public brand language, current NSF listings, Sprouts availability, and the four active Meta ads observed.

LIKELY: habit friction is underrepresented in the visible active sample and deserves a controlled test.

PLAUSIBLE ALTERNATIVE: the existing product-led message may already match the strongest buyer motivation, or inconsistent use may not be a meaningful objection for Rello's actual customers.

UNCONFIRMED: spend, CTR, hold rate, CPA, ROAS, audience breakdown, conversion volume, creative winner status, customer objections, creator rights, inventory depth, contribution margin, landing-page performance, and approved claims.

REVERSAL CONDITION: if account data or customer language shows no adherence friction, or if the current control clearly outperforms the habit concept under matched conditions, retire this angle. Active does not mean winning; repeated copy does not mean fatigue; public-facing language does not prove demand. No concept launches until the client supplies account context, approved claims, production constraints, and an approval owner.
