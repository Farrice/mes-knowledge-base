# Rello Creative Test Autopsy

Public-source specimen, checked September 3, 2026.

## The diagnosis

Rello is qualified for this specimen. The protein bars are available through the brand's storefront and Sprouts. Both flavors appear in NSF's current Certified for Sport directory. Rello is also running paid social: four active ads found in Meta's public library started between August 12 and August 18.

Those ads use the same primary copy and roughly 41-to-43-second lo-fi creator videos. That does not prove the ads are tired or ineffective. It shows one defensible test opening. The visible creative keeps protein, creatine, clean ingredients, strength, recovery, energy, and everyday performance together. Rello's own origin story gives us a narrower idea to test: make creatine easier to remember by putting it inside a protein bar the buyer already carries.

## Two concepts

### A. One less supplement ritual

This is for an active professional who already understands creatine but struggles to keep another powder in the daily routine. The obstacle is whether another scoop survives a real workday. Show the bar moving into a gym or work bag while the tub stays on the counter. Use the current product facts and NSF listing as proof.

### B. The protein bar with a performance layer

This is for a protein-bar buyer comparing labels. Most bars make protein the whole story. Rello lists 20 grams of protein, 1.2 grams of creatine, and 9 grams of fiber in one bar. Put that difference on the first screen, then let the product page carry the wider ingredient story.

## Six hooks

Concept A:

1. If the hardest part of creatine is remembering the scoop, put it in the snack you already carry.
2. My protein bar started replacing one more tub on the counter.
3. 20 grams of protein. 1.2 grams of creatine. No shaker bottle.

Concept B:

1. Most protein bars stop at protein. This one adds 1.2 grams of creatine.
2. The label is short. The job list is not.
3. A performance bar for people who are done managing a supplement stack.

## Creator script

Open on a creatine tub, shaker, and Rello bar sitting on a kitchen counter.

"If your creatine routine keeps dying at the scoop, this is the idea behind Rello."

Pack the tub away and put the bar in a work or gym bag.

"Another powder only helps if it survives your actual day. Rello puts 20 grams of protein and 1.2 grams of creatine into a bar you can carry."

Move into close shots of the wrapper and nutrition panel.

"The current bar also lists 9 grams of fiber. Both flavors appear in NSF's Certified for Sport directory."

Finish with the product in hand, without body-transformation imagery.

"One bar is not every supplement, and one amount does not fit everyone. This is a simpler way to put creatine into a snack routine you already have. If the scoop is the step you keep skipping, read the label and see whether Rello fits."

A creator may not present this as personal experience unless it is true and documented. Rello's reviewer approves every spoken claim before production.

## Static brief

Use a headliner static in 1:1 and 4:5.

Headline: "The protein bar with creatine built in."

Supporting line: "20g protein · 1.2g creatine · 9g fiber"

Place the headline first, then a centered wrapper and broken-open bar. Keep a shaker and scoop small and out of focus in the background. Use the current Rello package palette and generous empty space. The only call to action is "See the ingredients."

The one-second test is simple: a stranger should understand that this is a protein bar with creatine. Leave strength gains, cognition, longevity, skin, gut, energy, recovery, cost savings, and replacement language out of this asset.

## Test sequence

First, lock the exact label facts, NSF mark usage, creator language, offer, and landing-page match. Keep one current ad as the control.

Next, test the three Concept A openings with the same creator, body, edit, audience, offer, and call to action. The media buyer decides when spend is sufficient and reads the account-level result.

Move the winning Concept A wording into the headliner static. This separates the format test from the message test. If Concept A produces no useful signal, introduce Concept B in the better-performing format. Log the dates, spend, audience, hook definition, conversion result, fatigue state, and next decision.

## Proof boundaries

Verified: current product facts, public positioning, current NSF listings, Sprouts availability, and four active Meta ads.

Likely: the habit-friction message is underrepresented in the visible active sample and deserves a controlled test.

Unconfirmed: spend, click-through rate, hold rate, acquisition cost, return on ad spend, audience breakdown, conversion volume, winner status, customer objections, creator rights, inventory depth, margin, landing-page performance, and approved claim language.

Active does not mean winning. Repeated copy does not prove fatigue. Public language does not prove demand. Nothing launches until Rello supplies account context, approved claims, production constraints, and an approval owner.
