# Rello: Creatine Has a Tuesday Problem

Public-source specimen, checked September 3, 2026.

## The diagnosis

Rello is qualified for this specimen. The protein bars are available through the brand's storefront and Sprouts. Both flavors appear in NSF's current Certified for Sport directory. Rello is also running paid social: four active ads found in Meta's public library started between August 12 and August 18.

Those ads use the same primary copy and roughly 41-to-43-second lo-fi creator videos. That does not prove the ads are tired or ineffective. The visible work makes a rational case by combining protein, creatine, clean ingredients, strength, recovery, energy, and everyday performance. Rello's origin story contains the more human opening: nutrition should not feel like a second job.

Buyers may believe in creatine and still abandon the tub because the routine breaks before the ingredient does. The missed emotion is the quiet self-blame of another half-finished habit. That makes adherence friction worth testing against the current product-led control. A discipline-first explanation remains plausible, so this is a falsifiable creative hypothesis rather than an account diagnosis.

## Two concepts

### A. The tub you meant to finish

This is for an active professional with a half-used powder and a routine that keeps losing to real mornings. Every missed scoop can feel like a discipline failure. The emotional release is permission to fix the format instead of blaming the person. Show the abandoned tub before Rello enters the scene. Use current label facts, portable format, and the NSF listing as proof.

### B. Make the snack earn its place

This is for the practical optimizer who resents carrying several single-job products. A gym-bag snack should do more than occupy space. Rello lists 20 grams of protein and 1.2 grams of creatine in one bar. Put those two jobs on the first screen and let the product page carry the wider ingredient story.

## Six hooks

Concept A:

1. Your half-used creatine tub may have a format problem.
2. The best supplement routine is the one that survives a late Tuesday.
3. You remembered the gym bag. The creatine is still on the counter.

Concept B:

1. If a snack takes up room in your gym bag, make it earn the space.
2. This bar brought 20 grams of protein. It also brought creatine.
3. A protein bar for people who hate carrying the rest of the routine.

## Creator script

Open at 6:42 a.m. on a half-used creatine tub. A hand grabs keys and a gym bag, then leaves the scoop behind.

"The creatine tub is still half-full. Your calendar is not."

Follow the rushed kitchen exit while the tub remains framed on the counter.

"By the time you leave for work, the routine has already asked for a scoop, a shaker, water, timing, and a memory your morning did not have."

Drop a Rello bar into the side pocket of the bag.

"Rello puts 20 grams of protein and 1.2 grams of creatine into a bar that can leave the house with you."

Move into close shots of the wrapper and nutrition panel.

"The current bar also lists 9 grams of fiber. Both flavors appear in NSF's Certified for Sport directory."

Finish with the product in hand, without body-transformation imagery.

"It does not replace a complete supplement plan, and one amount will not fit everyone. It gives one part of the routine a better chance of surviving the day. If the tub keeps winning the counter, read the label and see whether Rello fits the day."

A creator may not present this as personal experience unless it is true and documented. Rello's reviewer approves every spoken claim before production.

## Static brief

Use a headliner static in 1:1 and 4:5.

Headline: "Creatine, for people who keep forgetting creatine."

Supporting line: "20g protein · 1.2g creatine · 9g fiber"

Place the headline first. Put a sharp wrapper and broken-open bar inside an open gym bag. Leave the half-used tub behind on the kitchen counter, small but recognizable. Use the current Rello package palette and generous empty space. The only call to action is "Meet Rello."

The one-second test is simple: a stranger should understand that this is a protein bar with creatine. Leave strength gains, cognition, longevity, skin, gut, energy, recovery, cost savings, and replacement language out of this asset.

## Test sequence

First, lock the exact label facts, NSF mark usage, creator language, offer, and landing-page match. Keep one current ad as the control.

Next, test the three Concept A openings with the same creator, body, edit, audience, offer, and call to action. The media buyer decides when spend is sufficient and reads the account-level result.

Move the winning Concept A wording into the headliner static. This separates the format test from the message test. If Concept A produces no useful signal, introduce Concept B in the better-performing format. Log the dates, spend, audience, hook definition, conversion result, fatigue state, and next decision.

## Proof boundaries

Verified: current product facts, public positioning, current NSF listings, Sprouts availability, and four active Meta ads.

Likely: the habit-friction message is underrepresented in the visible active sample and deserves a controlled test.

Plausible alternative: the current product-led message may already match the strongest buyer motivation, or inconsistent use may not be a meaningful objection for Rello's actual customers.

Unconfirmed: spend, click-through rate, hold rate, acquisition cost, return on ad spend, audience breakdown, conversion volume, winner status, customer objections, creator rights, inventory depth, margin, landing-page performance, and approved claim language.

Retire the angle if account data or customer language shows no adherence friction, or if the current control clearly outperforms it under matched conditions. Active does not mean winning. Repeated copy does not prove fatigue. Public language does not prove demand. Nothing launches until Rello supplies account context, approved claims, production constraints, and an approval owner.
