# Rello: open on the scoop. Pack the bar.

> Founder proof object · public-source only · window: Public sources checked September 3, 2026 · lens: Protein bar · creatine routine · active Meta creative · sources: Rello site + Meta Ad Library + NSF · compiled: September 3, 2026

Current public ads list the ingredients. This test puts the tub, scoop, shaker, and bar in one frame, then takes only the bar.

## the message gap
_What the public creative shows_
Rello already has a clear product difference: 20g protein and 1.2g creatine in one bar. Four active Meta ads observed September 3 lead with that ingredient stack, then add clean ingredients, strength, recovery, energy, and everyday performance. The facts are useful, but the viewer still has to translate them into a reason to care.

Rello's own brand story supplies that reason. It says powders, scoops, and separate products can make nutrition feel like a second job. The next test should put that problem on camera: the tub and shaker stay on the counter; the bar goes into the bag. That does not prove routine friction drives sales. It creates one clean challenger to the current product-led message.

## what supports the diagnosis
- **Rello is actively running paid social.** [VERIFIED] — Four active Meta ads were observed with start dates between August 12 and August 18, 2026. They repeat the same primary product copy. (https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=US&q=Rello&search_type=keyword_unordered)
- **The product and offer are live.** [VERIFIED] — Chocolate Crunch is listed at $53.99 for 12 bars with 20g protein, 1.2g creatine, and 9g fiber per bar. (https://relloco.com/products/chocolate-protein-bar?variant=47322736722107)
- **Reduced routine friction is owned brand language.** [VERIFIED] — Rello says nutrition should not feel like a second job and contrasts its format with powders, scoops, and separate products. (https://relloco.com/pages/about-us)

## two different tests
1. **A · Show the step Rello removes** — Audience: someone already familiar with creatine who does not want another powder-and-shaker step. First frame: bar, tub, scoop, and shaker on the same counter. Action: the bar goes into the bag; the other objects stay behind. Promise: protein and creatine without mixing. Proof: the current label and NSF listing.
2. **B · Make the ingredient difference impossible to miss** — Audience: a protein-bar buyer comparing products. First frame: Rello's front label beside an ordinary protein-bar silhouette. Message: most bars lead with protein; Rello contains 20g protein plus 1.2g creatine. Hold fiber, clean ingredients, recovery, energy, and the rest of the stack until after the difference is understood.

## the first three seconds
**Concept A · visual before explanation**
```
FRAME 1 · A Rello bar, creatine tub, scoop, and shaker on a kitchen counter.

FRAME 2 · A hand drops the bar into a work or gym bag. The tub and shaker stay on the counter.

ON-SCREEN · Protein + creatine. Nothing to mix.

WHY IT WORKS · The viewer sees the preparation step disappear before the ingredient list begins.

DO NOT SHOW · A body transformation, a supplement stack thrown in the trash, or any claim that one bar replaces an individual's full regimen.
```

## six example hooks
**Concept A · remove the mixing step**
```
A1  The creatine tub stays on the counter. This goes in your bag.

A2  If mixing creatine is the step you keep missing, Rello puts it in a protein bar.

A3  20 grams of protein. 1.2 grams of creatine. No tub or shaker to pack.
```
**Concept B · show the product difference**
```
B1  Most protein bars stop at protein. Rello adds 1.2 grams of creatine.

B2  Two numbers make this a different protein bar: 20g protein and 1.2g creatine.

B3  Rello puts protein and creatine in the same wrapper.
```

## what the test would teach
**Keep the body fixed; change the opening**
```
CONTROL · The current ingredient-led opening.

CHALLENGER · The counter-to-bag visual above, followed by the same creator, offer, body, and CTA.

MORE ATTENTION, SAME CONVERSION · The routine problem may earn the stop without strengthening the purchase case.

BETTER CLICK AND CONVERSION QUALITY · Build the next variants around the preparation step Rello removes.

NO MOVEMENT · Drop the angle and keep the product-led message. The point of the test is to learn, not to defend the idea.
```

## visible unknowns
UNCONFIRMED: spend, sales, winning-ad status, CPA, ROAS, audience mix, customer objections, creator rights, margin, inventory depth, and approved claims. Active does not mean winning. Repeated copy does not prove fatigue. The public evidence supports testing the removed mixing step; it does not prove routine friction is a real customer objection. If customer language or account data contradicts that premise, retire the angle. Brand review must also resolve the product page's 1g versus 1.2g FAQ wording.
