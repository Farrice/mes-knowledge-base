## the evidence verdict
_CATEGORY PROVEN · OFFER UNTESTED_
Buyers are budgeting for lead qualification, follow-up, CRM integration, proposals, onboarding, and workflow assessment. Sellers list simple builds from the low hundreds through $1,500, while broader systems and assessments reach several thousand. Farrice's wedge is not raw automation; it is diagnosis, an acceptance test, human review, documentation, and final payment tied to demonstrated behavior.

- **Professional-service firms report use-case and expertise friction.** [VERIFIED] — RSM reports 47% naming lack of in-house expertise and 42% difficulty identifying the right AI use case. (https://rsmus.com/content/dam/rsm/insights/industries/financial-services/infographic-2025-rsm-ai-survey-bps-insights.pdf)
- **SME use remains shallow in core activities.** [VERIFIED] — The OECD reports that only 29% of gen-AI-using SMEs use it in core business activities. (https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/ai-adoption-by-small-and-medium-sized-enterprises_9c48eae6/426399c1-en.pdf)
- **The exact offer is not proven.** [VERIFIED] — Marketplace budgets, supplier prices, reviews, and earnings establish category activity. They do not establish collected payment for Farrice's package. (evidence taxonomy)

## where the lane is
1. **Lead with the money-path handoff** — Inquiry, qualification, proposal, and follow-up appear repeatedly across buyer demand and can be demonstrated in five days.
2. **Price above commodity setup** — $1,500 is defended by diagnosis, tests, documentation, human hold, and acceptance-based payment—not by adding tools.
3. **Keep supplements second** — Real demand exists, but claim substantiation and regulatory boundaries raise proof and delivery burden.
