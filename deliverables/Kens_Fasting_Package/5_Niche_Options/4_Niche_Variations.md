# 4 Niche Variations — Choose Your Angle

*Pick ONE of these before you build anything. This decision determines your product name, content angle, and who you're talking to.*

---

## Option 1: Fasting for Shift Workers & Nurses (RECOMMENDED)

### Why This One

You ARE this. 10+ year psych nurse on 12-hour shifts. Zero competition. The SWIFt Study (2025, Monash University) provides peer-reviewed scientific backing for this exact angle. Your audience finds themselves instantly: "I have the same schedule!"

### The Numbers

- **Competition:** Zero dedicated products from practicing nurses about fasting
- **Demand:** "Intermittent fasting for nurses" is an active, rising search query
- **Reddit:** Shift worker posts in r/intermittentfasting consistently get high engagement
- **Scientific backing:** SWIFt Study validates IF specifically for shift workers

### The Risk

Smaller initial audience than going broad. But the conversion rate will be dramatically higher because the specificity creates instant trust. Someone who sees "The Night Shift Fast" and works night shifts thinks "this was made for me" — and that's worth more than a million generic impressions.

### Content Angle

**"The Night Shift Fast"** — every piece of content shows how you navigate fasting during 12-hour psych ward shifts. Real footage. Real meals. Real struggles.

### Product Name

**$7:** "The Night Shift Fast: A Nurse's Guide to Fasting When Your Schedule Says You Can't"
**Upsell:** "The 12-Hour Shift Fasting Protocol" — 30 days of specific meal timing for nurses and shift workers

### Sample Hooks

- "I eat one meal at 3 AM. Here's why I'm in the best shape of my life."
- "Every nurse on my unit struggles with weight. I do the opposite. This is what I do differently."
- "I fasted for 18 hours during a psych ward shift. Watch what happened."
- "Night shift destroys your metabolism. Unless you do this."

## Option 2: Fasting for Busy Professionals / Executives (BROADER)

### Why This One

Larger addressable market. "Executives" and "entrepreneurs" is aspirational positioning. More people identify as "busy professionals" than "shift workers."

### The Numbers

- **Competition:** Medium — several fasting influencers target this demographic
- **Demand:** High volume, but also high noise
- **Market size:** Much larger than the nurse niche

### The Risk

More competition. More generic. Harder to stand out. And here's the thing: you don't have corporate executive proof. You have nurse proof. Claiming to help executives when your experience is night-shift nursing creates a credibility gap.

### Content Angle

**"The Fasting Executive"** — positioning around high-performance decision-makers who don't have time for complicated diets.

### Product Name

**$7:** "The 72-Hour Fasting Pre-Flight Checklist for High Performers"
**Upsell:** "The 30-Day Fasting Lifestyle Blueprint"

### Sample Hooks

- "If you're working 50+ hours a week and your waistline is expanding... stop dieting."
- "I used to think losing weight meant Tupperware containers and 2 hours at the gym."
- "The biological loophole busy professionals are using to burn fat on autopilot."

## Option 3: Fasting for Men Over 35 in High-Stress / Physical Jobs

### Why This One

Bridges nursing with broader blue-collar and high-stress professions — firefighters, police, military, construction, EMS. Your psych nurse experience covers the "high-stress" angle. This is a bigger market than nursing alone, but still specific enough to build trust.

### The Numbers

- **Competition:** Low — some generic "fasting for men" content exists, but nothing specific to high-stress/physical professions
- **Demand:** Growing — these professions share the same schedule chaos as nursing
- **Crossover:** Firefighters, police, military all work 12+ hour shifts with unpredictable breaks

### The Risk

You'd need to extend your proof beyond nursing. "I'm a nurse" resonates with nurses. Does it resonate with firefighters? Maybe — the shift work connection is real. But it's a stretch compared to Option 1.

### Content Angle

**"Fasting Under Fire"** — the protocol for people who can't control their schedule, can't control their environment, and can't afford to lose focus on the job.

### Product Name

**$7:** "The Impossible Schedule Fast: How to Burn Fat When You Can't Control When You Eat"
**Upsell:** "The 30-Day Shift Protocol for High-Stress Professionals"

### Sample Hooks

- "I fast during 12-hour shifts where I can't take a break. Here's the system."
- "Your schedule is chaos. Your eating doesn't have to be."
- "The vending machine used to own me. Not anymore."

## Option 4: General Fasting for Beginners (WIDEST)

### Why This One

Maximum audience size. Easiest to start. Everyone knows what fasting is and plenty of people are curious.

### The Numbers

- **Competition:** Very high — thousands of fasting guides, courses, YouTube channels
- **Demand:** Massive but saturated
- **Market size:** Huge, but you're competing with everyone

### The Risk

Maximum competition. Completely undifferentiated. Why would anyone buy your guide over the 10,000 free YouTube videos? You have no unfair advantage in this space. Generic positioning = generic results.

### Content Angle

Standard "here's how to fast" content — lowest differentiation of all four options.

### Product Name

**$7:** "The 72-Hour Fasting Survival Protocol"
**Upsell:** "The 30-Day Fasting Lifestyle Blueprint"

### Sample Hooks

Generic hooks that compete with every other fasting creator online. Not recommended.

## Our Recommendation

**Start with Option 1 (Shift Workers/Nurses)** or **Option 3 (High-Stress Jobs).**

- **Option 1 is the "easiest win."** Your proof is undeniable. The competition is zero. The audience self-selects instantly. And you can always broaden later.

- **Option 3 is the "bigger play"** if you want growth room from day one. It includes nurses but extends to firefighters, police, military, and other shift workers.

**The key insight:** You can always go from narrow to broad. It's nearly impossible to go from broad to narrow. Start specific. Prove it works. Expand when you're ready.

## The North Star Test

*(From Nicolas Cole)*

> "You don't have a position until you can name a real human being who IS your ideal client."

Before you pick an option, answer this: **Can you name ONE specific person — a coworker, a friend, someone you know — who IS your ideal customer?**

If you pick Option 1 and immediately think of your buddy on the night shift who keeps complaining about gaining weight — that's your answer. That person becomes your North Star. Every piece of content, every line of copy, every product decision gets filtered through: "Would this help [that person]?"

If you can't name someone, the niche is too abstract. Pick the option where you can.
