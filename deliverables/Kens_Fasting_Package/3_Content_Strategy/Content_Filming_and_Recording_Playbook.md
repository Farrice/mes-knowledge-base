# Content Filming & Recording Playbook

*How to actually produce your content — equipment, methods, frameworks, and expert insights.*

---

## The Philosophy: Document, Don't Create

You don't need to "create content." You need to **document what you already do.** Every shift you work, every meal you prep, every fast you complete — that's content. You just need to capture it.

Production quality doesn't matter. Authenticity does. A shaky iPhone video of you breaking a fast at 3 AM in the hospital break room will outperform a professionally shot talking-head video every single time.

---

## Equipment (Keep It Simple)

| What | Recommendation | Why |
|------|---------------|-----|
| **Camera** | Your iPhone. That's it. | The audience expects authentic, not polished. iPhone quality is more than enough for Instagram Reels. |
| **Lighting** | Natural light when possible. No ring light needed. | Ring lights scream "influencer." Real environments (nurse station, kitchen, gym) feel genuine. |
| **Audio** | Built-in iPhone mic for most content. $15 clip-on lav mic for voiceovers. | Clear voice matters more than perfect audio. The lav mic is optional but helps in noisy environments. |
| **Editing** | CapCut (free) or InShot (free) | Both handle text overlays, trending audio, cuts, and transitions. CapCut is slightly better for Reels. |
| **Tripod** | $15 phone tripod from Amazon | For hands-free filming during meal prep, gym sessions, or direct-to-camera talking. |

**Total investment: $0-$30.** Don't let equipment be an excuse.

## The 4C Intro Framework (Use for Every Video)

*(From Caleb Ralston — Personal Brand Architecture)*

Every video should open with these 4 elements in the first 5-10 seconds:

1. **Credibility** — "I'm a 10-year psych nurse"
2. **Context** — "I work 12-hour night shifts"
3. **Challenge** — "Everyone told me I couldn't fast on this schedule"
4. **Commitment** — "Here's exactly what I do"

**Example opening:**
> "I've been a psych nurse for 10 years, working 12-hour night shifts. Everyone said fasting was impossible with my schedule. I've been doing it for 3 years. Here's my exact protocol."

You don't need all 4 every time — but the first video someone sees from you should hit all of them. After that, your audience already knows your credibility and context, so you can lead with the challenge or commitment.

## The Gift & Wrapping Paper Method

*(From Caleb Ralston)*

Think of every video as having two layers:

- **The Gift** = the actual value (your fasting protocol, your meal timing, your hydration strategy)
- **The Wrapping Paper** = the format that makes people click (trending audio, a specific hook structure, a visual gimmick)

**The gift stays the same. The wrapping paper changes weekly** based on what's trending.

**Example:**
- Gift: "How I time my eating window around a 12-hour night shift"
- Wrapping Paper v1: Time-lapse of your full shift with text overlays showing your fasting window
- Wrapping Paper v2: "POV: You're a nurse trying to fast on night shift" with trending audio
- Wrapping Paper v3: Direct-to-camera, myth-busting format: "Stop eating every 3 hours on night shift"

Same content, three different formats. Test which performs best. Double down on winners.

## The 8 Vicious Hook Principles

*(From Luke Iha — every video hook must follow these)*

1. **Relevant in the first line.** The viewer knows within 1 second this is for them.
2. **Emotionally charged language.** Use Germanic/earthy words, not corporate speak. "Crushed" not "impacted." "Starving" not "experiencing hunger."
3. **Natural intrigue.** The viewer can't predict what's coming next.
4. **Stakes.** Something real is at risk — health, energy, weight, career performance.
5. **Specificity as spice.** "I fasted for 18 hours during a psych ward shift" beats "I tried fasting at work."
6. **Doesn't read like an ad.** If it sounds like marketing, people scroll.
7. **Caveman-simple language.** Short words. Short sentences. A 12-year-old should understand every word.
8. **Consequence first, mechanism later.** Lead with what happened, not how. "I lost 20 pounds working night shift" THEN "Here's the protocol."

### Hook Examples for Your Content

- "I eat one meal at 3 AM. Here's why I'm in the best shape of my life."
- "Every nurse on my unit is overweight. I'm the opposite. This is what I do differently."
- "I fasted for 18 hours during a psych ward shift. Watch what happened."
- "The vending machine used to own me. Here's how I broke free."
- "I haven't eaten breakfast in 3 years. My doctor says my bloodwork is perfect."
- "Night shift destroys your metabolism. Unless you do this."
- "I used to crash at hour 8 of every shift. Now I have MORE energy at hour 12."

## Proof-First Architecture

*(From Luke Iha — Proof Ladder)*

The order matters. Most fasting content creators teach first and prove later. Flip it:

1. **Show your body** — what you look like, your energy, your health
2. **Show your results** — weight loss timeline, energy improvements, bloodwork
3. **Show your daily routine** — the actual fasting protocol in action
4. **THEN teach** — explain the why behind what they just saw

**Why this works:** People need to believe you before they'll listen to you. Proof creates belief. Teaching without proof is just another person with an opinion.

## Consumer Psychology: What They're Really Buying

*(From Dai Media — Consumer Posture)*

People don't buy fasting guides to solve a problem. They buy to access a **feeling**.

Your customers want to feel:
- **In control** — of their body, their schedule, their health
- **Disciplined** — like someone who has their life together
- **Energetic** — not dragging through shifts
- **Proud** — of how they look and feel
- **Like they have a secret weapon** — something most people don't know

**Film your content with these feelings in mind.** Don't just show what you eat. Show how it makes you FEEL. The energy at hour 12 of a shift. The confidence when you look in the mirror. The control of walking past the vending machine.

### Find ONE Real Person

Forget demographics. Find ONE specific person you know — a nurse coworker, a friend — who IS your ideal customer. Film every piece of content as if you're talking directly to them.

Ask yourself: **"What does my ideal customer think about at 2 AM when they can't sleep?"** That's the content that connects.

## Practical Filming Guide

### The "Document Don't Create" Method

1. Set your phone up (or have someone hold it)
2. Film your actual routine — meal prep, breaking your fast, working out, going through a shift
3. Add voiceover later in CapCut
4. Layer on text overlays with the teaching points

**Authenticity beats production quality every time.** A real 3 AM kitchen video > a studio-lit tutorial.

### Batch Filming

Film 5-7 Reels in one 2-hour session:

- [ ] Pick a day off
- [ ] Plan 5-7 concepts from your content pillars
- [ ] Film all direct-to-camera segments first (same location, same energy)
- [ ] Film all B-roll second (meal prep, cooking, gym, walking)
- [ ] Edit all 7 in one sitting
- [ ] Schedule them across the week

**Pro tip:** Wear the same outfit for videos you want to feel like they're from the "same day." Change outfits between batches for variety.

### The B-Roll Bank

Every shift, grab 30 seconds of B-roll footage. You'll use these clips in almost every Reel:

- [ ] Coffee pouring (black coffee = fasting staple)
- [ ] Walking hospital hallways
- [ ] The nurse station / break room
- [ ] Meal prep containers
- [ ] Checking your watch (time-restricted eating visual)
- [ ] Water bottle refills
- [ ] The gym (even just walking in)
- [ ] Sunrise/sunset from the hospital (shift transitions)
- [ ] Your meals — especially the big ones in your eating window

**Store these in a "B-Roll" album on your phone.** After 2 weeks, you'll have enough clips to make any Reel without filming new footage.

### Text Overlays (The Dominant Format)

The highest-performing Reels right now follow this pattern:

1. **Film the action** (you eating, working, exercising)
2. **Add the teaching through text overlays** in CapCut
3. **Use trending audio** or your own voiceover underneath

This works because viewers can watch on mute (most do) and still get the full message.

### Audio Strategy

Two approaches — test both in your first 2 weeks:

| Approach | When to Use | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------------|------|------|
| **Trending audio + text overlays** | Discovery Reels (Pillar 1: Myth-Busters) | Algorithm boost from trending sounds, works on mute | Less personal connection |
| **Direct-to-camera talking** | Trust Reels (Pillar 3: Lifestyle Proof) | Builds strongest connection, shows personality | Requires confidence on camera, needs clear audio |

**The 70/30 rule (from Caleb Ralston):** 70% proven formats that are already working for you. 30% experiments with new formats. Review weekly. Promote winning experiments to the 70%.

## Content Tagging System

*(From Grace Andrews — Media Company Architecture)*

Before filming ANY piece of content, tag it with its job on the trust pathway:

| Tag | Job | What It Looks Like |
|-----|-----|-------------------|
| **ATTENTION** | Stop the scroll, get new eyeballs | Myth-buster Reels, hot takes, surprising claims |
| **CONNECTION** | Make them feel like they know you | Behind-the-scenes, day-in-the-life, personal stories |
| **TRUST** | Prove you know what you're talking about | Educational carousels, deep-dive explanations, results |
| **CONVERSION** | Turn followers into buyers | Product mentions, CTA posts, testimonial shares |

**Weekly mix:** 2 Attention + 1 Connection + 1 Trust + 1 Conversion = 5 pieces/week

If you're not sure what job a piece of content is doing, don't film it. Every piece needs a clear purpose.

## The "Memorable Minutes" Rule

*(From Grace Andrews)*

> "Memorable minutes beat forgettable seconds."

Don't chase 7-second clips. Your 45-90 second Reels with real substance will outperform trend-chasing micro-clips because:
- Longer watch time = algorithm reward
- Substance = saves and shares (the metrics that matter most)
- Depth = trust (which converts to sales)

The sweet spot: **45-90 seconds, one clear idea, proof visible, hook in the first 2 seconds.**
