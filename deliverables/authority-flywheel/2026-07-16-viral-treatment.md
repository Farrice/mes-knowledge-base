## 3 RANKED HOOK OPTIONS
*(question-form + statement-under stakes stack; kid-plain diction, ≤1 flourish)*

**#1 — The keyboard contradiction (LEAD)**
- **Q (her line):** "I know my field better than anyone I follow. So why can't I make myself post?"
- **Stakes-under:** "She talks for ten minutes. She never touches the keyboard. It goes live in an hour."
- *Why #1: her own freeze, confessed in first person, forces the watch; the one-hour line adds a live clock. Disarms "I'm not a writer" by voicing it from the inside instead of aiming it at the viewer.*

**#2 — The louder-amateur callout**
- **Q:** "How does the worst trainer in your city have the biggest following?"
- **Stakes-under:** "He's wrong about half of it. He's also the one everyone books."
- *Why #2: hottest emotion (envy/injustice), but it centers the competitor, not the mechanism — slightly more seller-serving. Strong for cold reach, weaker for the demo.*

**#3 — The soundproof-room analogy**
- **Q:** "What happens to the smartest thing you know if you never say it out loud?"
- **Stakes-under:** "Nothing. That's the problem. Your best stuff is sitting in a room no one can hear."
- *Why #3: cleanest for a staged-analogy shoot (below), but the gap is softer — reflective, not urgent.*

**VIRAL SKELETON**
- **V** — founder mid-sentence, waveform live, hands working. Action already happening.
- **I** — no context, no intro; drop in at the peak of her explaining the one thing she's best at.
- **R** — no announcer caption; her own voice carries the gap: *"I've known this stuff for years. I just never say it anywhere people can hear."* How does the thing she just said out loud become the post?
- **A** (but/so, zero "and then") — *but* she says "I'm not a writer" → *so* she keeps talking anyway → *but* the screen splits and the post starts building itself from her words → *so* her ramble sharpens into clean lines in her own phrasing → *but* she deletes the one line she tried to make clever (strike-through beat).
