# The Angle Map: Client Decision Room

The Angle Map should lead with one idea: choose the campaign argument before
funding more creative around it.

That is the clearest expression of the service. It positions the work as a
decision product instead of another source of options. The evidence boundary
and the 48-hour delivery window make the offer more credible, but neither one
should replace the main argument.

## What we reviewed

The offer has a fixed scope and a fixed price. A client brings one live
occasion, one buyer, one message decision, one final decision-maker, and one
deadline. The work includes a live message read, three campaign angles, the
approved proof behind each direction, claim-safety boundaries, and a direct
recommendation about which angle to build first.

Delivery happens within 48 hours after the source pack is ready and payment has
cleared. No qualified market exposure has happened yet, so this review does not
treat the offer's readiness as proof that buyers want it.

## What the evidence supports

The service is operationally specific. It settles which campaign argument the
team should build first and shows what evidence may support that choice. It
does not promise campaign performance.

The proposed buyer problem is testable, but it is not yet qualified. There are
no direct customer interviews, purchases, or exposure results connecting that
problem to the proposed segment. That missing evidence stays visible because
it determines what the first market test is allowed to claim.

## The three angles

The first angle is "Decide before you fund more creative." Its underlying
belief is that a weak campaign can begin with an unresolved argument, not a
shortage of creative options. This is the strongest route for introducing the
offer and testing whether the problem feels real to the intended buyer.

The second angle is "Make the proof choose the message." It says that usable
evidence and claim boundaries should shape the campaign direction before the
copy expands. This route is useful for proof-sensitive buyers, but it cannot
imply legal or regulatory approval.

The third angle is "Settle the campaign argument in 48 hours." It gives the
offer urgency and a clear delivery window. Speed is helpful when a launch date
is close, but it should remain a feature rather than the main positioning.

## Recommendation

Lead with the first angle. It names the strategic mistake the service is
designed to avoid and makes the decision product easy to understand.

Use the second angle to explain why the recommendation is disciplined. Use the
third to show that the decision will not stay open indefinitely. Do not blend
all three into one headline. One idea should organize the test; the other two
should explain why the work is credible and timely.

## The first message-market-fit test

The message should say that the next campaign creates one decision before more
creative gets built: which buyer belief and approved proof should organize the
work first. The useful object is a one-page Decision Note showing the tradeoff,
the proof question, and what should not be forced into the same campaign.

Start with five qualified recipients. Keep the segment, problem, useful object,
and message fixed. No message should be sent until the batch and recipients are
approved separately.

Record meaningful responses, direct problem confirmations, Decision Note
requests, held conversations, unchanged $750 acceptances, and cleared payments
as different events. Continue when at least two of the first five recipients
respond meaningfully and at least one confirms the problem. If a gate misses,
repair only that gate. Retire the segment and problem hypothesis if ten
qualified exposures plus an approved follow-up produce no confirmations.

## Evidence boundary

This review confirms a clear service scope and a disciplined message
hypothesis. It does not prove that the proposed segment experiences the
problem, that the message will convert, or that buyers will pay. The three
angles remain candidates until real customer language and market behavior
exist.

The work maps editorial direction to approved evidence. Legal and compliance
approval remain with the client's qualified reviewer.
