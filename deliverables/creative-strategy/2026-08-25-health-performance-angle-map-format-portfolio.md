**Status:** Production-ready strategy; all publishing, outreach, paid spend, and confidential-input collection remain unapproved.

**Offer in scope:** The Angle Map. It is a $750 prepaid, 60-minute live read followed by three named angles, each with its core belief, hook directions, approved-proof direction, and an editorial claim-safety line, delivered inside 48 hours.

**Commercial evidence state:** `UNCONFIRMED`. The offer has identified and contact-verified prospects, but no sent offers, held calls, sales, or collected cash. This portfolio is a set of testable creative hypotheses, not evidence of demand or performance.

## Strategic Decision

The portfolio will not make four stylistic variations of the same ad. Each format owns a different buyer job while carrying one shared belief:

> **More creative volume cannot rescue an unmade belief decision.**

## Recommended Portfolio

### F01: Founder’s Ad, “Where Marketing Actually Lands”

**Role:** Scale anchor  
**SOURCE PRIOR:** S, 2026-08-13 source at 06:51  
**Target persona:** The translation-burdened founder or angle-starved growth lead  
**Mechanism:** A credible human origin story connects the buyer’s recurring problem to the offer’s method.

**Core message:** The product fact and the buyer belief are not the same sentence.

**Concept shape, not final script:**

1. Open with the lived observation: three years behind a supplement counter showed Farrice where brand language meets a real buyer question.
2. Surface the recurring gap: the brand may have product truth, research, and a creative team, yet still lack a deliberately chosen belief.
3. Name the consequence without inventing performance: more wrappers are produced around the same unresolved argument.
4. Reveal the decision surface: The Angle Map selects three belief-led directions and shows what proof each direction can responsibly carry.
5. Close with a readiness CTA only after activation gates clear; until then, invite a public-page or public-campaign diagnosis.

**Hook directions:**

- “Three years behind a supplement counter taught me where marketing actually lands: in a question the brand rarely hears.”
- “Your product fact and your buyer’s belief are not the same sentence.”
- “Five new assets can still be one old argument.”

**Required inputs:** Farrice on camera; verified founder-background facts; one approved public example or a clearly labeled illustrative example.

**Production boundary:** Do not imply Farrice sat inside a brand’s creative department, produced a client outcome, or can certify legal compliance. Load the Farrice Voice Card before scripting.

**HYPOTHESIS:** The founder-led explanation will generate more right-buyer recognition language than a faceless explanation of the same problem.

### V01: Problem-Solution VSL, “The Four Decisions Before Another Creative Round”

**Role:** Trust and education  
**SOURCE PRIOR:** S, 2026-08-13 source at 23:46  
**Target persona:** The growth lead who feels creative production is moving faster than message decisions  
**Mechanism:** A structured diagnosis reduces ambiguity and lets the buyer understand the service before a call.

**Core message:** Before another creative round, buyer belief, product truth, proof boundary, and executable direction must agree.

**60–90 second beat sheet:**

1. **Problem:** Creative volume is often treated as strategy.
2. **Hidden mechanism:** New hooks, edits, and creators can still repeat one unresolved argument.
3. **Decision frame:** Separate the buyer belief, product truth, evidence that may support the claim, and the next direction a team can actually make.
4. **Deliverable:** A 60-minute read becomes three named angles with core beliefs, hook directions, approved-proof direction, and an editorial claim-safety line within 48 hours.
5. **Boundary:** This is message and creative-strategy work; the client’s legal or compliance owner keeps the pen.
6. **CTA:** `DM ANGLE` only after payment and confidential-input procedures are active. Before that, invite a review of public material.

**Required inputs:** An approved sample Angle Map page; exact offer terms; no outcome claim.

**HYPOTHESIS:** The VSL will produce the clearest correct restatement of the offer: a decision-before-production service, not copywriting volume or compliance certification.

### I01: Educational Infographic, “Five Assets. One Argument.”

**Role:** Supporting cast  
**SOURCE PRIOR:** B, 2026-08-13 source at 20:40  
**Target persona:** The growth lead who needs to explain the bottleneck internally  
**Mechanism:** A visual distinction makes an invisible strategy problem easy to transmit.

**Visual concept:** A two-column comparison labeled **Illustrative model; not performance data**.

| New wrapper | New belief-led direction |
|---|---|
| New creator, same promise | Different buyer belief |
| New hook, same mechanism | Different mechanism interpretation |
| New edit, same proof | Different approved-proof direction |
| New format, same objection | Different objection or tradeoff |
| More output | More strategic options |

**Caption direction:** “Format diversity is not message diversity. Before adding assets, count the distinct beliefs your campaign is asking the buyer to hold.”

**Required inputs:** Brand-safe visual system; no fabricated charts, benchmarks, or lift claims.

**HYPOTHESIS:** The infographic will earn more saves, forwards, and internal-use language than direct selling, while supporting—not replacing—the offer conversation.

### A01: Amateur Investigation, “What Is This Campaign Actually Asking Me to Believe?”

**Role:** Bounded challenger  
**SOURCE PRIOR:** A, 2026-08-13 source at 01:30  
**Target persona:** The founder or growth lead who trusts demonstrated judgment more than credentials  
**Mechanism:** A visible discovery journey earns attention and proof-of-work without pretending to know private performance data.

**Concept shape:**

1. Select one current health/performance brand using only public landing-page, organic, and ad-library material.
2. Credit what the brand communicates clearly.
3. Extract the apparent buyer belief, product truth, proof direction, and unresolved tradeoff.
4. Label every diagnosis as observation or inference.
5. Offer one alternative angle direction without calling the original “wrong,” “noncompliant,” or a performance failure.
6. Invite correction from the brand and discussion from qualified operators.

**Required inputs:** A time-stamped public-source packet, screenshots or links, and a claim inventory for every repeated product statement.

**HYPOTHESIS:** A credit-first investigation will produce more qualified requests for a similar diagnosis than a generic expertise post.

**Kill boundary:** If the concept requires private data, a disease-adjacent claim, a public accusation, or an inference presented as fact, do not produce it.

## Learning Plan: Claim-Safe Test Plan

“Claim-safe” here means evidence-bounded editorial practice, not legal certification. FTC guidance says health-related advertising claims need competent and reliable scientific evidence, and FDA distinguishes structure/function claims from disease claims. The brand’s legal or compliance owner retains final approval.

### Gate 1: Claim inventory before drafting

Tag every express and implied claim:

| Claim class | Initial state | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Exact Angle Map scope, timing, and price | `APPROVED: LOCAL CANON` | Recheck against current offer canon before release |
| Farrice’s verified lived background | `APPROVED: LOCAL CANON` | Use the most conservative verified version; do not embellish |
| Client results, conversion lift, reduced spend, or scale | `OUT OF SCOPE` | No evidence exists |
| Named product efficacy or health outcome | `HOLD` | Requires exact claim, finished-product evidence, context, and owner approval |
| Disease language or medical implication | `OUT OF SCOPE` for this portfolio | Do not use as creative material |
| “Legally compliant,” “approved,” or equivalent assurance | `OUT OF SCOPE` | Use “editorial claim-safety line” and “I read for signal, not law” |

Ingredient research is not automatically finished-product substantiation. Testimonials do not establish efficacy. Disclaimers cannot rescue a misleading main impression.

### Gate 2: Four-asset portfolio pilot

Produce one lean version of each format around the shared belief. This is not a causal “winner” test because the formats perform different jobs.

| Asset | Learning question | Qualified signal | Vanity signal to ignore |
|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | Does the founder mechanism earn recognition? | A target-role reply names the same translation or belief problem | Views or generic encouragement |
| V01 | Is the offer understood correctly? | A target can restate the decision, deliverable, and boundary | Watch time without a buyer response |
| I01 | Does the distinction travel inside a team? | A target saves, forwards, or says they used it to explain the problem | Broad impressions |
| A01 | Does visible judgment create a qualified conversation? | A founder/growth lead asks for or corrects a similar diagnosis | Debate from non-buyers |

Use the assets within the first 10 qualified offer exposures. Record `sent`, `problem confirmed`, `held`, `price quoted`, `sold`, and `collected` separately. Content response is supporting evidence; payment behavior remains the commercial test.

### Gate 3: Isolate message from vessel

After the portfolio pilot identifies one promising message and one promising vessel, run a small 2×2:

| | Message A: unmade belief | Message B: proof boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Winning format | A1 | B1 |
| Alternate format | A2 | B2 |

- Same message across two formats tests the vessel.
- Two messages inside one format tests the argument.
- Do not declare a format winner when the message changed with it.
- Do not invent CTR, CPA, or conversion benchmarks for an organic, pre-revenue offer.

### Gate 4: Promotion, demotion, and kill rules

**Promote** a format when it repeatedly earns the qualified signal assigned to its job and the claim review stays clean.

**Demote** a format when the signal is mostly wrong-audience attention, the production burden exceeds the learning value, or another vessel expresses the same message more clearly.

**Kill or revise the segment/message hypothesis** when 10 qualified exposures produce no problem confirmation. Do not blame the format automatically.

**Stop publication** when:

- a health/product claim remains `HOLD`;
- the main impression exceeds the evidence;
- a public teardown presents inference as fact;
- a CTA implies a payment or confidentiality process that is not ready;
- Farrice has not approved publishing or outreach.

| Source format | Native destination | Mechanism preserved | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder’s Ad | LinkedIn native video, keynote opener, sales-page video | Human reason and shared-problem recognition | Runtime, CTA, and proof depth adapt to the surface |
| Problem-Solution VSL | Offer-page explainer, sales follow-up, workshop segment | Structured problem, mechanism, decision, and boundary | The sales environment controls length and next step |
| Educational Infographic | LinkedIn document, sales slide, internal strategy artifact | Compression of an invisible distinction | Visual density and terminology adapt to reader context |
| Amateur Investigation | Public teardown, newsletter case note, strategy workshop | Discovery journey and earned conclusion | Source rights, naming, and reputational review tighten by domain |

### Reuse rule

The reusable system is not “use these four ads everywhere.” It is:

1. Define the buyer job.
2. Audit evidence and access.
3. Select the format whose mechanism fits that job.
4. Bind message × persona × vessel.
5. Record a hypothesis, qualified signal, and kill rule.
6. Separate creative learning from commercial proof.

That sequence can route content strategy, sales enablement, creator work, product education, and creative strategy without pretending every domain shares the same format hierarchy.
