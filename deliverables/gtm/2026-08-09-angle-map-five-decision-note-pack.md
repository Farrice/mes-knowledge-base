## 1. GateDrop — Tim Near

**Verified trigger:** retail distribution expansion beyond the original action-sports context.  
**Public language:** “built for people in motion”; current founder coverage also describes broad appeal to anyone seeking a healthier daytime lift.  
**Decision inference:** the campaign must decide whether the literal gate-drop/action-sports identity leads the broader retail story or operates as the proof of a more universal portable-energy promise.

### One-Gap Decision Note

**Direction A — Own the gate-drop moment**  
Lead with controlled energy for the instant performance starts. This preserves the brand’s memorable origin and gives creators a demonstrable use moment.

**Direction B — Portable energy you control**  
Lead with a low-dose gummy that fits work, travel, outdoors, training, and convenience retail. This widens the audience but risks becoming generic “better energy.”

**Decision:** Which idea should a convenience-store buyer remember first: the performance-start ritual or the portable-control benefit?

**Proof question:** Which product facts and consumer evidence support the “control” promise beyond dosage format and caffeine amount?

**Do not combine:** Do not make every lifestyle scene equally primary. Use the chosen lead idea; let the other act as proof or expansion.

**Message one**

> Tim, GateDrop’s retail expansion is widening the buyer beyond action sports while the brand still carries the literal gate-drop moment. The decision I see is whether that origin leads the campaign or proves a broader “portable energy you control” promise. I mapped the tradeoff and the proof question into a one-page Decision Note. Want me to send it?
