## ICP

### Primary beachhead

An already-selling, founder-led or lean **supplement, sports nutrition, recovery, beauty, fitness, or performance-product brand** that:

- is actively running Meta ads now;
- can share recent creative-level data, or at minimum its five highest-spend and five weakest ads;
- has one SKU, a live product page, usable brand assets, and approved claims language;
- has a founder, growth lead, media buyer, or agency ready to launch the work;
- is spending enough on paid media or production that a $1,000 test pack is a small learning investment;
- wants strategically distinct tests, not cosmetic variations.

### Live buying triggers

- Creative fatigue or flattening performance.
- A new SKU or offer needs a test slate.
- The team is shipping high volume but cannot say what each asset is teaching.
- The media buyer asks for more creative and the founder keeps rejecting generic concepts.
- Competitor imitation has made the category visually and verbally interchangeable.
- The brand has product evidence but struggles to turn it into compliant, persuasive creative.

### Exclusions

- Pre-launch brand with no customer or performance signal.
- No one can launch the finished ads.
- Wants media buying, creator sourcing, filming, unlimited revisions, or a full content calendar.
- Demands a ROAS, CPA, revenue, platform-approval, or “winning ad” guarantee.
- Wants Farrice to invent, strengthen, or legally approve unsupported health claims.

## Avatar and buying group

### Economic buyer: the founder or head of growth

- **Objective reality:** Paid social is already consuming money, but the creative pipeline produces volume faster than insight.
- **Private frustration:** “We keep making ads, but I cannot tell whether we are testing a new belief or only changing the wrapper.”
- **Status risk:** Admitting the team lacks a learning system can feel like admitting the brand's growth engine is guesswork.
- **Current workaround:** Competitor swipes, more hooks, more freelancers, trend chasing, and founder rewrites.
- **Purchase trigger:** A live spend decision makes a bounded $1,000 pack cheaper than another unfocused production cycle.
- **Desired after-state:** Five distinct bets, assets ready to launch, and a map that tells the team what the results mean.

### Champion: media buyer, performance marketer, or creative strategist

- Needs a steady supply of testable hypotheses, not unstructured files.
- Wants filenames, variables, launch order, and a readout that protects interpretability.
- Fears being blamed for performance when the creative inputs are strategically repetitive.
- Becomes the internal advocate when the pack reduces clarification and makes the next test obvious.

### Hidden buyer: regulatory, legal, or scientific owner

- Does not care that AI made the process faster.
- Cares that supplied evidence, approved wording, implied claims, disclaimers, and review ownership remain traceable.
- Can veto the work if creative implication outruns the approved claim.

### Execution partners

- Designer/editor, UGC lead, brand marketer, and agency creative director.
- They need a brief they can execute without another strategy meeting.

## Productized offer

### Name

**The 15-Ad Creative Test Pack**

### Promise

> Turn one SKU, current ad-account evidence, and real customer language into five materially different creative bets, 15 launch-ready static ads, and a testing map in seven business days.

### Scope and terms

- One SKU.
- Five creative concepts.
- Three meaningful message, proof, or framing variants per concept.
- Fifteen finished static ads in agreed Meta placements.
- Creative performance read and customer/category signal file.
- Launch and learning map.
- One consolidated revision round.
- One post-launch readout within 30 days.
- **$1,000 prepaid founding pilot.** One active client at a time; maximum three founding pilots.

### Acceptance test

The build passes when:

1. all 15 assets map to one of five distinct hypotheses;
2. the media buyer can identify the variable and launch order without another strategy meeting;
3. every health claim comes from client-supplied approved language or is flagged for qualified review;
4. files, copy, and placements meet the agreed specifications;
5. the client can state what each test is intended to teach.

This proves delivery quality. It does not prove market demand or performance. The first commercial proof is collected payment.

## LinkedIn distribution, demonstration, and proof

LinkedIn's job is not to manufacture authority through volume. It is to let the buyer, champion, and hidden approver inspect how Farrice thinks before a call.

### Four content pillars

1. **Account learning teardowns:** Show why 15 visually different ads can still represent one repeated idea.
2. **Customer language to creative bet:** Take public reviews or comments and demonstrate how one phrase changes audience, mechanism, proof, and first frame.
3. **Evidence and claim boundary:** Show the difference between persuasive product truth and an implied claim that needs qualified review.
4. **Test-map demonstrations:** Display five hypotheses, the controlled variable, launch order, and what each outcome would teach.

### Proof ladder

1. **Specimen:** One clearly labeled public-material pack; no invented client data.
2. **Comprehension:** Five qualified buyers can explain the offer back accurately.
3. **Conversation:** A qualified buyer discusses a live SKU and supplies the required inputs.
4. **Commercial proof:** $1,000 clears before kickoff.
5. **Behavior proof:** The buyer launches the assets and participates in the readout.
6. **Renewal proof:** A second pack is purchased without a discount.

### CTA

> Already running Meta for a health or performance product? Bring one SKU, your five highest-spend ads, and five weak ads. I’ll tell you whether the account needs more creative or five genuinely different bets.

That CTA is a qualification mechanism. It excludes pre-launch founders and people seeking free generic brainstorming.

## Validation plan and kill gates

### First 20 qualified exposures

- Build one public-material specimen.
- Put it in front of 20 brands that are visibly running paid social and meet the input test.
- Use marketplace proposals, warm introductions, and controlled LinkedIn conversations; content alone is not the exposure count.
- Track `qualified exposures`, `replies`, `calls held`, `proposals`, `sold`, `collected`, `launched`, and `renewed` separately.

### Decision rules

- **Validate:** one qualified buyer prepays $1,000.
- **Strengthen:** three paid pilots and at least two launch the assets.
- **Renewal evidence:** one full-price repeat purchase.
- **Channel problem:** few qualified conversations from a verified list; change access before changing the offer.
- **Offer problem:** 20 qualified conversations with clear comprehension and zero payments; revise or park the pack.
- **Proof problem:** buyers want the work but refuse without case studies; produce one bounded paid or permissioned pilot rather than more thought leadership.
- **Delivery problem:** buyers treat the work as cheap design production or seven-day delivery is unsustainable; reduce asset volume or raise price after actual delivery evidence.
