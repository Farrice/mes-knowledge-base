## Compliance Rails

Current public checks used for this pass:

- DWA terms require clear material-connection disclosure near statements about the product.
- DWA terms prohibit false, misleading, or inaccurate marketing.
- DWA terms say income claims are not permitted for marketing unless handled with the required written disclaimer.
- DWA terms say sales are final and non-refundable.
- FTC guidance says disclosures should be easy to see, close to the endorsement, and not hidden in a profile, hashtag group, or "more" expansion.

Sources: https://digitalwealthacademy.com/terms-of-purchase, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers, and https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255

## Free Asset: DWA Buyer Question Map

Use this as the free asset body.

```markdown
# The DWA Buyer Question Map

17 questions to answer before you ask anyone for a DWA link.

Disclosure: I am a DWA affiliate. If you buy through my link, I may earn a commission. I do not make income claims, payback promises, or recommendations for every buyer.

## Why This Exists

I have gone back and forth on DWA.

The course has useful material in it.

The way it usually gets sold online is the part I do not want to copy.

Too many posts jump straight to:

"buy this, resell this, trust me."

That is exactly where people should slow down.

This map is the filter I would want before touching the link.

## 1. What Are You Actually Buying?

DWA is best understood as digital marketing education with an affiliate path attached.

Public DWA pages describe training around digital products, affiliate marketing, content, funnels, and online selling. Current public pages also mention community, updates, live calls, content resources, PLR-style assets, and an affiliate program.

Sources: https://digitalwealthacademy.com/ and https://rachell.digitalwealthacademy.com/

Do not treat the purchase as a business by itself.

The business is still:

- choosing a buyer;
- creating trust;
- making useful content;
- answering objections;
- routing people through a clear offer path.

## 2. Are You Buying Education Or A Shortcut?

If you are buying because you want structure, study it.

If you are buying because you need fast cash, pause.

I would rather tell you to wait than help you buy something from panic.

## 3. What Can You Actually Reuse?

Before buying, ask the seller:

- What assets can I use publicly?
- What assets cannot be reposted?
- Can I edit content-vault assets?
- Can I show course screens?
- Can I use sales-page screenshots?
- What brand/IP rules apply?

Do not assume course videos can be reposted.

## 4. What Costs Extra?

The course price is not necessarily the whole operating plan.

Ask what you may need for:

- domain;
- email address;
- payment processor;
- funnel page;
- email follow-up tool;
- design/content tools;
- time to post, reply, and follow up.

If the full setup would stress you out financially, that matters.

## 5. What If You Have No Audience?

If you have no audience, your first job is not reselling.

Your first job is proving you can:

- write useful posts for one specific buyer;
- get real questions, not empty likes;
- answer DMs without making income claims;
- explain the offer without sounding desperate;
- run a 14-day content test without quitting.

If you cannot do that yet, study first.

## 6. Is This Just A Digital MLM?

The clean answer:

- If you sell income dreams, it will feel MLM-ish.
- If you hide the affiliate connection, it will feel MLM-ish.
- If your only pitch is "buy this so you can sell this," it will feel MLM-ish.
- If you explain the education, risks, fit, costs, and alternatives, you create a cleaner path.

I do not recommend selling DWA as "join me and make money."

## 7. Should You Sell DWA Or Build Your Own Product?

Use this fork:

- If you need education: study first.
- If you already have an audience: an affiliate test may make sense.
- If you have a specific skill: build your own small product first.
- If you have no buyer, no content habit, and no budget cushion: skip for now.

## 8. What Should You Ask Before You Buy?

Ask these before asking for a link:

1. What changed in the current version?
2. What can I reuse publicly?
3. What cannot be reposted?
4. What costs extra after checkout?
5. Is there support if I get stuck?
6. What happens if I buy through the wrong affiliate?
7. Is there a refund?
8. What payment options exist in my country?
9. How would you use it if you were starting with no audience?
10. What would make you tell me not to buy?
11. What parts are education versus resale?
12. What parts are affiliate marketing?
13. What parts are PLR or content-vault assets?
14. What do I need to build myself?
15. What can I say publicly without misleading people?
16. What do you help with after checkout?
17. If I do not resell DWA, what do I still walk away knowing?

## 9. My Fit Buckets

### Skip

You need fast money, cannot afford the purchase, or want an income promise.

### Study

You want structured digital marketing education but should not sell yet.

### Build

You already have a skill, service, audience, or product idea that should become your own smaller offer first.

### Affiliate Test

You understand the risks, have a clear buyer or angle, and can run a no-income-claim content test.

## 10. My Recommendation

Do not ask me for the DWA link first.

Tell me which bucket you think you are in.

If you are not a fit, I will tell you.
```

## Comment-To-DM Flow

1. Public post invites `QUESTIONS`.
2. Public reply confirms asset delivery and tells them to start with fit buckets.
3. DM sends the Buyer Question Map with disclosure before any link.
4. DM asks for a numbered question category.
5. You answer the category without income claims.
6. Only after the fit check do you ask whether they want the DWA link.
7. If they say yes, send the stronger disclosure and link.
8. If they are not a fit, tell them and offer the lower-cost first step.

## Do Not Post

Do not post these angles:

- "DWA is back."
- "DWA is dead."
- "I found the clean way to make money with DWA."
- "The purchase covers itself."
- "You can do this faceless and get sales fast."
- "Comment if you want the income path."
- "People are sleeping on this."
- "This is not MLM because..."

They either pull in low-quality buyers, invite argument, imply results, or make the campaign sound like the behavior you are trying to avoid.

## Taste Test

This passes the current taste test if it feels like:

- "I am using an asset I own without becoming a hype account."
- "I can say this without cringing."
- "I would still respect someone who decides not to buy."
- "The post attracts people with real questions, not just curiosity."
- "The DM makes me look more trustworthy, not more desperate."

This fails if it feels like:

- you are defending DWA more than helping the buyer think;
- the content sounds embarrassed to exist;
- the CTA feels like a trap;
- you are relying on the course name instead of the buyer-question asset;
- you would feel weird if a coworker saw the post.
