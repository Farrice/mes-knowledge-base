## 1. Brand Essence

**The table where parents and teachers remember what matters.**

HVC exists at the intersection of two things the world is starving for: shared meals and shared values. Not curriculum. Not theory. A weekly practice of sitting down together — parents and teachers, side by side — and having the conversations that shape who our kids become.

## 2. Brand Story

Javier Payano is a music teacher at Lane Tech in Chicago. Not "was." Is. Every morning he walks into a classroom where students with disabilities play alongside honors students, each performing at their own level, making something beautiful together. That classroom is a living argument for what happens when you build a culture around human values instead of test scores.

But the argument didn't start in Chicago. It started in Thailand, at the Institute of Sathya Sai Education, where Javier studied a framework built on a simple premise: there is inherent goodness in every human being, and education's job is to draw it out. Five values — Truth, Love, Peace, Right Conduct, Non-Violence — not as rules imposed from above, but as capacities already inside every person, waiting to be named.

He brought that framework home. Not to a conference stage or a consulting firm — to his own classroom. Then to other teachers. Then to parents. And then something unexpected happened: he started inviting everyone to dinner.

Sunday dinner. Parents and teachers at the same table. No agenda. No PowerPoint. Just food, conversation, and a shared question: *How do we raise kids who are kind?*

In 2017, that dinner became Human Values Collective — a 501(c)(3) organization with a radical bet: that the most powerful force in education isn't a better app, a smarter test, or a new policy. It's a table. And the people willing to sit at it together.

Today, in a country where the Surgeon General has declared loneliness an epidemic, where 86% of parents want character education but can't find it, where teachers are burning out because they're asked to optimize outcomes instead of nurture humans — HVC is building something that doesn't exist anywhere else.

A community where the institutional meets the intimate. Where evidence-based values education is delivered not through worksheets but through Wednesday lesson plans and Sunday dinners. Where a music teacher who was recognized by 3Arts as an artist doing social change is still, every single day, in the room with the kids.

The table is set. The question is who's coming to dinner.

## 3. Mission / Vision / Values

### Mission

Human Values Collective equips teachers with ready-to-use values lessons and gathers parents and teachers around shared meals — so the adults raising the next generation can do it together, on purpose, rooted in what actually matters.

### Vision

A world where every neighborhood has a table where parents and teachers sit together weekly — and every classroom has a teacher who knows how to draw out the goodness already inside their students.

### Values

These are the five human values at the center of everything HVC does. They aren't aspirations. They're practices.

**Truth** — Say the real thing. In a world of performance, start with honesty. With yourself first, then with the people you're raising. Truth isn't a policy. It's the habit of not pretending.

**Right Conduct** — Do what you'd want your kids to see you do. Not perfection. Integrity. The kind of action that doesn't need an audience.

**Peace** — Before you can teach calm, you have to practice it. Peace isn't the absence of problems. It's the steadiness you bring to them. HVC protects this in teachers because you can't give what you don't have.

**Love** — Not the soft, decorative kind. The kind that shows up on Sunday when you're tired. The kind that sits with a struggling student for ten extra minutes. Love as a verb, not a poster.

**Non-Violence** — In thought, word, and deed. How you talk about your co-parent when they're not in the room. How you discipline without diminishing. The hardest value and the one that changes everything.

## 5. Voice & Tone

### Voice Attributes

**Warm. Grounded. Inviting. Direct. Practiced.**

HVC's voice sounds like a teacher who's been doing this for years and still loves it — someone who doesn't lecture because they know the real teaching happens in conversation. There's conviction without rigidity. There's depth without jargon.

### What HVC Sounds Like

- A trusted friend explaining why Sunday dinner changed her family
- A teacher in the hallway, honest about what's hard, clear about what works
- Someone who's read the research but leads with the story
- An invitation, never a sermon

### What HVC Does NOT Sound Like

- A school district memo ("implement values-aligned pedagogical frameworks")
- A wellness brand ("manifest your highest self")
- A guilt trip ("you're failing your kids if you don't...")
- A nonprofit annual report ("we served 2,400 stakeholders across 14 touchpoints")
- A spiritual community requiring belief to belong

### Example Sentences in the HVC Voice

> "You don't need a degree in moral philosophy to raise kind kids. You need a table and an honest question."

> "Every teacher in our program gets a values lesson they can use tomorrow morning. Not 'someday when I have time.' Tomorrow."

> "We bring parents and teachers to the same dinner because they're raising the same kids. Obvious when you say it out loud. Almost nobody does it."

> "Peace isn't something you teach from a poster. It's something your students see you practice when the fire alarm goes off during a lesson for the third time this month."

> "The Sunday dinner isn't a program. It's a practice. You keep showing up. The table does the rest."

## 6. Key Audiences

### Primary: Values-Hungry Teachers (The Unstoppable Teacher)

**Who they are**: K-12 educators, primarily in urban public schools, who entered teaching to make a difference and are drowning in administrative demands that have nothing to do with developing humans. They've Googled "character education lessons" and found either bureaucratic frameworks or religious materials — neither fits.

**What they need**: Ready-to-use values lessons that don't require a committee to approve. A community of teachers who get it. Permission to prioritize character alongside (not instead of) academics. Protection from burnout.

**How HVC serves them**: Unstoppable Teacher membership delivers done-for-you lessons tied to the five values. Weekly community. The knowledge that a fellow teacher (Javier, still in the classroom) built this for people exactly like them.

### Secondary: Intentional Parents (The Unstoppable Parent)

**Who they are**: Parents of school-age children who feel like they're losing the character-formation battle to screens, algorithms, and a culture that rewards performance over kindness. They want something structured but not sterile, values-based but not preachy. Skew 30-50, across race, income, and political lines — the hunger for this is universal.

**What they need**: A weekly practice they can actually sustain. Language and tools for hard conversations with their kids. Other parents who are trying too. A bridge to what's happening in their child's school.

**How HVC serves them**: Unstoppable Parent membership. Sunday Dinners as a gathering point. Conversation guides that turn "How was school?" into something real.

### Tertiary: Institutional Funders & Partners

**Who they are**: Foundations funding character development (Lilly Endowment, Fetzer Institute, John Templeton Foundation), school districts exploring SEL alternatives, university education departments.

**What they need**: Evidence of impact, scalable models, alignment with their funding priorities.

**How HVC serves them**: Classroom outcome data, the Sunday Dinner host-scaling model, Javier's institutional credibility (CPS Content Specialist + 3Arts recognition + SSEHV training).

### How the Pieces Relate

**Sunday Dinners** are the community engine and the emotional center of the brand. This is where parents and teachers meet. This is the experience people tell friends about. This is the movement. Everything else supports getting more people to the table — or bringing the table's spirit to people who can't be there in person.

**Unstoppable Teacher** is the product that scales the classroom impact. A teacher in Phoenix who will never attend a Chicago Sunday dinner can still get done-for-you values lessons and belong to the community. This is how HVC's values reach thousands of classrooms without Javier being in each one.

**Unstoppable Parent** is the mirror product. It gives parents the same framework, the same weekly practice, and creates the bridge between what's happening at home and what's happening at school.

**The Podcast** (currently dormant, 7 episodes) is the content engine waiting to be activated. The natural format: record the Sunday dinner conversations. Real parents and teachers, real food, real questions. The podcast becomes a weekly invitation to the table for people who aren't in Chicago yet.

**Music** is Javier's artistic identity and HVC's secret weapon. Music is how values land in his classroom — not as lectures but as songs, rhythms, collaborative creation. The 3Arts award validates that the art world recognizes this integration. Music isn't a side project. It's the medium.

### The Relationship

The Sunday Dinner is the center. The memberships are the products. The podcast is the megaphone. The music is the soul. Javier's classroom is the proof. Each piece feeds the others:

- Sunday Dinners generate stories that become podcast episodes and social content
- Podcast episodes introduce new people to the memberships
- Membership communities become the pipeline for new Sunday Dinner hosts in new cities
- Javier's classroom is where every lesson is battle-tested before it reaches a member
- Music gives everything an emotional texture that no spreadsheet or framework can replicate

## 8. Competitive Differentiation

### The Landscape

| Organization | What They Do | What's Missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Character.org** | 11 Principles framework, school certification | Zero community. Zero parent involvement. Institutional, top-down, cold. A teacher can get "certified" without ever sitting across from a parent. |
| **CASEL** | SEL frameworks, policy advocacy, school district partnerships | Enormous influence, zero warmth. CASEL is infrastructure — important but invisible to the humans it's meant to serve. No meals. No movement. |
| **SSEHV** (Sathya Sai) | Global values education, 120+ countries, 28 institutes | Powerful framework (it's HVC's source), but identity-bounded. You need to be part of the Sai community to access it. Not designed for public school teachers or secular parents. |
| **Family Dinner Project** (Harvard) | Research + resources for family mealtime | Family-only. No teacher bridge. No community gathering. A website with recipes and conversation starters — helpful, not transformative. |
| **OneTable** | Shabbat dinner gatherings, 700+ cities | Brilliant scaling model (HVC should study it), but Jewish-identity-specific. Not values-education. Not bridging parents and teachers. |
| **The Dinner Party** | Shared meals for people experiencing grief | Powerful peer model, but narrow use case. Not education-focused. |

### What Makes HVC the Only One

No organization in the landscape does all five of these things:

1. **Universal values framework** — not tied to a religion, political identity, or cultural tradition
2. **Shared meals** as the core community practice — not webinars, not conferences, not certifications
3. **Parents AND teachers together** — not parents-only programs or teacher-only PD
4. **Content-forward storytelling** — built to become a media brand, not just a service provider
5. **A founder still in the classroom** — every lesson is tested with real students before it reaches a member

The closest comparison isn't another education nonprofit. It's OneTable — but for human values instead of Jewish identity, and bridging the two groups (parents + teachers) that every other organization keeps separate.

## 9. The Line

> **"A group of parents and teachers in Chicago started having Sunday dinner together. It's turning into a movement."**

This works because:
- It's concrete (Sunday dinner, Chicago, parents and teachers)
- It implies a story people want to hear (why? what happens at those dinners?)
- It positions HVC as something emerging, not something selling
- "Turning into a movement" creates forward momentum and FOMO
- It's repeatable — someone could say this exact sentence at a party

### Alternate Lines (for different contexts)

**For teachers**: "What if you had a values lesson ready for Monday morning — built by a teacher who's still in the classroom?"

**For parents**: "What if once a week, you sat down with other parents and teachers and just talked about raising good kids?"

**For funders**: "We're building the infrastructure for a national Sunday dinner movement — parents and teachers at the same table, in every city."

**For press**: "In a country where the Surgeon General declared loneliness an epidemic, one music teacher in Chicago decided the cure was Sunday dinner."
