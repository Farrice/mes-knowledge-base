## 5. The Sunday Dinners — Why This Format Is Powerful

### The Science

**Robin Dunbar (Oxford, 2017)**: People who eat socially more often feel happier, more satisfied with life, more trusting of others, and have more friends they can depend on. The causal direction runs **from eating together to bondedness** — not the reverse. Mechanism: shared meals trigger the endorphin system (same pathways as oxytocin and dopamine).

**Harvard Family Dinner Project (15 years of data)**: Regular family meals linked to higher GPAs, greater resilience, lower depression, lower substance abuse, greater self-esteem.

### Why "Sunday Dinner" Specifically

- **Intentionality**: Not a rushed weeknight meal — a deliberate gathering
- **Cultural resonance**: Deep roots across Black, Italian, Southern, and many American traditions
- **Ritual without religion**: "Sunday" implies rhythm and reverence without doctrine
- **Weekly habit**: Creates anticipation, consistency, and deepening relationships over time
- **Conversational pace**: The leisurely format enables the laughter and reminiscing that Dunbar's research shows creates bonding

### The Positioning Line

> "In a world where screens are raising our kids, a group of parents and teachers started having Sunday dinner together."
