## 1. Competitive Landscape: Major Players & Comparable Programs

### Tier 1: National Character Education Organizations

**Character.org** (formerly CEP, founded 1993)
- The dominant institutional player in character education
- 11 Principles Framework for Schools adopted nationwide
- Certified 73 National Schools of Character in 2024; 98 state-level in 2025
- Claims impact on 3+ million students, staff, parents, and community members
- Recently merged with CharacterPlus
- [character.org](https://character.org/)

**CHARACTER COUNTS! (Josephson Institute)**
- Most widely implemented character education approach in the US
- Six Pillars: Trustworthiness, Respect, Responsibility, Fairness, Caring, Citizenship
- 30+ year track record; thousands of affiliated schools
- Orono Schools is one of only 3 US recipients of their Exemplary District Award
- [charactercounts.org](https://charactercounts.org/)

**CASEL (Collaborative for Academic, Social and Emotional Learning)**
- Dominates the SEL policy landscape
- Focuses on five core competencies: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making
- SEL market valued at ~$4 billion (2024), projected to reach $28 billion by 2033 at 24% CAGR
- More institutional/school-focused than community-focused
- [casel.org](https://casel.org/)

**Positive Action**
- Evidence-based whole-school reform strategy
- WWC "Positive Effects" rating from U.S. Department of Education
- Unique: Has both **family kit** and **community kit** extending beyond school walls
- Research: 17.2% decrease in family conflict, 9.8% increase in family cohesion, 14.2% increase in child/parent bonding
- [positiveaction.net](https://www.positiveaction.net/)

**The Positivity Project (P2)**
- Character education curriculum for PreK-12
- Focus on character strengths
- [posproject.org](https://posproject.org/)

### Tier 2: Values-Based / Eastern Philosophy Programs

**Sathya Sai Education in Human Values (SSEHV)**
- **This is the tradition HVC appears rooted in.** Five human values: Truth, Right Conduct, Peace, Love, Nonviolence
- 200+ Sai Centers across 10 US regions
- 3,000+ SSE students, 750+ volunteer teachers/coordinators
- Weekly classes September-June; centers organize under service, devotion, and education
- Institute of Sathya Sai Education-USA (ISSE-USA) provides training; completed 5 residential programs at UC Santa Barbara
- US and UK were the first countries outside India to start SSEHV (1981-83)
- Community gathering format includes shared meals (e.g., Dallas center hosted Ramadan Iftar dinner for ~100 people)
- [sathyasai.us/education](https://sathyasai.us/education) | [isseusa.org](https://isseusa.org/)

**The Virtues Project** (founded 1991, Linda Kavelin Popov)
- Active in 120+ countries; honored by the United Nations as a "model global program for families of all cultures"
- 52 universal virtues drawn from studying sacred traditions worldwide
- Resources: The Family Virtues Guide (translated into 12+ languages), Educator's Guide
- Cross-cultural, non-denominational approach
- [virtuesproject.com](https://www.virtuesproject.com/)

**Human Values Collective (HVC)**
- Promotes "Education in Human Values (EHV)" centered on love, peace, truth, non-violence, right action
- Two programs: **Unstoppable Teacher** (educators) and **Unstoppable Parent** (families)
- Membership-based model with free resources
- Explicitly frames parents and teachers working together
- [humanvaluescollective.org](https://humanvaluescollective.org/home)

**Roots of Empathy** (Mary Gordon, Toronto)
- Evidence-based classroom program using infant observation to develop empathy
- Scaled to 12 countries, reached 1+ million children
- Reduced aggression lasting 3+ years post-program
- HundrED recognized it as one of the most inspiring global K-12 innovations (2017)
- [rootsofempathy.org](https://rootsofempathy.org/)

**Mindfulness-Based Programs in Schools**
- Learning to BREATHE: adolescent social-emotional skills via mindfulness
- Smiling Mind: app-based program for ages 7-18
- Middle Way School (Khyentse Foundation): Buddhist-inspired education
- These are growing but remain primarily school-based, not community/family formats

### Tier 3: Community-Based Family Programs

**The Family Dinner Project** (Harvard/MGH, Dr. Anne Fishel, founded 2010)
- **Most directly comparable to the "Sunday dinner" format**
- Co-founded by a Harvard Medical School professor
- Reached 1.7 million people online
- Champions family dinner as a vehicle for connection, values transmission
- Backed by extensive research linking family meals to higher GPAs, resilience, self-esteem, lower depression/substance abuse
- Indiana implementation via Purdue University
- [thefamilydinnerproject.org](https://thefamilydinnerproject.org/)

**Be Strong Families**
- Community-focused family strengthening programs
- [bestrongfamilies.org](https://www.bestrongfamilies.org/)

### Gap Analysis: What's Missing

| Existing Programs | What They Do Well | What They Miss |
|---|---|---|
| Character.org / CHARACTER COUNTS! | Institutional school frameworks | Zero community/meal format; no parent-teacher gathering outside school |
| CASEL / SEL programs | Evidence base, policy influence | Purely institutional; no community warmth |
| SSEHV / Sai Centers | Human values framework, weekly format | Tied to specific spiritual tradition; limited mainstream appeal |
| Virtues Project | Cross-cultural, UN-recognized | Primarily curriculum/book-based; no gathering format |
| Family Dinner Project | Meal-based, research-backed | Family-only; no parent-teacher community bridge |
| OneTable / Dinner Party | Meal-based community scaling | Narrow demographics (Jewish millennials / grief) |
| Positive Action | Evidence-based, family+community kits | School-district driven; not grassroots community |

**THE GAP**: No organization combines:
1. Universal human values (not religion-specific, not SEL jargon)
2. A shared meal gathering format
3. Parents AND teachers together (not one or the other)
4. Community-driven (not institutional/school-district dependent)
5. Modern, scalable, content-forward storytelling

This is exactly where Human Values Collective can own the space.

## 2. The Sunday Dinner Format: Why It Works

### The Science of Shared Meals

**Robin Dunbar's "Breaking Bread" Research (Oxford, 2017)**

The landmark study using UK national survey data found:

- People who eat socially more often **feel happier**, are more **satisfied with life**, are more **trusting of others**, are more **engaged with their local communities**, and have **more friends they can depend on**
- A path analysis shows the **causal direction runs from eating together to bondedness** -- not the other way around
- Evening meals that create closeness involve **more people, more laughter, and more reminiscing**
- The mechanism: social meals trigger the **endorphin system** -- the same neurochemical pathways linked to oxytocin and dopamine (bonding, trust, pleasure)
- Social eating may have **evolved as a mechanism for facilitating social bonding**

Sources: [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40750-017-0061-4) | [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6979515/) | [Oxford News](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2017-03-16-social-eating-connects-communities)

**Additional Research:**
- Eating the same food increases **trust and cooperation** between strangers ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2017/02/02/512998465/why-eating-the-same-food-increases-peoples-trust-and-cooperation))
- Food sharing indicates **intimacy** and increases prosocial evaluation ([Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.02070/full))
- Shared meals are associated with **better cardiovascular health in teens**, more fruit/fiber/vegetables, lower fat/sugar/salt ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/health/article/communal-dining-loneliness-epidemic))

**Family Dinner Project Research (Harvard):**
- Dr. Catherine Snow tracked 65 families over **15 years**: family meal conversations promote **greater vocabularies** in children
- Regular family dinners linked to **lower rates of depression, anxiety, substance abuse, eating disorders, tobacco use, early pregnancy**
- Higher rates of **resilience and self-esteem**
- Yet only about **30% of families** manage to eat together regularly

### Why "Sunday Dinner" Specifically

The Sunday dinner tradition carries deep cultural resonance:
- It signals **intentionality** -- this is not a rushed weeknight meal
- It connects to **multi-generational tradition** across Black, Italian, Southern, and many other American cultures
- The word "Sunday" implies **ritual without being religious**
- It creates a **weekly rhythm** that builds habit and anticipation
- The leisurely pace allows for the **laughter and reminiscing** Dunbar's research shows creates bonding

### Comparable Scaling Models

**OneTable (Shabbat Dinners)**
- Founded 2014; scaled to **700+ cities, 300,000+ unique participants, 180,000+ total dinners**
- Model: Hosts receive $10-15/guest (up to $300/dinner) in nourishment support
- Platform connects hosts and guests (described as "Airbnb for Shabbat dinner")
- Provides coaching for hosts on rituals and logistics
- **Key lesson**: Subsidize the host, provide a platform for discovery, train hosts with a light touch
- Recently restructuring amid funding downturn -- cautionary lesson on donor dependency
- [onetable.org](https://onetable.org/) | [JTA](https://www.jta.org/2026/01/22/united-states/onetable-reimagines-shabbat-dinner-program-amid-layoffs-and-funding-downturn)

**The Dinner Party (Grief Support)**
- Founded 2010 by Lennon Flowers; started with one paella dinner on a backyard deck in LA
- Scaled to **234 tables across 90+ cities worldwide**
- ~10,000 participants, 4,000 regular members by 2018
- Lennon Flowers recognized as **Ashoka Fellow** and **Aspen Ideas Scholar**
- Model: Trained hosts facilitate intimate dinners around shared experience
- Now does corporate consulting and trainings
- **Key lesson**: Start with a profoundly relatable human experience, train hosts as facilitators, let the format replicate organically
- [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dinner_Party_(organization)) | [Ashoka](https://www.ashoka.org/en-us/fellow/lennon-flowers)

**The Supper Club Movement (2024-2025 Trend)**
- Post-pandemic explosion of intimate, community-driven dining experiences
- 78% of consumers globally say they **value real-world experiences** (2024)
- Social currency shifting from "booking the hottest table" to "being part of the right community"
- Framed as a cultural phenomenon about **belonging** in an increasingly digital world
- [homeandtexture.com](https://homeandtexture.com/dinner-club-trend/) | [maxwellsocial.com](https://www.maxwellsocial.com/soiree/supper-club-trend)

## 3. Audience Hunger: Evidence of Demand

### The Loneliness Epidemic (Macro Tailwind)

**Surgeon General Vivek Murthy declared loneliness an epidemic (2023-2024)**
- Approximately **half of US adults** experience loneliness
- Loneliness more widespread than smoking, obesity, and diabetes
- Social isolation among older adults costs an estimated **$6.7 billion** in excess Medicare spending annually
- Released the first-ever **National Strategy to Advance Social Connection**
- Sources: [HHS Advisory](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf) | [Harvard GSE](https://www.gse.harvard.edu/ideas/usable-knowledge/24/10/what-causing-our-epidemic-loneliness-and-how-can-we-fix-it)

### Parents Are Worried -- and Searching

**Pew Research Center (2024):**
- **86% of parents** say managing screen time is a daily priority
- **77% of parents** say ensuring good manners is a top priority
- **71% of parents** are at least somewhat concerned about their child spending too much time on screens
- Parents cite **"changing morals and values"** as a key reason parenting is harder now
- Top cited difficulties: impact of digital technology (26%), social media (21%), early exposure to content (14%)
- [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/03/11/how-teens-and-parents-approach-screen-time/)

**EdChoice Survey (2024):**
- **64% of school parents** think K-12 education is "on the wrong track" (up 12 points from 2022)
- **30% of homeschool parents** ranked "morals or values instruction" as a top factor in choosing to homeschool
- [EdChoice](https://www.edchoice.org/engage/beyond-enrollment-what-parents-really-want-from-their-childrens-education/)

**America Succeeds (2024):**
- **86-88% of parents** (bipartisan) say it's very/extremely important for schools to prioritize "durable skills" including **character**
- [America Succeeds](https://americasucceeds.org/new-polling-data-parents-want-schools-to-prioritize-durable-skills)

### Post-COVID Community Rebuilding

- Appetite for in-person gatherings in 2025 is **"stronger than ever"**
- Clear trend toward **smaller, hyper-focused, niche events** rather than large generic ones
- **80%+ of events** adopting hybrid models, but desire for in-person remains driven by deeper connection
- Niche networking events are **the fastest growing segment**
- [Eventflare](https://eventflare.io/journal/the-evolution-of-networking-events-in-the-post-pandemic-world) | [Redstone Agency](https://redstoneagency.com/6-trends-impacting-in-person-events-post-covid/)

### The Convergence

Three forces colliding at once:
1. **Loneliness epidemic** creating desperate hunger for belonging
2. **Parental anxiety** about values, screens, and moral development
3. **Post-COVID craving** for intimate, in-person community

A program that addresses all three -- community belonging via shared meals, focused on instilling human values in children, bringing parents and teachers together -- sits at the exact intersection of these trends.

## 4. Growth Playbook Patterns: What Worked for Similar Organizations

### Scaling Pathways (Wallace Foundation Framework)

Three proven models for community programs going from local to national:

1. **Branching**: Gradually establish in new locations (high fidelity, slow)
2. **Affiliate**: Partner with existing organizations in new locations (moderate fidelity, faster)
3. **Distribution Network**: Collaborate with established networks for rapid spread (lower fidelity, fastest)

Source: [Wallace Foundation](https://wallacefoundation.org/report/strategies-scale-social-programs)

### The "Pull Approach" (Stanford Social Innovation Review)

- Instead of pushing into new cities, **let communities ask you to come**
- "The goal was to pull nonprofits to communities that were ready to receive and support them"
- Trust local leaders; empower their decision-making
- Source: [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/scaling-nonprofits-pull-approach)

### What Worked for Comparable Programs

| Organization | Scaling Strategy | Key Success Factor |
|---|---|---|
| **OneTable** (700+ cities) | Platform + host subsidies + coaching | Made hosting frictionless; financial + logistical support |
| **The Dinner Party** (90+ cities) | Trained host facilitators + organic word-of-mouth | Emotional resonance of the core experience; Ashoka/media recognition |
| **Roots of Empathy** (12 countries) | Evidence base + government partnerships + teacher training | Research proving outcomes opened institutional doors |
| **Character.org** (nationwide) | Certification/recognition program + school adoption | Schools compete for "School of Character" designation |
| **SSEHV** (200+ US centers) | Volunteer-led centers + annual training institutes | Deep community commitment; low overhead |

### The Playbook for HVC

**Phase 1 -- Prove the Model (1-3 cities)**
- Run Sunday dinners consistently for 6-12 months
- Document everything: stories, outcomes, participant testimonials
- Build the evidence that this format works

**Phase 2 -- Train Hosts (5-15 cities)**
- Create a "Host Training" curriculum (like OneTable's coaching, Dinner Party's facilitator training)
- Provide a Sunday Dinner Kit: conversation guides, values frameworks, recipes, logistics checklist
- Let early participants become hosts in their communities

**Phase 3 -- Platform + Content (15-50 cities)**
- Build a discovery platform (find a Sunday dinner near you)
- Launch content engine (podcast, YouTube, social) to create pull demand
- Apply for institutional funding with Phase 1 evidence

**Phase 4 -- Affiliate Network (50+ cities)**
- Partner with schools, churches, community centers, parent groups
- Distribution through existing networks (PTA chapters, yoga studios, mindfulness centers)
- Balance model fidelity with local adaptation

## 5. Content & Storytelling Opportunities

### The Story to Tell

The most powerful frame: **"In a world where screens are raising our kids, a group of parents and teachers started having Sunday dinner together."**

This story hits because:
- **Contrast**: Technology vs. tradition, digital vs. human
- **Simplicity**: Anyone can understand and picture a Sunday dinner
- **Nostalgia**: Recalls a time when communities did this naturally
- **Agency**: Parents and teachers doing something about it, not waiting for institutions

### Platform Strategy

**Podcast (Primary Channel)**
- Format: "Sunday Dinner Conversations" -- actual recorded dialogues from dinners (with permission), interspersed with expert interviews
- Why podcasts: Nonprofits facing funding cuts are turning to podcasts as primary storytelling vehicles ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/09/02/nonprofits-youtube-podcasts-storytelling-funding-cuts-influencers/))
- The intimate, conversational format mirrors the dinner experience itself
- Successful nonprofit podcasts focus on **one audience** and ask "What does this group need that we could uniquely provide?"
- Target: Parents who feel isolated in trying to raise values-driven kids

**YouTube / Short-Form Video**
- Documentary-style mini-series: Follow 3-4 families through a season of Sunday dinners
- "What Would You Do?" values dilemma shorts (high share potential)
- Highlight reels from actual dinners (the laughter, the real conversations)
- Videos get **94% more views** than text and are shared **40% more often**
- Authenticity > production value for nonprofit content

**LinkedIn / Social Media**
- Parent stories: "My kid said this at the dinner table last Sunday..."
- Teacher perspectives: "What I wish parents knew about values at school"
- Data-driven posts: Share the Dunbar research, Family Dinner Project findings, Surgeon General data
- Community spotlights as each new city launches

**Newsletter**
- Weekly "Values Conversation Starter" -- one topic, one activity, one story
- Builds habit aligned with the Sunday rhythm
- Low-cost, high-engagement channel for community building between dinners

### Storytelling Principles for This Space

From nonprofit storytelling research:
- **Character-driven stories over institutional messaging** -- feature real parents, real teachers, real kids
- **Transparency about how it works** -- show the dinner setup, the awkward first moments, the breakthrough conversations
- Stories that provide **more transparency about how donations are used** connect people to the work
- "Storytelling is how we're able to draw people in and get them to connect to a deeper truth about themselves"
- Source: [Council of Nonprofits](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/articles/nonprofit-storytelling-six-reasons-start-telling-great-stories)

- Earned revenue (curriculum, training, consulting) alongside grants
- Community-funded model (each dinner partially funded by participants)

## 7. Key Insight: The Single Biggest Opportunity

### The White Space: "The Family Dinner Project" Meets "Character.org" -- With a Soul

The values-based education space is bifurcated:

**On one side**: Institutional programs (Character.org, CASEL, CHARACTER COUNTS!) that operate through schools and districts. They have evidence bases and policy influence, but they are **cold** -- frameworks, assessments, compliance. They don't create community. A parent never says "I feel less alone because my kid's school adopted the 11 Principles Framework."

**On the other side**: Spiritual/religious community programs (SSEHV, church groups, OneTable) that create genuine warmth and belonging, but are **bounded by identity** -- you need to be part of a specific tradition to participate. A secular parent looking for values education doesn't walk into a Sai Center.

**In the middle**: The Family Dinner Project proved that meals + values conversation = powerful outcomes, but it's **family-only**. It doesn't bridge the parent-teacher divide. It doesn't create inter-family community.

**The white space is a program that is:**
- **Warm like a spiritual community** (shared meals, ritual, belonging)
- **Universal like a secular framework** (human values, not doctrine)
- **Community-bridging** (parents AND teachers, across families)
- **Content-forward** (stories that spread, not just programs that serve)

### The Timing Could Not Be Better

- **$256 million** from Lilly Endowment alone flowing into character development (2024-2025)
- Surgeon General's **national loneliness strategy** creating institutional tailwind
- **86-88% of parents** (bipartisan) want schools to prioritize character
- **64% of parents** think education is on the wrong track
- Post-COVID appetite for **intimate, niche, purpose-driven gatherings** at an all-time high
- The supper club movement proving the **cultural appetite for meals-as-community**

### The Positioning

**Not** "another character education program."
**Not** "a religious values group."
**Not** "a parenting support group."

**Instead**: "The community you didn't know you were looking for. Parents and teachers, around a table, raising kids who know what matters."

The Sunday dinner format is the Trojan horse. People come for the meal, the warmth, the belonging. They stay for the transformation they see in their kids -- and in themselves.

### The Moat

Once you have **stories** -- real families, real teachers, real conversations, real transformations -- nobody can replicate that. Programs can be copied. Curricula can be cloned. But a community with stories is a living thing. The Dinner Party scaled because every dinner created a new story. OneTable scaled because every Shabbat table became someone's memory.

The content IS the growth engine. Every Sunday dinner is a potential podcast episode, a potential viral moment, a potential donor story, a potential new host's origin story.

**Build the table. The stories will build the movement.**
