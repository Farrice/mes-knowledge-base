## Verdict

Lead-to-proposal wins narrowly over lead-response and booking. Booking is easier to demonstrate, but it is more exposed to commodity builds and stops before the proposal bottleneck. The primary combines visible demand across qualification, follow-up, CRM, and proposals with a bounded implementation Farrice can show without a live client system.

The result is not a claim that this exact package sells. It is a decision about which hypothesis deserves the next 20 qualified conversations.

## Pressure-test findings

- **Remove “AI” from the headline:** The offer remains clear. AI is a mechanism, not the outcome.
- **30-second comprehension:** Buyer, leak, build, five-day timing, $1,500 price, risk reversal, and CTA are all explicit.
- **Commodity defense:** The paid scope includes diagnosis, acceptance criteria, human review, 18 test cases, documentation, and payment tied to a demo—not just a Zap or prompt.
- **One-operator delivery:** The cap of one workflow, two integrations, and one template prevents custom-app creep.
- **Numerical claims:** Five days, $1,500, test counts, and capacity are offer terms or test targets. No revenue lift is promised.
- **Proof honesty:** Marketplace budgets and supplier earnings prove category activity, not Farrice's offer.

## What would change the verdict

Another candidate must show both stronger buyer cash evidence and a faster, more credible demonstration using Farrice's current capabilities. No candidate met both conditions in this evidence pass.
