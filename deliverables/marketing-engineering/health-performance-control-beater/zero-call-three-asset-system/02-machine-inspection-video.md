## Record-ready script

The shift was simple.

More files do not produce more learning when every file repeats the same untested idea.

So the machine starts before design.

It starts with one current control, the evidence around it and a decision about what deserves to be tested next.

I call the bounded version the Control-Beater.

The name describes the job the creative is entering. It is not a promise that the work will beat the control.

The job is to build a disciplined outside round against it: five static concepts and one video concept, each tied to a distinct hypothesis, with enough source and ownership around the work that the account can learn from what happens next.

## Room one: fit before work

The first deliverable is not an ad.

It is a fit decision.

I need one already-selling brand, one priority SKU and one real buying event. Creative fatigue. A launch. A reformulation. Retail expansion. A new piece of evidence the market has not understood yet.

I also need a current Meta control.

Not “this one got good engagement.”

What makes it the control? Who defined that? Over what date range? What account outcome does the brand use to judge it?

If the brand cannot identify a control, that is not something I guess from the public ad library. The verdict becomes `DISCOVER_FIRST`.

That protects both sides.

The brand does not buy six speculative files.

I do not pretend public impressions are private account truth.

The same gate applies to ownership. I need to know who approves the work, who reviews product and claim language, who launches it and who returns the result.

If those names do not exist, the work stops at `HOLD`.

No amount of creative polish can replace an owner.

## Room two: read the evidence

Once fit is real, I inspect three bodies of evidence.

First, the control itself.

I am looking for the message, proof device, customer moment, mechanism, offer posture and execution choices that appear to travel together.

Second, recent winner and loser evidence.

This does not have to be a perfect research project. It does have to be honest. If a baseline is unavailable, it stays `UNVERIFIED`. The team cannot quietly turn a guess into a fact because the deck needs a complete row.

Third, approved product truth.

The label. Product page. Claims matrix. Product-specific studies or substantiation the brand actually relies on. Existing reviewer decisions. Brand voice and creative rules.

This matters in health and performance because a claim can be directionally familiar and still be wrong for the product, formula, dose, context or net impression.

I can screen, question, narrow and rewrite.

I do not certify legality. I do not replace the brand's qualified reviewer.

[ON SCREEN: three columns: `ACCOUNT TRUTH`, `PRODUCT TRUTH`, `APPROVAL TRUTH`.]

If any of those columns is empty, we know exactly what is missing before the clock starts.

## Room three: freeze the hypothesis map

Now the creative decision gets made.

The map has one row per concept.

Each row names the buyer moment, the belief the concept must change, the hypothesis under test and the evidence allowed to support it. It also records the execution that gives the idea a fair expression and the result that would change the next decision.

The important test is material difference.

Concept two is probably a variant when it can inherit concept one's headline without changing the reason to buy. Color, crop or creator differences do not change that. A painful-moment concept, a mechanism explanation and a proof-inspection concept may be distinct hypotheses.

May be.

The actual product and account evidence still decide whether they belong.

## Room four: freeze the claim boundary

Before the copy hardens, every claim enters one of three zones.

**Brand-approved.** The brand has supplied the source and approved wording for this product and use.

**Reviewer-needed.** There may be a supportable path, but a qualified person must decide the wording and net impression.

**Prohibited or unsupported.** Disease claims, guaranteed outcomes, ingredient-to-product leaps without a verified formula/dose match, or language the evidence does not carry.

The client owns substantiation and final approval.

I own the responsibility to not hide a risky claim inside a clever hook and hope nobody notices.

The permitted service claim is narrow: the supplied work is screened against the published guidance and platform rules available during the run, then handed to the client's qualified reviewer.

“Pre-cleared” is not permitted.

“Legally approved” is not permitted.

“Guaranteed to pass Meta” is not permitted.

This is a creative-strategy and production screen. It is not legal certification.

## Room five: make the six concepts

Now design starts.

Five static concepts. One video concept.

Each gets a concept line, hook, body direction, visual logic, source notes, claim state and the hypothesis it exists to test.

The goal is not to make six pieces look unrelated.

The goal is to make each hypothesis unmistakable while keeping the brand recognizable.

A weak process often does the reverse: the files look wildly different, but they all say the same thing.

Here, the strategic difference survives even if every piece uses the same typeface and palette.

For each concept I ask what the buyer notices first and which belief is doing the work. Then I check the evidence that earns that belief, separate expression from the actual test and name anything that would invalidate the concept before it ships.

The video concept gets the same discipline. Motion is not automatically a new hypothesis. If the video is just the static copy read out loud, it is another variant.

## Room six: review, launch and readout

Named owners receive the delivered pack.

Final claim and net-impression approval belongs to the qualified reviewer.

Brand fit belongs to the creative owner.

Media decides what launches, how it is exposed and which account outcome counts.

Farrice does not silently inherit any of those jobs.

The readout separates events that businesses often collapse:

`DELIVERED` means the agreed work was accepted.

`LAUNCHED` means the client confirms at least one planned test went live.

`OUTCOME` means an authorized account readout came back against the stated baseline.

`NO EVENT` means the work did not launch or the result did not return. It does not become a win or a loss through storytelling.

One correction memo closes the loop. It records what happened, what the evidence supports, what remains unknown and what the next round should preserve, change or stop.

That is how the batch becomes a learning event.

## What this can and cannot promise

This machine can promise a bounded job: a fit and input screen, a frozen hypothesis map, five static concepts, one video concept, a claim-boundary sheet and a launch/readout template. Delivery is due within 72 hours after cleared payment and complete accepted inputs. A policy rejection attributable to the supplied creative receives one bounded fix or replacement.

The machine cannot promise performance or certify compliance. It cannot manufacture a control, buyer insight or result the brand never supplied, and it cannot make a brand launch the work or return the data.

That boundary is not a disclaimer pasted at the bottom.

It is part of the method.

## Who fits the machine

This is built for an already-selling supplement, performance-nutrition or recovery brand with a current Meta control, one priority SKU and people who can review, launch and return results.

A pre-revenue brand that needs its first identity is too early. A team asking an outsider to infer private performance from public ads is missing the required evidence. I will not take on a product I would not defend or a buyer who wants a promise that six concepts will beat the control.

The whole point is to make the next round more legible.

If someone needs certainty before testing, this is the wrong job.
