# Cold Opens: Three Pilot Teasers
### Eric Roth Opening Scene Architect × Michael Connelly Slingshot Opener

> **Method**: Each cold open uses the Roth "Home Test" (place the audience in the world before telling them what it's about) combined with Connelly's "slingshot" technique (pull back, then release into the story at speed). Sensory-first. Theme-seeded. No exposition.

---

## POTLUCK — "The Good Chair"

### Sensory Palette
*Sounds*: A trumpet being played badly, then beautifully, in the same breath. The squeak of small shoes on linoleum. A phone vibrating on a metal desk. Laughter from two rooms away — the kind where you can tell someone's crying.

*Smells*: Lysol over cilantro. Coffee that's been on the burner since 6 AM. The specific sweetness of a public school hallway where someone microwaved Cup Noodles.

*Visuals*: Fluorescent light that buzzes. A classroom where the music stands share space with a secondhand couch. Faded motivational posters half-covered by newer, handmade ones that say things like "YOUR FEELINGS ARE NOT HOMEWORK (but they're still valid)."

---

### THE SCENE

**FADE IN:**

**INT. MARCUS'S MUSIC CLASSROOM — CRENSHAW ELEMENTARY — 3:47 PM**

The last bell rang seventeen minutes ago. The classroom should be empty. It is not.

MARCUS REYES-GOLDSTEIN (33, slight, wearing a button-down that's given up on its top button) sits in a child-sized plastic chair across from DENISE CARTER (40s, school lunch lady uniform, still wearing the hairnet). Between them: a music stand repurposed as a side table, holding two mugs of teacher's-lounge coffee and a box of off-brand tissues.

Denise is mid-sentence. She's been mid-sentence for eleven minutes.

**DENISE**: ...and I told him, I said, Ray, you cannot keep the dog in the apartment if the dog eats the couch. That is a boundary. I am *setting* a boundary. And you know what he said?

**MARCUS**: What'd he say?

**DENISE**: He said the dog was there first.

Beat. Marcus processes this.

**MARCUS**: ...The dog was there before the couch?

**DENISE**: The dog was there before *me*, Marcus.

**MARCUS** *(carefully)*: And how did that make you feel?

**DENISE**: Like I'm third in line behind a pitbull and a sectional from Rooms To Go.

Marcus nods. Not a therapist nod — an improv nod. The "yes, and" of a man who used to do open mics and now does this instead.

**MARCUS**: Can I reflect something back to you?

**DENISE**: That's why I'm in the good chair.

She pats the arm of her chair — an adult-sized office chair, the only one in the room, positioned exactly where a patient's chair would be. Marcus is in the kid's chair. He's always in the kid's chair.

**MARCUS**: You said Ray told you the dog was there before you. But you didn't leave. You're not here asking me whether to leave. You're here because you want to know if it's okay to *stay*.

Denise goes still. The hairnet crinkles.

**DENISE** *(quietly)*: ...Is it?

**MARCUS**: Is what okay?

**DENISE**: Staying somewhere you're third.

A long beat. Marcus fidgets with a guitar pick in his pocket — the nervous tic he can't quit.

**MARCUS**: I think... the question isn't the ranking. The question is whether being third in Ray's apartment feels worse than being first somewhere else.

Denise stares at him. Then her eyes fill.

**DENISE**: How you always know the thing to say?

**MARCUS** *(deflecting, fast)*: I don't. I really — that was mostly improv. I took classes.

**DENISE**: Where'd you get your degree?

**MARCUS**: My — ?

**DENISE**: Your psychology degree. Where from.

CLOSE ON Marcus's face. The guitar pick stops spinning. 

**MARCUS**: ...I'm gonna get you a refill on that coffee.

He stands — too fast — and crosses to the Mr. Coffee machine on the counter. His back is to Denise. We see what she can't: his hand is shaking.

Behind him, Denise takes out her phone and begins texting. We see the text:

*"Girl. You NEED to come see Dr. G."*

PULL BACK through the classroom door into the hallway, where a hand-written sign on the door reads: **"MR. REYES-GOLDSTEIN — MUSIC — OFFICE HOURS BY APPOINTMENT (Tuesdays & Thursdays, 3:30-5)"**

Below it, someone has written in Sharpie: **"The doctor is IN."**

Marcus hasn't seen it yet.

**SMASH CUT TO TITLE CARD: POTLUCK**

## DAD'S PLUS-ONE — "Picture Day"

### Sensory Palette
*Sounds*: A $200,000 car engine in a school zone, idling self-consciously. Children screaming with the specific frequency of 7:45 AM. A phone buzzing with seventeen unread texts, all from someone named "Publicist - Maya."

*Smells*: Leather interior meets sunscreen-and-goldfish-cracker, the olfactory evidence of a man whose car was recently claimed by a child. New-backpack plastic. Dry shampoo deployed in desperation.

*Visuals*: A Porsche Taycan in a line of Subarus and Hondas. A man in sunglasses who is clearly, even from behind, unreasonably handsome. A lunchbox that appears to have been packed by someone who has never been inside a grocery store.

---

### THE SCENE

**FADE IN:**

**EXT. WESTLAKE VILLAGE ELEMENTARY — DROP-OFF LINE — 7:48 AM**

A Porsche Taycan moves through the carpool line at the speed of institutional caution. Every other car in line is a Honda, a Toyota, or a Subaru with a soccer sticker. The Porsche looks like a panther at a petting zoo.

**INT. PORSCHE — CONTINUOUS**

DANIEL BANDAY (36, the kind of face that makes people walk into lampposts) grips the steering wheel with both hands. He is wearing a plain white t-shirt that costs more than the car behind him. His sunglasses are worth a mortgage payment. He is sweating.

In the backseat: CLEO BANDAY (9, braids, the composure of a C-suite executive). She is reviewing a printed document.

**CLEO**: Dad.

**DANIEL**: Yeah, baby.

**CLEO**: You signed the picture day form wrong.

**DANIEL**: What? No I didn't. I signed —

**CLEO**: You signed it "Daniel Banday." 

**DANIEL**: ...That's my name.

**CLEO**: It asks for "parent or guardian signature." You wrote your autograph. Your *movie* autograph. With the little star.

Daniel looks at the form. There it is: his agent-designed, PR-approved signature, with the trademark star flourish he uses for fans. On a school form. Next to the box that says "PHOTO PACKAGE SELECTED: A / B / C" where he's written nothing.

**DANIEL**: Which package is the right one?

**CLEO** *(already circling B)*: B. It's always B. A is too many wallet-sizes and C doesn't include the class photo.

**DANIEL**: How do you know this?

**CLEO**: Mom told me. Mom also told me to tell you that picture day is today, which I told you yesterday, which is why I'm wearing this —

She gestures to her outfit: carefully coordinated, clearly chosen by a nine-year-old who has accepted that no adult in this vehicle can be trusted with decisions.

**CLEO**: — and you're wearing *that*.

She looks at his outfit. He looks at his outfit.

**DANIEL**: What's wrong with this?

**CLEO**: You're going to walk me inside looking like you just came from a premiere. The other dads wear, like, North Face.

**DANIEL**: I own North Face.

**CLEO**: You own North Face because they sent it to you. You've never worn it.

**DANIEL**: I've worn it.

**CLEO**: Name one time.

Beat.

**DANIEL**: ...There was a ski thing.

**CLEO**: The Sundance gifting lounge is not skiing, Dad.

A CROSSING GUARD waves them forward. Daniel pulls up to the drop-off zone. A VOLUNTEER MOM approaches his window with a clipboard. She bends down, sees his face, and freezes.

**VOLUNTEER MOM**: Oh my — you're —

**DANIEL** *(press-junket smile, reflexive)*: Hey! Good morning.

**VOLUNTEER MOM**: I loved you in *Meridian*.

**DANIEL**: Thank you so much. That means —

**CLEO** *(from the back seat, opening her own door)*: His name is Daniel and I'm in Mrs. Patterson's class and he already signed the form. Bye, Dad.

She's out. Backpack on. Walking. She doesn't look back.

Daniel watches her go. The volunteer mom is still frozen at his window. Behind him, a Honda honks.

**DANIEL** *(to himself, quietly)*: ...Which one is Mrs. Patterson?

His phone buzzes. He looks down. A text from RACHEL:

*"Did she get there okay? Did you remember picture day? Did you pack the lunch I left in the fridge or did you make one?"*

Daniel looks at the passenger seat. On it: a lunchbox he packed himself. He opens it. Inside: a protein bar, a bag of almonds, and what appears to be sushi from last night's DoorDash.

He closes it.

He opens the glove compartment. Inside: the lunch Rachel left, perfectly packed, labeled "CLEO — MONDAY" in a mother's handwriting.

He grabs it, unbuckles, and sprints across the parking lot after his daughter — a $40-million movie star running through a school zone holding a lunchbox with a cartoon cat on it.

Three moms in the parking lot watch him run. One takes a photo. 

**SMASH CUT TO TITLE CARD: DAD'S PLUS-ONE**

## GUEST STAR — "The Procedural"

### Sensory Palette
*Sounds*: The hum of a sound stage — that specific electric silence that means cameras are about to roll. A walkie-talkie squawking. Someone saying "checking the gate" even though everything is digital now. A man breathing through a monologue he didn't write.

*Smells*: Craft services coffee (industrial, burnt, sacred). Hairspray on a wig that doesn't quite fit. The metallic tang of a prop gun that's been handled by forty people.

*Visuals*: The seam between reality and fiction — the back of a set where plywood meets paint, where the "brick wall" ends and the warehouse begins. A man in a detective costume sitting in a folding chair, eating a Kind bar, going over lines that will air once and be forgotten.

---

### THE SCENE

**FADE IN:**

**INT. SOUND STAGE 12 — WARNER BROS. LOT — 6:22 AM**

A crime scene. Yellow tape. A body under a sheet. Two detectives in overcoats standing over it.

Except: the body is a dummy, the tape is from the prop department, and one of the detectives is eating a breakfast burrito.

We're on the set of *BADGE & BONE*, a network procedural in its ninth season. The kind of show your parents watch. The kind of show that's always on. The kind of show where guest stars come to die.

JOSH MARIN (37, handsome in a way that's hard to remember, wearing a costume that says "DETECTIVE PARTNER" on the wardrobe tag even though his character has a name) stands in his mark. He's holding the script. We can see his lines — there are four of them, highlighted in yellow.

The LEAD ACTOR (50s, name-above-the-title energy, hasn't made eye contact with Josh) takes his mark across the fake crime scene.

**ASSISTANT DIRECTOR** *(over walkie)*: First positions, everyone. Going in five.

Josh looks at his four lines. He mouths them. He's done this forty-seven times on forty-seven different shows. He could do this in a coma. That's the problem.

**LEAD ACTOR** *(not looking up from his phone)*: You the guest?

**JOSH**: Yeah. Josh Marin. I'm playing —

**LEAD ACTOR**: Cool. Don't step on my cross. *(walks away)*

Josh stands alone on the mark. The dummy lies under the sheet at his feet.

**JOSH** *(to the dummy)*: You and me both, pal.

A HAIR AND MAKEUP ARTIST approaches, touches up Josh's face. She squints at him.

**MAKEUP ARTIST**: Wait. Were you on that thing? The show about the —

**JOSH**: Probably. Which one?

**MAKEUP ARTIST**: The one where you die.

**JOSH**: You're going to have to be more specific.

**MAKEUP ARTIST** *(laughing)*: No, the one where you die in the restaurant. And the woman —

**JOSH**: *Night Service*. Season 3, episode 7. I choked on a shrimp.

**MAKEUP ARTIST**: Yes! That was *you*!

**JOSH**: That was me.

**MAKEUP ARTIST**: You were great in that.

**JOSH**: Thank you. *(beat)* What was my character's name?

**MAKEUP ARTIST**: ...the shrimp guy?

Josh nods. Smiles. This is his life.

**DIRECTOR** *(over loudspeaker)*: Okay, let's go. Scene 14, take 1. Josh, you say your lines, Jake does his close-up, we move on. Simple.

Simple. Josh looks at his four lines:

*"Detective, I found something."*
*"It's a match. The DNA confirms it."*
*"What do you want to do?"*
*"Copy that."*

Four lines. In and out. Forgettable. Another credit on a résumé nobody reads.

**DIRECTOR**: Action.

Josh opens his mouth. His first line is "Detective, I found something."

Instead, he says:

**JOSH** *(in character, but different — slower, with weight)*: Detective... I think I found something. But I need you to come look. Because if I'm right... this changes everything we thought we knew.

The LEAD ACTOR stares at him. That wasn't in the script. The director doesn't cut.

**LEAD ACTOR** *(improvising, surprised, suddenly present)*: ...Show me.

Something shifts. The scene isn't procedural anymore. It's *alive*. The crew notices. A camera operator leans in. The script supervisor stops writing.

Josh adds a pause. Looks down at the dummy. Looks back up.

**JOSH** *(quiet, unrehearsed)*: I don't think she died the way we assumed.

The director watches his monitor. Doesn't call cut. The scene plays out — two minutes of unscripted, unplanned, *electric* television.

**DIRECTOR**: ...Cut. *(long pause)*. That was... What the hell was that?

**LEAD ACTOR** *(to Josh, for the first time making eye contact)*: Where did that come from?

Josh shrugs.

**JOSH**: I had four lines. I thought I'd try five.

CUT TO:

**INT. JOSH'S CAR — WARNER BROS. PARKING LOT — LATER**

Josh sits in his ten-year-old Camry in the lot outside Sound Stage 12. He stares through the windshield at nothing.

His phone buzzes. His AGENT.

**AGENT** *(V.O.)*: Joshy. The showrunner just called me. What did you do?

**JOSH**: I went off-script.

**AGENT**: I know you went off-script. She wants to make you recurring. Three episodes.

Josh closes his eyes. He didn't want this. He was supposed to go in, say his four lines, collect his check, and go home. That was the plan.

**JOSH**: ...I was trying to get fired.

**AGENT**: Well, you failed. Welcome back. *(hangs up)*

Josh sits in the car. Through the windshield, he can see Sound Stage 12 — the first stage he ever worked on, fourteen years ago. 

He reaches into his glove compartment and pulls out a folded piece of paper. We don't see what it is, but he looks at it the way you look at something that used to mean everything.

He refolds it. Puts it back.

Starts the Camry. It takes two tries.

**SMASH CUT TO TITLE CARD: GUEST STAR**
