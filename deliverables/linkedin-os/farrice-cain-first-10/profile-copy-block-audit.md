# Profile copy-block audit: v1 to v2

Internal diagnostic. The customer-facing rewrite is in `profile-v2-tension-pass.md`.

## Block map of the v1 About

1. **[Pain + Curiosity]** “Your product works. Getting a stranger to believe it is the whole job.”
2. **[Pain]** “Nobody you've hired so far has actually owned it.”
3. **[Promise]** “I own that job for supplement, recovery, and performance brands.”
4. **[Conditions]** The Control-Beater price, prepayment, and 72-hour delivery.
5. **[Conditions]** Required inputs and six deliverables.
6. **[Proof + Conditions]** Meta and FTC screening plus the rejection remedy.
7. **[Conditions]** The Angle Map price, prepayment, and 48-hour delivery.
8. **[Conditions]** Required inputs, live read, outputs, and scope guarantee.
9. **[Conditions]** The Proof-to-Market price and timeline.
10. **[Conditions]** Launch occasions and deliverables.
11. **[Proof]** Eighteen years as a trainer and supplement-counter experience.
12. **[Proof + Pain]** Half-finished tubs, researched doses, and eye-roll claims.
13. **[Constraints]** Editorial-versus-legal approval boundary.
14. **[Proof]** No revenue promise, invented science, or fake control result.
15. **[Conditions]** DM PROOF and the launch qualifier.

## Move 1: missing or thin blocks

### Curiosity is thin

Reader reaction: “I understand the service, but I already know what happens next.”

Fix:

> I spent years behind a Vitamin Shoppe counter. People did not talk like supplement ads.

> They asked if the pills were too big. Whether something mixed with their medication. Why this tub cost $20 more than the one beside it.

### Pain is accurate but not felt

Reader reaction: “Message clarity sounds useful, but postponing it does not feel costly.”

Fix:

> Brands answer it with more hooks and another batch of edits. But twenty versions of an undecided message are still one weak idea.

### Promise is clear but generic

Reader reaction: “Another strategist who owns messaging.”

Fix:

> I find the belief standing between the formula and the sale. Then I build the campaign around what the brand can honestly prove.

### Conditions dominate

Reader reaction: “This has become a rate card before I want the work.”

Fix: move the buyer scene, expensive shrug, and campaign mechanism ahead of the three offers. Keep every price, input, output, timeline, and boundary after desire exists.

## Move 2: strengthen what is present

### Pain: weakest dimension is visual

Before:

> Getting a stranger to believe it is the whole job.

After:

> A formula can be excellent and the label can survive review. The buyer can still shrug. That shrug gets expensive.

### Promise: weakest dimension is specificity

Before:

> I own that job.

After:

> I find the belief standing between the formula and the sale. Then I build the campaign around what the brand can honestly prove.

### Proof: weakest dimension is placement

Before: the 18-year buyer-side history appears after every offer.

After:

> I spent years behind a Vitamin Shoppe counter. Then I spent 18 years training the same buyer.

### Constraints: weakest dimension is relevance

Before: legal and performance disclaimers appear as a dense defensive block.

After:

> Claims screening is editorial, not legal advice. Your qualified reviewer keeps final approval. I do not promise revenue, invent science, or say a spec ad beat a control before it did.

### Curiosity: weakest dimension is specificity

Before:

> I know what buyers say when the label is no longer in the room.

After:

> They asked if the pills were too big. Whether something mixed with their medication. Why this tub cost $20 more than the one beside it.

### Conditions: weakest dimension is velocity

Before: the first offer begins in paragraph three and conditions occupy most of the page.

After: lead with six short paragraphs that alternate scene, proof, pain, curiosity, and promise. Then slow down deliberately for the three offer doors.

## Velocity

**V1 opening:** three blocks appear in the first five sentences, but Conditions begin at sentence four and run for seven consecutive sentences.

**V1 distribution:** Pain 3 · Promise 1 · Proof 4 · Constraints 1 · Curiosity 1 · Conditions 8.

**V2 opening:** the first five paragraphs move through Proof → Curiosity → Proof → Pain → Constraint, then land the Promise before the offer details.

**Pre-CTA slowdown:** deliberate. Price, scope, turnaround, guarantee, and approval boundaries appear after the mechanism has earned attention.

## Diagnosis

The v1 About is structurally sound but conditions-heavy. Its binding constraint is not clarity; it is curiosity. The profile needs to make the buyer feel the costly gap between a good formula and an unmoved customer, then reveal why Farrice sees that gap differently.

## Prioritized rewrites

1. Move the Vitamin Shoppe scene to line one.
2. Name the expensive enemy: twenty versions of one undecided message.
3. Turn the 18-year history into the reason the angle is different, not a résumé footnote.
4. Lead the ladder with the Angle Map, where Farrice's judgment is most visible.
5. Keep the conditions, but place them after desire.
6. Reduce DM PROOF to one strategic appearance.
