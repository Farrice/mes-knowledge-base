## Your taste test

### Profile verdict received: FIX, 7/10

**Keep:** the architecture, clarity, expertise, offer cards, concrete deliverables, and decision-ready structure.

**Change:** add enough tension, distinct point of view, and emotional pull that the reader remembers Farrice and wants the offer instead of merely understanding it.

**Why:** the first draft was logical, direct, and useful, but became dry and blasé halfway through. It was not forgettable, but it was not memorable enough to compel action.

**Repair shipped:** [Profile architecture v2: tension pass](profile-v2-tension-pass.md). The [copy-ready About](about-v2-copy-ready.md) isolates the live copy, and the [copy-block audit](profile-copy-block-audit.md) preserves the diagnosis and each structural repair.

Use three verdicts only:

- **PASS:** I would publish this with minor copy edits.
- **FIX:** The idea is right; name the exact line, beat, or feeling that fails.
- **KILL:** The premise, posture, or audience job is wrong.

## The first real review

After Farrice publishes the block and fills `posts.csv`, run the OS review. The system will compare follower efficiency, profile-view rate, saves, reposts, comments, DMs, leads, and revenue.

The next block changes one variable only. If the named-brand teardowns drive reach but not qualified profile views, the profile or audience match is weak. If authority posts earn saves but the offer post earns no buyer behavior, the offer remains unproven. If the personal posts dominate both reach and buyer DMs, buyer-side autobiography may deserve a larger share without turning the account into a journal.

Follower count is the scoreboard people notice. Buyer behavior is the scoreboard the business can use.
