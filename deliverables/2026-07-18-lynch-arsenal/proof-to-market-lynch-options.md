## Part 1 — The Cost + Royalty Version of the Sprint

Lynch's frame, verbatim: **"I'll do it for this and a little royalty."** The client reads the base as cheap; the royalty only pays if the work works. His DRTV range is 1-5% of attributed net sales depending on who initiated the deal. A positioning sprint has softer attribution than a call-center DRTV weekend, so the adaptation narrows the attributed base rather than the rate: royalty applies to **launch-window sales through instruments the sprint created**, never to brand-wide revenue.

### Option table

| Option | Structure | When it applies | Lynch analog |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Canonical (live)** | $2,500 flat, no royalty | Founding pilots, now through first 2-3 case studies | Pre-royalty career stage; the pilot IS the portfolio |
| **B — Base + launch royalty** | Full-tier base ($3,500-4,000) + **2% of attributed launch-window net sales**, 90-day window | Repeat client, or Standard-tier client with a dated launch and trackable DTC stack | Client-initiated: 1-3%, sweet spot 2% |
| **C — Reduced base + higher royalty** | ~$1,500-2,000 base + **3-4%** launch-window | Farrice initiates: he brings the occasion + AI-Search Shadow read to the brand unprompted (Dream 100 motion) | Strategist-initiated: 3-5% |
| **D — Greenfield / incubation-style** | Minimal base + **5% or small equity option**, 12-month term | Category with no language yet (e.g. Rejuvenate's GLP-1-support lane) where the messaging IS the category | Incubation: 5%+ / equity |

Assumption flag: dollar figures in B-D are Farrice's tier prices from the OS; the percentages are Lynch's ranges applied unchanged.

### Attribution that a supplement brand can actually audit

DRTV had a phone number per station. The sprint's equivalents, cheapest first:

1. **Launch discount code** — the Blueprint ships with a named code (ideally the positioning word itself). Every redemption is an attributed sale. Shopify/Amazon export makes it self-auditing.
2. **Dedicated launch landing page** — the page built on the new messaging, with its own URL/UTM. Sales through it during the window are attributed.
3. **Launch-window delta (fallback)** — for retail-heavy brands with no code path: net sales in the 90-day window vs the trailing-90 baseline, with a pre-agreed attribution fraction (assumption: 25-50%, negotiated per deal). Weakest instrument; use only when 1-2 are unavailable.

Contract terms lifted straight from the workflow: define "attributed net sales" (after returns) in writing, monthly reporting with dashboard access, quarterly audit rights, 90-day attribution window (vs Lynch's 12-24 month term — positioning decays into brand equity you can't meter), territory = the launch channels named in the Blueprint.

### When the royalty conversation happens

**Never on pilot #1.** The pilot's job is proof and a case study at a price a founder says yes to personally; adding a royalty negotiation adds friction exactly where the 25-day clock can't afford it, and no attribution rails exist yet. The conversation opens at the **repeat point** — the pilot client comes back for launch #2, or a Standard-tier client arrives with a case study in hand. By then Farrice has the Blueprint-to-sales receipts that make 2% feel like alignment instead of a tax.

### The framing, verbatim-style

"Positioning firms charge fifteen to fifty grand for this and walk away before the launch. I'll do it for $3,500 and a small royalty on launch sales through the code we write into the messaging. If the message doesn't sell, the royalty is zero and you got the deck cheap. If it sells, we both know exactly why."

Negotiation moves (from the decision tree): base too high → drop base, raise rate. "We don't do royalties" → base moves to $6-8K flat (assumption: ~2x, scaled to founder-band; Lynch's 3-5x multiplier reads as agency-scale). Lower rate requested → accept, add a volume-threshold bonus.

**Recommended default when the door opens: Option B.** It preserves the canonical ladder, uses the cleanest attribution instrument (the code), and puts Lynch's sweet-spot 2% on a base the buyer already accepted.

## Part 2 — Identity-First Upgrades to the Sprint Modules

Lynch's anchor: **"we didn't sell cameras. We sold bravery."** Applied here at two levels — who the *end customer* becomes when they buy the supplement, and who the *brand buyer* becomes when they buy the sprint.

### Module: Positioning (Stage 3 — Decide)

**Current framing:** position selection locked by proof foundation + differentiation; message architecture of headline + 3-5 core messages.
**Lynch upgrade:** before locking the thesis, force the one-word identity answer — what does the end customer *become*? A position that survives the proof stress-test but names no identity produces compliant wallpaper (Pillar 4's own diagnosis). Add one line to the Stage 3 gate: "the identity, in one word, is ___" — then run the counter-identity check, since the median supplement user is someone who forgets the tub in a cupboard; sell what the best 5% do with it.
**Example line:** for a creatine brand chasing the wellness rebrand — "We don't sell creatine. We sell *consistency* — the person who hasn't missed a day in a year. The tub is how they keep score."

### Module: Messaging + Proof Deliverables (Stages 3-4)

**Current framing:** proof hierarchy tiered by evidence strength (citations → third-party → proprietary data → testimonial); slide-by-slide messaging with example pitch/DM/retail copy.
**Lynch upgrade:** transformation-first ordering. He sold billions in appliances by leading with the food, never the machine — so the example messaging should open every asset with the *felt output* and let the ingredient science arrive as the "oh, you need this to do that" reveal. The claim-safe wedge benefits directly: general-wellness language (the safest FTC bucket) is naturally transformation language, so the compliant tier and the selling tier converge.
**Example line:** recovery supplement, retail pitch slide — "Your shopper isn't buying magnesium bisglycinate. She's buying the Tuesday she wakes up before her alarm. The label's job is to promise the Tuesday and let the ingredient panel prove it."

### Module: Buyer Motivations Audit (Stage 2 — Diagnose)

**Current framing:** why retailers, practitioners, and end-users actually buy.
**Lynch upgrade:** write the audit output in the buyer's own voice, not the brand's — his screenwriter move of narrating from inside the customer's head. A motivations audit that reads "consumers seek efficacious recovery solutions" fails; one that reads like the buyer's internal monologue hands Stage 4 its copy for free.
**Example line:** practitioner segment — "I'll recommend it if I never have to defend it. Give me the one-sentence answer for when a client asks 'is this snake oil?'"

### Module: AI-Search Shadow (Stage 2 — stays a module, per standing rule)

No restructuring. One sharpening: the live-query read should test *identity*, not just presence — does the machine describe the brand as an ingredient list or as the thing its customer becomes? "Named for its third-party testing" and "named for consistency-obsessed lifters" are different findings, and only the second proves the positioning landed.

### Customer-as-Media: Upsell, Not Sprint Scope — Verdict

The flywheel (contest funnel, UGC infrastructure, templates/tools) is **out of sprint scope** and belongs as a named follow-on module. Three reasons: the OS explicitly rules out content execution; a 10-day strategy sprint cannot build broadcast infrastructure honestly; and supplements are a *designed*-shareable-moment category (consumption is private — the shareable unit is the transformation, which takes engineering, not a hashtag). The economics still argue for building it later — Lynch, verbatim: **"I want to get my cost of acquisition to zero"** — and supplement COGS-to-retail spreads make a daily-giveaway contest funnel viable (assumption: typical spreads support giving product at cost; verify per brand).
**What stays IN the sprint:** one Blueprint slide naming the brand's shareable moment and the flywheel it implies, priced as the door to a second engagement. The sprint names the machine; the upsell builds it.
