# Proof-to-Market Sprint — The Ron Lynch Build

**What this is:** your offer, structured by Lynch — decisions made, not options. The canonical OS stays the spec of what you deliver; this is how the offer is *sold, priced, gated, and sequenced*. Ratify or strike sections; each names the pattern it runs on.

---

## 1. The identity sell (what the buyer BECOMES)

Lynch never sells the appliance. The sprint's buyer is a founder or Head of Brand about to spend $40K+ on launch creative while privately unsure what the market will actually hear.

**The identity, in one word: CERTAIN.**

You don't sell an 18-25 slide Blueprint. You sell the founder who walks into launch week already knowing what the market will hear — every asset saying one thing, the whole team pitching in one voice, the claims pre-cleared. The deck is how they get to be that founder.

Counter-identity check (GP-11): the median launch founder is a gambler who finds out after the spend. You sell the aspirational 5%: the operator who knew before the first dollar of creative.

## 2. The pitch spine (transformation-first, his register)

Never open with what the sprint contains. Open with the Tuesday:

> "Launch morning. Your sales lead pitches the retailer, your ads run, your PDP loads — and every one of them says the same thing, in words your buyer already uses, with claims legal already blessed. That's what you're buying. The way you get it is a 10-day sprint."

Then the wedge, Lynch's own deal framing adapted:

> "Positioning firms charge fifteen to fifty grand for this and walk away before the launch. I do it in 10 days for less than your photographer, and I stay pointed at the launch."

And his 2x/10x flip when they ask about outcomes: "I'm not here to promise your launch numbers. I want to know why *you're* here — if you're here to launch loud and right, this is the thing that does it if you implement it." (Power to the buyer; you want the testimonial to be *theirs*.)

## 3. The ladder (qualification → sprint → royalty)

Lynch's rule: never free-consult unknown quantities; each rung buys the next.

| Rung | What | Price | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | AI-Search Shadow read, delivered as 3 screenshots in a DM: "here's what the machines say you are — an ingredient list, not a brand" | Free | The shiny demo (snake sold the taste) |
| **Gate** | **Launch Readout** — 45 min + a 2-page written verdict on their launch messaging risk | **$495, credited to the sprint** | The $1,000-coffee, scaled to your stage; knocks out the 80% who'd never pay; the notes doc is the artifact |
| **The offer** | Proof-to-Market Sprint (canonical 5 stages, Blueprint two-column) | Tiers below | The one thing — no menu beside it |
| **Repeat** | Launch #2+ with a dated occasion and code-attribution rails | Base + 2% launch-window royalty | "I'll do it for [base] and a little royalty" — never on pilot #1 |

The customer-as-media module and the bridge SKU never appear on the menu — "choice causes confusion and confusion creates no's." One Blueprint slide names the UGC flywheel; that slide IS the door to engagement #2.

## 4. Pricing on the increment ladder (GP-15)

People think in $20 bills, then $100 bills. Your current ladder re-anchored:

- **Founding pilot: $2,500 stands** (it's a commitment gate as much as a price; do not touch the live 25-day motion)
- **Standard: $3,900** (not $3,500-4,000 — "$3,500? You just left $400 on the table." $3,900 owns the $3K-bill band ceiling)
- **Mature: $4,900** (reads as "under five grand" while collecting the whole band; $5,000 crosses a bill and demands new proof)
- Readout $495 credits in — so Standard nets $3,405 and the buyer feels the credit, which is the point
- Test UP first: the first post-case-study prospect hears $3,900 before you ever discount

## 5. Who qualifies (the four-criteria gate, GP-16)

Run every prospect through Lynch's picking test — his four zero-shows came from skipping it:

1. **Innovation** — commodity creatine in a new tub fails; a real mechanism/occasion passes ("commodities sell on price; innovation sells on life-changing opportunity")
2. **Audience** — an existing warm channel or retail conversation must exist to launch INTO
3. **Margin** — supplement COGS must support the anchor price and, later, the royalty and giveaway math; ask for the numbers in the Readout
4. **Story** — the founder has one and will say it on camera

Two or fewer = decline the sprint, keep them on the hook list. You are also picking royalty candidates two rungs early.

## 6. The proof plan (built now, harvested at pilot #1)

Design the testimonial relay before the first case study exists (GP-14). The one story, five beats, assigned at delivery time:

1. before — "every channel said something different" (founder)
2. skeptic — "another agency deck, I assumed" (founder or advisor)
3. first use — "the interview pulled words out of us we'd never written down" (team member)
4. result — "sales pitched the retailer straight off slide 9" (sales lead)
5. reaction — "the buyer repeated our line back to us" (the reaction IS the sell)

Collect one snippet per beat during the sprint itself — the case study assembles itself the day the Blueprint ships.

## 7. What I'd cut (Lynch's subtraction)

- Any second offer visible at first contact (menu = confusion)
- Outcome-percentage promises anywhere in copy (the 2x/10x flip replaces them)
- Feature-listing the 5 stages in outreach — stages are the appliance; the Tuesday is the food
