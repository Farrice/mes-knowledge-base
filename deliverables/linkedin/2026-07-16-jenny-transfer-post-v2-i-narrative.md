## THE POST

For eighteen years I was the best trainer in every gym I worked at. You've never heard of me.

Every one of those gyms had another guy. Half as careful with his programs, twice as loud about them. He filmed his sessions and posted every day. The clients I should have signed kept walking past me to him.

I had my reasons, and they sounded noble from inside. Sales felt beneath the craft. The results would speak for themselves.

Results don't speak. They whisper, to the twelve people who already knew you.

So every few months I sat down to write about my work. I typed a sentence, read it back, and heard it as bragging or obvious. Deleted it. Closed the tab. Told myself tomorrow.

I said that for years.

What I finally saw took the shame out of it. I explained hard things to clients every single day, across the table, in their language. The wall was somewhere else. I knew the work too well to say it small anymore. Every year of mastery buries the plain version deeper.

So I stopped typing and started talking. A client talked to me about her work for forty minutes. I turned twenty seconds of it into a post. Her DMs opened up within a week. She never learned to write. She stopped needing to.

If you're the best one in your building while a stranger with a ring light books the clients you should have, hear this the way I needed to hear it: nothing is wrong with you. You know the thing so well you can't say it small anymore. That's the whole problem. It has a fix.

Say it out loud once, to one person, this week.

Right now someone is typing their exact problem into a search bar at 11pm. It took me eighteen years to be findable. They deserve to find you faster.

### The Close Verdict

**"Somewhere out there a person is typing their exact problem into a search bar at 11pm. It took me eighteen years to be findable. They deserve to find you faster."**

**Verdict: KEEP, with one optional opener sharpen.** The close passes the wince test on balance. Reasoning:

- The bones are right and locked: concrete future-image (the 11pm searcher — a §3 "concrete future image" close), loop-back to the hook's "eighteen years," outward hand-on-shoulder turn that hands the arc to the reader. That's the direction he already PASSED.
- Paste test (the §6 FAIL-bank tool: "if it survives being pasted elsewhere, it's generic"): "They deserve to find you faster" *only* means something because "eighteen years" set up "faster." Strip the setup and the line dies — so it is anchored, not portable. It survives.
- The one soft spot: **"deserve"** is the single sentimental-leaning word in a post whose whole register is "emotionally precise without sentimentality" (§2). It's a faint benediction/greeting-card note. I ran three replacements ("Don't make them wait that long," "You can save them the wait," "You can get there faster than I did") — each either turns the beneficiary back to the reader and loses the outward generosity, or adds spine at the cost of the warmth the "hand on the shoulder" direction explicitly wants. None beat the original enough to justify the change. **"deserve" stays** — the outward generosity is consistent with his POV anchors (empathy = saying what they need to hear; family-first generosity), and the cost of removing it exceeds the faint sentimentality.
- **"Somewhere out there"** is a mild stock phrase (Fievel-adjacent) and the softest opener in the piece. Swapping to "Right now" kills the cliché and adds temporal urgency (the 11pm is happening *now* — a Stanton clamp lift). Optional, but it's the cleanest lift available on the close.
