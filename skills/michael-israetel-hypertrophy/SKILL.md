# Dr. Mike Israetel — Hypertrophy Science & Program Design

Dr. Michael Israetel (PhD Sport Physiology, co-founder Renaissance Periodization) built his authority on one move: strip fitness down to what the evidence actually supports, then deliver it as the *minimum effective dose* so real people with real schedules will do it. His system runs on a fixed sequence — needs analysis first, then specificity → overload → progression, with volume dialed by frequency and effort, fatigue managed by deloads and maintenance phases, and nutrition governed by calories-in/calories-out with protein as the one non-negotiable. This skill encodes that methodology so a coach can produce programs and nutrition plans that get amazing results with the least wasted work.

Load `genius.md` before producing any output — it carries the volume-landmark logic, the stimulus-to-fatigue reasoning, and the myth-busting frame that separate this from generic "eat clean and train hard" advice.

## Quick Reference

- **The fixed sequence**: Needs analysis → Specificity → Overload → Progression → Fatigue management → Nutrition. Never skip the needs analysis; without it "the plan writes itself" becomes guesswork.
- **Specificity is the #1 principle** in all of exercise science: name the exact target ("bigger biceps," "gain 5–10 lb muscle in 6 months"), then everything points at it.
- **Every working set must be challenging** — the last few reps close to technical failure. If an observer can't tell your warm-up from your working set, you have a problem.
- **Rep range is wide, not magic**: ~5–30 reps per set all grow muscle equally *if* taken close to failure. Pick what the equipment and joint tolerance allow.
- **Frequency beats magic set counts**: 2×/week per muscle is the floor; 2–4×/week is the sweet spot. Returns de-escalate exponentially after that (1×=results, 2×=~1.5×, 3×=a bit more, 4th/5th = only advanced lifters notice).
- **Minimal effective dose for a beginner is tiny**: 2 sessions/week, 2–3 sets per muscle → months of progress. 20-min full-body sessions ×2 = real transformation.
- **Muscle grows OUTSIDE the gym**: the gym is a catabolic *signal*; growth happens over the ~0.5–4 days after, driven by tension-sensing machinery (~80% of the stimulus), fed by protein/rest/sleep.
- **Volume landmarks (RP)**: MEV (minimum to grow) → MAV (most productive) → MRV (maximum recoverable) → deload. Ramp volume across the block toward MRV, then deload to shed fatigue.
- **Protein is the one supplement-tier non-negotiable**: ~0.7–1 g/lb bodyweight/day, spread over 3–5 meals. Creatine (5 g/day, no loading) is the only broadly worthwhile supplement; the rest are "not in the top 10."
- **Nutrition myth-busting**: Calories-in/calories-out is incontrovertible for weight change; macros + food quality + timing decide whether it's muscle or fat. Diet ≈ 80% of fat loss (you can't outrun a bad diet — ~100-150 cal/mile vs. 300-cal donut in 5 seconds).
- **Phase your diet**: after ~3 months of a hard cut, spend ~2-3 months at maintenance to let diet fatigue fall before the next push. Build habits, not permanent restriction — the maintenance plan is not the loss plan.
- **Muscle memory is real**: regaining lost muscle takes ~1/10th the original time. Two weeks off costs almost nothing; encourage lapsed clients back in.
