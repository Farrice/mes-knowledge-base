# Hidden Knowledge

## HK-1: Compliments Are A Trap

Customers often praise ideas because it is socially easy. The only useful signals are behavior, tradeoffs, commitments, and constraints.

## HK-2: The Old Way Must Break First

A new product rarely wins by being better in isolation. The founder must surface why the current way is becoming unacceptable.

## HK-3: Packaging Is A Learning Strategy

A concierge or Wizard of Oz MVP is not less serious than software. It is often the fastest way to learn what the software must become.

## HK-4: Price Is Part Of The Product

Price defines who the customer is, what pain is being solved, and how strongly the customer values the outcome.

## HK-5: Segment Mistakes Masquerade As Product Mistakes

The same capability can be weak in one segment and urgent in another. Steve's VR example moves from low-price developers to high-value architects because the problem economics change.

## HK-6: Founder Bias Increases After Building

The more a founder builds, the more they reinterpret weak evidence as validation. The system validates before emotional attachment hardens.

## HK-7: A Neutral Purchase Can Be A Goldmine

When customers buy despite tradeoffs, the remaining frustrations expose the next product edge.

## HK-8: Roadmaps Should Be Evidence-Gated

A roadmap that lists features is premature. A validation roadmap lists assumptions, experiments, thresholds, and decisions.

## HK-9: Customers Are Solution-Poor But Situation-Rich

Customers usually cannot design the product for you. They can, however, describe the exact situation, old solution, frustration, workaround, and purchase trigger that show what the product must do.

## HK-10: The Wrong Question Creates A Fake Market

"Would you use this?" and "Would you pay for this?" create politeness, lowballing, and speculative intent. Past behavior is harder to fake.

## HK-11: Spotlight Bias Makes Problem Interviews Dangerous

When founders ask customers to rate a preselected problem list, they often exaggerate the problem simply because the founder put a spotlight on it.

## HK-12: The Old Way Is The Competitor

The strongest competitor is often not another startup. It is email, spreadsheets, a consultant, a course, a habit, or doing nothing.

## HK-13: Repetition Beats Drama

One dramatic complaint can mislead. Ten people describing the same pet peeve, struggle, or workaround is a pattern worth selling against.
