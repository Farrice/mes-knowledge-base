# Jonah Berger — Contagious & The Catalyst

Jonah Berger is a Wharton marketing professor (PhD, Stanford GSB) who spent 20+ years applying hard-science methods — experiments, text analysis, data on millions of purchases — to one question: why do people talk about and share some things and not others? His answer: virality is not luck, it's a recipe. Six psychological drivers (STEPPS) explain sharing; five barriers (REDUCE) explain why minds don't change; and a "kernel-first" story discipline makes messages carry themselves — because great messages share themselves even when you're not in the room.

## Quick Reference

- **STEPPS** — Social currency (sharing makes the sharer look good), Triggers (top of mind = tip of tongue), Emotion (when we care, we share — high-arousal only), Public (easy to see = easy to imitate), Practical value (news you can use), Stories (information rides inside narrative).
- **REDUCE** — Reactance, Endowment, Distance, Uncertainty, Corroborating evidence. Great catalysts don't push harder; they find the parking brake and release it. Ask "why hasn't this person changed already?" — never "how do I push them?"
- **The kernel** — Before any story: if a customer could say ONE thing about you, what is it? Story is built around that moral, or the story goes viral and the brand gets nothing.
- **Psychology over technology** — 90% of word of mouth is offline/face-to-face. Platforms change; the drivers don't. Design for the driver, not the channel.
- **Message over messenger** — You don't have to be charismatic. A powerful message leaves the room with the audience and shares itself; charisma stays in the room with you.
- **Views ≠ needle** — Optimize for behavior change (calls, emails, purchases), not view counts. Many things are shared without even being watched.
