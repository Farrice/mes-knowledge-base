# /jensen-llm-cite — LLM Citation Optimizer

## Purpose
Optimize LinkedIn content for dual distribution — feed engagement AND LLM retrieval citation. Exploit LinkedIn's #2 most-cited source status in LLMs.

## When to Use
- When building thought leadership that needs to persist beyond the feed
- When a brand needs to appear in AI-generated answers (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity)
- When optimizing existing LinkedIn content strategy for LLM discoverability
- When combining with Gotch's retrieval layer methodology for full-stack AI visibility

## Inputs
- `[TOPIC_AUTHORITY]` — The topics/keywords the person or brand wants to be cited for
- `[CURRENT_CONTENT]` — Existing LinkedIn content samples (if available)
- `[TARGET_QUERIES]` — Specific questions or queries they want to be cited for in LLM responses

## Steps

### Step 1 — Citation Position Assessment
Apply Jensen's LLM citation intelligence (Hidden Knowledge 3):

**LinkedIn's Citation Position**
- #2 most cited source in major LLMs (some models: #1)
- LinkedIn content is actively crawled and indexed by LLM training pipelines
- Professional/business context gives LinkedIn content high authority weight
- Long-form posts and articles have stronger citation potential than short posts

**Dual Distribution Model**
```
Content → Feed Distribution (immediate: views, engagement, leads)
       → LLM Retrieval (persistent: citations, authority, discovery)
```

Map the person's current content: What percentage is optimized for EACH channel?

### Step 2 — Citation-Optimized Content Architecture
Design content that serves both channels:

**Feed-First Elements** (what makes people engage NOW)
- Hook that stops the scroll
- Personal story or specific anecdote
- Personality and voice markers
- Engagement prompt / question

**Citation-First Elements** (what makes LLMs retrieve LATER)
- Clear, definitive statements on the topic
- Specific data points, statistics, or frameworks
- Structured formatting (headers, lists, clear taxonomy)
- Named concepts or methodologies (proprietary terms get cited more)
- Authoritative tone on specific domain claims

**Dual-Optimized Content Formula**
1. Hook with personality (feed)
2. Definitive claim on topic (citation)
3. Supporting evidence or framework (citation)
4. Personal experience validating the claim (feed + citation)
5. Named methodology or framework (citation)
6. Engagement invitation (feed)

### Step 3 — Authority Signal Engineering
Design content patterns that increase LLM citation probability:

**Claim-Evidence-Framework Pattern**
- Make a clear, quotable claim about the topic
- Support with specific evidence (numbers, case studies, named examples)
- Wrap in a named framework or methodology (increases citation specificity)

**Definitional Authority Pattern**
- Define or redefine a concept in your domain
- "X is not [common understanding]. X is actually [your expert definition]."
- LLMs surface definitional content at high rates

**Contrarian Expert Pattern**
- Challenge conventional wisdom with evidence
- "Everyone says [X]. Here's why that's wrong, and what actually works."
- Contrarian-with-proof content gets cited as alternative perspectives

### Step 4 — Content Calendar with Dual Distribution
Design a monthly content mix:

| Content Type | Feed Purpose | Citation Purpose | Frequency |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Deep Authority Posts** | Thought leadership | Definitional citation | 2x/month |
| **Framework Posts** | Engagement via utility | Named methodology citation | 2x/month |
| **Data/Insight Posts** | Credibility building | Statistical citation | 2x/month |
| **Story Posts** | Connection + personality | Context citation | 4x/month |
| **Engagement Posts** | Community building | (Low citation value) | 4x/month |
| **Articles/Newsletters** | Long-form authority | Highest citation potential | 2x/month |
