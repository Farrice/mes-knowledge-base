# /jensen-song-sheet — B2B Song Sheet Architect

## Purpose
Build crisp, segment-specific core messages using Jensen's internal methodology. One message per audience that's "clear, exciting, not boring" — the song sheet checkpoint.

## When to Use
- Before any campaign, content series, or brand messaging project
- When messaging feels scattered across platforms or team members
- When a company can't articulate its value in one sentence per audience
- Before deploying StoryBrand (Miller), positioning (Dunford), or copy (Wiebe) workflows

## Inputs
- `[COMPANY/BRAND]` — The company, personal brand, or product
- `[AUDIENCES]` — 2-5 distinct audience segments (if unknown, Step 1 will discover them)
- `[CURRENT_MESSAGING]` — Existing taglines, positioning, value props (if any)

## Steps

### Step 1 — Audience Segment Discovery
If segments aren't provided, identify 2-5 distinct audience segments using Jensen's framework:
- **Who actually buys** (not who you wish bought)
- **Who influences the buying decision** (the committee, not just the signer)
- **Who uses the product daily** (end users with different needs than buyers)
- **Who advocates publicly** (your potential content amplifiers)

For each segment, capture:
- Core identity (who they ARE, not demographics)
- Primary anxiety (what keeps them up at night)
- Decision trigger (what tips them from "interested" to "ready")
- Emotional outcome (how they want to FEEL after buying)

### Step 2 — The Song Sheet Test
For each audience segment, draft ONE core message following Jensen's criteria:
1. **Crisp**: Can be repeated in an elevator in under 10 seconds
2. **Clear**: No jargon, no insider language, no ambiguity
3. **Exciting**: Makes someone lean in, not glaze over
4. **Not boring**: Passes the "would I stop scrolling for this?" test

Format per segment:
```
## Segment: [NAME]

### Core Message (The Song Sheet Line)
[One sentence. Maximum 15 words. Must pass all 4 criteria.]

### Reasons to Believe (3 max)
1. [Proof point that makes the message credible]
2. [Proof point that makes the message credible]
3. [Proof point that makes the message credible]

### Emotional Anchor
[The B2P feeling — what does this message make them FEEL?]
```

### Step 3 — The B2P Resonance Check
Apply Jensen's B2P Reframe (Genius Pattern 2) to every message:
- Does it appeal to the HUMAN, not the job title?
- Would it work for both "Doritos AND SaaS"?
- Does it fuse rational proof with emotional pull?
- Would a person share this at dinner, not just forward it in Slack?

Flag any message that's pure rational/product-spec → rewrite with emotional resonance.

### Step 4 — The 95/5 Distribution Design
For each song sheet line, design content distribution:
- **80% brand content** (for the 19/20 who aren't buying today): How does this message become thought leadership, stories, insights?
- **20% conversion content** (for the 1/20 who ARE ready): How does this message become a clear call to action?

### Step 5 — Song Sheet Delivery
Produce the final song sheet document:

```
# [COMPANY] SONG SHEET

## Mission (One Sentence)
[What the company exists to do]

---

## Segment 1: [NAME]
**Song Sheet Line**: [Message]
**Reasons to Believe**: [1-3 proof points]
**Emotional Anchor**: [Feeling]
**Content Split**: [Brand themes] | [Conversion hooks]

## Segment 2: [NAME]
[Same structure]

[Repeat for all segments]

---

## Usage Rules
1. Every piece of content maps to ONE segment's song sheet line
2. If you can't connect content to a song sheet line, don't publish it
3. Review and refresh quarterly — song sheets are living documents
4. Test the "repeat it back" test: ask 5 people to repeat your message
```

## Output Format
Complete song sheet document with one message per segment, B2P-validated, with brand/conversion content distribution designed.

## Quality Gate
- [ ] Each message passes the Crisp/Clear/Exciting/Not Boring test
- [ ] B2P resonance validated (human, not job title)
- [ ] Reasons to believe are specific proof points, not claims
- [ ] 80/20 content distribution designed for 7-month cycle reality
- [ ] "Repeat it back" test: Could a stranger repeat each message accurately?
