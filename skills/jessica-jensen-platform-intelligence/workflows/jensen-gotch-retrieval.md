# /jensen-gotch-retrieval — LinkedIn Retrieval Layer

## Purpose
LinkedIn-as-LLM-source strategy combining Jensen's citation intelligence with Gotch's retrieval layer methodology for maximum AI discoverability.

## Stacking Partners
- **Jessica Jensen** — LinkedIn LLM citation data, platform content architecture
- **Nathan Gotch** — Retrieval Layer SEO, Citation Intelligence, AI-first optimization

## When to Use
- When optimizing professional authority for AI-generated search results
- When building a "retrievable" LinkedIn presence (not just an engaging one)
- When a B2B brand needs to appear in AI answers to industry questions
- When combining LinkedIn authority with broader retrieval layer strategy

## Inputs
- `[AUTHORITY_TOPICS]` — 3-5 topics the person/brand wants to own in LLM responses
- `[TARGET_QUERIES]` — Specific questions prospects ask that LLMs should cite them for
- `[CURRENT_LINKEDIN]` — Current LinkedIn content and optimization state
- `[BROADER_PRESENCE]` — Website, other platforms (for cross-platform retrieval strategy)

## Steps

### Step 1 — Jensen Citation Intelligence Layer
Apply Jensen's insider data (Hidden Knowledge 3):
- LinkedIn = #2 most cited source in LLMs (#1 in some models)
- Professional authority weight gives LinkedIn content elevated citation priority
- LinkedIn Articles and Newsletters have highest citation potential
- Profile data (headline, about, experience) feeds LLM knowledge graphs

### Step 2 — Gotch Retrieval Layer Architecture
Apply Gotch's retrieval framework specifically to LinkedIn:

**Entity Establishment**
- LinkedIn profile as an "entity" in LLM knowledge graphs
- Headline → primary entity descriptor (optimized for retrieval matching)
- About section → entity context and authority signals
- Experience → entity credential validation
- Featured section → high-priority content for citation

**Content Retrieval Optimization**
- Articles and Newsletters (long-form, indexed, highest citation weight)
- Posts with definitive claims and named frameworks
- Comments on high-authority threads (citation by association)

### Step 3 — Cross-Platform Citation Architecture
Design the retrieval network:

```
LinkedIn Profile → Entity establishment
LinkedIn Articles → Deep citation content
LinkedIn Posts → Engagement + authority signals
LinkedIn Newsletter → Persistent, subscriber-distributed citation content
     ↓
Website/Blog → Reinforcing citation (Gotch domain authority)
     ↓
LLM Training Data → Dual-source citation increases retrieval probability
```

### Step 4 — Retrieval-Optimized Content Calendar
Design content specifically for LLM retrieval:

| Content Type | LinkedIn Format | Retrieval Optimization | Frequency |
|-------------|----------------|----------------------|-----------|
| **Definitional content** | Article | "What is [topic]? [Expert definition]" | 2x/month |
| **Framework content** | Article or long post | Named methodology with clear structure | 2x/month |
| **Data content** | Post with statistics | Quotable claims with specific numbers | Weekly |
| **Contrarian content** | Post | Alternative perspective on common questions | Weekly |
| **Profile optimization** | Profile sections | Entity descriptor updates | Monthly |
| **Newsletter** | LinkedIn Newsletter | Comprehensive topic coverage | 2x/month |
