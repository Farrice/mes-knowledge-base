# Tone Calibration Engine

> **Produces**: Content rewritten in the exact target tone with annotations showing WHY each word choice was made

## When to Use
- Repurposing content across platforms that require different registers (LinkedIn professional → Twitter casual)
- Client work where the brief specifies a tone you're not naturally writing in
- AI-generated content that came out in the wrong register
- Any content that "doesn't sound right" but you can't articulate why

## Input Required
1. **Source content** — the text to rewrite
2. **Target tone** — one of: casual, professional, entertaining, comedic, authoritative (or a blend like "casual-authoritative")
3. **Context** — where this will be published, who the audience is

## Execution Protocol

### Step 1: Tone Diagnosis
Analyze the source content's current register:

```
CURRENT TONE ANALYSIS
────────────────────
Register: [casual/professional/entertaining/comedic/authoritative]
Evidence: [3-5 specific word choices or sentence structures that signal this register]
Formality Level: [1-10 scale]
Authority Signal: [1-10]
Likability Signal: [1-10]
Gap to Target: [What specifically needs to change]
```

### Step 2: Register Shift Map
For each register, apply the corresponding linguistic toolkit:

**Casual**: Contractions (can't, gonna), colloquial terms, short/direct sentences, active voice, exclamations, bullet points. First-person pronouns. Sentence fragments allowed.

**Professional**: Formal vocabulary (no contractions), subordinate clauses permitted, passive voice where appropriate, semicolons, third-person or collective "we." Measured pacing. No slang.

**Entertaining**: Everyday words with first-person, varied sentence lengths, story-driven structures, italics/bold for emphasis, rhetorical questions. The rhythm creates energy.

**Comedic**: Exaggerated slang, playful substitutions, short punchy fragments, hyperbole, unexpected juxtapositions, rule-of-three (setup/setup/punchline). Break patterns for laughs.

**Authoritative**: Precise terminology, declarative sentences, downward inflection (GP-WM-08), data anchors, confident assertions without hedging. Third-person or "I" with evidence. No qualifiers.

### Step 3: Word-Level Rewrite
Rewrite the content in the target register. For every significant word choice, annotate inline why that specific word was chosen over alternatives.

### Step 4: Code-Switch Injection (Optional)
If the target tone benefits from texture, inject 1-2 register switches within the piece:
- Open casual → teach authoritative → close casual
- Narrate entertaining → prove authoritative → resolve casual
The switch itself creates energy. Monotone register is forgettable.

### Step 5: Formality-Trust Calibration
Apply the Likable Expert principle: informal sentence structure (short, direct) + formal proof (specific data, precise terminology). This is how you sound like a friend who happens to be the world's leading expert.

## Output Specification

**DELIVERABLE**: Fully rewritten content in the target tone plus:
1. **Tone scorecard** showing before/after register metrics
2. **Annotation layer** with 5-10 key word-level choices explained
3. **Code-switch map** showing where register shifts occur (if applied)

## Quality Gate
- [ ] Does every sentence sound like it was written in the target register?
- [ ] Are word choices consistent with the target register's toolkit?
- [ ] Would a reader immediately identify the tone without being told?
- [ ] Does the rewrite preserve the original meaning and content value?
- [ ] If code-switching was applied, are the transitions smooth?
