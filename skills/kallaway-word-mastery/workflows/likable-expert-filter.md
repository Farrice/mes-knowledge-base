# Likable Expert Filter

> **Produces**: Content that makes you sound like the smartest person in the room who you'd also want to grab a beer with

## When to Use
- Content sounds authoritative but cold — reader respects you but doesn't connect
- Content sounds friendly but lightweight — reader likes you but doesn't trust your expertise
- Need to solve the Likable Expert Paradox: authority + affinity simultaneously
- Ghostwritten content that sounds technically correct but emotionally flat

## Input Required
1. **Content to transform** — draft, article, post, script, email
2. **Authority signals available** — stats, credentials, proof points, case studies
3. **Target platform** (affects authority/affinity ratio)

## Execution Protocol

### Step 1: Authority-Affinity Diagnostic
Rate the current content on two axes:

```
LIKABLE EXPERT DIAGNOSTIC
──────────────────────────
Authority Score: [1-10] — Does this make me sound like an expert?
Affinity Score: [1-10] — Does this make me sound like someone you'd want to talk to?
Current Quadrant:
  [High Auth/Low Aff] = Cold Expert → Add warmth, admissions, HTBT
  [Low Auth/High Aff] = Friendly Amateur → Add proof, specificity, declaratives
  [Low Auth/Low Aff] = Invisible → Need full rebuild
  [High Auth/High Aff] = Likable Expert → Minor tune-up
Gap Analysis: [What's specifically missing]
```

### Step 2: Proof-Heavy Framing (GP-WM-12)
For every claim in the content:
1. **Attach specific evidence within 2 sentences** — numbers, examples, case studies
2. **Use ugly numbers**: $4,192 > $4,000 (specific = real; rounded = marketing)
3. **Stack proof types**: personal statistics + third-party data + concrete example
4. **Delete vague claims**: "I grew fast" → "I grew from 2,000 to 47,000 in 3 months"

### Step 3: Admission Line Injection (GP-WM-14)
Insert 1-2 genuine vulnerability moments:

**Pattern**: Admit struggle → Show how you solved it → Solution hits 3x harder

**Guardrails**:
- Must be genuinely felt, not fabricated
- Always paired with resolution (vulnerability without resolution = pity)
- Placed strategically — usually early (builds trust for everything after)
- One per piece, not a confessional

### Step 4: HTBT Energy Check (GP-WM-11)
Audit the *energy state* of the content:
- Does this read like the writer wanted to write it?
- Can the reader sense enthusiasm without the words "I'm excited" appearing?
- Does this feel like an invitation or an obligation?

If HTBT energy is missing, rewrite the opening to inject genuine enthusiasm. The trick: rewrite from the perspective of "I just figured out something really useful and I can't wait to share it with my friend."

### Step 5: Usefulness-First Reframe (GP-WM-13)
Check the content's energy polarity:

**Extraction energy** (taking from reader): "Follow me for more" / "Sign up now" / "Don't miss this"
**Service energy** (giving to reader): "Here's what I wish someone told me" / "This saved me 40 hours" / "You can use this today"

Rewrite any extraction-energy lines with service-energy framing. The reader should feel like you're doing them a favor by sharing, not asking for one.

### Step 6: The Likable Expert Fusion
Final pass combining all four layers:
- **Sentence level**: informal structure (short, direct, conversational) + formal proof (specific data, precise terminology)
- **Paragraph level**: admission vulnerability + proof-backed authority
- **Piece level**: HTBT energy + usefulness-first frame

This is how you sound like a friend who happens to be the world's leading expert.

## Output Specification

**DELIVERABLE**: Fully transformed content plus:
1. **Before/After authority and affinity scores**
2. **Proof audit** — every claim mapped to its evidence
3. **Admission placement** with rationale
4. **Energy polarity check** — extraction → service rewrites flagged

## Quality Gate
- [ ] Authority score ≥ 8?
- [ ] Affinity score ≥ 8?
- [ ] Every claim has specific proof within 2 sentences?
- [ ] At least one genuine admission line with resolution?
- [ ] HTBT energy is palpable — reader feels invited, not lectured?
- [ ] Content passes the "favor test" — giving, not taking?
- [ ] Formal proof + informal delivery = the Likable Expert fusion?
