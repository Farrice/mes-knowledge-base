# Energy Transmutation Engine

> **Produces**: Content that magnetically pulls readers in because the writer RADIATES service energy — the invisible difference between content that converts and content that repels

## When to Use
- Content feels subtly "salesy" even when it's supposed to be educational
- The writer is creating under obligation, not enthusiasm
- Content passes technical quality checks but readers don't engage
- You sense the content is "asking" rather than "giving"
- AI-generated content needs the human energy layer injected
- Launching into a monetization phase and need content that sells without selling

## The Insight

There are exactly two energy polarities in content:

**Extraction energy**: The writer wants something FROM the reader (views, follows, clicks, purchases). The content serves the writer's goals. Even well-disguised, readers sense this within seconds. The prose creates subtle pressure — like talking to someone at a party who keeps looking over your shoulder.

**Service energy**: The writer wants to be USEFUL TO the reader. The content serves the reader's needs. This creates magnetic pull — like talking to someone who makes you feel like the most important person in the room.

**The mechanism**: You cannot edit extraction energy out. You can only transmute it by genuinely shifting the writer's intention. This workflow changes the INTENTION embedded in the prose, not just the words.

## Input Required
1. **Content to transmute** — any draft
2. **What the writer actually wants** — be honest (followers? revenue? authority? all three?)
3. **What the reader actually needs** — the genuine value in this content
4. **The relationship** — is this a first-touch or existing audience?

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: Before executing, run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md` § Decision Framework. Confirm all diagnostic questions are answered.


## Execution Protocol

### Step 1: Energy Polarity Diagnosis
Map every paragraph to its energy polarity:

```
ENERGY POLARITY MAP
───────────────────
Para 1: [Extraction / Service / Neutral] — [Evidence: specific phrases]
Para 2: [Extraction / Service / Neutral] — [Evidence]
...
Overall Polarity: [Extraction-Heavy / Mixed / Service-Heavy]
Favor Test: Does this read like doing a favor or asking for one?
Hidden Agenda Detection: What unspoken goal is leaking through?
```

### Step 2: Identify Extraction Markers
Specific linguistic signatures of extraction energy:

| Marker | Example | Feels Like |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **CTA-first framing** | Opens with "subscribe/follow/buy" or everything points to conversion | "This exists to convert me" |
| **Engagement bait** | "Comment below!" "What do you think?" without genuine curiosity | "They need my engagement, not my opinion" |
| **Manufactured urgency** | "You NEED to know this" / "Don't miss this" | "They're afraid I'll scroll past" |
| **Authority posturing** | Leading with credentials rather than demonstrating expertise | "They're trying to convince me they're qualified" |
| **Hedge-free certainty** | Zero nuance, absolute claims, no admission of complexity | "They're performing confidence" |
| **Value extraction structure** | Free content is a trailer for paid content, not standalone value | "This is an ad disguised as a post" |
| **Metric broadcasting** | "I made $X / grew Y followers" as proof, not as story | "They're flexing, not teaching" |

### Step 3: Service Energy Injection
For each extraction marker, apply the transmutation:

**CTA-first → Value-first**: Restructure so the content delivers COMPLETE VALUE before any ask. The reader should feel served whether or not they take the CTA.

**Engagement bait → Genuine invitation**: Replace "Comment below!" with an observation that NATURALLY invites response. "I've noticed people split into two camps on this — curious where you land."

**Manufactured urgency → Genuine enthusiasm**: The difference: urgency says "you'll lose something." Enthusiasm says "I found something amazing and want to share it." Rewrite urgency as discovery-energy.

**Authority posturing → Authority demonstration**: Replace "I'm an expert in X" with specific, surprising observations that ONLY an expert would make. Show, don't claim.

**Metric flexing → Metric contextualizing**: "I grew to 47K followers" → "At 2,000 followers, I tried X. By 47K, I'd learned Y. Here's the specific thing that changed."

### Step 4: HTBT Energy Gate
Check whether the content was created with Happy-To-Be-There energy:

- Can the reader sense enthusiasm WITHOUT the writer saying "I'm excited"?
- Does the content feel like it was created by choice or by obligation?
- Is there genuine curiosity or discovery energy in the prose?

**If HTBT is missing**: Flag this honestly. No amount of editing fixes missing HTBT. Options:
1. Identify the ONE insight in this piece that genuinely excites the writer — restructure around that
2. Add personal context that reconnects the writer to WHY they care about this topic
3. In extreme cases: recommend shelving the piece and returning when HTBT is authentic

### Step 5: The Favor Test
Final verification. Read the entire piece and answer:

1. **Does this feel like the writer is doing the reader a favor by sharing?** Or asking for one?
2. **Would the writer share this with a friend for free, enthusiastically?** Or only if there was a business reason?
3. **If you stripped the CTA entirely, would this piece still feel complete?** Or does it feel like a trailer?

### Step 6: Energy Coherence Audit
Ensure the energy is consistent from first word to last:

- Opening energy = middle energy = closing energy (no dropping from service to extraction at the CTA)
- If there IS a CTA: it should feel like an EXTENSION of the service, not a pivot to extraction. "If this was useful, here's the deeper version" > "Now buy my course"
- The emotional temperature stays consistent — no sudden shifts from warm to transactional

## Example Output

**Context**: A newsletter paragraph about an upcoming writing course — currently reads like a sales pitch disguised as teaching

**BEFORE (extraction energy):**
> I've spent the last 6 months building the most comprehensive writing course I've ever created. It covers everything from hooks to closing lines — 47 lessons, 12 templates, and 3 live coaching calls. Early bird pricing ends Friday. If you're serious about improving your writing, this is the investment you need to make.

**ENERGY POLARITY MAP:**
```
Sentence 1: Extraction — "I've spent 6 months" = effort flex, centering the writer
Sentence 2: Extraction — feature list = selling, not serving
Sentence 3: Extraction — artificial urgency = pressure tactic
Sentence 4: Extraction — "if you're serious" = guilt leverage
Overall: Heavy Extraction — this is an ad pretending to be a paragraph
Favor Test: FAIL — asking for a purchase, not offering value
Hidden Agenda: Wants course sales, every word serves that goal
```

**EXTRACTION MARKERS:**
- Metric broadcasting: "6 months," "47 lessons, 12 templates, 3 live coaching calls" — flexing volume
- Manufactured urgency: "Early bird pricing ends Friday"
- Authority posturing: "the most comprehensive writing course I've ever created"
- Value extraction structure: Zero standalone value — exists only to sell

**AFTER (service energy):**
> Something I noticed while writing this newsletter: the sentences I wrote fastest were always the weakest. The ones I almost deleted? Those hit hardest. That pattern kept showing up — in posts, in client work, in my own morning journals. The stuff that feels effortless IS effortless. And readers can tell.
>
> I spent six months mapping that gap — why speed ≠ quality, and more importantly, what to do about it. I turned it into a course because some of this needs exercises, not just explanations. If that sounds useful, [it's here]. If it doesn't, the principle still applies: slow down on the sentences that come too easy.

**TRANSMUTATION NOTES:**
- "6 months" reframed from effort-flex to context for *why* it's a course, not evidence of quality
- CTA buried inside standalone insight — the paragraph teaches regardless of click
- Urgency removed entirely — replaced with genuine observation ("some of this needs exercises, not just explanations")
- Favor Test: PASS — the reader walks away with a usable insight even if they never buy
- Energy coherence: Teaching voice start to finish, CTA feels like a natural extension, not a pivot
