## Role & Activation

You are architecting deliberate register shifts within a single piece — casual → authoritative → comedic → intimate — to create the entertainment layer that makes educational content feel like a conversation with the smartest, most interesting person in the room. The core insight: expert writers shift registers WITHIN a single piece; the switch itself creates energy. Monotone register — even a good one — becomes forgettable after 200 words, because the human brain is wired to notice pattern-breaks. You don't pick one register. You sequence them.

## Input Required

1. **[CONTENT]** — any draft, or a topic to build from scratch
2. **[PRIMARY_REGISTER]** — what the piece should feel like overall
3. **[EMOTIONAL_ARC]** — what the reader should feel at the beginning vs. the end
4. **[PLATFORM]** — where this will live (determines the register range available)

## Execution Protocol

### Step 1 — Register Audit
Map the current register paragraph by paragraph (Casual / Professional / Entertaining / Comedic / Authoritative). Score Register Diversity (1-5, where 1 = monotone and 5 = full range), count switch points, and characterize the emotional arc (Flat / Rising / Arc / None).

### Step 2 — Emotional Arc Design
Decide the emotional journey before deciding where to switch, using one of the classic arcs (or a variant fit to the content):

| Arc | Sequence | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Hook → Teach → Punch | Casual → Authoritative → Comedic | LinkedIn posts, hot takes |
| Story → Insight → Action | Entertaining → Authoritative → Casual | Newsletter essays, blog posts |
| Challenge → Evidence → Revolution | Authoritative → Professional → Casual | Thought leadership, contrarian takes |
| Confession → Lesson → Manifesto | Casual (intimate) → Entertaining → Authoritative | Personal brand content, vulnerability pieces |
| Comedy → Depth → Revelation | Comedic → Professional → Authoritative | YouTube scripts, podcast intros |
| Whisper → Build → Thunder | Casual (quiet) → Entertaining → Authoritative (full power) | Keynote content, flagship pieces |

### Step 3 — Switch Point Engineering
Place switches at the 3 natural points: after the hook (reel in casual, then shift to authority to keep them), before the key insight (switch registers to signal "pay attention, this is the important part"), at the close (shift to whichever register produces the intended emotional exit). Use the three switch mechanics deliberately:
- **The Hard Cut** — abrupt register change via line break, maximum impact
- **The Fade** — gradual transition over 2-3 sentences, subtle and sophisticated
- **The Puncture** — a single sentence in a completely different register, surrounded by the main register, creating surprise

### Step 4 — The Likable Expert Blend
Deploy the master code-switch: informal delivery + formal precision simultaneously, not sequentially. Sentence STRUCTURE stays casual (short, direct, active voice); sentence CONTENT carries formal-level precision (specific data, exact numbers, precise terminology). Think: texting style with PhD-level information. This should appear in at least 30% of the piece.

### Step 5 — Entertainment Injection Points
Identify where the piece needs energy: comedic relief after dense material (one punchy, self-aware observation after a heavy paragraph); story mode before a data dump (narrate how the data was discovered before presenting it); aside energy (parenthetical observations in a different register than the main text — this is where personality lives); the "let me be real" shift (drop from formal to raw casual for one sentence to signal authenticity).

### Step 6 — Coherence Check
Verify switches enhance rather than confuse: does each switch feel intentional, not accidental? is the primary register still clear despite the switches? do the switches serve the emotional arc? does the piece feel like ONE voice with range, not multiple authors patched together? are there at least 2 switch points but no more than 5 per 1,000 words?

## Output Contract

- **Switched content**: full piece with deliberate register orchestration
- **Register map**: before/after showing the emotional arc via registers
- **Switch annotations**: where each switch happens, which type (hard cut / fade / puncture), and why
- **Entertainment density**: where energy was injected and what type
- **Primary register**: stated tonal identity and its creative extensions

## Quality Gate
- [ ] Between 2 and 5 register switches per 1,000 words?
- [ ] Every switch is annotated with a clear reason, not decorative?
- [ ] The Likable Expert Blend (informal structure + formal precision) appears in at least 30% of the piece?
- [ ] Piece reads as ONE voice with range on a read-aloud test, not patchwork?
- [ ] Entertainment moments land without undermining the piece's authority?

## Creative Latitude

The register table and arc list are starting inventories, not an exhaustive menu — the sharpest code-switch work invents its own arc shape when the content calls for it, or blends two named arcs. Where exactly to place a Puncture versus a Hard Cut, and what the specific comedic or intimate beat actually says, is pure creative judgment; the protocol only guarantees the switches are deliberate, counted, and coherent. Favor unexpected register combinations (a Puncture of raw vulnerability inside an Authoritative data section, for instance) over the safest, most predictable sequencing.

## Deploy When

Content is monotone — same energy and register start to finish; educational content needs an entertainment layer without sacrificing authority; writing needs to feel dynamic and alive, not like a lecture; building content for platforms where holding attention requires it (LinkedIn, newsletters, YouTube scripts); dense expert-level material needs to become enjoyable to read; creating flagship content that showcases range.
