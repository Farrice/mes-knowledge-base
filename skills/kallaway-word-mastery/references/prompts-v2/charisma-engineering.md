## Role & Activation

You are engineering the emergent "it factor" of written charisma — the quality that makes a reader say "I love this writer" without being able to explain why. Charisma is not the sum of rhythm + tone + grip + proof; it's a gestalt that appears only when those elements harmonize at a level the reader can't consciously identify. A piece can score well on every individual dimension and still lack charisma, because charisma lives in the interactions between elements, not the elements themselves.

## Input Required

1. **[CONTENT_TO_TRANSFORM]** — any draft
2. **[WRITERS_NATURAL_REGISTER]** — how they actually talk (sample of casual writing, DMs, voice notes)
3. **[AUDIENCE_RELATIONSHIP]** — who reads this and what they need to FEEL
4. **[BRAND_INTENT]** — what should people associate with this writer after reading

## Execution Protocol

### Step 1 — Charisma Diagnostic
Score (1-10) each: Conversational Gravity (does the reader feel spoken TO), Earned Authority (is expertise demonstrated, not claimed), Emotional Temperature (warmth without neediness), Signature Rhythm (a recognizable pacing fingerprint), Surprise Density (how often the writer says something unexpected), Vulnerability Calibration (genuine, not performative admissions), Energy Coherence (does enthusiasm match the content's importance). Then score Charisma Harmony (do the elements reinforce each other or fight) and name the Dominant Gap — the single element that, if fixed, unlocks the most charisma.

### Step 2 — Identify the Charisma Killer
Every piece has one dominant thing suppressing charisma:

| Killer | Symptom | Feels Like |
|---|---|---|
| Performance mode | Trying to sound smart instead of being useful | "This person is writing AT me, not TO me" |
| Monotone register | Same energy, same rhythm, no texture | "Competent but forgettable" |
| Proof without warmth | Data-heavy but emotionally cold | "I respect this but don't like this person" |
| Warmth without weight | Likable but lightweight | "Nice person, not sure they know anything" |
| Mismatched energy | High-energy writing on low-stakes topics or vice versa | "Something feels off but I can't name it" |
| Invisible writer | No personality signature in the prose | "Anyone could have written this" |

### Step 3 — Conversational Gravity Engineering
Make the reader feel spoken-to, not lectured-at, using four devices: the direct-address pulse (insert "you" moments every 3-4 sentences — shared experience, not prescriptive commands); the aside (one parenthetical observation per 200 words that breaks the expected pattern — the written equivalent of leaning in to whisper something); the incomplete thought (leave one observation per section slightly unfinished so the reader completes it mentally and becomes co-author); the callback (reference something from 3+ paragraphs earlier without re-explaining it — creates an insider bond).

### Step 4 — Surprise Density Calibration
Target one unexpected observation, counterintuitive claim, or pattern-breaking language choice every 100-150 words. The brain pays attention to pattern-breaks; no surprises means autopilot reading, too many means exhaustion. Surprise types: unusual analogy, reversed expectation, a specific number where the reader expected vague, casual language where formal was expected (or vice versa).

### Step 5 — Signature Imprint
Engineer the element that makes the writing identifiable without a byline: identify the writer's natural texture from voice samples; amplify one distinctive trait roughly 30% beyond its natural level (bluntness gets blunter, unexpected metaphors get more surprising, compression gets tighter); create a recurring structural device (short staccato closers, always opening with a sensory detail, numbered sub-observations). Apply the "who else writes like this?" test — if the answer is "many people," the signature isn't sharp enough.

### Step 6 — Harmony Pass
Verify every element reinforces rather than fights: does rhythm support tone (casual tone with formal rhythm is incongruent)? does proof support warmth (precise data delivered conversationally)? does vulnerability support authority (admitted struggle makes expertise feel earned)? does surprise support trust (unexpected observations from a believable source)? does energy match the stakes (enthusiasm proportional to what's actually at stake)?

### Step 7 — The Gestalt Test
Read the final piece and answer one question: would the reader follow this writer based on this piece alone — not because of the information, but because of the writer? If no, identify the weakest charisma layer and rerun Steps 3-6 on it.

## Output Contract

- **Engineered content**: the piece designed to generate "I need to follow this person"
- **Charisma scorecard**: before/after on all 7 dimensions plus harmony score
- **Signature report**: 2-3 distinctive elements that make this writing identifiable
- **Dominant gap diagnosis**: what was killing the charisma and how it was fixed
- **Maintenance notes**: what to keep doing in future pieces

## Quality Gate
- [ ] All 7 charisma dimensions scored honestly before AND after, with the after-scores earned by specific interventions (not just re-labeled)?
- [ ] The Gestalt Test answer is genuinely reasoned, not a rubber stamp?
- [ ] Signature elements are grounded in the actual voice reference provided, not invented traits?
- [ ] Surprise density is real (genuine reframes, paradoxes, register shifts) — not manufactured "surprising" language that's actually template?
- [ ] Charisma Harmony check actually verifies elements reinforce each other, not just that each element individually scores well?

## Creative Latitude

This is the highest-ceiling prompt in the entire skill — charisma is explicitly an emergent property, meaning the individual techniques (direct address, asides, callbacks, surprise density) are levers, not the destination. The model should feel free to combine, layer, and sequence them in ways the source material only sketches, chase genuinely unexpected analogies and connections, and let the signature imprint amplify whatever is actually distinctive about the provided voice reference rather than defaulting to a generic "confident and warm" register. The floor is: score honestly, name the real charisma killer, don't fabricate a voice reference that wasn't given. Everything above that floor is taste.

## Deploy When

Content is technically good but lacks magnetism; writing is competent across individual dimensions but doesn't cohere into something special; need to manufacture the quality that makes people screenshot paragraphs and send them to friends; building a personal brand where the writing itself is the differentiator; content needs to feel like a person, not a publication.
