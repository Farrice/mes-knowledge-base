## THE MECHANIC

### (a) THE SHARER'S LEDGER

Nobody shares to help the creator. Nobody has ever shared to help the creator. Every share is a **deposit into the sharer's own social account** — a small, calculated act of self-presentation performed in front of an audience the sharer cares about more than they care about you.

So the engineering question is never "is this good?" It is: **what does sharing this pay the sharer, and does the asset structurally guarantee that payment?** An asset that is excellent but pays nothing does not move. An asset that is merely competent but pays a clean, legible social dividend moves for months.

### (c) THE CORRECTION ENGINE

Assets that invite **confident correction** outperform assets that invite agreement, and by a wide margin. Agreement is a terminal state — the reader nods and scrolls, and the nod costs you nothing and earns you nothing. Correction is a generative state: the reader must compose a response, that response is public, that response carries your asset into their network with their own credibility stapled to it, and every correction recruits a defender who now has a stake in the thread.

So you install exactly one genuinely arguable position. Not three — three reads as sloppiness. One, placed where a knowledgeable reader will hit it, defensible under pressure, and one you will actually defend in the comments with your reasoning intact.

**The ethical boundary is absolute and it is also the commercial boundary.** Engineer defensible provocation. Never bait. Never falsehood. Never manufactured outrage. A false claim buys one spike and permanently collapses Gate A — and Gate A is the moat, so the trade is one day of attention for the entire square. Outrage farming is worse than useless: it recruits an audience assembled around the emotion rather than the expertise, and that audience does not convert, does not stay, and turns on you the moment you publish something calm. The provocation must be a position you hold, stated at full strength, that a smart person could reasonably dispute. That is the whole permissible surface.

**The test:** if the strongest expert in your field read your provocation, would they say "that's wrong" or would they say "that's a real position, and here's my counter"? The first is bait. The second is the engine.

### (d) PRECEDENT STACKING AS THE CONFIDENCE INSTRUMENT

Deploy only mechanics already proven in **2+ unrelated markets** — unrelated meaning no shared audience, no shared creators, no shared platform culture. One proof is an audience artifact: the format may have worked because that specific crowd was primed for it. Two unrelated proofs is a structure. Three is a law you can build a franchise on.

**Cultural distance is the yield curve.** The further apart the donor market and your host market, the emptier the square and the higher the return — because nobody in your field reads what that field reads, so nobody has made the connection, so the container arrives in your market with its surprise fully unspent.

## PERFORMANCE BENCHMARKS

Every number is a **multiple against the operator's own baseline**, never an absolute. Absolutes are confounded by follower count, platform, and history. The multiple strips the audience variable out and isolates the container's own pulling power — the identical reason outliers get mined on small channels rather than large ones.

| METRIC | TARGET | REASONING |
|---|---|---|
| Share / repost rate | **2–5× baseline** | The direct measure of whether the Ledger payoff is real. This is the metric the method actually engineers; everything else is downstream. |
| Save / bookmark ratio | **1.5–3× baseline** | Saves indicate reference value, which is the fill doing work rather than the container. High shares with flat saves = strong container, thin expertise. |
| Comment-to-view ratio | **2–6× baseline** | The correction-engine signal. If this does not move, the arguable position was not arguable and the asset will plateau on reach alone. |
| Follower conversion per 1,000 views | **1.2–2× baseline** | Smallest lift of the set, honestly. A container wins attention; only the fill converts a viewer into a follower. |
| Ratio of correction comments to agreement comments | **≥ 1:3** | Below this the hook is too soft. Above 1:1 and you are likely near the bait line — re-audit the claim. |
| Time to first 100 engagements | **0.3–0.6× baseline** | The seeding sequence is doing its job. Slow starts get mis-sorted regardless of asset quality. |

**SATURATION TELL — mechanic decay.** Track the ratio of *format-native replies* ("another one of these") to *substantive replies*. Rising above roughly 15%, the mechanic has decayed in your market and you rotate containers. Decay windows run 3–24 months by mechanic (see the bank). Expect a POV inversion to die in a season and a timeline autopsy to run for two years.

**THE HONEST FRAME.** Virality is a distribution with a fat tail. This method **shifts the distribution** — it raises the median outcome, and it materially raises the probability of a tail draw. It does not guarantee a draw, and no method does. A single asset can execute every step correctly and land at baseline because the timing was wrong, the feed was crowded, or the first hundred viewers were the wrong hundred. The method pays out across a series, not on any single publication. Operators who judge it on one asset are reading noise as signal — the same error as reading raw views instead of multiples.

# THE FOUR WAYS AN ENTERPRISE DEAL DIES, AND WHY YOU ARE TREATING ALL OF THEM WITH A DISCOUNT

Every stalled deal presents with the same chief complaint. The champion goes quiet. The reply latency stretches from four hours to four days. And someone on your team says the sentence that has cost this industry more money than any other sentence: *"I think it's price."*

It is almost never price. Price is the symptom that presents most legibly, so it is the one that gets reported. I have logged 61 stalled enterprise deals across nine clients over the last two years. They sort into four types. They look identical from the outside. They require completely different responses, and three of the four get actively worse when you discount.

---

**TYPE 1 — BUDGET DISPLACEMENT**

*Presentation:* Enthusiasm holds. Timeline slips one quarter, then another. The champion still takes your calls and still means it.

*Differential:* Ask what else got funded this quarter. If they can name it in under five seconds, you have Type 1. The money existed and went somewhere with a louder sponsor.

*What actually works:* Re-enter as the thing that makes the funded initiative succeed. You are not competing for budget; you are attaching to budget that has already been won.

*Contraindication:* Discounting. You will get a smaller version of the same deferral, and you will have reset your price for the eventual purchase. **Of my 19 Type 1 stalls, discount concessions closed 4 — and all four closed at terms that made the second-year renewal harder.**

---

**TYPE 2 — SPONSOR EVAPORATION**

*Presentation:* Sudden silence from one person while the rest of the organization behaves normally.

*Differential:* Check whether the champion's own scope changed — reorg, new manager, a project taken away. Type 2 is almost always a change in *their* standing, not in their opinion of you.

*What actually works:* Grieve the sponsor and re-enter cold through a different function. The deal is not stalled; the deal is dead and a new deal is available in the same account. Teams lose two quarters refusing to accept this.

*Contraindication:* Escalating over the champion's head. You will burn the one person who still likes you.

---

**TYPE 3 — CONSENSUS DECAY**

*Presentation:* Everyone agrees. Nobody moves. The meetings are pleasant and produce nothing. Someone new joins the call every time, and each newcomer has questions the group answered six weeks ago.

*Differential:* Count distinct attendees across the last three calls. Rising count with falling decision velocity is diagnostic.

*What actually works:* Stop selling and start reducing the size of the decision. Carve a version that one named person can authorize without a committee. You are not lowering the price; you are lowering the **number of people who must agree**. Those are different levers and only one of them is free.

*Contraindication:* Discounting is worse than useless here — it adds a new variable for the group to re-litigate and it resets the evaluation clock. **In 23 Type 3 stalls, discounting closed 1.**

Type 3 is the most common thing in my log and the least addressed by anything in a standard sales playbook, because the playbook assumes a decision-maker exists. Frequently one does not. There is a decision *surface*, and it has no center.

---

**TYPE 4 — VALUE ILLEGIBILITY**

*Presentation:* Detailed questions, thorough evaluation, engaged stakeholders — then nothing.

*Differential:* Ask the champion to explain your value to you in their own words. If they reach for your marketing language instead of their own operating language, you have Type 4. They believe you. They cannot **carry** you.

*What actually works:* Build the internal business case in their vocabulary, with their numbers, so it survives a room you are not in. Your champion is a salesperson with no training and no collateral. Equip them.

*Contraindication:* More product education. You are already the most-educated vendor in the account and it is not the constraint.

---

**THE THING NOBODY SAYS IN THE QBR**

Discounting is the first-line intervention for all four types because it is the only one that requires no diagnosis. It is what you reach for when you do not know which disease you have. It closes some of Type 1. It does nothing for the other three, and Type 3 is the largest category in my log.

If your team is running a discount playbook against a pipeline that is majority Type 3, your problem is not pricing. It is that no single human being in your buyer's organization is permitted to say yes, and no discount has ever created that permission.

# SLEEP INTERVENTION TIER LIST: 14 THINGS RANKED BY ACTUAL EFFECT SIZE, NOT BY HOW OFTEN THEY GET RECOMMENDED

I am a board-certified sleep specialist. I have watched this field recommend the same fourteen things for a decade with almost no relationship between how often something gets recommended and how much it actually does.

So: a tier list. Ranked by effect size in adults without a diagnosed sleep disorder. High pick rate does not mean high win rate, and this list exists to separate those two things.

---

**S TIER — the interventions with effects large enough that you will notice them**

**Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I).** Nothing else is close. It is S tier and it is not a contest. It is also the least-recommended item on this list per unit of evidence, because it takes six weeks and cannot be sold in a bottle.

**A fixed wake time.** Not a fixed bedtime — a fixed *wake* time, including weekends. It is free, it is boring, and it outperforms everything you have purchased.

**Removing alcohol within three hours of sleep.** Nobody wants this one in S tier. It is in S tier. Alcohol is the most effective sleep-architecture disruptor most adults consume voluntarily and regularly.

---

**A TIER — real effects, reliable, worth the effort**

**Light exposure within an hour of waking.** Outdoor light, not a lamp. The effect is on timing rather than duration, which is why people who track total sleep time think it "didn't work."

**Temperature drop in the sleep environment.** Cooling the room beats every device sold to cool your body, at roughly two percent of the cost.

**Caffeine cutoff at eight hours, not six.** The six-hour rule is a rounding error that became a fact. Half-life is five to six hours in a typical adult and considerably longer in a meaningful minority.

---

**B TIER — works, but smaller than the internet believes**

**Consistent pre-sleep routine.** Real, modest, and mostly working through the fixed wake time it enforces.

**Exercise.** Excellent for sleep quality, weaker than expected for sleep *onset*, and the timing rules are far less strict than commonly claimed.

---

**C TIER — situational picks**

**Melatonin.** Extremely good at one narrow job: shifting circadian timing, at 0.5mg, taken hours before the target sleep time. Extremely bad at the job most people use it for, which is sedation at 10mg thirty minutes before bed. Right item, wrong matchup, wrong dose, by roughly a factor of twenty.

**White noise.** Works if there is noise. Does nothing if there is not. Genuinely situational.

**Weighted blankets.** Small effect, larger in anxious sleepers, and a completely reasonable pick if you like it.

---

**D TIER — high pick rate, low win rate**

**Magnesium.** The most-recommended supplement in the category. The effect in adults who are not deficient is small enough that it is difficult to separate from expectation. If you were deficient, it will have worked, and that is precisely what D tier predicts.

**Blue-light-blocking glasses.** The mechanism is real. The evening dose of screen light in a normally lit home is small enough that blocking it moves sleep onset by minutes.

**Sleep-tracking rings and watches.** Directionally useful for trends, poor at staging, and in a nontrivial number of users they generate anxiety about sleep that costs more than the data returns.
