## THE MECHANIC

### (a) THE CONTAINER IS THE COMPARISON

Every category runs one container.

All consultancies sell the monthly retainer. All courses sell lifetime access with a 30-day refund. All agencies sell a scope of work against a rate card. All SaaS sells three tiers with an enterprise "contact us." The contents differ wildly — the container never moves.

That is the entire reason buyers compare on price. **Price comparison requires structural comparability, and the shared container supplies it.** When two things are the same shape, the only remaining variable is the number on them. Procurement is not being difficult; it is doing the only arithmetic the container permits.

So the move is not a better offer inside the container. It is a **bent container**. Import an acquisition structure from a culturally distant industry — allocation from luxury retail, staged release from clinical trials, risk transfer from insurance, earned tier from aviation loyalty, assessment-before-admission from surgical practice — and price comparison becomes structurally impossible, because there is nothing structurally comparable. This is **competitive-set escape** executed at the moment of payment rather than the moment of attention. Cultural distance remains the yield curve: the further the donor sits from the buyer's working life, the emptier the square and the more defensible the position.

### (c) THE NAMED MECHANISM RULE

An offer without a proprietary named mechanism is a commodity with a price tag on it. The name is what makes the buyer's brain file the offer as a *thing* rather than as an instance of a category — and things do not have market rates.

Three constraints, non-negotiable:

1. **The mechanism must describe a real process the operator actually runs**, step by step, on every engagement.
2. **The name must be legible without a paragraph of explanation** — legibility is the attraction engine; obscurity reads as confusion, not novelty.
3. **Naming a process you do not run collapses Gate A.** The named mechanism is the single highest-exposure element of an offer, because the buyer will experience the delivery and audit the name against it. A fake mechanism does not fail at the sale. It fails at month three, in front of a reference customer.

### (d) THE RISK-REVERSAL LADDER

| Rank | Instrument | Strength to buyer | Operator exposure | Deploy when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Performance bond** — stated sum forfeits on a missed standard | Maximum | Severe, capital at risk | Delivery is fully within operator control and historically consistent |
| 2 | **Outcome escrow** — payment releases on verified result | Very high | High, cash-flow drag | Result is objectively measurable by both parties |
| 3 | **Milestone gate with walk-away** — either party exits at a defined checkpoint, work delivered to date is retained | High | Moderate, revenue variance | Long engagements where scope risk is mutual |
| 4 | **Rework guarantee** — defined defect standard remediated at no charge, unlimited | High | Moderate, labor only | Craft work with an objective quality bar |
| 5 | **Fee-at-risk** — a stated percentage of fee contingent on a controllable output metric | Moderate-high | Bounded | Outputs are controllable; outcomes are not |
| 6 | **Assessment credit** — paid diagnostic credits fully against the engagement | Moderate | Low | Entry friction is the binding constraint |
| 7 | **Pro-rata exit** — cancel at any gate, refund of unconsumed period | Moderate | Low | Retainers, memberships |
| 8 | **Satisfaction refund window** | Low (universal, therefore invisible) | Low | Default only — never a differentiator |

**The honest note.** A guarantee's strength is bounded by what the operator can actually control. Guaranteeing an outcome that depends on client behavior — revenue, weight loss, hiring, adoption — is a liability, not a lever: it converts every disappointed client into a creditor and forces the operator to litigate the client's own effort. Guarantee **your output, your standard, your process, your responsiveness, your rework**. Where a result genuinely depends on the client, write the dependency into the terms in plain language and guarantee the operator's half of it. That reads as more credible than an unbounded promise, because sophisticated buyers know unbounded promises are unenforceable theater.

### (e) THE PRICE LOGIC FRAMEWORK

Price is set against one of three anchors — never against hours, never against competitor rates. Hours anchor to labor markets. Competitor rates anchor to the container you just escaped.

| Anchor | Formula | Use when | Copy that carries it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost of the problem persisting** | Quantified monthly bleed × realistic months of continued bleed, priced at a defensible fraction | The pain is measurable in money | "This costs less than one more quarter of the thing you already budgeted around." |
| **The alternative structure** | Cost of the in-house hire, the platform, or the incumbent path — fully loaded | The buyer's real alternative is a build or a hire | "A senior hire is $210k loaded, at four months to productive, with a hiring risk you own." |
| **Capacity scarcity** | Total revenue required ÷ seats the operator can genuinely serve at standard | Capacity is truly and verifiably constrained | "Four seats. The price is what four seats have to be worth." |

Always set a **walk-away floor** — the number below which the operator declines the work — and state it internally before any negotiation. An offer without a floor is a discount waiting for a buyer patient enough to find it.

## DEPLOYMENT PROTOCOL

**1 — STATE THE DEMAND THESIS AND BAND ITS EVIDENCE.** Write the thesis in one sentence, then label the evidence: **Hard** (money already spent on the problem), **Soft** (stated intent, survey, expressed interest), or **Assumed** (inference). Price and guarantee strength scale down as evidence weakens. Never architect an aggressive risk instrument on Assumed evidence.

**2 — NAME THE CATEGORY'S DEFAULT CONTAINER, HONESTLY.** State the structure every competitor uses, verbatim, and name who uses it. Then state the comparison it forces the buyer into. This is the square being vacated.

**3 — AUDIT REAL DELIVERY CAPABILITY (GATE A).** Score 1–5: can the operator fill this container with substance the category cannot fake? List the delivery steps actually performed today. **Below 4 — stop and select a lighter structure.** Never architect an offer the operator cannot fulfil.

**4 — RANK DONOR STRUCTURES BY CULTURAL DISTANCE AND TRUST REQUIREMENT.** Query the Structure Bank. Nominate three, plotting each on distance (yield) against trust required (cost). Select one. State the rationale in a sentence. Check **precedent stacking** — a structure proven in two or more unrelated industries is structural, not topical.

**5 — BEND IT.** Reduce the donor to its bracketed skeleton with zero industry words, then rebuild it in the host market. Carry the bend to **Layer 3 minimum** — the donor's *lexicon* enters the contract and the copy (seats, allocation, gates, editions, standby, admission). Layer 1 packaging alone decays in weeks. Reach Layer 4 where the donor's worldview can govern how the whole business operates.

**6 — NAME THE MECHANISM FROM THE REAL PROCESS.** Extract the operator's actual sequence, compress it to three to five stages, and name it. Verify Gate A against the name.

**7 — BUILD THE STRUCTURE AND TIERS.** Every tier gets: price, duration, who delivers, explicit deliverables, and **explicit boundaries** — what is not included, stated plainly. Boundaries prevent scope bleed and read as confidence.

**8 — SELECT THE RISK-REVERSAL INSTRUMENT AND STATE ITS EXPOSURE.** Choose from the ladder at the highest rung the operator's control genuinely supports. Write the worst-case cost in dollars and frequency. If the exposure is unsurvivable at 10% claim rate, drop a rung.

**9 — SET THE PRICE WITH ITS LOGIC AND THE WALK-AWAY FLOOR.** Name the anchor, show the arithmetic, state the floor.

**10 — WRITE QUALIFICATION CRITERIA, INCLUDING WHO IS REFUSED AND WHY.** Refusal is a selling instrument and a delivery safeguard. Name the disqualifying conditions explicitly.

**11 — WRITE THE CUSTOMER-FACING COPY.** Shippable prose. Real names, real numbers, real terms. Placeholders are failure.

**12 — RUN THE FULFILMENT STRESS TEST.** State what breaks at 3× volume — which person, which step, which promise — and either cap the offer or re-engineer the step.

**13 — RUN THE ETHICS AUDIT.** Every promise: controllable, true, and honorable? Every scarcity claim: real? Strike anything that fails.

**14 — STATE THE COMPETITIVE-SET ESCAPE.** Name who the operator no longer competes with, the comparison procurement can no longer force, and the new set the buyer sorts them into.

## PERFORMANCE BENCHMARKS

All targets are **multiples against the operator's own baseline** — the only honest unit, because absolute rates are confounded by market, price point, and traffic quality. No studies, no third-party claims, no revenue guarantees.

| Metric | Target range | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Close rate vs. own baseline** | 1.2×–2.0× | Qualification refuses poor fits, so fewer but better opportunities convert higher; total closes may fall while rate rises |
| **Average contract value shift** | 1.3×–3.0× | A non-comparable container removes the ceiling the category's rate card imposed |
| **Price-objection frequency** | 0.3×–0.6× of baseline | The dominant effect: objections migrate from "too expensive" to "am I eligible" |
| **Proposal-to-decision cycle** | 0.6×–1.4× | Gated structures shorten decisions; novel structures lengthen them where procurement has no precedent — expect both, by buyer type |
| **Refund / claim rate against the instrument** | Under 8%, monitored monthly | Above 8% signals a promise outside operator control or an unqualified pipeline; re-run steps 8 and 10 |

Structural bends compound over two to three sales cycles, not one. Measure at cycle three.

## EXAMPLE OUTPUT 1: Enterprise data-migration practice — clinical-trial staged-milestone structure

**Context:** 22-person practice migrating legacy ERP and data warehouses for $300M–$3B manufacturers and distributors. Current offer: fixed-bid SOW, $600k–$1.4M, 9–14 months. Losing 60% of deals to Big Four bids that come in lower and then change-order. Buyer: CIO, with CFO signature and a procurement gate.

**THE ACTUAL DELIVERABLE:**

**DEMAND THESIS (evidence: Hard).** Buyers are not afraid of the price. They are afraid of the *irreversibility* — of being nine months and $800k into a migration that cannot be stopped and cannot be finished. Four of the last six lost deals cited a prior failed migration.

**DEFAULT CONTAINER NAMED.** The fixed-bid SOW with a change-order clause. Accenture, Deloitte, and every regional SI use it identically. It forces one comparison: total number, on page one. Procurement then wins on that number, and the change orders arrive later.

**GATE A: 5/5.** The practice already runs a formal readiness assessment, a data-quality scorecard, and a parallel-run validation before cutover. The stages exist. They are simply invisible in the contract.

**DONOR SELECTED.** Clinical trial phasing — Phase I/II/III with pre-registered endpoints and a data safety monitoring board empowered to halt. Cultural distance: maximum. No CIO meets trial protocol at work; every CIO understands it from public life. Precedent stacking: staged-gate release is independently proven in pharmaceutical development and in construction draw schedules — two unrelated industries. Structural, not topical.

**SKELETON.** `Work proceeds in [phases]. Each [phase] pre-registers [an exit criterion] agreed before it begins. Funds release only when [the criterion] is met. [Either party] may halt at any gate, and [everything produced to that point] transfers regardless.`

### THE PROTOCOL — offer copy, ship-ready

> **We stopped selling migrations. We run them as a protocol.**
>
> You have been sold a nine-month migration before. Someone signed a fixed bid, the discovery came back worse than the sales deck, and by month five the only options were "spend more" or "explain to the board why we stopped."
>
> That is not a vendor problem. That is a contract-shape problem. A single irreversible bid on a system nobody has fully inventoried is a bet, and you are the only one holding it.
>
> So we borrowed the structure from the only industry that spends a decade on projects that can fail: pharmaceutical trials. Pre-registered endpoints. Staged funding. A stop rule that either side can pull.

**THE MECHANISM: THE FOUR-GATE MIGRATION PROTOCOL™** — Registration, Parallel Proof, Controlled Cutover, Sustained Run. Every engagement runs all four. We have run it 31 times.

| Phase | Price | Duration | Pre-registered exit criterion | Included | Not included |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0 — Registration** | $85,000 | 5 weeks | Complete source inventory, field-level data-quality scorecard, a written Migration Protocol with all downstream gates and a fixed price for Phases 1–3 | 2 architects, 1 data engineer; full inventory; risk register; the Protocol document | Any writes to production; any target-system build |
| **Phase 1 — Parallel Proof** | $240,000 | 10 weeks | 100% of Tier-1 objects reconciled to source within tolerance across two consecutive month-end closes | Target build, transformation logic, reconciliation harness, two closes run in parallel | End-user training; custom reporting rebuild |
| **Phase 2 — Controlled Cutover** | $310,000 | 8 weeks | Zero Sev-1 defects open for 14 consecutive days post-cutover; finance sign-off on one full close in target | Cutover runbook, rehearsal, live cutover, 14-day hypercare, defect triage | Third-party licence costs; org change management |
| **Phase 3 — Sustained Run** | $22,000/month, 6-month minimum | 6–18 months | Rolling — defect rate under agreed threshold, reviewed quarterly | Named engineer, 4-hour Sev-1 response, quarterly data-health review, backlog burn-down | New module implementations (quoted separately) |

**THE STOP RULE (terms language, verbatim from the MSA).**
*"At each Gate, either party may terminate this engagement by written notice within ten (10) business days of Gate review. On termination at a Gate: (a) Client owes only the fees for Phases commenced and completed; (b) no termination fee, cancellation charge, or wind-down cost applies; (c) Provider transfers to Client all work product created to that date — inventory, scorecards, transformation code, reconciliation harness, runbooks, and documentation — under a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free licence; (d) Provider will deliver a written Gate Report stating, without qualification, whether the exit criterion was met and, if not, the contributing factors including those attributable to Provider."*

**THE PHASE 1 REWORK GUARANTEE.** *"Should any Tier-1 object fail reconciliation tolerance at Gate 1 due to Provider transformation logic, Provider shall remediate at no charge and shall not invoice Phase 2 until reconciliation passes. This guarantee covers defects in Provider-authored logic. It does not cover source-system data that Client's own scorecard, delivered at Gate 0 and accepted in writing, identified as out of tolerance."*

**EXPOSURE STATED.** Worst case per engagement: roughly six engineer-weeks of unbilled remediation, approximately $54,000. Historic incidence across 31 runs: 3. Survivable at a 10% claim rate.

**PRICE LOGIC.** Anchor: the alternative structure. A stalled migration costs this buyer a fully-loaded internal team of six at roughly $1.1M annually plus the deferred ERP licence savings. Phase 0 at $85k prices at under 8% of one year of the alternative. **Walk-away floor: $68,000 for Phase 0, and we do not run Phases 1–3 without Phase 0 under any circumstance.**

**QUALIFICATION.** Admitted: manufacturers or distributors, $300M+ revenue, an executive sponsor at CIO level or above, and a finance owner named before Gate 0. **Refused, and told why:** (1) anyone requesting a fixed all-in price before Registration — we will not price what nobody has inventoried, and a firm that will is pricing the change orders instead; (2) anyone whose cutover date is set by a board commitment inside 6 months — the Protocol cannot compress below its gates and we will not pretend otherwise; (3) anyone unwilling to name a finance owner, because Gate 2 requires a close sign-off that only finance can give.

**FULFILMENT STRESS TEST.** At 3× volume the constraint is the reconciliation harness build in Phase 1 — currently authored by two senior engineers. Capped at 6 concurrent Phase 1 engagements until the harness is productized; Phase 0 scales freely.

**ETHICS AUDIT.** No outcome is promised. Every guarantee is bounded to Provider-authored logic. The stop rule is genuinely bilateral and costless. Gate Reports disclose Provider-attributable factors. No scarcity claim is made.

**COMPETITIVE-SET ESCAPE.** They no longer compete with SI fixed bids — the comparison procurement is trained to win by ranking one number. An $85,000 Registration is not comparable to a $900,000 SOW; the objects are different shapes. The new comparison set is staged capital deployment, which the CFO already understands and already approves. The question moves from *"why are you more expensive"* to *"what happens at Gate 1"* — and that question has no discount in it.

## EXAMPLE OUTPUT 2: Photography education business — apprenticeship-cohort-with-graduation structure

**Context:** Working editorial and wedding photographer, 14 years shooting, 40k Instagram following. Current offer: a $497 lifetime-access course on lighting, 30-day refund, roughly 60 sales a year, heavy refund pressure, near-zero completion. Audience: semi-pro photographers, 2–6 years in, charging $1,500–$3,000 a wedding and stuck there.

**THE ACTUAL DELIVERABLE:**

**DEMAND THESIS (evidence: Hard).** These photographers are not short of information — they own four courses already. They are short of **a credential and a critic**. Nobody has ever told them, with authority, that their work is or is not good enough. Evidence: 41 of 60 buyers last year had bought two or more competing courses.

**DEFAULT CONTAINER NAMED.** Lifetime access, video modules, private Facebook group, 30-day money-back guarantee. Every photography educator on the internet ships exactly this. It forces one comparison: hours of video per dollar. That comparison is unwinnable and it is why the price ceiling in this category is $997.

**GATE A: 5/5.** He already critiques work — informally, in DMs, constantly, and it is the only thing anyone thanks him for. The assessment labor is real and already happening for free.

**DONOR SELECTED.** The apprenticeship with graduation — trades and conservatoires. Cultural distance: high; no online course can be failed. Precedent stacking: proven independently in the building trades and in classical music conservatoires. Structural.

**SKELETON.** `Entry is [by portfolio]. Progression is [by demonstrated work, assessed]. Some do not [graduate]. The credential means something precisely because [it can be withheld].`

### THE ATELIER — offer copy, ship-ready

> **Most of you will not graduate. That is the point.**
>
> You own four lighting courses. You finished none of them. That is not a discipline problem — nobody ever checked, and a thing nobody checks is not a course, it is a video library.
>
> The Atelier admits sixteen photographers twice a year. You submit twelve images. I look at all of them personally. Roughly one in three gets in. Over nine months you produce four assessed bodies of work, and at the end I either sign your portfolio or I do not.
>
> Last cohort, eleven of sixteen were signed. I am not going to pretend that number is higher than it is. If it were sixteen out of sixteen, the signature would be worth nothing, and so would yours.

**THE MECHANISM: THE FOUR-BRIEF PROGRESSION™** — Portfolio Entry, Four Assessed Briefs (Available Light / Constructed Light / The Difficult Subject / The Personal Body of Work), Public Review, Signature. Each brief is submitted, critiqued on the record, and either passed or returned for a single resubmission.

| Tier | Price | Duration | Included | Boundaries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Portfolio Review** (entry, open to all) | $180 | 2 weeks | 12 images reviewed personally; a 20-minute recorded critique; a written verdict — *Ready*, *Not Yet with three named gaps*, or *Wrong Fit* | Not a coaching call; no follow-up questions included; does not guarantee admission |
| **The Atelier** (admission required) | $6,400, or 9 × $780 | 9 months, 16 seats | Four assessed briefs with written critique on each; one resubmission per brief; 9 monthly live critiques (max 16 people); one 1:1 per brief; the technical library; Signature on completion | Not business or pricing coaching; no unlimited feedback; no seat transfer |
| **The Signed Roster** (graduates only) | $95/month | Rolling | Listing on the public Signed Roster with referral routing; two open critiques a month; first admission to any new brief; alumni rate on future cohorts | Not available to non-graduates at any price |

**THE COMPLETION STANDARD (terms language, verbatim).**
*"Provider guarantees the following, without qualification: every brief submitted by the stated deadline receives written critique of no fewer than 400 words within 10 days; every participant receives four 1:1 sessions of 45 minutes; every live critique is held or rescheduled within 14 days. Should Provider fail any of these standards on two or more occasions in a cohort, the participant may withdraw and receive a full refund of all fees paid, and retains all materials. Provider does not guarantee graduation, Signature, or any commercial outcome including bookings, rates, or income. Signature is awarded on assessed merit and may be withheld."*

**THE ADMISSION CREDIT.** The $180 Portfolio Review credits in full against Atelier tuition for the next two cohorts. Declined applicants keep the recorded critique and the written verdict, and may reapply once per cohort at no further charge.

**EXPOSURE STATED.** Worst case: full refund of one cohort seat, $6,400, triggered only by his own missed service standards — entirely within his control. Historic incidence: zero. There is no outcome exposure because no outcome is promised.

**PRICE LOGIC.** Anchor: capacity scarcity. Four briefs × 16 participants × roughly 2.5 hours of assessment each, plus 9 live critiques and 64 one-to-ones, is approximately 260 hours per cohort — two cohorts is a substantial fraction of his working year. At $6,400 × 16 × 2 cohorts, the program returns $204,800 for capacity he genuinely cannot duplicate. **Walk-away floor: $5,200, offered only as the alumni rate. No cohort discounting, ever — a discount to one seat is a tax on the other fifteen.**

**QUALIFICATION.** Admitted: photographers with 2+ years paid work, a shootable camera and one light, and 6–8 hours a week available. **Refused, and told why:** (1) complete beginners — the briefs assume you can already expose and compose, and admitting you would mean failing you; (2) anyone who asks whether the briefs are optional — they are the program, the videos are the appendix; (3) anyone seeking business and pricing coaching, which this is not and which I will name in the verdict rather than take your money for.

**FULFILMENT STRESS TEST.** At 3× demand the constraint is critique hours, which are irreducibly his. Response: cohorts stay at 16 and cadence stays at two per year. Excess demand routes to Portfolio Review, which scales to roughly 25 per month, and to the waitlist. Capacity is never expanded by shortening critiques — that is the product.

**ETHICS AUDIT.** No income promise. No booking promise. The graduation rate is published honestly, including the failures. Sixteen seats is a real constraint derived from real assessment hours, not a countdown. Declined applicants receive full value for the $180 and a free reapplication.

**COMPETITIVE-SET ESCAPE.** He no longer competes with photography courses — a comparison won by whoever ships the most video hours for $497. A nine-month assessed apprenticeship that can be failed is not rankable against lifetime access with a 30-day refund; the objects are not the same kind of thing.
