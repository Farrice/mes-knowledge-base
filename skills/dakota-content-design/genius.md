# Dakota (Thief of Boredom) — Genius Context

Faith-based creative business coach and Instagram creator (@thiefofboredom, ~250K followers). Grew ~70K followers in 2025 posting only graphic carousels — 20M views in 90 days, 11M views and 54K followers in February alone, a 4.6M-view carousel on day three of the experiment. Every pattern below is lifted from his own walkthroughs of the system.

## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These patterns are intuition primitives, not a checklist to stamp onto a caption. Absorb them, then build originally. The test: would Dakota recognize this as a carousel he'd actually post — or as someone reciting "carousel-growth-strategy" vocabulary at a whiteboard? If it's the second, rebuild.

Specifically:
- Do NOT label slides "here's the hook," "here's the second-chance slide," "here's the revelational beat." Execute the move; never announce the machinery. Dakota's own framing: "it's not about having a ton of information on your carousels... it's more about how you present that information in a way that is easily digestible" — the presentation IS the invisibility.
- Do NOT reach for a polished, agency-grade sentence when a rougher, more specific one would actually get shared. Dakota's highest performers read plain and slightly rough — "it's harder to be silent before God than to perform for him" — not copywriting. Polish is the tell that breaks the found-not-manufactured feel his best slides carry.
- His texture is spoken and self-deprecating, not academic — on camera he calls his own arithmetic "super hard math" and admits "I'm not coherent enough to be doing math this quickly." Keep any voice-matched caption conversational, never consultant-clean.
- Never write a slide that needs the hook or prompt to make sense. That's the actual test for revelational-vs-informative, and generic AI carousel output usually fails it by defaulting to a numbered listicle because that's the easier structure to generate.
- Never let output density masquerade as value. Dakota's own standard is presentation, not information load — a slide that says less but stands alone beats a slide that says more but needs the deck.

## Genius Patterns

### Pattern: Carousel-Over-Reels Arbitrage
Reels get "tested" on a small follower slice before wider distribution; carousels go to your actual followers, get pushed to the Reels page when they perform (reels benefit without making a reel), and hit the Explore page more often. They are also low-impact content — no audio, self-paced reading — so they collect far more shares and saves per view.
**Execute**: Default growth content to carousels/graphic posts. Reserve reels for what genuinely needs motion. Compare formats on results-per-production-hour, not raw views.
**Success Metric**: Dakota: 45-minute carousel → 4.6M views vs full-day reel → 2.5M. No graphic post under 100K views in 5-6 months.

### Pattern: Second-Chance Slide Design
If a follower scrolls past slide 1 without engaging, Instagram re-serves the post showing slide 2. A reel scrolled past is dead; a carousel gets another at-bat. Most creators design slide 2 as a continuation that makes no sense cold.
**Execute**: Design slides 2 (and ideally 3) to function as independent cold hooks — a standalone line or re-hook that works with zero context from slide 1.
**Success Metric**: Multiple slides of one post appearing as entry points in analytics; carousel impressions exceeding follower-feed reach.

### Pattern: Revelational vs Informative Split
Two carousel species. Informative = cohesive listicle ("5 things...", "if I started over...") — builds authority, rarely gets story-shared because tip #4 means nothing alone. Revelational = each slide is a complete standalone thought that can be shared as its own post — every slide is an independent chance at virality.
**Execute**: For growth posts, write each slide so it needs zero context from the hook to land ("it's harder to be silent before God than to perform for him"). Use informative carousels deliberately for authority/nurture, not reach.
**Success Metric**: Revelational posts drive the follower spikes and story-shares; a 10-slide post = 10 share opportunities instead of 1.

### Pattern: "How I" Beats "How To"
Instructional hooks are preachy — people are told what to do all day and don't do it. Flipping to personal narrative ("how I lost 15 pounds in 3 weeks", "realizations I made following Jesus in my 20s") creates a hero the reader can relate to and borrow from.
**Execute**: Rewrite every "how to" hook as "how I" / "what I learned" / "things nobody told me". Pair the personal hook with a photo of a person — ideally your own face — on slide 1, because people engage with faces.
**Success Metric**: Personal-narrative hooks are Dakota's highest-view posts ("realizations I made following Jesus in my 20s" among his best hooks).

### Pattern: Shares-and-Saves North Star
Views and likes mislead — Dakota has had high-view posts do "almost nothing" for growth. A share says "someone else needs this"; a save says "I need to come back to this." Story-shares are the real engine: one follower with 1,000 followers sharing a slide to their story exposes you to 1,000 people you couldn't reach — and a revelational carousel can occupy multiple story slots at once.
**Execute**: Judge every draft slide with "would someone put THIS on their story?" Track shares and saves as primary KPIs; treat views/likes as secondary noise.
**Success Metric**: Benchmark ratios like Dakota's breakout post: 69K shares / 83K saves on 4.6M views; shares exceeding saves signals peak relatability.

### Pattern: Test-Verify-Scale Loop
Never bet the account on a guess. Post one test in the new format. If engagement clears your bar, post again the next day (the "verify"). If the verify grows, commit daily and don't touch the formula. Dakota: Feb 9 test (133K views, 893 shares, 2K saves) → Feb 10 verify (3x everything) → Feb 11 (4.6M views, 23K followers).
**Execute**: Define "good enough" BEFORE testing (Dakota's bar: strong share/save counts and ~1 like per 10 views). One test, one verify, then scale or kill. When something works, keep making pancakes — don't become a French restaurant (his Wendy's Diner rule; same reason Mr. Beast doesn't pivot to vlogs).
**Success Metric**: Format decisions made in 48-72 hours on real data; no premature pivots away from a working formula.

### Pattern: Film Aesthetic Treatment Stack
A fixed photo treatment makes any Pinterest image or iPhone selfie look branded and editorial: box blur (radius 2 for selfies, 4 for sourced images) → noise filter at 15% → exposure down ~1.2 so text pops → brightness pulled down (tames bright zones) → black & white conversion → paper texture overlay applied LAST. The image complements the words; the words are the focal point.
**Execute**: Apply the full stack to every image, in order, textures last (so alt-drag layer copying doesn't grab the texture). Keep everything B&W so contrast doesn't jump between slides and break the "set" feeling.
**Success Metric**: A recognizable visual signature — followers identify the post as yours before reading a word; the 10-slide set reads as one cohesive object.

### Pattern: Minimal Text, Grid Discipline
Virality is not information density — it's presentation that is "easily digestible, easily consumable, easily shareable." Canvas 2160×2700 (4:5 at high res). Grid overlay on; text sits 5 boxes down (or up) and 3 boxes in. Only two layouts (top-left + bottom-left, or centered-left). Type: kerning −80 to −100, line spacing ~18, all lowercase with capitalized "I" — customized enough that a stock font stops looking stock. Break the placement rule only when the photo demands it (put text in the open space).
**Execute**: Lock one canvas, one grid rule, two layouts, one type spec. Vary only words and image per slide. Slide in a single accent-font word for emphasis when a line needs it.
**Success Metric**: Design decisions per slide approach zero; the constraint set is what makes output "not something designed in Canva."

### Pattern: Pinterest Vibe-Mining + Artboard Assembly Line
Images come from Pinterest, not stock sites: click images matching your mood until the algorithm feeds you an endless supply of the same vibe, then copy → paste directly into Photoshop (no download step). Production is one artboard template — duplicate the artboard (option/alt-click), swap words and image, treatment stack persists. Content-aware fill (M → select → fill) rescues busy images. Save a lean template file; big multi-slide files eat RAM, so periodically restart from the template.
**Execute**: Maintain a trained Pinterest feed per brand vibe. Build one master artboard with images/adjustments/text/textures in separate folders; duplicate for each slide. Ten slides in under 45 minutes.
**Success Metric**: Sub-45-minute full carousel; "second design made in a couple of seconds"; daily posting is sustainable with no burnout.

## Anti-Patterns (What Breaks the System)

- **Never trust reels to reach your own followers** — Dakota calls the algorithm's pre-test "just stupid": "it kind of does this little test out on a few of your followers to see if they'll engage before Instagram decides to send it to more of your followers, which I think is just stupid" (source: YouTube transcript "everything I know about creating viral carousels in 29 minutes," https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kzu-QSaskK4, captured 2025-12-16).
- **Never open with an instructional "how to" hook** — "everyone's used to hearing how to, how to, how to"; his fix is to "flip the script and say how I, how I lost 10 pounds, how I lost 15 pounds in three weeks" (source: same transcript, captured 2025-12-16).
- **Never bury the payoff inside a numbered tip** — "if it's like five things you should do and you're like tip number four do this, no one's really going to share just tip number four" (source: same transcript, captured 2025-12-16).
- **Never optimize for views or likes as the success signal** — "I've had posts get a lot a lot a lot of views and do almost nothing for me"; shares and saves "indicate something to us much different than views or likes" (source: YouTube transcript "what I learned from 20 million views on Instagram in 90 days," https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7iz-3UCJrY, captured 2025-12-16).
- **Never let an unfollow wave or a single flat post trigger a format pivot** — "I've got 24,000 people who have unfollowed me, but that's a net growth of 8,000 followers... If you unfollow me, cool. Don't care" (source: same transcript, captured 2025-12-16).
- **Never abandon a working format to chase novelty** — the Wendy's Diner rule: "it doesn't mean stop making pancakes all together and go try to be a French restaurant. That's stupid and you're going to fail" (source: same transcript, captured 2025-12-16).
- **Never sell prints or merch made from Pinterest-sourced images** — "I don't own the rights to the photos... no, I will not make prints out of these these graphics that I make" (source: same transcript, captured 2025-12-16).

## Hidden Knowledge

### Insight: The "Other" Analytics Bucket Is the Reels Page
**Insight**: In post analytics, the huge unattributed "Other" views bucket (e.g., 881K of a 1M-view post) is largely the Reels page distribution of your carousel, plus story-shares — proof the carousel is riding reel distribution without being a reel.
**Deploy**: Read "Other" as your virality gauge. When "Other" dwarfs home/profile/explore, the format is being amplified — scale that exact post style.

### Insight: A Boring Baseline Compounds
**Insight**: Between viral hits, ordinary graphic posts reliably pull ~100K views and ~100 followers each. One post a day = ~3,000 followers a month = ~36K a year with ZERO viral outliers — the outliers are bonus, not the plan.
**Deploy**: Commit to daily cadence for the compounding floor; judge the system monthly on net follower growth, not on whether any single post popped.

### Insight: 133K Views Was "Good Enough"
**Insight**: View-count inflation makes creators discard working formats. Dakota's verify threshold on the post that changed his account was 133K views — what mattered was the RATIO (893 shares, 2K saves, ~1 like per 10 views), not a million-view vanity bar. "Some people work for three years to get 10,000 views."
**Deploy**: Set verify thresholds on engagement ratios relative to your own baseline (e.g., 3-5x your usual shares/saves), never on absolute view counts.

### Insight: Unfollows Are Exhaust, Not Signal
**Insight**: Dakota's 90-day readout: +33K follows, −24K unfollows, net +8K — and he ships anyway ("If you unfollow me, cool. Sayonara."). High-volume posting always churns; net growth is the only number.
**Deploy**: Report and act on net follower delta. Never let an unfollow wave trigger a format pivot that the share/save data doesn't support.

### Insight: Rights Boundary on Sourced Images
**Insight**: Pinterest-sourced images are workable for organic graphic posts because nothing is being sold — the moment you make prints/merch from them, you're monetizing photos you don't own. Dakota refuses print requests for exactly this reason.
**Deploy**: Sourced images for feed content only. Anything sold (prints, products, ads with licensing exposure) uses owned or licensed imagery.
