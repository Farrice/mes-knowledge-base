# High-Stakes VSL — Real Jeopardy Persuasion Copy (Bill Browder)

Browder wrote *Red Notice* as a delivery mechanism, not a monument — a 12-hour grip on a stranger's attention engineered for a purpose beyond itself: get the Magnitsky Act passed. A VSL is the same animal at one-hundredth the length. It is not a sales page; it is a campaign that has thirty seconds to earn the next thirty, then the minute after that, all the way to the button — for a stranger with no obligation and a thumb already moving toward the back arrow. Most VSLs lose because they do the two things Browder would never do: they *manufacture* the stakes ("you're losing $10,000 a month and don't even know it"), and they *assert* the claims ("this changes everything"). Both feel like persuasion and are actually leaks. A manufactured stake is a fake threat a skeptic deflates in one breath; an asserted claim is an opinion a hostile reader dismisses. This workflow builds the VSL the way Browder built the deposition chapter: find the *real* jeopardy buried in the prospect's situation, build the caring before the fall, route every consequential claim through a receipt the prospect can't argue with, and end every unit on an open loop so there is no comfortable place to stop scrolling — while copy-engine / luke-iha run the offer architecture, proof ladder, and CTA mechanics underneath. Browder runs the real-jeopardy narrative spine; the copy engines run the machinery. **The whole point: the craft makes a *true* stake land like a knife — it never invents one. A false stake told this well is the most dangerous thing this skill can produce, because the better the narrative, the more convincing the lie.**

## Pre-Flight

Read before executing — load these `genius.md` sections (do not paraphrase from memory; the patterns are precise, and this workflow's failure mode — a beautifully-built script standing on a fake stake or a bare claim — is the most dangerous in the entire skill):

- **Pattern 7 — The Stakes Architecture (Make Us Care Before the Fall)** — the spine of the lead: stakes are not the bad thing, they are the *caring you build before* the bad thing. The cost of inaction only lands if the prospect was rooting for someone (usually themselves) first. Restraint clause: a few details, not a biography.
- **Pattern 9 — Evidence as the Narrative Skeleton (You Can't Sue the Transcript)** — the honesty/legal spine: build the script on a skeleton of verifiable receipts; route every contestable claim through the evidence, not your assertion. Proof-as-narrative — show the receipt *inside* the story — out-persuades the bold claim and can't be debunked.
- **Pattern 4 — Relatable Anchors (Betrayal, Fear, the Familiar)** — the lead's mechanism: open on the emotion the ICP *already feels* (the betrayal by the last vendor, the fear of being found out), not the feature. Seed the specific recognizable detail of their world so they find themselves inside the story.
- **Pattern 1 — The Next-Sentence Test (Why Should Anyone Care?)** — the line-by-line discipline: at every sentence boundary, "does this make me need the next sentence?" The VSL's first three lines are the stack-it-or-scroll decision; every line after is a re-decision.
- **Pattern 5 — End Every Chapter on Uncertainty (The Cliff-Hang Spine)** — a VSL is a miniseries: each unit (lead → stakes → mechanism → proof → offer → CTA) ends on a live uncertainty that the next unit must pick up. Never resolve comfortably mid-script.
- **Cross-Domain Applications — the Copywriting (VSL/landing/email) row** — which Browder moves earn their place in conversion copy, and the standing rule: "Honesty spine: real jeopardy, real proof."
- **Bill Browder Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)** — especially **AN-2 (Manufactured or Un-Earned Stakes)** — the headline gate of this workflow. Also AN-4 (accusation/claim in your own voice instead of evidence), AN-5 (the comfortable stopping point), AN-6 (telling instead of showing), AN-3 (describing for the sake of describing).

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` — specifically **Q4** (*"Are the stakes real — and have I built the caring before the fall?"*) and **Q6** (*"Is the villain/claim rendered through evidence, not adjectives?"*) — before you write a single line. Confirm four things: **(1)** the jeopardy is *true* — the cost of inaction is a real, specific, documentable consequence for this ICP, not an inflated or invented number (honesty spine; AN-2). If you're reaching for "you're hemorrhaging money," stop and find the *true* size. **(2)** every consequential claim in the script — the mechanism works, the result is real, the proof is genuine — is *substantiated by a receipt* you have or can get (Pattern 9; if the load-bearing proof doesn't exist, this is a copy-engine / proof problem, not a Browder problem — route to `luke-iha-proof-mechanisms` / `proof-as-story` / `verify` BEFORE drafting). **(3)** you have someone for the prospect to root for — usually the prospect's own future self, or a relatable protagonist who was where they are — *that you establish before the fall* (Pattern 7). **(4)** the offer, price, guarantee, and CTA mechanics are owned by copy-engine / luke-iha — Browder does not invent the offer; he wraps it in the true-jeopardy narrative. **If the script only converts on a stake or a claim you can't substantiate, do not write it. A device makes a true thing ring; it must never be the reason a false thing sounds airtight.**

## Input Required

- **The offer** — what's actually being sold (product, service, program), the price, the core promise, the guarantee. Whole thing, not a summary. (Browder wraps the offer in narrative; he doesn't invent it. If there is no offer yet, this is a copy-engine job first.)
- **The ICP + the felt emotion** — who this is for, and the *one emotion they already feel* about this problem at 2am: the fear, the betrayal, the humiliation, the quiet dread. (This becomes the relatable-anchor lead. "Lead with the emotion the ICP already feels, not the feature.")
- **The real cost of inaction** (honesty spine) — what *genuinely* happens to this prospect if they do nothing — a true, specific, documentable consequence, stated at its *real* size, not its dramatic size. The thing you'll build into the jeopardy. If you can only state it vaguely ("they'll keep struggling"), dig for the specific true cost before drafting (see Step 2).
- **The proof inventory** (honesty/legal spine) — the *receipts*: case studies with real numbers, dated results, named testimonials, screenshots, before/afters, the mechanism's actual evidence, third-party data. List what you HAVE vs. what you NEED. If a claim the sale turns on has no receipt, route it to the proof workflows before writing the script — do not assert it.
- **The mechanism** — *how* the offer delivers the result (the unique process, the "why this works when nothing else did"). Browder's "drama excavation" lives here: the mechanism is the dry material you make exciting by finding the buried conflict inside it.
- **Format / length** — full long-form VSL (3–15 min) / short VSL / sales-page-with-video / spoken webinar pitch / cold-traffic vs. warm. (Drives how much runway you get to build caring before the fall — long-form VSL has minutes; a 60-second ad has one breath.)
- **Existing draft** (if rescuing) — the actual script as it stands, whole. We diagnose where the jeopardy went fake and where claims went bare, then rebuild those units — we don't carpet-bomb a working script.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Name what the script is really about, in one human sentence (Pattern 3, mini-book spine)

Before any line, state the VSL's core thread the way Browder names a chapter: not "a VSL for [product]," but the *one human drama* underneath it. A betrayal (the industry has been lying to them). A chase (they're racing a clock they can't see). A reversal (the thing they were told was the solution is the problem). The script is not "about" the product; it's about this drama, and the product is what resolves it.

Write it: **"This is really about ______."** If you can't, you don't have a spine yet — you have a feature list waiting to become one. Everything downstream orbits this sentence. (This is also the honesty checkpoint: the drama must be *true* of the ICP's real situation. A manufactured drama is just AN-2 wearing a story costume.)

### Step 2 — Build the real jeopardy: the cost of inaction, evidenced (Pattern 7 + Pattern 9 + AN-2 gate)

This is the headline step and the one where VSLs die. The lead must make the prospect feel a *real* stake — and the failure mode is manufacturing it. Run the cost of inaction through this gate, which separates a Browder stake from a copywriting-guru stake:

| The stake as most VSLs write it | Why it fails Browder's standard | The Browder rebuild |
|---|---|---|
| "You're losing $10,000/month and don't even know it." | Invented number, no receipt — a skeptic deflates it in one breath (AN-2) | State the *true*, specific, documentable cost — even if it's smaller. "The average [ICP] in this spot waits 14 months to fix it. [Cite where that comes from.]" Real and provable beats big and bogus. |
| "If you don't act now, everything you've built is at risk." | Abstract, inflated, nothing concrete at stake (AN-2 + AN-3) | Find the one concrete human thing that's truly lost. Not "everything" — the specific Tuesday-night moment this prospect already recognizes. |
| "This is the most important decision of your career." | Telling the size instead of building it (AN-6) | Don't tell them it's important. *Show* one real scene of the cost — let them supply "important." |
| The fall lands in line one, before any caring | No investment exists yet to be cashed (Pattern 7) | Build the rooting interest *first* — who they are, what they want, the future self they're trying to become — then land the cost as the threat to *that*. |

**The construction:** (a) establish the prospect's *want* — the concrete, admirable thing their future self is after (you cannot fear a loss for someone who wants nothing; the want is what inaction takes away). (b) Make them root for that future self with 2–3 recognizable details of their world — restraint, not a biography (AN-3). (c) *Then* land the real cost of inaction as the threat to the want — and **route it through a receipt** wherever it's contestable (a stat, a study, a named pattern, the dated industry reality). The honesty spine here: if the cost is real, you can cite it; if you can't cite it, interrogate whether it's real before you write it.

```
THE WANT (future self, concrete + admirable): ______
THE ROOTING DETAIL (2–3, recognizable to this ICP): ______
THE REAL COST OF INACTION (true, specific, sized honestly): ______
THE RECEIPT under it (what makes the cost provable, not asserted): ______
```

> **The AN-2 alarm**: if you find yourself stacking adjectives on the threat to make it feel bigger ("devastating," "catastrophic," "soul-crushing"), you are manufacturing size you didn't earn. Cut the adjectives. The size of the fall comes from the caring you built and the truth of the cost — never from modifiers. A true cost stated plainly outperforms a fake cost stated dramatically, and it can't be deflated.

### Step 3 — Lead on the felt emotion, not the feature (Pattern 4: the relatable anchor)

The first three lines decide stack-or-scroll. Open on the *exact emotion the ICP already carries* about this problem — the one you named in Input — rendered through a specific, recognizable touchpoint of their world, not an abstract claim. Browder's readers wrote "we have so much in common"; your prospect must feel "this is exactly what's happening to me" inside the first breath.

| The opener as most VSLs write it (feature/claim-first) | The Browder rebuild (emotion + recognizable detail) |
|---|---|
| "Introducing the [product] — the all-in-one system for [outcome]." | "It's 11pm and you're still staring at the [specific artifact of their world], knowing tomorrow you'll do it again." |
| "Are you struggling to [generic pain]?" (the dead-question open) | A single recognizable scene that *is* the struggle, so they nod before you've asked anything. |
| "What if I told you there's a better way?" | The emotion they already feel, named so precisely they wonder how you knew. |

Seed the *one* poignant, specific detail of their world (the named tool, the familiar Tuesday, the dreaded recurring moment) — the door they walk through into the story. One true detail beats five generic ones (AN-3). Then run the next-sentence test on every line of the lead (Step 6): the lead is where the script lives or dies.

### Step 4 — Excavate the mechanism; route every claim through a receipt (Pattern 2 + Pattern 9 = proof-as-narrative)

The mechanism ("how it works") is the dry material — the VSL equivalent of Browder's deposition or Chinese wall. Most scripts either skip it (and lose the skeptic) or dump it as a feature list (and lose everyone). Browder's move: find the buried conflict inside it and tell it *through* that conflict — then nail every result-claim to a receipt so the proof is the narrative, not a testimonials slide bolted on at the end.

**4a — Excavate the mechanism's drama.** Interrogate the mechanism: where is the "this doesn't make sense" tension (the preferred-shares anomaly)? What does the industry get wrong that this fixes? Who's the implicit adversary (the status quo, the incumbent method, the advice everyone follows that quietly fails)? Tell the mechanism as the *discovery of why the old way betrays them* — render the adversary (the broken default) through evidence, never slander (AN-4).

**4b — Build proof-as-narrative (the honesty/legal spine).** Take every consequential claim the sale turns on and route it through a receipt, told inside the story:

| Claim the sale turns on | Bare assertion (FAILS — AN-4) | Proof-as-narrative (the receipt inside the story) |
|---|---|---|
| "This works." | "This is proven to work." | The dated case: "[Named person] was at [the recognizable bad spot] in [month]. By [later month], [specific number]. Here's the screenshot." — the receipt carries the claim. |
| "Results are fast." | "Get results in days, not months." | One real timeline with a real artifact, shown — let the prospect supply "fast." |
| "It's different." | "Unlike everything else out there…" | Show the specific anomaly (4a) that no one else has, evidenced — the difference is demonstrated, not declared. |

The rule (Hidden Knowledge #3, applied to copy): the strongest claims are the ones you *never make in your own voice* — the receipt makes them. A claim with a receipt can't be debunked and can't be dismissed as hype. A claim without one is an opinion the skeptic discards. **If a load-bearing claim has no receipt, it does not go in the script as an assertion — it goes back to the proof workflows.**

### Step 5 — Architect the units as a miniseries; end each on an open loop (Pattern 5 + AN-5 gate)

A VSL is a serial. Map it into contained units and engineer the boundary between each so there is no comfortable place to stop. Inside each unit, raise and partially resolve a tension; at each unit's *edge*, leave a live uncertainty the next unit picks up.

| VSL unit | What it does | The open loop that ends it (no comfortable stop) |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead** (Step 3) | Felt emotion + recognizable scene | "...and you've tried everything. Which is exactly why what I found surprised me." |
| **Stakes** (Step 2) | The real, evidenced cost of inaction | "...the cost isn't the worst part. The worst part is *why* it keeps happening — which almost no one sees." |
| **Mechanism** (Step 4a) | The buried-conflict discovery | "...once you see the real reason, you can't unsee it. And it points to exactly one fix." |
| **Proof** (Step 4b) | Receipts inside the story | "...and [named person] wasn't the only one. The pattern held across [n] more — here's what they all did." |
| **Offer** (copy-engine) | The offer architecture, value stack | "...and the price is the part most people get wrong about — because of one thing I haven't told you yet." |
| **CTA** (copy-engine + Saxon close) | The close | The decision reframed: doing nothing is the risky choice, not the safe one. |

The AN-5 check: read every unit ending as a stranger with the back arrow ready. Could they comfortably stop here? Each comfortable ending is a leak — open a loop instead. Never end a unit on a resolved, satisfied note before the CTA.

### Step 6 — Run the next-sentence test on the whole script, line by line (Pattern 1)

This is the discipline that separates a Browder script from a competent one. Read the entire draft as a stranger with no obligation and a thumb moving. At every sentence boundary, ask: *"Does this line make me need the next one?"* Mark every place a prospect could comfortably stop — every slack line, every line that exists to sound good rather than to pull (AN-1).

| What you find at a sentence boundary | What it means | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| A line you'd skim past | It exists to be admired, not to grip (AN-1) | Cut it or rebuild it to create pull |
| A "comfortable" full stop | A leak — the prospect can leave here (AN-5) | End the unit on uncertainty instead |
| A claim you nod at but can't prove | Bare assertion (AN-4) | Route through a receipt or cut |
| A stretch of explanation | Telling, not showing (AN-6) | Embed the info in scene or evidence; reserve one "nail" sentence for the end |
| 4+ adjectives on a thing | Describing for the sake of it (AN-3) | Cut to the 2–3 most poignant |

The metric is not quality — it is *grip*. A first-time prospect should not be able to find a comfortable place to stop scrolling. The goal is "I couldn't stop watching," not "that was well written."

### Step 7 — Hand the offer + CTA mechanics to the copy engines (composition boundary)

Browder runs the narrative spine; he does not invent the offer, the value stack, the price justification, the guarantee, or the close mechanics. Hand those to the engines and let them install their machinery into the units you architected:

- **Offer architecture, value stack, price framing** → `copy-engine` / `luke-iha-vsl-leads` / `luke-iha-million-dollar-mechanisms`.
- **Proof ladder construction** → `luke-iha-proof-mechanisms` / `luke-iha-proof-ladder` / `proof-as-story` (feeds your Step 4b receipts).
- **The lead/big-idea mechanics** → `luke-iha-vsl-leads` (Browder's relatable anchor *is* the lead; let Luke's lead-types refine it).
- **CTA / close** → `copy-engine` close + optionally a **Saxon punch** (`ward-saxon-punch`) so the do-it word lands like a slammed door, with the cost-of-inaction reframe ("doing nothing is the risk") as the final open-loop-turned-resolution.
- **Emotional-architecture / sales-arc shaping** → `stanton-sales-arc` if the piece needs the change-engine beat structure underneath the jeopardy spine.

The boundary rule: **if a line needs a real claim or a real proof point to convert, that's the copy engines' job — Browder's spine makes a true offer grip; it cannot make a missing one exist.**

### Step 8 — The blunt-reader pass (Pattern 13: kill the fake stakes before delivery)

Browder never finishes alone — 10 blunt, non-literary friends mark it up before his editor sees it. For a VSL, the blunt reader is a *skeptic of the offer*, not a copy fan. Read the whole script aloud, cold, as the most skeptical version of the ICP. The one question that matters: **"Where did I roll my eyes?"** Every eye-roll is a manufactured stake, a bare claim, or a comfortable stopping point. Fix those before the script ships — the skeptic's eye-roll is the AN-2/AN-4 alarm firing in the wild.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Long-form VSL (3–15 min, video)** | Native habitat — full runway for stakes-before-the-fall. Build genuine rooting interest in the prospect's future self (minutes, not seconds) before the cost-of-inaction lands. Full miniseries unit map (Step 5). Proof-as-narrative carries multiple receipts threaded through the story, not a testimonials block at the end. Saxon close on the spoken CTA. |
| **Short VSL / cold-traffic ad-to-VSL (30–90s)** | One breath of runway. Lead = felt emotion + one recognizable detail (Step 3) doubling as the stake. One receipt, shown fast. One open loop into the offer. No room to build elaborate caring — pick the *single* truest jeopardy and the *single* hardest receipt. The next-sentence test is brutal here; every word earns the next or they're gone. |
| **Sales page with embedded video** | Video carries the lead + stakes + one hero receipt (the units that need *motion* and voice); the page below carries the proof ladder, offer stack, and FAQ-as-objection-handling. Open loops live at section breaks. The CTA button itself wants the Saxon punch. Devices and receipts land on the lines the eye *stops* on. |
| **Spoken webinar / live pitch** | The miniseries runs across the webinar arc — each teaching segment ends on an open loop into the next, the pitch lands as the resolution of the jeopardy built across the content. Reconstruct real scenes (yours or a student's) out loud; the recovered detail makes it come alive. Stakes-before-the-fall has the most room here. |
| **Email VSL / launch sequence** | Each email is a chapter ending on a live uncertainty (the PS that teases tomorrow *is* a chapter-ending cliffhang). One conversion turn per email: jeopardy email → cost of inaction; mechanism email → buried-conflict discovery; proof email → the receipt-as-story; close email → Saxon CTA. Don't stack the whole arc into one email — it's a series, run it like one. |
| **Social hook → VSL (LinkedIn/X → link)** | The hook's first three lines are the relatable anchor + felt emotion, ending on the open loop that earns the click. One true detail, one real stake — at hook length, a manufactured stake or a bare claim shows in a heartbeat. The VSL on the other side does the full arc. |

### Worked example A — A VSL lead for a real S&C coaching offer (build mode)

**Offer:** a strength-and-conditioning program for desk-bound professionals over 35. · **ICP + felt emotion:** the quiet dread of a body that's quietly failing while the calendar fills. · **Core thread:** *this is really about the betrayal of "I'll get to it later" — the slow theft of a decade you assumed you'd always have.*

**The real jeopardy (Step 2), honesty-checked:**
> WANT: to still be the dad who can get on the floor and back up without a plan. · ROOTING DETAIL: the knee that clicks getting out of the car; the "ten more minutes" you give the laptop instead of the gym. · COST OF INACTION (true, sized honestly): adults lose 3–8% of muscle mass per decade after 30, and it accelerates after 40 unless it's actively trained against — and the strength you don't build in your 40s is the independence you don't have in your 70s. [Cite: sarcopenia research.] · RECEIPT: the dated study, named in the script, not "you're falling apart."

**The lead (Step 3), emotion-first:**
> "You got out of the car today and your knee made that sound again. You told yourself it's nothing. You've been telling yourself it's nothing for about two years now. Here's the part nobody mentions: the body doesn't fall apart all at once. It leaves quietly, one skipped Tuesday at a time — and the bill doesn't come due until the one morning you can't get up off the floor with your kid. That morning has a date. You just don't know it yet."

**Why it works (Browder's standard):** It opens on the emotion the ICP already feels (the dread he's been suppressing — "telling yourself it's nothing"), rendered through two recognizable details (the knee, the skipped Tuesday — restraint, not a biography). The stake is *real and citable* (sarcopenia is documented; the number isn't invented), so a skeptic can't deflate it. The caring is built before the fall — we root for the dad who wants to get up off the floor — and the cost lands as the threat to *that* want. It ends on an open loop ("that morning has a date") with no comfortable stop. No adjective-stacking, no manufactured number, no claim in bare assertion. **Honesty spine intact: the jeopardy is true, and the craft makes the true thing land like a knife — it didn't invent the knife.**

### Worked example B — Rescuing a leaking proof unit (rescue mode)

**Before (the draft as it stood — bare assertion, AN-4):**
> "Our system is proven to deliver incredible results, faster than anything else on the market. Hundreds of clients have transformed their businesses. You owe it to yourself to act now before it's too late."

**Diagnosis:** Every sentence is a claim in the author's own voice with no receipt ("proven," "incredible," "faster than anything"). The stake is manufactured ("before it's too late" — no real, specific cost). It tells instead of shows (AN-6). A skeptic rolls their eyes at all of it (Step 8 fails).

**After (proof-as-narrative + real jeopardy):**
> "In March, Dana ran her agency the way you probably run yours — three tools, four spreadsheets, and a Sunday-night knot in her stomach because she could never tell, going into Monday, whether the month was going to clear payroll. Here's her dashboard from that March. [show] And here it is in July — same agency, same team, one change. [show] She didn't get 'incredible results.' She got Sunday nights back. The cost of the way you're doing it isn't dramatic. It's just every Sunday, for as long as you keep doing it."

**Why the rescue works:** The claim ("it works, fast") now lives entirely in the *receipt* — two dated dashboards, a named person, shown — so it can't be debunked or dismissed as hype (the accusation lives in the transcript, not Browder's voice). The stake is real and specific (the recurring Sunday-night knot, sized honestly: "not dramatic, just every Sunday") — not "before it's too late." It shows rather than tells, and ends on a quiet open loop ("for as long as you keep doing it") that pulls into the offer. **Honesty spine: the result is real and evidenced; the craft amplified a true claim and refused to manufacture an inflated one.**

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Bill Browder Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (rows: **Stakes & Honesty Spine**, **Villain Rendering & Evidence**, **Page-Turner Grip (Next-Sentence Test)**, **Unit-Ending Uncertainty**, **Drama Excavation**). Flag and fix before delivering.

- **Honesty spine — the headline gate (AN-2 + AN-4):** the cost of inaction is *true*, specific, and sized honestly — no invented numbers, no inflation, every contestable cost citable. Every consequential claim is carried by a *receipt*, not bare assertion. **If the script converts on a stake or a claim you can't substantiate, it fails this gate outright — no composite score rescues it.** The better the narrative, the harder this gate must hold.
- **Stakes built before the fall (Pattern 7):** the prospect is made to root for someone (usually their own future self) *before* the cost of inaction lands. The fall is sized by the caring built first, never by adjectives. No tragedy dropped on line one with no investment to cash.
- **Lead on felt emotion, not feature (Pattern 4):** the first three lines open on the emotion the ICP already carries, through one recognizable detail — not a feature, a generic pain question, or a "what if I told you." One true detail, not five generic ones.
- **Proof-as-narrative, not testimonials-bolted-on (Pattern 9):** the strongest claims live in the evidence, told inside the story; the receipt carries the claim. No load-bearing claim sits in the author's own voice.
- **Next-sentence grip on every line (Pattern 1):** a skeptic with the back arrow ready finds no comfortable place to stop; every line earns the next. No line exists to be admired (AN-1).
- **Every unit ends on an open loop (Pattern 5 / AN-5):** lead, stakes, mechanism, proof, offer — each boundary pulls forward. No comfortable resolution before the CTA.
- **Mechanism excavated, not dumped (Pattern 2):** the "how it works" is told through its buried conflict (the broken default it exposes), rendered through evidence — not a feature list, not slander of the competitor (AN-4).
- **Show, then nail once (Pattern 12 / AN-6):** the script dramatizes; it reserves at most one clean "nail" sentence per unit for the meaning, never tells in place of showing.
- **Restraint (AN-3):** scenes and details run to 2–3 poignant particulars, then move; no 4+ adjective pile-ups, no description for its own sake.
- **Composition boundary honored (Step 7):** Browder runs the narrative spine; copy-engine / luke-iha own the offer, value stack, proof ladder, and CTA mechanics. The spine makes a true offer grip — it never invents the offer or a missing claim.
- **Blunt-reader pass survived (Step 8):** read as the most skeptical ICP, there are no eye-rolls — every eye-roll is a manufactured stake, a bare claim, or a leak, and was fixed before shipping.

## Common Pitfalls

- **The manufactured stake that "tests well" (AN-2 — the most dangerous failure).** Inventing or inflating the cost of inaction because a real one feels too small to sell — "you're losing $10K/month," "everything's at risk," "before it's too late." The better your narrative craft, the more convincing this lie becomes, which is exactly why it's the gravest failure this skill can produce. **Recovery:** go back to Step 2's gate and find the *true* cost — even if it's smaller — and route it through a receipt. A real cost stated plainly (Worked Example A's sarcopenia data; Worked Example B's "every Sunday") outconverts a fake cost stated dramatically, and a skeptic can't deflate it. If you genuinely can't find a real, specific cost of inaction, the problem is the offer or the ICP, not the script — surface that, don't paper over it with manufactured drama.

- **Claims in the author's voice instead of the receipt (AN-4).** Asserting "proven," "incredible," "fastest," "transforms everything" — opinions a skeptic discards and a regulator may flag. **Recovery:** Step 4b — route every consequential claim through a receipt, told inside the story (the dated case, the named person, the shown screenshot). The claim you never make in your own voice is the one that can't be debunked. If a load-bearing claim has no receipt, that's a proof problem — route it to `proof-as-story` / `luke-iha-proof-mechanisms` / `verify`, not into the script as an assertion.

- **The feature-first lead (Pattern 4 violation).** Opening on the product, the offer, or a generic "are you struggling with…" — so the prospect never finds themselves inside the story and scrolls in the first three lines. **Recovery:** Step 3 — rebuild the open on the *exact emotion the ICP already feels*, through one recognizable detail of their world. Lead with the felt thing; the feature comes much later, as the resolution of the jeopardy.

- **The comfortable VSL — resolves too early and leaks (AN-5).** Wrapping up units neatly ("So that's how it works! Now here's the offer."), giving the prospect a dozen clean places to stop before the CTA. **Recovery:** Step 5 unit map — end every unit on a live uncertainty, and run Step 6's next-sentence test to mark every comfortable full stop. A VSL is a miniseries; there is no good place to stop until the button.

- **Carpet-bombing a working script in rescue mode.** When rescuing a draft that mostly works, rebuilding everything instead of the leaking units — and degrading the parts that already gripped. **Recovery:** diagnose first (Steps 2 and 6 against the existing draft), rebuild *only* the units where the jeopardy went fake or the claims went bare, and preserve the lines that already pass the next-sentence test. The felt verdict on the original spine wins; small surgical passes, not a teardown.
