# Insight Vector Framework — Reference Document

## The Systems Grammar

Insight Vectors exploit structural weaknesses in audience mental models. There are 4 categories, each containing 3+ vector types.

---

### Category 1: CAUSALITY VECTORS

These exploit flawed cause-and-effect beliefs.

#### 1.1 Reverse Causation
Flip the causal arrow. The audience thinks A→B; reality is B→A.
- **Template**: "You're not [suffering] because of [believed cause]. Your [believed cause] is [suffering] because of [actual root]."
- **Example**: "You're not fat because your metabolism is slow. Your fat is what SLOWS your metabolism."
- **Power source**: Uses the audience's existing variables. No new information needed — just a direction flip.

#### 1.2 Multiple Causation (Convergence)
The audience believes in a single cause. Reality involves multiple interacting causes.
- **Template**: "[Problem] isn't just [single cause]. It's [cause A] + [cause B] + [cause C] all converging."
- **Example**: "Weight gain isn't calories. It's sleep + hormones + inflammation + gut bacteria all converging."
- **Power source**: Explains why single-fix approaches failed. Positions multi-vector product as the real answer.

#### 1.3 Hidden Condition (Conditional Truth)
Something universally believed to be true actually requires a HIDDEN CONDITION.
- **Template**: "[Common advice] works — but ONLY if [hidden condition]. Without it, [failure mode with name]."
- **Example**: "Writing goals works — but ONLY if they stay visible. 72% fail through 'commitment evaporation.'"
- **Power source**: Transforms a general truth into a conditional one. Your product IS the hidden condition.

---

### Category 2: PATTERN VECTORS

These exploit pattern recognition and classification instincts.

#### 2.1 System Archetypes (Typing)
Create a classification system that makes the audience feel understood.
- **Template**: "What's your [domain] TYPE? [Type A]: [description]. [Type B]: [description]. [Type C]: [description]."
- **Example**: "What's your metabolism TYPE? Slow burn, crash cycle, or delayed spike?"
- **Power source**: People are compulsive self-classifiers. Types validate their intuition that they're different from average.

#### 2.2 Virtuous Cycle
Show the upward spiral your product initiates.
- **Template**: "[First step] → [benefit] → [enhanced capacity] → [bigger benefit] → [self-sustaining loop]."
- **Example**: "Better sleep → more energy → consistent exercise → deeper sleep → higher baseline energy."
- **Power source**: Creates hope through system dynamics. Once started, it sustains itself.

#### 2.3 Vicious Cycle
Make the invisible trap visible.
- **Template**: "[Quick fix] → [hidden cost] → [worse baseline] → [stronger craving for quick fix] → repeat."
- **Example**: "Crash diet → muscle loss → slower metabolism → weight rebounds higher → more extreme diet."
- **Power source**: Names the trap. Once they see the loop, they can't unsee it.

---

### Category 3: CONTROL POINT VECTORS

These identify leverage points the audience doesn't know about.

#### 3.1 Leading Indicator
The audience tracks lagging indicators. You reveal a leading one.
- **Template**: "Forget [lagging indicator]. [Leading indicator] tells you more and predicts [outcome] [timeframe] before [lagging indicator] moves."
- **Example**: "Morning temperature predicts weight gain 3-4 weeks before the scale moves."
- **Power source**: Creates urgency without selling. Feels like empowerment.

#### 3.2 Hidden Constraint
The REAL bottleneck is not what they think.
- **Template**: "The real limit isn't [perceived constraint]. It's [hidden constraint] blocking [process] downstream."
- **Example**: "The limit isn't willpower — it's cellular inflammation blocking nutrient absorption."
- **Power source**: Explains why effort alone hasn't worked. Frees them from self-blame.

#### 3.3 False Assumption
A linear relationship (more A = more B) is wrong.
- **Template**: "You think more [A] = more [B]. But [subtracting/changing C] gives you more [B] than [adding A]."
- **Example**: "More exercise ≠ more weight loss. Removing inflammatory foods outperforms adding workouts."
- **Power source**: Gives the audience a shortcut. Saves effort. Counter-intuitive = memorable.

#### 3.4 Missing Variable
The variable that actually matters isn't being tracked.
- **Template**: "Everyone tracks [obvious variable]. Nobody measures [missing variable] — which actually determines [outcome]."
- **Example**: "Everyone counts calories. Nobody measures omega-3:omega-6 ratio — which determines if those calories burn or store."
- **Power source**: Audience feels like they've been playing with incomplete information.

---

### Category 4: MODEL STRUCTURE VECTORS

These exploit the structure of mental models themselves.

#### 4.1 Model Limitations Reframe
A constraint that looks like a weakness is actually a strength.
- **Template**: "[Perceived limitation] isn't a weakness — it's [hidden advantage]. [Counter-intuitive explanation]."
- **Example**: "Fewer Lego pieces → more creative structures. Constraints are liberation."
- **Power source**: Meta-level reframe. Changes the audience's relationship with their perceived disadvantages.

#### 4.2 Structural Revelation
Reveal a hidden process or structure the audience didn't know existed.
- **Template**: "Your [system] runs a [named hidden process] — [what it does]. [When condition], [it stops/changes/fails]."
- **Example**: "Your body runs a 'night shift' crew for cellular repair. Eat late, and they stop burning to start digesting."
- **Power source**: Anthropomorphization of systems. Makes the invisible feel tangible.

## The 8-Fold Elaboration Structure

Every raw insight vector becomes deployable through 8 beats:

| Beat | What It Does | Example (Metabolism Reverse) |
|------|-------------|------------------------------|
| 1. **Paradoxical Question** | Hooks attention by violating expectations | "Why does eating fewer calories sometimes make you GAIN weight?" |
| 2. **UMP/UMS** | Names the mechanism | "Your fat tissue produces inflammatory cytokines that downregulate mitochondrial function — the Fat Brake." |
| 3. **Trigger** | Creates emotional urgency | "By the time most people notice the weight gain, the Fat Brake has been engaged for months." |
| 4. **Testable Proof** | Evidence that validates | "A 2019 Cell Metabolism study found that a 10% increase in visceral fat reduced metabolic rate by 8% within 6 weeks." |
| 5. **Intensifiers** | Makes it more relevant/urgent | "And it gets worse with age. After 35, the Fat Brake sensitivity increases by ~2% per year." |
| 6. **Myths & Mistakes** | Wrong beliefs corrected | "This is why 'just eat less' fails. You're fighting the brake with the accelerator." |
| 7. **Cause** | Root explanation | "The root cause is chronic low-grade inflammation creating a metabolic feedback loop." |
| 8. **Resolution** | What to do (your product) | "Breaking the cycle requires addressing inflammation FIRST, before any dietary changes take effect." |

## Deployment Rules

1. **Map first, mine second**: Always map the audience's mental model before generating vectors.
2. **Suspicion > Novelty**: Confirm half-suspected beliefs > introduce completely new information.
3. **Stack 3-5**: One vector creates interest. Three create conviction. Five create religious certainty.
4. **Name everything**: Unnamed processes don't survive in memory. Name the vector, the failure mode, the hidden mechanism.
5. **Test with the SIN filter**: Simple (12-year-old gets it) + Intuitive (feels right before proof) + New (they haven't heard this).
6. **Ground in reality**: Never fabricate systems, research, or mechanisms. Re-frame real truth, don't invent convenient fiction.
