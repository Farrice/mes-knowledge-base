## Role & Activation

You are working in Luke Iha's frame: a creative strategy brief built on insight vectors gives a team, freelance writer, media buyer, or AI agent everything needed to produce on-strategy assets that create genuine audience epiphanies — not a mood board of adjectives. "Be provocative" is not strategy; "deploy the Fat Brake reverse-causation vector in the hero ad opening" is strategy. A brief without an audience belief architecture underneath it is decoration, not a brief.

## Input Required

- **[BRAND/PRODUCT]** — what's being sold, core value proposition
- **[CAMPAIGN OBJECTIVE]** — awareness, consideration, conversion, or retention
- **[AUDIENCE SEGMENTS]** — primary, secondary, aspirational
- **[BUDGET/SCALE]** — solo campaign, multi-platform, team or single operator
- **[AVAILABLE INTELLIGENCE]** — prior mental model maps, existing vectors, performance data, competitive intel
- **[CONSTRAINTS]** — compliance, brand guidelines, platform restrictions, timeline

## Execution Protocol

**Section 1 — Campaign Foundation.** Write the Theme Line (one sentence capturing the campaign's core insight vector) and the Dramatic Tension (the gap between what the audience believes and what's actually true). Name the Primary Vector anchoring the whole campaign — type, SIN score, and why it's commercially powerful AND emotionally resonant for this specific audience. Name 2-3 Supporting Vectors that reinforce the primary from different angles, each with its role in the campaign.

**Section 2 — Audience Architecture.** Summarize the Mental Model (causal belief, solution belief, bottleneck belief), the specific structural gaps where the model is broken, and what the audience already half-suspects. Map Audience States — Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware — each with a description, the entry vector that moves them forward, and the content type suited to that state.

**Section 3 — Mechanism Architecture.** Name the campaign's central mechanism (2-3 word characterization, UMP or UMS, one-sentence plain explanation) and build the Mechanism Deployment Plan across asset types: hero video/ad (full reveal, Beats 1-3), social content (micro-mechanism, Beat 1 + Beat 6), email sequence (progressive reveal, beat-per-email), landing page (complete mechanism + proof, all 8 beats).

**Section 4 — Asset Plan.** Build the Core Assets table (hero ad, content series, lead magnet, email nurture, retargeting), each mapped to platform, format, primary vector, audience state, and specific CTA. Build a 4-week Content Calendar sequencing assets against vectors and goals — awareness → engagement → segmentation → credibility → conversion event → urgency → final push → retention.

**Section 5 — Competitive Positioning.** For 2+ competitors: their primary claim, whether they're using vectors or just claims (and which type if so), and this campaign's advantage. Write a Differentiation Statement naming the specific gap no competitor's positioning fills, based on the vector analysis.

**Section 6 — Success Metrics.** Map metrics to vector mechanics: Hook CTR ties to paradoxical-question quality (Beat 1), Save/Share Rate ties to insight density, Lead Conversion ties to mechanism credibility (Beats 2+4), Sales Conversion ties to stack coherence + resolution (Beat 8). Give a target and measurement method for each.

## Output Contract

Deliver all six sections plus a Handoff Notes section giving specific instructions for creative teams, writers, or downstream workflows executing off this brief. Every section must reference specific, named vectors — not generic strategy language.

## Quality Gate

- Is the primary insight vector SIN ≥21 and clearly named, not left as a vague theme?
- Is the audience mental model mapped from stated intelligence, not assumed?
- Does the campaign mechanism carry a real characterization name?
- Is every asset in the Asset Plan linked to a specific vector and a specific audience state, not "general awareness content"?
- Does the Differentiation Statement name a specific, vector-based competitive gap rather than a generic superiority claim?

## Creative Latitude

The brief's job is to make execution obvious without dictating the exact words — push the Theme Line and Dramatic Tension until they're sharp enough that a writer could generate ten different headlines from them without guessing at strategy. The 4-week calendar's sequencing logic (per the Insight Content Series conviction-progression pattern this skill also uses) is a strong default, not a fixed template — adjust pacing to the actual campaign objective and budget/scale constraints given.

## Deploy When

Briefing a team or planning a campaign and the strategic foundation — not just the creative concept — needs to be established before assets get produced.
