## McRaney Integration Framework

If the full McRaney skill isn't available, use this compressed belief architecture:

### McRaney's Core Insight: Beliefs Are Not Conclusions — They're Protective Layers

People don't hold beliefs because of evidence. They hold beliefs because those beliefs SERVE A FUNCTION:
- **Identity Protection**: "If this belief is wrong, who am I?"
- **Group Belonging**: "If I change this belief, my tribe rejects me"
- **Cognitive Economy**: "This belief is simple and it works well enough"
- **Emotional Regulation**: "This belief protects me from a fear I don't want to face"

### Belief Change Sequence (McRaney's Deep Canvassing Method)
1. **Acknowledge** the current belief without judgment
2. **Ask** what personal experience led them to this belief
3. **Share** a counter-narrative (NOT evidence — a STORY)
4. **Create space** for them to update their own belief (not forced)

### Key McRaney Principles for This Sprint:
- **The Backfire Effect**: Directly attacking a belief STRENGTHENS it
- **Elaboration Likelihood**: Deep processing requires PERSONAL RELEVANCE
- **Motivated Reasoning**: People reason TOWARD conclusions they want, not FROM evidence
- **Identity-Protective Cognition**: People reject evidence that threatens their identity
- **The Influence of Affect**: Emotional state determines receptivity to new information

## Phase 1: Belief Archaeology

### Step 1.1: Surface the Blocking Belief
What specific belief prevents conversion? Dig beyond the surface:

| Level | Belief | Function |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **Surface** | "I don't need this product" | [Cognitive economy — status quo bias] |
| **Mid-Level** | "I've tried something similar and it didn't work" | [Pattern recognition — past failure protection] |
| **Deep** | "People like me aren't the type who [buy this / do this / change this]" | [Identity protection] |
| **Root** | "If this works, that means I wasted [X years / money / effort] doing it wrong" | [Sunk cost protection + ego defense] |

### Step 1.2: Classify the Belief Type

| Belief Type | Description | Dissolution Approach |
|------------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Epistemic** | "I believe X because of evidence/experience" | Insight vectors work directly (new evidence reshapes beliefs) |
| **Identity-Linked** | "Believing X is part of who I am" | Insight vectors must REFRAME identity, not attack it |
| **Tribal** | "My group believes X" | Insight vectors must include social proof from IN-GROUP |
| **Emotional** | "Believing X protects me from [fear]" | Insight vectors must address the underlying fear first |
| **Samskara** (habitual groove) | "I've always done/thought X" | Insight vectors must create an alternative GROOVE, not just critique the old one |

### Step 1.3: Map the Belief's Protective Architecture

```
BLOCKING BELIEF: "[statement]"
│
├── What it protects: [identity / emotion / group / cognitive load]
├── What evidence strengthens it: [what they'd cite in defense]
├── What experience created it: [the formative moment]
├── What would have to be true for them to update: [the update conditions]
└── What emotional state enables updating: [safety / curiosity / hope / anger at being deceived]
```

| "They've never measured [critical variable]" | Incomplete model | [reasoning support] | **Missing Variable** |

### Step 2.2: McRaney-Aligned Vector Selection

Cross-reference vector candidates against McRaney's dissolution principles:

| Vector Candidate | Backfire Risk? | Requires Identity Shift? | Personal Relevance Pathway | Emotional Gate |
|-----------------|----------------|------------------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| [vector 1] | [low/medium/high] | [yes/no] | [how does this connect to their lived experience?] | [what emotion enables reception?] |

**Selection Rules:**
- **HIGH backfire risk** → Don't lead with this vector. Use it mid-sequence after trust is established.
- **Requires identity shift** → Frame the vector as EVOLUTION, not correction. "You weren't wrong — you had incomplete information."
- **Low personal relevance** → Add a "this happened to [person like you]" narrative wrapper.
- **Emotional gate needed** → Precede the vector with an emotional primer (acknowledge, validate, THEN reveal).

## Phase 3: Dissolution Sequence Design

### Step 3.1: Belief Dissolution Arc

Design a 5-7 step sequence that dissolves the blocking belief through progressive insight vectors:

| Step | Purpose | Vector (if applicable) | McRaney Principle | Content/Copy Element |
|------|---------|----------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | **Acknowledge** | [none — validation only] | Backfire prevention | "If you've tried [X] and it didn't work, you're not alone." |
| 2 | **Seed doubt** gently | [Paradoxical Question — from a low-backfire vector] | Elaboration Likelihood | "Have you ever wondered why [counter-intuitive question]?" |
| 3 | **Name the trap** | [Vicious Cycle or Hidden Constraint vector] | Motivated Reasoning (give them a NEW thing to reason toward) | "[Named mechanism] is why [their past approach] failed — it's not discipline." |
| 4 | **Offer a new identity** | [Model Limitation Reframe] | Identity-Protective Cognition (reframe, don't attack) | "The people who break through aren't more disciplined — they're [type]." |
| 5 | **Provide proof** | [Testable Proof + Leading Indicator] | Affect → Evidence (emotions precede reasoning) | "Here's how to verify this yourself in the next 48 hours..." |
| 6 | **Stack certainty** | [2nd and 3rd vectors pointing to same conclusion] | Convergent evidence dissolves single-vector skepticism | "[Multiple vectors converging]" |
| 7 | **Resolve** | [Resolution vector — product bridge] | The self-persuasion moment | "This is why [product] starts with [upstream mechanism], not [downstream symptom]." |

### Step 3.2: Emotional Arc Design

Parallel to the vector sequence, design the emotional arc:

```
EMOTIONAL ARC:
1. Frustration validated ("I see you, this is real")
2. Curiosity sparked ("Wait, what if...?")
3. Relief at explanation ("It's not my fault — there's a mechanism")
4. Hope from new path ("There IS a way through this")
5. Conviction from proof ("This checks out — I can verify it")
6. Agency from clarity ("Now I know what to do first")
7. Urgency from understanding ("I need to act on this NOW")
```

## Phase 4: Proof-Backed Elaboration

For each vector in the dissolution sequence, build the full proof architecture:

### Proof Stack per Vector:

| Proof Type | Content | Strength (1-10) | McRaney-Compliant? |
|-----------|---------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Research Proof** | "[Study/data with specific numbers]" | | [Does it trigger backfire?] |
| **Experiential Proof** | "[Observable experience they can verify]" | | [Does it connect to personal relevance?] |
| **Narrative Proof** | "[Story of someone who held this belief and updated it]" | | [Does the protagonist share their identity?] |
| **Logical Proof** | "[If mechanism X is true, then you'd predict Y. And that's exactly what we see.]" | | [Does it allow SELF-discovery?] |
| **Social Proof** | "[In-group members who've updated this belief]" | | [Is the social proof from THEIR tribe?] |

### McRaney-Specific Proof Rules:
1. **Never lead with data against an identity-linked belief** → Backfire risk
2. **Lead with narrative proof** when belief is emotionally charged → Stories bypass defenses
3. **Use experiential proof** for skeptical/analytical audiences → "Verify it yourself"
4. **Stack proof types** → Narrative → Logical → Research → Social (safest order)
5. **The strongest proof makes THEM the scientist** → "Try this test and see what happens"

## Quality Gate

- ☐ Blocking belief identified at ROOT level (not just surface)
- ☐ Belief type classified (epistemic/identity/tribal/emotional/samskara)
- ☐ All vectors checked for backfire risk using McRaney principles
- ☐ Dissolution sequence includes both vector arc AND emotional arc
- ☐ Proof stacks use McRaney-compliant ordering (narrative → logical → research → social)
- ☐ No vector directly attacks an identity-linked belief (reframes instead)
- ☐ Deployment guidance includes at least 2 format-specific mappings
- ☐ Risk assessment completed with specific mitigations

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: 
> - **"Facts will convince them"** → McRaney's #1 anti-pattern. Facts don't change beliefs; emotional experiences + identity-safe reframes do.
> - **"Attack the wrong belief"** → You're dissolving the BLOCKING belief, not trying to change all their beliefs.
> - **"Stack too many vectors"** → 3-5 in the dissolution sequence. More creates confusion, not conviction.
