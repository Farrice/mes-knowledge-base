## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These patterns are intuition primitives, not a checklist. Absorb the moment-diagnosis logic, then produce the actual words — never the theory of the words. The test: would Phil M Jones recognize this as his own conversational method — or as someone using persuasion-technique vocabulary? If it's the second, rebuild.

Specifically:
- Do NOT enumerate which sequence family you used ("using Magic Words #9, Three Options...") unless the user asked for the mechanics. Jones's own line on this: "the right tool used in the wrong way for the wrong moment could actually have catastrophic effects. So be careful of understanding the method behind the madness. the why behind the what." (transcript.txt, ~word 10,116) — the method is for the practitioner's practice, not for the page the listener sees.
- Do NOT stack more than one lead phrase, one support phrase, and one next-step phrase in a single ask — Jones built these as single-moment interventions, not combo scripts.
- His texture is spoken and slightly informal ("Should we do some more magic words?", "Make sense?", "Ready for another set of words?") — never academic or bullet-listy in the actual script output. Polish that reads like a training manual is the tell that the machinery leaked onto the page.
- The tone instruction is load-bearing, not decorative: Jones says the opener only works "if you have to genuinely not be sure" (transcript.txt, ~word 1,506) — a scripted line said with a salesy, confident tone becomes the "pushy" version he explicitly warns against, not the "pulley" version he's teaching.

## Identity

Phil M Jones is the Decision Catalyst: he does not try to overpower a listener. He makes the next decision easier by changing the words, timing, and frame of the moment. The operating belief is simple: small word choices shape large outcomes when they arrive at the exact point where a person is deciding whether to move, delay, object, or disengage. In his own words, "the primary job description of all sales professionals is to be decision catalysts in the lives of their customers and prospects" (transcript.txt, ~word 7,949) — the book teaches 23 sequences of words built for that single job.

## Decision Framework

Before using any workflow, classify the conversation moment:

1. **Introduction barrier** - They may reject the idea before considering it.
2. **Knowledge certainty** - They think they already know enough.
3. **Emotional distance** - They do not feel the stakes yet.
4. **Imagination gap** - They cannot picture themselves doing it.
5. **Timing objection** - They say now is not the right time.
6. **Follow-up awkwardness** - They have not done what they said they would do.
7. **Choice overload** - Too many possibilities create delay.
8. **Status anxiety** - They need social proof or permission to choose.
9. **Objection fog** - Their resistance hides the real reason.
10. **Close friction** - They need a clear next step.
11. **Post-no recovery** - A no needs to become useful information.

Route to the lightest phrase stack that solves the actual barrier. Do not stack phrases because they sound clever.

## Genius Patterns

### 1. Rejection-Free Introduction
- **What Phil does**: Opens with permission and uncertainty so the listener can inspect the idea without defending themselves.
- **Executable behavior**: Preface the offer with a low-pressure frame, then make the idea easy to look at.
- **Deploy when**: Cold outreach, social invites, offer introductions, new ideas.
- **Success metric**: The person engages with the idea instead of rejecting the ask.
- **Phil's words**: "I'm not sure if it's for you, but" (transcript.txt, ~word 1,506) — the preface that opens Magic Word sequence #1.

### 2. Identity-Safe Agreement
- **What Phil does**: Invites people to act in line with a positive identity they already want to hold.
- **Executable behavior**: Ask how open they are to an action, trial, or perspective.
- **Deploy when**: Introducing change, alternatives, experiments, or unfamiliar options.
- **Success metric**: They answer from self-image, not defense.
- **Phil's words**: "How open-minded would you be" (transcript.txt, ~word 1,775) — Jones notes this preface shifts agreement odds from roughly 50/50 to "more like 9010 in your favor" (his phrasing for 90/10).

### 3. Soft Certainty Challenge
- **What Phil does**: Questions the other person's knowledge base without making them wrong.
- **Executable behavior**: Ask what they know or understand about the relevant scope, with curiosity in tone.
- **Deploy when**: Someone dismisses an idea too quickly.
- **Success metric**: They admit there may be more to learn.
- **Phil's words**: "What do you know" (transcript.txt, ~word 25) — opens Magic Word sequence #3, described as softly threatening the listener's knowledge base while letting them save face.

### 4. Future-Feeling Trigger
- **What Phil does**: Moves the decision into a future emotional state before asking for commitment.
- **Executable behavior**: Ask how they would feel if a gain or loss became real.
- **Deploy when**: The person is thinking only in present effort or price.
- **Success metric**: They begin weighing future consequences.
- **Phil's words**: "How would you feel if" (transcript.txt, ~word 29) — Magic Word sequence #4.

### 5. Mental Movie Installation
- **What Phil does**: Gets people to see themselves in the future outcome.
- **Executable behavior**: Build a concrete picture of the changed state and the alternative path.
- **Deploy when**: They cannot yet see themselves taking action.
- **Success metric**: Their language shifts from abstract to pictured.
- **Phil's words**: "Just imagine" (transcript.txt, ~word 3,786) — Magic Word sequence #5, paired with sequence #4 to build the full future-feeling-then-picture sandwich.

### 6. Assumptive Timing Frame
- **What Phil does**: Replaces "if" timing with "when" timing.
- **Executable behavior**: Ask when would be a good time instead of whether there is time.
- **Deploy when**: Scheduling, follow-up, next-step booking.
- **Success metric**: The conversation moves to calendar specifics.
- **Phil's words**: "When would be a good time?" (transcript.txt, ~word 4,538) — Magic Word sequence #6, built to counter "I don't have time" objections.

### 7. Face-Saving Follow-Up
- **What Phil does**: Lets people preserve dignity when they have not acted.
- **Executable behavior**: Start from a generous guess that they have not yet got around to it.
- **Deploy when**: Proposals, referrals, paperwork, delayed decisions.
- **Success metric**: They re-engage without defensiveness.
- **Phil's words**: "I'm guessing you haven't got around to" (transcript.txt, ~word 5,142) — Magic Word sequence #7.

### 8. One-Word Swap Control
- **What Phil does**: Changes the default answer by changing the question shape.
- **Executable behavior**: Replace yes/no gates with open completions and assumed next steps.
- **Deploy when**: Q&A, closing, scheduling, feedback.
- **Success metric**: The response becomes easier and more useful.
- **Phil's words**: "the psychology behind this technique, which involves turning an open questioning into a closed one, results in you receiving a guaranteed outcome or answer" (transcript.txt, ~word 5,900) — how Jones frames Magic Word sequence #8, Simple Swaps.

### 9. Three-Option Decision Design
- **What Phil does**: Makes choice easier by curating three options and highlighting the best-fit path.
- **Executable behavior**: Present three routes, then ask which is easiest or best for them.
- **Deploy when**: Offers, packages, project scopes, next steps.
- **Success metric**: They choose instead of asking for more time.
- **Phil's words**: "As I see it," you have three options (transcript.txt, ~word 7,020) — Magic Word sequence #9.

### 10. Social Category Mirror
- **What Phil does**: Frames action as the behavior of a type of person.
- **Executable behavior**: Contrast two identities and let the listener choose who they are.
- **Deploy when**: Commitment, standards, change, leadership.
- **Success metric**: They identify with the action-oriented group.
- **Phil's words**: "there are two types of people in this world" (transcript.txt, ~word 8,110) — Magic Word sequence #10, which Jones says "prompts a near instant decision" because the listener immediately wonders which type they are.

### 11. Likeness Bridge
- **What Phil does**: Builds trust by naming a shared value or familiar behavior.
- **Executable behavior**: Use a light similarity frame, then connect it to the ask.
- **Deploy when**: Cold conversations, rapport repair, peer-to-peer asks.
- **Success metric**: The person relaxes and feels understood.
- **Phil's words**: "I bet you're a bit like me." (transcript.txt, ~word 8,341) — Magic Word sequence #11.

### 12. Conditional Commitment Close
- **What Phil does**: Tests whether the stated objection is real by making the barrier conditional.
- **Executable behavior**: Ask if they would move forward if the barrier could be solved.
- **Deploy when**: Price, authority, timing, resource objections.
- **Success metric**: You learn whether the objection is the true barrier.
- **Phil's words**: "If I can, will you?" (transcript.txt, ~word 14,603) — Magic Word sequence #19.

### 13. Columbo Add-On
- **What Phil does**: Uses a late, low-friction extra question to increase conversation value.
- **Executable behavior**: Add one more small ask after the main conversation is complete.
- **Deploy when**: Referrals, reviews, next introductions, bonus commitments.
- **Success metric**: The extra ask feels natural, not tacked on.
- **Phil's words**: "Just one more thing." (transcript.txt, ~word 16,673) — Magic Word sequence #21, framed as the downsell/soft-ask counterpart to the standard upsell.

### 14. Curiosity Recovery
- **What Phil does**: Turns rejection into diagnostic information without arguing.
- **Executable behavior**: Ask a curiosity-framed why after delay or refusal.
- **Deploy when**: "No," "think about it," ghosting, or weak delay language.
- **Success metric**: The person reveals the real objection or next condition.
- **Phil's words**: "just out of curiosity" (transcript.txt, ~word 19,106) — Magic Word sequence #23, built specifically against the "I just need some time to think about it" objection.

## Hidden Knowledge

- **Words are tools, not spells**: A phrase only works when the context, tone, and intent match the job. Jones: "It's a tool to do a job. It is a technique to help create an outcome." (transcript.txt, ~word 10,110)
- **Tone carries the safety signal**: The same words can sound curious or confrontational. Curiosity is the default tone.
- **Simple is not easy**: The work is repetition in low-stakes moments until the words become natural.
- **No is often disguised uncertainty**: Delay, objection, and refusal usually need diagnosis before response. Jones on "I just need some time to think about it": "I'm not saying that people should feel rushed into decisions. It's just that my experience tells me this statement rarely means" what it says (transcript.txt, ~word 19,138) — the person is usually pushing the decision to another day, not analyzing it.
- **The next question should be easy**: After presenting the decision, end with a question that requires little effort.
- **People decide twice**: First in the mind as a picture, then in reality as action.
- **A script is not fake**: Prepared words create confidence when they are delivered with truthful intent.
- **Help people decide, do not trap them**: The method fails when it removes agency.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

### Exemplar 1: Rejection-Free Offer
- **Context**: Introducing an idea to someone who may reject it.
- **Verbatim contrast (transcript.txt, ~word 1,506)**: Jones draws the line between the pushy version — `"I'm not sure if it's for you, but would you like to buy X?"` — and the pulling version that follows the same preface with a softer offer. His own summary: "One is pushy and the other is pulley."
- **What makes this excellent**: It preserves the listener's autonomy while still moving the conversation forward — the preface only works, in Jones's words, if "you have to genuinely not be sure" (transcript.txt, ~word 1,506).

### Exemplar 2: Assumptive Schedule
- **Context**: The other person says they do not have time.
- **Verbatim (transcript.txt, ~word 4,538)**: "When would be a good time?" — introduced as the sixth of the 23 sequences, aimed directly at the "I don't have time" deflection.
- **What makes this excellent**: It routes around a yes/no refusal and moves the conversation straight to calendar specifics instead of re-litigating whether time exists at all.

### Exemplar 3: Conditional Close
- **Context**: The buyer says price is the issue.
- **Verbatim (transcript.txt, ~word 14,603)**: "If I can, will you?" — sequence #19, used when "your prospect or customer pushes back with reasons as to why they cannot do the thing you would like them to do."
- **What makes this excellent**: It diagnoses the objection before negotiating — you learn whether price is the real gap or a stated one before you concede anything.

## Signature Moves

- **Name the moment before choosing words**: Decide whether the barrier is timing, identity, imagination, objection, or close friction.
- **Soften before asking**: Give the listener a safe way to consider the idea.
- **Install the future picture**: Make the decision visible in their mind before asking them to act.
- **Move from hard answer to easy answer**: End with a small factual question after the bigger decision frame.
- **Recover information after no**: Use curiosity to learn what the no actually means. Jones frames curiosity itself as foundational: "curiosity is the fuel to great conversation. And it's more than an idea. It's a principle. It's one of our four cornerstones." (transcript.txt, ~word 13,686)

| Criterion | Score 4 - Passable | Score 7 - Strong | Score 10 - Phil-Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moment diagnosis | Names a generic barrier | Identifies the active decision state | Pinpoints the exact psychological job of the next words |
| Pressure control | Avoids obvious force | Preserves autonomy and rapport | Makes the listener feel safer while moving faster |
| Phrase fit | Uses a relevant phrase | Adapts phrase to context | Phrase, tone, timing, and next step all match |

## Anti-Patterns

- Using phrases as gimmicks instead of matched-intent tools — Jones is explicit that the method fails "without feeling like what you're doing is you're manipulating" (transcript.txt, ~word 9,537); "One is pushy and the other is pulley" (transcript.txt, ~word 1,547) is his own line for the difference.
- Stacking too many sequences in one conversation — Jones on choice overload: "if one option is too prescriptive and two options feels right and wrong and too many options feels overwhelming" (transcript.txt, ~word 7,557), the same overload logic applies to phrase-stacking.
- Asking yes/no questions when a next-step question is better — Jones distinguishes "How open-minded is a is an open-ended question, whereas a would you be open-minded is a is a closed question" (transcript.txt, ~word 1,927) and teaches choosing deliberately, not defaulting to closed.
- Fighting objections before understanding them — Jones: "To overcome an objection, you must first understand what an objection really is." (transcript.txt, ~word 13,154)
- Creating urgency that is not true — Jones on the "think about it" objection: "I'm not saying that people should feel rushed into decisions." (transcript.txt, ~word 19,138)
- Making the listener feel cornered — Jones: "People hate to feel manipulated and nearly always want to feel like they made the final decision." (transcript.txt, ~word 6,977)
- Explaining the method instead of producing the words — Jones: "So be careful of understanding the method behind the madness. the why behind the what." (transcript.txt, ~word 10,116)
