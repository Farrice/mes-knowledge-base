# Genius Patterns

## 1. Rejection-Free Introduction

Open an idea with enough permission that the listener can inspect it without defending against it.

## 2. Identity-Safe Agreement

Invite a person to consider change through an identity they already want to hold: open, thoughtful, proactive, responsible, curious.

## 3. Soft Certainty Challenge

Question what the listener knows or understands without making them wrong. Tone must be curious, not superior.

## 4. Future-Feeling Trigger

Move the decision into a future emotional state so the person weighs consequence, gain, or relief.

## 5. Mental Movie Installation

Create a concrete future picture before asking for action. People must see themselves doing it before they do it.

## 6. Assumptive Timing Frame

Replace whether with when. Move from permission to schedule when a next step is legitimate.

## 7. Face-Saving Follow-Up

Restart momentum by preserving dignity when someone has not acted.

## 8. One-Word Swap Control

Change the answer by changing the question shape: from yes/no gates to open completions and next-step prompts.

## 9. Three-Option Decision Design

Reduce overload by presenting three honest paths and asking which is easiest or best-fit.

## 10. Social Category Mirror

Show two types of people or two standards, then let the listener choose the identity they want to inhabit.

## 11. Likeness Bridge

Build trust through truthful similarity before making the ask.

## 12. Conditional Commitment Close

Test whether the stated barrier is real by asking what would happen if it could be solved.

## 13. Columbo Add-On

Use a final small ask after the main value has been delivered.

## 14. Curiosity Recovery

Turn no, delay, or vague resistance into useful information with a soft curiosity question.
