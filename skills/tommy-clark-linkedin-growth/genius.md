## Genius Patterns

## 1. The "How I" Narrative Pivot
**What They Do**: Anchoring a take to a specific anecdote.

**Executable Behavior**: Replace "how to" content formats with "how I" formats. Pair every single opinion or framework with a specific narrative example.

**Deploy When**: Drafting MoF and ToF content.

**Success Metric**: High engagement on posts; comments referencing the specific story told.

## 2. The Stealth Hook
**What They Do**: Camouflaging proven psychological hooks in conversational text.

**Executable Behavior**: Write the optimal formulaic hook. Then, rewrite it to sound like a casual slack message to a peer while retaining the underlying psychological levers.

**Deploy When**: The first 1-3 lines of any post.

**Success Metric**: Increased read-more clicks; absence of marketing fatigue responses.

## 3. Audience Design Architecture
**What They Do**: Forcibly injecting targeted users into the content flywheel.

**Executable Behavior**: Send exactly 20 targeted connection requests every weekday to a specific ICP. Leave exactly 5 high-quality comments daily on posts from overlapping ICP hubs.

**Deploy When**: Daily growth routine, establishing an account.

**Success Metric**: High-quality inbound profile views from exact target roles.

## 4. The Content Interview System
**What They Do**: Separating idea extraction from drafting.

**Executable Behavior**: Schedule a bi-weekly 45-minute recording session using pre-planned prompts, transcribe, and use as the sole source material for two weeks of drafting.

**Deploy When**: Scaling founder-led content.

**Success Metric**: 5 weekly posts generated from a 2-hour weekly time commitment.

## Hidden Knowledge

## 1. The AI Saturation Floor
**Tacit Insight**: "Valuable content" is no longer a differentiator; it is the baseline. The only moat is personal narrative and idiosyncratic voice.

**Why Others Miss This**: People are trying to out-prompt AI rather than recognizing that AI cannot invent legitimate personal history.

**Deploy When**: During content ideation to vet if a topic is too generic.

## 2. The "Wonky Algorithm" Bypass
**Tacit Insight**: Relying solely on organic distribution is a failure point. Manually connecting with the target audience forces the algorithm to serve content to those individuals.

**Why Others Miss This**: Founders often mistake LinkedIn for a purely public feed rather than a targeted ABM (Account-Based Marketing) distribution channel.

**Deploy When**: Determining the ROI equation of audience engagement vs. content creation.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

**Exemplar 1: The "How I" Narrative Funnel**

> "Back in 2021, I was stuck. My LinkedIn content felt like shouting into the void – generic 'tips' that got crickets. Then, I met Sarah, a founder who was doing *exactly* what I was doing, but getting 10x the engagement. Her secret? She stopped trying to be a guru and started telling her messy, real stories.
>
> I remember one post where she detailed the *exact* screw-up that almost killed her Series A round. No sugarcoating. Just the raw, frustrating truth of how she pivoted in 48 hours. That post alone netted her 5 high-quality investor intros. I copied her, told the story of how our initial product launch flopped, and suddenly, my inbox wasn't just filled with spam, but founders asking *how* I recovered. It wasn't about the 'what,' it was about the 'how I' survived."
>
> **What makes this excellent**: This exemplar demonstrates the "How I" Narrative Pivot by sharing a personal journey of discovery and a client's specific success story. The hook is conversational ("Back in 2021, I was stuck") and immediately relatable, bypassing marketing fatigue. It implicitly teaches the methodology by showing, not just telling, and provides tangible proof (5 investor intros, founders asking "how I recovered"). It's AI-immune because it's rooted in specific, personal experience.

**Exemplar 2: The Stealth Hook in Action**

> "Honestly, when my client, Mark, first showed me his 'LinkedIn strategy,' I almost choked on my coffee. He was posting daily, but it was all generic '5 tips for X.' The problem wasn't the advice; it was how he packaged it.
>
> We rewrote his next post's opening. Instead of 'Unlock the power of thought leadership,' it started with: 'I just watched a $50k deal evaporate because of one bland LinkedIn post. Here's what we changed to fix it (and close a $100k deal last week).'
>
> That single tweak? His 'read more' clicks jumped 300% and he closed that $100k deal within 7 days. Turns out, people prefer stories about losing money to generic 'power of X' any day."
>
> **What makes this excellent**: This example perfectly illustrates the "Stealth Hook" and "Proof Before Claim" anti-pattern. The opening ("I almost choked on my coffee") is casual and disarming. The hook itself ("I just watched a $50k deal evaporate...") uses negativity bias and specific numbers, but feels like a direct, candid observation rather than a formulaic marketing ploy. It immediately backs up the claim with a specific, high-value outcome ($100k deal), making the advice credible and actionable.

**Anti-Exemplar: Generic "Valuable Content" Output**

> "5 Ways Founders Can Leverage LinkedIn for Growth
>
> In today's competitive landscape, leveraging LinkedIn is crucial for founders looking to optimize their professional network and generate leads. Here are five actionable strategies:
> 1.  **Optimize Your Profile**: Ensure your profile is complete and highlights your expertise.
> 2.  **Consistent Content Creation**: Share valuable insights regularly to engage your audience.
> 3.  **Network Strategically**: Connect with relevant industry leaders and potential clients.
> 4.  **Engage with Others**: Comment on posts and participate in discussions to increase visibility.
> 5.  **Utilize LinkedIn Analytics**: Track your performance to refine your strategy."
>
> **What makes this mediocre**: This is a classic example of "AI Saturation Floor" content. It's generic, lacks any personal narrative ("How I" pivot), uses buzzwords ("leverage," "optimize"), and offers no specific proof or anecdotes. The hook is bland, and the advice could apply to almost any social media platform. It fails to attract a specific ICP and offers no unique insight, making it easily forgettable and indistinguishable from AI-generated content.

## Signature Moves

1.  **The Conversational Camouflage**: Before publishing any content, Tommy instinctively rewrites the first 1-3 lines to sound like a casual, slightly irreverent Slack message to a trusted peer, even if the underlying psychological hook is highly engineered. → **Deploy when**: Crafting the opening lines of any LinkedIn post.
2.  **Narrative Anchor First**: When presenting a new framework, strategy, or opinion, Tommy's first impulse is to immediately recall and share a specific, often vulnerable, personal anecdote or client story that directly illustrates the principle, rather than starting with abstract definitions. → **Deploy when**: Introducing any new concept or giving advice in content.
3.  **ICP GPS Activation**: The very first action on any new growth project (or daily routine for established ones) is to precisely define the target ICP (e.g., "Heads of Marketing at Series B SaaS companies") and then initiate the daily protocol of 20 targeted connection requests and 5 high-quality comments on relevant "hub" accounts. → **Deploy when**: Starting a new LinkedIn growth initiative or executing daily audience building.
4.  **Founder Voice Extraction**: When tasked with generating content for a founder, Tommy never asks them to "write content." Instead, he schedules a conversational, recorded interview, using pre-planned prompts to extract raw insights, stories, and takes, treating the founder as the source of "marble" to be sculpted. → **Deploy when**: Scaling content creation for busy founders.
5.  **Proof Before Policy**: Any claim, framework, or recommendation is immediately followed by a specific, quantifiable result or a detailed, real-world example of its application and outcome. Theory is always substantiated by practice. → **Deploy when**: Making any assertion or offering strategic advice within content.
