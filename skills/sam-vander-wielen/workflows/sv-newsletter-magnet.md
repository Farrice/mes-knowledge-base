# /sv-newsletter-magnet — The Newsletter IS the Lead Magnet

Produces a **weekly list-growth loop that requires no freebie**: the forecast post, the exclusivity/urgency framing, and the reverse voice-of-customer play that decides what you write.

Sam's shift: *"Just getting into this mindset that my email is good and everybody should get it. And I think that's the way to sell it. I don't need to necessarily always have a freebie as a buffer."* She still uses freebies sometimes — this isn't a ban, it's a removal of the assumption.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Load `genius.md`. One honest question first:

> **Is the newsletter actually good enough to be the offer?**

Nathan's diagnosis of why most creators can't run this play: *"Most people, when they say 'sign up for my newsletter, I write every week' — it's not a compelling call to action. What value do we get? And then honestly, the newsletter is not that good."*

If the newsletter isn't good, **say so** and route to quality work first. This play amplifies whatever exists; it does not substitute for it.

## Execution

### Step 1 — The two dials

Every growth post pulls one or both:

- **Exclusivity** — *"That's the only place I'm sharing this information."*
- **Urgency** — *"If you don't sign up now, you're not getting my thing on Tuesday, and it'll be gone."*

The insight that makes this necessary: *"We really have no urgency with getting people to take action to sign up for our newsletter unless you tell them."* A newsletter CTA without one of these dials is a wish.

### Step 2 — The weekly forecast post

The core repeatable asset. Sam's shape:

> "Hey guys, next week I'm talking about **[SPECIFIC TOPIC]** in my newsletter. In case you don't know, I've got this weekly newsletter called **[NAME]** that **[N] of your peers** get. Comment **[KEYWORD]** and get it now."

Components:
- **The specific topic or the problem it answers** — not "great content"
- **Named newsletter** — a title makes it a thing, not a habit
- **Social proof by peer count** — *"66,000 of your peers"*
- **A keyword CTA** (comment/DM) plus a link variant for ads
- Optionally a screenshot or teaser of the actual issue

Nathan's framing of why this works: it turns each week's issue into **a fresh lead magnet** — *"that's basically a lead magnet. Someone's like, 'Oh, I want this' — a very specific thing. And that's just this week's newsletter."*

### Step 3 — The high-value transparency variant

Sam's biggest signup spike came from promising the list something she'd share nowhere else:

> "On Tuesday, I'm giving **just the people on my email list** the financial breakdown of what I spent, how much money, what went into ads, what I think contributed, and why webinars aren't dead."

Result: *"a crazy amount of signups, and my book sold out on Amazon."* Deploy after any notable result — a launch, an experiment, a failure.

### Step 4 — Reverse voice-of-customer

Before writing an issue, ask the audience what they want to know about the topic, then write that.

> "Next week I'm writing about **[TOPIC]**. What do you want to know?"

Sam's payoff: *"What they wanted to know about was not what I initially intended to write about."* She planned to share impressive launch stats; they wanted to know **how someone could do this without a team.**

Dual use: growth mechanic **and** research. The replies are also raw ICP language.

### Step 5 — The compounding argument

Set expectations honestly. Nathan: *"Even if it's getting 10 or 20 subscribers, that compounds."* Sam adds the sharing effect — *"they refer other people… people will send people my Instagram story or the post."*

### Step 6 — The cadence rule

*"The day that the promo ends, the very next day I turn back to newsletter growth."* Put it on the calendar.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Context | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Forecast post as a text post; keyword CTA in comments (algorithm-safe); link in a comment or profile |
| **Instagram** | Story + feed post; comment-keyword automation; screenshot teaser performs |
| **B2B / exec audience** | Drop "hey guys"; lead with the specific question the issue answers |
| **Podcast** | Forecast in the episode's own ad slots — Sam runs her own products in her ad inventory |
| **Newsletter with no name** | Name it first. The play needs a proper noun. |
| **Substack / Parallax** | Forecast the issue on social; exclusivity dial is weaker (archive is public) — lean on urgency and the reverse-VoC play instead |

## Quality Gate

Reject and rebuild if:
- The newsletter isn't good and the play ships anyway
- A forecast post names no specific topic ("great stuff coming")
- Neither dial (exclusivity or urgency) is pulled
- The newsletter has no name
- The exclusivity claim isn't true (if the content also goes on the blog, don't claim list-only)
- Reverse-VoC is used as a growth gimmick with no intent to actually write what they ask for
