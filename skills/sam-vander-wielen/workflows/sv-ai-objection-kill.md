# /sv-ai-objection-kill — "Do You Know What to Put in the Contract?"

Produces the **complete objection-handling asset set** for the 2026 substitution objection: marketing angle, the teardown video outline, the one-line kill shot, and the AI-positive integration that stops you from over-rotating.

The objection, in her customers' words: *"Why should I purchase this from you if I can do it on ChatGPT?"*

## Pre-Flight Gate

Load `genius.md`. Establish the **prerequisite insight** before writing anything:

> **What is the constant doubt your buyer has, independent of what the current substitute is?**

Sam's: *"My customer always has a seed of doubt in the back of their mind if they have not done this completely legitimately."* Before AI, the substitute was a friend's borrowed contract, or copying a stranger's privacy policy — *"but wait, what if Nathan took it from somebody else? What if Nathan never looked at his privacy policy? How do we even know Nathan's is good?"*

**The doubt is the constant. The substitute changes.** If you can't name the constant doubt, you can't run this play — you'll end up arguing about AI capability, which is a losing and dating argument.

## Execution

### Step 1 — Name the constant doubt

Write it in one sentence, substitute-independent. This becomes the spine of all the marketing, not just the objection response.

### Step 2 — Find the competence gap

The core reframe: **the buyer cannot evaluate the AI's output because evaluating it requires the expertise they're trying to buy.**

Ask: *what would the buyer need to already know in order to tell whether the AI's output is good?* If the answer is "the thing we sell," the play works. If a competent amateur could verify the output, **this play does not apply** — say so and route to a different differentiation angle.

### Step 3 — The teardown video

Sam's highest-leverage asset. Structure:

1. Prompt the AI, on camera, for the thing you sell
2. Show the output as-is
3. **Pull it apart** — name what's missing, clause by clause
4. Name the concrete scenarios that leave them uncovered
5. Show what it doesn't understand about *their specific industry* — Sam: *"it obviously doesn't understand our industry, the specifics of our industry, how we're creating content, what podcast stuff we're doing"*

Expect hate in the comments. **The hate is the setup.**

### Step 4 — The kill shot

The predictable critique is *"your prompt was bad — you should have told it everything that needs to be in there."* The response is one question:

> **"Do you know what things to put in the [DELIVERABLE]?"**

Then the follow-through:

> *"That's the point. You don't know what you don't know. You don't know what to tell it. Unless you're a [EXPERT], you don't know all the [PROVISIONS] that need to be in it. And so you're always going to be sitting there looking at it saying, 'Is this enough?' — and you're going to be right where you are right now."*

*"And that pretty much stopped that conversation."*

**Why it lands**: it converts the critic's own argument into evidence for the thesis. Never argue AI is incapable — demonstrate the buyer can't direct it.

### Step 5 — The do-not-banish rule (mandatory)

Sam explicitly refuses to become an anti-AI brand. She added AI guidance **inside her own product**: *"I actually wrote the prompts for them in the little description boxes under the video, so that I'm not saying to them like it's banished."*

Her diagnosis of the failure mode: *"I think people get way too caught up in trying to convince people — it's like an ego thing of like, now I need to convince you why I'm irreplaceable, so I'll start creating all this content about how stupid AI is."* And: *"My customer's probably pretty skeptical, but they're using it for other things — productivity, systematizing — and it's great."*

**Deliverable requirement: name at least two places where you actively recommend AI to your buyer.** Without this the asset set is incomplete.

### Step 6 — Stack the non-substitutable

What the substitute structurally cannot provide: support, updates, community, customization guidance, and *"you get it from somebody who's built a business like the one you're trying to build."*

Sam's practical test: can they have the *conversation*? *"If they have a client come back and say 'I don't understand this part, can we change it, can I delete this?' — are you going to go back to ChatGPT and ask it? You need to be able to have the conversation."*

### Step 7 — Cede the other buyer

Some buyers genuinely want fast-and-cheap-and-riskier, and a competitor should serve them. Sam: *"Great for them."* Naming this makes the whole position credible. Full treatment in `/sv-customer-personality-lock`.

## ⚠️ Factual Floor

Sam makes two legal-adjacent claims in the source — about AI chat logs being discoverable and about lawyers sanctioned for fabricated citations. Both are **UNCONFIRMED as stated**: she names no case. See `source-ledger.md` claims 34–35.

**Never reproduce them as fact.** The mechanic in Step 4 requires no legal claim at all, which is precisely why it's the durable asset.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Context | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| **Buyer CAN verify AI output** | This play does not apply. Say so; differentiate on speed, support, or taste instead |
| **Technical/dev audience** | The competence-gap argument is weaker (they can evaluate); pivot to accountability and maintenance |
| **Regulated vertical** | Teardown must avoid specific legal/medical/financial assertions; show omissions, not consequences |
| **Creative services** | Gap becomes taste and judgment rather than provisions; teardown shows what the output *doesn't notice* |
| **Low-ticket** | Skip the teardown video; the one-line kill shot in FAQ copy is enough |

## Quality Gate

Reject and rebuild if:
- The constant doubt isn't named substitute-independently
- The competence-gap test wasn't run (or was run and failed, and the play shipped anyway)
- The asset argues AI is *bad* rather than that the buyer can't direct it
- **The do-not-banish integration is missing** — this is the difference between a position and a tantrum
- Any UNCONFIRMED legal claim from the source appears as fact
- No buyer is ceded — a position that serves everyone convinces no one
