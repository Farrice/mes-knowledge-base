## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These twelve patterns are reflexes, not a checklist. Absorb two things until they're automatic: the stuck agent's problem is *audience*, not consistency, and specificity is *distribution*, not lost reach. Then build the specific post for the specific agent in front of you.

**Recognition test**: would Alyssa Stalker recognize this as a post she'd put in a Coffee & Contracts drop or on her own grid — or as an agent using her words about "niching down" while still posting for everyone? If the second, rebuild from the person, not from the pattern names.

Specifically:
- Never announce the machinery. No "applying the Topic + Who + Lens formula." Append the who, deliver the hook.
- Her register is casual, warm, a little self-deprecating ("literally one person wanted my lead magnet the day before" [18:53–18:57]). If the output reads like an agency deck ("optimize your content pillars"), it has failed.
- Feeling before solution. A comfort post that opens with the offer is a transactional post in a comfort costume.
- Lo-fi is the point. If a spec calls for motion graphics, a voiceover, and a template, it has drifted from the source: "someone took a picture in their camera roll and slapped some text on it" [37:14–37:18].
- Polish is the tell. Keep the spoken roughness — fragments, asides, one exclamation earned by content.

## The Stalker Operating System

One thesis: **stuck agents don't have a posting problem, they have an audience problem.** "Most of the time it's not a consistency problem... they really aren't speaking to anyone specific" [02:44–02:57]. The 2026 fix is mechanical because the platform now rewards it: Instagram tests content by interest for roughly a day after one search, so "the more specific you are, the more likely now you are to land in front of the right people. You don't have to fight for those views as hard" [12:58–13:05].

The path: **read your data → pick one person → frame every local post as topic + who + lens → make that person feel seen → mix local / listing / authority by goal → run one thing long enough to learn.**

## Genius Patterns (12)

### Pattern 1: "It's Just Data" — Outlier Audit First
**What it is**: Before any prescription, scan the last six months for the post that broke the flatline. "Are there any that stood out that had anything that took off? What are the attributes about them? That could be a leading indicator of a starting point" [02:34–02:41].
**Worked examples**: Eric's "AI won't replace real estate agents, but agents who use AI will replace those who don't" pulled ~800 comments and the most follows of his year; he moved to "70% more talking head yap style" voicing heavy opinions [03:43–04:20]. Alyssa's repeatable carousels outperformed everything for 18 months; she "just kept running with it" and went from ~10,000 to almost 15,000 [16:25–17:12].
**Why it's genius**: It replaces "what should I post" with "what did my audience already tell me." And the outlier can become a bit — Eric's line "has been used now like as a joke in all of my comments moving forward" [04:03–04:08].
**Deploy when**: any "stuck at N followers / N views" complaint.

### Pattern 2: Person, Not Demographic
**What it is**: "Upsizer or downsizer" is "too broad" [05:09–05:23]. Ask where they eat, where they hang out, what their Friday looks like: "playing pickleball on a Friday and joining a social club or... somebody who loves to couch rot on the weekend and have a beautiful living room all to themselves" [07:50–08:01].
**The rule**: "You have to start with one person to get to that point" [08:23–08:26] — the point where people call because they like you.
**Deploy when**: the agent says "I'll work with anybody" [08:10–08:11].

### Pattern 3: The One-Slide Reverse-Engineer
**What it is**: Compress the message to a single image post plus caption. "That's almost how you can reverse engineer who you're speaking to because you kind of got to fit everything into this one spot" [06:33–06:38].
**Context**: carousels outperform reels for her "by far"; "single image posts are back"; the 2026 look is "butter yellow basic fonts... one message to one person" [05:44–06:08].
**Deploy when**: a topic keeps needing seven slides to land.

### Pattern 4: Topic + Who + Lens — The Hook Grammar
**What it is**: The cheapest fix in the source. "Three things to do this weekend in city name" is broad; "three things to do this weekend in city name that you've probably never heard of, especially if you're a mom of two" adds "framing of who this is for" [12:12–12:32]. Eric's echo: "the three best things to do this weekend in Spokane if you're a mom with kids under five" [14:04–14:10].
**Three parts**: the topic; a who-it's-for clause; the agent's take ("that you've never heard of" = insider lens). "Specific to who you want to reach and has your personal point of view, your take or your framing or who you are attached to it" [13:21–13:29].
**Extreme form**: Gabe Einhorn's "if you're watching this video on August 15th or 16th" [14:46–14:50] — date as targeting; Eric calls it "a cheap way to game the algorithm" that still produces the "do they have a camera in my room" feeling [14:51–15:13].
**Fair-housing layer (system addition)**: when the post touches housing, frame by life situation or interest (renter, relocating, dog parent, midnight-Zillow scroller), not protected class.
**Deploy when**: any hook that reads like a listicle title.

### Pattern 5: Specificity Is Distribution — The 24-Hour Interest Test
**What she knows**: "I could be looking up one thing one time... and now for the next 24 hours it's testing similar type of content in front of me... it's really an even playing field to get in front of people" [12:38–12:57].
**Why it's genius**: It kills the TAM instinct. Eric: "you think your total addressable market will be bigger if you just keep it broad... but that is so broad that no one actually cares" [14:14–14:25].
**Deploy when**: an agent resists narrowing.

### Pattern 6: The Personal-Lens Inventory
**What it is**: "It's not what does the audience want to see, but also what does the audience need to know about me in order to like me, trust me, and then work with me" [09:32–09:40].
**Worked example**: Eric's background — Star Wars, USC, baseball cards, "books I've never read," the book he wrote, Yankees; the new baby and sports in stories; a bet at the end of every broadcast-channel post — "little hints of my personality that make people connect with the brand on a deeper level" [08:47–09:25].
**Alyssa's rule**: "sharing enough of yourself to where people feel like they get to know you" [09:50–09:52].
**Deploy when**: content is all "for them" and none "about you."

### Pattern 7: Comfort Creator — Connection Over Transaction
**What it is**: "Nobody comes to my account for tips anymore... they follow along my content because they actually feel seen" [16:35–16:43]. The term: "being a comfort creator" [21:10]. "It's connection content versus transactional content" [21:38–21:40].
**Her hooks (verbatim)**:
> "It's crazy what an 'I found you on Instagram' can do for my mental health as a real estate agent." [18:16–18:22]

> "People only post their wins on social. So here are some of my recent flops." [18:39–18:42]

> "Self-care as a realtor — I don't have to earn my rest." [19:05–19:09]

**Why it works**: "A lot of people are always yelling at real estate agents about what to do... I want people to feel comfort, or laughter, or yap when they come to my account... a space where they feel like they can grow without pressure" [19:15–19:32].
**Deploy when**: the audience is over-lectured and under-recognized.

### Pattern 8: The Consumer Flip — "You're Not Behind. That's Exactly Who I Help."
**What it is**: Comfort content for buyers. "Self-care as someone in their mid-30s who's yet to buy a house... take care of their mindset... start the process... in the end you're offering them a solution by saying, 'Don't worry, you're not behind.' Lots of people are like that and guess what? That's exactly who I help" [20:10–20:51].
**Structure**: hook names the private state → "that's me, I'll keep swiping" → cheeky or supportive middle → solution LAST, as permission.
**Contrast**: "That does so much better than FOMO or rent shaming or 'you have to do this now before school starts'" [20:52–20:59].
**Deploy when**: any consumer-facing carousel for a hesitant buyer.

### Pattern 9: Create-Mode Text Post — The Ricky Carruth Format
**What it is**: All-black background, text only, made in Instagram create mode. Eric reads three aloud, ~11,000 and ~12,000 likes [22:04–22:38] (full text in `references/comfort-content-exemplars.md`).
**Production**: "If you have an idea, just one thing, tweet it out, screenshot it, and then post it" [23:09–23:13].
**Why it works**: "really easy for an agent to share and kind of feel like, hey, at least someone kind of sees me" [22:49–22:53].
**Deploy when**: one true sentence exists and no production time does.

### Pattern 10: The Share-to-Stories Test
**What it is**: "Would a real estate agent want to share this to their stories or repost this so that their potential clients see it and it would make them look good" [23:36–23:46]. "The reason my content gets shared a lot by other agents is because I'm actually helping positioning them" [23:48–23:53].
**Consumer version**: "meeting them where they're at, describing the feeling, and helping them connect with it" [24:19–24:23].
**Eric's operationalization**: "What is the action someone's going to take with this? Share in the DMs? Share to their story? Comment? Or is this just blasting them with information?" [24:28–24:42].
**Deploy when**: judging any comfort or authority piece before it ships.

### Pattern 11: Goal-Tagged Content Mix — Local / Listing / Authority
**What it is**: Coffee & Contracts' mix: "mostly local content, listing content because you got to show what it is that you sell, and then a little bit of authority building content and that's the smallest percentage" [25:59–26:10]. "Mostly for them with a little bit about you" [26:22–26:25].
**Goal map**: grow → local, "the fastest way to attract new followers" [26:47–26:51]; convert → listing [26:53–26:57]; nurture → authority [26:59–27:01]. "Every post has a different goal... you're not going to get a ton of likes on authority-building nurture style content" [27:04–27:10]. Authority "once every month or something" [28:04–28:06]; it "hits them in their head" even when no one shares it [28:27–28:34].
**Deploy when**: reviewing any calendar or any "this post flopped" complaint.

### Pattern 12: Authority as Story, Never Billboard
**Review**: "take one line out of the review, find the hook line, make that the hook of a post, and then break down the story of the transaction and why they said that about you" [30:52–31:04].
**Award**: green-screen the article or make it "look like a news-based announcement... then tell a story about what you did differently to get there" [29:19–29:29]. "You want to know how I got on this list? Well, let me tell you" [29:49–29:51].
**Breaking-news frame**: Tatiana Londono's "millionaire real estate agent gives her three social media tips" → "you won't believe what this client said about her agent," then swipe to the review [32:11–32:32].
**Capture timing**: "do it when they're happy at closing time... when you're handing them the keys" [32:44–32:52]; pull the phone, ask questions, clip later [33:05–33:17].
**Comedic variant**: ask one question, cut the answer to a different question (Katie Day / Tom Ferry room) [33:20–34:02].
**Deploy when**: a review, ranking, award, or closing lands.

## Hidden Knowledge

- **The misdiagnosis**: stuck agents blame consistency; the real gap is "they aren't speaking to anyone specific" [02:44–02:57].
- **Narrow buys reach**: interest-testing distribution rewards specificity [12:35–13:05].
- **Reshare is the engine**: comfort content spreads because the reader uses it to position themselves to their own audience [23:36–23:53].
- **Authority is memory, not engagement**: "no one's going to share that post, but... it hits them in their head" [28:27–28:34].
- **Reviews are invisible to non-buyers**: "swipe, it's irrelevant to me" — disguise them as normal content [31:11–31:44].
- **One-slide test = niche finder** [06:29–06:38].
- **An outlier can become a recurring bit** [04:03–04:08].

## Decision Heuristics (when X → do Y because Z)

- When an agent is stuck → audit six months for the outlier before touching format, because "they keep repeating the same things and they're not learning from what they're posting, which is actually the definition of insanity" [02:09–02:17].
- When a hook reads "3 things to do in [city]" → append who it's for and your take, because the algorithm "is testing similar type of content" by interest and broad hooks land nowhere [12:12–13:05].
- When the audience is over-lectured → lead with the feeling, not the tip, because "they follow along... because they actually feel seen" [16:41–16:43].
- When writing for buyers who feel behind → hold the offer to the last slide as permission, because "'you're not behind... that's exactly who I help' does so much better than FOMO or rent shaming" [20:41–20:59].
- When a review arrives → pull the hook line and tell the transaction story, because a screenshot is "swipe, it's irrelevant to me" to anyone not hiring [30:52–31:31].
- When an authority post gets few likes → check its goal tag before calling it a flop, because "every post has a different goal" [27:04–27:06].
- When five ideas compete → run one until it yields data, because "you don't really form a strategy when you're trying like five different things at once" [38:33–38:39].
- When using AI → reframe an existing hook "for your audience, for your ideal client, from your point of view," never generate the post, because "people want real" [36:21–37:06].

## Anti-Patterns (what she would never ship)

1. **The billboard grid** — "just sold, just listed style content... your awards and just you... an entire billboard about yourself when someone comes to your grid" [34:30–34:39]. Source: Alyssa, 2026-08-20.
2. **The broad local hook** — "three things to do this weekend in city name... that's very broad" [12:12–12:17]. Source: Alyssa, 2026-08-20.
3. **The ChatGPT stat dump** — "that chat GPT script first-time buyer education or... five different statistics that no one really cares about" [24:43–24:57]. Source: Eric, 2026-08-20.
4. **The screenshot review** — "post a screenshot of a review of Zillow, Google review and it looks awful" [30:00–30:06]; "swipe, it's irrelevant to me" [31:28–31:31]. Source: Eric/Alyssa, 2026-08-20.
5. **FOMO and rent-shaming** — "FOMO or rent shaming or 'you have to do this now before school starts.' That sort of content doesn't really work" [20:52–21:01]. Source: Eric, 2026-08-20.
6. **AI-written posts** — "I don't like AI written content. I don't like AI generated content... people are tired of it" [36:45–37:04]. Source: Alyssa, 2026-08-20.
7. **Five experiments at once** — "be careful of spreading yourself too thin, trying too many different ideas" [35:48–35:52]. Source: Alyssa, 2026-08-20.
8. **Look-at-me-only accounts** — "'Hey, look at me. Look at me. Look at me. Here's a new listing. Here's a new award'... that becomes nauseating" [27:54–28:01]. Source: Eric, 2026-08-20.
9. **Over-produced captions** — "three years ago was the highly edited kind of Hormozi style where you had to pay someone to do captions. You no longer have to do that" [10:09–10:16]. Source: Eric, 2026-08-20.

## Signature Moves

- **Breathe, then grid** → opens with "it's just data" and a six-month scan [01:58–02:44]. Deploy on any stuck complaint.
- **Append the who** → no local hook ships without a who-clause and a lens [12:12–13:29]. Deploy on listicle-shaped hooks.
- **Run the share test** → "would an agent share this to their stories to look good?" [23:36–23:46]. Deploy before any comfort or authority post.
- **Feeling first, offer last** → private state in the hook, solution on the final slide [20:10–20:51]. Deploy on consumer carousels.
- **Tag the goal** → grow / convert / nurture before reading numbers [26:45–27:35]. Deploy in every review.
- **Pull the hook line** → one sentence from the review becomes the hook [30:52–31:04]. Deploy when a testimonial lands.
- **Phone at the keys** → film at handoff, not weeks later [32:44–33:17]. Deploy on closing day.

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4 — Acceptable ("the template drop") | 7 — Good ("the tuned template") | 10 — Savant ("the camera in my room") |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience specificity | Names a segment | Names a life stage + habit | One person you could picture at one table |
| Hook framing | Topic only | Topic + who | Topic + who + lens in one breath |
| Feel-seen factor | Gives a tip | Names a shared struggle | Names the private feeling nobody says out loud |
| Shareability | Informative | Reader sends it to one friend | Reader reshares because it positions THEM |
| Lo-fi authenticity | Clean template | Camera-roll photo + text, one message | Reads like a tweet screenshot; unmistakably human |
| Goal clarity | A goal exists somewhere | Goal stated | Goal stated and the like-count expectation pre-declared |
| Opinion / lens | Reports | Adds "here's what I think" | Stands for something the audience would defend for you |
