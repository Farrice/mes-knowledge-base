# Reference — The Four Money Formats (Mechanics)

Every format here **demonstrates expertise on screen**. That is the non-negotiable. A format that prints views without it is rejected (see Conversion Gate in `genius.md`).

The selection logic: match the format to (a) the skill being sold and (b) the conversion register (relatable for low-ticket, premium/filtering for high-ticket). Most businesses should test all four and keep the one that passes the 2x test AND fits the operator's personality.

---

## Format 1 — Reaction Videos

**Mechanism:** Continuation of the scroll, not interruption. The viewer is already on the platform to watch videos; reacting to one continues their flow. The *original* clip keeps them watching — you sneak expertise in while their attention is on someone else, and they can't scroll without missing what they came for.

**The build:**
1. **Pick a source clip that opens with conflict/tension** (inherently a strong hook). For Pack Leader Dogs: dog-conflict clips.
2. **Film at 45°** — advisor on the same side of the table, not authority across it.
3. **One full watch**, then **open the talking head with a bold claim** to re-hook.
4. **Label concepts/jargon, not entertainment** — show your work. This is the difference between Sky (150 sales calls) and Adam (50M views, low conversion).
5. **Break it down frame-by-frame** (iPad + pencil for visual formats). *Share*, don't teach: "I want to show you this," never "you should."

**Length rule:** None. The video runs as long as it takes to satiate the avatar's appetite. Reaction breakdowns can run long because the source is viral AND the breakdown adds value.

**Pass test:** *Did the viewer learn something only someone with my expertise could teach?* If no → it's entertainment.

**Best for:** Broad niches; relatable/low-ticket conversion; operators who think out loud well.

## Format 2 — Step-by-Step Process Breakdowns

**Mechanism:** The most direct proof you can do the thing. The viewer watches you do paid consulting for free. By the 3rd one they've watched, they feel they already know how you'd handle their account before booking.

**The build:**
1. **Pick ONE specific deliverable** you create for a client.
2. **Open with a before/after hook** or a conceptual map you made yourself (Sam O'Leary: a category map of Yeti's ads).
3. **Walk it step-by-step on screen with REAL artifacts** — Google Docs, ad accounts, edits, spreadsheets. No green-screen abstraction.
4. **Don't water it down.** Hold nothing back. "There's no place to hide" — if it's true it resonates; if it's AI-slop nobody watches.

**Visual-activity match:** The visual must match the activity. Pull up the actual ads, the actual doc. The viewer reads "expert at work" from the visual matching the claimed activity.

**Best for:** Service businesses with a demonstrable deliverable (ads, copy, design, training, landscaping). Works in any niche: dog trainer → how I train a breed; copywriter → break down a high-converting sales page you wrote.

## Format 3 — Authority Hijacking Clips

**Mechanism:** Borrow a bigger creator/authority's credibility as the hook. Framing transfer — "if [revered figure] said it, it must be true." A new creator in the feed gets ignored; an in-group authority's words carry credibility you then inherit.

**The build:**
1. **Find the clip with a search string:** `[famous authority name] + [your niche keyword]` (e.g., "Jordan Belfort sales," "Kevin O'Leary entrepreneurship"). The clip already exists.
2. **Use the authority's statement as the hook**, then pause and introduce yourself as the person who does/started this (Musa: famous streamer talks clipper pay → "I'm the guy who started this model").
3. **Or inject a framing keyword** the avatar reveres directly into your own content (the "Citadel" lever — one word, 50K→600K, no production change).

**Key insight:** Framing > production. When the frame is strong, the script and edit can be loose. Find the single prestige/aspiration keyword in your niche and weaponize it.

**Best for:** Niches with recognizable authority figures or prestige vectors; new creators with no proof of their own yet (borrow it).

## Format 4 — Live Strategy Sessions

**Mechanism:** The "streamer" of business content. (Mr. Beast's Sidemen anecdote: shorts creators got little applause, YouTubers more, streamers standing ovations — because audiences spent real unpolished time with them.) Talking heads and process breakdowns are saturated; polished now reads as *fake*. Unpolished + concrete outcome + sounding smart in real time = "real person who knows their stuff."

**The build:**
1. **Pick a very specific question** a real client/ideal prospect asked.
2. **Solve it live, raw, unpolished** — show your process in real time. The buyer's real question is "can this person help someone just like me?"
3. **For the B-roll variant** (Nosh's 43M-view egg-timer build): break your process step-by-step, record B-roll of you doing it, voiceover on top, open with a relatable/skit hook.

**Cautionary (Nosh):** She demonstrated expertise but attached NO authority or offer → the 43M views produced nothing. Always pair the demonstration with positioning + an offer.

**Best for:** High-ticket conversion; filtering for serious buyers; operators comfortable being unscripted. Move fast — this format is newer and less saturated (first-mover window).

## Selection Matrix

| If the business… | Lead with | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Sells a visible deliverable | Process Breakdown | Direct proof, "free trial" of expertise |
| Is in a broad niche, low-ticket | Reaction | Relatable, leeches viral attention |
| Has no proof of its own yet | Authority Hijack | Borrow credibility while you build yours |
| Sells high-ticket / needs to filter | Live Strategy Session | Unpolished real-time work reads as genuinely expert |

**Always:** test all four (the 2x/4-video test), keep the one that converts AND fits the operator, then saturate.
