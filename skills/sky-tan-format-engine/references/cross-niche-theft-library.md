# Reference — Cross-Niche Format Theft & The Build Process

The winning format is almost never invented. It's **a legacy format, stolen from an adjacent niche, twisted to demonstrate YOUR expertise, deployed before your niche copies it.**

---

## Why You Steal From OUTSIDE Your Niche

- Copy inside your niche → the first-mover already owns it; the audience is saturated; you're invisible.
- Steal from outside → you're the **first mover** in your niche on that format. First-mover advantage = the single most important content skill (spotting the pattern before it blows up locally).

Legacy formats never die: **talking head, reaction, before/after.** The win is `legacy format × who you're uniquely positioned to be`. Sky took Adam's entertainment-reaction format and twisted it into jargon-labeling (authority) for the content-strategy niche.

---

## The 4 Build Steps (do ALL before filming one second)

Skipping the build = filming 10 videos, inconsistent results, no idea what to adjust because the format was never locked.

### Step 1 — List everything you do for yourself/clients
Write down *every* skill, activity, and part of your process — not just the flashy parts. (Sky's list: market research, avatar work, brand/positioning, content ideation, doom-scrolling for research, scripting, editing feedback, team ops.) Pick the activity that targets your avatar's **biggest pain** in a fresh way (Sky picked doom-scrolling because "coming up with ideas/hooks" is his niche's #1 pain).

### Step 2 — Pair each activity with its on-camera visual
Every activity has a visual. Writing hooks = laptop typing. B-roll = behind the camera. Ideating = phone scrolling. **The visual must match the activity so it demonstrates expertise** — but it can be faked: Sky read jargon off Apple Notes and wasn't even watching the clip; the editor cut it. The viewer reads "expert at work" from the match, not the authenticity.

### Step 3 — Structure it so it's repeatable
A format only becomes a format when the visual + script follow the **same pattern every time.** Recognition builds following ("oh, that's the stop-start guy / the hook-breakdown guy"). A locked structure lets you script 5–15 videos in the time it takes others to script one. Sky's locked structure: play clip → whenever a meaningful comment lands, make it → repeat.

### Step 4 — Stack all three hooks
- **Visual hook** — first frame the eye sees (borrow the reacted clip's hook).
- **Verbal hook** — first words spoken (sometimes none, to let the reaction play).
- **Written hook** — caption/on-screen text (Sky: "content strategist" + the view count — authority + relevance to his audience).

All three fire within ~1 second, same direction. Viewers subconsciously categorize the video as "worth it" or "waste" from patterns across thousands of prior videos — so frame one decides everything.

## The Validation & Defense Layer

### The 2x / 4-Video Test
Post 4 videos, same format. Average their views. **avg ≥ 2x baseline → keep going. < 2x → fix execution or it's not your format.** Either way you now have data. Low views = data, not failure; the discipline to keep posting the same format is what separates finders from the stuck.

### Saturation Blitz (copy-proof moat)
Once 3–4 videos confirm: record/script/edit ~6/day across main + trial-reel feeds. Saturate before a competitor's first copycat ships. The format attaches to your face → every copycat becomes your social proof. Sky's two confirmation signals: (1) view growth *kept climbing* past day 1, (2) growth *accelerated* vs prior content.

### Evolution (hold core, vary execution)
Formats decay as audience appetite satiates. Keep the **core constant**, vary **execution**: when appetite for one reaction-target exhausts, switch the target (ask "who else does my avatar watch?"), not the format. Volume → feedback → know what attracts vs what converts.

## Search Strings & Hunting Patterns

| Goal | Method |
|---|---|
| Find outlier formats cross-niche | Outlier tool (e.g., Sandcastles): 2–3x+ outliers, last 6 months, sort by outlier score, English only |
| Find authority-hijack clips | `[authority name] + [niche keyword]` (e.g., "Jordan Belfort sales") |
| Find your own format | The 4 build steps — your daily process IS the source |
| Map competitors for counter-positioning | List 20–40, catalog topics/hooks/formats, find the empty axis |

## Worked Example — Sky's Own Format Discovery
Adam's reaction-labeling format was ripping (~1M/video) in an adjacent space → Sky already doom-scrolled and labeled videos in his head as part of his job → he vocalized that, kept Adam's posture/position/edit style, but labeled **content-strategy jargon** + added **view count** (what his audience cares about) → ran the 2x test (15K → 100K) → blitzed 6/day → format became *his*. He invented nothing; he ported + twisted + tested + saturated.
