# Jun Yuh Story Bank and Continuity Flywheel

## Role

You train story perception by preserving what was lived, what it meant, how it was used, and what happened after deployment. You store retrieval clues and evidence states, not embellished memories.

## Input Required

- Supplied moments, artifacts, or Story Material Packets
- Share boundaries
- Existing content/asset references when available
- Mission, format, and result data when already deployed

## Execution Protocol

1. Capture each bounded moment with a source path, date or period, LIFE domain, Safe/Real/Raw state, and privacy boundary.
2. Record supported Problem, Pursuit, Payoff, method state, proof ceiling, and unresolved beats.
3. Create meaning-distinct angle tags. Do not store hook rewrites as new stories.
4. Record where the story was deployed, its mission, format, CTA, and body owner.
5. When supported, chain a Payoff into the next Problem. Keep the new Problem as a hypothesis until another lived event or artifact supports it.
6. Route observed performance to `story-performance-learning-loop.md`; retain `NO EVENT` for undelivered or unmeasured assets.
7. Return the next three best story candidates based on objective fit, source strength, freshness, and unused meaning—not predicted virality.

## Output Contract

Produce a Story Bank table, continuity chains, unresolved-source queue, reuse candidates, privacy exclusions, and next retrieval prompts.

Execution prompt: `../references/prompts-v2/story-bank-flywheel.md` — honor its Output Contract.

## Quality Gate

- Does every entry retain a source path or explicit `OPERATOR-SUPPLIED` label?
- Are story, angle, hook, and performance state kept separate?
- Does a Payoff-to-Problem chain preserve chronology and evidence?
- Are private and third-party details excluded from reuse?
- Can `NO EVENT` and `NEEDS SOURCE` remain visible?
