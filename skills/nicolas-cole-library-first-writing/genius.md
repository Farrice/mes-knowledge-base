## How to Use This Skill

Cole's five formats are not a checklist. Absorb the progression: compress the thought, practice the integrated craft, add visual sequencing when the idea fits, expand proven material into a long-form anchor, then harvest a repeatable process into an asset. The output should feel commercially useful and plainspoken, not like a content-strategy matrix for its own sake.

The recognition test is simple: did the run make the library more valuable, or did it only manufacture more posts?

## Genius Patterns

### The Library Unit Comes Before the Platform

Preserve the reusable material—claim, proof, story, mechanism, and permissions—before selecting a container. A post is one assembly of the unit, not the unit itself.

### Two Clocks

Craft principles and distribution signals decay at different speeds. Keep compression, format fit, taste ownership, and save-back durable. Date-stamp platform rankings and performance claims.

### The Format Ladder

- **Short post:** tests compression and memorability.
- **Atomic essay:** practices headline, hook, structure, rhythm, and scanning in one small container.
- **Carousel brief:** adds visual sequencing, slide constraints, and taste ownership.
- **Article:** becomes the nuanced long-form leverage hub.
- **Prompt candidate:** packages a proven, articulated task for personal, marketing, or product use.

This is a dependency ladder, not a mandatory sequence for every idea.

### One-Rung Advancement

Choose the format that adds the next needed capability. A beginner may need an atomic essay even when a carousel has stronger reach. A mature library unit may deserve an article without being rewritten as a tweet first.

### Idea-Format Fit

Classify the thought before production:

| Idea shape | Strong default |
|---|---|
| One sharp contrast or observation | Short post |
| Compact lesson needing integrated practice | Atomic essay |
| List, steps, mistakes, prompts, visual comparison | Carousel brief |
| Nuanced argument, story, framework, or anchor | Article |
| Repeated task with clear inputs and a proven quality test | Prompt candidate |

### Bounded Multiplication

Reuse is selective. Produce one primary asset and at most two derivatives unless the user explicitly wants a full content pack. Each derivative must perform a different reader or business job.

### Taste Ownership

AI may assist with assembly. The operator or fitting craft owner owns brand coherence, visual selection, exceptions, and final taste. File creation is not a visual PASS.

### Article as Leverage Hub

Promote an idea to long-form when it has enough proof or nuance to support reuse. Articles may feed newsletters, chapters, scripts, short posts, and product assets, but only justified derivatives should be made.

### Articulate Before Automate

A prompt candidate requires a manual method, clear inputs, a bounded task, an observable quality test, and at least one internal run. Automation is downstream of competence.

### Triple-Leverage Asset

A proven prompt can serve three roles:

1. personal leverage—faster or more consistent execution;
2. marketing leverage—a useful free asset with an honest proof claim;
3. business leverage—a standalone or bundled product component.

Choose the role supported by results and permissions; do not assume all three.

### Save-Back Compounding

Every finished asset updates the library unit with stronger language, proof, examples, reusable blocks, performance data, and failed hypotheses. Without save-back, reuse becomes duplication rather than learning.

## Hidden Knowledge

- The source's premise about shifting platforms logically forbids hard-coding its 2026 recommendations as timeless rules.
- A format may be worth using for skill acquisition even when it is not the best distribution bet.
- The content library is a decision system, not a folder of finished posts.
- Prompt products are byproducts of demonstrated work, not speculative prompt packs.
- Multiplying a weak claim increases claim risk and cleanup cost.
- Visual difficulty can be a moat only when the operator is willing to develop taste, not merely automate production.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **Omnichannel spray:** one idea becomes twelve generic posts with no format rationale.
2. **Algorithm fossil:** a dated platform recommendation becomes a permanent workflow rule.
3. **Orphan output:** a post ships without updating the source unit or performance evidence.
4. **Prompt-first product:** an untested task becomes a lead magnet or product because prompts are fashionable.
5. **Visual false green:** a rendered carousel is called excellent without human or craft review.
6. **Proof multiplication:** one unverified claim is repeated across a thread, carousel, article, and newsletter.
