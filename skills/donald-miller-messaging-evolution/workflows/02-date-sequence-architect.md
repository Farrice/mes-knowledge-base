# 02 — Date-Sequence Architect

The flagship net-new framework. Messaging is a relationship that unfolds in stages, and each stage has a strict information budget. Miller: *"There are things you say to get them to go on a date with you, and then on the date there are things you say — and you can't mess this up. If you say all the fourth-date stuff on the first date, they're not going to go out with you again."*

## Pre-Flight Gate

Run this when:
- A landing page or ad is overloaded — it tries to explain the whole product before earning the click.
- Conversion drops between the click and the call (or vice versa) and the message sequence is suspect.
- Building a new funnel and you need to decide *what goes where*.
- A great offer "isn't landing" and front-loading is the likely culprit.

**Do NOT** use this to write the copy itself — it *allocates* messages to stages. Hand the allocated messages to `donald-miller-storybrand/05-sales-funnel-architect` or `donald-miller-cognitive-load/02-peace-soundbite-generator` to write them.

## Skill Acquisition

Load `genius.md` Pattern 1 (Date-Sequence) + the "completeness trap" anti-pattern. Cross-reference `donald-miller-cognitive-load` for scoring whether a first-date message is truly zero-load.

## Execution

**Step 1 — Inventory every touchpoint** the customer hits, in order: ad → landing page → lead magnet → sales call → proposal → onboarding → expansion. This is the relationship timeline.

**Step 2 — Assign a date to each.** The rule: **earlier date = fewer ideas.**
- **First date** (ad / landing page hero): exactly ONE idea — the single hook that earns the click. Belay led with *"fractional"* and nothing else.
- **Second date** (sales call): handle the objection the first date *created*. (Belay's "fractional" hook created "but how does this work remotely?" — answered only here.)
- **Third/fourth date** (proposal / onboarding): the full nuance the customer can only absorb once committed.

**Step 3 — Trace the objection chain.** Every first-date hook plants a predictable objection. Write it down. The second-date message exists to answer *that specific objection* — not to re-pitch. Map hook → objection → second-date answer explicitly.

**Step 4 — Find the front-loading.** Scan every asset for fourth-date nuance sitting on a first-date surface (pricing tiers, integrations, edge cases on a hero section). Move each one back to its correct date. The landing page should survive on one idea; if it only makes sense *with* the nuance, the nuance is misplaced.

**Step 4.5 — Write the second date to formula (the color key).** The single highest-leverage second-date asset is the follow-up message after a sales conversation. Miller's fixed 6-part order — documented in *How to Grow Your Small Business* as "The Customer Is the Hero" **color key**, so you can audit any draft at a glance:
- **(1) the customer's problem — RED** (stated first, *not yours*; this is the hook)
- **(2) your product as the solution — PURPLE**
- **(3) a three- or four-step plan — BROWN** (dissolves the "how do I even buy this" cognitive dissonance; "three-step plans work well; five-step plans hardly work at all")
- **(4) the negative stakes of not acting — YELLOW**
- **(5) the positive stakes of acting — BLUE**
- **(6) a single, direct call to action — GREEN**

*"If you do those five things in that order, you will close sales."* The diagnostic: highlight the draft by color — if it comes back **mostly purple**, you've only talked about yourself, and it's the opposite of a million-dollar pitch. Two colors minimum to count as a story; all six = the full second-date message. Map this onto whichever touchpoint is the brand's true second date.

**The proposal-and-follow-up pair** (from the *Close the Client Kit*): the second date is usually two assets — a clear proposal, then a follow-up email whose two jobs are **(a) drive a deadline** ("people are more likely to take action when there is a deadline") and **(b) initiate communication** ("don't just send the proposal and then go silent"). Put an expiration date on the proposal; the follow-up reminds them why they need you with urgency. This is the second-date asset most brands get wrong by sending a limp "just checking in."

**Step 5 — Output the date map.** A table: touchpoint → date → the one-to-few messages allowed → the objection it sets up or resolves. Anchor it in the relationship-phase logic (Source 2): first date = *curiosity*; middle dates = *enlightenment* / due-diligence (where objections resolve); commitment is the close, and *"commitment does not come fast."* The funnel's job is to automate curiosity + enlightenment so trust precedes the ask.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Funnel type | Date emphasis |
|---|---|
| Lead-gen → sales call | First date = one hook; second date (call) = objection handling |
| E-commerce / self-serve | Compress dates onto the page in scroll-order; hero = first date, FAQ = third date |
| High-ticket / enterprise | More dates, slower disclosure; proposal is third date, not first |
| Webinar / VSL | Open = first date hook; body = second date; offer = third date |
| Two-sided marketplace | Run the sequence *separately* for demand and supply audiences |

## Output Requirements

- **Date map table** (touchpoint → date → allowed messages).
- **Objection chain**: each first-date hook → the objection it plants → its second-date answer.
- **Front-loading fixes**: every fourth-date message currently misplaced, with its correct date.
- A one-line **first-date message** that survives on a single idea.
- A **color-coded second-date asset** (red→purple→brown→yellow→blue→green) — the follow-up email/proposal, written to formula.

## Quality Gate

Reject if (per `genius.md`):
- The first-date surface needs the nuance to make sense (completeness trap — it's a fourth-date message in disguise).
- The second-date message re-pitches instead of answering the planted objection.
- The second-date asset color-checks **mostly purple** (all about you, no red problem / no blue payoff).
- Stages aren't tied to an actual relationship timeline (dates assigned arbitrarily).
