# 05 — Message-Siloing System

One message to everyone becomes high cognitive load for everyone — including your own salespeople. The fix: **a dedicated message per segment, and a dedicated team to deliver it.** Miller's summary principle: *"Silo your messaging — one message for finances, one for the executive assistant, one for the marketing assistant... a message for a Donald Miller prospect that's different than a Google prospect."* Belay's breakthrough: the year it split the sales team, *"sales grew. When we reduced the cognitive load on our internal team and on the customer, sales went up."*

## Pre-Flight Gate

Run this when:
- One sales team / one message is serving multiple distinct buyer types with different pain points.
- The company has scaled past the "everyone's a generalist" stage and a general message now underperforms.
- Pain points don't transfer across segments (assistant buyers feel *overwhelm*; financial buyers feel *"I lack expertise"*).
- A marketplace needs *both* a demand-side and a supply-side message.

**Do NOT** use this for a single-segment business — siloing a homogeneous audience adds complexity for nothing. Confirm genuinely distinct segments first.

## Execution

**Step 1 — Map the distinct segments.** List every buyer type AND every acquisition source. Belay siloed by *product* (assistant vs. financial) AND by *prospect origin* (a Donald Miller lead ≠ a Google lead ≠ enterprise vs. small-business). A segment is real only if its **pain point differs**, not just its demographic.

**Step 2 — Write the pain point per segment.** For each, the *internal problem* in the buyer's words. The siloing test: if two segments share a pain sentence, they may be one silo; if they diverge (*"I'm overwhelmed"* vs. *"I lack the expertise"*), they are separate silos with *"two completely different second dates."*

**Step 3 — Build one message per silo.** Each silo gets its own hook, objection chain (run `02-date-sequence-architect` per silo), and vernacular. Belay: assistant = *"customized match"*; financial = *"holistic solution."* No cross-bleed — a financial message in an assistant funnel is noise.

**Step 4 — Specialize the team (the hidden lever).** Map who *delivers* each silo. The customer-facing message is only half; the bigger unlock is putting a *specialist* on each silo so the rep isn't carrying all segments. Belay put former CPAs on financial calls, EA-specialists on assistant calls — *"an educational consultative conversation"* vs. *"overwhelm, getting your time back."* Recommend the team split explicitly; it's where the growth came from.

**Step 5 — Add supply-side silos (if two-sided).** For marketplaces, the message that creates *inventory* is its own silo. Belay recruited talent with a *lifestyle* message ("pick your journey," work-from-home career) and spent $0 on recruiting. Treat supply as a first-class silo with its own pain ("I want my career AND my family") and its own funnel.

**Step 6 — Output the silo matrix.** Segment → pain point → message/vernacular → delivering team/specialist → channels.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Business shape | Siloing emphasis |
|---|---|
| Multi-product (one company) | Silo by product pain; P&G/J&J logic — separate the messages, maybe the brands |
| Multi-segment (one product) | Silo by buyer pain + acquisition source (Don Miller lead ≠ Google lead) |
| Two-sided marketplace | Demand silo + supply silo, each a full message system |
| Enterprise + SMB | Silo by sophistication; consultative vs. simple, specialist reps per tier |
| Agency / services | Silo by deliverable expertise; specialist closes each line |

## Output Requirements

- **Silo matrix**: segment → distinct pain point → message + vernacular → delivering specialist → channels.
- **Team-split recommendation** — who delivers each silo and why a generalist underperforms here.
- **Supply-side silo** (if two-sided) with its own pain and funnel.
- **Cross-bleed flags** — any message currently serving two silos at once.

## Quality Gate

Reject if (per `genius.md`):
- Segments are split on demographics, not divergent pain points.
- It optimizes customer copy but leaves one overloaded generalist team (the internal silo is half the unlock).
- A single message is stretched across genuinely distinct pains (high internal + external load).
- A two-sided business gets only a demand-side message.
