# 03 — Bridge-Word Engine

When you sell something the market assumes is worse than what it replaces, you don't win the quality argument with specs — you **import the trust of the old category with one word.** Miller's case: Milwaukee's battery tools stalled until they were renamed *"fuel,"* bridging gas-tool trust into the electric world. *"They built a bridge from the old world to the new world with just a word."*

## Pre-Flight Gate

Run this when:
- Your category triggers a subconscious quality objection ("battery isn't as good as gas," "fractional isn't as good as full-time," "AI isn't as good as human," "virtual undermines the person").
- You're creating or entering a new category the market doesn't trust yet.
- A name/label "feels limited" or "undermines the quality" of what you actually deliver (Belay on "virtual": *"it undermines the human element and quality of person"*).

**Do NOT** use this for ordinary naming/taglines with no category-trust gap — use `donald-miller-cognitive-load/02-peace-soundbite-generator` or `name-framework`.

## Skill Acquisition

Load `genius.md` Pattern 2 (Bridge Word) + the "spec-sheet persuasion" anti-pattern. The whole move lives in two coordinates: the *subconscious objection* and the *trusted old-world referent*.

## Execution

**Step 1 — Name the subconscious objection.** What does the market *assume* about your category, below the surface, that suppresses orders? Phrase it as the customer's unspoken thought. (Milwaukee: "battery can't match gas." Belay: "fractional/virtual = chewing-gum security guard, not quality.") If you can't name the objection, there is no bridge to build — stop.

**Step 2 — Name the trusted old-world referent.** What is the *familiar, trusted* category your new thing actually performs *as well as*? (Battery performs *as* gas. A vetted fractional CFO performs *as* a full-time CFO.) The referent must be one the customer already trusts.

**Step 3 — Generate bridge-word candidates.** Find words that **carry the connotation of the trusted referent without naming the distrusted new category.** "Fuel" carries gas-trust without saying "battery." Generate 10–15 candidates that span the gap. Techniques:
- Borrow the old category's vocabulary (gas → fuel).
- Borrow a quality/trust marker (vetted, certified, experienced, managed).
- Borrow the human/premium frame the new category lacks ("educated human staffing," "outsourced team" vs. "virtual assistant").

**Step 4 — Test each candidate against the objection.** For each, ask: *does saying this word make the subconscious objection quieter?* Kill any that require explanation (Miller on "managed service": *"too high cognitive load, would require too much explanation"*). The bridge word must be instantly legible — it does the persuasion *before* the spec sheet.

**Step 5 — Output the bridge word + the bridge it builds.** State the chosen word, the objection it silences, the old-world trust it imports, and 1–2 runners-up.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Situation | Bridge emphasis |
|---|---|
| New tech category (AI, battery, no-code) | Borrow the incumbent's quality vocabulary |
| Premium-but-cheaper-coded service (fractional, remote) | Bridge on the human/quality axis ("vetted," "experienced human") |
| Rebranded commodity | Bridge on the upgrade the customer already wants |
| Category rename mid-evolution | Pair with `06-rebrand-patience-protocol` — the bridge word eases the 3-year cost |

## Output Requirements

- **The subconscious objection** (customer's unspoken thought).
- **The trusted old-world referent.**
- **Chosen bridge word** + why it silences the objection without explanation.
- **2 runners-up**, each with the bridge it would build.
- A one-line rationale: "[old world] trust, imported into [new world], via [word]."

## Quality Gate

Reject if (per `genius.md`):
- The candidate answers the quality objection with features/specs instead of a single legible word.
- The word requires explanation to land (cognitive-load failure).
- No subconscious objection was named (there's nothing to bridge — this isn't a bridge-word problem).
