# 1-Page Avatar Template

> Per Omar: "I see all these reports being like 70 pages long but nobody reads them. I just need one page with all this condensed data. It's like condensed juice."

**Critical constraints:**
- Maximum 1 page per avatar
- 2-4 avatars total per brand (not more)
- Verbatim customer language only (no marketer-imagined language)
- Top 3 emotional triggers only (not 10)

---

## Template

### Avatar [Name] (Pick a real-feeling name + descriptor — e.g., "Marcus, the Recovery-Focused Creator")

**Who they are (2-3 sentences max)**
[Demographic + life-stage + identity — in customer's own framing if possible. Not "millennial professional" but "32, just bought their first house, working hybrid, optimizing their morning routine because everything else feels chaotic."]

**Problem in their own words (verbatim from research)**
"[Direct quote or paraphrase that captures how they describe the problem to a friend.]"

**What they've already tried (and why it didn't work)**
- [Solution 1]: [Why it failed for them — verbatim language]
- [Solution 2]: [Why it failed]
- [Solution 3]: [Why it failed — if 3 attempts present in research]

**Their language (5-10 verbatim phrases)**
- "[verbatim phrase 1]"
- "[verbatim phrase 2]"
- "[verbatim phrase 3]"
- "[verbatim phrase 4]"
- "[verbatim phrase 5]"
[Pull these from review CSV — these go directly into ad copy]

**What would make them buy today**
[Single sentence: the specific trigger / proof / mechanism that breaks their hesitation. Often discovered in support tickets or 5-star reviews.]

**What's stopping them**
[Single sentence: their dominant pre-purchase objection, in their own words.]

**Top 3 Emotional Triggers (rank-ordered)**
1. **[Primary trigger]** — [Why this trigger lands for THIS avatar specifically]
2. **[Secondary trigger]** — [Context]
3. **[Tertiary trigger]** — [Context]

## Worked Example: Wellness Brand (Whoop-Adjacent Performance Wearable)

### Avatar: Marcus, the Recovery-Focused Creator (35-44)

**Who they are**
Mid-career creative professional or entrepreneur, 35-44, household income $150K+, prioritizes physical performance because cognitive performance pays the bills. Cold plunges, lifts 4x/week, tracks sleep obsessively. Identity is "high-performer who takes recovery seriously."

**Problem in their own words**
"I can tell when I'm overtraining but by then I've already had two bad weeks. I want to know BEFORE my body falls apart, not after."

**What they've already tried**
- Apple Watch: "Too much data, no insight. I have HRV but I don't know what to do with it."
- Garmin: "Great for workouts, terrible for recovery — I don't run marathons, I run my company."
- Manual journaling: "Inconsistent. I miss days when I need it most."

**Their language**
- "Recovery is the new performance"
- "Sleep is my new HRV"
- "I optimize what I measure"
- "My body is my most expensive asset"
- "I want signal not data"

**What would make them buy today**
A clean dashboard that turns biometric data into a single readiness score they can trust without analysis — and a community of people they respect already using it.

**What's stopping them**
Skepticism that "another tracker" can deliver real insight without becoming yet another anxious data feed.

**Top 3 Emotional Triggers**
1. **Identity reinforcement** — "I am the kind of person who takes this seriously" — content showing high-performers using product validates self-image
2. **Cognitive offloading** — "I want decisions made for me when my data is complex" — readiness score does the thinking
3. **In-group recognition** — "People I respect use this" — micro-influencer / niche celebrity authority

## Avatar Quality Checks

Before deploying an avatar, run these checks:

### ✅ Pass Conditions

- Could a copywriter who never met this customer write a hook from this avatar? (Should be YES)
- Are 5+ phrases in the avatar verbatim from research data? (Should be YES)
- Does the avatar have a unique pre-purchase objection? (Should be YES, distinguishes from other avatars)
- Are the top 3 emotional triggers different from your other avatars' triggers? (Should be YES — if all avatars have the same triggers, you have one avatar, not multiple)

### ❌ Fail Conditions (rebuild)

- Avatar reads like demographic data ("28-35 women interested in wellness") — too generic
- Triggers are universal ("they want to feel good") — not differentiated
- No verbatim customer language — written from imagination
- Longer than 1 page — over-elaborated, becomes theoretical

## When to Build Multiple Avatars

| Brand Stage | Number of Avatars | Reason |
|-------------|-------------------|--------|
| Pre-launch / early scale ($0-$50K/mo) | 2 | Focus matters more than coverage |
| Growth ($50K-$500K/mo) | 3 | Diversification across awareness stages |
| Scale ($500K+/mo) | 4 | Need pyramid coverage (most-aware → unaware) |
| Mature ($5M+/yr) | 4 maximum | Beyond 4, returns diminish — better to deepen 4 than add 5th |

## When to Update Avatars

- Quarterly review minimum
- After any significant performance shift (CAC change > 25%)
- After a major product/positioning pivot
- When new customer language patterns emerge in reviews/tickets

## Avatar → Hook Bank Bridge

Once avatars are built, generate hooks per avatar using the **Neural Marketing Trigger Map** reference. Target: 20 hooks per avatar, each tagged to one of their top 3 emotional triggers.
