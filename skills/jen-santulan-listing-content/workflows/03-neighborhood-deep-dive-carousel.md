# Workflow 03 — Neighborhood Deep-Dive Carousel

> Multi-slide Instagram carousels that establish Jen as the SFV specialist, not just "another LA agent." High save rate, durable authority asset, returns search traffic when buyers Google "Living in [neighborhood]."

## When to Use

- Jen wants to OWN a specific SFV neighborhood in search and saves
- New buyer asks "Should I look in X or Y?" — answer becomes a carousel
- Jen has firsthand knowledge of a neighborhood (recent showings, recent client closing, lived there)
- Need authority content that isn't tied to a specific listing's expiration

## Why Carousels Specifically

Reels die in 24-48 hours. Carousels live for months and resurface in saves and shares.

**Carousel-specific algorithmic edge:**
- Multi-slide format = higher dwell time per impression
- Save rate is 3-5x higher than Reels for educational content
- Reappears in feeds when followers save it (algorithm rewards save-worthy)
- Indexed by Instagram search — buyers searching "Sherman Oaks" find it months later

## The 7-Slide Carousel Architecture

7 slides is the optimal save-worthy length. Fewer feels thin. More gets dropped halfway. Calibrated for Instagram's max swipe completion rate.

### Slide 1 — The Hook (Cover)
**Purpose**: Stop the scroll. Make people swipe.
**Format**: Bold statement + neighborhood name + visual punch
**Voice rule**: Genius Pattern 2 (Wait, What? Setup) or Pattern 3 (Most People Think... But Actually)

Examples:
- "Why my first-time buyers pick **Reseda** over Sherman Oaks." (contrarian)
- "Living in **Lake Balboa**: what no one tells you." (curiosity)
- "**Encino** vs **Sherman Oaks**: which one is actually the better buy?" (comparison)

**Production note**: Slide 1 is the only slide that has to work as a thumbnail. Test it small.

### Slide 2 — The Pain or Question Setup
**Purpose**: Pre-load WHY the reader cares.
**Format**: Name the buyer's confusion or assumption explicitly.

Examples:
- "Most of my FTHB clients walk in saying they want Encino. Median price: $1.2M. Out of budget."
- "Lake Balboa flies under the radar — and that's exactly why my clients are buying there before everyone else catches on."

This is genius.md Pillar 8 (Permission Mechanic) at neighborhood scale: name the buyer's stuck position before reframing.

### Slide 3 — The Numbers (Establish Authority)
**Purpose**: Specificity = trust. Show you actually know the market.
**Format**: 3-5 data points, each one specific

Required data points:
- Median home price (cite source: Redfin/Zillow current)
- Year-over-year appreciation
- Average days on market
- Median rent (for rent-vs-mortgage relevance)

Optional bonus data:
- School ratings (GreatSchools or similar)
- Median household income of the neighborhood
- Walk score / commute time to specific job centers

### Slide 4 — The Lifestyle Reality (SHOW Don't Tell)
**Purpose**: Make the reader picture their actual Saturday morning here.
**Format**: 4-6 vivid, specific micro-moments. Real place names only.

This is the Reels Lifestyle hook stretched into carousel form, but with MORE depth — you're not constrained to 18 seconds.

Example for Sherman Oaks:
- "Saturday morning at the farmer's market on Magnolia"
- "Coffee at Aroma Coffee on Tujunga before walking your dog to Westfield Fashion Square"
- "Dinner at Vitello's or Saddle Ranch — both walking distance from most listings"
- "Kids at Notre Dame High or Buckley — top-rated, both private but accessible"
- "15 minutes to Studio City for late-night, 25 to the Westside via 405"

### Slide 5 — The FTHB-Specific Angle
**Purpose**: Connect this neighborhood specifically to her ICP.
**Format**: Why does THIS neighborhood work for FTHBs in particular?

Possible angles:
- Price-per-sqft compared to aspirational neighborhood
- Programs that work here (HOP80/120 income limits, LIPA eligibility for City of LA neighborhoods)
- Inventory pattern that favors FTHBs (less bidding war pressure, longer DOM)
- Specific home types available (townhomes, condos, smaller SFRs that fit FTHB budgets)

This is the slide that turns the carousel from "neighborhood guide" into "buyer strategy" — the move competitors don't make.

### Slide 6 — The Honest Trade-Off
**Purpose**: Trust through honesty. Most agent content lies by omission.
**Format**: One real downside, named clearly.

Examples:
- "Trade-off: traffic on Ventura Blvd during rush hour is brutal. If you commute downtown, factor in 45 min."
- "Honesty: Reseda doesn't have the dining scene Sherman Oaks does. You're getting price for that trade."
- "Real talk: Lake Balboa schools are mid. If schools are #1, look at Encino instead."

This is Jen's trust architecture (genius.md Pillar 6) at work. ONE real downside earns the right to all the upsides.

### Slide 7 — The Action Slide
**Purpose**: Save, share, OR take next step. Pick one — don't dilute.
**Format**: Single clear ask + Jen handle + hashtags

Three valid Action templates:

**Action A — Save**:
> "Save this for when you're ready to look in [neighborhood]. I update these every quarter."

**Action B — Send**:
> "Send this to anyone who's stuck on a neighborhood that's out of budget. There's always another play."

**Action C — Schedule**:
> "Want to walk a few [neighborhood] homes? Send me a DM — let's make a list."

Always end with: `#realestatewithjing #[neighborhood-tag] #sanfernandovalley`

## Voice Quality Gate

Run through genius.md Section 10 quality bar plus carousel-specific checks:

**Carousel-specific MUST PASS:**
- ☐ Slide 1 works as a thumbnail (visible at 250px)
- ☐ Slides 2-6 each have ONE clear idea (no slide doing two jobs)
- ☐ At least 3 specific data points in Slide 3 (cite source if non-obvious)
- ☐ At least 4 named places in Slide 4 (restaurants, parks, schools, shopping)
- ☐ Slide 6 honest trade-off is REAL (not a humble-brag disguised as honesty)
- ☐ Slide 7 has ONE action, not multiple
- ☐ Total length: ~250-400 words across all slides

**Anti-pattern check (auto-reject if present):**
- Slide 6 fake honesty ("the only downside is it's TOO good!" 😬)
- Slide 4 generic adjectives ("amazing restaurants," "great schools") instead of names
- Slide 1 motivational poster aesthetics ("Find Your Dream Home in [neighborhood]!")
- Multiple competing CTAs across slides
- Same hashtag pattern as last 3 carousels (rotate)
