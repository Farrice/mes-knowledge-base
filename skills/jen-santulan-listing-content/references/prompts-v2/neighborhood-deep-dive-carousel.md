## Role & Activation

You are building a 7-slide Instagram carousel in Jennifer Santulan's voice that establishes her as the San Fernando Valley specialist — not "another LA agent." Unlike Reels, which die in 24-48 hours, carousels live for months: higher dwell time per impression, 3-5x the save rate of Reels for educational content, resurfacing in feeds as followers save it, and indexed by Instagram search when buyers Google "Living in [neighborhood]." This is a durable authority asset, not a listing pitch.

## Input Required

```
[TARGET NEIGHBORHOOD] — required (e.g. Sherman Oaks, Encino, Canoga Park, Reseda, Tarzana, Lake Balboa, West Hills, Woodland Hills, Van Nuys)
[SUB-ANGLE] — required (e.g. "Why my FTHB clients pick this," "Sherman Oaks vs Encino," "Hidden gems in Reseda," "Living in Lake Balboa")
[COMPARISON NEIGHBORHOOD] — if applicable: the aspirational neighborhood being compared against
[RECENT FIRSTHAND EXPERIENCE] — recommended: last 30 days of showings, listings seen, client conversations to draw specifics from
```

## Execution Protocol

Build exactly 7 slides — the calibrated optimal length for Instagram's max swipe completion rate. Fewer feels thin; more gets dropped halfway.

### Slide 1 — The Hook (Cover)
Purpose: stop the scroll, make people swipe. Bold statement + neighborhood name + visual punch. Use genius.md Pattern 2 ("Wait, What?" Setup) or Pattern 3 ("Most People Think... But Actually" Reframe). This is the only slide that has to work as a thumbnail — test it small. Examples of the register (write new, don't reuse): "Why my first-time buyers pick Reseda over Sherman Oaks" (contrarian); "Living in Lake Balboa: what no one tells you" (curiosity); "Encino vs Sherman Oaks: which one is actually the better buy?" (comparison).

### Slide 2 — The Pain or Question Setup
Purpose: pre-load why the reader cares. Name the buyer's confusion or assumption explicitly — this is the FTHB Permission Mechanic (genius.md Pillar 8) at neighborhood scale: name the reader's stuck position before reframing.

### Slide 3 — The Numbers (Establish Authority)
Purpose: specificity = trust. Required data points, each cited to source (Redfin/Zillow/current): median home price, YoY appreciation, average days on market, median rent (for rent-vs-mortgage relevance). Optional bonus: school ratings, neighborhood median household income, walk score / commute time to specific job centers. If a [COMPARISON NEIGHBORHOOD] was given, include its equivalent figures side by side.

### Slide 4 — The Lifestyle Reality (Show, Don't Tell)
Purpose: make the reader picture their actual Saturday morning here. 4-6 vivid, specific micro-moments using real, named places only — no "great restaurants" or "amazing parks." This is the Reels lifestyle hook stretched into carousel form with more room to breathe.

### Slide 5 — The FTHB-Specific Angle
Purpose: connect this neighborhood specifically to the first-time-buyer ICP — the move that turns "neighborhood guide" into "buyer strategy." Draw from: price-per-sqft vs. the aspirational neighborhood, which programs work here (HOP80/120 income limits, LIPA eligibility for City of LA neighborhoods), inventory patterns that favor FTHBs (less bidding pressure, longer DOM), specific home types available that fit FTHB budgets.

### Slide 6 — The Honest Trade-Off
Purpose: trust through honesty — most agent content lies by omission. Name ONE real downside clearly (e.g. dining scene gap, commute time, school ratings, traffic pattern). This must be a genuine trade-off, not a humble-brag disguised as honesty ("the only downside is it's TOO good!"). This is genius.md's trust architecture (Pillar 6) at work — one real downside earns the right to all the upsides.

### Slide 7 — The Action Slide
Purpose: save, share, OR take next step — pick exactly ONE, never dilute across multiple CTAs. Choose one template:
- **Save**: "Save this for when you're ready to look in [neighborhood]. I update these every quarter."
- **Send**: "Send this to anyone who's stuck on a neighborhood that's out of budget. There's always another play."
- **Schedule**: "Want to walk a few [neighborhood] homes? Send me a DM — let's make a list."

Always end with: `#realestatewithjing #[neighborhood-tag] #sanfernandovalley` — rotate the hashtag set so it doesn't repeat the last 3 carousels verbatim.

### Close with Production Notes
Suggested visuals per slide, photos Jen needs to capture/source, and a pairing recommendation — which listing post should run within 7 days of this carousel (the carousel does the trust work, the listing Reel converts the saved viewer).

## Output Contract

Exactly 7 slides in the order above, plus a self-assessed voice match score (1-10) and Production Notes. Total length across all slides: ~250-400 words. Slide 3 carries a minimum of 3 specific data points with sources. Slide 4 names at least 4 real places. Slide 6 carries exactly one honest trade-off. Slide 7 carries exactly one action.

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Slide 1 reads as a working thumbnail hook (visible/legible at small size, not a motivational-poster line like "Find Your Dream Home in [neighborhood]!")
- [ ] Slide 3 contains at least 3 specific, sourced data points — no unsupported claims
- [ ] Slide 4 names at least 4 real places (restaurants, parks, schools, shopping) — no generic adjectives standing in for specifics
- [ ] Slide 6's trade-off is a genuine downside, not a disguised humble-brag
- [ ] Slide 7 has exactly one action — no competing CTAs
- [ ] Total length falls in the ~250-400 word band and hashtags don't repeat the prior 3 carousels' set

## Creative Latitude

Slides 3-5 are where the SFV-specialist authority is actually built — push for the comparison, program detail, or inventory insight that a generic LA agent genuinely couldn't produce, not just the first data point that comes to mind. Slide 6's honest trade-off is the highest-leverage line in the piece: the sharper and more specific the downside, the more the upsides are believed — don't soften it into vagueness. Slide 4's five micro-moments should feel like they came from an actual recent Saturday, not a list of category placeholders (coffee shop, park, restaurant) — chase the specific texture (which coffee shop, which regulars, which exact walk).

## Deploy When

Jen wants to own a specific SFV neighborhood in search and saves; a buyer asks "should I look in X or Y" and the answer deserves a carousel instead of a DM; Jen has fresh firsthand knowledge of a neighborhood from a recent showing or closing; she needs durable authority content that isn't tied to a listing's expiration.
