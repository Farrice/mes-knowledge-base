# Concept and Title Compression

## Role & Activation

Execute the children’s-story reset as a diagnostic: recover person, want, obstacle, stakes, and one concrete generative object/action; then rebuild adult depth through consequence. Do not infantilize the audience or copy Haig’s titles and plots.

## Input Required

- `[CURRENT PREMISE]`
- `[WHAT IS NOT WORKING]`
- `[TARGET AUDIENCE AND FORMAT]`
- `[EMOTIONAL PROPOSITION]`
- `[NON-NEGOTIABLE FACTS OR THEMES]`

## Execution Protocol

1. Identify complexity that does not change a choice.
2. Write the story in four child-clear sentences: person, want, obstacle, consequence.
3. Find one concrete object or action that can carry the premise.
4. Remove lore, systems, and abstraction that do not create motion.
5. Rebuild adult depth through contradiction, cost, implication, and relationship.
6. Generate twelve titles across object, place, action, paradox, and emotional-question families.
7. Select three and explain what each clarifies and preserves.
8. Produce the revised premise and opening movement.

## Output Contract

Return: complexity diagnosis; four-sentence reset; generative object/action; adult-depth rebuild; revised premise; twelve-title field; top three; selected title; finished opening movement.

## Quality Gate

- [ ] The reset can be followed without theme vocabulary.
- [ ] One object or action creates story motion.
- [ ] Adult depth returns through consequence, not jargon.
- [ ] Titles clarify without exhausting mystery.
- [ ] No Haig title, plot, or stylistic surface is imitated.

## Creative Latitude

The generative object may be ordinary, magical, social, procedural, or spatial. Choose the object that creates the most choices and consequences, not the one that sounds most whimsical.

## Deploy When

Use for `/haig-child-story-reset` and concept/title work when complexity has replaced motion.
