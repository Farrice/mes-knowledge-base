# Feeling Carrier Scene

## Role & Activation

Execute story as a vehicle for feeling. Draft as the first reader, convert explanation into pressure and consequence, and end on residue. Preserve rough life; never smooth the passage into a Matt Haig imitation.

## Input Required

- `[LIVE FRAGMENT OR DRAFT]`
- `[TARGET FEELING]`
- `[CHARACTER OR HUMAN CENTER]`
- `[CONCRETE SETTING OR OBJECT]`
- `[FORMAT AND LENGTH]`
- `[CONSTRAINTS]`

## Execution Protocol

1. Identify the warmest image, action, title, or question.
2. State the target feeling privately; do not put it in dialogue or narration as a thesis.
3. Give a person a want, obstacle, and consequential choice.
4. Use the concrete setting/object as the carrier.
5. Draft without midstream polish; follow discoveries that intensify pressure.
6. Replace abstract emotional explanation with action, image, or consequence.
7. Preserve one productive irregularity.
8. End with an image, shared word, or unresolved action that echoes.

## Output Contract

Return: live premise; three unresolved tensions; finished scene/passage within `[FORMAT AND LENGTH]`; discoveries; protected irregularity; ending-residue note; next live scene. Mark the draft `DISCOVERY DRAFT` unless the user requested revision.

## Output Skeleton

```markdown
# <working title>
**Live premise:** <>
**Open tensions:** <> · <> · <>

<finished scene or passage>

## Discovery note
- New information: <>
- Protected irregularity: <>
- Residue: <>
- Next live scene: <>
```

## Quality Gate

- [ ] A concrete choice or consequence carries the feeling.
- [ ] The draft discovered something not supplied in the notes.
- [ ] No paragraph explains what the reader should feel.
- [ ] The final line leaves residue rather than summary.
- [ ] The prose retains nonuniform human rhythm.

## Creative Latitude

Follow the surprising image or turn when it deepens the emotional pressure. The input is a launch point, not a cage. Do not borrow plots, phrases, aliens, libraries, or magical trains from Haig’s published work.
