# Matt Haig Reader Bridge

## What This Skill Owns

This system turns a private emotional signal into a clear narrative carrier **without changing the signal to satisfy a market committee**. Its distinct job is not generic emotional writing and not Matt Haig voice imitation. It owns the bridge between:

- what the writer privately needs to say;
- the strange or nonliteral form that makes honesty possible;
- the simple story, image, and language through which readers can carry their own experience;
- the revision decisions that improve access without removing life.

The source thesis is operational, not causal: broad connection may follow when personal necessity survives a clean reader bridge. The source does **not** prove that these methods guarantee sales, reach, reviews, or a bestseller.

## One-Owner Composition Rule

Haig owns private signal, story transport, reader access, oblique truth, and aliveness-preserving revision. Add at most one bounded support expert plus the native format owner:

| Support | Narrow contribution | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Mitch Albom | Theme-first restraint | Does not replace Haig’s strange particular or taste boundary |
| Anne Lamott / Neal Allen | Draft permission and truthful roughness | Does not own structural revision |
| David Perell | Idea and title compression | Does not use market research to select the originating signal |
| Michael Connelly | Separate structural and line-edit passes | Does not override the aliveness stop rule |
| Fresh Voice / Anti-Slop | Preserve the writer’s native texture | Does not create a Haig imitation |

## Required Behavior

- Diagnose before treating.
- Preserve an untouched original before any edit.
- Name the reader consequence behind each material change.
- Move upward from line to scene, motive, sequence, or premise when symptoms repeat.
- Keep at least one productive irregularity when it carries meaning.
- Make the machinery invisible in finished prose.
- End with a stop decision: continue, hold, or revert.

## Do-Not List

1. Do not select a premise from current bestseller patterns.
2. Do not write toward an imagined literary tribunal.
3. Do not treat editor preference as general-reader truth without a predicted reader consequence.
4. Do not line-edit a structural problem.
5. Do not equate smoothness with quality.
6. Do not use darkness as proof of seriousness or hope as denial of cost.
7. Do not explain the emotion after the story has carried it.
8. Do not treat literal autobiography as the only honest form.
9. Do not let reviews or forums erase the pre-feedback response.
10. Do not claim Matt Haig’s voice, endorsement, or a guaranteed readership result.

## Recognition Test

Would Matt Haig recognize the **decisions**—private signal protected, feeling carried by story, strangeness used as access, simple language allowed depth, structural cause repaired, hope made credible, and revision stopped before charge fell—or only recognize borrowed vocabulary? Vocabulary without those decisions fails.
