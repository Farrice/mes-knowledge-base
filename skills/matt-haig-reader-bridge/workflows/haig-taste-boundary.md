# Taste Boundary

## Pre-Flight Gate

Require the work, the writer’s pre-feedback response if available, and the feedback or market signal under consideration. Read `../genius.md` Patterns 3, 14, 15, and 18.

## Skill Acquisition

Load `../references/hidden-knowledge.md` and `../references/rubric_v1.md`.

## Execution

1. Record the unpolluted response: strongest image, live edge, dead spot, unresolved question.
2. Sort each external note into personal preference, predicted reader friction, factual correction, safety constraint, or market lag.
3. Require a reader consequence for every proposed creative change.
4. Identify mass-appeasement and prestige-performance contamination.
5. Protect decisions supported by private necessity and coherent craft.
6. Accept, test, or reject each note with reasons.
7. Produce the smallest evidence-bearing next version; do not rewrite by committee.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Manuscript feedback | Separate editor taste from multi-reader friction |
| Reviews/forums | Preserve first response before reading consensus |
| Market research | Use comps as constraints, never premise origin |
| Client feedback | Factual and brand constraints retain veto power |
| Personal writing | Felt truth remains human-owned |

## Output Requirements

Pre-consensus verdict; classified feedback ledger; protected decisions; accepted tests; rejected changes; next version or exact test; remaining uncertainty.

Execution prompt: `references/prompts-v2/reader-bridge-diagnostic.md` — honor its Output Contract and use the feedback-ledger branch.

## Quality Gate

- Is the original response inspectable?
- Does every accepted creative note predict a reader consequence?
- Were factual corrections separated from taste?
- Did the system avoid both reflexive defiance and people-pleasing?
- Is no consensus presented as proof of correctness?
