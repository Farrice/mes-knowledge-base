# Percolation Draft

## Pre-Flight Gate

Require one live fragment: title, scene, image, question, or premise. If none has heat, return an incubation card rather than manufactured prose. Read `../genius.md` Patterns 1, 2, 10, and 16.

## Skill Acquisition

Load `../references/exemplars.md` and the feeling-carrier prompt.

## Execution

1. Name the warm fragment without explaining it.
2. Write three unresolved tensions that can keep percolating.
3. Choose the opening with the most exposure or curiosity.
4. Draft rapidly as the first reader; do not repair elegance midstream.
5. Follow surprises that deepen the emotional pressure.
6. Stop after one complete scene or coherent short piece.
7. Record what the draft discovered and the next scene with the most heat.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Novel | Draft the charged scene out of sequence |
| Essay | Begin with the live incident, not the thesis |
| Memoir | Permit a nonliteral mask if direct telling tightens the guard |
| Post | Produce one scene-sized movement, not an outline thread |
| Concept | Return an incubation card if no scene or image has heat |

## Output Requirements

Live premise; three tensions; discovery draft; discoveries; protected irregularities; next scene; explicit “not yet revised” status.

Execution prompt: `references/prompts-v2/feeling-carrier-scene.md` — honor its discovery-draft branch.

## Quality Gate

- Did the draft discover something absent from the notes?
- Is there a concrete scene or action?
- Was mid-draft polishing avoided?
- Did nervousness get investigated rather than romanticized?
- Is the next live move clear?
