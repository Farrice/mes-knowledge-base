# Child-Story Reset

## Pre-Flight Gate

Require the current premise and what is not working. This is a concept diagnostic, not an instruction to infantilize language or audience. Read the *Midnight Train* exemplar in `../references/exemplars.md`.

## Skill Acquisition

Load `../genius.md` Patterns 11 and 13 plus the concept-title prompt.

## Execution

1. Strip the premise to who wants what, what blocks them, and what happens if they fail.
2. Explain the story to a bright child in four sentences without theme vocabulary.
3. Identify one magical or concrete object/action capable of carrying the premise.
4. Remove systems, lore, or abstraction that do not change a choice.
5. Rebuild adult complexity through consequence, contradiction, and implication—not explanatory machinery.
6. Generate a title set that names the object or emotional question without exhausting it.
7. Produce the revised premise and opening movement.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Novel/film | Object + desire + obstacle + irreversible action |
| Essay | Concrete question + lived test + consequence |
| Product/brand story | Human problem + one visible mechanism; no false fable |
| Complex concept | Analogy remains bounded; technical truth retained separately |
| Children’s work | Age-appropriate reader knowledge becomes an explicit constraint |

## Output Requirements

Complexity diagnosis; four-sentence child version; generative object/action; adult rebuild; revised premise; title set; opening movement.

Execution prompt: `references/prompts-v2/concept-title-compression.md` — honor its Output Contract.

## Quality Gate

- Can the story be followed without abstract explanation?
- Did simplification restore action?
- Did adult depth return through consequence rather than jargon?
- Is the child lens a diagnostic rather than a tone imitation?
- Does the title preserve mystery?
