# Feeling Carrier

## Pre-Flight Gate

Require the stated idea or theme, the intended feeling, and the existing scene or premise. Read `../genius.md` Patterns 7, 8, 9, and 11.

## Skill Acquisition

Load `../references/exemplars.md` and `../references/rubric_v1.md`.

## Execution

1. Translate the thesis into a private target feeling.
2. Identify where the draft currently explains that feeling.
3. Choose a carrier: choice, consequence, relationship, concrete object, or irreversible action.
4. Build the scene so pressure reveals the feeling.
5. Replace abstraction with the smallest accurate images and words.
6. End on residue rather than interpretation.
7. Remove the sentence that explains what the reader should feel unless comprehension truly fails without it.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Fiction | Character choice under pressure carries the feeling |
| Memoir | Concrete remembered action outranks retrospective thesis |
| Essay | One lived incident carries the argument |
| Brand story | Customer consequence carries the value claim |
| Short post | One object, one turn, one residue ending |

## Output Requirements

Target feeling; explanation audit; selected carrier; finished scene/passage; removed explanation; ending-residue check.

Execution prompt: `references/prompts-v2/feeling-carrier-scene.md` — honor its Output Contract.

## Quality Gate

- Can a reader feel the pressure before the thesis is named?
- Is there a concrete choice or consequence?
- Did simple language remain precise?
- Is the ending free of explanatory summary?
- Is the predicted feeling labeled UNTESTED until readers confirm it?
