# Genius Patterns

> Quick-reference companion to `genius.md` (re-forged 2026-06-14 against the full Kobi Brown / AstroKobi interview transcript). One compact block per pattern: name + one-line behavior + one verbatim cue. Full evidence and deployment notes live in `genius.md`; verbatim sources in `references/source-quotes.md`. Universal — Kobi's space/science work only illustrates; apply to any educator, coach, consultant, or knowledge worker. Every output should clear `references/anti-slop-aha-gate.md` (verdict SLOP / NOISE / SIGNAL / BREAKTHROUGH) and aim for genuine cognitive change — aha → perspective → identity shift — not noise.

## 1. The Vegetable Smuggle
Entertainment is the doorway; the hard, true idea is the payload. Open with appetite (question, anomaly, stakes, striking visual), then deliver the real undiluted idea — never "Today I'll explain X."
Cue: *"sneak the vegetables in education as well, that was my goal."*

## 2. Earn Attention the Hard Way
Rigor can win — ask whether the genuine complexity itself is the spectacle before reaching for a cheap hook. The real mechanism often grips harder than the gimmick.
Cue: *"real serious hard physics in a way that is engaging... it's not the only way to earn attention."*

## 3. The Goal-Definition Gate
Restraint is a strategy, not a reflex. Name the goal first (durable authority vs. short-term spike); let it dictate which constraints are non-negotiable. Don't moralize — decide.
Cue: *"it's all about the goal you define... to build a long-term career communicating science accurately."*

## 4. Earned Legitimacy, Never Claimed
Show, don't announce, why you're trustworthy. Pair every legitimacy signal (access, proximity, credential) with an act of verification — proximity alone is not accuracy.
Cue: *"going to NASA doesn't necessarily grant you accuracy... you still need those elements of journalism."*

## 5. The Commoditization Escape (the anti-slop spine)
When your craft edge gets copied or lowered by AI, consciously migrate to what's expensive to fake: original data, first-hand access, lived proof, real relationships. Be there instead of commentating.
Cue: *"AI slop... my distinguishing factor was visuals... even those barriers are being lowered. So it was very much a conscious thing."*

## 6. Core Idea Over Hook
Pressure-test the idea before the hook — a great hook on a thin idea is still thin. Spend disproportionate effort upstream on idea selection and narrative architecture.
Cue: *"what actually makes a good idea fundamentally... was the biggest shift."*

## 7. Idea–Format Fit
Ask "what is the natural size of this idea?" Let small ideas stay small (short, post, slide); reserve long-form for ideas that can carry the weight. No padding, no truncating.
Cue: *"leaving them as a short-form instead of trying to stretch them out into a long-form."*

## 8. Short-Form as Lab, Long-Form as Legacy
Run short-form as cheap hypotheses and top-of-funnel reach; invest real attention in long-form where craft, trust, and longevity compound. Graduate the signal into depth.
Cue: *"posted every day for like 500 days... a big part was that I shifted a lot of my focus to long-form."*

## 9. Visual Accuracy as Epistemic Discipline
Treat visuals as part of the method, not decoration. Build the "ugly accurate" version first (e.g. a rough Python sim that gets scales right), then make it beautiful without breaking the truth.
Cue: *"a really ugly simulation, but it gives them the idea... then convert that into something beautiful."*

## 10. The Restraint Cut (Leave It There)
Define the line in advance. Cover a hot topic honestly once, then stop — refuse the milking sequence even when the math says it would work.
Cue: *"showed what it truly is... then I left it there [interstellar comet] — could've got a few extra million views... but that's where it crosses the line."*

## 11. Misconception Correction & Meaning (the two aha forms)
Two highest-leverage shapes: (a) correct a widely-held wrong belief, walked back step by step; (b) the meaning piece — emotional, perspective-shifting, left a little rough.
Cue: *"'Could the Big Bang happen again?'... what most people think the Big Bang is isn't what it is."* / *"'The stars are disappearing'... rough around the edges, which on YouTube is sometimes good."*

## 12. Make What Needs to Exist
Reserve capacity for the asset only you can make — the definitive guide, the thing the niche is missing — even with uncertain ROI, backed by consistent baseline output.
Cue: *"a video I've worked on for nearly 2 years... thought needed to be made for 10 years that I never saw anyone make. I still don't know if it'll do well."*
