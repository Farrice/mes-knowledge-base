# Kobi Brown Educational Virality User Guide

## Fast Start — two front doors

**`/aha-engine` (universal).** Use on *any* task — a LinkedIn post, a client memo, an essay, a deck, a sales page, a lesson — when you want it to create a real aha, perspective shift, or identity shift instead of being noise. It returns the rebuilt asset plus an anti-slop verdict (SLOP / NOISE / SIGNAL / BREAKTHROUGH).

```text
/aha-engine Rewrite my LinkedIn post arguing that most strength coaches over-program. Audience: gen-pop lifters who think more is better. I want them to rethink their whole approach.
```

**`/educational-breakout-system` (educational router).** Use when the objective is specifically educational/coaching/consulting content and you want the system to pick the right specialist workflow.

```text
/educational-breakout-system I want to explain why AI agents fail without context engineering to founders who think prompts are enough. Make it work for LinkedIn and a short video.
```

## Direct Commands
- `/breakout-diagnostic` for diagnosing why a useful idea is ignored.
- `/curiosity-clarity-script` for writing a stronger educational asset.
- `/legitimacy-ladder` for proving why the audience should trust the teacher.
- `/simulation-analogy-lab` for making an abstract concept visible.
- `/shorts-to-longform-loop` for turning short-form signal into long-form authority.
- `/accuracy-without-clickbait` for keeping the hook truthful.
- `/knowledge-work-platform-adapter` for adapting one lesson across platforms.
- `/educator-series-architect` for building a serialized teaching arc.
- `/educational-virality-stacking` for combining Kobi with other Antigravity skills.
- `/aha-engine` for re-architecting ANY task for cognitive change + an anti-slop verdict.

## Inputs That Work Best
- Topic or idea.
- Audience.
- Platform.
- Desired outcome.
- What makes the idea hard to understand.
- Proof or credibility assets available.
- Any claims that must be handled carefully.

## Quality Bar
The final content should make the audience:

1. Want the answer.
2. Understand the mechanism.
3. Trust the teacher.
4. Remember the visual or analogy.
5. Have a reason to save, share, reply, book, subscribe, or watch the longer piece.
