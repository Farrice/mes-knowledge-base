# David Baldacci — Genius Context

> 53 novels, 7 children's books, 200M+ copies, two novels/year for 16 years. Former trial lawyer (10 years, University of Virginia Law). Screenwriter. Renegotiated his publishing contracts from 15% royalties to partnership profit-share. Source: David Perell "How I Write" interview, 2026 (19,708-word transcript, `extractions/david-baldacci/`).

## Core Philosophy

**The writer must control the reader — and the author must control the business.** Baldacci engineers the reader's surrender (plant wrong assumptions, shatter them, take control) and runs his career on one mantra: *"No one should make more off of the book than the person who wrote it."* Craft and commerce are one system: *"Upset people don't tend to write as best as they can."*

## The Decision Framework (Pre-Flight for every workflow)

Before producing anything under this skill, answer:
1. **Passion tank**: Is the writer/operator genuinely fascinated? ("If I'm fascinated, everyone else will be too. If I'm not... nobody else will be.") Not full → percolate, don't produce.
2. **Premise food**: Does the premise contain every thread the piece needs? (Jacket-copy test: crime/conflict + maximal stakes + witness/protagonist = "off to the races.") If stuck at page 50, fix the premise, not the discipline.
3. **Plausibility law**: Does every extreme act carry motivation of MATCHING magnitude? The only binding constraint.
4. **Word earning**: Would every word survive "why does it have to be in the film?" — "I don't know" is not an answer.
5. **Reader location**: Is the output shaped for where the audience actually consumes? Never force a format.

## Genius Patterns (operating instructions)

1. **The Big Pop** — Open with engineered ambiguity; cajole the reader into 2+ unfounded assumptions; shatter them via the least likely direction. When they lose the bet, they surrender analysis and immerse. (Decker: two bodies → two UNRELATED killers. Street scene: the "hinky" one is the victim.)
2. **Write in the Trenches** — No outlines ("Tom Brady in practice" vs. 11 guys trying to kill him). Draft linearly; make decisions when words count; no kicking cans. Heavy lifting happens in edit.
3. **Edges Inward** — A novel is "a series of short stories with connectivity." Firm the puzzle edges, work in. Write 2 chapters, assess, repeat; mid-build the mind becomes a computer (page 20 known, page 315 planned). Shrink the big thing; never hold it all at once.
4. **Baggage Unrelated to Plot** — Series leads carry pre-existing wounds independent of the current plot (Decker's trauma, Pine's kidnapped twin). Two layers per book: plot + emotional unpeeling. Baggage is the renewable series fuel; plots are disposable.
5. **Motivation-Magnitude Matching** — Incredible acts require equal motivation. Reader standard: "Would I have made that decision? Maybe not. But I understand why it's plausible."
6. **Core-With-Flexibility** — Characters have core principles plus the latitude to change; the reader must SEE how they got from A to B. Reveal the true core by colliding two good values (loyalty vs. justice).
7. **The Gettysburg Standard** — 277 words beat two hours. Dialogue moves action or reveals character; speeches are lazy writing. Career discipline: write it in 10 words where you once used 100.
8. **Two-Purpose Scene Rule** — Every scene does ≥2 of: advance plot / flesh character / arm the reader. From film rooms: one purpose isn't good enough. Reorder scenes for micro-timing — one page's difference in reveal changes everything.
9. **The 99% Research Cut** — Research everything, print 1%. Over 3-4 sentences of atmosphere = the page flip. One eyewitness detail (the mechanical cowboy) buys total suspension of disbelief.
10. **Pen Endings** — Ship the placeholder ending; disassociate (walk, shower, other project); the subconscious grinds 4-5 plausible endings; return and amalgamate the best pieces.
11. **Percolation Gate** — Ideas must percolate until the pull is physical ("running to my desk"). No word quotas — "what if they all suck?" Writing fast = crystallized = good; slow = upstream defect.
12. **Knowledge Pots** — Originality = combining YOUR unique knowledge stores ("nobody else's knowledge pots are like mine") + answering the big unanswered question of a true story (Panama Papers → "what do you do with $30 trillion?" → The 6:20 Man).
13. **One-Day Eyewitness Research** — Walk the character through ONE full day (clothes, food, transit, streets) = 90% of world-building. Primary artifacts only: the 1958 Esso map, 1953 Vegas footage frame-by-frame, face-to-face interviews ("everybody lies... body language counts"). Hardware (do the thing) + software (ask why they do it).
14. **Slow-Motion Peak (Unforgiven Principle)** — Finite go-for-it setups per work; wait for them, then SLOW DOWN. Sped-up action loses consequence; the five-minute death gives the whole story gravitas. Hitchcock: "no terror in the bang, only in the anticipation." Leave the smash to imagination.
15. **Author Economics** — The math: $30 hardcover → publisher receives ~$15 wholesale → 15% list royalty ($4.50) = ~30% of publisher net; they keep ~70%. Sequence: build fan base (10 years touring, going everywhere) → gain leverage → raise the deal ("we're not doing royalties anymore; we're partners — you get a 30% return on your money, I get the rest"). Negotiation vectors: books per contract, advance vs. back-end split, genre-adjusted pricing, release-window fit, creative-arc fit. "They have no chance to agree to it if you never raise it."
16. **Find Readers Where They Are** — "A good story will sell — you just have to find the readers wherever they might be." Format follows audience (audio/Spotify, ebook, print); marketing changes, writing doesn't. Hold distributors accountable annually: Why hasn't the marketing plan changed in 10 years? Why this release date? Why am I not in those outlets? You're marketing me as X when I'm Y.

## Hidden Knowledge (the apprentice layer)

- **Control transfer requires a lost bet** — readers surrender only after being proven wrong; engineered humility opens immersion.
- **Compression budget = character equity** — one line does the work of ten ONLY for a pre-built character/brand. Terse voice is spent equity, not style.
- **Premise as plant food** — first chapters must contain every thread's nutrition; "stuck" is a premise defect.
- **Fairness is a creative input** — fix the deal to protect the prose.
- **Deletion-first editing** — first drafts are overwritten; cut, then reorder, then add.
- **Read the masters, not the manuals** — reverse-engineer great novels ("why was that a great story?"); the best books grow with you.
- **Two hats or no career** — content creator AND business person; know more than your agent; read the damn contracts; no one cares about your career more than you.
- **Words win on visceral reaction** — "if you can get the person reading them to have a visceral reaction, mission accomplished." Books move mountains.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars (calibration anchors)

1. **Amos Decker Big Pop** — two bodies, two floors; reader assumes one killer + connected deaths; both wrong. "That puts me, the writer, back in control."
2. **Absolute Power jacket copy** — "…an unthinkable abuse of power and criminal conspiracy: a vicious murder involving the U.S. president… But one unlikely witness saw everything." Premise = story engine = sales asset in one paragraph.
3. **The Ellen Burstyn Cut** — hurricane-wrecked schedule, Academy Award winner waiting; he cut the page to ONE line and let her act. "One line of dialogue can do the work of 10."
4. **1953 Vegas frame-by-frame** — 25-minute dashcam film studied frame by frame; harvested the mechanical cowboy; one detail = full period immersion.

**Anti-exemplar**: The two-hour speaker before Lincoln at Gettysburg — remembered by no one. Also: research dumps ("textbook = flip book"), outline-as-procrastination, word-count quotas, the author who never asks why royalties are falling.

## Anti-Patterns (reject on sight)

- Openings that confirm the reader's first guess (no pop, no control).
- Dialogue speeches; exposition through talk; 100 words doing 10 words' work.
- Scenes justified by "I did the research" or "it's beautiful" — ego writing.
- Rushed climaxes — consequence-free action ("12 guys are dead, so what?").
- Baggage-free protagonists (robots walking a plot).
- Motivation smaller than the act it must justify.
- Trend-chasing premises with an empty passion tank.
- Signing any deal without mapping the actual money flow; creative-only self-image ("I'm not a business person" = future unhappy person).
- Forcing an audience to consume in a format they've left.

## Quality Rubric (score every deliverable)

| Criterion | 4 (Acceptable) | 7 (Good) | 10 (Savant) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening control | Hooks with novelty | Wrong assumption induced, later corrected | 2+ planted assumptions shattered early; reader surrenders |
| Word earning | Clean, some flab | Most lines self-justify | Every word earns its place; 100→10; skim at your peril |
| Scene purpose | Entertains | One clear function each | ≥2 purposes each; order micro-timed to the page |
| Character plausibility | Consistent | Motivation stated | Motivation magnitude = act magnitude; "I see exactly how they got there" |
| Stakes & consequence | Something at risk | Choices cost | Faustian choice, no clean exit; consequences land in slow motion |
| Detail immersion | Setting described | Researched texture | One eyewitness detail does a paragraph's work; 99% invisible |
| Emotional two-layer | Plot resolves | Character changes | Plot + baggage unpeel together; suffering → response → earned change |
| Zeitgeist connectivity | Topical nods | Echoes current anxieties | "This matters for my life right now" |
| Business fairness (deal work) | Terms understood | Money flow mapped | Leverage → partnership economics; author keeps the lion's share |

## Voice & Style DNA

Plain-spoken, blunt, story-first. Sports and trades analogies (Tom Brady, 800-lb squat, hockey misdirection). Self-deprecating about early work ("I would have edited the hell out of them"). Zero preciousness about process, total preciousness about words earning their place. Teaches by anecdote, never by framework name.
