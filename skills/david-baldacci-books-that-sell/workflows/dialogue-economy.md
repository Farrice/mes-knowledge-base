# /baldacci-dialogue-economy — The Gettysburg Compression Pass

## Pre-Flight Gate (genius.md Decision Framework)
- Compression budget check: is the character/brand pre-built? One line does the work of ten ONLY with established equity. Unbuilt entity → build first (or compress less aggressively).

## Skill Acquisition
- Load `genius.md` (pattern 7; hidden knowledge: Compression Budget = Character Equity; exemplar 3)
- Load `references/source-quotes.md` § Dialogue & Scenes

## Execution
1. **Flag every speech.** Any dialogue run > 2-3 sentences is suspect: "People don't just sit there and give speeches." Lengthy dialogue = lazy writing doing work that belongs to action or interior monologue.
2. **Reassign the cargo.** For each flagged run, decide what the speech was carrying: plot info → action or narration; emotion → gesture/silence/context ("let her act, let her emote"); character reveal → one carefully chosen line.
3. **Run the Burstyn cut.** Draft the full version, then cut everything except the ONE line that matters. Check what the surrounding context (built character, scene pressure) now does for free.
4. **Apply 100→10.** Line-level compression across all remaining dialogue. Target: every word "has earned its right to be there." Character-calibrate: a man of few words (Decker) spends words like currency; when he speaks, it matters.
5. **Verify function.** Every surviving exchange moves action forward or reveals character — ideally both. Anything else goes.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Fiction dialogue | Full protocol |
| Video/reel script | Spoken lines against visual context — let the footage act |
| Ad copy / CTA | The one-line standard: cut until the single loaded line remains |
| Email | Dialogue = your voice on the page; compress the ask to one sentence |
| Speech/keynote | Gettysburg vs. the two-hour man: 277 words that get remembered |
| Client testimonials | Cut to the one line that carries the whole transformation |

## Output Requirements
- The compressed text (before/after for each major cut)
- Compression ledger: words in → words out, cargo reassignment notes
- Equity note: where compression relied on built character/brand (and where it couldn't)
