# /baldacci-audience-downshift — Simple, Never Dumb (Amplify addition)

> Source vein (thinner than the core workflows — one focused passage, applied honestly): "The only difference between adult fiction and writing for younger audiences is word choice. All the rest is the same, if not even more heightened... you write down to them at your peril. Just because they might be 10, 11, 14 doesn't mean they don't know what a good book is."

## Pre-Flight Gate (genius.md Decision Framework)
- Is the target audience actually down-register (kids, beginners, lay readers) — or just busy? Busy experts need compression (`/baldacci-dialogue-economy`), not downshift.
- The work must already pass its craft gates at adult/expert level; downshift is a translation pass, not a rescue.

## Skill Acquisition
- Load `genius.md` (patterns 7, 14; Recognition Test)
- Load `references/source-quotes.md` § Pacing & Peaks (Hitchcock lead-up)

## Execution
1. **Move ONE dial: word choice.** Swap vocabulary the audience hasn't earned; keep sentence rhythm adult. Never simplify the ideas, stakes, or structure — "kids are incredibly sophisticated these days."
2. **Heighten, don't relax, the standards.** Attention must be captured quickly and kept — "it's not like I can have a 50-page buildup for a 14-year-old." Tighten the opening; the Big Pop budget shrinks to the first page/paragraph.
3. **Suspense without explosions.** Hold attention "not necessarily by blowing stuff up or somebody dying" but by interesting characters doing interesting things + the felt sense "that something significant is coming — they just have to hold on a little bit longer" (Hitchcock lead-up, drain-not-knife).
4. **Leave imagination room.** Don't spell everything out; the reader completes the picture — this is MORE important down-register, not less.
5. **Peril check.** Read the result hunting for any line that talks down — explained jokes, moralizing, over-signposted transitions. Cut on sight.
