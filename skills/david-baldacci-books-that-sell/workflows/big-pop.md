# /baldacci-big-pop — The Assumption-Shatter Opening

## Pre-Flight Gate (genius.md Decision Framework)
- Passion tank full for this piece? If not, percolate — don't force an opening onto a dead premise.
- Is there a premise with food in it? A Big Pop can't rescue a threadbare premise.
- Cold audience? The Pop is built for readers with preconceptions to weaponize. Warm-audience continuations may not need it.

## Skill Acquisition
- Load `genius.md` (patterns 1, 14; exemplar 1; anti-patterns)
- Load `references/source-quotes.md` § Reader Control

## Execution
1. **Enumerate the bets.** List the 2-4 assumptions a cold reader WILL make from the situation in the first lines. (Decker: "one killer to watch for" + "the two deaths are connected" — neither grounded in any fact.) These must be assumptions the reader forms naturally — cajoled, not forced.
2. **Design the ambiguity field.** Open a scene/lead with multiple live directions. Do not confirm any assumption; give the reader rope.
3. **Pick the least likely direction** that remains fully plausible under the motivation-magnitude law. The Pop fails if it's a cheat; it must be retro-obvious.
4. **Time the shatter early** — first act, not midpoint. The Pop's job is to end second-guessing at the start: "sit back, relax, don't second-guess me anymore."
5. **Bank the surrender.** Immediately after the shatter, advance into the story proper while attention is at maximum ("that's when I get their full undivided attention").

## Content Type Adaptations
| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Novel/ebook chapter 1 | Full scene-level Pop; assumptions about genre/killer/direction |
| Sales page / VSL lead | Assumption about what's being sold or who's to blame; shatter reframes the mechanism |
| Email | Subject line plants the bet; first two lines break it |
| LinkedIn/short post | One planted assumption max; shatter by line 3 (hook budget) |
| Case study | Reader assumes the obvious cause of the win; reveal the unlikely lever |

## Output Requirements
- The opening itself (scene, lead, or hook) — finished prose, not a plan
- The bet sheet: each planted assumption + where it's induced + where it shatters
- One-line plausibility check: why the reveal is fair (motivation matches)

## Quality Gate (genius.md rubric: Opening control)
- [ ] ≥2 assumptions are PLANTED (cajoled by the text), not merely possible
- [ ] The reveal takes the least likely — yet fully plausible — direction
- [ ] Shatter lands early enough to transfer control for the whole piece
- [ ] No cheat: rereading, the truth was available (motivation-magnitude holds)
- [ ] Every word of the opening earns its place (no throat-clearing)
