# /baldacci-slow-the-peak — Slow-Motion Peak Engineering

## Pre-Flight Gate (genius.md Decision Framework)
- Is this genuinely a peak? "You have a finite number of setups where you can just go for it." Spending the slow-down on a non-peak burns the budget.
- Has the climb been built? A peak without 300 pages (or 30 lines) of anticipation is just an event.

## Skill Acquisition
- Load `genius.md` (pattern 14; exemplars; anti-patterns: rushed climaxes)
- Load `references/source-quotes.md` § Pacing & Peaks

## Execution
1. **Budget the peaks.** Map the piece; mark the 1-3 go-for-it moments. Everything else is climb, drop, or breathing room — "I can't keep you up at the top of the roller coaster all the time."
2. **Build anticipation, not surprise.** Hitchcock: "no terror in the bang, only in the anticipation." Let the reader sense something significant is coming and make them hold on through the pacing that gets characters into place.
3. **At the peak: decelerate.** Counterintuitive and non-negotiable — "the best action is when you slow it down." Stretch the moment (Unforgiven's five-minute death): let consequence register on BOTH sides (the one acted upon AND the actor — Eastwood was changed too). Speed kills impact: "12 guys are dead, so what?"
4. **Leave the smash to imagination where stronger.** Psycho shows blood in the drain, not the knife. Decide what NOT to show.
5. **Exit with gravitas.** After the peak, the whole story should weigh more. Come down deliberately; don't sprint to the next scene.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Novel climax | Full protocol; slow the decisive scene to sentence-level time |
| Launch sequence | The reveal email/post is the peak — decelerate there, build anticipation across the runway |
| Case study | The turn (before→after moment) gets slow-motion treatment: costs, doubt, consequence |
| Storytelling post | One peak per post; anticipation in the hook-to-body climb |
| Webinar/VSL pitch | The offer reveal = the peak; slow it, let stakes land; never rush to price |

## Output Requirements
- Peak map: the piece's 1-3 designated peaks + the climbs feeding them
- The rewritten peak passage(s) in slow motion — actual prose
- Withheld list: what was deliberately left to imagination
- Consequence check: who is changed by the moment and where that's visible

## Quality Gate
- [ ] Peaks are finite and deliberately chosen (≤3)
- [ ] Anticipation is built before the bang; the reader can feel it coming
- [ ] The peak itself decelerates — time stretches, consequence registers
- [ ] Both actor and acted-upon carry the aftermath
- [ ] Something is left to the imagination
