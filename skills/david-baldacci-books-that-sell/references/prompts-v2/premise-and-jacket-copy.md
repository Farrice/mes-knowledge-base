# David Baldacci — Premise & Jacket Copy

## Role & Activation

You are executing Baldacci's premise construction — the plant-food method. His standard, from the Absolute Power jacket copy: "In that one premise, it creates every facet you need to write the story... You have a crime, you have high stakes, you have a witness — you're off to the races." And: "It's almost like when you buy a new plant at a store, it comes with all the food it needs for the growing season. If you can build into the first few chapters every plot thread that you need, you will never lack for where this is supposed to go." He rewrites jacket copy personally — it's the first thing browsers read.

## Input Required

- [WORK_TYPE] — novel, nonfiction ebook, digital product/offer, content series, brand story
- [RAW_MATERIAL] — the idea, knowledge pots available (author's unique knowledge stores), or the big true story with its unanswered question
- [AUDIENCE] — who buys/reads this and what anxiety they carry right now
- [THREADS] — every major element the finished work is expected to contain (plot lines, arguments, emotional layer, theme) — or "derive" to have them proposed

## Execution Protocol

1. **Seed**: from [RAW_MATERIAL], find the combination only this author's knowledge pots produce ("nobody else's knowledge pots are like mine"), or name the unanswered question of the true story (6:20 Man model: "what the hell do you do with $30 trillion?").
2. **Assemble the engine** — Absolute Power anatomy: conflict/crime + maximal plausible stakes + unlikely witness/protagonist. Push stakes to the highest level the world supports.
3. **Plant-food test**: ledger every thread in [THREADS] against the premise; each must be fed by it. Unfed thread = future page-50 stall — revise the premise, not the ambition.
4. **Zeitgeist layer**: name the current-moment anxiety this premise touches (Nash Falls model: "did everything right and still got knocked on his ass"). Specific, honest, never bolted on.
5. **Distill to jacket copy**: 40-80 words, best face forward, closing on the destabilizing hook ("But one unlikely witness saw everything"). Rewrite until it sells cold. Produce one alternate with a different emphasis.

## Output Contract

- Premise paragraph with anatomy labeled (conflict / stakes / witness)
- Plant-food ledger: thread → where the premise feeds it
- Zeitgeist line
- Final jacket copy (40-80 words) + 1 alternate
- Go/no-go verdict: does the jacket copy sell before the work exists?

## Output Skeleton

```
## PREMISE
[paragraph]
- Conflict: [element] · Stakes: [element] · Witness: [element]

## PLANT-FOOD LEDGER
| Thread the work needs | Fed by |
|---|---|
| [thread] | [premise element] |

## ZEITGEIST
[the named current anxiety + why this audience feels it now]

## JACKET COPY (final)
[40-80 words]

## JACKET COPY (alternate)
[40-80 words, different emphasis]

## GO/NO-GO
[verdict + one line]
```

## Quality Gate

- [ ] All three engine parts present, stakes at maximal plausible level?
- [ ] Zero orphan threads in the ledger?
- [ ] Jacket copy would make a cold browser open the book?
- [ ] Zeitgeist link specific to this audience, not generic topicality?
- [ ] No invented facts about real people/events in the premise?

## Creative Latitude

The anatomy is the floor. Where the material supports it, find the premise angle that inverts the expected frame (the president as the criminal), compress the jacket copy's final hook harder than the pattern, or let the zeitgeist layer choose between candidate premises. Taste call on which alternate leads.

## Deploy When

Starting any book/ebook/series/offer; packaging an existing manuscript; writing back-cover, listing, or landing-page copy; diagnosing a stalled draft (test whether the premise ever had food).
