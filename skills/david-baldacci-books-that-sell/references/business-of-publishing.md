# The Baldacci Author-Economics Model

The business layer most craft extractions lack. Source-grounded from the Perell interview; extend to any creator-distributor relationship (trad publishing, KDP, licensing, platform rev-share, client retainers).

## The Mantra

**"No one should make more off of the book than the person who wrote it. No one."**

For most authors it's the flip: publisher keeps the lion's share. The fix is sequenced — leverage first, then restructure.

## The Royalty Math (know it cold before any negotiation)

- $30 retail hardcover → publisher sells to wholesaler at ~$15 (nobody receives list price).
- Author's "15% royalty" is computed on the $30 list = $4.50/copy.
- $4.50 against the publisher's $15 receipts = author gets ~30% of publisher revenue; **publisher keeps ~70%** (minus print/distribution costs — real, but nowhere near 70%).
- The advance must earn out before any royalty flows. A book can net the publisher $30M while the author "never sees another cent" past the advance.
- Baldacci's counterfactual: staying on royalties = "nine figures left on the table."

## The Leverage Sequence (non-skippable order)

1. **Build the fan base first** — 10 years of touring, self-funded travel, saying yes to every room. No leverage, no renegotiation.
2. **Know the money flow better than your agent** — "so you can ask intelligent questions so that years later you won't be surprised."
3. **Raise the deal** — "We're not doing royalties anymore. We're partners. You get a ~30% return on your money; I get the rest. If you can find a guaranteed 30% return elsewhere, feel free." They did the deal.
4. **Stay partners** — reciprocity: when the publisher asks you to go to Iowa and Nebraska, go. "You're together, you're building something."

## Negotiation Vectors (the fault lines to walk into any deal knowing)

| Vector | Question to settle |
|---|---|
| Contract scope | Book-by-book vs. multi-book (Baldacci: 6-book/3-year deals)? Where does it land in your creative arc? |
| Advance vs. back-end | Lower advance for richer profit share? Unequal splits are negotiable ("maybe I get more than you because you're paying me less upfront"). |
| Genre-adjusted pricing | A 1950s detective series prices differently than a flagship thriller — don't accept flat terms across unlike books. |
| Release timing | Christmas window = heavy-hitter territory; spring/summer may fit the content better. Wrong slot = getting creamed. |
| Positioning | "You're marketing me as this when I think I'm really that." |
| Distribution | Which outlets, which formats, what metadata; audit annually. |

## The Annual Accountability Questions (put to any distributor/platform)

- Why hasn't the marketing plan changed in 10 years?
- Why am I not in the outlets I used to be in?
- Why this release date and not that one?
- Why are sales/royalties trending down — and what specifically changes?

Authors whose careers "went down the toilet" didn't ask — "they didn't know to ask."

## The Two-Hats Law

- Hat 1: content creator (most important job).
- Hat 2: business person (second most important job).
- "If you want to do this for free, live in the dream state... If this is your livelihood, you have two hats." Read the damn contracts; ignorance is no excuse after signing.
- Fairness protects the art: "Upset people don't tend to write as best as they can."

## Distribution Doctrine

- "A good story will sell — you just have to find the readers wherever they might be."
- Format follows audience (audio/Spotify surging; ebook; print) — **marketing changes, the writing never does**.
- Deliver in the reader's format as efficiently as possible; engineer the path to the NEXT purchase.
- Metadata is now table stakes; 20 years ago nobody knew the word.

## Transfer Map (Antigravity deployments)

| Baldacci move | Farrice deployment |
|---|---|
| Royalty math before signing | KDP royalty tiers (35/70%), Gumroad/Substack fees, platform rev-shares — map net per unit before pricing |
| Fan base → raise the deal | Audience growth → renegotiate client retainers, licensing, affiliate splits |
| Annual accountability questions | Quarterly channel audit: why is this platform's reach declining; which format is the audience moving to |
| Release-window fit | Launch timing vs. niche seasonality; don't launch against heavy hitters |
| Partnership reciprocity | Over-deliver for platforms/clients that structurally over-pay you |
