# Baldacci — Verbatim Source Quotes (Perell interview, 2026)

Grounding corpus for prompts and workflows. Quote, don't paraphrase, when an exemplar is needed.

## Reader Control / The Big Pop
- "I love to make readers — I sort of cajole them into making assumptions about the plot early on. When they turn out to be wrong, that's when I get their full undivided attention."
- "Open up a scene that has a lot of ambiguity and multiple directions it could go in and then just blow them out of the water by picking probably the least likely one."
- "You're not going to figure any of this out. You don't know where this is going. So sit back, relax, don't second guess me anymore. And then enjoy the ride."

## Drafting
- "I like to write in the trenches — I like to write when the words actually count... Doing an outline is like Tom Brady in practice."
- "If you're in the story, there's no figuring out later... when you're in the moment, immersed like that, my synapses fire off better. I see the whole field of possibilities and I make better choices."
- "I'm not writing a novel. I'm writing a series of short stories that have this connectivity between them."
- "You're going from multiplication tables to physics... You write the big stuff by making it smaller as much as you can."
- "Would you try to do an 800 lb squat on your first day in the gym?" (write short stories before novels)
- "First drafts are usually overwritten."
- "If I write fast, that is a good thing because it is crystallized and I know where I want to go."
- "What if they all suck, but the next word was going to be just awesome?" (against daily quotas)

## Character
- "They all have baggage unrelated to the plot." (Decker, Pine, King & Maxwell)
- "You shouldn't have a character just to be a robot going through the plot."
- "They want to see them suffer. They want to see them redeem themselves."
- "If I'm asking a character to do something that's an incredible act... I have to have motivation to match that action."
- "Core principles, core standings, core beliefs — with flexibility."
- "The only thing I'm bound by is plausibility."
- "I want to get as far away from perfection as I can... vulnerable and fragile and uncertain."
- "I don't want to put people against the wall. I want to put them through the wall and then see what comes out the other side."
- "You never will know the real person if you've only seen them in good times."

## Dialogue & Scenes
- "The Gettysburg Address is 277 words long. The guy who spoke before Lincoln spoke for two hours. Nobody remembers who the hell he was."
- "Every word had earned its right to be there."
- "I cut everything out except one line of dialogue. And then I let her do what she does." (Ellen Burstyn)
- "One line of dialogue can do the work of 10."
- "If I used to write it in a 100 words, I do my very best to write it in 10."
- "If a scene doesn't have at least two purposes, why is it in the movie?"
- "If a scene or even a line of dialogue doesn't advance the plot, doesn't flesh out a character or doesn't give the reader information they need... why is it there?"
- "Research is important for a book. You have to leave 99% of it out. Otherwise you're writing a textbook... The textbook is a flip book."
- "It's almost like micro timing. You release that information just a few beats too soon... a page difference can have enormous impact."
- "You can skim my books at your peril."

## Pacing & Peaks
- "You have a finite number of setups in a novel where you can just go for it."
- "I can't keep you up here all the time at the top of the roller coaster."
- "When you write action, the best action is when you slow it down... When you speed it up, people miss the impact. They miss the consequence." (Unforgiven)
- "There was no terror in the bang, only in the anticipation of it." (Hitchcock, quoted by Perell, affirmed)
- "People don't remember snapshots, they remember longer form stuff."
- "When I write those scenes, I don't rush through them. I slow it all down. I want it to last."

## Endings & Process
- "I write what's called a pen ending. I put a pin in it... four or five different scenarios, all of which are plausible... or an amalgam."
- "I solved more plot problems and pen endings in the shower than any other place."
- "I've learned over time to let it percolate a little longer. Sometimes I would pull the trigger too soon and write myself into a wall."
- "If I am fascinated by what I'm writing about, everyone else will be fascinated too. If I'm not interested in it, I don't care how well I write it, nobody else is going to be interested either."
- "I have all these little knowledge pots across this huge spectrum... I put it together and it's totally original because nobody else's knowledge pots are like mine."
- "The question they didn't answer was: what the hell do you do with $30 trillion?" (The 6:20 Man seed)

## Research
- "I put Archer to bed and I woke him up in the morning and I took him through one day... That gave me pretty much 90% of the stuff I needed to create that world of the 1950s."
- "I went on Etsy searching for a map of Los Angeles during that time period... that became my Bible geographically." (1958)
- "I went frame by frame on that video... I was like an eyewitness. I was there in 1953 walking down that street." (Vegas; the mechanical cowboy)
- "If you're going to go more than three or four sentences describing stuff... you're going to get the page flip."
- "Body language counts for a lot with me... I just assumed everybody was lying to me." (face-to-face interviews)
- "The hardware... jumping off parachute towers, walkalongs with police. The software... you're 18, we're in the middle of a war in Iraq — why are you volunteering?"

## Premise & Packaging
- "Set in Washington D.C., this fascinating thriller... an unthinkable abuse of power and criminal conspiracy: a vicious murder involving the U.S. president and a cover-up orchestrated by his zealously loyal chief of staff and the Secret Service. But one unlikely witness saw everything." (Absolute Power jacket copy)
- "In that one premise, it creates every facet you need to write the story... You have a crime, you have high stakes, you have a witness — you're off to the races."
- "It's almost like when you buy a new plant at a store, it comes with all the food it needs for the growing season. If you can build into the first few chapters every plot thread that you need, you will never lack for where this is supposed to go."
- "I've rewritten jacket copy because I want it to present the best face forward."

## Business of Publishing
- "No one should make more off of the book than the person who wrote it. No one."
- "I spent 10 years building a fan base up... even when I wasn't on tour, I did my own travel, publicity, marketing."
- "We're not going to do royalties anymore. We're going to be partners — you're going to get a 30% return on your money, and I'm going to get the rest... They were like, 'We'll do the deal.'"
- Royalty math: "$30 book... the publisher sells that to the wholesaler for 15 bucks... you're getting 15% of that $30 even though the publisher is only getting $15... they're keeping 70%."
- "They could have made $30 million on one book and I never saw another cent... I would have left nine figures on the table."
- "Work your ass off, build your fan base up, and when it comes time to renegotiate, just say: we're not doing the royalty thing anymore. We're partners in a business."
- "They have no chance to agree to it if you never raise it."
- "No one's going to care about your career more than you do."
- "Upset people don't tend to write as best as they can."
- "Why has your marketing plan not changed in the last 10 years? Why am I not in these outlets I used to be in? You're marketing me as this when I think I'm really that."
- "You have to be the content creator and you have to be the business person. If you don't do both, you're not going to be the happy person."
- "Read the damn contracts... Ignorance is no excuse once you sign the document."
- "Bring the same level of attention to detail to the business side as you do to your books."
- "A good story will sell, you just have to find the readers to read it wherever they might be... You can't force them to read in a format they don't want to read in."
- "Writing the books is the most important thing you're going to do. The second most important thing is understanding how the business works for you — or doesn't."

## Reading & Mission
- "It's not reading a book to learn how to write a book. It's reading a book just to read a book... then ask yourself: why was it a great story?"
- "The best books grow with you." (Mockingbird at 10 vs. in college)
- "Words are powerful if you can get the person who's reading them to have a visceral reaction."
- "Bombs can eliminate mountains, but books can move them. And I'd rather move mountains than eliminate them."
- "Books are like body armor against bigotry... against every bad thing in the whole world."
- "Travel is fatal to prejudice... I traveled the world without a plane ticket or passport through reading books."
