# Lamott → Wright Thompson Observation Chain

## Role
Lamott captures raw material — the rag bag of scraps, threads, and silk. She teaches you to *notice*. Wright Thompson teaches you to make one detail do the work of fifty. This stack sources widely (Lamott) then deploys surgically (Wright Thompson). The result: writing dense with observation but never cluttered.

**Why this order**: You can't deploy details you don't have. Lamott fills the well. Wright Thompson draws from it with precision.

## When to Use
- Building a long-form piece that needs both richness and economy
- Your writing has good structure but feels thin — it needs sensory weight
- You want to write the kind of detail that unlocks memory

## Input Required
1. **[Project]**: What you're building toward
2. **[Subject/Setting]**: The person, place, or situation at the center
3. **[Access Level]**: Are you observing firsthand? From memory? From research?

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: The writer needs at least 48 hours between Phase 1 (Lamott sourcing) and Phase 2 (Wright deployment). The observation must settle before selection. Do not rush.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Lamott Sourcing (Wide Net)
Run `/lamott-observe` — the full Observation Pipeline.

1. **Observation Walk**: 10 minutes minimum in or near the subject's world. If writing about a person, visit their neighborhood. If writing about an event, go to the venue empty.
2. **Eavesdropping Station**: If the subject involves people, listen to how they talk. Capture syntax, vocabulary, rhythm.
3. **Memory Trawl**: What do you already know about this subject from your own life? Use "There was a tree" as a prompt.
4. **Bank Target**: 25+ observations tagged and categorized. Be greedy. Capture everything. You'll use 10% of what you capture.

### Phase 2: Wright Thompson Selection (Narrow Focus)
Switch experts. Now apply Wright's detail economy.

1. **The Detail Audit**: Spread all 25+ observations on the table. For each, ask Wright's question: "Does this detail do the work of fifty? Or does it just... describe?"
2. **Three Categories**:
   - **Load-Bearing Details**: Details that reveal character, set mood, AND advance the story simultaneously. These stay.
   - **Atmospheric Details**: Details that set mood but don't do additional work. Keep 1-2. Cut the rest.
   - **Decorative Details**: Details that exist because the writer liked them. Cut all.
3. **The Wright Test**: For each load-bearing detail, verify: "If I remove this detail, does the scene lose something the reader can't get anywhere else?" If yes, it's essential. If the reader can infer it from context, cut it.
4. **Detail Stacking**: Wright's technique — place the selected detail at a structural pressure point. Details hit harder at transitions, revelations, and endings than in descriptive passages.

### Phase 3: Integration
Write (or rewrite) the piece deploying the selected details.

1. **Density Rule**: Maximum 2 load-bearing details per page. More than that creates clutter. The power of a detail is inversely proportional to how many surround it.
2. **Placement**: Details at scene openings set mood. Details at climax points amplify emotion. Details at endings create residue.
3. **Subtext**: The best details carry subtext — they say one thing on the surface and mean something else underneath. Wright's "making a detail do the work of fifty" means the reader feels the weight without being told what to feel.
4. **Polaroid Check**: After placing the details, reread. Do any new associations surface — things you didn't consciously intend? (Lamott's Polaroid Development.) If yes, consider incorporating them. The subconscious is still working.

## Output Contract
1. **Full Observation Bank**: The 25+ Lamott-sourced observations (preserved for future use)
2. **Selected Details**: The 5-8 load-bearing details chosen for this piece
3. **Deployed Draft**: The piece with details placed at structural pressure points
4. **Detail Map**: Where each selected detail lives in the piece and what work it does

## Quality Gate
1. **Sourcing breadth**: At least 25 raw observations captured (Lamott standard)
2. **Selection ruthlessness**: No more than 30% of observations made it to the draft (Wright standard)
3. **Each detail does triple duty**: Character + mood + story in every load-bearing detail
4. **Density discipline**: Max 2 load-bearing details per page
5. **Residue test**: After reading, can you still see at least one image from the piece hours later?

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: If the piece feels like a catalog of beautiful observations, Lamott dominated and Wright was underdeployed. If the piece feels sparse and cold, Wright dominated and Lamott's warmth was cut. The target: rich but disciplined. A warm room with few, perfect objects.
