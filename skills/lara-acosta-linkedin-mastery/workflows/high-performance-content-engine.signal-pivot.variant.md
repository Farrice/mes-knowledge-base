## Workflow

### Phase 1: The Positioning & Lead Magnet Foundation
*Genius Pattern: Position Before Content. Consistency without clarity is just noise.*

1. **Refine Positioning**: Validate the input positioning against the "Authority Trigger Stack." Ensure it uses specific numbers and clear outcomes.
2. **Architect the Lead Magnet**: Design one high-value asset (Template, Checklist, Swipe File, or Mini-Guide) that serves two functions:
    - **Function 1 (Capture)**: Solves a specific ICP problem for a quick win.
    - **Function 2 (Viral)**: Designed as a standalone content piece that IFPs will want to bookmark/share.
3. **Define the "Fusion Zone"**: Identify the 3-5 core arguments where your ICP's pain meets the IFP's desire for "sharable truth."

### Phase 2: The 4-3-2-1 Monthly Matrix
*Genius Pattern: The 4-3-2-1 Content Ratio. 4 Educational, 3 Story, 2 Opinion, 1 Promotional.*

Generate a 4-week (20-post) or 30-day calendar following the exact ratio:
- **40% Education (The "How-To")**: Frameworks, methodologies, and deep dives.
- **30% Story (The "SLAY")**: Personal journeys, client wins, and "Social Proof Archaeology."
- **20% Opinion (The "Hot Take")**: Contrarian perspectives and industry observations.
- **10% Promotion (The "Ask")**: Direct lead magnet drops and service highlights.

### Phase 3: Dual-Persona Content Engineering
*Genius Pattern: Every post serves TWO audiences. Reach AND Revenue.*

For every post in the calendar, engineer the "Dual-Persona Layer":
- **ICP Layer**: Annotate how this post solves a buyer's problem or demonstrates methodology.
- **IFP Layer**: Annotate why an amplifier would share this (Does it make them look smart? Does it articulate a shared feeling?).
- **The Reframe**: Adjust the angle to ensure the post hits the "Fusion Zone."

### Phase 4: High-Performance Copy Execution
*Genius Pattern: SLAY Framework + First-Principle Hook Testing.*

Execute the highest-priority posts using specific frameworks:
1. **Hook Engineering**: For every post, generate 3 viral variations using the 7 categories (Curiosity, Specific Number, Relatable Pain, etc.). Apply the "Stranger Test": Would this stop someone who has never heard of you?
2. **SLAY Storytelling**: For all "Story" posts, apply the SLAY structure:
    - **S (Story)**: Raw narrative with sensory details and tension.
    - **L (Lesson)**: The insight that transforms experience into authority.
    - **A (Application)**: Actionable steps for the reader.
    - **Y (Your Turn)**: The engagement-driving question.
3. **Authority Stacking**: Embed specific proof (numbers, names, timelines) into the body copy.

### Phase 4b: Proof Layer Audit
*Genius Patterns: P5 Authority Trigger Stack + P6 Social Proof Archaeology.*

After copy execution, run every post through this mandatory proof pass:

1. **Claim Scan**: Identify every assertion in the post. For each one:
   - Is it backed by a specific number, named example, or verifiable timeline?
   - If not, either add proof OR reframe as opinion ("In my experience..." / "What I've seen...")
   - Apply the Authority Trigger Stack: specific numbers > vague claims, named clients > "clients", timeline specificity > "eventually"

2. **Proof Archaeology**: For posts that feel proof-thin:
   - Mine for micro-proof: DM screenshots, email replies, before/after metrics, client quotes
   - Stack at least ONE of: specific metric, named transformation, time-bounded result
   - If no hard proof exists, apply "borrowed authority" — cite industry data, reference known figures, or use "when I worked with [type of client]" framing

3. **Skeptic's Red-Pen**: Read each post as someone who thinks personal branding is a scam. Mark every sentence where they'd write "prove it" or "says who." Address each mark with specificity or reframing.

### Phase 5: The Growth Engine Integration
1. **Sequence for Narrative Flow**: Ensure the Education posts build the logic for the Promotional posts.
2. **Lead Magnet Teasers**: Create "Snippet" posts that pull high-value sections from the lead magnet to create desire before the "Drop" post.
3. **Algorithm Optimization**: Format for "Dwell Time" (white space, punchy sentences, no external links).

### Phase 6: LinkedIn Signal-to-Pivot Adaptation Protocol [NEW — Cross-Pollinated]

**Why this exists**: Content plans die on Week 3. Not because the plan was bad, but because creators keep executing a static calendar while LinkedIn is handing them clear data about what their audience actually wants. This phase builds the feedback metabolism into the system itself — tuned specifically for LinkedIn's signal landscape.

*Genius Patterns: P13 Algorithm Optimization (first-hour window), P22 Post & Ghost Kill Switch (engagement protocol), P21 1+3 Comment Formula (comment strategy), P2 Dual-Persona Content Design (ICP/IFP split reading).*

#### 6A. LinkedIn Signal Classification Framework

After each content week (or after the first batch launches), classify each post's engagement into its dominant signal type:

| Signal Type | LinkedIn Indicators | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Resonance Signal** | Comments that share personal stories ("this happened to me"), tag colleagues, or say "needed this today." Comment-to-impression ratio >2%. | You hit a nerve. The TOPIC is working. Double down on this angle next week — don't just repeat the pillar, repeat the specific emotional trigger. Check: are these ICP or IFP comments? ICP resonance = money signal. |
| **Amplification Signal** | High reposts/shares relative to comments. Save count above your baseline. Content appears in "shared by" feeds. | The FORMAT is working — the content is "share-worthy" but not "reply-worthy." Extract the structural skeleton (was it a list? a contrarian frame? a visual pattern?) and reuse the format with a different pillar. |
| **Pipeline Signal** | DMs saying "tell me more," "do you offer this as a service?", new connection requests from ICP-matching profiles, profile view spikes (>2x your weekly average). | Your ICP is moving from awareness to consideration. This is the revenue signal. Respond to every DM within 24 hours. Next week: create a follow-up post that goes one layer deeper on the same topic, with Lead Magnet CTA. |
| **Ghost Signal** | High impressions (algorithm pushed it), low engagement. Likes-to-comments ratio >20:1. No saves, no shares, no DMs. | The hook worked but the body didn't deliver — or the topic was interesting but not actionable. Keep the hook angle, rewrite the body with more specificity and a stronger CTA. Apply the "Stranger Test" to the body, not just the hook. |
| **Silence Signal** | Low impressions AND low engagement. Algorithm didn't push it. First-hour engagement was flat. | Either the hook failed (LinkedIn killed it in the first 60 seconds), the timing was wrong, or the topic missed entirely. Test: repost the SAME core idea with a completely different hook and at a different time. If it dies again, the topic is wrong for this audience right now — kill it. |
| **Noise Signal** | Engagement from outside your target personas — random accounts, engagement pods, international accounts with no profile overlap. Lots of "Great post!" with no substance. | Algorithmic pollution. Do NOT optimize toward this. If a TAM-pillar post attracts only noise, narrow the topic closer to your Growth pillar. Check: is the P22 Post & Ghost engagement protocol being followed? If you're not engaging with comments yourself, LinkedIn fills the void with noise. |

#### 6B. LinkedIn Adaptation Moves (One Variable Per Cycle)

Based on signal classification, execute ONE of these moves per content cycle. The one-variable rule is non-negotiable — it prevents "system panic" where one bad post causes a full calendar rewrite.

1. **Pillar Weight Shift**: If Sales/Growth posts consistently generate Pipeline Signals while Education posts generate Ghost Signals, shift from 4-3-2-1 to 3-3-3-1 for two weeks. Track whether Pipeline Signals maintain. Rebalance after 2 cycles.

2. **Format Transfer**: If a specific format (numbered list, personal story opener, contrarian hook) shows Amplification Signals, replace the weakest-performing post's format with the winning one. Keep the pillar and persona targeting the same — isolate the format as the variable.

3. **Hook Angle Rotation**: If Ghost Signals dominate (high impressions, dead engagement), the hook-to-body connection is broken. Test 3 different hook angles for the same topic: pain-based, curiosity-based, proof-based. Run for one week. The winner becomes the default hook angle for that pillar.

4. **Depth Escalation**: If Pipeline Signals or Depth comments ("can you write more about X?") appear, create a 2-post mini-series the following week. Post 1 = the original angle restated with new proof. Post 2 = the deep-dive with Lead Magnet CTA. This directly activates P23 Revenue Bridge Architecture.

5. **Topic Kill**: If a topic generates Silence Signals on two separate posts, remove it from the rotation permanently. Replace with a topic pulled from your Pipeline Signal backlog (subjects that triggered DMs or connection requests). Dead topics don't come back — they get recycled through a different angle only.

6. **Persona Rebalance**: If IFP engagement is high (shares, saves, broad reach) but ICP engagement is flat (no DMs, no Pipeline Signals), add one "ICP-only" post that is deliberately narrower and more technical. Accept lower impressions. Track for DM/connection request lift over 2 weeks.

#### 6C. The Weekly LinkedIn Signal Read (5-Minute Protocol)

Every Friday (or end of content week), before planning the next cycle:

1. **Pull the numbers**: For each post — impressions, comments (count AND quality), reposts, saves, profile views that day, DMs received, connection requests from ICP-matching profiles, Lead Magnet clicks if applicable.
2. **Classify each post**: Assign the dominant signal type from 6A. A post can show multiple signals — pick the LOUDEST one.
3. **Map the ICP/IFP split**: For posts with Resonance or Pipeline Signals, note whether the engagement came from ICP profiles (revenue potential) or IFP profiles (reach potential). This tells you whether you're building audience or building pipeline.
4. **Identify the week's dominant signal**: Which signal type appeared most often across all posts?
5. **Select ONE adaptation move** from 6B that responds to the dominant signal.
6. **Log it**: "Week N: [Dominant Signal] on [Post Type/Pillar]. Adapting: [Move Selected]. Rationale: [one sentence]."

**Baseline expectations for a growing LinkedIn account** (adjust for follower count):
- Education posts: expect Ghost or Amplification signals first. Resonance comes after trust builds.
- Story posts: expect Resonance signals. If you get Pipeline signals from a story post, that topic is gold.
- Opinion posts: expect Amplification OR Silence. Hot takes are binary — they either fly or die.
- Promotion posts: expect Pipeline signals from warm audience, Silence from cold audience. This is normal.

## Quality Gate
1. **The Stranger Test**: Do the hooks work without the author's name or avatar?
2. **The IFP Filter**: Is there a clear reason for a non-buyer to share this post?
3. **The 4-3-2-1 Check**: Does the monthly mix avoid being "too salesy" or "too generic"?
4. **Positioning Integrity**: Does every post reinforce the core "I help..." statement?
5. **SLAY Compliance**: Do the story posts bridge directly into actionable authority, or are they just "diary entries"?
6. **Skeptic Scan**: Could a LinkedIn cynic dismiss any post as "guru platitudes"? If yes, it needs specificity.
7. **Proof Density Check**: Does every post contain at least ONE of: specific metric, named example, timeline, or explicit opinion framing? Zero-proof posts fail this gate.
8. **[NEW] The Adaptation Test**: Does the system include clear instructions for what to do AFTER the first week? Can the creator answer "What do I change if this post gets high impressions but zero comments?" without needing to hire a strategist?
9. **[NEW] The One-Variable Rule**: Does the adaptation protocol prevent the creator from changing more than one thing per cycle? Panic-rewriting a full calendar after one bad post = system death.
10. **[NEW] The Signal-Specificity Test**: Are the signal definitions tied to actual LinkedIn metrics (DMs, saves, profile views, connection requests) — not vague platitudes about "engagement"?
