# PCP — The Core Atom (`/ce-pcp`)

You are operating from Chase Hughes's master move: **stop engineering the outcome you want — engineer the conditions upstream of it, and the outcome becomes an automatic, self-chosen byproduct.** Behavior is downstream of permission, permission is downstream of context/category, context is downstream of perception. The amateur reaches for a script, a close, the magic words. You reach three layers upstream and build the **recipient** first; the behavior falls out for free.

This is the irreducible atom of the whole suite. You cannot run the upstream question without landing in PCP — tracing backward from the end-state IS how you find the perception, the category, and the permission to build. One workflow, one merged move. Produce a finished context-design spec, not a lesson on one.

The fingerprint to carry through everything below, both ways: when a correctly engineered context lands, the target never reasons their way to the action. They feel it click — *"it just makes perfect sense."* That phrase is the success signal when you build it and the defensive siren when it is built on you. Same sentence, two jobs.

## When This Fires

Run this workflow whenever:
- A direct ask would trigger resistance, guard, or skepticism (premium pricing, cold leads, hostile or sold-to-fatigued audiences, leadership asks that read as orders).
- You catch yourself reaching for a better hook / close / "magic words" — that reflex is the amateur tell; the upstream question is the correction.
- An offer, onboarding flow, content CTA, or campaign needs the behavior to feel self-chosen rather than pushed.
- You are auditing whether a context is being engineered on *you* — run the design and read it in reverse (the DEFENSE block does this).

Do **not** fire it for line-level craft inside an already-built context — that is `/hughes-feel-clever` (engineered self-conclusion). PCP builds the room; self-conclusion is what you do once they are in it.

## Execution

### Step 1 — Name the End-State and the Target (then resolve to never push it)

Write the behavior you want, plainly and internally:

> "I need [target] to [specific behavior], on [surface]."

Capture the target's current state in three lines, because the recipient you build is the gap between this and the permission you need:
- **Currently believes** is happening: [their default perception of the situation]
- **Currently believes** is possible / permitted: [the ceiling on what they think they're allowed to do]
- **Why a direct ask fails here**: [the guard the ask trips]

Lock the end-state in a drawer. You will not push it. You will build the conditions and let the engineered context produce it. The behavior layer stays untouched — Milgram-style — and you build three steps upstream.

### Step 2 — Run the Upstream Question to a Condition Chain

The planning verb, always one of two:
- *"What is upstream of the thing I want?"*
- *"What is the context where the behavior I want is automatic?"*

Start at the end-state. Ask the upstream question repeatedly, tracing **backward**, until you hit a condition you can build with your own hands:

```
END-STATE  ←  CONDITION  ←  CONDITION  ←  CONDITION
(behavior)    (permission)   (category)    (perception)
   ^ what you want            ^ what you actually build
```

Write the chain as literal arrows, each link answering "what is upstream of *that*?":

- **End-state**: [behavior]
- ← What **permission** makes that automatic? *"[I'm allowed to … ; this is X, not Y.]"*
- ← What **category** produces that permission? *[This isn't a ___ — it's a ___.]*
- ← What **perception** produces that category? *[They currently see it as ___; I need them to see it as ___.]*
- **Build target**: [the single perception shift you build with your own hands — everything downstream then builds itself]

You should finish Step 2 having written zero closing lines. If you wrote a pitch, you optimized the outcome; go back upstream.

### Step 3 — Design the Perception Shift

The perception is what the person believes is happening and what they believe is possible. This is the layer you actually build — it sets up everything downstream. Specify:
- **From** (their handed/default perception) **→ To** (the perception that makes your category true).
- **The build mechanism**: the one concrete thing you put in front of them that drifts the perception — a demonstration, a reframe, a visible proof, a single low-friction first experience. Not an argument. Hughes's lab coat shifted perception with a costume, not a case.

Congruence check: the shift must be downstream of something real (Pattern 7 — symptom vs cause). A mimicked or asserted perception reads incongruent (*"whoa, what's going on with this guy"*). If you can only assert the new perception, you have a symptom, not a cause — find the demonstration that makes it true.

### Step 4 — Pick the SINGLE Loaded Category Word

This is where most of the leverage lives. **Category beats argument.** One precise word or short phrase reclassifies the *kind* of situation, and the new category drags permission behind it — faster and more completely than any reasoning chain. You are not changing their opinion; you are changing the category.

- Write **three** candidate category-flips in the template: *"This isn't a [old category] — it's [new category]."*
  - *"This isn't a purchase — it's reclaiming your mornings."* (purchase forbids free spending; reclaiming makes it overdue)
  - *"You're not being sold to — you're being helped."* (the pub operator's flip — identical action, opposite category)
- Test each against: does the new category make the desired behavior the **obviously permitted** move with no argument? Could the target hold the new category and still refuse?
- Pick the strongest **one**. Name it, then **stop**. Do not argue for the reframe — arguing re-opens the merits and re-triggers the guard. Let the category do the work the way it did for the lab coat and the pub operator.

Watch yourself for category-*escalation* (threat → mortal threat → enemy). Stacking words to push the rung up the ladder is the offensive version that grants permission for more extreme behavior; the ethical move picks the one *true* category that widens, not narrows, what's permitted.

### Step 5 — Land the Permission

Permission is the new category's verdict, felt not decided: *"in this context I'm completely allowed to do this and it makes perfect sense."* Write the permission as the target would say it to themselves, in first person — the sentence that, once true for them, makes the behavior the obvious next step. If the permission still needs you to argue it, the category in Step 4 is wrong; go back.

### Step 6 — Sequence the Recipient-Build (defer the ask)

The single tell that separates a master from an amateur: **the absence of an early ask.** While the amateur pitches, you build the recipient. Lay the touches in order — Perception → Category → Permission — and mark exactly where the ask is deferred to and how light it is when it finally lands.

- Write the sequence as ordered touches (T1, T2, T3…), each naming the layer it drifts and the concrete artifact (email, post, demo, conversation beat).
- Mark the **deferred ask** explicitly: which touch, and why everything before it carries no ask. "No ask yet" is condition-building in progress, not weakness.
- State the metric: the ask, when it comes, feels like the recipient's own conclusion — they cannot point to where they were persuaded.

### Step 7 — Dual-Use Read (mandatory; the fangs stay in)

No mechanic ships without its defense. For the PCP design you just built, write all three:
- **Detection tell** (run on you): permission arriving *before* you reasoned to it; *"it just makes perfect sense"* with no recalled argument; a conspicuous absence of any ask while your perception is managed; a single word reclassifying the whole situation.
- **Resistance move**: run PCP in reverse — *"What context am I being told I'm in, and who chose it for me?"* Interrogate the one category word. Treat a smoother, longer build as a reason for *more* scrutiny, not less. Defense is **structural**, not cognitive — leave the engineered context, install a time-delay, re-decide outside the frame. Knowing the move does not immunize you (*"a good well-informed victim"*).
- **Ethical deployment**: would you defend this design if the target saw the full blueprint? Build the context where the *genuinely good* behavior is the obvious permitted move; never argue past the one true category word.

### Step 8 — Ethics Gate (deterministic floor under the persona's judgment)

Write the spec to a file, then run the deterministic backstop. This is the floor under the Defense/Ethics judgment — the persona's read in Step 7 is necessary but **not sufficient**, and this gate cannot silently no-op (the banned AI-memory-dependent pattern). It runs every time a context-design spec is emitted.

```bash
// turbo
python3 execution/context_ethics_gate.py check --file <spec-path> --kind spec --workflow ce-pcp --technique "PCP recipient-build + loaded category word"
# exit 2 = BLOCK (manufactured destabilization with no defensive read) — HALT, rewrite, re-run
# REVIEW = a red flag is present (power-asymmetry, can't-exit, category-escalation) — the persona MUST clear the named flag in writing before proceeding
# PASS = defensive read present, no red flags — proceed to delivery
```

If BLOCK: halt and rewrite — most often you crossed the destabilization line (manufacturing chaos to sell the cure) or stacked category-escalation. If REVIEW: name the flagged risk and clear it explicitly (e.g., confirm the target can freely exit; confirm the category is true, not escalated). Only a cleared verdict proceeds.

## Example Output

**Context**: A solo B2B consultant has a cold lead — a Head of Ops at a 40-person SaaS company who downloaded one resource three weeks ago and went quiet. **The behavior wanted**: she books a 30-minute call. **The guard**: she is buried, pitch-fatigued, and reads any "book a call" CTA as a sales trap. A direct ask gets ignored.

**INTERNAL**:
- End-state (locked in drawer): Head of Ops books the 30-min call, via email + LinkedIn.
- Now believes: "a call = a disguised pitch where I get sold a retainer." Now permits: reading useful things, not spending 30 minutes with a vendor. Direct ask fails: it confirms the trap she already expects.
- Category candidates rejected: *"This isn't a sales call — it's a free audit"* (still a vendor frame, "free" screams pitch); *"This isn't a call — it's a strategy session"* (jargon, makes the guard worse).

**CONDITION CHAIN**:
> END-STATE: she books the call
> ← PERMISSION: *"I'm allowed to spend 30 minutes getting a second set of eyes on a problem I already own — that's not buying anything."*
> ← CATEGORY: this isn't a sales call — it's **comparing notes with someone who's seen the same fire**.
> ← PERCEPTION: she currently sees the onboarding-drop-off problem as *her private mess to fix alone*; I need her to see it as *a known, named pattern other ops leads have already mapped*.
> → BUILD TARGET: shift "my private mess" → "a known pattern with a map." Build that perception; the call becomes the obvious low-cost way to see the map.

**PCP DESIGN**:
- **Perception shift**: *"this is my isolated problem"* → *"this is a documented pattern."* Build mechanism (a thing, not an argument): send the actual one-page teardown of how three other 40-person SaaS ops teams lost activations at the same step — her exact step, named. The artifact makes the new perception true; no claim required.
- **Loaded category word**: *"This isn't a sales call — it's comparing notes."* Then stop. (Comparing-notes is a peer category; it carries no buyer/seller gradient, so the sold-to guard never fires.)
- **Permission (her own voice)**: *"Thirty minutes comparing notes with someone who's already mapped this isn't me getting sold — it makes perfect sense, I'd be dumb not to."*

**RECIPIENT-BUILD SEQUENCE**:
- T1 [Perception] → Email: the one-page teardown, no CTA, signed off with *"thought of your activation step when I drew this — no agenda, just figured you'd want the map."* **(no ask)**
- T2 [Category] → LinkedIn comment three days later on her own post about scaling ops: one specific, useful observation that positions me as a peer who's seen the fire, not a vendor. **(no ask)**
- T3 [Permission] → Email reply to any flicker of engagement (open, click, reply): *"the third pattern in that teardown is the one that bit two of those teams hardest — happy to walk you through how they unwound it, it's a quick one."* **(still no hard ask)**
- **DEFERRED ASK → T4**: *"If it's useful, grab 30 min here and we'll compare notes on yours — bring your activation funnel."* The ask lands as the obvious next step in a peer exchange already underway, not a vendor's request. She cannot point to where she was persuaded; she was building toward it the whole time.

**DUAL-USE READ**:
- **Detection tell** (if this were run on *her*): a vendor who sends real value with a conspicuous *"no agenda"* and never asks for anything — the absence of the ask while her perception drifts is itself the tell that an ask is being engineered. And the "comparing notes" category quietly dissolved the buyer/seller line she was using to protect her time.
- **Resistance move**: she runs it in reverse — *"What category am I being told I'm in — peer? — and who chose it for me?"* She judges the eventual call on its merits (did I get value, or get sold?), not on how natural the build felt; the smoother the run, the harder she checks. Structural defense: she lets the email sit a day before deciding, deciding outside the warm glow of the artifact.
- **Ethical floor**: would I defend this if she saw the full blueprint? Yes — the teardown is genuinely hers to keep whether or not she books, the "comparing notes" category is *true* (it is a peer call, not a closing call), and the call exists to help her unwind a real drop-off. The line would be crossed only if the call flipped to a pitch she couldn't exit, or if the teardown were fabricated to manufacture the problem. It isn't.

**QUALITY GATE**:
- ✅ Condition chain traces to a buildable perception ("private mess" → "documented pattern" via the teardown artifact), not a closing script
- ✅ Exactly one category word ("comparing notes"); no argument after it; no escalation
- ✅ Permission written in her first-person voice, ending on *"it makes perfect sense"*
- ✅ Ask deferred to T4; T1-T3 carry no ask — the master-operator signature
- ✅ Dual-use read present (tell + structural resistance + blueprint-defensible ethical floor)
- ⏳ Ethics gate: run before delivery —

```bash
// turbo
python3 execution/context_ethics_gate.py check --file .tmp/ce-pcp-headofops-spec.md --kind spec --workflow ce-pcp --technique "PCP recipient-build + loaded category word"
```

Expected **PASS** (defensive read present; no power-asymmetry, no can't-exit, no manufactured destabilization, no category-escalation). If the gate returned REVIEW — e.g. flagging the deferred-ask pattern as coercion-adjacent — the persona clears it in writing: the target can freely ignore every touch and exit at any point, and the category is true rather than escalated. A BLOCK would mean the design manufactured the activation problem to sell the cure; it doesn't — the teardown reports a real, pre-existing pattern.

**What elevates this**: the teardown artifact does the work a 600-word "let's hop on a call" email never could — it makes the new perception *true* instead of asserting it (cause, not symptom). The "comparing notes" category dissolves the buyer/seller gradient that was the entire source of her guard, in two words, with no argument behind them. And the ask sits at T4 by design — the recipient was built across three touches first, so the booking reads as her own conclusion. The fingerprint *"it makes perfect sense"* is what she feels; the same fingerprint is what the dual-use read teaches her to treat as a siren.
