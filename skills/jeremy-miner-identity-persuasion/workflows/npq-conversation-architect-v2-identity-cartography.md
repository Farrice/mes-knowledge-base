# NPQ Conversation Architect v2 — Identity Cartography

> Everything in v1 (8 stages, tonal annotations, 3-Layer Drill, branching logic) remains unchanged.
> This variant adds **Stage 0: Identity Cartography** — a pre-conversation diagnostic that makes every subsequent question surgically targeted.

## What Changed (Additive Only)

**Added**: Stage 0 — Identity Cartography (runs before the call or before writing)
**Unchanged**: Stages 1-8 from `npq-conversation-architect.md`

## Stage 0: Identity Cartography (Pre-Conversation)

> "If identity is upstream of everything, map the identity BEFORE you open your mouth."

### Purpose

Most NPQ practitioners enter conversations with good questions but no map. They discover the prospect's identity architecture reactively — through trial and error during the call. Identity Cartography front-loads this discovery so every question is placed with surgical intent.

### Step 0.1: Identify the Protected Identity

**The Protected Identity** is who the prospect BELIEVES they are, and will defend under pressure. It's the identity that generates their resistance.

**Signal Sources** (use whatever you have pre-call):
- How they describe themselves (LinkedIn, bio, intake form)
- What they've tried before (reveals what they see as "their kind of approach")
- Their industry's status hierarchy (what's respected vs. looked down on)
- How they booked the call / what language they used

**Diagnostic Question** (internal — never ask the prospect this directly):
> "If I told this person they need X, what identity would feel THREATENED?"

**Template Output:**
```
PROTECTED IDENTITY: "[Role] who [defining belief]"
Example: "Serious S&C coach who believes results speak for themselves"
DEFENSE TRIGGERS: [Words/concepts that will activate resistance]
Example: "content creator," "personal brand," "social media strategy," "influencer"
```

### Step 0.2: Identify the Shadow Identity

**The Shadow Identity** is who they WANT to become but can't yet claim — because it conflicts with their protected identity. This is the aspiration hiding behind the resistance.

**Signal Sources:**
- What they admire in others (who do they follow, reference, respect?)
- What they complain about ("I see these coaches with huge followings who know half of what I know")
- What they've tried and stopped (reveals desire they couldn't reconcile with identity)

**Diagnostic Question** (internal):
> "What does this person secretly want that they believe is incompatible with who they are?"

**Template Output:**
```
SHADOW IDENTITY: "[What they want to become]"
Example: "Recognized authority whose expertise reaches thousands"
ADMISSION BARRIER: [Why they can't claim this yet]
Example: "Claiming you want visibility feels vain / 'not what real coaches do'"
```

### Step 0.3: Design the Identity Bridge

**The Identity Bridge** is the reframe that connects the protected identity to the shadow identity WITHOUT threatening either. This is the single most important output — it becomes the spine of the entire conversation.

**Design Rule:** The bridge must make pursuing the shadow identity feel like a NATURAL EXTENSION of the protected identity, not a betrayal of it.

**Template:**
```
IDENTITY BRIDGE: "[Protected Identity] naturally leads to [Shadow Identity] because [logical connection that honors both]"

Example: "The coaches who take their craft most seriously are EXACTLY the ones 
whose thinking deserves to reach more people. Translation isn't selling out — 
it's the final act of mastery."
```

**Test:** Read the bridge aloud. If the prospect would feel VALIDATED (not attacked), it works. If they'd feel defensive, rewrite.

### Step 0.4: Map the Emotional Residue Chain

**Emotional Residue** = the feeling left over after answering a question. This residue becomes the emotional fuel for the next question.

**Design Rule:** Sequence questions so each answer's emotional output (frustration, recognition, longing, urgency) is the exact emotion the next question needs as its entry point.

**Template:**
```
Q1 → Expected Residue: [emotion]
    ↓ (this emotion makes Q2 land harder because...)
Q2 → Expected Residue: [emotion]
    ↓ (this emotion makes Q3 land harder because...)
Q3 → Expected Residue: [emotion]
...
```

**Example Chain:**
```
Q1: "Are you 100% happy with how many of the right people 
     know what you actually do?"
→ Residue: FRUSTRATION (they know they're invisible)
    ↓ Frustration makes them want to explain WHY

Q2: "What do you think has prevented your work from reaching 
     the people who need it most?"
→ Residue: RECOGNITION (they see the gap isn't about quality)
    ↓ Recognition opens them to considering a new frame

Q3: "When you see coaches with half your knowledge getting 
     10x your reach... what comes up for you?"
→ Residue: INJUSTICE + LONGING (they feel the unfairness)
    ↓ Injustice creates readiness for a solution

Q4: "What if the problem isn't your expertise — it's that 
     nobody's translating it into language that stops the scroll?"
→ Residue: RELIEF + CURIOSITY (the identity stays intact, 
     the blame shifts to translation)
    ↓ Relief opens them to hearing the bridge

Q5: "What would change in your business if the right people 
     could feel your coaching BEFORE they ever met you?"
→ Residue: DESIRE + VISION (they see the future state)
```

### Step 0.5: Pre-Map Objection Identity Sources

Using the Protected Identity from Step 0.1, predict which objections will surface and identify which identity each objection is protecting.

**Template:**
```
OBJECTION: "[Expected objection]"
IDENTITY SOURCE: "[What identity is this protecting?]"
PREVENTION FRAME: "[Pre-frame that makes voicing this objection identity-threatening]"

Example:
OBJECTION: "I don't need help with content — my results speak for themselves"
IDENTITY SOURCE: "Serious professional who doesn't need marketing"
PREVENTION FRAME: "The coaches I work with don't need help with content either. 
They need their EXISTING expertise translated so it reaches people who can't 
find them yet. It's not about creating content — it's about making your 
real work visible."
```

## How Stage 0 Feeds Into Stages 1-8

| Existing Stage | What Identity Cartography Adds |
|---|---|
| Stage 1 (Opening) | Avoid defense triggers identified in Step 0.1. Use language that signals respect for the protected identity. |
| Stage 2 (Connection) | Frame the end result using Shadow Identity language — what they want but won't say. |
| Stage 3 (Situation) | Ask situation questions that surface the gap between protected identity and shadow identity. |
| Stage 4 (Problem Awareness) | Use the Emotional Residue Chain from Step 0.4 to sequence the 3-Layer Drill. Each layer's questions are designed to produce specific emotional residue. |
| Stage 5 (Gap Amplification) | The gap is now framed as an identity gap, not just a business gap: "You ARE the expert, but nobody can find you." |
| Stage 6 (Identity/Objection Prevention) | Deploy Prevention Frames from Step 0.5 BEFORE objections form. |
| Stage 7 (Solution Bridge) | Present the solution as the Identity Bridge from Step 0.3 — natural extension, not a new direction. |
| Stage 8 (Commitment) | The prospect commits from their shadow identity (what they want) rather than fighting their protected identity. |

## Quality Gate (Additive to v1 gate)

- [ ] Is the Protected Identity specific to THIS prospect, not generic?
- [ ] Is the Shadow Identity something the prospect would RECOGNIZE but hasn't articulated?
- [ ] Does the Identity Bridge honor both identities without threatening either?
- [ ] Does each question in the Emotional Residue Chain build on the previous answer's emotion?
- [ ] Are defense triggers identified AND avoided in the opening?
- [ ] Are objection prevention frames rooted in identity analysis, not generic pre-frames?

> **All v1 quality gates also apply.** This is additive.
