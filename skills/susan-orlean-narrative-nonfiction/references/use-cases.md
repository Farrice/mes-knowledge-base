# Susan Orlean — Use Cases & Workflow Routing

> Specific deployment scenarios mapped to the matching `orlean-*` workflow. Use this to route: read the scenario closest to your task, run the named workflow. Read `genius.md` before any workflow. The honesty spine (the wait-what / scroll-stopping detail must be *real*) is enforced as a HARD gate inside the lead and content workflows — it is not optional.

The nine workflows divide cleanly across Orlean's three-phase method:

- **CHOOSE** (before any research): `orlean-in-love-test`, `orlean-telling-subject`
- **STRUCTURE** (digestion phase): `orlean-three-phase-process`, `orlean-card-structure`
- **PRODUCE** (writing phase): `orlean-wait-what-lead`, `orlean-yarn-engine`, `orlean-curiosity-content`
- **POLISH** (revision phase): `orlean-pull-the-punch`, `orlean-be-brutal-edit`

If you're unsure where you are, you're probably earlier than you think — Orlean's signature error to avoid is drafting before you've chosen and structured. When two workflows could apply, run the earlier-phase one first.

---

## CHOOSE — what to write about at all

### `orlean-in-love-test`
**The go/no-go subject gate. Runs BEFORE any research or structure.**

Use when:
- You (or a client/founder) are about to invest real time in a piece, book, series, or campaign and you want to confirm the subject is worth it *before* you sink weeks into it.
- Something feels marketable or dutiful ("it's about time I wrote about X") but you can't tell if you actually care.
- A ghostwriting subject was assigned and you suspect the client's enthusiasm is borrowed or performed.
- A founder's origin story or brand premise feels off and you can't name why (likely: the inauthenticity stench).

Produces: a clear verdict (GO / NO-GO / RE-CHOOSE), the prism/dimensionality check, and a flag for the inauthenticity stench audiences can smell.

Routing notes: This is the first gate in any serious project — if it returns NO-GO, *stop*; no downstream craft survives a subject you don't love (AN-6). On a GO, proceed to `orlean-telling-subject` (if the subject still needs sharpening into the small-thing/large-theme form) or straight to `orlean-three-phase-process` (if the subject is already locked).

---

### `orlean-telling-subject`
**Choose the subject by finding the small overlooked specific (door) that carries a large universal (house).**

Use when:
- You have a broad topic or theme and need to find the *specific entry point* — the overlooked detail that opens onto it (not "write about resilience" but "write about the orchid that survived the fire").
- You're picking a content pillar, a brand-story angle, a newsletter spine, a book or feature subject and want the one that has depth, not just width.
- Your instinct is to write the obvious high-demand subject and you want to deliberately reject it for the overlooked one that out-sticks it.
- You need to confirm a subject is book/series-worthy vs. one-piece-worthy (the prism test: 4–6 facets that are all one story, or it's a magazine piece, not a book).

Produces: the chosen telling subject, the prism test result (the facets named), the in-love check, and the deeper undertone named *to vibrate* — never to be stated as thesis.

Routing notes: Often run right after `orlean-in-love-test` returns GO. If the prism test fails (only one angle), the subject is one-piece scope — route to `orlean-curiosity-content` (short-form) or `orlean-yarn-engine` (single feature) rather than a multi-part build. If it passes, proceed to structure.

## STRUCTURE — decide the spine before drafting

### `orlean-three-phase-process`
**Stand up (or rescue) a substantial nonfiction project as three hard-walled phases.**

Use when:
- You're beginning a large project (book, long feature, research-heavy report, multi-part series) and need a plan that separates research / digestion / production so you don't draft-to-figure-it-out.
- A project has stalled or sprawled and you need to diagnose which phase failed (usually: skipped digestion, or collapsed the walls and started drafting too early — AN-4).
- You have a deep-research dump or knowledge base and need a disciplined pipeline to turn it into narrative without writing during research.
- You're facing an overwhelming whole and need it converted into a known story delivered a thousand words at a time.

Produces: a phased project plan — Phase 1 research (zero words, reported to the iceberg ratio), Phase 2 digestion (cards/clusters, no prose, the phase that "doesn't look productive"), Phase 3 writing to a daily word quota.

Routing notes: The container workflow for big projects. Phase 2 of its plan hands off to `orlean-card-structure` for the actual card work; Phase 3 hands off to `orlean-yarn-engine` (or `orlean-curiosity-content` for short-form units) for production, then to the polish workflows. Use this whenever the scope is "more than one sitting."

---

### `orlean-card-structure`
**The Card Spread: decide a complex piece's structure physically, before drafting a sentence.**

Use when:
- You have all your material gathered and need to find the structure (you're in the digestion phase — Phase 2 of the three-phase process).
- You have a pile of research strands, interviews, anecdotes, and facts and can't see how they form one story.
- You're sequencing a multi-strand or non-chronological piece and need to find the theme clusters and the order before you draft.
- A draft feels like an assemblage with visible seams and you suspect the structure was invented inside the prose rather than decided first.

Produces: material atomized into chunks-of-thought, clustered into themes, sequenced (chronological or not), with the connective tissue mapped so the seams disappear — Orlean's ~700-index-card method adapted for digital cards.

Routing notes: The workhorse of the digestion phase. Run it before any drafting on a complex piece. Its connective-tissue output feeds directly into `orlean-yarn-engine` (the drafting engine that needs the spine and the seams already decided). If you find yourself reaching for this *during* drafting, stop drafting — you skipped structure.

## PRODUCE — draft the piece

### `orlean-wait-what-lead`
**Engineer the opening line for a subject the reader doesn't think they need.**

Use when:
- You're opening any piece on a low-demand or unfamiliar subject and the lead has to do all the work.
- You need a scroll-stopping first line for a post, newsletter, profile, or feature — built on a real detail, not a manufactured hook.
- An existing opener is informational, generic, or scene-setting and triggers no curiosity.
- You want the involuntary "wait, what?" reaction (usually achieved by leading on a *person* through one true, jarring, picture-able contradiction).

Produces: 3 lead drafts (standard, person-led, ultra-compressed) with a HARD honesty-spine gate confirming the jarring detail is real.

Routing notes: The narrow, surgical lead-builder. Use it standalone to fix an opener, or as a component inside `orlean-yarn-engine` and `orlean-curiosity-content` (both call the wait-what move). If the lead won't come, the problem may be upstream — the subject may not be a telling subject (route back to `orlean-telling-subject`).

---

### `orlean-yarn-engine`
**Lift a pile of gathered facts into a told story.**

Use when:
- You have the research and the structure, and now need to *write* the piece — turning facts into narrative (the Phase 3 production move for a long-form unit).
- A draft reads like bullet-point information (a "snoozefest") and you need to lift it into a yarn — spun mood, foregrounded characters, significance conveyed subtly (AN-3 fix).
- You're converting a knowledge base, report, or data set into a brand story or feature.
- You want the "parts that lead to a sum bigger than the parts," told as if across a dinner table.

Produces: a told story with a wait-what lead, the reader-as-proxy stance, a spun mood, foregrounded characters, and significance conveyed subtly — never as thesis.

Routing notes: The primary drafting engine for long-form. Expects structure to already exist — run `orlean-card-structure` (or the full `orlean-three-phase-process`) first, or the yarn will have no spine. It incorporates `orlean-wait-what-lead`. After drafting, route to `orlean-pull-the-punch` and/or `orlean-be-brutal-edit` for polish.

---

### `orlean-curiosity-content`
**Engineer a curiosity-driven short-form post from ONE overlooked, true specific.**

Use when:
- You're producing a single feed-ready piece (LinkedIn / X / Substack post, short content piece) and want Orlean's literary method ported into short-form.
- Everyone is posting the same obvious take on a topic and you want to deliberately reject it for the overlooked specific that beats it.
- You need the full short-form stack in one pass: telling-subject hook + wait-what lead + reader-as-proxy voice + exactly one vivid image hit hard.

Produces: a feed-ready piece + the rejected-obvious-take it beats, with a HARD honesty-spine gate confirming the scroll-stopping detail is real.

Routing notes: The short-form counterpart to `orlean-yarn-engine` — use it for single social/content units, not multi-strand features. It bundles the choose + lead + proxy + pick-one moves for speed, so for a one-off post you can often start here directly. For a *series* of such posts, run `orlean-card-structure` first to give the series a spine, then produce each unit. Polish with `orlean-pull-the-punch`.

## POLISH — turn the dial higher each pass

### `orlean-pull-the-punch`
**An overwriting diagnostic and rewrite.**

Use when:
- A draft feels lush, exhausting, or "boom boom boom boom" — relentless intensity with no quiet to make any hit land.
- The prose is reaching to impress (the ninth-grade-English-teacher tell — AN-1) and you need to cut it to plain, confident prose.
- Adjectives or examples are stacked and going mushy and need consolidating to one clean image hit hard (AN-2 / Pick-One).
- The pacing is flat and you need to install the passive-comfort / strong-hit / comfort rhythm so the punches actually land.

Produces: the "look at me" prose flagged and cut to plain confident prose, every adjective/example pile consolidated to one clean image, and the passive/hit/passive rhythm installed.

Routing notes: The tone-and-restraint pass. Run it on any draft that feels overwritten — including outputs of `orlean-yarn-engine`, `orlean-curiosity-content`, or any non-Orlean engine. Often paired with `orlean-be-brutal-edit`: pull-the-punch fixes *tone and restraint*; be-brutal fixes *line-level crutches and boredom*. Run pull-the-punch first (fix the overwriting), then be-brutal (catch what's left).

---

### `orlean-be-brutal-edit`
**The editing engine: multiple read-aloud passes that cut crutches and kill boredom.**

Use when:
- You have a finished draft and need the final, ruthless revision pass(es).
- A draft is technically fine but flabby, passive, or boring — you need the read-aloud test (boredom while reading = boring writing) to find the dead passages.
- You need to hunt and kill the habitual "would" (AN-5) and the passive/adjective crutches (P12).
- You're running the dual-QA stage of a quality pipeline and need the line-level editing pass.

Produces: a ruthlessly cut draft plus a change ledger of what you killed and why.

Routing notes: The terminal polish workflow — the last thing before delivery. Distinct from `orlean-pull-the-punch` (which is about overwriting/restraint/tone): be-brutal is about *line-level crutches, repetition, and boredom*. The clean sequence is produce → `orlean-pull-the-punch` → `orlean-be-brutal-edit` → ship. Can also be run standalone on *any* draft (Orlean or not) that needs a hard line edit; it's the natural fit for the dual-QA / prose-doctor stage of the content quality pipeline.

## Common Routing Chains

| Goal | Chain |
|---|---|
| **Big project from scratch (book / long feature / research report)** | `orlean-in-love-test` → `orlean-telling-subject` → `orlean-three-phase-process` → `orlean-card-structure` → `orlean-yarn-engine` → `orlean-pull-the-punch` → `orlean-be-brutal-edit` |
| **Single social / content post** | `orlean-curiosity-content` → `orlean-pull-the-punch` (→ `orlean-be-brutal-edit` if needed) |
| **Content series** | `orlean-telling-subject` → `orlean-card-structure` (series spine) → `orlean-curiosity-content` per unit → polish each |
| **Fix a boring/bullet-point draft** | `orlean-yarn-engine` (lift into story) → `orlean-be-brutal-edit` |
| **Fix an overwritten / exhausting draft** | `orlean-pull-the-punch` → `orlean-be-brutal-edit` |
| **Fix a weak opener** | `orlean-wait-what-lead` (standalone) |
| **Validate a subject before committing** | `orlean-in-love-test` (→ `orlean-telling-subject` on GO) |
| **Brand / founder story** | `orlean-in-love-test` → `orlean-telling-subject` → `orlean-yarn-engine` → `orlean-pull-the-punch` |
| **Final QA pass on any draft** | `orlean-be-brutal-edit` |

---

## Routing Heuristics

- **Don't skip CHOOSE.** Most failures are subject failures, not craft failures. If the piece won't come together, the problem is usually that it failed the in-love test or isn't a telling subject — go back, don't push harder.
- **Don't draft before you structure.** If you're reaching for `orlean-card-structure` mid-draft, you started writing too early (AN-4). Stop, structure, restart.
- **Short-form vs. long-form fork:** `orlean-curiosity-content` for single feed units; `orlean-yarn-engine` for multi-strand features. The prism test (in `orlean-telling-subject`) tells you which — one facet = short-form, 4–6 facets = long-form.
- **Polish order is fixed:** restraint/tone (`orlean-pull-the-punch`) before line-edit (`orlean-be-brutal-edit`). Fixing crutches in prose you're about to cut wholesale is wasted work.
- **The honesty spine is a hard gate, not a guideline.** The wait-what and curiosity-content workflows will not pass a manufactured detail. If the only intriguing detail you have is invented, the answer is more reporting (the iceberg ratio), not a fabricated hook.
