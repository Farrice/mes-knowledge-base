# Benjamin Hardy — Identity-Based Goal Psychology

Dr. Benjamin Hardy (PhD, organizational psychology; author of *Willpower Doesn't Work*, *Be Your Future Self Now*, and co-author with Dan Sullivan of *10x Is Easier Than 2x* and *Who Not How*) treats the past and future as psychological tools: your goal shapes your frame, your frame determines what you can even see, and transformation starts the moment you set a goal big enough and urgent enough to filter your entire present. His method is confrontational without judgment — you are a work in progress, not a finished product, and all progress starts by telling the truth about what you're doing that contradicts who you say you want to become.

Use this skill for goal-setting, scaling decisions, elimination audits, identity work, and any moment someone is "working hard but nothing is changing."

## Quick Reference

- **The future is a tool.** "Psychologically, the future is a tool to shape and direct the present." It only works if the goal is big enough AND urgent enough to force a hard look at everything you're doing.
- **10x over 2x.** 2x goals let you keep your current path and decisions. 10x goals invalidate most of what you're doing — that's the point. The decisions you'd make at 10x are the *opposite* of the ones you're making now.
- **If a timeline is long, it's wrong.** 10-year goals are never dealt with today. Compress to 3 years or less; arbitrary timelines hide false requirements.
- **Frame → Floor → Focus.** Goal shapes frame (what you see). Floor divides relevant from irrelevant. Everything below the floor is a no. The first tangible step of change is always subtraction, never addition.
- **All progress starts by telling the truth.** Saying yes to anything below your floor is lying to yourself. No judgment — humans are works in progress that think they're finished (Gilbert).
- **Be the buyer, never the seller.** Buyers know what they want and can walk away. If you can't walk away, you're the seller — and you'll keep lowering your price.
- **Goals are filters, not verdicts.** Miss the impossible goal and you've still eliminated the waste. Don't convert a filtering tool into a failure test.
