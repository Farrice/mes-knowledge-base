# Signature Series Architect

> **Expert**: Oren — Content-Team Architecture
> **Produces**: Signature Series Bible + Episode Framework + Production Protocol
> **Use When**: Designing a repeatable, branded content format that becomes the pod's anchor show
> **Load First**: [genius.md](../genius.md) — Patterns 10 (Anchor Strategy), 6 (10-Asset Standard), 11 (Content-Product Integration)

---

## Step 1: Series Discovery

Collect:
1. **Brand** — what do you sell? What world do you inhabit?
2. **POV** — what do you believe that most people in your space don't?
3. **Audience** — who watches? What do they care about?
4. **Existing hits** — what content has performed best in the last 90 days?
5. **Competitive gap** — what is no one else doing in your space?
6. **Founder/talent available** — who can be the face of the series?
7. **Frequency target** — weekly? biweekly? daily?

---

## Step 2: Series Concept Generation

Generate 5-7 series concepts using these architecture patterns:

### Pattern A: The Show
A recurring format with a consistent structure, host, and visual identity.
- Example: Flamingo Estate's "What We're Eating" — recurring food content.
- Structure: Same intro → different topic/guest → consistent outro
- Works for: Brands with a charismatic founder or host

### Pattern B: The Deep Dive
One topic explored thoroughly per episode.
- Example: Tracksmith's "The Lane" — long-form running culture
- Structure: Research → narrative → insight → product connection
- Works for: Brands with strong editorial POV

### Pattern C: The Challenge
Recurring format where the brand tests, compares, or experiments.
- Example: "We tried X for 30 days" series
- Structure: Setup → experiment → results → implications
- Works for: Product-heavy brands with things to demonstrate

### Pattern D: The People
Customer, community, or team spotlight series.
- Example: Represent's athlete/creator spotlights
- Structure: Introduction → story → connection to brand values
- Works for: Brands built on community identity

### Pattern E: The Behind
Making-of, process, decision-making transparency.
- Example: "Why we designed it this way" series
- Structure: Problem → options considered → decision → result
- Works for: Craft-forward brands

For each concept, specify:
- **Title** (branded, memorable)
- **Logline** (one sentence — what is this?)
- **Pattern** (A-E)
- **Format** (video length, platform)
- **Frequency** (weekly/biweekly)
- **Host/talent** (who appears)
- **Episode count for pilot** (recommend 4-6)

---

## Step 3: Series Selection Criteria

Score each concept:

| Criteria | Weight | Score 1-5 |
|:---------|:-------|:----------|
| Audience pull — will people subscribe/follow for THIS? | 3x | |
| Brand alignment — does this reinforce our POV? | 2x | |
| Production feasibility — can we make this weekly? | 2x | |
| Uniqueness — is anyone else doing this? | 2x | |
| Product integration — can we naturally weave product in? | 1x | |

**Select the concept with the highest weighted score for pilot.**

---

## Step 4: Series Bible

For the selected concept, build the complete bible:

```
SERIES TITLE: [Name]
LOGLINE: [One sentence]
FORMAT: [Video type, duration, platform(s)]
FREQUENCY: [Weekly/biweekly]
HOST: [Who appears]

VISUAL IDENTITY:
├── Intro treatment (first 3 seconds)
├── Lower thirds / text style
├── Color palette (must match brand but have distinct series feel)
├── Music/sound design
├── Thumbnail template
└── Outro treatment (CTA structure)

EPISODE STRUCTURE:
├── Cold open: [Hook format — question, statement, provocative frame]
├── Segment 1: [Setup — what are we exploring today?]
├── Segment 2: [Depth — the insight, the experiment, the story]
├── Segment 3: [Payoff — what did we learn? what does it mean?]
├── Product moment: [Natural integration point — not forced]
└── Close: [CTA — subscribe, save, follow, link in bio]

EDITORIAL GUARDRAILS:
├── Always: [What every episode must include]
├── Never: [What no episode should include]
├── Tone: [Specific tonal range]
└── Length: [Min/max duration]

PILOT PLAN:
├── Episode 1: [Topic]
├── Episode 2: [Topic]
├── Episode 3: [Topic]
├── Episode 4: [Topic]
└── Decision gate after 4 episodes: continue, adjust, or kill
```

---

## Step 5: Pilot Execution Protocol

### Pre-Production (1 week)
1. Finalize series bible
2. Create visual assets (intro, lower thirds, thumbnail template)
3. Brief the production team (creator, editor, strategist)
4. Script/outline first 4 episodes
5. Schedule production blocks

### Production (2-4 weeks)
1. Shoot episodes 1-2 first
2. Edit episode 1, publish while editing episode 2
3. Review analytics on episode 1 after 7 days
4. Adjust episodes 3-4 based on learnings
5. Complete publishing of all 4 pilot episodes

### Post-Pilot Decision Gate
After 4 episodes, evaluate:
- **Continue as-is** — if performance exceeds baseline by 20%+
- **Adjust and continue** — if performance is at baseline but audience response is positive
- **Kill and redesign** — if performance is below baseline with no positive signals

Metrics to evaluate:
- Completion rate vs. non-series content
- Saves and shares vs. non-series content
- Comment sentiment (are people requesting more?)
- Subscriber/follower growth during pilot period

```
[Runner-up concepts preserved for future development]
```

---

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Selected concept carries the highest weighted score from the Step 3 rubric, not just the most-liked idea in the room
- [ ] Pilot plan commits to exactly 4-6 episodes with a decision gate — never open-ended
- [ ] Decision gate applies the stated thresholds (Continue if +20%, Adjust if at baseline, Kill if below with no positive signal) — no ad hoc verdict
- [ ] Series bible's "Never" list is populated, not left blank — editorial guardrails require both Always and Never
- [ ] Runner-up concepts are preserved in the output for future development, not discarded
