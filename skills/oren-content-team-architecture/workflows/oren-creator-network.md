# External Creator Network Architecture

> **Expert**: Oren — Content-Team Architecture
> **Produces**: Creator Sourcing Strategy + Onboarding Protocol + Performance Management System
> **Use When**: Building a network of external content creators (not influencers — creators)
> **Load First**: [genius.md](../genius.md) — Patterns 8 (External Creator Integration), 4 (Strategist-Creator Dyad), 6 (10-Asset Standard)

---

## Step 1: Creator Need Assessment

Collect:
1. **Brand** — what do you sell? Visual style?
2. **Current content team** — internal pod composition
3. **Gap** — what the internal team CAN'T produce (capacity, aesthetic, audience, platform)
4. **Budget for external creators** — per-piece or monthly retainer
5. **Volume target** — how many pieces per month from external creators?

---

## Step 2: Creator Sourcing Channels

### Tier 1: Discovery Platforms
- **miniocial** — marketplace for real creator content at scale
- **Super Affiliate** — affiliate-driven creator network
- **Outer Signal** — curated external creator network
- **Platform search** — scroll TikTok/Instagram in your niche, screenshot creators whose aesthetic matches

### Tier 2: Direct Sourcing
- **Existing customers** — people already posting about your product organically
- **Competitor's creator network** — who's creating for your competitors?
- **Adjacent-niche creators** — creators in a related space with transferable aesthetic
- **Your own comment section** — engaged followers who also create content

### Tier 3: Paid Talent
- **Contra** — freelance creative marketplace (for designers, editors, animators)
- **Production houses** — for high-production shoots
- **Film school/creative program graduates** — for emerging talent at lower cost

### Sourcing Criteria (Must Match)
```
NON-NEGOTIABLE:
├── Aesthetic alignment — their existing content fits your visual world
├── Platform fluency — they understand the platform's native format
├── Production quality — they can produce at your minimum standard
└── Reliability — they can hit deadlines consistently

BONUS:
├── Existing audience in your target demographic
├── Willing to work from briefs (some creators refuse direction)
├── Multi-platform capability
└── On-camera comfort if needed
```

## Step 3: Onboarding Protocol

### The Creator Onboarding Kit

Every new external creator receives:

1. **Brand one-pager** — POV, visual language, tone, product overview
2. **Content guidelines** — what we do and don't do (with examples)
3. **Brief template** — the exact format they'll receive briefs in
4. **Reference library** — 10-15 examples of content that represents our standard
5. **Technical specs** — aspect ratios, duration, caption style, audio rules
6. **Process flow** — brief → draft → review → revision → delivery → payment
7. **Payment terms** — per-piece rate, payment timeline, usage rights

### The Test Assignment

Before committing to ongoing work:
1. Brief the creator on one concept
2. They deliver one piece
3. Pod strategist reviews against quality standard
4. Three outcomes: Approve (welcome to the network) / Revise (try one more) / Decline (not a fit)

---

## Step 4: Briefing Protocol

Every brief to an external creator includes:

```
BRIEF TEMPLATE:
├── Concept title
├── Platform(s)
├── Format type (e.g., "get ready with me" / "POV" / "vlog style")
├── Hook (exact first 3 seconds/line)
├── Key message (one sentence)
├── Product feature (if applicable)
├── Visual reference (link to 2-3 examples)
├── Do's (specific guidance)
├── Don'ts (specific guardrails)
├── Duration: [15s / 30s / 60s]
├── Deliverable format: MP4, [resolution], captions Y/N
├── Due date
└── Payment: $[amount] upon delivery approval
```

### Brief Quality Check
The strategist writes the brief. Before sending:
- Could a stranger produce work from this brief? If no → add more detail.
- Is there creative room for the creator's personality? If no → loosen guardrails.
- Is the hook worth filming? If unsure → rewrite the hook.

## Step 5: Performance Management

### Creator Scorecard (Monthly)

```
CREATOR: [Name]
PIECES DELIVERED: [N]
ON-TIME DELIVERY: [%]
FIRST-DRAFT APPROVAL: [%]
REVISIONS NEEDED: [avg per piece]

CONTENT PERFORMANCE:
├── Avg views: [N]
├── Avg saves: [N]
├── Best performer: [link + metrics]
└── Graduated to paid: [Y/N, which pieces]

OVERALL GRADE: A / B / C / D
├── A: Increase volume, consider retainer
├── B: Maintain current volume
├── C: Coaching needed, reduce volume
└── D: Offboard
```

### Network Health Dashboard

```
TOTAL ACTIVE CREATORS: [N]
TOTAL PIECES/MONTH: [N]
AVG COST PER PIECE: $[N]
GRADUATION RATE (organic → paid): [%]
TOP 3 CREATORS: [Names + data]
PIPELINE: [N creators in onboarding]
```

## Step 6: Scaling the Network

### From 3-5 Creators to 10-20+
- Assign a "creator ops" role within the strategist's responsibilities
- Template everything — briefs, reviews, payments, scorecards
- Create a creator community (Slack channel, WhatsApp group, monthly creator call)
- Share performance data with creators — they improve when they see what works
- Introduce cross-pod creator sharing — one great creator can serve multiple pods

### Creator Retention
- Pay fairly and on time
- Give creative freedom within guardrails
- Share performance wins ("your piece got 500K views!")
- Offer graduated rates for top performers
- Give first look on big campaigns

---

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Sourcing channels are ranked by expected yield (Step 2 tiers), not listed alphabetically
- [ ] Every creator brief includes hook, do's, don'ts, and payment amount (Step 4 template) — incomplete briefs get rejected before sending
- [ ] The Test Assignment outcome (Approve / Revise / Decline) is a real decision recorded per creator, never left pending
- [ ] Creator Scorecard grade (A-D) matches its own stated criteria — an A-grade creator must carry an "increase volume/retainer" recommendation
- [ ] Scaling plan phases each carry a creator-count target AND a date — never one without the other
