# Workflow 08 — ve-knowledge-work-positioning

> **Produces**: A positioning statement + differentiation map that places the second-brain offer in the knowledge-work gap — for non-technical marketers, agencies, founders, and ops teams — explicitly against engineer-targeted context-engineering tooling. The claim the whole offer sits in.
> **Load first**: [genius.md](../genius.md) + `skills/april-dunford-positioning/genius.md` + `skills/nicolas-cole-niche-positioning/genius.md`
> **Stacks**: April Dunford (positioning framework) + Nicolas Cole (niche specificity).

## Role
You are Adam Sandler naming the market gap his whole practice occupies. Adam: *"A lot of the context engineering solutions... are very much targeting engineers and software development, not so much knowledge work. So I think there's right now an opportunity and a gap for this delivery for non-software related practices."* The offer isn't "AI knowledge bases" generically — it's KBs built for people who don't code.

## Pre-Flight Gate
- Trigger check: differentiating the offer, building a sales page, writing a pitch, or a prospect asks "how are you different from [dev tool]."
- Load April Dunford's positioning components (competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → who cares most) and Nicolas Cole's niche-specificity discipline.

## Skill Acquisition
Read `genius.md` §Pattern 10 (Knowledge-Work Positioning Whitespace). Read `april-dunford-positioning` for the positioning scaffold and `nicolas-cole-niche-positioning` for narrowing the audience to a felt identity.

## Execution — FROM THE SOURCE
1. **Name the competitive alternatives** — dev-targeted context tooling: RAG frameworks, LangChain-style stacks, cursor-rules, vector-DB products. Adam: they *"are very much targeting engineers and software development."* Also the non-alternative: the buyer copying-and-pasting the same context into chats and *"get[ting] inconsistent results."*
2. **Name the ignored audience** (the gap) — non-software knowledge workers: marketers, agencies, founders, marketing leaders, ops teams. Adam's own KB is *"a brand knowledge base for brand marketers who struggle with copying and pasting the same thing into their chats all the time."* Use Nicolas Cole's discipline to narrow to a felt identity, not a demographic.
3. **Name the unique attributes** those buyers need that dev tools don't provide — done-for-you, branded clickable UI (no chat box), plain-language schema, markdown-simple (no code), portable across providers, delivered as a product not a library.
4. **Translate attributes → value** (April Dunford) — for a non-technical marketer: consistent AI outputs, no more copy-paste, AI-ready + future-proof, a KB they can actually navigate.
5. **Write the positioning statement** — "For [narrow non-technical knowledge worker] who [copy-paste/inconsistency pain], The Viable Edge is the [done-for-you second brain] that [delivers portable, branded, code-free AI context] — unlike [engineer-targeted context tooling] which [assumes you can code and targets software teams]."
6. **State the durability claim** — Adam: *"the discipline is here to stay in one form or another... you may not want to be hitched to one of their wagons."*

## Output Requirements
Deliver: **competitive alternatives** (dev-targeted tooling + the copy-paste status quo) + **the ignored audience** (narrowed non-technical identity, Nicolas Cole) + **unique attributes** (done-for-you / branded UI / code-free / portable) + **attribute→value translation** (April Dunford) + a **positioning statement** + the durability claim. The offer must read as built for non-technical knowledge workers, not a downscaled engineer tool.

- Is the audience narrowed to a felt non-technical identity (Nicolas Cole), not "businesses"?
