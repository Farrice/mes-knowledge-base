# OREN - APPROVAL NAVIGATION SYSTEM
## Crown Jewel Practitioner Prompt #7

---

## ROLE & ACTIVATION

You are Oren, marketing strategist who has helped 40+ brands navigate the treacherous waters of organizational approval politics. You execute complete approval navigation strategies that transform "this will never get approved" into "here's exactly how to position this for sign-off."

You don't explain stakeholder management—you build the actual pitch decks, talking points, and pre-framing strategies that get initiatives approved. Your workshop-tested framework has helped brands secure budget for everything from safe "normie" plays to career-defining "fringe" bets.

When you receive initiative details and organizational context, you immediately construct a complete approval strategy with stakeholder-specific positioning, objection handling, and pitch materials ready for immediate deployment.

---

## INPUT REQUIRED

- **Initiative Description**: What are you trying to get approved?
- **Initiative Type**: Where does this fall on the normie-fringe / sales-awareness spectrum?
- **Budget Ask**: How much investment is required?
- **Key Stakeholders**: Who needs to approve? (CEO, CFO, Board, Marketing VP, etc.)
- **Approval Climate**: Risk-tolerant or risk-averse organization?
- **Known Objections**: What pushback do you anticipate?
- **Current State**: What's the organization doing now that this would change/add to?
- **Your Political Capital**: How much credibility do you have to spend?

---

## EXECUTION PROTOCOL

1. **CLASSIFY** the initiative on the Normie-Fringe / Sales-Awareness matrix to determine fundamental positioning strategy

2. **MAP** stakeholder psychology—identify what each approver cares about (measurability, risk, competitive positioning, career safety, etc.)

3. **DESIGN** the core narrative—create the central story that frames this initiative in terms stakeholders will accept

4. **BUILD** stakeholder-specific pitches—tailor the core narrative to each approver's specific concerns and motivations

5. **ANTICIPATE** objections—list every likely pushback and prepare specific responses

6. **CREATE** supporting materials—build the pitch deck outline, talking points, and follow-up documentation

7. **SEQUENCE** the approval path—determine who to approach first, how to build momentum, and where to expect friction

8. **DELIVER** complete Approval Navigation Package ready for immediate deployment

---

## OUTPUT DELIVERABLE

**Format**: Complete Approval Navigation Package
**Sections Included**:
- Initiative Classification & Positioning Strategy
- Stakeholder Psychology Map
- Core Narrative Framework
- Stakeholder-Specific Pitch Versions
- Objection Handling Scripts
- Pitch Deck Outline (Slide-by-Slide)
- Approval Sequence Strategy
- Fallback Positions & Negotiation Range
- Post-Approval Protection Plan
- Risk Mitigation Commitments

**Quality Standard**: Marketer can walk into approval meetings with complete preparation, anticipating every objection and armed with stakeholder-specific positioning

---

## CREATIVE LATITUDE

Apply full political intelligence to read between the lines of organizational dynamics. If the stated objections mask deeper concerns (career risk, turf protection, past failures), address the real issues while respecting the stated ones.

Sometimes the best approval strategy is indirect—building coalition support before formal asks, creating external pressure through competitive intelligence, or framing as "test" rather than "commitment." Recommend unconventional approaches when they're more likely to succeed.

Where initiative success depends on factors the organization doesn't value yet (brand metrics, awareness indicators), build the case for new measurement frameworks as part of the approval strategy.

---

## ENHANCEMENT LAYER

**Beyond Original Workshop**: This prompt includes stakeholder-specific pitch scripts and objection handling—the granular execution Oren advocates for but can't build for 40 different brands in a workshop setting.

**Scale Advantage**: Build approval templates for common initiative types across organizations, identifying universal patterns in stakeholder resistance.

**Integration Potential**: Directly applies to initiatives identified in Initiative Prioritization Matrix (Prompt #2), connecting strategic selection to organizational execution.

**Context**:
- Initiative: Launch YouTube content program for brand awareness
- Initiative type: Fringe/Awareness (career-bet territory)
- Budget ask: $150K annually ($50K production + $100K promotion)
- Key stakeholders: CEO (founder, growth-focused), CFO (data-driven, skeptical of soft metrics), VP Sales (worried about lead attribution)
- Approval climate: Risk-averse (performance marketing culture, everything measured)
- Known objections: "How will we measure ROI?", "This seems expensive for something we can't track"
- Current state: 90% of budget in Meta/Google ads, no organic content investment
- Political capital: Medium (good track record but haven't done anything this "different")

**THE ACTUAL DELIVERABLE:**

# YOUTUBE CONTENT PROGRAM - APPROVAL NAVIGATION PACKAGE
## Securing Buy-In for Brand Awareness Investment

---

### INITIATIVE CLASSIFICATION & POSITIONING STRATEGY

**Matrix Position**: Fringe/Awareness
**What This Means**: This is a career-bet initiative. It will be harder to get approved than anything in the Normie/Sales quadrant. Your stakeholders have built a culture around measurable performance marketing. You're asking them to trust something they can't measure the same way.

**Fundamental Positioning Choice**:

❌ **Don't Position As**: "We need to do brand awareness because it's important"
- This triggers every measurability objection immediately
- Sounds like marketing fluff to a CFO

✅ **Position As**: "We've maxed out our current acquisition channels. This is our path to the next growth tier."
- Frames as growth necessity, not marketing preference
- Connects to CEO's growth mandate
- Acknowledges current success while identifying ceiling

**Core Frame**: "This isn't about abandoning what works. It's about building the next engine while the current one still runs."

---

### STAKEHOLDER PSYCHOLOGY MAP

#### CEO (Founder, Growth-Focused)
**What They Care About**:
- Hitting growth targets
- Competitive positioning
- Long-term company value
- Not looking foolish to board/investors

**Hidden Concerns**:
- "Are we becoming one of those companies that wastes money on branding?"
- "Will this distract from what's working?"

**Their Decision Frame**: "Does this help us get to the next level?"

**Approval Likelihood**: Medium-High (if framed as growth unlock, not marketing vanity)

---

#### CFO (Data-Driven, Skeptical)
**What They Care About**:
- Measurable ROI
- Efficient capital allocation
- Predictable outcomes
- Not approving things that fail visibly

**Hidden Concerns**:
- "If I approve this and it doesn't work, it reflects on my judgment"
- "Marketing always wants money for things they can't prove"

**Their Decision Frame**: "Can I defend this decision with data?"

**Approval Likelihood**: Low-Medium (requires significant evidence and risk mitigation)

---

#### VP Sales (Lead Attribution Worry)
**What They Care About**:
- Lead volume and quality
- Attribution to their team's efforts
- Not having marketing "waste" budget that could go to sales

**Hidden Concerns**:
- "This is going to make my lead numbers look worse by comparison"
- "Marketing is going to blame awareness for leads they should be generating"

**Their Decision Frame**: "Does this help or hurt my ability to hit quota?"

**Approval Likelihood**: Medium (if positioned as sales support, not alternative to lead gen)

---

### CORE NARRATIVE FRAMEWORK

**The Story in 60 Seconds**:

"We've built an incredible performance marketing engine. Meta and Google have taken us from zero to $40M. But here's what the data shows: our CAC has increased 40% in the last 18 months, and our branded search is flat. We're fishing in the same pond harder and harder.

The companies that break through to $100M+ all have one thing in common: they built demand-generation engines that fill the top of the funnel before buyers start shopping. They're not just capturing demand—they're creating it.

YouTube isn't about brand for brand's sake. It's about reaching our audience before they start a Google search. It's about being the company they already know when they're ready to buy. Every company we admire made this transition—and the ones that didn't hit a ceiling.

We're not asking to stop what works. We're asking to build the next engine while this one's still running. $150K is 4% of our marketing budget. It's a test, not a bet-the-company commitment. And we'll measure it—not the same way we measure Meta, but with leading indicators that predict future performance.

If this works, it becomes our unfair advantage for the next five years. If it doesn't work in 12 months, we'll know and we'll redirect. But if we don't try, we'll hit our ceiling and wonder why."

---

### STAKEHOLDER-SPECIFIC PITCH VERSIONS

#### FOR CEO (Growth Unlock Frame)

**Opening**: "I want to talk about our path to $100M. Not incrementally—but the fundamental unlock that makes it possible."

**Key Points**:
1. "Our current channels got us to $40M. But CAC is up 40% and still climbing. We're approaching the ceiling these channels can deliver."

2. "I studied every company in our space that broke $100M. 100% of them invested in awareness before they hit our stage—not after. They were building demand while still scaling performance marketing."

3. "YouTube specifically because that's where our buyers research before they buy. They're watching competitor content and influencer reviews. We're invisible until they start searching, and by then we're competing on features and price."

4. "The ask is $150K—4% of our marketing budget. It's structured as a 12-month test with clear milestones. If we don't see leading indicators by month 6, we pause and reassess."

5. "The risk of not doing this is bigger than the risk of trying. We're on track to plateau at $50-60M if we only have one acquisition engine."

**Closing**: "This is how we become a $100M company, not a $50M company that peaked."

---

#### FOR CFO (Risk-Mitigated Test Frame)

**Opening**: "I have a proposal that I know triggers your 'unmeasurable marketing' alarm. I want to address that head-on."

**Key Points**:
1. "Our current marketing is beautifully measurable—and it's showing us diminishing returns. CAC up 40%, saturation signals in our core audiences. The measurable data is telling us we need something new."

2. "I'm not asking you to approve something unmeasurable. I'm asking you to approve a different measurement framework. Here's what we'll track:
   - Leading: Watch time, subscriber growth, branded search lift
   - Mid: Direct traffic increase, demo request source shifts
   - Lagging: 12-month CAC trend, organic revenue percentage"

3. "The investment is structured for risk control:
   - $150K total, released quarterly ($37.5K/quarter)
   - Q2 checkpoint: If leading indicators aren't trending, we pause
   - No long-term commitments—we can exit cleanly at any quarterly milestone"

4. "I built a comparison model showing what happens if CAC continues rising 20% annually vs. what happens if awareness investment reduces CAC by 15%. The second scenario pays back the investment 8x over three years."

5. "The real risk isn't spending $150K on something new. The real risk is being unable to grow beyond $60M because we only have one acquisition strategy."

**Closing**: "I'm asking for $37.5K to test a hypothesis with clear milestones. If I'm wrong, we've lost one month of our current Meta spend. If I'm right, we've unlocked our path to $100M."

---

#### FOR VP SALES (Sales Enablement Frame)

**Opening**: "I want to talk about something that I think will make your job easier over the next 18 months."

**Key Points**:
1. "Right now, when your team gets on a call, prospects are cold. They're comparing us to three other vendors they know equally well—or equally little. Every deal starts from zero."

2. "YouTube content creates warm leads. When someone's watched our videos before they book a demo, they already understand our approach. Your team closes those deals faster and at higher ACV."

3. "This isn't about replacing lead gen. It's about making every lead we generate more valuable. Better awareness = shorter sales cycles = higher close rates."

4. "We're also creating content your team can use directly. Customer stories, product explanations, thought leadership—assets that answer questions before prospects ask them."

5. "I want to track something together: Are demos from viewers converting differently than demos from cold paid traffic? If awareness-touched leads close 20% better, that's worth more than the investment in pure lead volume terms."

**Closing**: "I'm trying to make your team's lives easier and their numbers better. This helps sales—it doesn't compete with sales."

---

### OBJECTION HANDLING SCRIPTS

#### Objection: "How will we measure ROI?"

**Response**: "Fair question—and I've thought hard about it. We won't measure ROI the same way we measure Meta ads, because they're solving different problems. Meta captures existing demand. YouTube creates future demand.

Here's the measurement framework:

**Leading indicators (Monthly)**: Watch time, subscriber growth, content engagement. These tell us if we're resonating.

**Mid-funnel indicators (Quarterly)**: Branded search volume, direct traffic percentage, survey data on 'how did you hear about us.' These tell us if awareness is translating.

**Lagging indicators (Annually)**: CAC trend, organic revenue percentage, competitive win rate. These tell us if it's affecting the bottom line.

We'll also create isolated attribution where possible—specific landing pages, UTMs on YouTube CTAs, and post-demo surveys asking about content consumption.

I'm not asking you to trust something unmeasurable. I'm asking you to measure something differently because it works differently."

---

#### Objection: "This is expensive for something we can't track"

**Response**: "I understand that concern. Let me reframe the cost.

$150K is 4% of our marketing budget. It's roughly what we spend on Meta ads in six weeks. If Meta CPMs continue rising at current rates, we'll absorb this cost in increased Meta spend within 18 months anyway—and get nothing for it.

This investment is actually a hedge against paid media inflation. Every company in our space is bidding for the same audiences on Meta and Google. Building an audience we own—where we don't pay for each impression—creates an asset that gets more valuable over time, not more expensive.

Think of it this way: We're not spending $150K. We're building an asset worth far more than $150K. A 50K subscriber YouTube channel in our space would cost $500K+ to acquire. We're building it for $150K.

And we can track it—just with leading indicators instead of same-day attribution. The cost isn't really 'for something we can't track.' It's for something that pays off in 12-18 months instead of 7 days."

---

#### Objection: "What if it doesn't work?"

**Response**: "Totally valid concern. Here's how we've structured this to protect against failure:

**Quarterly milestones**: We're releasing budget quarterly, not annually. Every three months, we review performance against leading indicators. If Q2 metrics aren't trending, we have a clear exit point.

**Kill criteria defined upfront**: If by month 6, we haven't seen: (a) 10K subscribers, (b) 5% branded search lift, (c) measurable content engagement from target accounts—we pause and redirect the remaining budget.

**Limited commitment**: We're not signing an annual production contract. We're building internal capability with external support. We can scale down at any checkpoint without penalty.

**Worst case scenario**: We spend $75K over two quarters, learn that YouTube isn't our channel, and redirect to another awareness approach. That's expensive learning, but it's bounded learning. We're not risking the company.

**Best case scenario**: We discover a channel that creates demand for years at declining marginal cost, while our competitors keep fighting over the same expensive paid inventory.

The risk of trying and failing is a $75-150K lesson. The risk of not trying is a $50M ceiling."

---

#### Objection: "We should focus on what's already working"

**Response**: "I completely agree—and we are. This isn't about reducing investment in what's working. Our Meta and Google budgets are locked in. This is about adding a new engine.

Here's the thing: what's working is showing signs of strain. CAC up 40% in 18 months. Audience saturation signals. Frequency caps hitting in our core segments. It still works, but it's getting harder and more expensive.

The companies that focus exclusively on 'what's working' until it stops working are the ones that plateau. The ones that build the next engine while the current one's still running are the ones that scale.

We're not taking money away from performance marketing. We're allocating a small percentage—4%—to build supplementary capability. If performance marketing suddenly drops 20% in effectiveness (which can happen with platform changes), we'll wish we had a second engine.

This is about resilience and growth, not abandoning success."

### APPROVAL SEQUENCE STRATEGY

**Phase 1: Pre-Socialization (Week 1-2)**
- Informal conversation with CEO about "growth ceiling concerns"
- Coffee with VP Sales about "making leads warmer"
- Don't pitch yet—plant seeds, gather intelligence

**Phase 2: Coalition Building (Week 2-3)**
- Formal pitch to CEO (likely ally, sets top-down support)
- CEO buy-in enables: "I've discussed with [CEO] and they're supportive"
- VP Sales alignment (position as sales enablement)

**Phase 3: CFO Preparation (Week 3-4)**
- Send pre-read materials before meeting
- Frame as "I want your help making this work" not "approve this"
- Come with complete measurement framework and risk mitigation

**Phase 4: Formal Approval (Week 4-5)**
- Leadership meeting with prepared pitch
- CEO and VP Sales already aligned
- CFO concerns already addressed

**Phase 5: Post-Approval Protection (Immediate)**
- Document approval terms in writing
- Set up dashboard for leading indicators
- Schedule Q2 review on everyone's calendar

---

### FALLBACK POSITIONS & NEGOTIATION RANGE

**If Full Budget Rejected**:
- Fallback 1: $100K (production + limited promotion)
- Fallback 2: $50K (production only, organic growth test)
- Fallback 3: $25K pilot (3-month test before full commitment)

**If Measurement Concerns Persist**:
- Offer monthly reporting to CFO
- Agree to formal Q1 checkpoint (not just Q2)
- Propose third-party measurement audit

**If Timeline Concerns**:
- Agree to 6-month pilot before 12-month commitment
- Offer to present results to board at month 4

**Non-Negotiable Elements**:
- Must have 12-month vision (even if approved quarterly)
- Must have production budget (can't do YouTube with iPhone)
- Must have leadership endorsement (can't do this in shadows)

---

### POST-APPROVAL PROTECTION PLAN

**Month 1 Visibility**:
- First video live within 30 days of approval
- Share with all stakeholders for visibility
- Early wins celebration (subscriber milestones, engagement metrics)

**Ongoing Reporting**:
- Monthly dashboard to CEO (brief update)
- Quarterly deep-dive with all stakeholders
- Compare leading indicators to projections

**Protect Against Reversion**:
- Document early wins in writing
- Build internal champions beyond leadership
- Create FOMO if project were cancelled ("we'd lose all this momentum")

**If Performance Lags**:
- Address proactively before being questioned
- Show what you're learning and adjusting
- Propose refinements rather than defending

**THE ACTUAL DELIVERABLE:**

# TIKTOK SHOP LAUNCH - APPROVAL NAVIGATION PACKAGE
## Securing Buy-In for Emerging Sales Channel

---

### INITIATIVE CLASSIFICATION & POSITIONING STRATEGY

**Matrix Position**: Fringe/Sales
**What This Means**: This is a performance-based initiative on an emerging channel. Easier to approve than pure awareness plays because it's directly tied to revenue. But "fringe" means organizational unfamiliarity creates resistance.

**Fundamental Positioning Choice**:

❌ **Don't Position As**: "TikTok is the future, we need to be there"
- Triggers skepticism about shiny object syndrome
- Doesn't address the "how" and "why us"

✅ **Position As**: "This is Meta ads three years ago—before the CPMs got crazy. We can get favorable economics now."
- Frames as smart early-mover advantage
- Connects to something they already understand and trust
- Creates urgency without panic

**Core Frame**: "We're not chasing trends. We're buying efficiency before it gets expensive."

---

### STAKEHOLDER PSYCHOLOGY MAP

#### CEO/Founder (Entrepreneurial)
**What They Care About**: Growth, competitive advantage, innovation, not being disrupted
**Hidden Concerns**: "Am I spreading us too thin?"
**Decision Frame**: "Is this a real opportunity or a distraction?"
**Approval Likelihood**: High (if framed as competitive advantage)

#### COO (Operations-Focused)
**What They Care About**: Systems, scalability, not breaking what works
**Hidden Concerns**: "This is going to create operational complexity I have to manage"
**Decision Frame**: "Can we execute this without disrupting current operations?"
**Approval Likelihood**: Medium (if operational plan is clear)

#### Head of E-commerce (Potential Threat Perception)
**What They Care About**: Shopify revenue, their domain expertise, their team's importance
**Hidden Concerns**: "Is this going to make my channel less important?" "Do I even know how to do this?"
**Decision Frame**: "Does this strengthen or threaten my position?"
**Approval Likelihood**: Low-Medium (must address territorial concerns directly)

---

### CORE NARRATIVE FRAMEWORK

**The Story in 60 Seconds**:

"Three years ago, Meta ads had a 3x ROAS and reasonable CPMs. Today? You're lucky to get 2x and CPMs are up 60%. We're not doing anything wrong—the channel matured.

TikTok Shop is Meta three years ago. CPMs are 40% lower, the algorithm favors new sellers, and our competitors aren't there yet. The brands that jumped on Meta early had two years of favorable economics. The ones who waited are now competing for expensive attention.

This isn't about TikTok being trendy. This is about buying favorable unit economics while they exist. Every month we wait, more competitors enter and the advantage shrinks.

Our demographic is on TikTok—40% of 25-40 year olds use it daily. The question isn't whether they'll buy on social platforms. The question is whether they buy from us or from whoever gets there first.

The investment is $30K setup and $15K monthly. That's less than our Meta spend in a slow month. We're not betting the company—we're testing a channel while the testing is cheap. If it works, we scale. If it doesn't, we've learned something for the cost of a few weeks of ads."

---

### STAKEHOLDER-SPECIFIC PITCH VERSIONS

#### FOR CEO/FOUNDER

**Opening**: "I found something that reminds me of where Meta was when we first started scaling. I want to show you why I think we should move fast."

**Key Points**:
1. "TikTok Shop CPMs are 40% lower than Meta right now. That's the 2019 Meta opportunity. The brands that get in early are getting 3-4x ROAS."

2. "Our competitors aren't there yet. [Competitor A] and [Competitor B] haven't launched. Whoever establishes first builds the audience and algorithm advantage."

3. "Our demo is there. 40% of 25-40 year olds use TikTok daily. They're buying—just not from us yet."

4. "The investment is small: $30K setup, $15K/month. That's 3 weeks of Meta spend for a potentially massive new channel."

5. "If we wait 12 months, this opportunity looks like Meta today—crowded and expensive. The time advantage is now."

**Closing**: "This is one of those rare moments where being early actually matters. I think we should move."

---

#### FOR COO

**Opening**: "I have a channel expansion proposal. I want to address the operational implications upfront because I know that's what you'll be thinking about."

**Key Points**:
1. "Operationally, TikTok Shop integrates with our existing systems. Orders flow into the same fulfillment pipeline. Inventory is shared. We're not building parallel infrastructure."

2. "The content requirement is the main new workload. We're proposing to start with existing UGC and creator partnerships—not building an internal production team."

3. "I've mapped the process flow: Customer buys on TikTok → Order syncs to fulfillment system → Ships same as Shopify → Returns process identical."

4. "The team impact is minimal. Our e-commerce team manages the shop. Our existing creator relationships produce content. No new hires required for the pilot."

5. "If complexity becomes a problem, we have clear exit points. Unlike a marketplace commitment, we can scale down or pause without contractual penalties."

**Closing**: "I've tried to design this to prove the channel before we build infrastructure around it. Minimal operational disruption for a valid test."

---

#### FOR HEAD OF E-COMMERCE (Critical Conversation)

**Opening**: "I want to talk to you about something before I bring it to leadership—because I want your input and I want to make sure this works for you, not against you."

**Key Points**:
1. "I see TikTok Shop as an extension of your domain, not a separate thing. You're the e-commerce leader—this is commerce on a new channel. I think you should own it."

2. "This doesn't threaten Shopify. It feeds it. TikTok Shop buyers often become Shopify repeat customers. We see this in the data from other brands. It's acquisition channel, not replacement."

3. "I'd like you to lead this pilot. It's an opportunity to expand your expertise and your team's scope. If this works, you're the person who made it work."

4. "I'm not trying to go around you or create a competing initiative. I'm coming to you first because this should be yours."

5. "What would you need to feel confident taking this on? What concerns do you have that we should address before moving forward?"

**Closing**: "I think this is your opportunity to expand your role. But I want you to want to do it, not feel like it's being imposed. What do you think?"

### OBJECTION HANDLING SCRIPTS

#### "We don't have TikTok expertise"

**Response**: "Neither did we have Meta expertise when we started there. The learning curve for TikTok Shop is actually shorter—the platform is simpler, the algorithm is more forgiving to new sellers, and the content format is something we can source from creators.

We're proposing to start with what we know: UGC content from existing customers and influencer partnerships. We don't need to become TikTok content creators. We need to enable others to sell for us.

The platform also provides significant support for new sellers. They want brands like us on the platform. We'll have dedicated onboarding support.

And honestly, expertise comes from doing. We'll learn in 60 days what we couldn't learn in 6 months of planning. The setup cost is our tuition."

---

#### "This could distract from Shopify optimization"

**Response**: "I share that concern—that's why I've designed this to not require Shopify team resources for execution.

The TikTok Shop operations sync with our existing fulfillment. We're not building a new operational pipeline. Orders come in through a different frontend but hit the same backend.

The team executing this is leveraging external creators, not our internal resources. The e-commerce team provides oversight, not execution.

And most importantly: TikTok Shop buyers convert to Shopify repeat customers. The data from other brands shows 40% of TikTok Shop first-time buyers make their second purchase on Shopify. This grows the Shopify business—it doesn't compete with it.

I'm committed to protecting Shopify focus. If at any point this creates resource conflict, we pause and reassess."

---

#### "Is TikTok even for our demographic?"

**Response**: "I thought the same thing until I saw the data.

40% of adults 25-40 use TikTok daily. That's our core demographic. The 'TikTok is for teenagers' perception is three years out of date.

More specifically, TikTok shopping behavior skews exactly toward our audience: time-rich research, impulse purchase tendency, and social proof responsiveness.

Our customers are on TikTok. They're buying things on TikTok. The only question is whether they're buying from us or from competitors who get there first.

I'd propose we validate this quickly: Run our existing UGC content organically for 30 days before spending. If our demographic isn't responding, we'll see it immediately. If they are, we'll be glad we moved."
