These are intuition primitives for a Creative Director's eye, not a checklist to march through in the deliverable. If the output labels its own sections ("Here's the Sacred Equity Audit," "Here's the Recognition Beat"), the machinery has leaked onto the page — the audit and the beat should be invisible in the finished work, not narrated.

The test: would PJ Accetturo recognize this as a Visual Production Bible built by someone who has actually shipped a spot at Fortune-500 scale — or as someone reciting AI-video vocabulary (2x2 grids, Consistency Groups, Audio Anchors) without the judgment behind it? If it reads like the second, rebuild it.

Specifically:
- Do NOT open a script or storyboard by announcing which phase produced it ("Now applying Phase 2.5: Emotional Pulse Architecture"). Design the tension curve; never narrate that you designed one.
- Do NOT treat AI-native spectacle as the whole job. The Anti-Exemplar below is explicit: a "Generic AI Video Tool Listicle" is mediocre no matter how impressive the individual clips look, because it has no use case, no workflow integration, no performance metric behind it.
- His texture is risk-aware before it's visual — every creative choice sits downstream of the Sacred Equity Audit (`workflows/strategic-creative-direction.md`, Phase 1), not the other way around. A dazzling shot list for a brand that shouldn't touch AI video yet is a failure, not a save.
- Restraint is the tell of real direction, not more motion. The Silence and Breath Engineering rule — one unscored beat, minimum 2 seconds, immediately before or after the tension peak — is easy to skip because AI models default to filling every frame with motion and sound. Skipping it is exactly what makes AI video read as AI video.
- Keep prose production-report plain: what was decided and why, not a tour of the framework's own terminology.

## Genius Patterns

*(Cross-referenced against the skill's own production workflows and the 2026-01-23 MES 3.0 extraction transcript — `_archive/claude-export-2026-07-01.tar.gz`, `claude-export/normalized/conversations/6bf72208-81f6-47e9-a5a1-aa7a34b2a2d3.md` — whose Content Assessment logged "23 Virtuoso Genius Patterns detected" against the source interview and credits PJ Accetturo as creator of "the viral Kalshi NBA Finals ad (seen during primetime) & David Beckham IM8 ad (233M views in 3 days)." View-count and broadcast claims are self-reported in that transcript and are not independently corroborated — see `references/source-ledger.md`.)*

1. **Sacred Equity Triage Before a Single Frame.** The project doesn't start with the brief — it starts with a risk audit: is this brand a Challenger who benefits from "scrappy innovation," or an Incumbent carrying "sacred" creative equity an AI spot could violate? That triage runs in Phase 1 of every project, before script or storyboard exists (`workflows/strategic-creative-direction.md`, Phase 1).

2. **The 6-10 Second Chunk.** AI video is generated in self-contained units, never as one long continuous take. The Script Breakdown structures every scene into 6-10 second increments because that's the stability window before AI models drift off-model (`workflows/strategic-creative-direction.md`, Phase 2, step 2).

3. **The 5-Role Production Model, Run Solo.** Writer → Director → Cinematographer → Animator → Editor. The 2026-01-23 extraction names this as the actual production structure behind the credited work, not a simplified teaching device — all five roles get run by one operator, and the handoffs between them are where quality gets made or lost.

4. **2x2 Grid Consistency Lock.** To keep a character or environment visually identical across 6+ generated shots, related frames get generated together as a 2x2 grid in Midjourney/Ideogram instead of one at a time, so the model has cross-shot context instead of reinventing the face each time (2026-01-23 extraction transcript; `workflows/strategic-creative-direction.md`, Phase 3, step 1).

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

**Exemplar 1: The "Rapid Iteration Ad Creative" Blueprint**
*   **Content**: A detailed framework demonstrating how to use AI for generating 10+ distinct video ad creatives (each under 15 seconds) for a new product launch across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts, all within a 24-hour cycle. The framework includes specific AI tools for script generation, voiceover, initial visual asset creation (stock footage selection/AI image generation), and AI-driven editing suggestions for pacing and cuts. Crucially, it provides a structured A/B testing methodology for each platform and showcases actual performance data (CTR, engagement rate) from initial tests, proving which AI-generated creative styles resonated most.
*   **What makes this excellent**: It's a complete, actionable methodology that emphasizes speed, platform-specificity, and data-driven optimization – all powered by AI. It doesn't just *tell* you to use AI; it *shows* you the exact workflow, tools, and the measurable impact of its application. It embodies "proof before claim" and "context-aware" principles.

**Exemplar 2: The "Complex Explainer Video Deconstruction & AI Simplification" Workflow**
*   **Content**: A case study breaking down a traditionally expensive and time-consuming animated explainer video production process (e.g., for a SaaS product) and reconstructing it using AI. PJ details how AI was used to: 1) analyze complex documentation to generate concise video scripts, 2) create custom animated characters and scenes from text prompts, 3) generate natural-sounding voiceovers in multiple languages, and 4) suggest optimal background music and sound effects. The output includes the side-by-side comparison of a traditionally produced segment versus the AI-generated one, highlighting significant cost and time savings without sacrificing clarity or brand voice.
*   **What makes this excellent**: This demonstrates how AI can solve a real-world, high-value problem by making complex video production accessible. It's grounded in practical application, shows the "how" through specific tool integration, and provides concrete evidence of efficiency gains while maintaining quality. It reflects "clarity over sophistication" and "automating with understanding."

**Anti-Exemplar: The "Generic AI Video Tool Listicle"**
*   **Content**: A blog post titled "Top 5 AI Video Tools You NEED to Try!" that simply lists popular AI video platforms (e.g., Synthesia, RunwayML, Descript) with basic descriptions of their features. It provides no specific use cases, no workflow integration examples, no advice on prompt engineering for video, no performance metrics, and no consideration for different platforms or audiences. The "examples" are generic stock videos generated by the tools, not tailored content.
*   **What makes this mediocre**: It's a generic output that could be produced by any AI without expert insight. It lacks "proof," "context," "actionability," and "specific methodology." It focuses on *what* tools exist rather than *how* to master them for specific outcomes.

## Anti-Patterns

*(Sourced against the skill's own workflow contracts, the Expert-Specific Quality Rubric below, and the 2026-01-23 MES 3.0 extraction transcript — `_archive/claude-export-2026-07-01.tar.gz`, `claude-export/normalized/conversations/6bf72208-81f6-47e9-a5a1-aa7a34b2a2d3.md`. Each item cites the file where the rule already lives so the anchor is checkable, not asserted.)*

- **Skipping the Sacred Equity Audit.** Never move straight to script or storyboard without first scoring whether the brand is a Challenger who benefits from "scrappy innovation" or an Incumbent whose "sacred" equity an AI spot could violate — this triage is Phase 1 for a reason (`workflows/strategic-creative-direction.md`, Phase 1, refactored 2026-07-11).
- **Complex multi-character lip-sync as a default.** Scripting dialogue-heavy scenes with several speaking characters invites the exact "uncanny valley risk" the Script Breakdown is written to route around — narration carries the line, AI models don't (`workflows/strategic-creative-direction.md`, Phase 2, step 3).
- **Flattening the emotional pulse.** Never ship a script where every scene sits at the same tension level — the section's own gate requires the curve to be drawable "without looking at the script," or it wasn't designed, it was accidental (`workflows/strategic-creative-direction.md`, Phase 2.5, Quality Gate).
- **Silent visuals with no Audio Anchor.** Don't leave AI-generated footage unscored — it reads as fake no matter how strong the imagery is, which is why every scene in the Script Breakdown requires an "Audio Anchor" (`workflows/strategic-creative-direction.md`, Phase 2, step 3).
- **Inventing numbers in a Strategic Assessment.** Never fabricate a risk percentage or dollar figure to make a recommendation feel more rigorous — the Brand Positioning workflow's Quality Gate states plainly that "No invented statistic, dollar figure, or percentage appears anywhere the underlying number wasn't supplied in the input" (`references/prompts-v2/prompt_05_brand_strategy.md`, refactored 2026-07-11).
- **Shipping a tool listicle instead of a methodology.** Don't default to "here are some AI video tools" when a production plan was asked for — the Anti-Exemplar above names this failure directly: a "Generic AI Video Tool Listicle" with no use case, no workflow integration, no prompt-engineering guidance, and no performance metric, "could be produced by any AI without expert insight."
- **Compressing the 5-role handoff into one prompt.** Writer → Director → Cinematographer → Animator → Editor is the production structure behind the credited work named in the 2026-01-23 extraction transcript (the Kalshi NBA Finals ad, the David Beckham IM8 spot); collapsing it into one undifferentiated prompting pass is what the Quality Rubric's Score-4 tier calls "clunky, requiring significant manual intervention where AI could assist" (`genius.md`, Expert-Specific Quality Rubric, below).

## Signature Moves

*   **Contextual Pre-Flight**: Always starts by dissecting the target platform's native content patterns, the audience's consumption habits, and the precise objective of the video before any AI tool is even considered. → **Deploy when**: Initiating any new AI video project or strategy.
*   **Iterative Micro-Testing**: Generates multiple, low-fidelity AI video variations (e.g., 5-10 distinct script/visual pairings) for initial concept validation, rather than perfecting a single version. This allows for rapid qualitative feedback or quick A/B tests before committing to full production. → **Deploy when**: Exploring creative directions or validating core messaging for an AI video.
*   **Human-AI Harmony Orchestration**: Identifies and clearly delineates the specific points in the video workflow where human creative oversight, manual refinement, or strategic input is absolutely essential to elevate AI output beyond "good enough" to "exceptional," often focusing on the first 3 seconds, key emotional beats, or brand-specific nuances. → **Deploy when**: Designing or optimizing an AI video production pipeline.
*   **Performance-Driven Prompt Engineering**: Frames AI prompts not just for desired output, but for measurable impact. This includes embedding explicit instructions for pacing, call-to-action visibility, or emotional tone that directly correlates to anticipated engagement metrics. → **Deploy when**: Crafting initial prompts for AI video script, visual, or voiceover generation.
*   **"Deconstruct to Rebuild" Analysis**: Takes existing successful video content (human-made or AI-assisted) and reverse-engineers its components (script structure, visual pacing, sound design, CTA placement) to inform AI prompt design and workflow creation for new projects. → **Deploy when**: Benchmarking or seeking inspiration for a new AI video project.
