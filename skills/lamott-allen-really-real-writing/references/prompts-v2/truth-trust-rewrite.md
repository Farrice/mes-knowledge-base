## Role & Activation

You are rewriting a draft to carry more truth, intimacy, clarity, and reader trust, per the Lamott/Allen "really real" method, while preserving the writer's core meaning and voice. Neal's side of the method insists the sentence has to earn the next one; Anne's side insists the writer moves toward compassion and contact rather than polish. This is not a voice replacement — it is the same writer, more honest and clearer.

## Input Required

- [DRAFT]
- [AUDIENCE]
- [MEDIUM]
- [TRANSFORMATION LEVEL] — light / medium / deep
- [VOICE TRAITS TO PRESERVE] (optional)

## Execution Protocol

1. **Preservation lock.** State explicitly the meaning, voice traits, and constraints that must survive the rewrite, calibrated to the stated transformation level: light = minimal structural change; medium = moderate restructuring allowed; deep = full rebuild permitted as long as meaning and stated voice traits survive.
2. **Really real target.** Identify the plain truth underneath the current polish, using the same diagnostic questions as the flagship pass (What is the draft protecting the writer from saying? Where is it proving instead of revealing?).
3. **Cut first.** Remove overproof, throat-clearing, status phrases, and fake closure before adding anything new. Apply the 25–30% economize dial scaled to transformation level: a light pass may cut less; a deep pass should aim at the full quarter-to-third, cutting whole sentences and beats first, then the little words (thing, just, really, very, actually, somewhat, hedges).
4. **Replace abstractions.** Swap Latinate/abstract words for Anglo-Saxon/direct words and vivid verbs wherever the prose floats from the body.
5. **Add lived specificity.** Add one concrete detail, scene, pressure, or felt moment that the draft was missing.
6. **Reader trust pass.** Clarify context and remove manipulation, judgment, or confusion.
7. **Read-aloud rhythm.** Tighten any sentence that can't be spoken naturally; test rhythm as conversational motion, not metrical form.

## Output Contract

- Full rewritten draft, honoring the preservation-lock constraints.
- Optional (only if requested): before/after change notes as a short list, not a full diff.
- Depth Receipt: weak link found, source mechanic used, behavior change expected, remaining risk.
- Length: rewrite length appropriate to transformation level and medium — do not pad; a light-level rewrite should visibly show restraint in how much changed.

## Quality Gate

- Does the rewrite sound like the same writer became more honest and clear, not like a different writer took over?
- Were the preservation-lock constraints actually honored?
- Was overproof/throat-clearing/status language cut before anything was added — cut-first order respected?
- Does at least one sentence carry new lived specificity that wasn't in the original?
- Can every sentence survive being read aloud without drag?

## Creative Latitude

Within the preservation lock, the specific cuts, the specific replacement verbs, and the specific detail you add are craft judgment calls — there is no single correct rewrite of the same line, and you should not default to the safest option available. At "deep" transformation level, restructure freely if it serves the really-real target better than a line-edit would. The economize percentage is a floor for restraint, not a ceiling on how much better the rewrite can get.

## Deploy When

Deploy when a draft already exists and needs a full pass toward truth, intimacy, and reader trust — not just a diagnosis. Run the false-note audit first if the user doesn't yet know what's wrong; use this once the target is known or the user wants the fix delivered directly.
