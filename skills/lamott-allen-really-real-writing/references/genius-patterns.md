# Genius Patterns

12 executable patterns from Anne Lamott + Neal Allen's "really real" writing source package.

---

## Pattern 1: Really Real Target
**Execute**: Identify the sentence, idea, or paragraph that sounds polished but protected. Ask: "What is this trying not to say?" Rewrite it in plainer, more compassionate language.

**Success Metric**: The revised line feels more true without becoming melodramatic or self-indulgent.

---

## Pattern 2: Next-Sentence Persuasion
**Execute**: For each paragraph, mark the reason a reader would continue: motion, question, image, emotional stake, useful tension, or voice. Repair any sentence that has no continuation force.

**Success Metric**: The reader can move sentence to sentence without boredom, confusion, or forced effort.

---

## Pattern 3: Compassion Target
**Execute**: Find snark, loftiness, superiority, or clever self-protection. Replace it with the more humane recognition underneath.

**Success Metric**: The piece becomes warmer and more credible without losing edge.

---

## Pattern 4: Plain-Force Language
**Execute**: Replace inflated abstractions with shorter, direct words where the abstract word weakens contact.

**Success Metric**: Meaning lands faster and feels closer to lived experience.

---

## Pattern 5: Vivid Verb Repair
**Execute**: Circle weak verbs, especially forms of "be," "have," "make," "do," and "go." Replace only the verbs where motion or feeling is currently dead.

**Success Metric**: The sentence gains force without sounding over-written.

---

## Pattern 6: Anti-Overproof Cut
**Execute**: Remove the second explanation, defensive caveat, throat-clearing, and repeated proof where a friendly reader can infer the point.

**Success Metric**: The prose becomes shorter and more trusting without losing clarity.

---

## Pattern 7: Music Diagnostic
**Execute**: Score the draft for melody, rhythm, and harmony. Add the missing layer with one concrete revision: a fresh leap, a read-aloud rhythm fix, or a warmer reader relationship.

**Success Metric**: The piece feels more alive but still sounds like the writer.

---

## Pattern 8: Hard-Stuff Gate
**Execute**: Name the hard subject the draft avoids: fear, loss, hope, shame, tradeoff, desire, mortality, risk, or failure. Add one sentence that touches it directly.

**Success Metric**: The piece gains stakes without exploiting pain.

---

## Pattern 9: Reader-Trust Repair
**Execute**: Find where the reader may feel confused, judged, manipulated, rushed, or abandoned. Add context, restraint, or humility to keep them held.

**Success Metric**: A skeptical reader can follow and stay emotionally open.

---

## Pattern 10: Reader-Completed Silence
**Execute**: Cut one over-explained emotional beat and let a specific detail, pause, or ending carry the implication.

**Success Metric**: The reader feels the meaning more strongly because they complete it themselves.

---

## Pattern 11: Humane Ending
**Execute**: Remove fake closure, moralizing, and summary endings. End at the recognition the piece earned.

**Success Metric**: The ending feels inevitable, clear, and emotionally fair.

---

## Pattern 12: Blind-Spot Edit
**Execute**: Review the draft from the reader's side. Name one blind spot the writer cannot see from inside the piece, then revise without taking ownership away from the writer.

**Success Metric**: The revision improves trust, clarity, or depth while preserving the original voice.
