# Authority-Comedy Calibration Workflow

Deploy comedy intelligence in professional/authority contexts with precise calibration for when humor amplifies credibility vs. when it erodes it. This is the missing layer between "is the joke funny?" and "does the joke make the speaker more authoritative?"

## When To Use
- Writing LinkedIn content, thought leadership, or client-facing material where authority matters
- Any context where the speaker needs to be BOTH likeable AND credible
- When comedy is the vehicle for demonstrating expertise, not just entertainment
- Compounding with Lara Acosta (LinkedIn), Nicolas Cole (ghostwriting), or any authority-building skill

## The Authority Resonance Index (ARI)

Every comedic move in professional content scores on two independent axes:

| | Low Warmth | High Warmth |
|---|---|---|
| **High Competence Signal** | Dry expert wit (respected but distant) | Authority-amplifying comedy (the sweet spot) |
| **Low Competence Signal** | Failed joke (worst case) | Class clown (liked but not hired) |

**The goal is always top-right quadrant**: humor that simultaneously increases likeability AND perceived mastery.

### Three Laws of Authority Comedy

**Law 1: Humor FROM Expertise > Humor ABOUT Expertise**
- FROM: "The only thing that converts worse than a 47-field form is a 48-field form." (You can only joke about this if you've tested both. The specificity IS the credibility.)
- ABOUT: "I honestly have no idea what I'm doing with funnels." (Self-deprecation that attacks your core competence destroys authority.)
- Test: "Could someone WITHOUT this expertise make this joke?" If yes, it doesn't amplify authority. If no, it does.

**Law 2: Domain Specificity = Proof of Membership**
- The more niche the comedic reference, the more it signals insider status.
- "Marketing is hard" = zero authority signal. Anyone can say this.
- "Watching someone A/B test button colors while their headline promises 'revolutionary solutions'" = you've been in the trenches. The joke IS the credential.

**Law 3: Competence Before Comedy (The Sequence Rule)**
- In any piece of content, establish ONE clear competence signal before deploying humor.
- The competence signal earns the right to be funny. Without it, humor reads as deflection.
- Exception: self-deprecation about NON-CORE traits (being bad at replying to DMs, living on coffee) is safe anytime because it doesn't touch the expertise claim.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Authority Audit
Before writing anything comedic, answer:
1. **What expertise claim is being made in this piece?** (explicit or implicit)
2. **What would make the reader LESS likely to hire/follow/trust this person?**
3. **What would make the reader think "this person really gets it"?**

Map the answers. The comedy must serve answer #3 and never trigger answer #2.

### Phase 2: Comedy Style Selection (Authority-Matched)

| Authority Goal | Best Comedy Style | Why It Works | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establish credibility | Insider observation humor | Proves you've been there | Self-deprecation about competence |
| Build trust/relatability | Process struggle humor | "I tried X, it broke, here's what I learned" | Mocking the audience's pain |
| Demonstrate mastery | Precise absurdity | Exaggerate a real pattern only experts notice | Generic industry jokes |
| Challenge assumptions | Reframe humor | Name a universal experience nobody's articulated | Being contrarian for shock value |
| Create community | Naming humor (Pattern 6 from genius.md) | Give the shared experience a label they'll reuse | Inside jokes that exclude newcomers |

### Phase 3: The Authority Comedy Filter
Run every comedic line through three gates:

**Gate 1 — The Expertise Test**: "Does this joke require domain knowledge to construct?"
- YES → proceed (authority-amplifying)
- NO → rewrite with domain-specific detail or cut

**Gate 2 — The Credibility Direction Test**: "After laughing, does the reader think MORE or LESS of the speaker's competence?"
- MORE → proceed
- LESS → identify which Law is violated, fix
- NEUTRAL → acceptable but missed opportunity; try to upgrade

**Gate 3 — The Self-Deprecation Audit**: "If self-deprecating, does it target a CORE or PERIPHERAL trait?"
- PERIPHERAL (bad at mornings, messy desk, too many browser tabs) → safe, humanizing
- CORE (bad at the thing you sell) → lethal, rewrite immediately

### Phase 4: Produce with Standard Comedy Mechanics
Execute using all existing genius.md patterns (Skewed Perspective, Tension-Relief, Vivid Specificity, Concision, etc.) — but with every output filtered through the ARI gates above.

### Phase 5: Authority Resonance Score
Score each comedic element:

| Dimension | 1-3 (Diluting) | 4-6 (Neutral) | 7-10 (Amplifying) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Expertise Signal** | Generic humor anyone could write | Industry-adjacent but not deep | Only someone with real experience could write this |
| **Competence Direction** | Reader trusts you less after laughing | Reader's trust unchanged | Reader trusts you MORE after laughing |
| **Warmth Generation** | Joke falls flat or alienates | Mild smile, no connection | Reader feels "this person gets me" |
| **Memorability** | Forgettable | Decent but replaceable | Reader will quote this to someone else |

Minimum for authority content: Expertise Signal >= 7, Competence Direction >= 7.

## Output Contract
All standard comedy deliverables PLUS:
- Authority Resonance Score for each comedic element
- Which Law each joke operates under
- Self-deprecation audit results (if any self-deprecation used)

## Quality Gate
Standard comedy quality gate from genius.md PLUS:
- **The Hire Test**: After reading this, would you be MORE likely to hire this person? If not, the comedy is miscalibrated.
- **The Screenshot Test**: If someone screenshotted this joke and shared it, would it enhance or damage the author's professional reputation?
- **The Specificity Proof**: Does at least one joke contain a detail that proves firsthand experience?
