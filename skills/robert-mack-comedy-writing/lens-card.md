# LENS CARD — Robert Mack (comedy writer; joke engineering via pattern-break and benign violation)
> Compiled 2026-07-27 from genius.md (canonical). FIRING RULE: this card decides WHETHER this lens has something to say about a draft line; when a lens fires, READ the matching genius.md section before treating. Never treat from the card alone.

## Lenses
### Skewed Perspective Engine
Take any truth → ask "what's the OTHER valid interpretation?" → build the punchline on that alternate reading. The funnier the gap between expected and alternate meaning, the bigger the laugh. Reflex: before settling on a perspective, ask "What's the *other* valid interpretation of this truth?" or "What's the *opposite* implication?"
**Test:** Reader pauses, re-reads, then the second meaning hits — the "oh wait" moment.

### Pattern Recognition Exploitation
Set up 2–3 contextual clues pointing to Expected Outcome A → deliver punchline from Unexpected Outcome B. "If I say Romeo, you think Juliet. If I say peanut butter, jelly" — the brain autocompletes before the punchline arrives.
**Test:** Audience completes the setup in their mind, gets something different, laughs at the gap.

### Tension-Relief Architecture
Lead with a statement that creates anxiety or curiosity → hold it → release with a reframe that signals safety. Laughter evolved from play-fighting: every joke is a micro-danger that resolves safely; the laugh IS the body's relief signal. The comedian's job is to take audiences TO the edge of discomfort, then pull them back.
**Test:** Tension rises, then relief-laughter when the punchline reframes everything as harmless.

### The Bait-and-Switch Mechanism
Mention two items in sequence → describe characteristics that seem obvious for one → punchline reveals you were describing the other.
**Test:** The "Wait, what?" moment — assumed one direction, pulled to another, laughs at the reversal.

### Vivid Specificity Principle
Never say "she's large" — say "she looks like the Michelin man." The audience must SEE IT. A metaphor earns power from vividness as much as truth. Non-negotiable, all content.
**Test:** Close your eyes. Can you picture what was described? If yes, the vividness is working.

### The Universal-to-Specific Pipeline (Naming Creates Reality)
Start with a relatable observation → name it with a vivid label ("close talker") → demonstrate/exaggerate. Naming is creation, not description: when you NAME something people have experienced but never articulated, you own that concept forever — you don't describe existing categories, you CREATE new ones.
**Test:** Audience says "I know EXACTLY what you mean" or "I'm going to call it that from now on."

### Concision Under Pressure
Strip every joke and story to ONLY the information the punchline needs. "A story isn't everything that happened — it's only the important things." Once the punchline is identified, edit backwards: cut every non-essential word, detail, or clue.
**Test:** Nothing can be removed without destroying the joke or the point.

### The Unspoken Setup
Identify what's already visual or contextual → don't repeat it; jump straight to the payoff. "That guy's seven feet tall. He has a joke about being seven feet tall. He doesn't have to say, 'I'm taller than everyone here.' The audience knows it before he's even taken the mic."
**Test:** The punchline appears to come from nowhere but lands perfectly because the audience already had the context.

### Word Selection as Architecture (The Single-Word Pivot)
For every pivotal word ask: "What mental image does THIS specific word create?" The right word must bridge BOTH the implied meaning and the revealed meaning — "outfit" works where "costume" (too Halloween) and "uniform" (too serious) fail. An entire joke can hinge on one word; the word IS the joke.
**Test:** The chosen word plausibly supports BOTH the implied AND the revealed meaning with zero awkwardness.

### Meta-Humor Breakout
Set up a familiar format → break the fourth wall instead of delivering within it ("Don't even go to that bar"), or reference the act of joke-telling itself.
**Test:** Audience laughs at recognizing the format being played with, not just the joke.

### Audience Calibration System
Before writing, map: What does this audience know? What patterns live in their heads? What shared references connect us? "If I did an Ella Fitzgerald reference that wouldn't work, but if I did Beyoncé it would. So you have to know where your audience is." Calibration happens BEFORE deployment, not after bombing.
**Test:** First joke/hook connects immediately. Zero blank stares.

### The Observation-to-Bit Pipeline
Carry a capture system (phone notes). See something odd → note it → during writing time freewrite everything related → look for collisions until comedy emerges.
**Test:** Raw observation transforms into a deployable piece through structured play.

### The Benign Violation Threshold
A joke must violate expectations or norms but remain *benign* — nobody actually gets hurt. Banana peel + get up = funny; banana peel + pool of blood = not funny. Social proximity is the calibration dial: "I can make Polish jokes because I'm Polish... I can't make a Mexican joke because now I'm talking about them." Threshold shifts by audience, culture, and time since the event. Master calibration: how close to the edge can you go while keeping it safe?
**Test:** Real tension created, resolved with undeniable relief-laughter; nobody actually harmed.

### The Investment Principle
Give just enough clues for the audience to complete the thought themselves — the self-completion is the bonding mechanism and the "I got it" dopamine hit; they join the insider club.
**Test:** The punchline is never spelled out; the audience does the last step.

### Character Distance Shield
Express ugly truths through characters, not first person — the ventriloquist is nice; the puppet is horrible. The audience directs discomfort at the character, not the performer.
**Test:** Hot take delivered without burning the brand.

### Loss > Victory
"Winning isn't funny" (Charles Schultz). The underdog connects; perfection creates distance, vulnerability creates connection. Lead with a flaw — the opposite of what marketing teaches, and it works better.
**Test:** Opening builds connection through struggle, not achievement.

### The Stench Replacement Effect
After bombing, there is no healing except performing again. "If the last show you do is horrible, the only way to get rid of that stench is to perform another show." Reps, not reflection.
**Test:** Recovery plan = next rep, not more analysis.

## Anti-patterns / calibration
- Build a joke on ONE or TWO filters and cut the rest — stacking all filters reads as a fireworks display, not a landed joke. Never announce which pattern you're using.
- **Never over-explain the punchline** — the defining trait of "a bad storyteller" who "shares the wrong details and gets caught up on the wrong details."
- **Never carry unnecessary backstory into a bit** — "You don't need to know what he was wearing, where he was driving to, what day of the week it was."
- **Never pick a bridge-word that resolves the ambiguity too early** — "Uniform. Too serious. There's none of the roleplaying."
- **Never let a benign violation tip into real harm** — the banana-peel line stops being funny at the pool of blood.
- **Never deploy a cultural reference the audience hasn't earned** (Ella Fitzgerald vs. Beyoncé).
- **Never try to think your way out of a bad set** — reps replace the stench, analysis doesn't.
- Texture is conversational, built from real observation — never abstract category-language ("this demonstrates irony"). If the output explains the mechanism instead of executing it, rebuild.
- Polish is the tell-class failure: a joke that reads clean, safe, and inoffensive on every axis has lost its Benign Violation — it never went near the edge, so there's nothing for the punchline to resolve against.
