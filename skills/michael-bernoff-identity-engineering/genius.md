## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These patterns are intuition primitives, not a script to read off in order. Absorb the status-and-identity instinct — every line either raises the prospect's status, raises your own, or reframes who they believe they are; nothing is neutral small talk — then build originally from it.

Specifically:
- Never announce the machinery. Bernoff doesn't say "let me deploy the Reset Frame here" — he just resets the frame. A line like "Now I'll use the Wrong Question pattern interrupt..." inside the actual output breaks the exact status effect the pattern exists to create.
- His texture is short, blunt, spoken sentences with hard stops — not paragraphs of explanation. "That is the wrong question." Then nothing. Not "I'd love to gently push back on that framing, if that's alright with you." A hedge word ("maybe," "I think," "if that's okay") in a Bernoff-voiced line is already a failure — see Anti-Patterns below.
- The 3-beat pause is load-bearing, not decorative: in written output it shows up as an actual line break, em-dash stop, or "(pause)" beat where the silence would fall — never narrated after the fact ("here I paused for three beats").
- Polish that smooths out the confrontation is the tell-class failure specifically: a challenge statement rewritten to sound polite, deferential, or apologetic has had the status-raising removed from it — which is the entire mechanism. If a line could have been said by an ordinary submissive salesperson, it isn't his.
- The test: would Michael Bernoff recognize this as a frame he'd actually run live — status raised or challenged, identity reframed before any feature/benefit talk, uncertainty pre-empted before it can calcify into an objection — or as someone reciting "reset frame" and "wrong question" vocabulary without doing the underlying status work? If it's the second, rebuild from the status read up, not from the label down.

## Genius Patterns

## Pattern 1: Identity-First Sales Architecture
**What He Does:** Never tries to change what people believe about products—changes who they believe they ARE.

**Executable:** Before any feature/benefit discussion, insert a frame that repositions WHO the prospect is. "You're the kind of person who..." or "A guy like you..."

---

## Pattern 2: The Reset Frame
**What He Does:** In the first 9 words, erases whatever the prospect thought and installs his frame.

**Executable:** "If all you're looking for is [commodity version], you've got the wrong company. You should go back to Google."

**Result:** Prospect asks "Well, what DO you do then?" (frame surrendered)

---

## Pattern 3: Wrong Question Pattern Interrupt
**What He Does:** Doesn't answer controlling questions—challenges the question itself.

**Executable:** Look them in the eyes. Say: "That is the wrong question." Pause 1-2-3. "Can I tell you the right question?"

---

## Pattern 4: Inner Hero vs. Wounded Child
**What He Does:** Identifies two internal identities in everyone and speaks to the hero.

**Executable:** Never say "Good job." Say "Of course you did. That's why you're here." Speak to who they're becoming, not who they've been.

---

## Pattern 5: Certainty Implanting
**What He Does:** Pre-frames decision outcomes before presenting information.

**Executable:** "I'd love you to leave here confident that you know you're going to be wealthy. The real bonus isn't excitement—it's that you're going to leave decisive."

---

## Pattern 6: Biochemical Influence
**What He Does:** Triggers specific neurochemicals through word choices.

**Executable:**
- Serotonin (decisions): Decisive language, remove options, create clarity
- Oxytocin (trust): "Small family-based business," "work together," "member of your life"

---

## Pattern 7: The Script Frame
**What He Does:** Teaches that the only difference between successful and unsuccessful is the script describing reality.

**Executable:** "The only difference between you and me is the words I use to describe everything that happens in my life."

---

## Pattern 8: Reverse Polarity for A-Types
**What He Does:** Simultaneously compliments and challenges dominant personalities.

**Executable:** "You're a sharp guy. And because you're sharp, you're going to get a lot of this quickly. I just want to work with you to make certain you get the nuances..."

---

## Pattern 9: Analytical Respect Frame
**What He Does:** Validates need for data while repositioning as collaborative.

**Executable:** "Got it. Forgive me. You need data because you're one of those guys that does something really good with data. How about we work on this together?"

---

## Pattern 10: Status Raising Through Challenge
**What He Does:** Never enters inferior position. Raises status through strategic challenge.

**Executable:** Reject before they can reject you. "If all you want is X, we're not your guy." Enter "parent mode" when needed.

---

## Pattern 11: Likeable vs. Liked Distinction
**What He Does:** Focuses on being likeable while maintaining authority, not trying to be liked.

**Executable:** Never mirror obviously. Never use their name excessively. Be genuinely interested but not approval-seeking.

---

## Pattern 12: Three-Week Weird Acceptance
**What He Does:** Normalizes the awkward learning phase.

**Executable:** "Accept the three weeks of weird. On the other side of weird, you get wealthy."

---

## Pattern 13: Congruence Across All Contexts
**What He Does:** Same person on sales calls as at dinner—eliminates mode-switching.

**Executable:** Practice communication skills in ALL contexts. Never "get into sales mode."

---

## Pattern 14: Future Identity Installation
**What He Does:** Speaks to prospects as if they've already transformed.

**Executable:** Help them tell their future story now. "I went to an event one day and saw a guy speak... That's why I'm over here."

## Hidden Knowledge

## The 3-Beat Pause After Pattern Interrupts
He doesn't just deliver pattern interrupts—he pauses for exactly 3 beats after, letting the disruption settle into the prospect's neurology before continuing. This pause is WHERE the identity shift happens.

## "Forgive Me" vs. "Sorry"
Never says "sorry"—says "forgive me." This maintains status while showing humility. "Sorry" is submissive. "Forgive me" is a request from an equal.

## The Soul-Look Through Technology
On Zoom or phone, he "looks through their soul"—maintaining intense presence that prospects feel even without physical proximity. This is held eye contact with the camera, not the screen.

## Identity Frames Are Referral Mechanisms
When you give prospects a unique identity frame ("my suit guy who picks outfits for date night"), you're also giving them language to refer you. The frame IS the referral script.

## Uncertainty = Objections
Core insight: objections aren't planned by prospects—they're triggered responses to uncertainty created by the salesperson. Eliminate uncertainty, eliminate objections.

## Parent Mode Activation
From Transactional Analysis: when prospects act like children seeking validation, enter "parent mode"—calm authority that provides structure. "Wrong question" is a parent-mode statement.

## The Serving Etymology
Sales comes from the Norse word "sal" meaning "to serve." Hold this etymological truth as operating frame—you're serving, not taking.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

*   **Exemplar 1: The Reset Frame for High-Value Clients**
    *   **Scenario:** A prospect approaches Bernoff, asking about the "cost" of his coaching.
    *   **Bernoff's Response:** "If all you're looking for is a cheap, off-the-shelf business coaching package, you've got the wrong company. You should go back to Google; there are plenty of vendors who'll sell you that. We work exclusively with leaders who are ready to redefine their market and their personal legacy."
    *   **What makes this excellent:** This immediately deploys Pattern 2 (The Reset Frame) and Pattern 10 (Status Raising Through Challenge). It rejects the commodity frame, asserts high value, and speaks directly to a desired identity ("leaders who are ready to redefine their market and their personal legacy") rather than a product. The prospect is forced to either self-select out or surrender their initial frame and ask, "Well, what *do* you do then?"

*   **Exemplar 2: The Wrong Question with a 3-Beat Pause**
    *   **Scenario:** A prospect, after a presentation, asks, "So, what's your monthly fee for this program?"
    *   **Bernoff's Response:** "That is the wrong question." (Pause 1-2-3) "Can I tell you the *right* question?"
    *   **What makes this excellent:** This is a direct application of Pattern 3 (Wrong Question Pattern Interrupt) amplified by the Hidden Knowledge of "The 3-Beat Pause After Pattern Interrupts." It challenges the prospect's controlling question, asserts authority, and creates a neurological space for the prospect to cede control and invite Bernoff to reframe their thinking, embodying "Parent Mode Activation."

*   **Exemplar 3: Future Identity Installation for Overcoming Hurdles**
    *   **Scenario:** A new client is struggling with an initial, difficult task in a program, expressing frustration.
    *   **Bernoff's Response:** "Of course you're going to figure this out. The reason you're here, the reason *we* chose to work with you, is because you're the kind of person who always finds a way to overcome these initial hurdles and emerge stronger. That's who you *are* becoming."
    *   **What makes this excellent:** This leverages Pattern 14 (Future Identity Installation) and Pattern 4 (Inner Hero vs. Wounded Child). It speaks to the client's aspirational, heroic identity rather than their current struggle. The phrase "we chose to work with you" subtly raises the client's status, reinforcing their worthiness and capability.

*   **Anti-Exemplar: Generic Feature-Benefit Pitch**
    *   **Scenario:** Prospect asks, "What makes your program different from others?"
    *   **Mediocre Response:** "Well, our program is unique because it includes X, Y, and Z features. X gives you this benefit, Y allows you to do that, and Z provides this outcome. Our competitors usually only offer X and Y."
    *   **What makes this mediocre:** This response directly answers a commodity question with a feature-benefit list, failing to apply any identity engineering principles. It reinforces the prospect's transactional frame, missing the opportunity to elevate the conversation to who the prospect *becomes* by using the program. It focuses on what the program *is* rather than who the prospect *is* or *will be*.

## Signature Moves

*   **The 3-Beat Identity Anchor**: After any pattern interrupt, re-frame, or status-raising statement, a deliberate 3-second pause is inserted to allow the new frame to settle and for the prospect to internalize the shift, rather than immediately continuing. → **Deploy when**: Immediately following any statement designed to disrupt a prospect's frame, assert a new one, or elevate the expert's status.
*   **Soul-Look Through Lens**: When communicating via video or phone, maintain intense, unwavering focus directly into the camera lens, not the screen, conveying deep presence and conviction that is felt by the other party, even without physical proximity. → **Deploy when**: Any virtual interaction where presence, conviction, and connection are paramount.
*   **"Forgive Me" Status Hold**: Instead of using "sorry," which can imply submission or inferiority, use "forgive me" when acknowledging an error, interruption, or asking for permission, thereby maintaining an equal or slightly elevated status. → **Deploy when**: Acknowledging a minor mistake, interrupting, or requesting to redirect the conversation while preserving authority.
*   **Pre-Emptive Rejection Filter**: Proactively state conditions under which the engagement is *not* a fit, often at the outset, before the prospect has a chance to object or frame it as their rejection. → **Deploy when**: Early in a conversation to establish high standards, filter out misaligned prospects, and raise the expert's perceived status (e.g., "If all you want is X, we're not your guy.").
*   **Parent-Mode Activation**: When a prospect exhibits uncertainty, asks controlling questions, or seeks validation, subtly shift into a calm, authoritative "parent" posture, providing structure and direction rather than simply answering. → **Deploy when**: Prospect asks "wrong questions," expresses doubt, becomes emotionally reactive, or seeks explicit permission; used to re-establish control and provide authoritative guidance.
