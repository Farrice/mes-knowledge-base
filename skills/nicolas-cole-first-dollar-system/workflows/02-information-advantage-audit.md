# Information-Advantage Audit

Use this when the user has several interests or cannot choose what knowledge to monetize.

## Input Required

- Jobs, roles, projects, hobbies, repeated responsibilities, and lived experiences
- Observable results, artifacts, credentials, access, and permissions
- Topics the operator wants to keep learning for years
- Existing posts, conversations, search questions, inquiries, or buyer signals
- Sensitive or regulated-topic constraints

## Protocol

1. Inventory jobs, hobbies, and lived experiences.
2. For each candidate, separate:
   - firsthand context,
   - operational competence,
   - qualified authority,
   - independently verifiable proof,
   - borrowed proximity.
3. Reject any candidate whose required claims exceed evidence or qualification.
4. Stack-rank the remaining candidates by working knowledge.
5. Keep the top three to five and define one comparable public or direct-conversation test for each.
6. Compare relative outliers against the operator's own baseline; do not demand celebrity-scale numbers.
7. Select the candidate that best combines knowledge, observed pull, and sustained curiosity.
8. Hand the winner to `nicolas-cole-niche-positioning` for the Named Person test.

## Output Contract

Produce a ranked matrix with candidate, earned context, proof, authority boundary, reader/buyer signal, personal endurance, risk, test, and decision. Finish with one winner, one runner-up, and a Named Person handoff.

## Quality Gate

- Borrowed proximity is never labeled expertise
- Regulated claims have a credential/evidence boundary
- No more than five active candidates
- Tests are comparable and bounded
- Relative signal is separated from market proof
- Final niche remains provisional until a real buyer problem is observed
