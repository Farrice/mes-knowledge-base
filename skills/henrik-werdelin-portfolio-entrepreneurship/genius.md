# Henrik Werdelin — Genius Context

## Genius Patterns

### Pattern: Customer-First Origination
Werdelin refuses every conventional starting point: "You can't start with an idea. You can't start with a technology. You can't start with a market size. You have to start with: who do you want to serve for the next 10 years?" The ten-year horizon is the filter — it forces a customer you genuinely care about, because nothing else survives a decade of attention.
**Execute**: Before evaluating any venture idea, name the specific human being served. Ask: would the founder still want to talk to this person in year ten? If the answer is a market segment instead of a person, restart.
**Success Metric**: The venture brief opens with a named customer and the founder's authentic connection to them; the idea appears second.

### Pattern: The Five Ps Inventory
Ventures are extracted from the founder, not invented for a market. Five places to dig: **Powers** (what you're innately good at), **Passions** (what you'd do unpaid), **Possessions** (assets, audiences, IP you already hold), **Positions** (roles, access, credibility you occupy), **Potentials** (latent capabilities not yet expressed). "These are five ways you can look at yourself and figure out where do I have something innate in me that I can extract from and then build something around."
**Execute**: Run all five inventories before ideation. The strongest venture candidates sit at intersections of two or more Ps — a Power aimed at a Position, a Possession that serves a Passion.
**Success Metric**: Every proposed venture traces to at least one named P; ventures with no P-lineage are cut.

### Pattern: Donkeycorns Over Unicorns
The revolutionary goal is not one unicorn but "a million people making a million-dollar-turnover business — what we call a donkeycorn." Roughly 60% of Americans say they'd like to start something; only about 8% do, historically because it required resources and specific capabilities. "Now with AI, there's increasingly less of an excuse." Design for sustainable founder freedom, not venture-scale exit odds.
**Execute**: Size the ambition to ~$1M sustainable turnover per venture. Strip every cost and complexity that only exists to chase venture-scale. Optimize for durability of income and founder autonomy, not valuation.
**Success Metric**: The business model works — pays the founder well — at donkeycorn scale with no requirement to raise or exit.

### Pattern: Relationship Capital as the Only Moat
When AI commoditizes execution, the defensible asset is relationship capital, scored on three Ds: **Depth** (how well you actually know the customer), **Density** (how interconnected the relationships are with each other), **Durability** (how long the relationships survive stress and time).
**Execute**: Audit any business by scoring the three Ds separately. Invest in whichever D is weakest: depth via genuine knowledge of individual customers, density via connecting customers to each other, durability via consistency through downturns.
**Success Metric**: A competitor with identical product and lower price could not take the customer — because the relationship, not the feature set, holds them.

### Pattern: The Nike Hotel vs Hilton Shoe Test
Brand durability equals expansion permission. Nike could plausibly open a hotel — you can imagine what a Nike hotel feels like. Hilton could not credibly launch a shoe. The difference is whether the brand relationship encodes a worldview or merely a category.
**Execute**: Before adding a venture or product line, run the test: does the existing relationship give permission for this move? Describe what the expansion would feel like to the customer; if nothing specific comes to mind, permission is absent.
**Success Metric**: Expansions launch to existing customers who find them obvious ("of course they'd do that") rather than confusing.

### Pattern: Dunbar Squared Niche Sizing
Dunbar's number (~150 stable relationships) squared ≈ 22,500 — the TAM sweet spot for an AI-era relationship business: small enough that relationship capital can actually be held across it (especially with an agentic layer extending your reach), large enough to build real wealth at donkeycorn economics.
**Execute**: Define the niche so its realistic addressable population is in the tens of thousands, not millions. If the market is bigger, narrow by identity, situation, or worldview until it isn't.
**Success Metric**: The founder can describe the niche so precisely that members recognize themselves instantly, and ~1% penetration supports a $1M business.

### Pattern: The Cool Shit Paradox
"Every time I try to build something to make it successful, I end up not building something great. Every time I go for 'I want to build cool shit with people I like,' I end up doing something that seems to resonate." Optimization-first ventures die of joylessness; resonance-first ventures compound.
**Execute**: Validate opportunities on two axes before any market math: (1) does building this genuinely excite the founder, (2) are the people involved people the founder wants around for years. Kill ventures that pass the spreadsheet but fail the resonance check.
**Success Metric**: The founder would keep working on the venture even in a flat quarter — enthusiasm survives contact with drudgery.

### Pattern: The Label Model
Structure founder/studio relationships like a music label signs artists: provide infrastructure, distribution, and craft support; take royalties on revenue rather than controlling equity. The founder stays the artist; the studio stays the producer. This inverts the incubator paradigm of control-for-capital.
**Execute**: When supporting other founders, price the relationship as a percentage of revenue tied to value delivered, keep ownership and creative control with the founder, and make the studio's services genuinely re-purchasable rather than locked in.
**Success Metric**: Founders renew the relationship voluntarily because the infrastructure earns its royalty — not because a cap table forces them to stay.

### Pattern: The Supporting Actor
Werdelin identifies as "the studio producer who finds uncut gems," not the performing artist — the Motown model. The highest-leverage role is finding talented people and helping them find their thing, creating environments (physical and social) where the magic happens.
**Execute**: In any collaboration, ask whether the bigger win is performing or producing. As producer: scout latent talent, name what they're uniquely good at before they can, build the room where their work becomes inevitable.
**Success Metric**: People credibly say this person changed their trajectory — and the portfolio compounds through their successes.

### Pattern: Multi-Agent Venture Architecture
"There won't be that many one-agent businesses. A founder will serve a customer and then they will have multiple agents that will be part of this portfolio of tools they offer to their customers." The unit of design is one founder + one customer + many AI agents, each covering a function or offering.
**Execute**: For each venture, map the functions a 10-50 person company would staff, then assign each to an agent, an automation, or the founder's irreplaceable judgment. The founder keeps only what requires their relationship capital and taste.
**Success Metric**: One person operates a multi-offering business where every offering is agent-supported and the founder's hours go to relationships and direction, not production.

## Hidden Knowledge

### Insight: The In-Between Time Is Sacred
**Insight**: The vulnerable period after an exit or the end of a chapter — when identity is untethered from work — is where most founders make their worst decision: committing prematurely to the wrong next thing just to escape the discomfort of being nobody-in-particular.
**Deploy**: Treat the in-between deliberately. Run the Five Ps and micro-moments inventories during it rather than after committing. Set an explicit floor (e.g., a season, not a weekend) before signing up for the next chapter.

### Insight: Micro-Moments Reveal the Right Decision
**Insight**: Big decisions are better read from accumulated micro-moments of flow — the small instances where time disappeared and energy rose — than from pro/con analysis. The body's data about what generates flow precedes the mind's story about what should.
**Deploy**: Before a major life/business decision, inventory the last 90 days of flow moments and drain moments. Weight options by how much of their daily reality is made of the flow list.

### Insight: Stated vs Revealed Preference Is AI's Edge
**Insight**: The social web read superficial clicks (revealed behavior at its shallowest); conversational AI can read depth of language — what people actually mean, fear, and want. This is why relationship capital can now scale: an agentic layer can genuinely know thousands of people the way a good shopkeeper knows a hundred.
**Deploy**: When designing an agentic customer layer, instrument it to capture and act on language-level understanding (context, situation, stated goals) — and design it to build the relationship (remember, follow up, connect) rather than commoditize it into transactions.

### Insight: The Relevancy Club
**Insight**: Werdelin's stated fear is losing access to "the club of people who think about new stuff." Idea-driven entrepreneurs don't run on information — they run on being inside the conversations where the new thing is discussed eighteen months before it's obvious.
**Deploy**: Treat conversation access as an asset with maintenance costs: keep making things (the entry fee), keep being useful to other members, and budget real time for undirected exploration with smart people.

### Insight: Startups as a Way of Life, Not a Means to an End
**Insight**: The portfolio model only works when building is the life, not the toll paid for a later life. Ventures structured for exit force optimization behavior (see Cool Shit Paradox); ventures structured as a way of life allow compounding relationship capital across decades — and each new venture launches easier than the last because it inherits the customer relationships of the previous ones.
**Deploy**: Sequence portfolio ventures for the same customer so relationship capital compounds — the nth venture should launch into a warm audience the first venture built. Design ownership and pace to be sustainable indefinitely.
