# David Placek — Brand Naming Science

## Who Is David Placek?

David Placek founded Lexicon Branding in 1982 after working at Foote, Cone & Belding on the Levi Strauss account. He built the world's premier naming firm — 18 full-time staff, 250+ global linguists, proprietary sound symbolism research spanning 30+ years and multiple languages. His hit names include Swiffer ($5B brand), BlackBerry, Impossible Burger, Intel Pentium, Febreze, Sonos, Microsoft Azure, Windsurf, Vercel, SlimFast, Dasani, PowerBook, and Turo.

## Genius Patterns

1. **Ship of Gold Treasure Hunting** — Map the entire ocean of possibilities before diving. Generate 2,000+ candidates across multiple treasure-hunting zones. Never stop at 50-100.
2. **Surprisingly Familiar** — Names must satisfy a triad: original in category, processing fluent (easy to pronounce/understand), and unexpected. The brain gets lazy processing → then gets jolted.
3. **The Comfort Trap** — If everyone agrees a name is "fine," it's invisible. Polarization signals energy. Target the tension zone.
4. **Two-Person Teams, Three Divergent Briefs** — No brainstorming sessions. Run three parallel teams with deliberately different creative frames to maximize synchronicity.
5. **Approximate Thinking** — Ideas don't have to be fully baked. A spectrum from bizarre → approximate → safe. Give teams permission to stay in the middle.
6. **Sound Symbolism Engineering** — Specific phonemes carry measurable psychological weight: K/P/B = reliable/fast, Z = attention, X = innovation, V = vibrancy, CVCV = maximum processing fluency.
7. **Proof-of-Concept Contextualization** — Never present names on a spreadsheet. Show them in Wall Street Journal headlines, product mockups, endorsement ads. One rule: "Is this believable?"
8. **The Courage Culture** — "I wouldn't have thought of that" (credits). "I wish we could make that work" (redirects). Speculate, don't evaluate. Separate divergent from convergent phases.

## Signature Frameworks

- **The Strategic Question Sequence**: How do you define winning? → What do you *have* to win? → What do you *need* to win? → What do you *need to say*?
- **The Surprisingly Familiar Triad**: Original + Processing Fluent + Unexpected
- **The Comfort Trap Axis**: Tension Zone (energy, polarizing) vs. Invisible Zone (consensus, safe)
- **Sound Symbolism Scoring**: Speed, reliability, innovation, memorability, CVCV structure
- **The Divergent Brief Architecture**: Three teams × three creative frames × 2,000+ candidates

## When to Deploy This Skill

### Core Domain (Brand Naming)
- Naming a new company, product, feature, or content property
- Evaluating whether an existing name is helping or hurting
- Deciding whether to rename
- Running a creative team and wanting to increase output quality
- Building a named mechanism for marketing (e.g., "The Swiffer Method")
- Scoring name candidates using linguistic science instead of gut feel
