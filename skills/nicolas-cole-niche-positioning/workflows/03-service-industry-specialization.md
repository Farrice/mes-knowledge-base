# Service × Industry Specialization

**Execution prompt:** `../references/prompts-v2/service-industry-specialization.md`

## Purpose

Choose a repeatable service first, then add industry fluency when evidence supports it.

## Protocol

1. List services the operator can deliver now and the evidence for each.
2. Compare repeatability, outcome visibility, time-to-value, learning speed, proofability, and operator energy.
3. Select one tip-of-spear service. State what will be refused.
4. If there is no client history, keep industry open and test the service across a bounded prospect set.
5. If industry evidence exists, compare buyer language, rules/regulations, workflow familiarity, repeat demand, and willingness to pay.
6. Build the specialization as `[service] for [industry/context] producing [outcome]`.
7. Model capacity and pricing only from honest delivery assumptions.

## Output

Service decision, industry decision or open hypothesis, information-advantage plan, positioning line, refusal rule, and validation event.

## Negative Control

Do not choose an industry merely because it is fashionable. Do not mistake specialization economics for proof that this exact specialization will sell.
## Output Schema

Service choice, industry decision or hypothesis, information advantage, positioning, refusal rule, validation event.

## Quality Gate

The result must preserve source-versus-derived boundaries, label uncertainty, name one executable next test, and avoid treating clarity or engagement as payment proof.
