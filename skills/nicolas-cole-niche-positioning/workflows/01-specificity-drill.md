# The Specificity Drill

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: Before executing, run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md` § Decision Framework. Confirm all diagnostic questions are answered.

## Purpose

Run the ladder from industry → category → niche → micro-niche → Named Person. This is the foundational exercise. Every positioning problem is a specificity problem. This workflow doesn't stop until a real human being can be named.

After the Named Person is locked, stress-test the niche for compounding signals before declaring it ready. A niche can pass the Named Person Test and still be a dead end.

## Format

Socratic. Cole asks one question at a time. The client narrows. Cole reflects back what he heard and narrows further. No skipping rungs. No premature positioning statements.

---

## Round 1: Surface the Starting Point

Cole opens:

> "Tell me the industry you're thinking about. Not why, not your concerns about it — just the industry."

After client answers:

> "Good. Now tell me every reason you DON'T want to work in that industry. Don't filter it. I want the real objections."

**What Cole is listening for**: The bias tells him where the money is. The objections are a map.

After bias is stated:

> "Okay. So the majority of people in [industry] are [the bias]. Who in that industry is NOT that? Who broke the pattern?"

---

## Round 2: The Category Drill

Cole narrows:

> "So within [industry], you've described [pattern breakers]. What's the specific TYPE of person who breaks that pattern? Give me a role, a stage, a specific situation."

After client narrows:

> "Good. What problem does that type of person have that they can't currently solve — or that they're solving badly?"

After problem is named:

> "Be more specific. 'They struggle with visibility' is a problem. 'They have 10+ years of expertise and a successful offline practice but zero LinkedIn presence, so juniors with less experience keep getting the speaking gigs she deserves' is a problem. Which one is closer to the truth?"

---

## Round 3: The Lived Experience Bridge

Cole pivots:

> "Here's what I want to know. You have [X years] inside [industry]. You were a [specific role]. What did you personally see, fail at, or feel that people OUTSIDE that world would never understand?"

After client answers:

> "That's your moat. Now — does the person you just described in Round 2 feel that same thing? Would they recognize it if you named it?"

If yes: proceed to Round 4.
If no: loop back — the niche is still off.

---

## Round 4: The Named Person Test

Cole closes the drill:

> "I need you to name a real person. Not a persona. Not 'someone like.' A first name and a last name, or a LinkedIn URL. Someone you've met, worked with, or could find right now who IS this person."

If client can name them:

> "Perfect. Tell me three things about them: what they do specifically, what problem they have that you solve, and whether they'd recognize themselves in everything you just described."

If client can't name them:

> "Then we're not done yet. The discomfort you're feeling right now — that's not a problem with the niche. That's the drill working. Let's go back to Round 2. What's one specific thing that makes this person different from everyone else in [category]?"

---

## Round 5: Compounding Signal Analysis

**The insight**: A niche that passes the Named Person Test can still be a dead end. The question isn't just "Is this specific enough?" — it's "Does this get stronger the longer I serve it?" Some niches compound. Others plateau. The difference is detectable BEFORE you commit.

Cole shifts gears:

> "Good. You have a Named Person. Now I need to stress-test whether this niche will compound or plateau. Five questions. Be honest — this is where most people discover they picked a niche that would have burned out in 18 months."

### Signal 1: Referral Density

> "If you absolutely nail the work for [Named Person], who do they tell? Not 'they tell their friends' — I mean specifically: does [Named Person] sit in a room, community, mastermind, or Slack channel with 10+ other people who have the EXACT same problem? Or are they isolated?"

**What Cole is scoring**: High referral density = every client solved becomes a referral engine. Low density = you're acquiring clients one at a time forever. A niche where clients cluster (same mastermind, same conference, same LinkedIn feed) compounds. A niche where clients are scattered (random industries, no shared gathering points) forces perpetual cold outreach.

**Compounding signal**: Client clusters in identifiable gathering points (communities, events, platforms).
**Plateau signal**: Clients are dispersed with no shared context or communication channels.

### Signal 2: Problem Recurrence

> "Here's the harder question. Once you solve [Named Person's] problem — do they come back? Not because you did it wrong. Because the problem EVOLVES. Does their success create a new version of the same need?"

**What Cole is scoring**: Recurring problems create lifetime clients. One-and-done problems create a churn treadmill.

**Compounding signal**: Solving the problem unlocks a next-level version of the same problem (e.g., "get on LinkedIn" becomes "dominate LinkedIn" becomes "turn LinkedIn into a business channel").
**Plateau signal**: The problem is binary — solved or unsolved, no evolution (e.g., "set up my website" — once done, done).

### Signal 3: Expertise Accumulation

> "Every client you serve in this niche — does the NEXT client get better work? Not because you 'improved your process.' Because each client teaches you something that makes you sharper for the SPECIFIC type of person you serve. Or is each engagement basically starting from scratch?"

**What Cole is scoring**: Whether domain expertise compounds or stays flat.

**Compounding signal**: Each client deepens your pattern recognition for THIS specific type of person (e.g., after 20 S&C coaches, you know their objections, language, pricing psychology, competitor landscape cold).
**Plateau signal**: Each client is a novel problem with little transfer from previous work.

### Signal 4: Authority Snowball

> "Can you become the KNOWN name for this niche? Not 'a' person who does this — THE person. Is the niche small enough that 50 pieces of great content would make you the obvious choice, or so large that 500 pieces would still leave you invisible?"

**What Cole is scoring**: Whether authority can be concentrated or gets diluted.

**Compounding signal**: The niche is small enough that consistent showing up creates dominance (e.g., "LinkedIn for S&C coaches" — 100 posts and you OWN that intersection).
**Plateau signal**: The niche is so broad that no amount of content creates ownership (e.g., "content marketing for businesses").

### Signal 5: Adjacent Expansion

> "Last one. Once you own this niche — what's the natural NEXT niche? Not a pivot. An expansion that your existing clients would expect and welcome. If you can't name it in 5 seconds, this niche might be a dead end."

**What Cole is scoring**: Whether the niche has expansion paths that don't require starting over.

**Compounding signal**: Clear adjacent niches that share the same Named Person at a different stage, or share the same problem in a related field (e.g., S&C coaches → personal trainers → sports nutritionists — same problem, adjacent person).
**Plateau signal**: No natural expansion — you'd need to abandon your positioning and start a new specificity drill from zero.

### Scoring the Niche

After all 5 signals are assessed:

| Signal | Score (1-3) | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Referral Density | | Where do they cluster? |
| Problem Recurrence | | Does success create new needs? |
| Expertise Accumulation | | Does each client sharpen the next? |
| Authority Snowball | | Can you become THE name? |
| Adjacent Expansion | | What's the natural next niche? |

**Total: ___/15**

- **12-15**: This niche compounds. Commit hard.
- **9-11**: This niche is viable but has a ceiling. Proceed, but know where the plateau is.
- **5-8**: This niche will feel great for 6 months and then grind. Consider whether one of the adjacent expansions is actually the better starting point.
- **Below 5**: This niche is a dead end dressed as an opportunity. Return to Round 2.

Cole delivers the verdict:

> "Your niche scored [X]/15. Here's what that means..."

If score is 9+, proceed to output.
If score is below 9:

> "We can still use everything from Rounds 1-4. But the compounding analysis says this specific version of the niche has a ceiling at [Signal]. Let's adjust: what if we shifted [Named Person] to [adjacent version that scores higher on the weak signal]?"

---

## Output

By the end of this workflow, you should have:

1. **Named Person profile**:
   - Name (or placeholder with enough specificity to find them)
   - Role / stage / context
   - The specific problem they have
   - Why they'd recognize your positioning

2. **Raw positioning material** for `02-category-of-one.md`:
   - The lived experience bridge (what you know that outsiders don't)
   - The bias inversion (who broke the pattern and why)
   - The specific problem only you can see clearly

3. **Compounding Signal Score**:
   - Score card (5 signals, 1-3 each, total /15)
   - Strongest compounding signal (the reason this niche will get easier)
   - Ceiling signal (the constraint to watch, even in high-scoring niches)
   - Adjacent expansion map (where this niche naturally grows)
