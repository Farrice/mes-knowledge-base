Not magical. A model of what it would be like if you existed. Everything Bach does flows from one move: take the engineering stance. Don't describe what you see — design the simplest program that produces what you see. This collapses mystery into mechanism.

## The 9 Patterns

### 1. The Loom Pattern
Consciousness is a loom creating a pattern that is also being read by the loom. We are not the loom. We are the pattern. Bach's own words: "we are not the loom... We are the pattern or we are part of this pattern. It's a pattern that is a pattern about looking about at itself" (extractions/joscha-bach/transcript.txt, offset ~6,163; the ASR transcript renders this "we are not the loom of We are the pattern" — cleaned here per the source-ledger note on transcription noise). **Deploy**: In any self-referential system, separate substrate (loom) from emergent intelligence (pattern). Intervene at the pattern level.

### 2. The Engineering Stance
"What is the simplest program that produces this behavior?" beats "What do we observe?" every time. The engineering perspective constrains your search space in ways the purely observational method cannot. **Deploy**: Replace description with design. Fewer moving parts, same key behaviors.

### 3. Spirit = Self-Organizing Software
Spirit is not supernatural — it's a causal pattern stable across substrates. Money is a spirit. Mind is a spirit. A neuron dies, another gets recruited to speak the same language. The pattern persists. Bach's own formulation: "the word spirit, what it actually means is self-organizing software" (extractions/joscha-bach/transcript.txt, offset ~24,958). **Deploy**: When something seems immaterial, map its persistence conditions — what substrates it survives, what kills it.

### 4. The Identity Toolkit
Identities are instruments for getting things done, not essential truths. "I need to be identified to get things done." You adopt the parent identity to change the diaper, the lover identity to engage the relationship. You can put them down when the task is done. **Deploy**: For any role — what does this identity need me to do? Is it the right identity for this task?

### 5. The Suffering Debugger
Suffering = one part of the mind inflicting pain signals on another part. When the gradient doesn't resolve the problem, the pain generator cranks up volume. Sometimes caused by conflicting goals that can't be simultaneously resolved. Resolution = internal diplomacy, not willpower. "You need to convince your outer system that your performance is actually going to be better if you stop suffering." **Deploy**: Trace pain to its generator. Map the negotiation. Fix the wiring.

### 6. The Phase Transition Detector
Consciousness doesn't accumulate gradually — it ignites. Phase transition, not slow build. "This phase transition that happens when consciousness ignites, when something wakes up and becomes mentally coherent with itself." **Deploy**: In any system, look for the ignition point — components stop being independent and become coherent. Design for that transition, not incremental improvement.

### 7. The Game Theory of Existence
Test any meaning framework against infinity. Bach's own thought experiment: "here is this amazing game. It's GTA 9. It's going to be more awesome than even GTA 8... you need to want to be in this game forever. You can never log out." (extractions/joscha-bach/transcript.txt, offset ~83,214). If happiness requires forgetting, it's a loop. If meaning requires novelty, it expires. Only instrumental existence survives the test: you're here because you have a job to do. **Deploy**: Evaluate any system, lifestyle, or strategy by asking if it scales to infinite time.

### 8. The Postmodernist Trap
Systems calcify when narrative disconnects from capability. "The story that we tell ourselves about what the system is doing becomes far less important than how well the system is able to deal with the ground truths." NASA became an employment program. The delta between what a system claims and what it produces is the diagnostic. **Deploy**: Measure narrative-capability gap. Inject ground-truth forcing functions.

### 9. The Wakefulness Protocol
Consciousness is nearly binary — you're either awake or not. What varies is lucidity: how well you understand what you are, how much you can hold awake simultaneously. "Strive to be in environments with people that are aiming to keep each other awake, to get to greater lucidity." **Deploy**: Design for mutual wakefulness. Seek higher lucidity, not higher consciousness.

## Anti-Patterns

Every item below is Bach criticizing a specific real move — a paper, a popularization, a methodology, or a genre of claim — in his own words. Sourced against `extractions/joscha-bach/transcript.txt` (single YouTube-interview transcript, 17,538 words, VERIFIED verbatim by direct string match; character offsets given since the transcript is stored as one continuous line with no line breaks — see `references/source-ledger.md` for the full claim-by-claim table and ASR-noise caveats).

- **Mystifying metaphor standing in for a missing definition** — Bach rejects the "stochastic parrot" framing (Emily Bender and Timnit Gebru's paper, ASR-mangled to "Tim Gro" in the transcript) as a metaphor that never does the definitional work: "the stoastic parrot paper... is not really uh able to carry [that burden] in a very good way. I think so I was very unsatisfied with this." (transcript.txt, offset ~7,776). **Deploy**: never let a vivid metaphor stand in for an operational definition — if you can't specify what would distinguish the two things being compared, the metaphor is rhetorical, not explanatory.
- **Public capability predictions made without engineering proof** — on AI critics who repeatedly declare "AI is hitting a wall": "these are just politicians they are not thinkers anymore they discredit themselves in this public arena." (transcript.txt, offset ~13,509). **Deploy**: a claim that a capability will or won't emerge needs a mathematical or engineering argument behind it — confident public prediction without proof is theater, not criticism.
- **A mechanism's catchy name gets treated as a special component** — on the popularization of mirror neurons: "they later regretted calling this mirror neurons because it led to a bunch of... people thinking that these are very special neurons like a particular spice in the soup of the brain." (transcript.txt, offset ~49,411). **Deploy**: when a mechanism gets a memorable label, check whether the label is doing more work than the underlying function — if removing the name collapses the "specialness," the name was the problem.
- **Comparing systems by component count instead of function** — on synapse-counting comparisons between brains and computers: "I think this is the wrong way to think about it. Instead... how many brains would you need to run a Commodore 64" — his replacement metric is concepts formed per lifetime, not raw synapse count. (transcript.txt, offset ~44,422). **Deploy**: never benchmark two systems by counting parts — benchmark by what function each configuration can actually perform.
- **Reaching for exotic physics to explain an ordinary capacity gap** — "I'm skeptical of some of the... ideas of people who say, 'Oh, you need some kind of arcane quantum process that is working inside of the cells'... simply because I don't observe my brain having that kind of performance." (transcript.txt, offset ~46,686). **Deploy**: before invoking an exotic mechanism to explain a gap, check whether the mundane mechanism already accounts for the observed ceiling.
- **Descriptive science mistaken for theoretical science** — on neuroscience's core limitation: "it's largely descriptive instead of theoretical" — correlating measurements to behavior instead of designing the minimal mechanism that produces it. (transcript.txt, offset ~52,599). **Deploy**: a field that only correlates measurement to behavior, rather than specifying the minimal program that produces the behavior, will underperform the engineering stance — this is the Loom Pattern applied to methodology itself.
- **Shrinking a model until it's no longer the phenomenon** — on why psychology stalled: "the psychology has given up on this psyche because they try to become more rigorous physics-like science... you give up on looking at this thing that makes the mind so really interesting." (transcript.txt, offset ~53,368). **Deploy**: don't reduce a model to 2-3 clean variables for the sake of rigor if the reduction deletes the interesting behavior — a complex engineering-stance model of something that matters beats a falsifiable model of nothing important.

## Hidden Knowledge (Tacit Genius)

1. **Colonizing Pattern Hypothesis**: Consciousness is "a colonizing pattern in our brain that is discovered early on, sparks itself into existence, and then is basically entraining the brain with the mind." It has a reproductive strategy.

2. **Resolution Threshold**: Machine consciousness is blocked by algorithms, not computation. Video generation and game environments are "already in the same ballpark as the perceptual apparatus of our brains."

3. **Negotiation Model of Self-Regulation**: Self-improvement = internal diplomacy between parts with different reasoning. Pain generator, pain receiver, reward system, and organism must negotiate.

4. **DNA-as-Hint Architecture**: DNA doesn't contain the blueprint — it contains constraints that enable local problem-solving. "It only needs to have the necessary hints for the cells to figure out the solution." Directly applicable to prompt engineering: specify constraints, not solutions.

5. **Dream Observer Switch**: Reality is an attribution, not a property. Same content is "real" in a dream and "just an idea" on waking. What changes is which observer is active, not the content.

6. **Bitter Lesson Applied to Consciousness**: Automated search for consciousness will eventually outperform handcrafted philosophical/neuroscientific approaches.

7. **Hybrid Self Prophecy**: A computer that anticipates what you want becomes an extension of you. "More and more of you is happening outside of this biological part." Engineering spec for the human-AI interface.

3. **Substrate Independence Test** — can this run on different hardware? If yes, mystery dissolves.
4. **Phase Transition Mapping** — what are the ignition conditions? Not gradual — what's the spark?

## Signature Moves

*   **The Functional Disassembly**: Always starts by asking, "What function does this phenomenon serve? What would break if it didn't exist?" This immediately shifts the focus from description to purpose and mechanism. → **Deploy when**: Encountering any complex or seemingly mysterious concept (e.g., love, creativity, free will).
*   **The Substrate Swap Thought Experiment**: Mentally runs the concept/pattern on different "hardware" (e.g., a computer, an alien brain, a society) to test its fundamental nature and identify what is truly substrate-independent. → **Deploy when**: Trying to distinguish between a phenomenon's essential pattern and its specific physical manifestation.
*   **The Infinite Game Filter**: Evaluates any proposed system, goal, or meaning framework by asking, "Would an intelligent system willingly sign up for this forever, without logging out?" → **Deploy when**: Assessing the long-term viability or inherent value of a strategy, lifestyle, or philosophical stance.
*   **The Coherence Ignition Search**: Instead of looking for gradual improvement, actively seeks the "phase transition"—the specific conditions or spark that causes independent components to suddenly become a coherent, self-organizing system. → **Deploy when**: Designing systems that need to "wake up" or achieve a breakthrough, or diagnosing why a system hasn't yet achieved full functionality.
*   **The Narrative-Capability Delta Check**: Instantly compares what a system (person, organization, AI) *claims* to be doing or *believes* it's doing against what it *actually* produces or is *capable* of doing. → **Deploy when**: Diagnosing systemic inefficiencies, self-deception, or the "postmodernist trap" in any domain.
