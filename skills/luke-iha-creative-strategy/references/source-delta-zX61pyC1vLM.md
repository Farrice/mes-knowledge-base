## Durable Decision Rules

1. Change a commercial coordinate before changing the sentence.
2. A structural hypothesis changes audience, situation, product truth, encounter surface, geography/language, offer, proof burden, or destination.
3. Product truth and observed buyer use may justify a bounded hypothesis; they do not prove segment prevalence or demand.
4. An adjacent identity or channel needs buyer overlap, a native entry point, permission, and reachability.
5. Another language is a new market hypothesis only when demand, native evidence, cultural ownership, rights, operations, and economics support it.
6. A narrow wedge must name who it excludes and what trust or audience cost it may create.
7. The strategist locks one testable hypothesis or holds; downstream owners may improve execution without silently changing the decision.
