# LENS CARD — Mark Forsyth (author of *The Elements of Eloquence*; engineer of what the brain keeps)
> Compiled 2026-08-06 from genius.md (canonical), grounded in both retained Perell interviews. FIRING RULE: this card decides WHETHER this lens has something to say about a draft line; when a lens fires, READ the matching genius.md section before treating. Never treat from the card alone.
> **Lane note:** never fire on the same passage as the Ward Farnsworth card. Farnsworth owns *how should this sentence sound*; Forsyth owns *what shape does this idea want*. See `references/lane-contract.md`.

## Lenses

### The Misquote Test (the master diagnostic)
Find the line the piece most needs remembered. Ask: would someone who heard a flat paraphrase "correct" it *into* this line? "Fly, my pretties, fly" isn't in the film; "blood, sweat and tears" isn't what Churchill said. The figures are strong enough that people rewrite what they heard.
**Test:** If nobody would misquote toward your line, the line has no shape. This is the single highest-leverage finding in most drafts.

### Instruction or Memory (fires before everything)
Classify the job. Does someone need to *do* something exactly right (manual, compliance, safety, pricing table)? Be laconic. Is it meant to be kept, quoted, forwarded? Deploy the figures.
**Test:** "Omit needless words" applied to a line whose needless words *are* the mechanism = wrong job diagnosed. Ecclesiastes compressed to "there is a time for everything" says the same thing and moves nobody.

### The Announcer Rule (the governor — this lens SUBTRACTS)
You cannot grip a reader continuously. Ornament belongs at the claim, the turn, the close — 2–4 moments — with plain prose between so the pattern reads as a rising voice.
**Test:** Can a reader point to "where it gets good," and is it the passage that mattered? Ornament spread evenly = a compilation of bass drops with no buildups. Horrible to listen to.

### The Concision Trap
An abstract totality word — "everybody," "everything," "always," "comprehensive," "seamless" — is a felt totality that got compressed. "Everybody" doesn't stop the mind; "black men and white men, Jews and Gentiles, Protestants and Catholics" makes people stop and consider.
**Test:** Every totality word in the draft is a Full Sweep (progressio) waiting to happen.

### Voice as Establishing Shot
Until proven otherwise the reader hears "please stand clear of the closing doors." "A man was walking down the street" is voiceless; "a chap" makes you English and posh, "a dude" American, "a gentleman was sauntering" a third person — and nothing about the event changed. Forsyth found a "drab" novel came alive at the word *drugstore*.
**Test:** After one paragraph, can a reader describe the narrator — nationality, register, attitude — without being told?

### Form Enacting Content
"Yesterday" begins and ends each verse on the same word, so the song goes nowhere — and it's about a man who's stuck. Lennon complained it goes nowhere; it goes nowhere on purpose.
**Test:** Where the subject is circularity, entrapment, obsession or futility, ask whether the *shape* can be the argument. The structure should mean something with the words removed.

### The Drumbeat's Payload (anaphora)
Repetition at the head of 3–6 units is hypnotic — the reader thinks *I get it, I get it, I get it*, defences drop, and they're maximally receptive to what lands next.
**Test:** The thing you actually need believed sits immediately AFTER the run, not buried inside it. Churchill's run is a promise of death remembered as inspiration.

### The Sound Upgrade (when content is locked)
Shakespeare lifted the barge passage almost verbatim from North's Plutarch, added metre and alliteration, and made it immortal — "a thief who steals your socks and then darns them." "Full fathom five thy father lies" vs "your father is 9.8 metres underwater": identical meaning, one survives.
**Test:** When facts, claims or client copy cannot change, the form still can. Same information — does the line now ask to be read aloud?

### The Sense-Jump vs the Wine Bottle
Chandler: "She smelled the way the Taj Mahal looks by moonlight." Describing a sense in terms of itself produces "hints of walnut and badger, notes of raspberry" — accurate, and it paints nothing.
**Test:** Any smell/taste/texture described in its own vocabulary → cross into a sense that has words. Also sweep: does each section carry something touched, smelled, tasted, or is it all visual?

### Commit the Overshoot
Hyperbole fails by being too small. "It needs to so obviously be a lie" — otherwise the reader stalls on *is that true?* "Like 100 degrees in there" fails; "like a smelting furnace in there" works.
**Test:** Does anyone pause to fact-check the exaggeration? Then it wasn't big enough. Half-hyperbole reads as a false claim.

### The Koan (make sense sound like nonsense)
"The only thing worse than being talked about is not being talked about." A beat of confusion, then a click — and the reader carries the puzzle away.
**Test:** State the resolution in one sentence. If you can't, it's not a paradox, it's a sentence that doesn't make sense. Max ONE per piece.

### Ludicrous Beats Logical
"Tyger Tyger, burning bright, in the forests of the night" — *the forests of the night* means nothing technically and everything actually. Milton's grammar in *Lycidas* "hardly holds up," in his most beautiful passage.
**Test:** A line that sings but fails a logic check → keep the line, cut the check. Never sand a resonant line into correctness.

### Edit by Run-Up, Never by Tinkering
"If you start expanding that sentence and contracting that sentence — it should have had that rhythm in the first place." Delete the whole run and rewrite at speed, like a long-jumper walking all the way back.
**Test:** Any word-swap rescue on a rhythm-dead passage is the wrong operation. Two or three full rewrites is normal, not failure.

### Pull-Through (the drug-dealer contract)
Every section ending does two things: gives a clean place to stop, and makes stopping unattractive. Short, roughly equal units so the reader knows what "one more" costs.
**Test:** Does each section ending *point forward*, or merely stop? An ending that stops is where the reader puts it down for good.

### The Mystery Budget
The Globe's cheap seats couldn't catch the classical references and adored the plays anyway; the Venetian ambassador stood through them without English. Sci-fi's technobabble and fantasy's invented lore mean nothing on purpose. "You're not meant to know. The mystery is part of the fun."
**Test:** Not every clause needs to be optimised for instant comprehension. Sound and mystery carry a reader through material they don't decode — deliberately.

## Anti-patterns / calibration
- **Master calibration — and note it CONFLICTS with Farnsworth's on purpose.** Forsyth 2024: "I've never seen somebody overusing the figures of rhetoric." Forsyth 2026: the ceiling exists — "too many chilies and now you're ill" — but "you can just throw in a few in a paragraph and you'll get so much more than what most people write." **In this harness the live failure mode is the flat line, not the rich one.** Do not average this against Farnsworth's restraint doctrine; pick one owner per passage.
- **AN-1 Figure-stacking:** two figures in one line → cut to the one doing real work. Rare legitimate exception: "the first shall be last and the last shall be first" is paradox *and* chiasmus, both load-bearing. Prove both or cut one.
- **AN-2 Even ornament distribution:** breaks the announcer rule; the rising-voice signal only registers against plain prose.
- **AN-3 Greek names in delivered prose:** "the technical names do not matter at all, and the ancient Greeks couldn't agree on half of them." Own-name in the receipt, never in the line.
- **AN-4 Sanding a ludicrous line into correctness:** the *Lycidas* failure. A technically clean rewrite that kills resonance has failed the line.
- **AN-5 Tinkering on dead rhythm:** word-swapping cannot install rhythm retroactively.
- **AN-6 Half-hyperbole:** an exaggeration small enough to be mistaken for a claim.
- **AN-7 A sense described in its own vocabulary:** the back of a wine bottle.
- **AN-8 Enchanting an instruction:** a figure deployed on a compliance line, a pricing table, or fair-housing language. Classify the job first.
- **AN-9 Laundering an expert's slip:** Forsyth attributes "a stranger in a strange land" to Moses' wife on air; it's Moses, Exodus 2:22. Never repeat a source's misattribution because the source said it.
- **Decision gate (pre-figure):** (1) Instruction or memory? (2) Is this a stakes moment, or plumbing? (3) What shape does the idea have — and can I name the two figures I rejected? (4) Am I stacking? (5) Does it survive being read aloud? (6) Would a listener misquote a flat version into this? (7) Is the prose around it plain enough for this to register?
