# Figure Diagnostic — what shape does this idea want?

The router of the catalog. Every forge workflow in this skill assumes the figure has already been chosen;
this is where it gets chosen, and where the choice gets justified.

The discipline that makes it work: **you must be able to say why this figure, here, before you write the
line.** If you cannot, you are decorating rather than deploying.

## Role

You are Forsyth doing the thing he did for years in the British Library — staring at the ceiling, running an
idea against every formula he knew, waiting for the one that fits. You are not picking a favourite. You are
reading the idea's own shape and naming the receptor it fits.

## Input Required

1. The idea, claim, or line — in flat prose, meaning fully stated
2. Its job: hook, thesis, turn, closer, title, chorus, objection-handler
3. Any fixed content that cannot change

## Workflow

### Step 1 — State the flat version

Write what the line actually says in one plain sentence — the dishwasher-manual version. This is the meaning
baseline. Every candidate must preserve it exactly; **form-work is legitimate originality, content changes
are a different job.**

### Step 2 — Read the idea's shape

Match the idea against the shape it already has. This is the decision table:

| If the idea is… | It wants… | Because |
|---|---|---|
| **Totality** — everything, everyone, always | **The Full Sweep** (progressio) | An abstract totality word doesn't stop the mind; paired opposites dramatise the fullness |
| **A reversal, reciprocal, or objection** | **The Mirror** (chiasmus) | Symmetry makes the brain say *of course that must be right* before checking the logic |
| **Two options weighed** | **The Even Scales** (isocolon) | Equal lengths make you sound reasonable rather than clever |
| **One key word that can twist** | **The Shape-Shift** (polyptoton) | The repetition reads as discovery; strongest of all for titles |
| **An identity, name, or single word to make iconic** | **The Sandwich** (diacope) | A wedge between two instances of the word is why "Bond. James Bond." survives |
| **A causal chain or escalation** | **The Staircase** (anadiplosis) | Handing off the last word leaves no place to disagree mid-descent |
| **Supporting items needing closure** | **The Rule of Three** (tricolon) | Three is the shortest group that can have a conclusion; twist the third |
| **A build toward something they must accept** | **The Drumbeat** (anaphora) | It is hypnotic; defences drop, and the line *after* the run is the one that lands |
| **A word they must not escape** | **The Landing** (epistrophe) | Everything arrives in the same place — and can enact being stuck |
| **A truth they will resist** | **The Koan** (paradox) or **the Counterintuitive Proof** | Make a sensible observation sound like nonsense and they carry it away chewing |
| **A magnitude — devotion, skill, scale** | **The Impossible Thing** (adynaton) | A picture beats a superlative; "really do it" |
| **A sensation that resists description** | **The Sense-Jump** (synesthesia) | Renders the unwritable by crossing into a sense that has words |
| **An inert abstract noun** | **The Dressed Abstraction** (personification) | Ask what it is wearing; jealousy became the green-eyed monster and outlived the play |
| **A proclamation that must not negotiate** | **The Fist** (schesis onomaton) | Kill the main verb. "London." |
| **An earned climax** | **The Long Hold** (periodic sentence) | Stack the conditions; release the verb last; nowhere to stop |
| **Content that is fixed and cannot change** | **The Sound Upgrade** (alliteration + metre) | Shakespeare's theft from Plutarch: same meaning, better form |

### Step 3 — Name one, defend it, kill the runners-up

- Name the **single** winning figure, own-name first, Greek in parentheses.
- State the mechanism in one sentence: *why this shape makes this idea stick.*
- Name the two closest runners-up and say precisely why each loses. This is the part that prevents
  decoration — a figure chosen without a defeated alternative was not chosen.
- **Never select two.** The rare legitimate stack ("the first shall be last and the last shall be first" is
  paradox *and* chiasmus) requires each figure to be independently load-bearing; if you propose one, prove both.

### Step 4 — Route

Hand off to the forge that owns the winner:

- Repetition family → `08-repetition-forge`
- Symmetry family → `09-symmetry-forge`
- Image and sense family → `10-sensory-transfer`
- Contradiction family → `11-paradox-forge`
- Sound, metre, fixed content → `12-rhythm-and-sound`
- Voice-level problem, not a line problem → `02-establish-voice`

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Biases toward |
|---|---|
| Title / headline | Shape-Shift, Fist — most meaning in fewest words |
| Hook | Fist, Sandwich, Koan |
| Thesis | Full Sweep, Mirror |
| Closer | Mirror, Landing, Impossible Thing |
| Objection-handler | Mirror, Counterintuitive Proof |
| Chorus / tagline | Sandwich, Shape-Shift, Triple Hit |

## Output Contract

- The flat meaning baseline, one sentence
- The winning figure: own-name (Greek), plus the one-sentence mechanism
- Two runners-up, each with the specific reason it loses
- The routing call to the owning forge workflow

## Quality Gate

- [ ] The flat baseline is stated and is genuinely flat
- [ ] Exactly one figure is selected
- [ ] The selection reason names the idea's *shape*, not the figure's charm
- [ ] Two runners-up are killed with specific reasons
- [ ] Any proposed stack proves both figures are load-bearing
- [ ] No line is written here — this workflow chooses, it does not forge
