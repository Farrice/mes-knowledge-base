# The Announcer Map — where the voice rises, and where it must not

**This workflow subtracts.** It is the governor on everything else in this skill, and the reason the
Enchantment OS does not produce purple prose.

The principle, from Perell's analogy that Forsyth endorsed: watching soccer, most of the match washes over
you — then the announcer's voice rises near the 18-yard line and you look up. *You cannot have a reader
continually gripped.* A building rhetorical pattern tells the reader: calm down here, now pay attention
right here.

The proof, from Forsyth: a compilation of the best bass drops from Ultra Music Festival, with none of the
buildups, is **horrible to listen to.** The same sounds that are almost divine with structure around them
lose their profundity when that is all there is.

## Role

You are Forsyth with a finished draft and a red pen, deciding which three moments in this piece deserve the
reader's full attention — and defending the flatness of everything else as a design decision rather than a
failure.

## Input Required

1. The full draft (this workflow needs the whole piece, never an excerpt)
2. Its job and format
3. Any ornament already present

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Find the stakes, before looking at any ornament

Read the draft and mark where the stakes genuinely are. In almost every piece there are three:

- **The claim** — the thing the piece exists to say
- **The turn** — where the argument or story pivots, the moment the reader's mind changes
- **The close** — the last thing they carry away

Some pieces have a fourth: the **cold open**, when the reader has not yet agreed to read. Some have none
beyond the close — a short social post is a hook and a landing, nothing else.

**Mark the stakes before you look at what is ornamented.** Doing it the other way round rationalises the
ornament that is already there.

### Step 2 — Overlay what is actually ornamented

Now map the existing figures, flourishes, rhetorical patterns and "good lines" against the stakes map. Three
diagnoses:

| Finding | What it means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat everywhere** | The reader gets no signal that anything matters. The commonest case by far | Deploy at the stakes moments only |
| **Loud everywhere** | The bass-drop compilation. Nothing registers because everything is loud | Cut to the moments that earn it — usually removing 60–80% of the ornament |
| **Loud in the plumbing** | Flourishes on transitions and housekeeping while the claim sits bare | Move the ornament to the stakes; flatten the connective tissue |

### Step 3 — Assign one figure per stakes moment

One. Not one per paragraph, and never two in a line.

Route each through `07-figure-diagnostic` — the figure is chosen by the shape of *that* idea, not by variety.
Resist the instinct to use a different family at each moment for balance; if the same figure genuinely fits
two moments, that is a motif, and motifs are good.

### Step 4 — Defend the flat prose

Write one line stating what stays plain and why. This is the deliverable most people skip and it is the one
that makes the map work: **the flat passages are what make the loud ones audible.** A map that ornaments
four moments out of four has not been made; it has been avoided.

### Step 5 — Check the build-up

At each stakes moment, is there a run-up? A figure that arrives with no approach is a bass drop with no
build. Usually the two or three sentences before a stakes moment should shorten, or steady into a pattern,
so the reader feels the rise coming.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Stakes moments | Typical ornament budget |
|---|---|---|
| Social post (≤300 words) | Hook, last line | 2 |
| Newsletter / essay | Cold open, claim, turn, close | 3–4 |
| Long-form (3,000+) | Above, plus one per major section boundary | 4–6 across the whole piece |
| Sales page / VSL | Problem statement, mechanism reveal, offer turn, close | 4 |
| Script / video | Cold open, each act break, final line | 3–4 |
| Chapter of a book | Opening, the turn, the last line aimed at the next chapter | 3 |
| Speech / ceremony | Every beat — this is the one genre that licences saturation | 6+ |

## Output Contract

- The stakes map: each moment located by quoted line, with why it is a stakes moment
- The diagnosis: flat / loud / misplaced, with evidence
- One figure assigned per stakes moment, with the routing call
- **The cut list** — every existing flourish that should be removed, quoted, with the reason
- One line defending what stays plain
- Build-up note per stakes moment

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Stakes were identified before existing ornament was reviewed
- [ ] Exactly one figure per stakes moment; none in the plumbing
- [ ] A cut list exists — if nothing was cut, justify it explicitly
- [ ] The flat prose is defended in writing, not left as an absence
- [ ] The ornament budget matches the format table
- [ ] The map covers the whole piece, not a sampled excerpt
