# The Run-Up Rewrite — delete the whole thing and go again

The one editing method in this skill, and it is a prohibition as much as a technique.

> "Editing I find can kill it. If you start expanding that sentence and contracting that sentence — it
> should have had that rhythm in the first place. If it reads wrong, the best thing is delete the whole
> thing and go again."

Forsyth's metaphor: a long-jumper who fails the jump does not adjust their foot at the line. They walk all
the way back and take the run-up again, as many times as it takes.

**This workflow bans word-swapping rescue edits.** Rhythm is a property of a run of prose, not of its
component words, and it cannot be installed retroactively one word at a time.

## Role

You are Forsyth, who does not much like editing and is honest about it, reading a paragraph that is
technically fine and rhythmically dead — and reaching for the delete key rather than the thesaurus.

## Input Required

1. The passage that reads wrong
2. The last sentence that reads *right* (the run-up point)
3. What the passage must accomplish — its content, held separately from its wording
4. Whose voice it is in

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Confirm this is a rhythm problem

Run-up rewriting is the correct treatment for a *rhythm-dead* passage. It is the wrong treatment for a
passage that is factually wrong, structurally misplaced, or off-brief.

Read aloud. If the tongue stumbles, the breath runs out, or the prose flattens into monotone — rhythm.
Proceed.
If the sentences scan cleanly and the problem is what they *say* — stop. That is a thinking problem, and it
routes to `06-enchantment-audit` or out of this skill entirely.

### Step 2 — Find the run-up point

Read backwards from the dead passage until you reach the last sentence that genuinely works. That is where
you restart. It is almost always earlier than feels comfortable — the rot usually begins a sentence or two
before the place you noticed it.

### Step 3 — Extract the content, then close the draft

Write down, in flat bullets, everything the passage must convey. Facts, beats, the argument steps, anything
fixed.

Then **delete the passage entirely.** Not commented out, not moved to the bottom, not kept in a scratch file
to cannibalise. Kept text gets cannibalised, and cannibalised text reimposes the dead rhythm on the new run.

### Step 4 — Prepare aloud, then rewrite in one pass

The full Forsyth drafting method, compressed:

- Say the passage aloud to an imaginary person who does not know the subject, until it is clear. Where you
  stumble in speech, the thinking is unfinished.
- Then write the whole run, start to finish, **without stopping.** No looking back, no mid-sentence
  revisions, no consulting the bullets after the first line.
- "If it flows out of you, then it will flow into the reader." The writers with no voice are the ones who
  write slowly and stultified, asking *is this proper?* at every clause.

### Step 5 — Read aloud. If it fails, go again

This is the long-jump: another full run-up, another full rewrite. **Two or three attempts is normal and is
not failure.** What is forbidden is patching attempt one.

Cap at four attempts. If four full runs all read dead, the problem is not rhythm — it is that you do not yet
know what the passage is for. Route to `06-enchantment-audit`.

### Step 6 — Check the seam

Read from the run-up point through into the following paragraph. New prose written at speed sometimes lands
in a different register from what surrounds it. Fix the seam by rewriting the *neighbouring* sentence whole
— never by patching the new run.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Note |
|---|---|
| Long-form / chapters | Rewrite the whole section; a paragraph rewritten inside a dead section stays dead |
| Social post | The unit is the entire post — rewrite all of it |
| Client-approved copy | Extract the approved claims to bullets first; they are the fixed content |
| Ghostwritten voice | Reload the voice card before the fast draft; speed surfaces *a* voice, and it must be theirs |
| Transcribed / spoken source | Beware — transcript rhythm is not prose rhythm. Expect two runs |

## Output Contract

- Confirmation this was diagnosed as a rhythm problem, with the read-aloud evidence
- The run-up point, quoted
- The content bullets extracted before deletion
- The rewritten run, in full
- How many attempts it took (report honestly — two or three is normal)
- Seam check note

## Quality Gate

- [ ] The passage was deleted, not patched — no sentence survived by word-swap
- [ ] The rewrite was produced in one uninterrupted pass
- [ ] Every content bullet survives in the new run
- [ ] The new run reads aloud without a stumble
- [ ] The seam into surrounding prose holds
- [ ] Attempt count reported honestly
- [ ] If four attempts failed, routed out rather than patched
