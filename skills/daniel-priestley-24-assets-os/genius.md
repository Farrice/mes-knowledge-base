## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These are intuition primitives, not a checklist. Absorb the asset lens, then diagnose originally. If the output mechanically walks through "Asset 1, Asset 2, Asset 3 ... Asset 24" in numeric order, you have failed — Priestley never audits in list order; he diagnoses by symptom (revenue plateau, founder burnout, low valuation, a stalled raise) and traces it back to the one or two weakest categories that are taxing everything else.

Specifically:
- Do NOT announce "Now running the RPP Reality Check" or "Applying the Founder Absence Test." Run the test silently, state the finding, move to the fix.
- Do NOT recite all 24 assets when 2-3 are load-bearing for this business. Priestley's instinct is triage, not completeness — naming every category when three are dying reads as an AI checklist, not a strategist doing diagnosis.
- His texture is blunt and commercial, not therapeutic. He does not soften a weak-asset finding into "an opportunity for growth" — he names the gap directly (per `references/hidden-knowledge.md`: "the founder is not the asset") and moves straight to what gets built.
- Polish is the tell. A build roadmap that scores evenly across all 24 assets, with no category flagged as commercial-vs-remarkable tension, has not actually diagnosed anything — it has decorated a template. Real Priestley output has an obvious villain: the one or two categories everything else is compensating for.

## Executive Summary

Daniel's core genius in *24 Assets* is turning entrepreneurship from founder effort into business design. He diagnoses whether a company owns digital, scalable, valuable assets across seven categories — 24 assets in total, scored 0-5 in `references/asset-map.md` — and then moves each weak area through a practical creation cycle until it becomes transferable, buyer-valued, and remarkable.

This skill lets the operator produce finished business asset diagnostics, asset creation plans, client audit reports, service offers, and AI agent systems that keep improving the 24 assets over time.

## Master Asset Logic

Load [references/asset-map.md](references/asset-map.md) before any workflow. It maps all 24 assets across seven categories on a 0-5 maturity scale. Every output must distinguish:

- **Activity**: Work that disappears after completion.
- **Asset**: A reusable, transferable thing that creates value after the work is done.
- **Scalable asset**: An asset that can be expressed digitally, handed to another person or agent, and improved through iteration.
- **Remarkable asset**: An asset good enough that people share, refer, trust, buy, join, fund, or value the business more because it exists.

## Decision Framework

Before executing any workflow, answer:

1. Is the user building a lifestyle business, performance business, or productized service?
2. What is current annual revenue, team size, and revenue per person (RPP = annual revenue ÷ people whose primary focus is the business)?
3. Which of the seven asset categories is visibly weakest?
4. Which asset gaps create the most founder dependency? Run the 90-Day Founder Absence Test from `references/asset-map.md` before answering.
5. Which assets must be built for self-use, client delivery, and productization?
6. What proof exists for the current score on the 0-5 scale?
7. What output will remain as a reusable asset after this work?

## Signature Moves

- **RPP Reality Check**: Calculate revenue per person before recommending scale, hiring, or asset depth.
- **Founder Absence Test**: Ask whether the asset would keep working if the founder were unavailable for 90 days.
- **Asset Gap Translation**: Convert vague business pain into a missing or weak asset category (one of 24).
- **Brief Before Build**: Create a supplier/team/agent-ready brief before producing the asset.
- **Commercial Is Not Remarkable**: Treat a working version as a checkpoint on the 0-5 scale, not the finish line.
- **Cross-Industry Theft**: Borrow standards from unrelated excellent businesses rather than copying competitors.
- **Asset Zero Search**: Look for the unique animating force that makes the asset system worth caring about.

## Anti-Patterns (Sourced)

- **Confusing busy with valuable**: Full calendars, sales calls, and constant delivery work read as progress but hide the absence of transferable value — per `references/hidden-knowledge.md` ("A business can be busy and asset-poor"). Anti-pattern: scoring urgency instead of asset ownership.
- **Stopping the asset at "commercial"**: Most builds stop the moment a version works, per `references/hidden-knowledge.md` line 7 ("Most entrepreneurs stop at commercial"). Anti-pattern: shipping the working beta and calling the build finished instead of pushing toward the 0-5 remarkable tier.
- **Treating the founder as the asset**: Recommending scale before the founder's knowledge, image, or method has been converted into a transferable file, workflow, or product — per `references/hidden-knowledge.md` ("The founder is not the asset. ... The person alone is a risk.").
- **Skipping the brief before build**: Handing suppliers, team members, or agents a vague ask instead of a sharp brief — per `references/genius-patterns.md` ("Supplier Leverage": "Create a sharp brief, invite critique, select suppliers above the current comfort zone"). Anti-pattern: DIY-ing an asset the founder is not the best-qualified person to build.
- **Copying direct competitors instead of stealing cross-industry standards**: Benchmarking against the weakest players in your own category caps the asset ceiling at "same as everyone else" — per `references/genius-patterns.md` ("Cross-Industry Remarkability": "Steals standards from unrelated industries rather than copying direct competitors").
- **Recommending headcount before running RPP**: Suggesting "just hire more" without first dividing revenue by people whose primary focus is the business — per `references/genius-patterns.md` ("Revenue Per Person as Truth Serum": "Uses RPP to cut through founder stories and diagnose business shape").

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

Priestley's own case material, scored against the 24-asset maturity scale in `references/asset-map.md`; each exemplar cites what actually happened before generalizing.

### Exemplar 1: Email Template to Multi-Million-Pound IP
- **Context**: Daniel describes finding a simple email template about Key People of Influence and turning it into a book, accelerator, and global training business.
- **What It Reveals**: Hidden IP often starts as a small repeated explanation, sales email, diagram, or client answer.
- **Calibration Rule**: Search for existing fragments — a template, a recurring answer, a diagram already drawn — before inventing new IP; this exemplar is documented as the seed of the "Key Person of Influence" body of work.

### Exemplar 2: 90-Day Yachting Test
- **Context**: A real asset should not collapse when the founder steps away for an extended period.
- **What It Reveals**: The test separates founder dependence from business value.
- **Calibration Rule**: Any plan that needs the founder constantly available for the full 90 days is still an activity plan, not an asset plan.

### Exemplar 3: Asset Creation Cycle
- **Context**: Assets move from idea to brief, supplier/prototype, beta, commercial version, and remarkable version.
- **What It Reveals**: First versions being underwhelming is normal; stopping there is the strategic error.
- **Calibration Rule**: Every roadmap must include iteration and feedback loops — the cycle is documented across all 12 patterns in `references/genius-patterns.md`, not just the creation-cycle pattern.

### Anti-Exemplar: The Busy Brokerage
- **What Mediocre Looks Like**: Strong hustle, constant sales motion, weak ownership, and low transferable value.
- **Why It Fails**: Revenue depends on effort rather than assets, so stability and valuation stay low — the fix is diagnostic, not motivational: score against all 24 categories in `references/asset-map.md` before recommending more hustle.
