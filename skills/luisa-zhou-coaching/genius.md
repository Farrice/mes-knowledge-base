# Luisa Zhou — Genius Context

## Genius Patterns

### Pattern: Coach-Sulting (Guide AND Tell)
The coaching world changed and nobody talks about it: clients who only get questions think "just tell me what works" — pure Socratic coaching now reads as a scam. Coach-sulting blends traditional coaching (guiding clients to their own answers) with consulting (using your experience to tell them the things they don't need to re-figure-out on their own). "Why not teach someone how to fish and give them dinner, too?" This is why her five-figure private coaching stayed fully booked for years.
**Execute**: For every client problem, split it in two: (a) decisions where the client's own answer builds their capability — coach those with questions; (b) solved problems where trial-and-error is pure waste — consult those with direct prescriptions ("this is exactly what you should do"). Say the prescription plainly, then explain why, so the client learns the reasoning too.
**Success Metric**: Clients get faster results because they skip trial and error, AND leave able to succeed without you. Client feedback sounds like: "I love that you tell me what to actually do and you help me figure out things for myself."

### Pattern: The Welcome Questionnaire Secret Weapon
The first call is made or broken before Zoom opens. Every new client completes a welcome questionnaire (a simple Google Form — the tech doesn't matter, the questions do) at least two business days before the first call, giving you time to review answers and map the entire client journey. Favorite questions: goals for coaching at 1/3/6 months; current weaknesses; belief stories learned from parents (e.g., money stories); "What do you want from me specifically as your coach?"; "What coaching style do you respond to best?"; biggest anticipated obstacles; biggest strengths and weaknesses related to the goal.
**Execute**: Derive the questions from one filter — "What do I need to know to help my client achieve their goals?" Include the coaching-style questions non-negotiably (she learned this after a client said "you are so mean" — clients don't all share your learning style). Read the answers for meta-signals: self-awareness, confidence level, humility/openness to guidance.
**Success Metric**: Questionnaire returned 2+ business days pre-call; you walk into call one with a mapped journey and 2-3 insights the client hasn't seen themselves. Client reports getting more value from one call than from previous coaching.

### Pattern: The Three S's First Session
A nervousness-proof structure for the first call (she invented it because she was terrified of forgetting something on hers). **Share**: your big takeaways from the questionnaire — dig deeper, ask clarifying questions, deliver an immediate insight ("I'm noticing you're not getting enough protein — we're adding that in right now"). **Strategy**: a tentative, results-oriented, high-level phase map ("month one, sugar cleanse; month two, intuitive eating; month three, workouts") — explicitly labeled tentative because everyone moves at their own pace. **Specific actions**: what they do between now and the next call, with actual dates on everything.
**Execute**: Run the S's in order. Throughout, share why each step matters and pre-empt the fears you saw in the questionnaire (put yourself in their shoes: excited, nervous, just invested money on faith). Never end without action items.
**Success Metric**: Client's face "lights up" at the Share step (proof you prepared); client leaves knowing the full arc and holding dated action items; the little voice asking "is this really going to work for me?" is answered in call one.

### Pattern: Teach Clients to Be Coachable
Coaching is a partnership — and clients must be taught that from day one, or they'll expect done-for-you service. She learned this the hard way with a client who wanted everything done for her: "I am not here to do things for you. I'm here to teach you how to fish." That conversation preceded the client building a multi-six-figure business she still runs.
**Execute**: In the first session, state the partnership explicitly: the client's job is to ask good questions, use your support strategically, and lead themselves while leveraging your expertise. "Help them to help you help them." When a client slides into delegation mode, name it directly and reset the frame.
**Success Metric**: Clients arrive at calls with thought-through questions rather than raw dependency; clients outgrow you ("great coaches create clients who outgrow them").

### Pattern: Boundaries That Improve Client Results
New coaches promise 24/7 availability out of fear and burn out; worse, it teaches clients not to think for themselves. Boundaries are a deliverable, not a defense: define when you check support messages, your response time, and which channel to use. When she started checking messages once a day, clients thought through their questions more, asked more strategically, and pulled better answers from her.
**Execute**: In onboarding, state the three logistics explicitly: channel, check frequency, response time. Frame it as "I've got your back, AND here's what to expect." Hold the line through the initial discomfort.
**Success Metric**: No coach burnout; measurably sharper client questions; client feedback like "your boundaries make me feel like you've got my back while showing me it's possible to have peace of mind in my business too."

### Pattern: Experience Is the Qualification
The credential question ("am I even qualified?") is the #1 launch blocker and almost always a phantom. Her first coaching offer was Microsoft Excel help — zero certification, but she was the person everyone at work came to for formulas and macros. Rare licensed industries aside (and "if you're in one, you already know"), what qualifies you is experience helping people get a specific result. The routing question: "What experience do you have that you can leverage to help others get a specific result?"
**Execute**: Inventory three sources: current job skills, personal obstacles overcome, and skills picked up along the way. Watch for the tell: "my friends ask me about X all the time." For abstract offers (confidence, happiness), force tangibility: "What does someone's life actually look like after working with you? What are the tangible day-to-day changes?"
**Success Metric**: A named coaching offer tied to a concrete, observable result — without a single certification purchased or months lost to credential anxiety.

### Pattern: Pick a Start, Not a Forever
She wasted two years on a gray sofa trying to invent "the next Facebook." The perfect coaching offer is already staring you in the face — coaching results are straightforward (find a relationship, lose 20 lbs, land higher pay). She started with Excel not out of passion but because it was the easiest place to start where she knew she could deliver results. "Taking imperfect action beats perfect planning every single time."
**Execute**: Choose the offer that scores highest on (ease of start × confidence you can deliver the result), not passion or novelty. Explicitly tell yourself: this is a starting point, not a life sentence. Launch within 24 hours of deciding.
**Success Metric**: Offer chosen in one sitting; first outreach message sent within 24 hours; zero months spent hunting a never-done-before idea.

### Pattern: The 3-Month / $1,500 Starter Container
She tested every program length from 2 weeks to a year: 3 months is the sweet spot — long enough for real results, short enough to not overwhelm either party. Structure by reverse-engineering: "What can I actually help someone achieve in 3 months?" then "What must happen in months 1, 2, 3 to get there?" Delivery = weekly calls + one day-to-day support channel (Voxer/email/WhatsApp). Price = $1,500 flat for new coaches: after her $5,000 first client came 30 straight rejections ("you're too expensive"); $1,500 was "the lowest price I'd offer without resenting my clients" — and her next three clients closed immediately.
**Execute**: Define the 3-month result → set monthly milestones by reverse-engineering → weekly calls + one async channel → price at $1,500 (higher only if you can genuinely carry it). Frame: you're optimizing for learning, not profit; raise after the first few clients when you actually know your value.
**Success Metric**: Package defined in under a day; price never becomes the sales bottleneck; price raised from evidence (client results) rather than formula anxiety.

### Pattern: The Free Taster Session
Adapted from Rich Litvin's *The Prosperous Coach* (2-hour free sessions felt too long, so she compressed it). A 30-60 minute free session laser-focused on ONE takeaway that is both genuinely valuable and demonstrates expertise. Open with the frame: "On this call we're going to make sure you walk away with this takeaway I promised. At the end, if you found it helpful, I'd also love to share how I can help you further through my coaching. Does that sound good?" Expectations set, no bait-and-switch. Her first $5,000 client came from freely answering a stranger's ad questions until the client wrote: "You have helped me so much for free. How can I hire you?"
**Execute**: Pick one high-value takeaway per prospect. State the frame in the first minute. Deliver the takeaway completely (generosity is the pitch). Close by offering the next step; if they don't buy, ask for a testimonial on the session itself.
**Success Metric**: Prospect walks away with a usable result whether or not they buy; conversion happens from demonstrated value, not persuasion; every session yields either a client or a testimonial.

### Pattern: Systematize Knowledge in Plain Docs
Focus on content, not packaging: she still uses Google Docs for coaching materials a decade in. Create resources as needed before each call (food lists, go-to recipes, templates), never stressing about polish — then reuse them for every future client. The Google Docs from her very first clients became the material for a $3,000 group program.
**Execute**: Before each client call, ask "what resource would make this repeatable?" and draft it in a plain doc. File everything. Periodically scan the doc library for group-program / course raw material. Never let tooling or design delay a client deliverable.
**Success Metric**: Every resource created once, used many times; a leveraged product (group program/course) eventually assembled from documents that already exist.

### Pattern: The Rapid Content Engine (Take Inspiration, Ship on a Timer)
Content creation is a skill, not a talent — she went from 3 hours per newsletter to publishing 3 pieces every weekday. The system: (1) don't wait for inspiration, take it — end each day jotting story-worthy moments into a spreadsheet ("if I had to tell a 5-minute story from today, what's the moment?"), building a story bank; (2) one idea → many pieces (email angle → short → long-form video), each retelling improving the communication; (3) one piece = ONE takeaway, decided before creating; (4) never start from scratch — Hook (stop the scroll) → Meat (tips, one story, or one example that delivers the takeaway) → CTA; (5) fixed time and place daily, ideally first thing (Stephen King's 2,000 words, Dan Kennedy's first hour); (6) hard deadline — 45-minute timer, then it ships ("good enough is good enough"); (7) draft out loud as if talking to your best friend, so it sounds like you on the first try.
**Execute**: Install the nightly story-bank habit first. Then set the daily slot, the 45-minute timer (with a 5-minute warning), and the Hook/Meat/CTA scaffold. When done, immediately list 2-3 other formats the same idea can become.
**Success Metric**: Daily publishing sustained without heroics; per-piece creation time trending down (45 → 30 minutes); story bank never empty when you sit down to create.

## Hidden Knowledge

### Insight: Preparation Is the Charisma
**Insight**: What clients experience as "an amazing coach" in call one is almost entirely pre-work they never see — the questionnaire review and journey mapping. "Being a great coach is not about knowing all the information in the world. It's about knowing how to customize what you know for your client." Most coaches with 10x her competitors' knowledge skip this and feel generic; she's coached 10+ years and says you'd be amazed how many never bother.
**Deploy**: Budget real calendar time between questionnaire receipt and call one (that's WHY the 2-business-day rule exists). The deliverable of preparation is customization: every insight delivered on the call should be about THIS client's answers, never generic.

### Insight: Rejection Volume Is Tuition, and Price Is the Dial
**Insight**: Her arc — $5,000 first client (lucky, hour-rate formula), then 30 consecutive rejections, then instant traction at $1,500 — encodes two lessons: (1) rejection desensitization is a real asset ("sales only feel scary until after you've done it… another day, another no"); (2) when rejections cluster around "too expensive," the fix is a price you can offer without resentment, not better persuasion.
**Deploy**: Pre-commit to a rejection budget before launch so the first no's are expected data, not verdicts. If you hit a rejection streak with a consistent objection, change the offer variable being objected to; "lowest price I wouldn't resent" is the floor.

### Insight: Productive Procrastination Is the Real Enemy
**Insight**: The scariest step (selling) gets displaced into work that feels productive but lands no clients — website tweaks, design polish, perfect-idea hunting. "Until you make that first sale, you do not have a business." Same disease in content: waiting for the muse, or polishing one piece for hours.
**Deploy**: Impose the 24-hour launch clock: idea identified, package priced, and ONE outreach message sent to a real person within 24 hours. Website/branding are explicitly deferred until after paying clients exist. Any task that doesn't put you in front of a potential client is post-revenue work.

### Insight: The Abstract-Offer Tangibility Test
**Insight**: Coaches with intangible offers (confidence, happiness, mindset) stall because they can't state a result. The unlock question: "What does someone's life actually look like after working with you — what are the tangible changes in their day-to-day?" (e.g., "confident" becomes "speaks up in work meetings").
**Deploy**: Run every offer, package milestone, and piece of content through this test before shipping. If you can't name the observable day-to-day change, the offer isn't ready to sell and the content has no takeaway.

### Insight: Client Docs Are a Compounding Asset Class
**Insight**: The hidden economics of one-on-one coaching: every resource made for one client is R&D for the leveraged business. "It all started with just helping one client at a time and documenting what worked" — her first clients' Google Docs literally became a $3,000 group program. This reframes low-leverage early clients as product development you're being paid for.
**Deploy**: Treat documentation as part of delivery, not overhead. After each client engagement, tag which docs were reused; the most-reused documents are the curriculum of your future group program or course.
