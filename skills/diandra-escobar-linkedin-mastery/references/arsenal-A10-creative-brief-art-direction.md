## 📊 EXAMPLE OUTPUT 1: Profile visual system + carousel brief — "The Anti-Dashboard" consultant (ties to A1/A5)

**Context:** The analytics consultant now positioned as "The Anti-Dashboard Consultant" (A1) with the "Pipeline Mirage"-style lexicon. ICP: revenue leaders. Needs a visual system that says "clarity, not clutter."

**THE ACTUAL DELIVERABLE:**

**Mood-Board Direction:** *Mood:* calm, decluttered, confident — the visual opposite of a busy BI dashboard. *References:* clean editorial layouts, lots of white space, one bold accent. *Palette:* off-white base, charcoal text, one signature **electric-coral** accent (deliberately *not* the corporate-blue every analytics brand uses — instant feed recognizability). *Typography:* a clean grotesk for headers, high legibility.

**Profile Art Direction:**
- *Photo:* zoomed to face, warm genuine smile, off-white/coral-tinted background, approachable not corporate.
- *Banner concept:* a near-empty frame with three clean numbers highlighted in coral and 37 greyed-out dashboards faded behind them. On-image copy: **"Kill the 37 reports nobody reads. Keep the 3 that change Monday."**
- *Color system:* coral = the signal you keep; grey = the noise you kill (the palette *is* the message).

**Carousel Brief (6 slides, Authority):** *Objective:* prove the anti-dashboard method. *Visual hook (slide 1):* a wall of tiny grey charts with one coral number huge in the center — "39 dashboards. 1 decision." *Slides 2–6:* one idea per slide, massive type, coral accent, radical white space. *Must-haves:* every slide passes the 3-second scroll test; the coral signals the "kept" metric throughout.

**Result + formula:** The coral-in-a-blue-niche choice made his content *recognizable* mid-scroll before his name registered; the palette-as-message banner passed the 10-second gut check instantly. Paired with the A1 positioning, profile-to-DM conversion rose because the *look* confirmed the *claim.* **Formula:** `pull the position → pick a signature color the niche doesn't own → make the palette itself carry the message (kept vs killed) → engineer slide-1 as a visual hook → codify into a playbook.`

## 📊 EXAMPLE OUTPUT 2: Video thumbnail + visual playbook — B2B founder creator (with metrics)

**Context:** Founder posting B2B video content, ICP = other founders. Videos underperform because thumbnails are generic screenshots.

**THE ACTUAL DELIVERABLE:**

**Mood-Board Direction:** *Mood:* bold, high-contrast, human. *Palette:* deep navy base, high-contrast white, one signature **acid-yellow** accent for the eye-catch. *Typography:* heavy condensed sans for 3-word thumbnail hooks.

**Thumbnail Brief (visual hook engineering):**
- *Rule:* max 3 words on a thumbnail; the founder's face (reacting, not posing) takes 40% of frame; acid-yellow highlights the one key word.
- *Example:* face mid-"are you serious" expression + text **"FIRE THE SDRs?"** with FIRE in acid-yellow.
- *Visual hook logic:* the thumbnail is the video's 50-word audition — it must create a curiosity gap in one glance.

**Creative Brief (objective/audience/message/specs):** *Objective:* stop the scroll and earn the play. *Audience:* founders. *Message:* contrarian B2B takes. *Must-haves:* face + 3 words + one acid-yellow word + navy field. *Specs:* 1280×720, text legible at thumbnail size, safe margins.

**Visual Playbook (do/don't):** *Do:* real facial reactions, 3-word max, one accent word, consistent navy/yellow. *Don't:* stock photos, full sentences, more than one accent color, generic screenshots.

**Result + formula:** Standardizing thumbnails on the face + 3-word + acid-yellow system (recognizable + curiosity-gapped) lifted play-through because the visual hook did the same job as a copy hook. **Realistic pattern: consistent, hook-engineered thumbnails materially raise click/play rate vs generic screenshots, and the signature color builds feed recognizability over time.** **Formula:** `treat the thumbnail as a visual hook → face reaction + 3-word curiosity gap + one signature accent → codify as a playbook so every video compounds the same recognizable system.`
