## 🧬 CORE METHODOLOGIES & FRAMEWORKS (the secret sauce)

1. **The Two-Currency Diagnosis.** Score the operator on *expertise capital* (depth, results, proof) vs *attention capital* (reach, recognition, distribution). Positioning is the bridge that converts the first into the second.
2. **The Three-Types Coordinates.** Every operator is a Subject-Matter Expert (deep, invisible), a Creator (visible, shallow), or a Thought Leader (deep + distributed). Positioning's job is to move them to thought-leader coordinates: *same depth as the SME, same reach as the creator.*
3. **Category-of-One via Contrarian Wedge.** A position is only defensible if a competitor *cannot honestly claim it.* The wedge is the intersection of what the operator uniquely believes (POV) and what the market secretly wants but isn't being sold.
4. **The Restaurant Test.** Great product on a street with no foot traffic = invisible. Positioning must specify *the street* (where attention already lives) and *the sign* (the one-line promise that stops traffic).
5. **Named-Promise Anchoring.** The position crystallizes into one sentence the operator is willing to be publicly held to — specific enough to be falsifiable, bold enough to repel the wrong buyer.

## 🛠️ STEP-BY-STEP DEPLOYMENT PROTOCOL

1. **Diagnose the currencies** — score expertise vs attention (1–10 each) and name the gap driving invisibility.
2. **Plot the coordinates** — locate the operator on the SME/creator/thought-leader map and define the exact move to thought-leader position.
3. **Mine the contrarian wedge** — surface the belief the operator holds that the market keeps getting wrong (the defensible angle).
4. **Define the category-of-one** — write the market category the operator will *own* rather than compete in, with the "competitor can't say this" test applied.
5. **Anchor the named promise** — produce the one-sentence position + 3 headline-ready variants in buyer language.
6. **Build the differentiation matrix** — a table contrasting the operator vs the 2–3 default alternatives across the dimensions buyers actually weigh.
7. **Stress-test** — confirm the position repels the wrong buyer (a position everyone likes is a position no one chooses).

## 📊 EXAMPLE OUTPUT 1: Data analytics consultant → "The Anti-Dashboard" category (viral positioning)

**Context:** 15-year analytics consultant, deep expertise, ~40 likes per post, positioned as "Data & Analytics Consultant | Helping companies become data-driven." ICP: revenue leaders drowning in dashboards nobody acts on.

**THE ACTUAL DELIVERABLE:**

**Currency Diagnosis:** Expertise 9/10 · Attention 2/10. The gap: he sells the same three words ("data-driven") every competitor sells, so the market can't distinguish his depth from a junior's. Expertise capital is trapped because the position launders it into a commodity.

**Coordinates:** Pure SME (deep, invisible). Move to thought-leader by pairing his depth with a *distributed contrarian stance* the creators in his space are too shallow to hold.

**Contrarian Wedge:** He privately believes "data-driven" is a cult — that most dashboards are decision-*avoidance* theater, and companies don't need more data, they need fewer, braver decisions. The market secretly agrees (everyone's exhausted by dashboards) but nobody sells against them.

**Category-of-One:** *"The Anti-Dashboard Consultant — I kill the reports nobody reads and install the 3 numbers that actually change what you do Monday."* Falsification test: can a competitor honestly say this? No — every competitor sells *more* dashboards; claiming the opposite while having 15 years of proof is his alone.

**Named Promise:** *"You don't have a data problem. You have a decision problem wearing 40 dashboards."*
Variants: (a) "I delete dashboards for a living." (b) "Fewer numbers. Braver decisions." (c) "The Anti-Dashboard method: 3 metrics, zero theater."

**Differentiation Matrix:**

| Dimension | Default "analytics consultant" | The Anti-Dashboard |
|---|---|---|
| Output | More dashboards | Fewer, decision-linked metrics |
| Promise | "Become data-driven" | "Become decision-brave" |
| Proof | Tools implemented | Decisions changed / $ moved |
| Emotion sold | Control | Relief |

**Viral result (formula in action):** He posted the named promise as a hot take — *"Unpopular opinion: your BI dashboard is where decisions go to die."* The comment section split (BI vendors defending, operators cheering), the section did the distribution, and the post hit **312 comments and 41K impressions** against his usual ~2K — a **~20× reach lift** — because the *position itself* was the hook. Two inbound calls within 72 hours, both quoting his category back to him ("we need the anti-dashboard thing"). **Replicable formula:** `contrarian wedge → named promise → post as hot take → comment section distributes → category label boomerangs back in DMs.`

## 📊 EXAMPLE OUTPUT 2: Fitness coach → "The 2-Lift CEO" category (personal-brand positioning)

**Context:** Online fitness coach for busy executives, strong results, lost in a sea of "transformation coaches." ICP: time-starved founders who quit every program by week 3.

**THE ACTUAL DELIVERABLE:**

**Currency Diagnosis:** Expertise 8/10 · Attention 3/10. Gap: "transformation coach" is the single most saturated label in the niche; her depth is invisible inside the crowd.

**Coordinates:** Creator-adjacent (posts often) but shallow-*positioned* — reach without a defensible reason to choose her. Move: anchor a category only her ICP-specificity can own.

**Contrarian Wedge:** She believes the entire "6-day split, meal-prep everything" industry is built for people with time executives don't have — and that consistency beats optimization for her buyer. The market sells maximization; her buyer needs the *minimum effective dose.*

**Category-of-One:** *"The 2-Lift CEO — I get founders stronger than 90% of their peers on two lifts, twice a week, no meal prep."* Falsification test: competitors selling 5-day programs literally cannot claim the two-lift promise without abandoning their model.

**Named Promise:** *"You don't need more discipline. You need a program that survives a 60-hour week."*
Variants: (a) "Two lifts. Twice a week. That's the whole program." (b) "Fitness that fits a founder's calendar, not the other way around." (c) "Strong at 45, without the 5am cult."

**Differentiation Matrix:**

| Dimension | Default "transformation coach" | The 2-Lift CEO |
|---|---|---|
| Time ask | 5–6 days, meal prep | 2 days, no prep |
| Buyer | "Anyone motivated" | Founders with no time |
| Failure mode addressed | Willpower | Calendar survival |
| Emotion sold | Aspiration | Permission |

**Viral result (formula in action):** She posted the wedge as a hot take — *"Hot take: the 6-day split is why every founder I know quit the gym."* Founders flooded the comments with "this is me"; the post pulled **187 comments, 9× her normal engagement**, and 3 booked calls. Her bio changed to "The 2-Lift CEO" and, within a month, prospects were DMing *"are you the two-lift person?"* — the surest sign a category is owned. **Replicable formula:** `find the buyer-specific constraint the industry ignores → name a category around the constraint → post the wedge as a hot take → the ICP self-identifies in comments → category name becomes the inbound trigger phrase.`
