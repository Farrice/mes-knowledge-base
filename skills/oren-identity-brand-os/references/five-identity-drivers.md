# The Five Identity Drivers — Working Grid

The five consumer-side motives for attaching identity to a brand [12:35–14:31 of gtrkRnsGZWY]. **Never called "archetypes"** (that term is owned by `oren-brand-archetypes`' five creator content roles: Oracle / Performer / World Builder / Catalyst / Helper — a separate framework). These are motive drivers, **not demographic segments**: one person flips between them per purchase. Diagnose a **primary plus a secondary** per segment, never a pile.

Oren's own summary line [14:20]: *"Fitting in, being better than somebody, not playing by anybody's rules, standing out, or having pride in a subculture."* His hedge on naming: he calls them "five archetypes, for lack of a better term" — we rename to the Five Identity Drivers per the binding disambiguation.

---

## 1. Belonging

- **Verbatim anchor** [12:41]: *"I want to fit in with people like me. This is sneakers."*
- **Example brands (verified spellings):** sneakers / Jordans, Stanley, Owala, Represent (24/7 sets), Outdoor Voices.
- **Diagnostic questions:** Who does the buyer want to be seen as one of? What in-group does owning this admit them to? Would a stranger who owns the same thing recognize them as "one of us"?
- **Content / creative implications:** homie-lookbook casting (people who look like the buyer's friends); in-group codes deployed casually; the product shown as a recognition token in shared settings, not isolated on white.
- **Failure mode:** casting polished, unreachable models re-introduces distance and kills the belonging signal; or the product has no visible social surface, so the recognition can never fire.

## 2. Better-than-the-others

- **Verbatim anchor** [13:13]: *"Brands like The Row that position themselves entirely — I'm more tasteful than everyone else in my friend group... a bag that not everyone would know."*
- **Example brands (verified spellings):** The Row (founded 2006 by Mary-Kate and Ashley Olsen; logoless leather, anonymity as luxury); Maison Margiela (blanc-stitch / four-stitch recognition without a logo).
- **Diagnostic questions:** What does owning this signal that only a few peers would even decode? Is the status in what is present, or in what is deliberately missing? Would the wrong person fail to recognize it, and is that the point?
- **Content / creative implications:** subtlety over loudness; owned color and pattern over logo; insider references deployed, never explained; deliberately anti-viral, legible-to-peers-only presence.
- **Failure mode:** explaining the code (an outsider tell); reaching for a loud logo where the driver rewards quiet recognition.

## 3. Rebel

- **Verbatim anchor** [13:32]: *"I don't play by your rules... Which is Liquid Death to some regard."*
- **Example brands (verified spellings):** Liquid Death (canned water as heavy-metal counterculture in a mundane category).
- **Diagnostic questions:** What category convention does this brand refuse? Who is the establishment it positions against? Does owning it signal "I opted out of the default"?
- **Content / creative implications:** counterculture tone in an ordinary category; the enemy named clearly; irreverence as the through-line, consistent enough to be a moat.
- **Failure mode:** manufactured edginess with no real convention broken; rebellion as costume rather than position.

## 4. Standout / flare

- **Verbatim anchor** [13:47]: *"The I'm-special call... I own that jacket cuz I know I can go on a podcast and it'll stand out on a feed."*
- **Example brands (verified spellings):** BAD SON (independent Utah label, founded 2020 by Hieu Tran and Conner Lobato; reworked / distressed Carhartt outerwear — the "flare jacket that stands out on a podcast/feed"). Rick Owens (the transcript rendered "Grant Owens"; almost certainly Rick Owens, avant-garde exaggerated-silhouette outerwear — label LIKELY, flag if cited). **Note:** BAD SON and Maison Margiela are two separate brands; the raised-stitch-you-recognize-without-a-logo mechanic belongs to Margiela (see driver 2), the stand-out-on-a-feed flare jacket belongs to BAD SON.
- **Diagnostic questions:** Does this make the owner visibly distinct in a feed or a room? Is the piece a conversation-starter by design? Would the owner choose it *because* it draws the eye?
- **Content / creative implications:** bold, feed-legible silhouette or color; the piece framed as the thing people ask about; designed for the podcast-thumbnail moment.
- **Failure mode:** loudness with no taste behind it reads as trying-too-hard; flare that does not survive a second look.

## 5. Subculture-pride

- **Verbatim anchor** [14:03]: *"If you know, you know. And you can base that around geography, around race, around hobbies, around gender."*
- **Example brands (verified spellings):** the International Stüssy Tribe (top-down, engineered subculture badge); Polo Ralph Lauren as adopted by the Lo Lifes (bottom-up, subculture built the identity itself — crew formed 1988, credited to Thirstin Howl the 3rd and Rack-Lo).
- **Diagnostic questions:** What specific in-group (place, hobby, heritage, scene) does this signal membership in? Is the pride legible only to insiders? Does the brand host or merely reference the subculture?
- **Content / creative implications:** "if you know, you know" register; artifacts and references only the subculture decodes; third-space hosting so the congregation reproduces the signal beyond one-to-one advertising.
- **Failure mode:** appropriating a subculture the brand has no authentic claim to (fails the mirror test); referencing the scene without hosting or belonging to it.

## Cross-driver rulings

- **Not segmentation.** Do not map drivers to demographics. A single buyer can be Belonging on sneakers and Better-than-the-others on a bag in the same week.
- **Primary plus secondary only.** Naming three or more for one segment is the failure mode Oren flags.
- **Identity beyond affiliation is a sixth, private lane** (not one of the five): some identity is internal, resolving a private weak point rather than signaling to others (Lift Foils). It runs alongside these five, gated by the mirror test. See `case-receipts.md`.
