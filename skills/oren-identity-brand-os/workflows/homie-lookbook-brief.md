# Homie Lookbook Brief

"Their lookbooks were what we now call the homie lookbook, where it's your friends and members of your community versus traditional models" [07:39]. But casting friends isn't enough on its own — the companion craft-level teardown sharpens it: "it's not just hey I'm going to use my friends in my photos versus employing a real model — it's more of, is there a story that we can tell underneath that" [companion 11:56–12:32]. This workflow builds the actual casting brief, filtered through that story-underneath test.

## Pre-Flight Gate

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: cite genius.md **Pattern 5 — The Homie Lookbook**. Do not proceed to a shot list until every proposed cast member has passed the story-underneath filter (below) — casting people purely because they "look right" recreates the distance the homie lookbook exists to eliminate, even if they're technically friends rather than agency models.

## Input Required

- The community/friend pool available for casting (real people, not agency talent)
- The product or campaign concept the shoot is built around
- Any existing recurring visual themes the brand already owns (see `signature-design-audit`)

## Execution Steps

**Step 1 — Long-List the Cast.** Pull from the brand's actual community — friends, collaborators, customers, not a casting agency. This is the raw pool before any filtering.

**Step 2 — Run the Story-Underneath Filter (companion source, ~11:56–12:32).** For every candidate, answer one question: what did this person actually do that's interesting? Not "do they look right" — a specific, tellable activity or trait. The clearest model is **Brigade NYC**: a garment designed *because of* a specific friend's specific interesting activity, with the lookbook built around that story [companion 11:21–13:07]. Cut anyone from the long list who only passes on looks.

**Step 3 — Physical Membership Token (optional but strong).** If budget allows, give cast members something that marks belonging the way Stussy's International Tribe received personalized varsity jackets with their own names [VERIFIED, genius.md Exemplar 1] — this converts a one-time shoot into an ongoing identity relationship, not just a photo credit.

**Step 4 — Shot Logic.** Real locations, outdoor/in-motion bias — Stussy's own imagery runs overwhelmingly outdoor and in-motion, not studio. Apply the recurring-theme toolkit where it fits: urban action-and-people (let the place or scene tell the story instead of the model posing), vehicle-as-frame (shoot through or around a car door or truck bed rather than centering the vehicle, for partial-reveal intrigue), or a non-serious recurring bit (mascot/surrealist beat) if the brand has one. Avoid: posed studio setups, direct-eye-contact catalog shots, anything that reads as agency casting.

**Step 5 — Production Notes for the UGC Look.** Favor real, uncontrolled light over studio lighting; keep motion and candid framing over posed symmetry; avoid over-retouching — the whole mechanism depends on the cast reading as real community members, not styled talent. If collaborators are involved (a guest artist, a co-branded partner), draw them from the partner's actual scene rather than a generic celebrity cameo, the way Stussy's Dries Van Noten collab drew from Flea's real world rather than a stock celebrity appearance [companion 10:46].

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Apparel lookbook | Full brief as written |
| Social / UGC campaign | Story-underneath filter still applies to who gets reposted or featured, not just who's shot |
| Event photography | Shot logic applies live — brief photographers to catch candid story moments, not posed group shots |
| Email / owned-media lookbook | Pair with Rhode's "showing a life" mechanism (genius.md Exemplar 3) — one profiled person per send, not a group shoot |

## Output Requirements

- Cast long-list with story-underneath answer per person (cut list noted, with reason)
- Physical-token recommendation (yes/no, and what)
- Shot-logic brief (location, motion bias, which recurring-theme device if any)
- Production notes for maintaining the UGC look
- Explicit flag if any collaborator/celebrity cameo was sourced from a real scene vs. a generic booking
