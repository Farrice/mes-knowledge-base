# Signature Design Audit

Dior didn't need a logo to sell luxury off a $50 lip gloss — a charm keychain did the job, "a luxury signaler from their logo on a low-cost purchase" [10:52]. Most brands reach for a logo first because it's the cheapest, loudest lever. The sophisticated ones reach for it last, after color and pattern have already done the work of making a stranger say "I'm going to get the green one" [11:26]. This workflow forces that order: menu first, logo last, then stress-tested against Oren's own craft-level art-direction audit so the signature isn't just visible, it's ownable.

## Pre-Flight Gate

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: cite genius.md **Pattern 7 — Signature-Design Recognizability Ladder** and **Signature Move 7 — Own-A-Color** before opening any design conversation. Do not let the brief default to "we need a logo" — that is the anti-pattern this workflow exists to intercept. If the brand cannot articulate what it wants to look like on a shelf or in a feed at a glance, stop and run this audit before any asset gets produced.

## Input Required

- Brand or product under audit (current visual assets if any)
- Category and demo maturity (young/loud vs. established/sophisticated)
- Any existing signature elements already in market

## Execution Steps

**Step 1 — Recognizability Menu Pass [10:42].** For each of the five levers — colorway, form factor, pattern, tag, iconography/logo — document current state and one candidate move. Do not skip to iconography; work the list in order.

**Step 2 — Color-Ownership Test [11:26].** Can this brand claim one shelf-clarity color the way Nerf owns orange, Zuru red, and Gel Blaster green claim theirs (individual color assignments are LIKELY, not independently verified — the mechanism is sound regardless)? The test passes when a stranger can say "I'll get the green one" without reading a label.

**Step 3 — Logo-vs-Subtlety Axis [12:20].** Plot the brand against category maturity and demo. Loud/young/easy-to-copy categories reward iconography and logo (White Fox, Scuffers, Nude Project). Sophisticated/durable categories reward owned color and pattern — Maison Margiela's logoless blanc-stitch recognition is the model. Note: BAD SON's standout flare jacket [13:47] is a separate Utah label (founded 2020, Hieu Tran & Conner Lobato) — never conflate it with Margiela; they demonstrate different ends of this same axis.

**Step 4 — Stussy 5-Question Art-Direction Audit** (companion source, ~19:32 runtime): (a) **Iconography** — what is it, can it be fabricated at scale, does it become archive-worthy over time [companion 16:06]; (b) **Logo worship** — would a real customer want this physically on their body, not just on a product [companion 04:49, 16:42]; (c) **Lifestyle-adjacency** — define the category one level wider than the product itself, the way Stussy claimed "action sport" instead of "streetwear" [companion 07:11]; (d) **Portrait philosophy** — how are the brand's people captured (hand off to `homie-lookbook-brief` for the full casting brief); (e) **Recurring themes** — name 2-3 non-serious visual bits the brand can return to (a mascot/surrealist beat, urban action-and-people, a vehicle-as-frame device) [companion 13:43–16:06].

**Step 5 — Physical-Signal Check [11:44].** Does the packaging or product surface itself earn UGC on its own — Oren's own consult advice was a matte-finish plus emboss/deboss pass that reportedly quadrupled customer photos. Treat the multiplier as illustrative, not a guarantee.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Physical product / apparel | Full menu as written; color-ownership test is primary |
| Packaging / CPG | Weight Step 5 heaviest; iconography becomes the unboxing moment |
| Digital product / app UI | Form factor → UI shape language; tag → in-app badge/mark; color-ownership still primary |
| Service brand / agency | Iconography and logo worship map to the name/wordmark; lifestyle-adjacency defines the category claim |
| Content/thumbnail brand | Pattern and colorway become the recurring frame treatment; portrait philosophy maps directly to on-camera casting |

## Output Requirements

- Five-lever recognizability table: current state + recommended move per lever
- Color-ownership verdict (pass/fail + the claimed color if pass)
- Sophistication-axis placement with one-line rationale
- Full 5-question audit answers
- One prioritized next move, not a list of five
