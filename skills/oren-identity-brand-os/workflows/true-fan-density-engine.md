# /true-fan-density-engine — True-Fan Density Engine

Build cult-following mechanics on purpose: tribe design, a real-world congregation, a peer-proximity proof loop, and an operating cadence that prices depth over reach. This is the net-new gap no existing Oren asset systematizes.

## Pre-Flight Gate (genius.md decision framework)

- Does a locked identity seed and a confirmed primary driver exist (from `identity-zero-point` and `identity-driver-diagnostic`)? Depth-over-reach only works when there's a real identity to concentrate around — running this on an undifferentiated brand just produces an expensive fan club for nothing distinct.
- **Labeling discipline, binding:** every case fact below carries its verification label from `enrichment-research.md`. VERIFIED facts may be asserted plainly; LIKELY facts get flagged; UNCONFIRMED names ("Vervont," the exact "$100/fan" figure as a guarantee) are never presented as fact.
- Farrice fast-path: Parallax (Substack) is already a direct-relationship channel — audit its direct-relationship percentage in Step 5 before adding any intermediary platform. Kevin Kelly math should run against the actual $200–300/wk near-term target from `FARRICE-MASTER-CONTEXT.md` §8, not an abstract $100K/year headline.

## Execution

1. **Control-fork decision.** Name which fork the founder needs, per **Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up Tribe Control** [07:07]: **top-down** (Stussy — surgical, engineer the tribe, choose chapters, cast the lookbook) when precision and brand control matter; **bottom-up** (Polo → Lo Life — VERIFIED bottom-up co-opting, formed 1988 by Ralphie's Kids and Polo USA/United Shoplifters Association) when identity is already spreading organically and imposing control would kill it. Trying to control a bottom-up movement kills it; trying to seed-and-release when precision is required dilutes the brand.

2. **Tribe design (top-down path).** Apply **[VERIFIED]** International Stüssy Tribe (IST) mechanics: hand-pick culturally-credible nodes — not the biggest names, the right-fit ones ("not necessarily the biggest, but the ones that would fit into their existing community"); issue a physical membership token (IST's personalized varsity/tour jackets are the real precedent); anchor chapters in specific places (IST's five: Los Angeles, New York, London, Tokyo, Paris — NYC crew dated 1987, London crew dated 1989). Translate the token and chapter concept to the founder's own category — it doesn't have to be a jacket, it has to be a physical or unmistakably-exclusive marker of membership.

3. **Congregation / third-space hosting.** Apply **Third-Space Replacement** [02:38]: name the specific congregation the founder's category has lost (a bar, a run club, an in-person peer community), then host its replacement. **[VERIFIED]** about:blank (London, founded 2021, Perry Davies & Jack Henderson) ran a real pop-up (Aug–Sep 2024) seeded by DJ/creative-community adoption of its signature tee; **[LIKELY]** the "house-music events as a branded program" framing — treat as community-seeded, not a documented event series. The failure mode is hosting an event where no congregation-hunger already exists.

4. **Homie-lookbook proof loop.** Cast real tribe members, not models, in every piece of proof content [07:39]. Apply the **"story underneath"** casting filter from the Stussy art-direction companion source: select people for what they've actually done or represent, not for how they look — a garment or feature designed around a specific member's specific story outperforms generic peer-casting.

5. **Depth-over-reach cadence.** Build the operating rhythm around direct-relationship depth, not audience size. **[VERIFIED]** Kevin Kelly's two load-bearing conditions (published *1,000 True Fans*, March 2008): (a) produce enough each year that a true fan can spend roughly $100 on you, (b) maintain a **direct** relationship so you keep the full amount rather than losing a cut to an intermediary. Audit the founder's current channels for which are direct (owned email/Substack/community) versus rented (algorithm-dependent reach).

6. **Kevin Kelly math, labeled.** State the math plainly and label it: 1,000 true fans × ~$100 profit/fan/year = $100,000/year. **[LIKELY — caveat]** modern critique (Dave Karpf) notes real per-fan yield is often lower after platform fees, and "buy anything" fandom is rarer than assumed — present the figure as an illustrative unit of the *principle* (depth of affiliation beats breadth of reach), never as a guaranteed outcome.

7. **Density scorecard.** Track true-fan count, direct-relationship percentage, and cadence depth (touches per true fan, not impressions) as the operating dashboard — vanity reach metrics are explicitly excluded.
