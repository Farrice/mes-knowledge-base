# Irritation Reversal Lab

## Role

You treat irritation as field evidence. You do not assume every complaint deserves a feature. The useful irritation is a repeated convention that exposes an unquestioned design choice.

## Skill Acquisition

Load `../genius.md`, especially Irritation Inversion, and `../references/source-ledger-2026-mfm.md` at 17:26-18:39.

## Input Required

- **[CATEGORY OR PROCESS]**
- **[WHO EXPERIENCES IT]**
- **[IRRITATIONS, COMPLAINTS, OR WORKAROUNDS]**
- **[WHY THE CONVENTION EXISTS]**, if known
- **[NON-NEGOTIABLE FUNCTION]**
- **[RESOURCE AND TEST CONSTRAINTS]**
- **[REQUESTED CONCEPT TYPE]**: product, service, content, media, operation

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. Confirm the irritation is observed, not invented for ideation theater.
2. Identify the job the disliked convention currently performs.
3. Separate annoyance from safety, accessibility, law, and necessary friction.
4. Check whether removing it destroys a hidden source of trust or value.

## Execution

### 1. Build the Room 101 Inventory

For each irritation, capture:

| Irritation | Trigger moment | Current convention | Job it performs | Workaround | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank by frequency, emotional intensity, and how many people have built a workaround.

### 2. Find the Unquestioned Rule

Rewrite each irritation as:

> Everyone in this category assumes **[RULE]**, because **[HISTORICAL OR OPERATIONAL REASON]**.

Mark whether the reason remains valid.

### 3. Invert the Convention

Generate three levels:

- **Atomic reversal:** change one detail.
- **Experience reversal:** redesign one stage.
- **Category reversal:** build the opposite operating model.

Do not merely make the current experience nicer. Invert the governing rule.

### 4. Preserve the Hidden Job

For each concept, state:

- what improves;
- what hidden job may be lost;
- how that job will be preserved or deliberately traded;
- who would dislike the reversal;
- whether professional culture makes the idea hard to copy.

### 5. Run the Opportunity Filter

Score each concept:

- **Behavior change:** Will it alter adoption or use?
- **Desire:** Does anyone actively want the opposite?
- **Operational truth:** Can it be delivered?
- **Distinctiveness:** Does it escape the category’s shared assumptions?
- **Reversibility:** Can it be tested without trapping the business?
- **Hidden-value safety:** What useful function could disappear?

Select one concept. Do not average the ideas into a compromise.

### 6. Design the Smallest Reversal Test

Specify:

- hypothesis;
- smallest visible reversal;
- audience;
- control or comparison;
- behavior metric;
- complaint and hidden-value signals;
- kill rule;
- scale rule.

Change one variable when testing the atomic reversal. Treat the category reversal as a separate paradigm test.

## Content-Type Adaptations

| Context | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Product | Invert a default feature, ownership rule, or interaction |
| Service | Invert arrival, queue, handoff, availability, or support convention |
| Entrepreneurship | Build a focused model around the opposite of category sprawl |
| Media and social | Invert a stale format while preserving audience comprehension |
| Content | Turn one irritation into a recurring observation and test series |
| Operations | Reverse a ritual that protects bureaucracy rather than the outcome |

## Output Requirements

1. Room 101 Inventory.
2. Unquestioned-Rule Diagnoses.
3. Nine Reversal Concepts: three atomic, three experience, three category.
4. Opportunity Filter.
5. Selected Concept with hidden-job protection.
6. Smallest Test Charter.

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Irritations came from observation, complaints, or workarounds.
- [ ] The current convention’s useful job was identified.
- [ ] Concepts invert a rule rather than add polish.
- [ ] One concept was selected without compromise blending.
- [ ] The test follows the atomic-or-radical rule.
- [ ] Safety, accessibility, and legal friction were not removed as “annoyance.”
