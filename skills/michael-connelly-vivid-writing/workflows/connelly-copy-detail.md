# Connelly Copy Detail — The Telling Detail Engine for Converting Copy (Michael Connelly)

A piece of converting copy already has its claim — the offer works, it saves the hour, it cuts the churn. What kills it is the way the claim is *delivered*: as a stacked list of abstractions. "Save time. Reduce stress. Scale faster." Three benefits, told flat, that the reader's skepticism slides right off — because nobody believes a benefit they're handed. Connelly's whole career is the fix. In fiction he never wrote "the detective was exhausted and stressed from decades of violence"; he wrote the groove worn into the earpiece of the glasses where the jaw clenched — *and that was all he needed to say about the guy.* The reader builds the exhaustion, the twenty years, the cost, from the one particular. In copy, the abstract-benefit-list is the exact equivalent of the descriptive paragraph Connelly subtracts: too much telling, no window for the reader's imagination, all speed bumps. This workflow ports the move. You take the abstraction, find the one concrete, *true* detail that — when shown — makes the reader infer the whole benefit set, and you let it carry the line. A benefit inferred is a benefit believed.

This is the **copy-specialized** variant of the telling-detail move — distinct from `telling-detail-engine.md` (fiction/general). Connelly supplies the *detail-selection* layer that drops inside copy-engine's or luke-iha's offer/proof architecture; he tunes *which particular survives*, he never manufactures the claim. The honesty spine is load-bearing here in a way it never is in fiction: a false detail that "feels real" is the single most dangerous output this engine can produce, because the better the craft, the more convincing the lie. So the spine is a hard Pre-Flight gate, and every surviving detail gets a per-detail real/substantiated confirmation before it ships.

The lesson that decides this whole engine: **high-load details are BEHAVIORS, not OBJECTS.** A prop is decoration; an observed behavior is a diagnostic event. Specifically confirm **Q1** (you are selecting ONE detail per benefit-cluster, not stacking — if two details are firing where one would carry the inference, the machinery is showing), **Q5** (you are *showing* the moment the benefit happens, not *stating* the benefit — "saves time / reduces stress / scales faster" is the copy form of "he felt X"), and **Q7 (the honesty spine, non-negotiable)**: every candidate detail must be a *real, substantiated* particular — drawn from the product's actual behavior, a real customer's real moment, or a documented spec. **The craft makes a true detail land harder; it must never manufacture a detail to fake authenticity.** If the only "telling detail" available is invented, STOP — the problem is a missing proof point, which is copy-engine / luke-iha's job (get a real mechanism or a real customer moment), not Connelly's. A telling detail amplifies a true claim; it cannot make a missing one exist.

## Input Required

- **The copy as it stands** — the actual VSL lead, fascination bullets, landing section, or email body. The real lines, not a summary — we're replacing specific abstractions with specific particulars.
- **The abstract benefit list** — the flat claims this copy is currently leaning on ("save time, reduce stress, scale faster," "powerful, intuitive, all-in-one"). These are the targets.
- **The real-world substance behind each claim** (the honesty spine source) — for each benefit, *what actually happens* that makes it true: the real product behavior, a real customer's real moment, a documented spec, a logged metric. **If you can't supply the real substance, the detail can't be written** — that's a proof gap to route back, not a detail to invent.
- **The format** — VSL lead / fascination bullet / landing proof / email body / social (drives how much runway the detail gets and whether it sits in a sentence or a bullet).
- **The buyer's lived moment** — the concrete situation the buyer is *in* when the benefit lands (the 2am desk, the Sunday-night dread, the inbox at 6:47am). The detail wants to live *inside* that moment, not float above it.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Map the abstraction-stack; mark which claims are the conversion turns (the AN-2 gate for copy)

A benefit list is the copy form of adjective stacking — "the cold, dark, lonely, rain-slicked alley" becomes "save time, reduce stress, scale faster, all in one place." Connelly's rule: max one. So first, find every place the copy *tells* a benefit instead of *showing* the moment that benefit happens, and mark which of those benefits the sale actually turns on. You don't replace every abstraction — you replace the load-bearing ones, and you let the rest go plain or get cut.

Read the copy and build the conversion-turn map. The abstract claim is the symptom; the buyer's moment is where the detail will live; the benefit is what the reader must *infer* (never be told).

| Conversion moment | Where it lives in the copy | The abstract claim (the symptom) | The benefit the reader must INFER (never told) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The lead / hook** | VSL open, landing headline, email subject+first line | the broad promise ("save 10 hours a week") | the one moment that *proves* the hours back — shown, not summed |
| **The mechanism beat** | where you explain *how* it works | "powerful / intuitive / automated" | the one concrete thing the product *does* that makes "intuitive" self-evident |
| **The proof / bullet block** | fascination bullets, results section | "scale faster / reduce churn" | the one customer moment or logged particular that *is* the result |
| **The desire pivot** | where logic meets longing | "less stress / peace of mind" | the one image of the buyer's life *after* — the Sunday with the phone in the drawer |

> **The AN-2 rule for copy:** if you removed an abstraction and replaced it with one true detail, and the line got *more* believable, the abstraction was decoration. Three benefits told flat train the reader to skim; one detail shown makes them *build*. Mark **at most one detail per benefit-cluster**. A VSL lead can carry two or three across its length; a single bullet carries *one*; an email body two at most. More details than conversion-turns = the abstraction-stack came back wearing a new coat.

### Step 2 — Generate candidate details for each load-bearing claim (mine the REAL, never invent)

For each abstraction you're replacing, generate three to five candidate concrete particulars — and **every candidate must be sourced from the real-world substance you supplied in Input** (real product behavior, a real customer's real moment, a documented spec). This is the honesty-spine step: you are *finding* details, never *fabricating* them. Connelly "endeavors to get the details right… plant the character's feet in as real a world as possible." In copy that means the detail's feet are planted in something you could verify.

Push past objects toward behaviors — the Evolution Log lesson is that behaviors out-score objects on narrative load, and the same holds in copy. "The dashboard has 40 integrations" is an object (a spec). "She closed the laptop at 5:15 for the first time in two years" is a behavior (a moment) — and it carries the spec's benefit *plus* the stakes, the relief, the before/after, the picture. Generate both, but know which family wins.

| Abstract claim | Candidate concrete details (all must be REAL/substantiated) | Object or Behavior? |
|---|---|---|
| "saves you time" | (a) "exports the report in one click" · (b) "the Friday 6pm reconciliation she used to dread is done before her coffee's cold" · (c) "47 fewer tabs open" | a=object, b=behavior, c=object |
| "reduces stress" | (a) "alerts before the deadline, not after" · (b) "he stopped checking Slack from bed" · (c) "the red error badge that used to greet every morning is gone" | a=object, b=behavior, c=behavior |

For each candidate, write it in one line, concrete and picture-able — something a camera could capture or a log could prove. If you catch yourself reaching for an invented specific to make it land ("she wept with relief"), stop: that's the dangerous failure mode. Cut it back to what's substantiated.

### Step 3 — Rank candidate details by Narrative Load (Evolution Log 2026-04-09); behaviors beat objects

Before the 3-Question Filter, score each *true* candidate against the 7 vectors and count how many it fires across simultaneously. This is what transforms detail selection from "find one that's concrete" to "find the one that makes the reader build the most." In copy the vectors read slightly differently than in fiction — same engine, copy-tuned:

| Vector (copy reading) | What it asks of the detail |
|---|---|
| **Character (the buyer)** | Does it reveal *who the buyer is* and the life they're living — not just what the product does? |
| **Stakes** | Does it show what the buyer was losing before (the cost the product ends)? |
| **Tension / before-state** | Does it carry the friction or dread of the old way inside it? |
| **Foreshadowing (the after-life)** | Does it hint at the life-after without spelling it out? |
| **World-building (the buyer's world)** | Does it ground the claim in the buyer's actual environment (the desk, the inbox, the Sunday)? |
| **Subtext (persuade without selling)** | Does it imply the benefit so the reader *concludes* it, rather than being told? |
| **Mood (the felt shift)** | Does it move the emotional temperature — relief, control, calm? |

The detail with the highest vector count is your primary candidate. A 2-vector detail decorates (usually an object/spec). A 5+-vector detail does the conversion's structural work (almost always a behavior — a buyer's observed moment). **When two candidates tie, the behavior beats the object** — "she closed the laptop at 5:15" out-converts "saves 2 hours/day" because the behavior carries the stakes, the before, and the after in one image the reader's imagination finishes.

| Candidate detail | Char | Stakes | Tension | Foreshadow | World | Subtext | Mood | Load |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "exports the report in one click" | – | – | – | – | ✓ | – | – | 1 |
| "the Friday 6pm reconciliation she dreaded is done before her coffee's cold" | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 7 |

The 7-vector behavior is the survivor. Carry it to Step 4.

### Step 4 — Filter the survivor through Connelly's 3-Question gate (Principle 1)

The highest-load detail still has to pass the filter — high-load details almost always do, but the gate catches the rare decorator that scored high by accident. Run the survivor through the three questions (copy-tuned):

1. **Does this detail reveal the buyer's lived moment AND the feature simultaneously?** The earpiece groove revealed the man *and* the work in one object; the copy detail must reveal *what the buyer's life is like* AND *what the product does* in one particular. If it only describes the product (a spec) — it's a feature, not a telling detail; demote it.
2. **Does it open a window of imagination — does the reader BUILD the benefit from it?** Give them the one detail and they should construct the saved hours, the lowered shoulders, the reclaimed Sunday — the whole benefit set you deleted. If you have to also *tell* them the benefit, the detail isn't telling enough.
3. **Does removing it lose something irreplaceable?** Cut it. If the line still converts on the bare claim, the detail was decoration. If the line collapses back into "saves time" without it — it's load-bearing. Keep it.

If the survivor fails the filter (rare), drop to the next-highest-load *true* candidate. Never reach for an invented detail to fill the gap — that's the spine breach.

### Step 5 — Replace the abstraction with the survivor; let the detail carry the line (subtext over surface)

Now do the actual swap. Delete the abstract benefit *entirely* — do not keep it as a "for clarity" backstop next to the detail. The whole mechanism is that the reader *infers* the benefit; if you also state it, you've answered the question their mind was about to answer, and the trust transfer dies. Connelly: the reader's mind does the construction work — and they trust what they build over what they're told.

- **Show the moment, cut the summary.** "Saves you 10 hours a week" → "She closed the laptop at 5:15 for the first time in two years." The hours are *inside* the detail; the reader does the math and believes their own arithmetic.
- **Let subtext do the persuading.** The line should be *about* the buyer's moment on the surface, and *about* the benefit underneath — the same two-layer move as Connelly's dialogue. Surface: a woman closing a laptop. Underneath: this product gives you your evenings back. The reader feels the undertow without being sold to.
- **Keep momentum — no speed bumps.** One detail per beat; the line pulls into the next. If the reader hits a stacked clause re-explaining what the detail already showed, that's a speed bump where they bail. Cut it.
- **Hold the honesty line in the rewrite.** The rewritten detail must still be the *true* one from Step 2 — it's tempting in the polish pass to sharpen "before her coffee's cold" into "before her first sip," which is fine *if true*, and a breach if you invented the coffee. Polish the language, never the facts.

### Step 6 — The per-detail honesty-spine confirmation (Q7 gate; the hard ship-block)

Before delivery, every surviving detail gets an individual real/substantiated confirmation — not a blanket "the copy is honest," but a line-by-line ledger. This is the gate that makes the engine safe to point at converting copy. For each detail, name *what makes it true*:

```
DETAIL: "she closed the laptop at 5:15 for the first time in two years"
SOURCE OF TRUTH: customer testimonial (Maria R., logged in case-study file) — "I left the office on time for the first time in two years"
VERDICT: REAL ✓  (drawn from a real customer's real words; "5:15" is illustrative-specific — confirm it's either her actual time or flagged as representative, not invented precision)
```

Run this for every detail in the piece. Three verdicts are possible:
- **REAL ✓** — sourced from real product behavior, a real customer moment, or a documented spec. Ships.
- **REPRESENTATIVE (flag it)** — the *pattern* is true (customers leave on time) but a specific (the exact "5:15") is illustrative. Acceptable *only* if the surrounding copy doesn't present it as a single named person's verified fact, or you soften the precision. When in doubt, generalize the specific until it's true.
- **INVENTED ✗** — manufactured to make the line land. **FATAL. Cut on sight and route the gap back to copy-engine / luke-iha for a real proof point.** A flawless telling detail on an unsubstantiated claim is the most dangerous output here — the better the craft, the more convincing the lie (genius § Q7).

> The asymmetry that makes this gate non-negotiable: in fiction, an invented detail is the *job*. In copy, an invented detail is fraud wearing Connelly's craft. The engine's power — making a detail feel undeniably real — is exactly why a false one is so dangerous. Verify every particular before it ships.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Channel | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Social** (LinkedIn / X / Substack hook into copy) | The hook opens on ONE concrete, true particular instead of the abstract promise — "the groove in the earpiece," not "after decades of stress." The detail *is* the scroll-stopper because it makes the reader build the story. One detail only; at hook length the abstraction-stack shows in a heartbeat. The benefit stays unstated — the inference *is* the click. |
| **Marketing** (ads / VSL lead / landing proof) | The VSL lead can carry two or three details across its length (it has runway), spread apart with plain copy between — never two showpiece details in the same breath. Landing proof: the detail replaces the benefit headline above the fold and the stat-block becomes the one customer moment. Every detail through the Step 6 ledger before it goes live; ad platforms and a skeptical buyer both punish a particular that's invented. |
| **Copy** (fascination bullets / sales letter body) | The fascination bullet is the purest form of this move: each bullet is *one* telling detail that implies a benefit the reader infers ("The 6pm reconciliation, done before your coffee's cold" beats "Saves you hours every Friday"). One detail per bullet, no stacking. The body distributes details across beats and lets subtext persuade so the letter never *sounds* like it's convincing. |
| **Ghostwriting** (a client's voice) | The detail must be drawn from the *client's* real world and real moments — their actual customer, their actual product behavior, their actual day. Cut the client's abstract benefit list in half and replace the load-bearing claims with their lived particulars. The voice reads as theirs because the details *are* theirs — and the honesty ledger protects the client from shipping a claim they can't stand behind. |
| **Content** (newsletter / thought-leadership / email body) | Tightest momentum demand — one speed bump and they archive. Open the section on the trigger detail, carry one particular per beat, end each section pulling into the next. Replace the abstract framework statement with the one concrete instance that *is* the framework (the single specific anecdote out-persuades the principle stated five ways). Every anecdote-detail is real or it's flagged. |

### Worked example A — VSL lead for a SaaS productivity tool

**Abstract benefit list (the target):** "Save 10+ hours a week. Reduce stress. Finally scale your operations." · **Buyer's lived moment:** the ops manager doing manual Friday-evening reconciliation alone in the office.

**Detail 1 · the lead**
> ABSTRACTION (cut): "Save 10+ hours a week and reduce the stress of manual reporting."
> CANDIDATES (all sourced from real onboarding-survey data): (a) "exports the weekly report in one click" [object, 1 vector] · (b) "the Friday-evening reconciliation she used to do alone in an empty office is finished before her coffee's cold" [behavior] · (c) "the report builds itself overnight" [object]
> NARRATIVE LOAD: survivor = (b) at 7 vectors — it carries who she is (the manager left alone Friday night), the stakes (her evenings, gone), the before-tension (dread), the after (home for dinner), her world (the empty office), the subtext (this gives you your Fridays back), and mood (relief). The behavior beats both objects.
> AFTER: *"Last quarter, Maria ran the Friday reconciliation alone in an empty office until eight. This quarter it was done before her coffee went cold."*
> BENEFIT THE READER INFERS (never told): the hours saved, the stress gone, the operation that now runs without her chained to it — all of it, built by the reader from one Friday.
> *Why it converts:* the reader does the math on "until eight" vs. "before her coffee went cold" and believes their own arithmetic — a saved-hours number they computed beats one they were handed. The "scale your operations" claim is delivered by implication: an operation that no longer needs Maria's Friday night *is* an operation that scales.
> ── HONESTY LEDGER ── SOURCE OF TRUTH: onboarding survey, customer Maria R., "I used to stay until 8 every Friday closing the books; now it's automatic." VERDICT: REAL ✓ ("before her coffee went cold" is representative-illustrative of the speed she described — flagged, and acceptable because it dramatizes a true before/after she stated herself, not an invented metric.)

### Worked example B — Fascination bullets for a premium ghostwriting service

**Abstract benefit list (the target):** "Authority-building content. Save time. Sound like the expert you are. Grow your audience." · **Buyer's lived moment:** the founder who knows their stuff but stares at a blank LinkedIn box at 11pm.

**The bullet block — one true telling detail per bullet, abstraction deleted:**
> BEFORE (abstraction-stack): "Save hours every week, build your authority, and finally post consistently without the stress of writing."
> AFTER (each bullet = one detail, benefit inferred):
> • *"The post you've rewritten four times and still haven't shipped — written, in your voice, before you finish your morning email."* (infers: time saved + consistency + your-voice — none stated)
> • *"A reader DMs you a screenshot of your own post and says 'this is exactly what I've been trying to explain to my team.'"* (infers: authority + resonance + the content actually landing — none stated)
> *Why it converts:* each bullet is a single concrete moment the founder recognizes from their own life; the benefit isn't claimed, it's *concluded* by the reader, which is the only kind of benefit a skeptical buyer trusts. No bullet stacks two details — one image, one inference, one beat of momentum into the next.
> ── HONESTY LEDGER ── Bullet 1 SOURCE: real client behavior (drafts delivered next-morning per the service's actual SLA) → REAL ✓. Bullet 2 SOURCE: a real client DM on file (anonymized) → REAL ✓. (If no such DM existed, bullet 2 would be INVENTED ✗ → cut, and the "authority" claim routed back to luke-iha for a real proof point.)

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Connelly Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` — especially **AN-2 (Adjective/benefit Stacking)**, **AN-1 (Decorative Prose)**, **AN-3 (Explaining Feelings — the copy form is *stating* the benefit)** — and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (rows: **Telling Detail Economy**, **Prose Transparency**, **Subtext Density**, **Economy of Expression**). Flag and fix before delivering.

- **One detail per beat, not a stack (AN-2 / Q1):** every load-bearing abstraction was replaced by *one* telling detail, not a smaller list of details. Remove the detail and the line must get *worse* at converting; if it doesn't, the detail was decoration — cut it. More details than conversion-turns = the abstraction-stack came back.
- **The detail SHOWS the moment, never STATES the benefit (Q5 / AN-3):** "saves time / reduces stress" is the copy form of "he felt X." If the benefit is still spelled out next to the detail, the inference is dead and the trust transfer with it — delete the stated benefit, keep only the shown moment.
- **The reader INFERS the benefit (Principle 1, Q2):** give the reader only the detail and they construct the full benefit set you deleted. If they need the claim told to them too, the detail isn't telling enough — find a higher-load one.
- **Narrative Load Ranking was run; the survivor is (almost always) a behavior (Evolution Log):** 3+ true candidates scored against the 7 vectors; the kept detail fires across 5+; where a behavior and an object tied, the behavior won. A spec that survived is suspect — re-check it isn't a decorator that scored high by luck.
- **Prose stays transparent, momentum intact (AN-1):** the detail does its work and the line pulls into the next; no re-explaining clause, no decorative flourish on the particular itself. The reader sees *through* the detail to the benefit, not *at* the writing.
- **Honesty spine — per-detail ledger complete (Q7):** every surviving detail has an individual SOURCE OF TRUTH and a verdict (REAL ✓ / REPRESENTATIVE-flagged / INVENTED ✗). Zero INVENTED details shipped. Any gap that could only be filled by an invented detail was routed back to copy-engine / luke-iha as a proof-point gap — never papered over with a fabricated particular. **This row is a hard ship-block: a false detail that feels real is fatal regardless of how well the rest scores.**

## Common Pitfalls

- **Replacing one abstraction-stack with a smaller pile of details (AN-2).** Swapping "save time, reduce stress, scale faster" for "one-click export, overnight reports, real-time alerts" — still a list, just more concrete. The reader still skims; the imagination-window never opens because three details give the mind nothing to build. **Recovery:** re-run Steps 3–4. Rank by narrative load and keep *the single survivor* — the one behavior that carries all three benefits by implication. One detail the reader finishes beats three they scan.
- **Stating the benefit next to the detail "so they don't miss it" (Q5 breach).** "She closed the laptop at 5:15 for the first time in two years — saving her over 10 hours a week." The tacked-on summary answers the exact question the detail was making the reader's mind answer, and the trust transfer (they believe what they conclude) collapses. **Recovery:** delete the stated benefit entirely. Trust the reader to do the arithmetic — they trust their own number, never yours.
- **Inventing a vivid specific to make the line land (Q7 — the fatal one).** Sharpening a true pattern into a fabricated particular: the real "customers save time" becomes an invented "Maria wept when she saw her first free Friday." It reads beautifully and it's a lie wearing Connelly's craft — the most dangerous output this engine can produce. **Recovery:** run the Step 6 ledger on every detail. If a particular has no SOURCE OF TRUTH, it's INVENTED ✗ — cut it, and route the underlying claim to copy-engine / luke-iha for a *real* proof point or customer moment. A telling detail amplifies a true claim; it can't conjure a missing one.
- **Choosing the object over the behavior (Evolution Log lesson).** Keeping "the dashboard has 40 native integrations" because it's impressively specific, over "she stopped opening four other apps before noon." The spec is true and concrete but it's a 1–2-vector decorator; the behavior is the diagnostic event that carries the stakes, the before, and the after. **Recovery:** when a candidate object and candidate behavior both pass honesty, default to the behavior — re-score on the 7 vectors and the behavior almost always out-loads the spec. The reader builds a life from a behavior; they file a spec and move on.
