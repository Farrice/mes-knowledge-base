# Connelly Brand Momentum — The Speed-Bump Audit for Brand & Content Narrative

An About page, a manifesto, a content-series opener, a founder's origin story — most of them die the same death. Not from a bad claim. From a *comfortable place to stop*. The reader hits the values list, the eye glazes, they tab away "to come back later," and later never comes. Connelly built a 100-million-copy career on one law that has nothing to do with crime: *"maybe the key thing is momentum… a lot of details create speed bumps."* He prints his draft, reads it at a reader's speed, and marks every place his own attention slows — then he doesn't polish those spots, he **rewrites the whole paragraph** (RW) or flags it not-good-enough (NSG) and goes again. This pass ports that exact mechanic onto brand and content narrative. It does not rewrite your message and it does not add claims. It finds every speed bump — the exposition dump, the founder-bio paragraph, the values list, the three-equal-weight sentences where one is dead weight, the paragraph-ending that releases instead of pulls — marks it with Connelly's shorthand, prescribes the fix, and delivers a verdict on one binary: *can the reader find a good place to stop?* If yes, you failed. Connelly is the man auditing the prose for forward energy, not the man writing your brand story.

### Where this sits vs. the general momentum-audit (read this once)

This is **not** the general-prose momentum pass (`momentum-audit.md` in this skill — fiction/blog/email, six speed-bump *types*, a 1-10 scale). This is the **brand & content-narrative cross-domain specialization**: the speed bumps it hunts are the ones that live in *brand* writing — exposition dumps as values lists and founder bios (AN-5 is the headline offender here), the "good place to stop" the reader takes mid-page, the manifesto paragraph that's three equal-weight sentences with no forward pull. The notation key (SB/RW/NSG), the Density-Threshold split rule, and the paragraph-ending forward-pull check are the brand-specific apparatus. Run that one for a chapter or a newsletter; run **this** for an About page, manifesto, content series, or long-form brand narrative.

And it **composes with `stanton-clamp-audit`** — they are not redundant, they operate at different scales:
- **Clamp (Stanton) = per-unit grip.** Walks each beat and asks *does this beat make me want the next one?* — closing the tension loop *inside* each unit so the beach ball doesn't drop within it.
- **Momentum (Connelly) = inter-unit pull.** Audits whether the loops *chain* — whether each unit's ending physically pulls into the next so the reader can't disengage in the *seam between* clamped units.
- **The order**: clamp first (make each unit grip), then momentum (make the grips chain). A piece can pass the clamp audit beat-by-beat and still leak at every paragraph-ending — that's the gap this pass closes. Also pairs with `real-world-anchor` (a true verifiable detail that replaces an exposition dump without slowing) and `character-through-place` (depth that reveals the brand without a bio paragraph — depth that *doesn't* cost momentum).

## Input Required

- **The narrative as it stands** — the actual About page / manifesto / content-series opener / founder story / long-form brand narrative. The whole thing, not a summary; we mark *specific lines and paragraph-endings*, so we need the real text with its real breaks.
- **The format** — About page / manifesto / content series (which installment?) / origin story / long-form post / email-as-narrative. (Drives the stopping-point tolerance: a manifesto can carry one breath; an About page should pull straight through.)
- **The reader's entry state** — cold (never heard of the brand) / warm (on the site, deciding) / committed (already a reader of the series). A cold reader has the lowest patience and the most exits; mark to *their* attention, not the committed reader's.
- **The one thing the reader must carry to the end** — the single belief/feeling the narrative exists to land. Momentum is in *service* of reaching it; if a speed bump sits before it, the reader never arrives.
- **Proof status (honesty spine)** — confirm the claims in the narrative are true/substantiated *before* the pass. We re-order and compress true material; we do not manufacture bridges. Flag any paragraph that stalls because its underlying claim is thin — that routes out, not through this pass.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Print it and read at the reader's speed (the Reading Test, not the editing read)

Connelly prints the draft and reads it at a reader's pace — *not* the writer's careful, sentence-admiring crawl. The point is to feel where *your own attention* slows, because that's exactly where the reader's will. Read the whole narrative once, fast, as the **cold reader** would. Do not fix anything yet. Mark — physically, in the margin — every place you:

| Marker | Reader symptom | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **SB** (Speed Bump) | You paused, slowed, or your eye drifted before the point landed | A momentum leak — the engine lost compression here |
| **STOP** (Good Place to Stop) | You felt you *could* set it down here and "come back later" | The failure state — Connelly's headline test. The page just lost the reader |
| **RW** (Rewrite Whole) | A whole paragraph is bumpy enough that line-edits won't save it | Don't polish — the paragraph gets rewritten from scratch (Connelly's actual mechanic) |
| **NSG** (Not So Good) | A line/section is weak but you're not yet sure of the fix | Flag-and-return; comes back on the next pass, not patched in place |

> **The "Good Place to Stop" Test is the headline mechanic.** *If the reader can find a comfortable stopping point, you've failed.* Every STOP mark is a wound, not a note. Most brand narratives are riddled with them and the writer can't see it — because the writer reads at writer-speed and never reaches a stop (they know what's coming). Read at reader-speed, cold, and the STOPs surface.

### Step 2 — Hunt the four brand-narrative speed bumps (Macro-Erosion, ported)

This is Connelly's Pass-2 Macro-Erosion read, retargeted at the speed bumps that specifically infest brand writing. Walk the marked draft and classify every SB by which of these it is — the fix differs by type:

| Speed-bump type | What it looks like in brand narrative | Why the reader stops | Fix direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exposition Dump (AN-5)** — *the #1 brand speed bump* | The values list ("We believe in honesty, innovation, and customer obsession"). The founder-bio paragraph ("After 12 years at McKinsey, Sarah…"). The "our mission is…" block. Information delivered *to inform*, not to move. | The eye recognizes a list/résumé/mission-statement shape and pre-decides it's skippable. This is the classic STOP-generator. | Embed the value in *one observed action or true anecdote* (route to `/real-world-anchor` for the real detail); cut the résumé to the one fact that reveals character; delete the mission statement and let the narrative *enact* the mission. |
| **Attention-Wander** | A "beautiful" paragraph admired for its own sound (AN-1); a metaphor that calls attention to itself; three sentences saying the same thing prettily. | The reader stops to appreciate the writing — which means they stopped reading the *story*. Transparent prose is the goal; admired prose is a re-read trap. | Cut to the one transparent sentence that moves. If a line's primary job is to be admired, it's a speed bump no matter how good it is. |
| **Density-Threshold violation** | A sentence carrying 3+ ideas the reader has to unpack; a paragraph of 4+ equal-weight sentences with no forward lean. | The reader re-reads to parse (sentence overload) or the eye drifts because nothing is pulling (flat-paragraph overload). | **>2 ideas per sentence → split** into separate sentences. **>3 equal-weight sentences in a paragraph → one is dead weight**: find it, cut it, or subordinate it so the others lead somewhere. (Step 3.) |
| **Character-Vacuum** | The brand/founder voice disappears for 3+ sentences of generic industry-speak, feature description, or category exposition. | The reader loses the thread of *who is talking* — momentum is character-borne; when the character vanishes, the pull goes with it. | Reshape the stretch so the brand's character surfaces within 3 sentences — an observation only *this* brand would make, a stance only it holds. (Pair with `character-through-place` for depth without slowing.) |

### Step 3 — Apply the Density Threshold (the split-and-cut rule)

The Density Threshold is the one mechanical, non-judgment rule in the pass — run it on every paragraph carrying an SB:

- **The sentence rule: >2 ideas → split.** Count the distinct ideas in each sentence. Three or more, and the reader is decoding instead of reading. Split into separate sentences; the white space *is* the momentum. (Connelly: spare prose = momentum; dense prose = speed bumps.)
- **The paragraph rule: >3 equal-weight sentences → one is a speed bump.** When a paragraph runs four or more sentences that all carry the same weight and none leans forward, one of them is dead weight. The test: cut each sentence in turn and re-read — if the paragraph still does its job, that sentence *was* the bump. Cut it, or subordinate it so the survivors build toward the paragraph-ending.

| Move | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| **Split (3 ideas → 3 sentences)** | "Our platform automates your invoicing, integrates with your CRM, and learns your cash-flow patterns so you never chase a late payment again." | "Our platform automates your invoicing. It learns your cash-flow patterns. You never chase a late payment again." |
| **Cut the dead-weight sentence (4 equal → 3 that lead)** | "We started in a garage. We were broke. We had a laptop and an idea. We believed in the mission." | "We started in a garage, broke, with a laptop and an idea." *(The "we believed in the mission" sentence was the bump — it tells what the first three already showed.)* |

### Step 4 — Check every paragraph-ending for forward pull (the seam test)

This is the inter-unit pass that `stanton-clamp-audit` doesn't run. Clamp grips *within* a unit; momentum lives in the *seam between* units. Connelly's law: *every paragraph ending should pull into the next.* Go ending by ending and mark each one:

| Paragraph-ending verdict | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **PULL** | The last line opens a loop, raises a question, or leans into the next paragraph. The eye *has* to continue. | Keep. This is the win state. |
| **REST** | The last line *resolves* — it's a tidy summary, a complete thought, a satisfying period. | This is a STOP in disguise: a "good place to stop." Re-end the paragraph on the line that *doesn't* resolve — move the open loop to the end, or cut the resolving line entirely. |
| **DRIFT** | The ending trails into a qualifier, a hedge, a "…and that's important." | Cut to the hard last word (the seam wants a forward edge, not a soft landing). End on the line that pulls. |

> The most common brand-narrative failure: paragraphs that *each resolve cleanly* — which feels like good writing and is actually a chain of STOP signs. A well-resolved paragraph-ending is the most polite way to lose a reader. Re-end on the loop.

### Step 5 — Rewrite the worst (RW the paragraph, don't patch it)

Connelly's mechanic is not line-editing. When a paragraph is marked **RW**, the paragraph gets *rewritten whole* — you don't move commas in a dead paragraph, you write a live one. Take the top 2-3 worst speed bumps (rank by: STOP > RW > SB by reader-exit cost) and rewrite each from scratch, applying the fix direction from Steps 2-4. Then **re-read at speed** — the rewrite isn't done when it's prettier, it's done when *the eye can't stop on it and the ending pulls into the next paragraph*. **NSG** items get a second look only after the RWs land; don't patch a flagged-weak line in place.

> Honesty re-check at every rewrite: a rewritten paragraph must say the same *true* thing the original meant, only with the momentum restored. If the rewrite flows better because it dropped a caveat the claim needed, or added a vivid detail that isn't real, you've traded a speed bump for a lie. Route the claim out, don't smooth over it.

### Step 6 — Score the momentum verdict (the one binary, then the rubric)

Deliver the verdict on Connelly's binary first, then the rubric for nuance:

- **The binary (headline):** *Can the reader find a good place to stop?* **YES = FAIL** (count the STOPs; name the worst one). **NO = PASS** (the reader is pulled through to the must-carry belief).
- **The Momentum Score (1-10), brand-narrative calibration:**

| Score | State | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **9-10** | No stopping point exists | Zero STOPs, zero REST endings before the payoff. The reader physically cannot put it down before the belief lands. |
| **7-8** | Propulsive with 1-2 minor SBs | Mostly pulls; one or two speed bumps the reader rides over. No STOP before the payoff. |
| **5-6** | Several comfortable exits | Multiple STOP / REST endings. The cold reader leaves mid-page "to come back." (Most unaudited About pages live here.) |
| **3-4** | Exposition-dump dominant | Values list / founder bio / mission block carrying the weight; the reader skims to the bottom or bails. |
| **1-2** | Dead narrative | The reader forces themselves through or doesn't finish. STOP at nearly every paragraph. |

If the verdict is FAIL (or score < 7), the deliverable is the marked draft + ranked fixes + the rewrites of the worst offenders — not a pass certificate.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Social** (LinkedIn / X brand thread) | Highest STOP-cost: every line break is a potential scroll-away. Run Step 4 on *every* line, not just paragraphs — each line-ending must pull to the next or the thumb wins. Density Threshold is brutal here: one idea per line. Exposition dumps (the credential drop, the "here's my background") are instant scroll-offs — replace with one true detail. |
| **Marketing** (campaign manifesto, brand film script, key-message doc) | The manifesto can carry *one* engineered breath before its climax — but only one, and only if the line after it pulls hard. Hunt the values-list speed bump first (manifestos love a triad of abstract values); enact each value in a concrete line instead. Every section-ending is a seam — REST endings between sections are where the read dies. |
| **Copy** (About page, landing-page story block, origin section) | About pages are the native habitat of the exposition dump (founder bio + mission statement + values list, stacked). This is where the pass earns its keep. Cut the résumé to the one fact that reveals character; delete the mission statement; re-end every paragraph on a loop. Honesty spine is load-bearing — an About page that flows the reader into an inflated origin story is the dangerous failure. |
| **Ghostwriting** (a client's brand narrative in their voice) | The character-vacuum is the risk: a ghostwritten narrative drifts into generic brand-speak the moment the writer loses the client's voice. Mark every 3-sentence stretch where *the client* disappears. The momentum must be *theirs* — re-end paragraphs on the loop only *this* person would open. Cut the client's self-description in half; the longest brand bios pull the least. |
| **Content** (series opener, newsletter-as-narrative, long-form post) | The series opener has one job: end with such a hard PULL that the reader needs installment two. Audit the *final* paragraph-ending hardest of all — a REST ending on a series opener kills the series. Inside the piece, the Density Threshold keeps a long-form post from sagging in the middle third (where readers bail). One STOP in the middle and the back half goes unread. |

### Worked example A — About page with an exposition dump (the values-list + founder-bio stall)

**Narrative:** SaaS company About page · **Reader entry state:** cold · **Must-carry belief:** *this team has actually felt the pain they're solving.*

**MARKED DRAFT (excerpt):**
> Founded in 2019, Acme is a mission-driven company built on three core values: transparency, innovation, and relentless customer obsession. `[STOP — exposition dump, values triad: the eye recognizes a values list and skips]` Our founder, Sarah Chen, spent twelve years in enterprise finance before realizing the industry was broken. She holds an MBA from Wharton and previously led teams at two Fortune 500 firms. `[RW — founder-bio résumé block; character-vacuum, no forward pull] [ending: REST — résumé resolves, reader sets it down]` We believe finance software should be simple, powerful, and beautiful. `[SB — density: 3 equal abstract ideas; ending: REST]`

**MOMENTUM VERDICT:** GOOD PLACE TO STOP? **YES = FAIL.** Score **4/10** — exposition-dump dominant. Worst STOP: the values triad in line one — a cold reader leaves before reaching any reason to care.

**REWRITE (#1, RW the whole opening):**
> Sarah Chen spent twelve years watching enterprise finance teams reconcile invoices by hand at 11 p.m. on the last day of the quarter. She built Acme the night she realized the software they'd paid six figures for couldn't do what a spreadsheet did in 1998.

**What changed:** The values triad (transparency/innovation/obsession) is gone — the *belief* it claimed ("customer obsession") is now *enacted* by the watched-them-suffer detail, which a cold reader can't skip because it's a scene, not a list. The Wharton/Fortune-500 résumé is cut to nothing; the one character-revealing fact (she built it the night she snapped) carries more authority than the credentials did. The ending — "couldn't do what a spreadsheet did in 1998" — is a PULL (it opens the loop: *so what did she build?*), replacing two REST endings. **Honesty:** every fact is the same true material (12 years in finance, founded the company) — re-ordered and rendered as scene, with nothing invented; if "11 p.m. last day of the quarter" isn't a true particular of Sarah's story, it routes to `/real-world-anchor` for the real one, not fabricated here.

### Worked example B — Content-series opener that stalls (the middle-third sag + REST ending)

**Narrative:** Opener for a 6-part newsletter series on creative burnout · **Reader entry state:** warm (subscribed, deciding whether to read on) · **Must-carry belief:** *I need installment two.*

**MARKED DRAFT (excerpt):**
> Burnout is one of the most discussed topics in the creative world today. `[STOP — abstract preamble, generic; ending: DRIFT]` There are many factors that contribute to it, including overwork, lack of boundaries, perfectionism, and the pressure of social media comparison. `[SB — density: 4 ideas in one sentence, reader decodes a list]` In this series, I'll explore each of these in depth and offer practical strategies you can apply. `[STOP — exposition dump: a table-of-contents paragraph; ending: REST — fully resolved, reader has the gist and can leave]`

**MOMENTUM VERDICT:** GOOD PLACE TO STOP? **YES = FAIL.** Score **3/10** — the opener resolves itself (it *tells* the reader what's coming, so they don't need part two). Worst STOP: the "in this series I'll explore…" paragraph — a resolved table of contents is a permission slip to unsubscribe.

**REWRITE (whole opener, RW):**
> The first time I burned out, I didn't notice. I noticed the second time, three years later, because I was sitting at my desk at 2 p.m. and couldn't remember the last idea I'd had that excited me. I've since learned there were five warnings before that afternoon. I missed every one. This series is the five I missed.

**What changed:** The "most discussed topic" preamble is gone (a cold/warm reader has heard it; it generates a STOP on contact). The four-idea density sentence is dissolved — instead of *listing* the factors, the opener *dramatizes* one moment and promises the five warnings, so the list becomes a forward-pulling loop instead of a resolved summary. The ending — "This series is the five I missed" — is a hard PULL: the reader cannot get the five warnings without installment two, which is the opener's only job. **Honesty:** the structure (six parts, the contributing factors) is unchanged and true; nothing about the author's experience is invented to manufacture the scene — if the "2 p.m., couldn't remember" moment isn't real, it gets replaced with the writer's true one, never faked.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review the audited/rewritten narrative against `genius.md § Connelly Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` — especially **AN-5 (Exposition Dumps)** and **AN-1 (Decorative Prose)** — and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (rows: **Narrative Momentum**, **Prose Transparency**, **Character-Driven Revelation**). Flag and fix before delivering.

- **The binary is answered honestly (Q3 / Principle 2):** you read at the *cold reader's* speed, not the writer's, and the verdict states whether a good place to stop exists. A "PASS" that the writer reached only because they knew what came next is a failed audit — re-read cold.
- **Every STOP is named, not just counted:** each STOP mark points to the exact line where the reader leaves and *why* (which type). An unnamed STOP can't be fixed.
- **Exposition dumps are gone, not gilded (AN-5):** no surviving values list, mission-statement block, or résumé-shaped founder bio. Each was either embedded in a true observed action or cut. If one was made *prettier* but still informs-rather-than-moves, it's still a speed bump — re-do it.
- **Density Threshold applied mechanically (Principle 2):** every >2-idea sentence split; every paragraph of >3 equal-weight sentences had the dead-weight one cut or subordinated. Report the counts.
- **Every paragraph-ending pulls (Step 4 / Connelly's law):** no REST or DRIFT endings survive before the must-carry belief lands. Resolved paragraph-endings are STOPs in disguise — re-ended on the loop.
- **Rewrites are whole, not patched (the RW mechanic):** RW-marked paragraphs were rewritten from scratch, then re-read at speed to confirm the eye can't stop and the ending pulls. Comma-moving in a dead paragraph is not the mechanic.
- **Prose is transparent, not admired (Q6 / AN-1):** no rewrite traded a speed bump for a "beautiful" line that makes the reader pause to appreciate it. The eye should see *through* to the brand's story, not *at* the writing.
- **Honesty spine intact (the load-bearing check):** every rewrite says the same true thing with momentum restored — no invented bridging detail, no fabricated founder anecdote, no dropped caveat the claim needed. Any stall caused by a thin/missing claim was routed to copy/brand/anchor, not smoothed over here. A speed bump removed by a falsehood is the one failure that fails the whole pass regardless of score.
- **Composition respected:** if `stanton-clamp-audit` ran first (per-unit grip), this pass confirms the grips *chain* (inter-unit pull) and didn't re-litigate beat-internal clamp — they stack, they don't duplicate.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Auditing at writer-speed (the invisible-STOP trap).** Reading carefully, sentence by sentence, admiring the craft — which is exactly how the writer never *hits* a stopping point, because they already know what's coming and the prose pulls *for them*. The cold reader has none of that momentum-from-knowledge. **Recovery:** print it (or read it on a different device), read it *fast* and *cold* as someone who's never heard of the brand, and mark the first instinct to glance away. The STOPs you'd never catch at writer-speed are the ones losing you readers.
- **Mistaking a clean resolution for good writing (the REST-ending trap).** Re-ending every paragraph on a tidy, complete thought because it *feels* polished — and building a chain of comfortable stopping points. A well-resolved paragraph is the most polite way to lose a reader. **Recovery:** Step 4 — tag every ending PULL/REST/DRIFT and re-end every REST on the open loop. The narrative should resolve *once*, at the very end, on the must-carry belief — never mid-page.
- **Removing a speed bump with an invented detail (the honesty-spine breach — the worst one).** A founder-bio exposition dump stalls, so you bridge it with a vivid origin anecdote that *sounds* true and isn't — momentum restored, but the prose now flows the reader straight into a fabrication, and the better the craft the more convincing the lie. **Recovery:** before any rewrite, confirm the underlying claim is true; if the stall is because the real material is thin, that's a `/copy-engine` or `/real-world-anchor` job (get the *real* detail), not a momentum fix. Connelly makes a *true* detail do more work; he never invents one to patch a seam.
- **Treating this as the general momentum-audit and stacking it onto a clamp pass redundantly.** Running the six-type general scan on a brand About page (wrong target), or re-checking per-beat grip that `stanton-clamp-audit` already covered (duplicated work, no inter-unit gain). **Recovery:** use this pass for *brand/content narrative* specifically (its speed bumps are exposition-dump-as-values-list and the comfortable mid-page STOP), and let it do the *inter-unit seam* work clamp doesn't — check that the grips chain, don't re-grade each grip.
