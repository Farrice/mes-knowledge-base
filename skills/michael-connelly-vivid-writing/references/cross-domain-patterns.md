# Michael Connelly — Cross-Domain Patterns

> How a crime novelist's craft transfers to the work you actually ship. Connelly's four load-bearing moves — the **telling detail**, **sacred momentum**, **character-through-everything**, and **subtext** — operate on the *reader's attention and imagination*, not on the subject matter. That makes them domain-agnostic. This file is the concrete before→after for each business-writing domain.
>
> **The honesty spine holds in every cell below.** Connelly's craft makes a *true* detail do five paragraphs of work; it never invents a detail to fake authenticity. In fiction a detail can be imagined. In copy, brand, and ghostwriting it must be a *real, verifiable particular* — the better the craft, the more convincing a false detail becomes, which is the most dangerous failure mode. Run the Decision Framework (genius.md) Q7 before shipping any detail in a non-fiction domain.

---

## The Four Moves in One Line Each

1. **Telling detail** — ONE concrete particular that reveals character AND situation simultaneously, replacing a paragraph of abstraction. The reader's mind builds the rest.
2. **Sacred momentum** — the reader physically cannot find a comfortable place to stop. Every line ending pulls into the next. Spare prose = momentum; dense prose = speed bumps.
3. **Character-through-everything** — every element (setting, conflict, observation) reveals who the subject IS. No bio paragraphs, no values lists.
4. **Subtext** — the line is "about this on the surface, but really about that." Surface ≠ meaning. Trust the reader to feel the undertow.

## Domain 1: Social Media (LinkedIn / X / Substack)

**Moves that earn their place**: Telling detail · slingshot opener · momentum · subtext
**Matching workflows**: `telling-detail-engine`, `slingshot-opener`, `momentum-audit`

The hook lives or dies on whether it opens on ONE true particular instead of an abstract claim. Stacking three details kills the imagination-window — the whole point is the reader does the construction work.

**Before:**
> After years of working with founders, I've learned that the biggest barrier to growth isn't strategy — it's the mindset that holds them back from delegating effectively and trusting their team to execute at a high level.

**After:**
> He'd hired a COO eight months ago. His laptop still had her job's password saved under his own login.

What changed: the abstract claim ("the biggest barrier is mindset") became one concrete behavior that reveals the founder's character (can't let go), his situation (already paid to delegate), and the post's whole thesis — without naming any of it. The reader infers "trust issue" themselves, and a conclusion they reach is one they believe. The opener is also a slingshot: it drops the reader into a question (why?) they have to scroll to answer.

**Anti-pattern to kill:** adjective-stacked openers ("the hard, lonely, misunderstood reality of founding a company"). Find the right noun and the adjectives become unnecessary.

## Domain 2: Marketing / Brand Narrative

**Moves that earn their place**: Character-through-everything · momentum · real-world anchor
**Matching workflows**: `character-through-place`, `real-world-anchor`, `outsider-lens-filter`, `momentum-audit`

The brand IS a character. Reveal it through setting, conflict, and observed detail — never a "Our Values" block. Run every element through "what does this reveal about who this brand IS?"

**Before:**
> We're a mission-driven company committed to quality, transparency, and putting our customers first. Our team is passionate about delivering exceptional experiences that exceed expectations every single time.

**After:**
> The founder still answers the support line on Saturdays. Not as a stunt — because the first time a customer caught a defect she'd missed, she decided she never wanted that call routed to someone who didn't build the thing.

What changed: "transparency" and "customer first" (claims anyone can type) became one anchored behavior that *demonstrates* both, while revealing the brand's character (accountability as a wound, not a slogan). It reads as true because it's specific and Google-able — the real-world anchor sells the brand for free. Every clause pulls into the next; no comfortable stopping point.

**Honesty spine:** if the founder does NOT answer the line on Saturdays, you cannot write this. Find the detail that *is* true. The craft makes a true detail land harder; it never manufactures one.

## Domain 3: Copywriting (VSL / Landing / Direct Response)

**Moves that earn their place**: Telling detail (replaces benefit lists) · subtext · momentum
**Matching workflows**: `telling-detail-engine`, `subtext-dialogue-builder`, `momentum-audit`, `newspaper-dialogue-economy`

One concrete detail *implies* the feature set. Show the moment the product changes the buyer's life; don't enumerate specs. The reader infers benefits — and trusts an inferred benefit more than a claimed one. Subtext lets copy persuade without sounding like it's convincing.

**Before:**
> Our project management tool helps you save time, stay organized, reduce stress, and collaborate seamlessly with your team. With powerful features and an intuitive interface, you'll wonder how you ever worked without it.

**After:**
> Friday, 5:58 PM. You close the laptop without checking it twice. For the first time in a year, you actually believe nothing's on fire.

What changed: four abstract benefits (time, organization, stress, collaboration) collapsed into one telling moment that the buyer *feels*. The reader supplies "this tool gives me confidence and peace" — a benefit they constructed, therefore believe. The clock detail does the work of five bullet points. Every line drops into the next; momentum is the conversion engine.

**Honesty spine:** the moment described must be one the product actually produces. A vivid false promise is the most dangerous copy there is.

## Domain 4: Ghostwriting (Writing in a Client's Voice)

**Moves that earn their place**: Dialogue economy · outsider lens · subtext · character lens
**Matching workflows**: `outsider-lens-filter`, `newspaper-dialogue-economy`, `character-design-blueprint`, `subtext-dialogue-builder`

Cut the client's copy in half — the most over-explained writing is the longest and the lowest-trust. Find the gap between what the client *has access to* and *why they feel they don't belong*. That tension IS the voice. The output must read as *theirs*, never as the ghostwriter's showpiece.

**Before** (a finance exec's drafted post):
> As someone who has spent over two decades in institutional investing, I believe it is critically important for retail investors to understand that the market is not as accessible or fair as it may appear, and that there are structural advantages held by large players that the average person simply cannot compete with.

**After:**
> I spent twenty years on the inside. The thing nobody tells you: the game isn't rigged the way you think. It's rigged in a more boring way — the big players just get the news four seconds earlier.

What changed: the hedged, credential-leading sentence became lean and characterized. The outsider lens surfaces ("I was inside, and I'm telling you what I saw" = access without comfort) — that gap is the voice. Subtext: surface is "markets aren't fair," but it's really "I'm letting you in on something my industry doesn't say out loud." Dialogue economy cut ~60 words to ~40 with zero information loss.

**Honesty spine:** "four seconds earlier" must be a true particular the client would stand behind, not invented for punch.

## Domain 5: Entrepreneurship / Founder Storytelling

**Moves that earn their place**: Real-world anchor · telling detail · outsider lens · character-through-everything
**Matching workflows**: `real-world-anchor`, `character-through-place`, `outsider-lens-filter`, `telling-detail-engine`

Founder narratives die in the abstract origin story ("I was frustrated by the lack of X, so I built a solution"). Replace the abstraction with the one true scene that *is* the origin. Anchor it in a Google-able world so it reads as fact, not pitch-deck mythology.

**Before:**
> I started this company because I was passionate about solving a real problem I saw in the industry. I knew there had to be a better way, so I took the leap and decided to build something that would make a meaningful difference.

**After:**
> I quit the morning I watched my manager bill a client $400 for a one-line email I'd written in the elevator. I drove home, sat in the driveway for an hour, and opened a doc titled "the version where the person who does the work gets paid."

What changed: the universal founder-cliché became one anchored scene (specific dollar amount, specific physical location — driveway, elevator) that reveals character (a fairness wound, the same engine as Bosch) and is verifiable in texture. The outsider lens is built in: insider access (knew the work was worth more) plus refusal to belong (quit). No comfortable stopping point — the driveway hour pulls you to the doc title.

## Domain 6: Content Creation (Blog / Newsletter / Long-Form / Essays)

**Moves that earn their place**: Momentum · slingshot · telling detail · exposition-as-action · subtext
**Matching workflows**: `slingshot-opener`, `momentum-audit`, `connelly-rewrite-protocol`, `telling-detail-engine`

Long-form has the tightest momentum demand — one speed bump and they archive. Open with the trigger (slingshot), carry one telling detail, distribute context through example and observation (never an exposition dump), and end every section pulling into the next.

**Before** (newsletter intro):
> In today's edition, I want to talk about the importance of consistency in building an audience. Consistency is one of those things that everyone knows matters, but few people actually understand how to implement effectively over the long term. Let me explain why it's so crucial and break down a framework for thinking about it.

**After:**
> The creator who beat me to 100k followers posted worse content than I did. I checked. She just never missed a Tuesday. I missed eleven.

What changed: the throat-clearing meta-intro ("in today's edition I want to talk about") became a slingshot opening on one concrete, slightly humbling detail. "Consistency matters" (told) became "she never missed a Tuesday, I missed eleven" (shown, with a number that makes it real). The exposition about consistency is now embedded in a mini-story — context delivered through action, not lecture. The reader cannot stop at the end of those four sentences.

**Anti-pattern to kill:** the "Here's what / Here's why / Here's how" scaffold and the meta-announcement of what the piece will cover. Connelly never stops the story to tell you what the story is about.

## Quick Routing Table (Symptom → Move → Workflow)

| If the writing feels... | Reach for | Workflow |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract, claim-heavy, no images | Telling detail | `telling-detail-engine` |
| Slow, skimmable, easy to leave | Momentum audit | `momentum-audit` |
| Flat opening, reader not hooked | Slingshot opener | `slingshot-opener` |
| Dialogue/quotes too long, over-explained | Dialogue economy | `newspaper-dialogue-economy` |
| On-the-nose, no depth, surface = meaning | Subtext | `subtext-dialogue-builder` |
| Voice has no edge, reads "comfortable" | Outsider lens | `outsider-lens-filter` |
| Story feels invented / unbelievable | Real-world anchor | `real-world-anchor` |
| Brand/founder revealed via bio not behavior | Character through place / blueprint | `character-through-place`, `character-design-blueprint` |
| Whole draft needs a tightening pass | Dual-pass erosion | `connelly-rewrite-protocol` |

## The Universal Caution (applies to every domain above)

The craft is **subtraction**, not addition. If an output mechanically applies all four moves in sequence, it has failed — that produces over-detailed prose that announces its own craft, the exact opposite of cinematic. Deploy ONE move where it carries the load. Most business writing needs the telling detail and momentum; it rarely needs all four at once. Would Connelly recognize this as transparent, propulsive prose — or as someone filling vivid-writing slots? If unsure, the answer is the second. Cut.
