# History Lineage Map

The final foundation step. You've declared identity, copied quality, and extracted rules. Now trace how those rules got there. Every design choice, naming convention, and creative standard has ancestors. This workflow maps them.

Sam Parr's lineage philosophy: "The iPod connects to a 1953 radio connects to 1919 Bauhaus connects to rejection of Victorian ornament." Nothing is original. Understanding the thread gives you authority to work within AND against tradition.

## Pre-Flight Gate

- User should have completed `/taste-rules` or have a named rule set
- Read `genius.md` — focus on Pattern 9 (History-as-Constraints), Pattern 11 (Lineage Thread), Exemplar 1 (Braun T3 → iPod), Exemplar 3 (Dr. Dre Sampling)
- User must provide: Their domain, identity statement, and key rules from `/taste-rules`

## Execution

### Step 1: Rule Origin Audit
Take the top 5-7 rules from `/taste-rules` and research their origins:
- When did this rule first appear?
- WHO established it? (Name an individual, school, or movement)
- WHAT were they reacting against? (Every aesthetic rule was born from rejecting something)
- What preceded it?

### Step 2: Lineage Threading
For each rule origin, trace backward at least 3 generations:
- Generation 0 (Now): Your exemplars
- Generation -1: Who your exemplars were influenced by
- Generation -2: Who influenced THEM
- Generation -3+: Keep going until you hit a foundational shift

Use Sam Parr's format: "X → Y → Z → A" with one sentence per transition explaining the innovation.

### Step 3: The Break Points
Identify where the lineage SPLITS — where someone broke tradition to create a new branch:
- What rule did they break?
- What did they keep?
- Why did it work? (Usually: they'd mastered everything they were breaking)

These are the "great taste" moments — the cover → sampling → creation progression from genius Pattern 12.

### Step 4: Counter-Lineage
For every tradition you're within, trace the OPPOSING tradition:
- If you're in minimalist design, trace maximalism's lineage
- If you're in formal writing, trace casual voice's lineage
- Understanding what you're NOT choosing strengthens your commitment to what you ARE choosing

### Step 5: Personal Lineage Statement
Synthesize into a lineage declaration:
> "My [domain] taste descends from [tradition], which emerged when [person/movement] rejected [prior norm] in favor of [principle]. I honor [specific rules from this tradition] and I break [specific rule] because [reason that demonstrates mastery]."

### Step 6: Canon List
Produce the 10-15 works, people, or moments that define your lineage:
- These become your reference library
- Read these, study these, return to these
- They're the "conversations at the dinner table" from Sam Parr's frame

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content Type | Lineage Starting Points |
|-------------|------------------------|
| Fashion | Start with your style label → trace through fashion history books → reach the cultural moment that created it |
| Web design | Start with current design trends → trace through design movements → reach Gutenberg's typographic decisions |
| Writing | Start with your favorite authors → trace their influences → reach the literary or journalistic tradition |
| Music | Start with your preferred genre → trace sampling/influence chains → reach the cultural origin |
| Brand | Start with brands you admire → trace their positioning heritage → reach the business philosophy |
| Naming | Start with name patterns you like → trace phonetic traditions → reach the linguistic/cultural origin |

## Output Requirements

1. **Lineage Maps** — 5-7 visual threads tracing rules backward 3+ generations
2. **Break Point Analysis** — 3-5 historical moments where someone broke tradition successfully
3. **Counter-Lineage** — The opposing tradition you're choosing NOT to follow, and why
4. **Lineage Declaration** — Your personal positioning within the historical thread
5. **Canon List** — 10-15 essential works, people, or moments to study deeply

## Quality Gate

Reference genius.md quality rubric:
- **Score 4**: Knows current exemplars only
- **Score 7**: Traces lineage back 1-2 generations
- **Score 10**: 3+ generations deep with break point analysis
- **Minimum to pass**: Score 7. If lineage is shallow, more research is needed before proceeding.
