# Creative Lineage Sprint

A universal lineage-tracing workflow that can be applied to ANY creative work, style, product, or movement. Unlike `/taste-lineage` (which traces YOUR personal taste lineage), this traces the lineage of any creative artifact — for analysis, inspiration, or authority-building.

## Pre-Flight Gate

- Read `genius.md` — focus on Pattern 11 (The Lineage Thread), Pattern 12 (Cover-to-Chop Progression), Exemplar 1 (Braun → iPod), Exemplar 3 (Dr. Dre)
- User must provide: The creative work, style, product, brand, or movement to trace
- Works with ANY creative expert in the system (Roth for writing lineage, Grace for media lineage, Oren for luxury lineage, etc.)

## Execution

### Step 1: Artifact Identification
Name the creative work and its key characteristics:
- What is it? (A song, a website, a brand, a fashion item, a book, a product)
- When was it created?
- Who created it?
- What is it known for — what makes it distinctive?

### Step 2: First-Generation Mining (Parents)
Identify the direct influences:
- What did the creator(s) explicitly cite as inspiration?
- What were they reacting AGAINST?
- What contemporaries were doing similar work?
- What tradition were they trained in?

Research sources: creator interviews, biographies, industry histories, academic analyses.

### Step 3: Second-Generation Mining (Grandparents)
For each parent influence, trace back one more generation:
- Who influenced the influencers?
- What movement or cultural moment enabled their innovation?
- What constraints were they operating within?

### Step 4: Deep Thread (Great-Grandparents+)
Keep tracing until you hit a foundational moment — a cultural, technological, or philosophical shift:
- Print revolution (Gutenberg)
- Industrial Revolution (manufacturing as aesthetic)
- Modernism (rejection of ornament)
- Post-war movements (rebellion, democratization)
- Digital revolution (new medium, new constraints)

### Step 5: Innovation Mapping
At each generation, identify the innovation pattern:
- What RULE did this generation follow from the previous?
- What RULE did they break?
- What NEW rule did they establish?
- Was the innovation: technical, aesthetic, philosophical, or some combination?

Use the Sam Parr progression: cover → sample → create. Where does each generation sit?

### Step 6: Lineage Visualization
Produce the thread in Sam Parr's format:
> **Work Z** (rule-break: Y) → **Movement W** (rule-break: V) → **Artist U** (rule-break: T) → **Tradition S** (rule-break: R) → **Foundational Shift Q**

Each arrow represents an innovation. Each label names what was broken and what was kept.

### Step 7: Pattern Extraction
From the full lineage, extract transferable insights:
- What's the cycle length? (How long do conventions hold before someone breaks them?)
- What's the typical innovation pattern? (Usually: one person masters the rules, then breaks one specific element)
- What's coming NEXT? (If the lineage shows a pattern, where is it headed?)

### Step 8: Application (if requested)
If the user wants to APPLY the lineage to their own work:
- Which generation are YOU most aligned with?
- Which rule from this lineage could you honor?
- Which rule could you break?
- What would that look like?

## Content Type Adaptations

| Domain | Lineage-Tracing Specifics |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Writing | Trace prose style through literary movements: Hemingway → Journalism → Naturalism → Realism |
| Music | Trace genre/production: EDM → Techno → Kraftwerk → Musique Concrète → John Cage → Experimental |
| Web design | Trace through design movements: Current trends → Material/Flat → Skeuomorphism → Swiss → Bauhaus |
| Brand | Trace through positioning philosophies: Purpose-driven → Heritage → Luxury → Artisan → Guild |
| Product | Trace through design philosophies: Current → Rams → Bauhaus → Arts & Crafts → Industrial Rev |
| Fashion | Trace through style movements: Streetwear → Hip-Hop → Workwear → Military → Function-first |

## Output Requirements

1. **Artifact Profile** — the creative work and its distinctive features
2. **Full Lineage Thread** — 3+ generations with innovation at each step
3. **Innovation Map** — what was kept vs. broken at each generation
4. **Lineage Visualization** — the formatted thread with rule-break labels
5. **Transferable Patterns** — cycle length, typical innovation pattern, predicted next movement
6. **Application Brief** (optional) — how to apply the lineage to the user's own work

## Quality Gate

- Does the lineage go at LEAST 3 generations deep?
- At each generation, is the RULE BREAK identified (not just "they were influenced by")?
- Does the lineage end at a foundational shift, not just "someone earlier"?
- Is the innovation map specific enough that someone could USE it to innovate in the same tradition?
