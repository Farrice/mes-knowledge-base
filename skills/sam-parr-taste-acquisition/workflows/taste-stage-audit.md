# Taste Stage Audit

Sam Parr draws a hard line between good taste (follow the rules) and great taste (break them). Most people try to break rules before they've mastered them — the result is amateur, not innovative.

This workflow diagnoses which stage you're at and whether you've earned the right to innovate.

## Pre-Flight Gate

- Read `genius.md` — focus on Pattern 10 (Good-to-Great Progression Gate), Pattern 12 (Cover-to-Chop Progression), Exemplar 3 (Dr. Dre)
- User must provide: Domain + examples of their recent work in that domain
- Optional: Rule set from `/taste-rules`

## Execution

### Step 1: Rule Mastery Assessment
Test whether the user has internalized the fundamental rules:
- Can they name 5+ rules that govern quality in their domain? (If no → still at copy stage)
- For each rule: Can they explain WHERE it came from historically? (If no → still at rules stage)
- Can they produce work that follows ALL identified rules without reference? (If no → practice more)

### Step 2: Copy Fidelity Assessment
Evaluate the quality of their reproductions:
- Have they done formal copying (not just "studying")? How many exemplars?
- Can they reproduce a high-quality exemplar from memory? (Not perfectly, but structurally)
- Does their original work show the "texture" of their copied exemplars?

### Step 3: Rule-Following Demonstration
Ask the user to produce ONE piece that intentionally follows all their identified rules:
- No rule breaks. No personal flair that violates conventions.
- Pure "good taste" execution.
- If they can't produce this, they're NOT ready to break rules.

### Step 4: Stage Diagnosis

**Stage 1 — Discovery**: User has preferences but can't name rules
- Prescription: Return to `/taste-copy` and `/taste-rules`
- Timeline: 2-3 more months of foundation work

**Stage 2 — Good Taste**: User follows rules reliably, produces quality work within conventions
- Prescription: Continue producing rule-following work. Begin identifying WHICH rules feel constraining and WHY.
- Timeline: 1-2 months of rule-following production before attempting breaks

**Stage 3 — Transition**: User can follow all rules and has identified 1-2 they want to break with justification
- Prescription: Execute ONE controlled rule-break in a low-stakes context. Get feedback. Evaluate.
- Timeline: Individual rule-break experiments over 1-2 months

**Stage 4 — Great Taste**: User breaks rules WITH mastery — the break WORKS because convention is understood
- Prescription: You're here. Produce, innovate, teach. But continue studying new domains at Stage 1.

### Step 5: The Dre Test
Apply the Dr. Dre progression to the user's work:
- **Cover phase**: Can they produce "covers" of great work (exact reproductions)?
- **Sample phase**: Can they take elements from great work and recombine them into something recognizable but different?
- **Create phase**: Can they produce original work that FEELS like it belongs to the tradition but is genuinely new?
- Most people are at cover or early sample. Great taste is late sample / early create.

### Step 6: Progression Plan
Based on diagnosis, produce:
- Current stage with evidence
- Specific activities for leveling up
- The ONE rule to attempt breaking first (if Stage 3+)
- A quality check for the rule-break: "How do I know if my rule-break worked vs. was just sloppy?"

## Output Requirements

1. **Stage Diagnosis** — which of the 4 stages the user is at, with evidence
2. **The Dre Test Results** — cover/sample/create positioning
3. **Rule Mastery Score** — how many rules can they name, source, and follow
4. **Progression Plan** — specific activities for the next stage
5. **First Rule-Break Candidate** — if Stage 3+, which rule to break and why

## Quality Gate

This workflow is a DIAGNOSTIC, not a production tool. Quality criteria:
- Is the diagnosis honest? (Don't flatter — most people are Stage 1-2)
- Is the evidence specific? (Not "you're doing great" but "you named 7 rules and followed 5 of them")
- Is the progression plan actionable? (Exact activities, not vague encouragement)
