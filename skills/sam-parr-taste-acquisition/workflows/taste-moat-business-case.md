# Taste-as-Moat Business Case

Sam Parr's opening thesis: "Taste is the biggest moat you could possibly have." In the AI era, execution is commoditized. The differentiator is taste — the ability to make quality judgments that machines can't make. This workflow builds the economic case.

## Execution

### Step 1: AI Commoditization Audit
Map which parts of the user's business are currently being commoditized by AI:
- Content production? (AI writes/designs/creates)
- Customer service? (AI handles support)
- Data analysis? (AI processes information)
- Marketing? (AI generates ads/emails/campaigns)

For each commoditized function, ask: "If everyone has access to the same AI tools, what differentiates you?"

### Step 2: Taste Gap Analysis
In the user's industry, assess:
- How much does taste currently matter to pricing? (Commodity vs. premium — can the customer tell the difference?)
- Who in the industry is KNOWN for taste? (Name competitors who charge premium because things are "just better")
- What's the taste gap between top 10% and average players? (Is it wide or narrow?)
- What would it COST to close the taste gap? (Sam Parr's claim: 3-5 months)

### Step 3: Taste Moat Components
Identify where taste creates defensibility in this specific business:

| Taste Application | Competitive Impact | Imitation Difficulty |
|------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Product design/UX | Premium pricing, lower churn | High — taste is tacit |
| Brand language | Recognition, affinity, trust | High — voice is personal |
| Naming and positioning | Memorability, SEO, Word-of-mouth | Medium — names can be studied |
| Content quality | Engagement, authority, conversion | High — texture can't be templated |
| Hiring/curation | Team quality, culture | Very High — judgment compounds |
| Customer experience | Loyalty, NPS, referrals | High — details are infinite |

### Step 4: ROI Framework
Build the economic case:

**Investment**:
- Time: 3-5 months of systematic taste development (X hours/week)
- Resources: Books, subscriptions, tools, courses
- Opportunity cost: What else could this time be used for?

**Returns**:
- **Pricing premium**: What's the % premium taste-leaders charge in this industry? (Usually 30-300%)
- **Conversion improvement**: Better taste = better landing pages = higher conversion (quantify if possible)
- **Churn reduction**: Higher-taste products have lower churn (Apple vs. commodity)
- **Word-of-mouth coefficient**: Taste creates remarkability (people talk about things with taste)
- **Hiring advantage**: Tasteful brands attract better talent (reverse moat)

### Step 5: Competitor Taste Audit
Score 3-5 direct competitors on taste across:
- Visual design quality (1-10)
- Verbal/copy quality (1-10)
- Product/service experience quality (1-10)
- Naming and positioning quality (1-10)
- Overall coherence (1-10)

Identify where the taste gap is widest — that's the highest-leverage investment area.

### Step 6: Business Case Document
Produce the presentation-ready argument:
- The Problem: AI commoditizes execution. Everyone has the same tools.
- The Opportunity: Taste is unteachable by AI and compounds over time.
- The Evidence: [competitor audit, industry premiums, specific case studies]
- The Investment: [time, resources, roadmap from `/taste-roadmap`]
- The Returns: [pricing premium, conversion, churn, WOM, hiring]
- The Ask: Commit to 3-5 months of systematic taste development in [highest-leverage area]
