# Naming Taste Engine

Sam Parr's Swiffer insight: "The Swiffer wasn't different from any other mop. We just named it something kind of interesting." Names are disproportionately high-leverage taste applications — small investment, massive distinctiveness. This workflow applies the full 4-step taste process to naming.

## Execution

### Step 1: Naming Identity (Compressed Taste Declare)
Before generating names, clarify:
- What emotion should the name evoke? (Not "trust" — specifics like "quiet authority" or "irreverent warmth")
- What world does your audience live in? What language do they already speak?
- Who are you trying to attract vs. repel? Great names filter.
- What's the name's job: differentiation, memorability, explanation, or aspiration?

### Step 2: Copy Phase — Name Collection
Gather 50-100 names from the domain and adjacent domains:
- 20 from direct competitors/peers
- 20 from the "best in class" regardless of industry (brands, products, frameworks known for great names)
- 10 from history (classic names that endured: Kodak, Xerox, Tesla, Chanel)
- Write each one by hand. Feel the phonetics. Notice which syllables you speed through and which you linger on.

### Step 3: Pattern Mining
From the 50-100 collected, identify patterns:
- **Phonetic patterns**: Hard vs. soft consonants, syllable count, stress patterns, mouth feel
- **Semantic patterns**: Compound words, invented words, surname brands, metaphorical names, action names
- **Cultural patterns**: What languages do great names steal from? What sounds signal premium?
- Cluster the names you LIKE and the names you DON'T. Name the difference.

### Step 4: Rule Extraction for Naming
Produce 5-10 naming rules:
- Length (how many syllables?)
- Sound (which consonant/vowel patterns?)
- Meaning (literal, metaphorical, or invented?)
- Uniqueness (searchable? trademarkable?)
- Cultural resonance (what does the sound FEEL like in English? Cross-linguistically?)

### Step 5: History Layer
Research naming in the relevant tradition:
- Brand naming history: P&G systematic naming → Silicon Valley portmanteau era → DTC minimalism → modern naming agencies
- What naming convention is your industry currently IN? What comes NEXT?
- Counter-position: If everyone uses X naming convention, what's the high-taste alternative?

### Step 6: Name Generation
Produce 30-50 candidate names across categories:
- 10 names that follow ALL identified rules (good taste)
- 10 names that break ONE rule intentionally (great taste candidates)
- 10 wildcard/experimental names (push the edges)
- Score each against: Phonetic appeal, memorability, searchability, identity alignment, mouth feel

### Step 7: Final Shortlist
Present 5-7 finalists with:
- The name
- Its lineage (what tradition does this sound belong to?)
- Which rules it follows and which it breaks
- A one-sentence identity test: "Does this name say what I want to say?"

## Content Type Adaptations

| Naming Target | Specific Approach |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Product name | Prioritize memorability + phonetics; test for mouthfeel in support calls |
| Brand name | Prioritize identity alignment + cultural resonance; test for visual impact |
| Framework/method name | Prioritize clarity + acronym potential; test for "would someone Google this?" |
| Newsletter/series name | Prioritize curiosity + repeatability; test for email subject line energy |
| Business name | Prioritize domain availability + trademark; test for "can my mom pronounce it?" |

## Output Requirements

1. **Naming Identity Brief** — emotion, audience, job of the name
2. **100-Name Collection** — handwritten/typed with phonetic annotations
3. **Pattern Analysis** — naming rules extracted from collection
4. **30-50 Candidates** — across rule-following, rule-breaking, and experimental categories
5. **5-7 Finalists** — scored with lineage, rule compliance, and identity alignment

## Quality Gate

- Does the naming process START with identity, not brainstorming?
- Are names SOURCED from systematic copying, not free association?
- Can the user explain WHY each finalist works using extracted rules?
- Does at least one finalist intentionally break a convention with justification?
