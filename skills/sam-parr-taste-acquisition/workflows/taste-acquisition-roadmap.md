## Execution

### Step 1: Domain Assessment
Evaluate the user's starting position:
- Has the user done ANY systematic taste work in this domain before?
- Can they name 3 things they consider quality and explain why?
- Do they have an existing identity statement or brand direction?
- Score: 0 (blank slate), 1 (has preferences but no framework), 2 (has some framework)

### Step 1.5: Taste Transfer Inventory (NEW — Phase 0.5)

Before entering the Copy phase, map the user's EXISTING taste intelligence from other domains. Taste is not domain-locked — it runs on deeper perceptual primitives that transfer.

**5 Perceptual Primitives That Transfer Across Domains:**

| Primitive | What It Detects | Example Domains Where It's Already Trained |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Contrast Sensitivity** | When two adjacent elements create tension or relief — the gap between loud and quiet, dense and sparse, rough and polished | Music (dynamics), fashion (texture mixing), photography (light/shadow), writing (sentence length variation) |
| **Proportion Awareness** | Whether the ratio of elements feels balanced or intentionally unbalanced — the sense that something is "off" or "just right" | Architecture, typography, cooking (seasoning ratios), photography (rule of thirds), fashion (silhouette ratios) |
| **Rhythm Detection** | Recurring patterns, breaks in pattern, and the tension between expectation and surprise | Music (obviously), poetry, UI design (visual rhythm), conversation (comedic timing), fashion (pattern mixing) |
| **Coherence Intuition** | Whether all elements belong to the same world — the feeling of "this goes together" vs. "something doesn't fit" | Interior design, brand identity, playlist curation, outfit assembly, writing voice consistency |
| **Tension-Resolution Sense** | The feeling of something building, the anticipation of release, and the satisfaction (or productive dissatisfaction) of how it resolves | Storytelling, music, comedy, architecture (entryways), product design (unboxing), cooking (meal progression) |

**Execution:**

1. **Inventory existing taste domains**: Ask the user: "Where do you ALREADY have strong taste — where can you instantly tell good from great?" List all domains, even informal ones (e.g., "I can tell a good taco from a great one" = trained Contrast Sensitivity + Coherence Intuition).

2. **Map primitives to the new domain**: For each primitive the user already has trained, show how it applies to the target domain:
   - "You can hear when a song's dynamics are flat → You can see when a webpage has no visual contrast"
   - "You know when a meal has too much of one flavor → You'll feel when copy leans too hard on one rhetorical device"

3. **Identify the transfer gap**: Which primitives does the new domain demand that the user's existing taste domains DON'T train? These are the actual learning targets. The Copy phase focuses HERE — not on everything.

4. **Generate 3 "Already Trained" recognition exercises**: Quick exercises (5-10 min each) where the user applies their existing primitive to the new domain, proving to themselves they already see more than they think:
   - Show 2 examples from the new domain — one with strong contrast, one flat. Ask which has more energy and why.
   - Show 2 examples — one with coherent elements, one "almost right." Ask which feels off and locate the break.
   - Show 2 examples — one with clear rhythm/pacing, one monotonous. Ask which holds attention and why.

5. **Compress the Copy curriculum**: Based on the transfer inventory, reduce the Blind Copy Sprint:
   - **Full transfer** (3+ primitives already trained): Cut copy phase to 15 days (10 exemplars, focus on gap primitives)
   - **Partial transfer** (1-2 primitives): Cut to 20 days (12 exemplars, broader coverage)
   - **No transfer** (blank slate): Full 30-day sprint as designed

**Why this works**: Sam Parr's "texture" (Pattern 5) is the felt sense of these primitives operating. A musician entering web design already has Rhythm Detection and Contrast Sensitivity — they don't need 30 days of copying to learn what "too monotonous" feels like. They need 15 days focused on Proportion Awareness and domain-specific Coherence rules.

### Step 2: Timeline Design
Build the sprint calendar based on starting position AND transfer inventory:

**Score 0 + No Transfer — Full 5-Month Roadmap:**
- Month 1: Identity Declaration (Weeks 1-2) + Feed Purge & Exemplar Collection (Weeks 3-4)
- Month 2: Blind Copy Sprint (all 4 weeks)
- Month 3: Rule Extraction (Weeks 1-2) + Counter-Example Analysis (Weeks 3-4)
- Month 4: History Lineage Research (Weeks 1-2) + Canon Study (Weeks 3-4)
- Month 5: Integration Sprint — produce 3 original works using all accumulated taste intelligence

**Score 0 + Partial Transfer — 4-Month Roadmap:**
- Month 1: Identity Declaration (Week 1) + Transfer Inventory exercises (Week 2) + Feed Purge (Weeks 3-4)
- Month 2: Compressed Copy Sprint — 20 days focused on gap primitives (Weeks 1-3) + Rule Extraction starts (Week 4)
- Month 3: Rule Extraction completion (Week 1) + History Lineage (Weeks 2-4)
- Month 4: Integration Sprint — produce 4 original works + cross-domain synthesis piece

**Score 1 + Transfer — 2-Month Accelerated Roadmap:**
- Month 1: Transfer Inventory (Week 1) + Compressed Copy Sprint on gap primitives only (Weeks 2-3) + Rule Extraction (Week 4)
- Month 2: History Lineage (Weeks 1-2) + Integration Sprint (Weeks 3-4) — produce 5 works including 1 that deliberately bridges old and new taste domains

**Score 2 + Transfer — 4-Week Intensive:**
- Week 1: Transfer Inventory + gap-focused Copy Sprint (2 exemplars/day, gap primitives only)
- Week 2: Rule Extraction + History Lineage (parallel)
- Week 3: Integration Sprint — produce 3 original works
- Week 4: Cross-Domain Synthesis — produce 1 work that fuses taste from both domains (this is the PLAY phase — where transfer becomes creative fuel, not just acceleration)

### Step 3: Weekly Rhythm Design
For each month, define:
- **Time commitment**: Minimum hours per week (realistic, not aspirational)
- **Practice activities**: Exactly what the user does and how long
- **Reflection activities**: Texture journal, rule naming, lineage research
- **Transfer check-in**: "Which primitive clicked this week? Which still feels foreign?" (tracks acceleration)
- **Milestone**: What "done" looks like for this week

### Step 4: Resource Kit
Compile the resources needed for the full sprint:
- Books/references for the domain (from `/taste-lineage` canon)
- Tools needed (Figma for web, notebook for writing, etc.)
- Accounts to create/curate (Instagram, Pinterest, Spotify, etc.)
- Communities to join (who is having taste conversations in this domain?)
- **Transfer bridge resources**: Works that SPAN the user's existing taste domain and the new one (e.g., musician entering design → album cover art books, sound visualization projects)

### Step 5: Stage Gates
Build in decision points where the user evaluates progress:
- **After Transfer Inventory**: "Can I identify which primitives I already have and which are gaps?" (If no, do more recognition exercises)
- After Month 1/2: "Can I name 10 things I consider quality and explain why?" (If no, extend copy phase)
- After Month 2/3: "Can I articulate 5+ rules from copying?" (If no, copy more)
- After Month 3/4: "Can I trace my preferences back 3 generations?" (If no, research more)
- Final gate: "Can I produce original work that follows the rules and ONE that breaks a rule intentionally?"
- **Transfer gate** (new): "Can I produce one work that uses taste from BOTH domains simultaneously?" (This proves transfer, not just parallel development)

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content Type | Roadmap Adjustment |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Fashion | Add "wear it" phase — producing taste means wearing your choices publicly for feedback |
| Web design | Add "ship it" phase — build 3 actual sites applying taste, not just Figma files |
| Writing | Add "publish it" phase — taste means submitting to real readers for judgment |
| Brand | Add a "pitch it" phase — present the brand system to 3 people for reaction |
| **Content (premium)** | Add "curate it" phase — build a personal canon of 20 premium content pieces and explain what makes each one earn its price point |

## Output Requirements

1. **Transfer Inventory** (new) — mapped primitives, gap analysis, recognition exercise results
2. **Personalized Roadmap** — month-by-month or week-by-week plan with exact activities (compressed based on transfer)
3. **Time Budget** — total hours and weekly commitment
4. **Resource Kit** — books, tools, accounts, communities, transfer bridge resources
5. **Stage Gates** — decision points with pass/fail criteria including transfer gate
6. **Integration Goals** — what the user will PRODUCE at the end, including cross-domain synthesis piece

## Quality Gate

The roadmap itself is evaluated:
- Does it have realistic time commitments?
- Does it include ALL FOUR steps (Decide, Copy, Rules, History)?
- Does it include the Transfer Inventory (Phase 0.5) with specific primitives mapped?
- Does the timeline actually compress based on transfer findings (not just add Phase 0.5 as overhead)?
- Does it end with a production requirement (not just consumption)?
- Does it end with a cross-domain synthesis requirement (proving transfer, not just speed)?
- Does it have explicit stage gates with go/no-go criteria?
