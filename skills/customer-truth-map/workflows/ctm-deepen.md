# /ctm-deepen — Drive the Map to Identity-Level Depth

**This is how the Customer Truth Map exceeds its own source.** The expert's guide is brilliant and it *stops at the surface*: a verbatim empathy map (Say / Think / Feel / Do + Pains / Gains) with the patterns named. That's the right floor — real language, organized, never invented. But a surface map tells you *what the customer says*; it doesn't tell you *what belief makes them resist*, *what identity makes the words mean what they mean*, or *whether the real-world facts riding alongside the language are true.* This workflow drives the surface map down to identity-level depth — and it does it by **composing skills we already have, never by reimplementing them.**

Three layers get added on top of the surface map:
1. **Belief layer** — hand the map's beliefs and pains to **`/mcraney-deep-canvass`** for belief-change excavation, the resistance hierarchy, and the processing routes (how this person actually updates a belief).
2. **Posture / identity layer** — enrich with **`/consumer-posture-profile`** (consumer-posture-research skill): the occupation, activity, and thought-process identity layer that explains *why* the surface language sits where it does.
3. **Verified-claim layer** — run the **Step 5.5 Verification protocol** (`directives/verification-agent-protocol.md`) on any real-world claim that rides alongside the language (a stat, a named competitor's pricing, an event), labeling each VERIFIED / LIKELY / UNCONFIRMED.

This is the explicit **surpass move** in the skill's six-phase method: the orchestrator notes that `/ctm-deepen` "is the enrichment layer that takes the surface map down to identity-level resistance via the belief/posture stack." It composes; it does not duplicate. If you find yourself re-deriving McRaney's resistance hierarchy here, stop — call the skill.

> **Honesty spine.** The quotes in the worked thread below are tagged `[illustrative]` to teach the moves. In a real run the belief and posture layers are built *from* the harvested, word-for-word map lines — they enrich real language, they don't replace it with inference. And the verified-claim layer is the spine made literal: any real-world claim that can't be verified is labeled UNCONFIRMED, never quietly upgraded.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Load [../genius.md](../genius.md) if it is not already hot in this conversation. Do not enrich a single category before all six questions below are answered on paper. These are the Decision Framework from [../genius.md](../genius.md), scoped to the deepen job.

1. **Is there a finished surface map?** Deepen operates *on* a built map (six categories, patterns named). No map → run `/customer-truth-map` BUILD first; you can't excavate a belief you haven't surfaced.
2. **Which layer(s) does this run need?** Belief, posture, verified-claim — or all three? Add the layer the downstream job actually needs (see the "which depth layer to add when" table); don't bolt on all three reflexively.
3. **One narrow customer?** Identity-level depth blurs instantly on a broad audience; McRaney and posture both need a specific person. Confirm narrowness or split the map first.
4. **Are the pains/beliefs map-traceable?** The belief layer is only as honest as the lines it's built from — confirm every input belief points at a real harvested quote, not a remembered impression.
5. **Are there real-world claims riding along?** Stats, competitor facts, events embedded in the language. If yes, the Step 5.5 Verification protocol is mandatory before any output asserts them; if it's pure language, that layer can skip.
6. **Which output does the deep map feed?** Copy / content / positioning / offer — the belief layer feeds resistance-aware copy, the posture layer feeds identity-aware positioning, so know what's downstream.

## Execution

Three enrichment passes, composed in order. A worked example threads through — audience: **first-time homebuyers in the San Fernando Valley who keep getting outbid** (the surface map at [../references/worked-exemplar-jen-fthb.md](../references/worked-exemplar-jen-fthb.md)). The quotes below are tagged `[illustrative]`; a real run builds the layers from harvested lines.

### Step 1 — Belief layer (compose `/mcraney-deep-canvass`)
**Move.** Pull the map's THINK and PAINS — the implied beliefs and the stated problems — and hand them to `/mcraney-deep-canvass`. It returns three things the surface map cannot: the **belief** under the pain, the **resistance hierarchy** (what they'll defend hardest, in what order), and the **processing routes** (how this person actually changes a belief — through identity, evidence, story). Attach the output as a belief annotation on the relevant map patterns. **Do not derive the hierarchy yourself; call the skill and carry its result back.**

**Diagnostic:**
1. For each top pain, what belief is underneath it — and what would the customer have to *stop believing* to act?
2. What's the highest rung of the resistance hierarchy — the belief they'll defend before they defend any of the others?

**Template (vary; the belief must trace to a real map line):**
> Surface pain: *"we keep losing to all-cash offers"* `[illustrative]` → **belief (via deep-canvass):** "people like us don't get to win against investors" → **resistance rung:** identity ("I'm an outsider to this game") sits above tactics → **processing route:** a same-situation winner's story moves this belief faster than data.

### Step 2 — Posture / identity layer (compose `/consumer-posture-profile`)
**Move.** Pull the map's DO and SAY — the behaviors and the stated language — and hand them to `/consumer-posture-profile` (the consumer-posture-research skill). It returns the **occupation / activity / thought-process identity layer**: who this person is when they're *not* shopping your category, what their day's posture is, how they think before your problem even comes up. This is the layer that explains why the surface words mean what they mean. Annotate the map with the posture read. **Do not reimplement the posture method; call it and integrate the return.**

**Diagnostic:**
1. Who is this person in the hours they're *not* dealing with my category — and how does that posture shape the words they use about it?
2. Does the identity layer explain a surface pattern the empathy map left unexplained?

**Template (vary; posture enriches real language, doesn't replace it):**
> Surface DO: *"I made a spreadsheet ranking every house we lost"* `[illustrative]` (a `⚠ WORKAROUND`) → **posture:** detail-oriented, control-seeking professional applying work-self rigor to a process that won't reward it → **identity read:** the spreadsheet is an attempt to make an emotional, relationship-driven contest feel like a solvable analytical one — that mismatch is the real wound.

### Step 3 — Verified-claim layer (compose the Step 5.5 Verification protocol)
**Move.** Scan the deepened map for any **real-world claim** riding alongside the language — a statistic the customer cites, a named competitor's pricing, a market event, a "studies show." Route every one through the Step 5.5 Verification protocol (`directives/verification-agent-protocol.md`) and carry back **VERIFIED / LIKELY / UNCONFIRMED** labels. Pure language (a quote, a feeling, a wish) needs no verification — but the moment the map asserts something about the *world*, it gets labeled before anything downstream uses it. **This is the honesty spine made literal at the depth layer.**

**Diagnostic:**
1. Which lines assert a fact about the world (vs. report a feeling)? Those, and only those, go through the Step 5.5 Verification protocol.
2. Is any UNCONFIRMED claim about to be treated as load-bearing in a downstream output? (If so, flag it hard.)

**Template (vary; never upgrade a label to be convenient):**
> Riding claim: *"investors are buying 30% of homes in my zip"* `[illustrative]` → **Verification protocol (Step 5.5) → LIKELY** (directionally supported regionally; exact figure unconfirmed) → **use rule:** safe as a directional frame in copy, never as a hard stat in a claim.

## Content-Type Adaptations

This table is the workflow's "which depth layer to add when" key. Add the layer the job needs; you rarely need all three for every output.

| Downstream job | Which depth layer to add | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Objection-handling / resistance-aware copy** | Belief layer (`/mcraney-deep-canvass`) | You need the resistance hierarchy to know which belief to dissolve first. |
| **Positioning / brand / offer identity** | Posture layer (`/consumer-posture-profile`) | Positioning lands on identity; you need who they are off-category. |
| **Any claim-bearing content (stats, comparisons, "studies show")** | Verified-claim layer (Verification protocol, Step 5.5) | A wrong fact riding with real language poisons the whole map's credibility. |
| **Belief-shift long-form / deep narrative** | Belief + posture | The story has to move a defended belief *and* fit the reader's identity. |
| **High-stakes campaign or VSL** | All three | Resistance order, identity fit, and verified proof all carry weight at scale. |
| **Fast social / hooks off a vivid line** | Usually none — surface map suffices | A vivid harvested line stops the scroll on its own; don't over-engineer a tweet. |
| **Sub-group / segment play** | Posture layer | The segment *is* an identity cut; posture defines the boundary. |

## Output Requirements

Return the **depth-enriched map** — the surface map with three annotation layers integrated, plus a layer-coverage note:
1. **Surface language** — the original six-category map, real quotes intact (carried, not rewritten).
2. **Belief layer** — for the top patterns: the underlying belief, the resistance-hierarchy rung, and the processing route (sourced to `/mcraney-deep-canvass`).
3. **Posture / identity layer** — the occupation / activity / thought-process read enriching the relevant DO/SAY patterns (sourced to `/consumer-posture-profile`).
4. **Verified-claim layer** — every real-world claim labeled VERIFIED / LIKELY / UNCONFIRMED (sourced to the Step 5.5 Verification protocol), with a use-rule on any non-VERIFIED claim.
5. **Coverage note** — which layers were added and which were honestly skipped (and why), so a downstream user knows the depth they're standing on.

## Quality Gate

Score against the [../genius.md](../genius.md) rubric; name the matching anchor for any dimension ≥8 (can't name it → lower it).
- **Verbatim Integrity (the veto)** — every enrichment traces to a real surface-map line; the belief/posture layers *annotate* real language, they don't replace it with invented inference. Any fabricated or paraphrased customer quote is an automatic fail, regardless of every other score.
- **Composition Discipline** — the belief layer came from `/mcraney-deep-canvass`, the posture layer from `/consumer-posture-profile`, the claim layer from the Step 5.5 Verification protocol; nothing was reimplemented inline. Re-deriving a called skill's method caps the score.
- **Job Depth** — pains are excavated to defended beliefs and identity, not left at surface complaint; the resistance hierarchy is named and ordered.
- **Factual Grounding** — every real-world claim is labeled VERIFIED / LIKELY / UNCONFIRMED; no UNCONFIRMED claim is treated as load-bearing downstream. A real-world claim shipped unverified is a hard fail of this dimension.
- **Layer Fit** — the layers added match the downstream job (per the table); the run didn't bolt on all three when the job needed one, nor skip the claim layer when facts were riding along.

**Self-check (one line):** *Could a skeptic trace every belief and identity read back to a real harvested map line, confirm each depth layer was produced by the composed skill (not faked inline), and find every real-world claim honestly labeled?* If yes, ship. If no, the unsourced inference goes back to its layer — and any UNCONFIRMED claim that snuck into load-bearing position gets flagged before handoff.
