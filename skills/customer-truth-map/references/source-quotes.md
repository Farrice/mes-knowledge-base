# Source Quotes — Customer Truth Map (verbatim anchors)

Verbatim lines from the expert (Blazing Zebra), to keep this skill grounded in the actual source.
Video: `youtube.com/watch?v=GAVILEkfsvE` (transcript: `extractions/customer-truth-map/transcript.txt`).
PDF: "The Customer Truth Map." Use these for calibration; never paraphrase them into the method and
then claim they're the customer's — these are the *expert's* words about the method.

## The problem (the canyon, the curse, the tax)
- "It's not the pricing, it's not the funnel… it's the gap between the person selling a product and
  the person who actually needs it. Those two people are standing on opposite sides of a massive
  canyon and almost everything that we call marketing is just an attempt to throw a rope across that
  canyon."
- "We would build a campaign that we thought was super sharp… and it would land with a thud. The
  words were fine, they just didn't sound like anything that was happening inside of the customer's
  head."
- "Once you've solved a problem, you lose the ability to see it with fresh eyes… The more expert you
  become, the further that you drift from the person who's still stuck at the beginning."
- "I call it the guessing tax. You… invent what your customer is thinking. You imagine their problem
  in your language and then you answer your version of the problem instead of theirs."

## Why it beats surveys
- "The moment that you write a survey question, you've already decided what's worth asking about.
  You're already handing people your categories and you're asking them to color inside of your lines.
  You learn what you thought to ask and you miss everything else."
- "Instead of asking people to react to your questions, you go listen to them talk. This is the
  one-star review written at midnight, the frustrated thread in a forum, and an offhanded comment
  made when the customer didn't feel like they were under a microscope."

## The honesty spine (organize, never invent)
- "AI is very good at one specific job, taking large, messy piles of human language and organizing it
  into something that you can actually use. Not inventing it, organizing it. Hold on to that
  distinction because that's the whole game."
- "The customer's words are the gold and the AI is the tool that helps you sort through all of the
  different pebbles and rocks to find that gold faster than ever before."
- "The minute that you let AI make up the words instead of organizing the real ones, you've thrown
  out the entire advantage and you've gone back to guessing just with a faster guessing machine."

## Gather
- "Collect a pile of unedited real customer language… the actual sentences, typos and all. The raw
  phrasing is the part that has the selling power."
- "The number one best tool for this is Notebook LM because for some reason it can access Reddit and
  some of these other forum sites a lot better than any other AI tool."
- "Your past conversations… your sales call transcripts, support emails, DMs, posts, reviews of your
  product and competitors product. That's the customer talking directly about your specific space."

## Clean (the verbatim rule)
- "Return these sentences word for word. Do not paraphrase. Do not fix the grammar. Do not summarize.
  That single rule is the whole point."
- "If you ever notice it summarizing instead of quoting, stop it and remind it to give you the raw
  sentences."

## Build the map
- "The all-powerful empathy map created by Dave Gray and his team back in the mid-2000s… organize
  what you know about a person into a few human categories instead of a flat list."
- "Then I add on two more. These are the pains and the gains."
- "A workaround is a problem that someone cared about enough to solve it badly. And this is a flashing
  sign pointing at exactly where every existing solution, maybe including yours, is failing. So make
  sure to circle those."

## Deeper job + gaps
- "People do not really want your product. They hire it to make some progress in their life… the
  product is just a means to an end."
- "The rows with the widest gaps are your short list. Those are the problems that you lead with…
  you've just turned a pile of complaints into a ranked view of exactly where to act."

## Put to work
- "The words are already proven because a real person already said them."
- "[Give me content ideas] with a specific quote for each idea, so you know exactly what it's been
  grounded in."
- "The reason that the output of this process sounds like your customer instead of sounding just like
  this AI voice is that the AI isn't inventing anything."

## Keep it fresh
- "That phrase that worked and converted well in January can sound super dated by summer."
- "The things that change themselves are a signal… exactly the kind of insight that your competitors
  are not tracking."
- "A map that you refresh is worth 10 times the map that you build once and forget."

## Close
- "Start small, pick one customer, pick one problem, pick one community, pull 50 real sentences this
  week, and build your first map."
- Recommended reading: **The Mom Test** by Rob Fitzpatrick — "it'll teach you how to get the honest
  answers out of people instead of the flattering useless ones."
