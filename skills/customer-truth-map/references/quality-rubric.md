# Quality Rubric — The Scoring Instrument (9 criteria)

Score each criterion **1–10**. For any score **≥8, name the matching anchor below** (3 / 6 / 9). If
you can't name which anchor it clears, lower the score. The nine criteria and their order are exactly
those in [`../genius.md`](../genius.md) (§ "Quality Rubric").

---

## ⛔ THE VETO — Criterion 1 is a kill switch

**Verbatim Integrity is not just scored, it is a veto.** *Any fabricated or paraphrased customer
quote is an automatic FAIL of the entire deliverable, regardless of how high the other eight score.*
A map that scores 9/9 on everything else and contains one invented line is a failed map — it has
"thrown out the entire advantage and gone back to guessing with a faster machine." Check criterion 1
first; if it fails, stop — the composite is moot.

---

## 1. Verbatim Integrity  *(VETO criterion)*
Every quote is real, word-for-word, source-traceable; zero paraphrase or invention.

- **3 —** Quotes are present but several are smoothed, summarized, or have "fixed" grammar; some lines
  can't be traced to a source. *(Already a fail — drift this far is fabrication territory.)*
- **6 —** Quotes are mostly verbatim and sourced, but a few were lightly tidied or a couple lack a
  clear source. *Still a fail under the veto — "mostly verbatim" is not verbatim.*
- **9 —** Every line is byte-for-byte the original (typos, slang, caps preserved); each is traceable
  to a named source; brackets used only for sense, never to paraphrase. Spot-check 5/5 match.
- **VETO:** any invented or paraphrased customer line → automatic fail of the whole deliverable.

## 2. Unprompted Sourcing
Language is mostly unsolicited (reviews / threads / DMs / own-data), not survey-shaped answers.

- **3 —** The bulk of the language came from surveys or leading interview questions; the customer was
  answering *our* categories.
- **6 —** A mix; some unprompted reviews/threads but a meaningful share is interview/survey-derived.
- **9 —** ≥80% of lines are genuinely unsolicited — midnight reviews, peer-to-peer threads, DMs,
  own-data venting — captured where no one imposed a category on the speaker.

## 3. Narrowness
Target is specific enough to produce non-blurry patterns.

- **3 —** Target is a broad segment ("small business owners," "homebuyers"); patterns are generic and
  could describe anyone.
- **6 —** Target is narrowed to a role or situation but still blends two sub-audiences whose patterns
  bleed together.
- **9 —** One customer, one problem cluster — passes the "solo bookkeeper who just lost a big client"
  test; patterns are sharp and unmistakably about *this* person. Multiple audiences → separate maps.

## 4. Map Completeness
All six categories populated; 2–3 named patterns each; vivid/repeated quotes flagged.

- **3 —** Only some categories filled; it's effectively a flat list with no named patterns.
- **6 —** All six categories have quotes, but patterns are unnamed or uneven, and vivid/repeated lines
  aren't flagged.
- **9 —** Say/Think/Feel/Do + Pains/Gains all populated; 2–3 named patterns per category; the
  unusually vivid or frequently-repeated quotes are flagged as copy gold.

## 5. Do-Category Mining
Workarounds explicitly surfaced and circled as unmet-need signals.

- **3 —** The DO category is thin or missing; described workarounds went uncaptured.
- **6 —** Workarounds are present in DO but not marked or carried forward as unmet-need signals.
- **9 —** Every DIY fix / duct-tape routine in DO is circled as "a problem solved badly" and routed
  into the gap table as an unmet-need flag.

## 6. Job Depth
Pains reframed to outcome-level jobs that open positioning, not just features.

- **3 —** Pains are restated as feature requests ("they want a reminder feature").
- **6 —** Pains are reframed into JTBD format, but the outcome clauses still point at features, not
  positions.
- **9 —** Each top pain becomes `"When… I want… so I can…"` and the outcome clause opens a positioning
  angle or offer (e.g., "stay on their radar without feeling pushy"), not just a feature.

## 7. Gap Ranking
Gap table built; widest rows identified as the shortlist with a reason.

- **3 —** No gap table; pains treated as an undifferentiated list with no prioritization.
- **6 —** A `Pain/Job → Current Fix → Gap` table exists but rows aren't ranked, or "widest" is
  asserted without a reason.
- **9 —** Gap width scored and sorted; the shortlist is the widest rows *with a stated reason* for why
  each is where to lead, build, or position.

## 8. Put-to-Work Fidelity
Outputs carry the customer's voice and each is grounded in a named quote/pattern; nothing generic.

- **3 —** Outputs are generic marketing copy/ideas in *our* voice with no grounding line.
- **6 —** Outputs reference the map loosely, but some lines are paraphrased into "marketing voice" or
  lack an attached source quote.
- **9 —** Every copy slot / content idea / offer angle carries its specific source quote or pattern
  and reads in the customer's register — indistinguishable from something a real customer said.

## 9. Freshness Discipline
Dated change-log present (or scheduled); cross-source confidence labels applied where multiple sources
exist.

- **3 —** No change-log, no date, no refresh plan; single-source map presented as settled truth.
- **6 —** Dated once at build, but no refresh cadence scheduled; or multiple sources merged without
  confidence labels.
- **9 —** Dated change-log of adds/shifts present (or a refresh cadence registered via `/schedule`);
  every cross-source pattern labeled Consistent-Truth (high-confidence) vs Source-Specific (lower).

---

## Scoring procedure

1. **Run the veto first.** Score criterion 1. If it does not clear a true 8+ (every line verbatim and
   sourced), the deliverable **fails** — do not compute a composite.
2. Score criteria 2–9. For each score ≥8, **name the anchor** (e.g., "8 — clears the #4 nine-anchor:
   all six categories, named patterns, vivid flagged"). Can't name it → lower it.
3. Composite = mean of all nine. **Composite ≥ 7 and no single criterion < 6** to ship. (Criterion 1
   < 8 already failed at step 1.)
4. Below threshold → retry the weakest criterion's section once, re-score.

**One-line gate:** Real language, sorted not invented (1) · from where they talk unprompted (2) ·
about one narrow person (3) · across all six categories with named patterns (4) · workarounds circled
(5) · pains reframed to positioning-level jobs (6) · widest gaps ranked with reasons (7) · put to work
in their own voice with a quote per output (8) · dated and confidence-labeled to stay fresh (9).
