# The Customer Truth Map — Canonical Source

> **Primary source of truth.** This is a faithful rendering of the expert's own guide ("The
> Customer Truth Map," Blazing Zebra) plus the matching video (`GAVILEkfsvE`). Where any workflow
> or `genius.md` ever diverges from this doc, **the doc wins.** Verbatim prompts live in
> [prompt-library.md](prompt-library.md); verbatim quotes in [source-quotes.md](source-quotes.md).

A standalone method for capturing how your customers actually describe their problems, organizing
it into one working map, and using that map to sharpen everything you write and offer.

**Stop guessing what to write.** Most of us build copy, content, and offers on what we *assume*
customers think, then wonder why the words fall flat. The Customer Truth Map replaces the
guesswork with the customer's own language. You gather real words from where customers already
talk, organize them into a single map of what they say / think / feel / do (plus pains and gains),
then use that map to shape messaging, content, positioning, and offers.

**Not a one-time exercise.** The way people describe their problems is shifting fast; the exact
phrases they use this month carry more selling power than anything written a year ago. The map is
a **living document** — build it once, refresh it often, so your words always match the language
customers are using *today*.

---

## Why This Matters
There is a quiet tax most entrepreneurs pay without noticing — the **guessing tax**. You sit to
write, and the first thing you do (usually without noticing) is invent what the customer is
thinking, in your words, then answer your version of the problem. The copy is clear, grammatical,
and slides right past the reader because it doesn't sound like anything happening in their head.

The customer's real language is hard to reach because it's scattered: a Reddit thread at midnight,
a one-star review written in frustration, an offhand comment on a sales call, a support email you
skimmed and forgot. No single one is a strategy. Together they are the most valuable marketing
asset you have, and almost nobody collects them on purpose.

Write from real language instead of imagined language and three things change: copy starts to
sound like the reader's own thoughts (instant trust); content lands on problems people genuinely
have; offers sharpen because you can see the gap between what people want and what they can get.

Worth doing **now**, and **often**, because the words are moving — new tools, fears, and
possibilities change how people talk month to month. A static brand-voice document can't keep up.
A map you refresh can.

> **Note from the video — why this beats surveys.** The old gold standard for voice-of-customer was
> expensive, slow surveys. The flaw was baked in: *the moment you write a survey question, you've
> already decided what's worth asking about — you hand people your categories and ask them to color
> inside your lines. You learn what you thought to ask and miss everything else.* Listening to
> unprompted talk produces sharper results, faster, for almost nothing.

## The Map in One Picture
**Left → Middle → Right.**
- **Left — Raw customer language** (where they already talk, unprompted): Reddit & forum threads ·
  reviews (yours & rivals') · sales & discovery calls · support tickets & emails · social comments & DMs.
- **Middle — The Customer Truth Map** (organized truth, one file you keep and refresh): their real
  words sorted into **Say · Think · Feel · Do** and **Pains · Gains**.
- **Right — What you make** (sharper output): sales & landing copy · emails & outreach · content &
  posts · positioning angles · offer & feature ideas.

Build the middle well and the left becomes a repeatable routine while the right becomes almost
easy — you're no longer starting from a blank page, but from a page full of your customer's words.

## Setup: What You Need
1. **A capable AI assistant.** Any frontier chat tool does the analysis. A few steps go faster if it
   can browse the web or read uploaded documents — flagged honestly where a feature helps and where
   it doesn't.
2. **A place to keep the map.** A single plain-text/markdown file (e.g., `customer-truth-map.md`),
   kept somewhere you'll actually open it.
3. **A specific customer in mind.** The map weakens the broader the target. "Small business owners"
   is too wide; "solo bookkeepers who just lost a big client" is the sharpness that produces a useful
   map. Serve several customers → build a separate map for each.

Pace it: a narrow first pass with a few good sources is quick; a thorough map across communities
takes real sitting time. **Stop when the map stops surprising you.**

## Phase 1 — Gather Raw Customer Language
Goal: a pile of **unedited, real** customer language — the actual sentences, typos and all. Raw
phrasing is the gold; that's what you'll feed back to people in your copy.

- **Step 1 — Name the problems worth researching.** Have AI list 15–20 distinct problems/
  frustrations/fears phrased the way the customer would say them *to a friend*, not in marketing
  language; then pick the 2–3 most worth researching deeply. Depth on a few beats a thin sweep.
- **Step 2 — Find where they talk.** Have AI list specific subreddits, forums, Facebook groups,
  review sites, YouTube comment sections, niche communities — with what language/candor to expect.
  Don't forget your own richest source: past sales/discovery calls, support tickets/emails, comments/
  DMs on your posts, and reviews of your product *and competitors'.*
- **Step 3 — Collect the actual words.** Honest about AI's limits here:
  - **Reliable manual method** — open threads/reviews/comments yourself, copy blocks of real
    conversation (not single lines) into a doc. Low-tech, always works; an hour beats most automation.
  - **NotebookLM (the expert's pick for gathering)** — add the specific Reddit threads/review pages as
    web links or paste text; it answers *only* from sources you give it and cites the exact lines, so
    it won't invent a quote nobody wrote. Ask it to pull every sentence describing a problem,
    frustration, or wish — and check its citations. Limit: it works only from sources you hand it.
  - **Purpose-built Reddit tools, if you do this often** — the category churns (Reddit tightened data
    access; **GummySearch shut down**), so search for what's currently active before paying. **F5Bot**
    is a free keyword-alert option worth knowing.
  - However you collect, **keep the raw text** for the next phase. *Transition note: NotebookLM is
    great for gathering, but drafting/reorganizing is better in a normal chat window — move the work
    there once your quotes are pulled.*

---

## Phase 2 — Clean the Noise into Signal
Raw community text is mostly noise (jokes, tangents, arguments, "thanks for sharing"). Strip it to
signal while keeping the original wording intact. **Work in chunks** (~a few thousand words at a
time — smaller chunks extract cleaner). For each chunk, ask AI to extract only the sentences that
reveal thoughts, feelings, actions, experiences (pains, desired outcomes, emotional reactions), under
three rules: **(1) return the original sentences word-for-word — no paraphrase, summary, or grammar
fixes; (2) drop chitchat/jokes/off-topic; (3) if a gold quote needs a few words to make sense, add
them in brackets.** The verbatim instruction matters more than anything else: the moment AI
paraphrases, you lose the exact phrasing that makes this worth doing. If you notice it summarizing,
stop and re-issue the rule. Collect all extractions into one running list — the raw ore.

---

## Phase 3 — Build the Customer Truth Map
The heart of the system, built on the **Empathy Map** (Dave Gray + XPLANE, mid-2000s, popularized in
*Gamestorming*, 2010). Our twist: keep the categories, fill them **only with real, sourced quotes**,
and add an explicit **pains/gains** split so the opportunity is visible. Feed the cleaned list to AI
and sort every quote (a quote may appear in more than one category) into:
- **SAY** — stated out loud / in writing (direct quotes)
- **THINK** — beliefs/assumptions implied but not said directly
- **FEEL** — emotions, frustrations, hopes, fears
- **DO** — actions and behaviors, **including workarounds and DIY fixes they cobbled together**
- **PAINS** — specific problems, obstacles, risks
- **GAINS** — outcomes, wins, "if only" wishes

After sorting, write a short summary under each category naming the **2–3 strongest patterns**, and
flag any quote that is unusually **vivid or repeated** (most useful in copy). Save the result as your
Customer Truth Map — the document you keep open and reuse.

> **The "Do" category hides the best material.** A workaround — the spreadsheet, the manual checklist,
> the duct-tape routine — is *a problem someone cared about enough to solve badly*, a flashing sign at
> exactly where existing solutions (maybe yours) fail. **Circle those.**

## Phase 4 — Find the Deeper Job and the Gaps
A map of quotes tells you *how* customers talk; this phase tells you *what to do about it.*

- **Step 1 — Reframe pains into jobs (Jobs-to-be-Done).** People don't want your product; they
  "hire" it to make progress. Underneath every pain is a job. Rewrite each pain as:
  `"When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [desired outcome]."`
  *Example:* "I keep forgetting to follow up with leads" → "When a promising lead goes quiet, I want
  to stay on their radar without feeling pushy, so I can win the work without nagging." Then, per job,
  suggest one or two angles you might not have considered. Jobs point at outcomes, where bigger,
  more durable ideas live — a reminder feature solves "I keep forgetting"; "stay on their radar
  without feeling pushy" is a whole positioning angle, maybe a whole offer.
- **Step 2 — Map the gaps.** Build a 3-column table: **Pain/Job** → **Current Fix** (how they handle
  it today, incl. competitor products and DIY workarounds) → **The Gap** (where the fix falls short /
  frustrates / leaves them wanting). Focus on the **widest-gap rows** — they're your shortlist: which
  problems to lead with in copy, which topics to build content around, where a new/repositioned offer
  meets real demand. You've turned a pile of complaints into a ranked view of where to act.

## Phase 5 — Put the Map to Work
One well-built map feeds almost everything you produce. Keep the map in a file, paste it where a
prompt calls for it, run one at a time. (Left out on purpose: impressive-sounding moves that don't
hold up — e.g., redesigning UX from quotes alone or predicting conversion rates. Those need real
testing and real eyes, not a prompt.)
- **For copy** — pull the 10 quotes most powerful to use (lightly edited) and assign each a slot:
  headline / subhead / objection-handling line / testimonial-style proof point, keeping the customer's
  voice. Plus: from FEEL + PAINS, write 8 headline options in the customer's own words and emotional
  register; avoid jargon and hype.
- **For content** — 15 content ideas (posts/emails/short videos) that speak to problems the customer
  actually raised, **each with the specific quote or pattern it's built on**. Plus: take the single
  widest-gap row and outline one long-form piece that names the problem in the customer's words,
  validates the frustration with their current fix, and points toward a better way of thinking.
- **For positioning & offers** — draft 3–5 sharp positioning angles, each aimed at a specific
  underserved gap, with one sentence you could say to a prospect to make them feel understood. Plus:
  suggest 3 ways to adjust/extend the offer to close a gap customers clearly care about, noting the
  exact pain/wish each responds to and which are simple vs. a major undertaking.

The thread through all of it: **the AI is not inventing anything — it's reorganizing your customer's
real words into the shape you need.** That's why the output sounds like your customer, not like AI.

---

## Phase 6 — Triangulate and Keep It Fresh
- **Triangulate.** A map from one community can mislead — every forum has its culture, loud voices,
  blind spots. Build from several sources, then merge: separate **CONSISTENT TRUTHS** (patterns/
  language across most/all sources → high-confidence, build core messaging here) from
  **SOURCE-SPECIFIC** (one source only → lower-confidence, hold loosely, note which source; useful for
  reaching a sub-group).
- **Keep it fresh** (the part most people skip, and it matters most). **Quarterly:** a short session
  pulling recent quotes from 1–2 best sources, watching for new phrasing/worries/wishes. **1–2× a year
  (or on a big market shift):** a deeper rebuild on fresh material; compare new map to old — *what
  changed is itself a signal* about where the customer's world is moving (insight competitors aren't
  tracking). **A dated log** at the top of the map of what you last added and what shifted becomes its
  own asset over time. **A map you refresh is worth ten times a map you build once and forget. The
  freshness is the edge.**
