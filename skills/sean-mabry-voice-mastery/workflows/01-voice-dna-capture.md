# Voice DNA Capture

> End-to-end voice capture from raw source material (voice memos, podcasts, interviews) to deployable Voice DNA Document for ghostwriting production.

## When to Use

- Onboarding a new ghostwriting client for Authority Flywheel or long-form content
- Starting any engagement where you'll write in someone else's voice
- Building a voice profile from voice memos, podcast appearances, or interview transcripts

## Required Input

1. **Source material** — Voice memos, podcast transcripts, interview recordings, or written content (minimum 3 pieces, ideally 5+). Voice/audio sources are highest value.
2. **Client niche** — Industry, audience tier, voice sensitivity level
3. **Production target** — What you'll be producing (LinkedIn posts, newsletter, book chapters, email sequences)

## Execution

### Phase 1 — Content Saturation & Surface Layer

Analyze all source material. Document:

**Speech Patterns**
- Average sentence length + variation range
- Punctuation habits (em dashes, ellipses, exclamation frequency)
- Signature phrases (repeated across multiple sources)
- Words conspicuously absent
- Sign-off and transition patterns

**Energy Signature**
- Primary register (motivational / contemplative / irreverent / authoritative / conversational)
- Pace (rapid-fire / measured / rambling-then-punchy)
- Formality gradient (where on the academic-to-casual spectrum)
- Emotional warmth level (guarded / open / selectively vulnerable)

### Phase 2 — Controversy Line Map

Map every expressed opinion into three zones:

| Zone | Definition | Content Usage |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| **Flag-plant** | Core identity. They go hard. Repeated across content. | Hooks, bold openings, polarizing takes |
| **Nuanced** | Engage carefully with caveats and conditions | Depth pieces, educational content |
| **No-go** | Topics conspicuously absent from all content | Never touch regardless of engagement potential |

### Phase 3 — Value Hierarchy + Story Bank

**Value Hierarchy** (rank 1-5, most fundamental to most contextual):
1. Core belief — never changes
2. Strong conviction — rarely compromised
3. Operating principle — guides decisions
4. Current emphasis — may shift
5. Contextual stance — depends on audience/timing

**Story Bank** — Two categories:
- **Their Picks**: Stories they deliberately tell
- **Hidden Gems**: Stories told casually that reveal character, contain concrete detail, or made the listener react. Target 2:1 ratio (your finds to their suggestions).

### Phase 4 — Cognitive Architecture Mapping

> This is the layer that separates voice capture from voice MASTERY. Surface style is what they sound like. Cognitive architecture is how they THINK — and thinking is what makes voice irreplaceable.

Analyze source material for three structural dimensions:

#### 4A — Argument Construction Pattern

Identify the client's characteristic reasoning sequence. Everyone has a default architecture:

| Pattern | Description | Signal in Source Material |
|---------|------------|--------------------------|
| **Principle-First** | States the rule, then illustrates with example | "Here's the thing... [principle]. Let me give you an example..." |
| **Story-to-Lesson** | Tells the story, lets the lesson emerge | Begins with "So last week..." or "I had a client who..." before the point arrives |
| **Challenge-then-Reframe** | Names the common assumption, demolishes it, offers alternative | "Everyone thinks X. But here's what actually happens..." |
| **Analogy Bridge** | Explains complex ideas by mapping to familiar domains | "It's like..." or borrows from sports, cooking, music, parenting |
| **Diagnostic-then-Prescriptive** | Identifies what's broken, then gives the fix | "The problem is... Here's what I'd do instead..." |
| **Accumulation** | Stacks small observations until they reach a tipping point | Multiple short examples before the synthesis |

**Document**: Their PRIMARY pattern (used 60%+ of the time) and SECONDARY pattern. Note: most people oscillate between two. The ratio IS the fingerprint.

#### 4B — Humor Deployment Logic

Humor is not random. Map when, how, and why they deploy it:

- **Humor Type**: Self-deprecating / Observational / Absurdist / Deadpan / Exaggeration / Callback
- **Trigger Context**: When do they go funny? (After making a serious point? To defuse tension? When the topic gets too abstract? As a bridge between ideas?)
- **Recovery Pattern**: What happens AFTER the humor? (Return to serious immediately? Let it hang? Use it as a springboard to the next point?)
- **Frequency**: Rare and strategic vs. constant seasoning
- **Boundaries**: What they NEVER joke about (often reveals deepest values)

**Document**: The humor-seriousness rhythm. Some people go serious-serious-funny. Others go funny-serious-funny-serious. The PATTERN is the fingerprint.

#### 4C — Vulnerability Architecture

How someone reveals personal information is as distinctive as a fingerprint:

- **Depth Gradient**: How deep do they go? (Surface anecdotes / Admitted mistakes / Emotional exposure / Core wound territory)
- **Entry Pattern**: How do they ENTER vulnerability? (Suddenly? With a disclaimer? Through someone else's story first? Only when prompted?)
- **Trigger Topics**: What subjects crack them open? (Family? Early career failure? Client transformations? Their own limitations?)
- **Recovery Move**: How do they EXIT vulnerability? (Quick pivot to action? "Anyway..." dismissal? Let it sit? Reframe as strength?)
- **Boundary Markers**: Where do they STOP? (The line between "authentic share" and "too far" — this is as important as the Controversy Map no-go zone)

**Document**: The vulnerability-to-authority ratio. Some people use vulnerability as the ON-RAMP to authority. Others use authority as the FRAME for vulnerability. The architecture determines which.

### Phase 5 — Prediction Generation

Generate 10 prediction scenarios using the full cognitive architecture:

> "If [client] were asked about [topic they haven't addressed], they would:"
> - **Open with**: [Based on their argument construction pattern]
> - **Navigate tension by**: [Based on their humor deployment logic]
> - **Go personal at this point**: [Based on their vulnerability architecture]
> - **Land on**: [Based on their value hierarchy]
> - **Controversy zone**: [Flag-plant / Nuanced / No-go]
> - **Confidence**: [High / Medium / Low]

### Phase 6 — Voice DNA Document Assembly

Compile everything into a deployable Voice DNA Document:

1. **Voice Identity Summary** (3-5 sentences — how they sound AND how they think)
2. **Surface Layer** (speech patterns, energy signature)
3. **Controversy Line Map** (three-zone)
4. **Value Hierarchy** (ranked 1-5)
5. **Story Bank** (Their Picks + Hidden Gems)
6. **Cognitive Architecture Profile**:
   - Argument Pattern: [Primary] / [Secondary] at [ratio]
   - Humor Logic: [Type] deployed [when], recovered [how]
   - Vulnerability Architecture: [Depth] / [Entry] / [Recovery] / [Boundaries]
7. **10 Predictions** (using full architecture)
8. **Voice Don'ts** (what breaks fidelity at EVERY layer — surface, belief, AND cognitive)
9. **Calibration Plan** (4-week timeline with cognitive architecture practice milestones)

## Output Schema

Single deployable **Voice DNA Document** (markdown, one file per client) containing exactly the nine numbered sections built in Phase 6, in that order: (1) Voice Identity Summary — 3-5 sentences; (2) Surface Layer — speech patterns + energy signature as bulleted sub-fields; (3) Controversy Line Map — three-row table (Flag-plant / Nuanced / No-go) with at least one real example per zone; (4) Value Hierarchy — ranked list, 5 entries, most-fundamental to most-contextual; (5) Story Bank — two labeled sub-lists, "Their Picks" and "Hidden Gems," Hidden Gems entries outnumbering Their Picks at the target 2:1 ratio or the gap is named; (6) Cognitive Architecture Profile — three fields (Argument Pattern with Primary/Secondary and a ratio like "70/30," Humor Logic, Vulnerability Architecture), each populated from Phase 4, never left as the template placeholder; (7) 10 Predictions — each following the Phase 5 six-line block (Open with / Navigate tension by / Go personal at / Land on / Controversy zone / Confidence) with no field skipped; (8) Voice Don'ts — a bulleted list covering surface, belief, and cognitive breaks (minimum one item per layer); (9) Calibration Plan — a 4-week timeline with a named milestone per week. If source material is thin (<3 pieces), the document still ships all nine sections but Section 6 and its dependent Section 7 predictions are marked "provisional — confirm after first review call" per Creative Latitude below, never silently omitted.

## Quality Gate

The Voice DNA Document passes when:
- Predictions use the client's argument construction pattern (not just their vocabulary)
- Humor placement follows their actual deployment logic (not generic comedic timing)
- Vulnerability depth matches their architecture (not deeper or shallower than they'd go)
- A reader familiar with the client would recognize the THINKING, not just the tone

**The test**: Could you swap this voice profile's surface layer (vocabulary, rhythm) with someone else's — and still identify the original client from the cognitive architecture alone? If yes, you've captured voice DNA. If no, you've captured style, not voice.

## Creative Latitude

- For voice memo sources: prioritize cognitive architecture analysis (memos reveal thinking patterns more purely than polished written content)
- If only written content is available: note that humor and vulnerability patterns may be performative — flag for live interview confirmation
- For high-sensitivity niches: spend 60% of analysis time on Phase 4 (cognitive architecture), 40% on Phases 1-3
- Minimum viable capture: If source material is thin (< 3 pieces), produce Phase 1-3 only and mark Phase 4 as "provisional — confirm after first review call"
