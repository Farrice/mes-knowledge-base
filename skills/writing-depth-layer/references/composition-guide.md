# Composition Guide

The depth layer is not a writer. It is a conductor. It does not teach a draft how to write a vivid detail or compress a sentence — the owner skills already do that better than any summary could. What the conductor does is harder and rarer: it reads a finished-feeling draft, names the one or two places where *depth* is actually missing, then composes the existing craft experts in the legal order and the right dose, and hands back a deepened draft plus a change-map called a Depth Receipt.

This file exists so the move is concrete, not theoretical. Three worked examples follow, end to end — flat draft, diagnosis against the 8 deficits, the ordered chain, the deepened prose, the Receipt. One is social (light and fast), one is converting copy (depth without losing the sale), one is a book paragraph (architecture first). Read them as proof that the layer can deepen any draft across verticals while obeying the ordering law and the anti-duplication contract. The prose in the before/after blocks is invented but plausible; treat it as a calibration sample for what "deepened, not lengthened" actually looks like on the page.

A reminder that governs all three: **deepen is not lengthen.** Two of the three examples below end *shorter* than they began. The conductor's most common move is the cut.

---

## How to read each worked example

Every example runs the canonical **Deepen Loop** in order:

1. **DIAGNOSE** — score the draft against the 8 deficits, name the 1–3 weakest links. Diagnose before treating; never refine slop on a misdiagnosed draft.
2. **SELECT + ORDER** — pick only the owners for the *confirmed* deficits, sequence them by the ordering law (architecture → scene → line → truth/voice), set the dose by vertical.
3. **APPLY** — load each owner's `genius.md` and the named workflow, apply the move *into* the prose, preserve the writer's core meaning and voice. No expert names leak onto the page.
4. **RECEIPT** — end with the Depth Receipt (weakest link, moves applied, dose/vertical fit, remaining risk).

> **🔒 Ordering Gate**: In every example below, architecture is treated FIRST and truth/voice LAST. If you ever find a worked chain that runs line-craft before it has confirmed the piece has a spine, you are looking at well-crafted sentences with no reason to exist. Stop and re-order. Architecture first, always.

## Example 1 — Social (LinkedIn): light and fast, fix 1–2 deficits, preserve the hook

**Vertical**: SOCIAL. Dose: LIGHT. The shape, brevity, scannability, and hook stay intact. We do NOT turn a 150-word post into an essay. Truth slot, if needed, is `/really-real-social` only.

### The flat draft (before)

> Most people think consistency is the key to growth on LinkedIn.
>
> But here's the truth: consistency without strategy is just noise.
>
> I used to post every single day. I was showing up. I was doing the work. But nothing was happening. My engagement was flat and my following wasn't growing.
>
> Then I realized something important. It's not about how often you post. It's about whether what you post actually matters to your audience.
>
> So I stopped chasing volume and started focusing on value. And everything changed.
>
> The lesson? Quality over quantity. Always.
>
> What's been your experience? Let me know in the comments.

### DIAGNOSE (score the 8 deficits)

| # | Deficit | Present? | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No architecture | Partial | There *is* a spine (consistency ≠ growth) but it's stated as a slogan, not earned. Acceptable for social; not the weakest link. |
| 2 | Hollow / generic | **YES — weakest** | "I was showing up. I was doing the work. But nothing was happening." Could be anyone. No concrete stakes, no number, no image. The whole middle is abstract. |
| 3 | Emotionally unearned | Partial | "Everything changed" asserts a transformation it never shows. Flatness, not melodrama. |
| 4 | No signature voice | Mild | Reads like every growth post. But voice is downstream of specificity here — fix #2 first and voice partly returns. |
| 5 | Over-explained / bloated | **YES** | "It's not about how often you post. It's about whether what you post actually matters." restates the hook. "Quality over quantity. Always." restates it a third time. The post says its one idea three times. |
| 6 | Weak rhythm | Mild | Tolerable. Short lines already. Not worth a pass on a post this size. |
| 7 | Missing telling detail | **YES (= cause of #2)** | Zero concrete image. This is the *mechanism* behind the hollowness. |
| 8 | No reader trust | **YES** | "What's been your experience? Let me know in the comments." — a cheap question signoff that throat-clears the close. Banned move. |

**Weakest links named (max for social): #2 Hollow/generic (via #7 missing telling detail) and #8 reader trust (the limp close).** We will NOT chase rhythm or full voice reconstruction — over-deepening a short post is itself an anti-pattern.

### SELECT + ORDER

The spine is fine, so we do not call Hawley — there's nothing architecturally broken to fix, and adding architecture to a working hook would bloat it. The legal order for the confirmed deficits:

1. **Scene / telling detail** (deficit #2 via #7) → Connelly `/telling-detail-engine`: replace the abstract middle with ONE concrete particular that reveals character *and* situation. One, not five.
2. **Compression** (deficit #5) → Cole `/atomic-compression-density-audit`: cut the two restatements; the idea earns its keep by being said once, well.
3. **Truth + close** (deficit #8) → `/really-real-social` (the social truth slot): kill the cheap question, land on an image/declaration that lets the reader be intelligent.

Dose held LIGHT: three surgical moves, no architecture pass, post stays under ~120 words and keeps its hook-first shape.

### The deepened draft (after)

> Most people think consistency is the key to growth on LinkedIn.
>
> Consistency without strategy is just louder noise.
>
> For four months I posted every morning at 6am before my kids woke up. 122 posts. My following went from 1,400 to 1,460. I have the screenshot of the flat line; it looks like a heart monitor on a body that's already gone.
>
> The posts weren't bad. They just weren't *for* anyone. I was writing to the algorithm instead of to a person.
>
> The day I wrote one post for one specific freelancer who couldn't price her work, it outperformed the previous month combined.
>
> Sixty posts shouting past everyone lost to one post that found someone.

### Depth Receipt

- **Weakest link found**: Hollow / generic (deficit #2), caused by a total absence of telling detail (deficit #7); secondary, the cheap-question close (deficit #8).
- **Moves applied**:
  - Hollow/generic → swapped the abstract "I was doing the work, nothing happened" for one concrete particular (122 posts, 1,400→1,460, the 6am screenshot that "looks like a heart monitor") → reader now *sees* the failure instead of being told about it → Connelly telling-detail (one image doing the work of a paragraph).
  - Over-explained → cut both restatements of the thesis ("It's not about how often… it's about whether…" and "Quality over quantity. Always.") → the idea lands once and hits harder; the post drops ~30 words → Cole compression / redundancy elimination.
  - No reader trust → deleted "What's been your experience? Let me know in the comments." and closed on a declarative image ("Sixty posts shouting past everyone lost to one post that found someone.") → reader leaves with a felt line, not a homework prompt → really-real reader-trust + social truth slot (`/really-real-social`).
- **Dose / vertical fit**: LIGHT, as social demands. Hook preserved verbatim. No architecture pass (spine was already sound; adding one would bloat a 100-word post). Stayed scannable and platform-native; the deepened version is *shorter*, not longer.
- **Remaining risk**: The "1,400 to 1,460" specifics are invented — if used for a real person they must be true or replaced with their real numbers, or the post commits emotional fraud. The "heart monitor" image is one beat from melodrama; if the writer's voice runs drier, soften it. No truth-slot trauma was manufactured — the failure shown is ordinary and real-sized.

## Example 2 — Converting copy (email/landing): depth WITHOUT losing the sale

**Vertical**: COPY. Dose: conviction, specificity, and voice added; offer logic, proof, and clarity-to-action stay intact. Literary flourish never beats clarity-to-action. Truth slot is `/really-real-marketing`.

### The flat draft (before)

> **Stop Wasting Time on Manual Bookkeeping**
>
> Are you a small business owner who's tired of spending hours every week on bookkeeping? You're not alone. Studies show that small business owners spend up to 120 hours a year on financial admin.
>
> That's where LedgerFlow comes in. Our powerful, all-in-one platform automates your bookkeeping so you can focus on what really matters: growing your business.
>
> With LedgerFlow, you get:
> - Automated transaction categorization
> - Real-time financial reporting
> - Seamless integration with your bank
> - 24/7 customer support
>
> Join thousands of happy customers who have transformed their business with LedgerFlow.
>
> Sign up today and get your first month free!

### DIAGNOSE (score the 8 deficits)

| # | Deficit | Present? | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No architecture | Partial | Problem → product → features → CTA. The skeleton works; it's not the weak link. We protect it. |
| 2 | Hollow / generic | **YES — weakest** | "focus on what really matters: growing your business" and "thousands of happy customers" are interchangeable across every SaaS site. The pain ("tired of spending hours") is named but never *felt*. |
| 3 | Emotionally unearned | **YES** | "transformed their business" asserts a feeling the copy hasn't earned. Performed empathy ("You're not alone") instead of real recognition. |
| 5 | Over-explained / bloated | Mild | "powerful, all-in-one platform" — adjectives that prove nothing. Some cuttable filler, but copy can't go too lean or it stops selling. |
| 8 | No reader trust | **YES** | The 120-hours stat floats with no source; "thousands of happy customers" is unfalsifiable. The reader's skepticism reflex fires. In copy, vague proof *reduces* conversion. |

Other deficits (4 voice, 6 rhythm, 7 telling detail) are present but secondary — telling detail is the *lever* that fixes #2 and #3 at once.

**Weakest links named: #2 Hollow/generic and #8 reader trust — fixed primarily via a telling detail (#7) and concrete, falsifiable proof.** The constraint that governs everything: do NOT sand off the offer, the bullets, or the CTA.

### SELECT + ORDER

Architecture stays (Hawley not called — the funnel logic is sound and re-architecting copy that converts is reckless). Legal order:

1. **Scene / telling detail** (deficit #2, #3) → Connelly `/telling-detail-engine`: replace abstract pain with one concrete moment the ICP recognizes from their own Sunday night.
2. **Conviction reframe** (deficit #3, generic value) → Rory Sutherland `/conspiratorial-reframe-engine`: reframe the boring category ("bookkeeping software") into a felt human want, conspiratorially, without overpromising.
3. **Compression, lightly** (deficit #5) → Cole `/atomic-compression-density-audit`: cut empty adjectives ("powerful, all-in-one") — but keep the bullets and CTA fully intact.
4. **Truth + honest proof** (deficit #8) → `/really-real-marketing` (the marketing truth slot): make the proof falsifiable and the offer honest enough to believe; keep clarity-to-action untouched.

Dose: depth serves conversion. Every move is justified by "does this make the reader more likely to believe, then act?" If a literary move would soften the CTA, it does not ship.

### The deepened draft (after)

> **You didn't start a business to spend Sunday night in a spreadsheet**
>
> It's 9:40 on a Sunday. The kids are down. You open the laptop "just to reconcile last week" and it's 11:15 before you look up, three receipts still unaccounted for, the same knot in your stomach you've had since March. You are very good at your actual job. This is not your actual job.
>
> LedgerFlow does the part you keep doing at night. It categorizes every transaction automatically, reconciles against your bank in real time, and hands you a clean P&L you can read in the time it takes to drink a coffee.
>
> - Automated transaction categorization
> - Real-time financial reporting
> - Seamless integration with your bank
> - 24/7 customer support
>
> We won't say "thousands of happy customers." We'll say this: the average LedgerFlow user closes their books in 9 minutes a month instead of 9 hours — and you can check that number against your own first month, free, before you pay us a cent.
>
> **Start your free month →**

### Depth Receipt

- **Weakest link found**: Hollow / generic (deficit #2) and no reader trust (deficit #8) — the pain was named but not felt, and the proof was unfalsifiable.
- **Moves applied**:
  - Hollow/generic + unearned emotion → replaced "tired of spending hours every week" with one concrete scene (9:40 Sunday, kids down, 11:15 before you look up, the knot since March) → the ICP recognizes their own life instead of reading a category cliché; emotion is now *earned by specificity*, not asserted → Connelly telling-detail.
  - Generic category value → reframed "automate your bookkeeping so you can focus on growing your business" into "the part you keep doing at night" / "This is not your actual job" → shifts the felt frame from feature to identity-relief without overpromising → Rory Sutherland conspiratorial reframe.
  - Bloat → cut "powerful, all-in-one platform" and the empty "transformed their business" → tighter, more credible; bullets + CTA preserved verbatim → Cole compression.
  - No reader trust → swapped "thousands of happy customers" (unfalsifiable) and the floating 120-hours stat for a falsifiable, checkable claim ("9 minutes instead of 9 hours… check it against your own free month") → the proof now *invites* verification, which raises belief and conversion → really-real-marketing truth slot.
- **Dose / vertical fit**: COPY dose. Offer logic intact (free first month), feature bullets untouched, single clear CTA preserved and made *more* prominent. Depth was added only where it increases belief-then-action; no literary flourish was allowed to soften the ask. The "9 min / 9 hours" claim is positioned as user-verifiable rather than as a hype stat, which is both more honest and more persuasive.
- **Remaining risk**: The "9 minutes a month" and "average user" claims are invented placeholders — they MUST be backed by real product data or replaced before shipping, or the honesty move backfires into a bigger lie than the original vague one. The Sunday-night scene assumes a specific ICP (owner-operator with kids); if the real ICP is a 50-person firm's office manager, the scene must be re-cast to their recognized moment. Conversion is preserved on the page but should still be A/B-tested against the original control before declaring the deepen a win.

## Example 3 — Book / long-form (memoir paragraph): architecture FIRST, full stack

**Vertical**: BOOK. Dose: full, layered. Architecture (Hawley) matters MOST here — a flat memoir paragraph usually has no spine, only events. Scene = Connelly/Roth. Truth slot is `/really-real-book`. This is the example where the ordering law does the heaviest lifting.

### The flat draft (before)

> My father was a difficult man. He worked at the steel mill for thirty-two years and he never really talked about his feelings. When I was a kid, I always wanted him to be proud of me, but he rarely said anything nice. I remember he would come home from work tired and just sit in his chair and watch TV. We didn't have a close relationship. It wasn't until I was much older that I started to understand him a little better and realize that he probably had his own struggles that he never shared. Looking back now, I wish we had been able to connect more. There's a lot I never got to say to him before he passed away, and that's something I'll always regret.

### DIAGNOSE (score the 8 deficits)

| # | Deficit | Present? | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No architecture | **YES — weakest, and the root** | This is a list of facts about a father (mill, 32 years, quiet, the chair, regret) with no question underneath them, no theme organizing the selection. It's a summary of a relationship, not a piece *about* anything. Everything else is downstream of this. |
| 2 | Hollow / generic | **YES** | "difficult man," "never talked about his feelings," "his own struggles," "I wish we had connected more" — every phrase is the generic memoir register. No image owns the page. |
| 3 | Emotionally unearned | **YES** | "that's something I'll always regret" *states* the grief the paragraph never makes us feel. Telling, not earning. |
| 5 | Over-explained / bloated | **YES** | "It wasn't until I was much older that I started to understand him a little better and realize that he probably had his own struggles" — pure summary-explanation, the enemy of scene. |
| 6 | Weak rhythm | YES | Monotone. Every sentence the same medium length, all declarative, no breath. |
| 7 | Missing telling detail | **YES** | "sit in his chair and watch TV" is the *only* near-image, and it's generic. No particular does any work. |
| 8 | No reader trust | Partial | The fake-closure of "I'll always regret" hands the reader a bow instead of a real ending. |

**Weakest link: #1 No architecture — there is no question this paragraph is answering.** Until that is fixed, polishing the sentences would produce well-crafted lines with no spine — the exact inversion the ordering law forbids. So we start with Hawley, not with Cole.

### SELECT + ORDER (the full stack, in legal order)

1. **Architecture FIRST** → Hawley `/hawley-theme-engine` + `/hawley-ending-first`: find the question this paragraph is secretly answering, and the ending it's driving toward. The question here: *can you love someone you never reached?* The ending image must deepen that question, not resolve it into "I regret it."
2. **Scene + telling detail** → Connelly `/telling-detail-engine` and Roth `/visual-prose-for-copy` (visual prose): replace the summary of thirty-two years with ONE scene carrying one telling detail that reveals the father *and* the distance.
3. **Line / rhythm** → Cole `/terminal-power-rhythm-engineering` + read-aloud (Lamott-Allen technical craft): vary sentence length so the prose breathes; land power words last; cut the bloat-summary.
4. **Truth / voice LAST** → `/really-real-book` (the book truth slot): name the hard thing in plain words, remove the theatrical "I'll always regret," let the omitted grief be *felt* through restraint instead of asserted. End on earned human recognition, not a bow.

Dose: full and layered, as long-form earns. But still governed by restraint — deepening the memoir means cutting the summary and trusting one scene, not adding more telling.

### The deepened draft (after)

> The question I have never been able to put down is whether you can love someone you never once reached.
>
> Thirty-two years he gave the mill, and the only thing it gave him back that I ever saw was the way he washed his hands. He'd come in the side door and go straight to the kitchen sink, not the bathroom, and scrub past the wrists with the green bar soap, the water running gray, then black, then almost clear, and he'd keep going a while after it ran clear. I used to stand in the doorway and watch. I thought he was just dirty. I know now he was buying three quiet minutes before he had to be anybody's father.
>
> I wanted him to say he was proud of me. He never did. I have spent a long time deciding that the hands were the saying — that a man who scrubs that long is a man holding something he can't put words to.
>
> I don't know if that's true. I made it up so I'd have somewhere to put the love. He died on a Tuesday and I never asked him, and the not-asking is the closest thing to him I have left.

### Depth Receipt

- **Weakest link found**: No architecture (deficit #1) — the draft was a list of facts about a father with no question organizing them, which made every other deficit (hollow, unearned, bloated) inevitable.
- **Moves applied**:
  - No architecture → installed a spine before touching a sentence: the invisible question *can you love someone you never reached?* now organizes every detail, and the ending deepens that question ("I don't know if that's true… the not-asking is the closest thing to him I have left") instead of resolving it into "I'll always regret" → reader feels a real inquiry, not a summary → Hawley theme-first + ending-first.
  - Hollow + missing telling detail → cut the generic "difficult man / sat in his chair / watched TV" and built ONE scene around a single telling detail (washing his hands past the wrists, the water running gray then black then clear, scrubbing after it ran clear) that reveals the father *and* the distance at once → the reader's mind builds the whole man from one window → Connelly telling-detail + Roth visual prose.
  - Over-explained → deleted the "it wasn't until I was much older that I began to understand he probably had his own struggles" summary → the understanding is now *enacted* through the hands theory, not narrated → Cole compression / really-real economy.
  - Weak rhythm → varied sentence length deliberately (the long water-running sentence against the short "I used to stand in the doorway and watch."), landed power words terminally ("father," "clear," "left") → the paragraph now breathes and can be read aloud → Cole terminal-power rhythm + read-aloud test.
  - Emotionally unearned / fake closure → removed "that's something I'll always regret"; the grief is now felt through restraint — what's withheld (he never asked, the father never said it) does the work → really-real-book truth slot, ending on earned recognition.
- **Dose / vertical fit**: BOOK dose — full stack, architecture-led, because long-form lives or dies on the spine. Even at full dose the governing move was *subtraction*: thirty-two years of summary became one scene. Voice was preserved (first-person, plain, working-class register — no literary varnish that would betray the speaker).
- **Remaining risk**: The hand-washing detail is invented; in real memoir it must be a true remembered detail or an honestly-flagged composite, never a manufactured one — fabricating the load-bearing image would be exactly the false-vulnerability anti-pattern. The "made it up so I'd have somewhere to put the love" admission is the riskiest line: it's the really-real center, but if the surrounding chapter doesn't support that level of candor it could read as a too-neat epiphany — verify against the book's overall restraint level. Architecture is now sound for *this paragraph*; it must still cohere with the chapter's larger spine.

## Cross-example pattern (what the three prove together)

| | Example 1 (Social) | Example 2 (Copy) | Example 3 (Book) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weakest link** | Hollow / generic (+ cheap close) | Hollow / generic (+ no proof trust) | No architecture (the root) |
| **Did we call Hawley?** | No — spine was sound | No — funnel logic was sound | **Yes, FIRST** — no spine existed |
| **Dose** | Light, 3 surgical moves | Medium, conversion-protected | Full stack, architecture-led |
| **Net length change** | Shorter | About even (cut adjectives, added one scene) | Shorter (summary → one scene) |
| **Function protected** | Hook, scannability | Offer, bullets, CTA, proof | Voice, register |
| **Truth slot used** | `/really-real-social` | `/really-real-marketing` | `/really-real-book` |

Three lessons the conductor never violates, visible across all three:

1. **Architecture is called only when it's actually broken** — twice we did NOT call Hawley because re-architecting a sound spine bloats the piece. The ordering law says architecture *first when present as a deficit*, not architecture *always added*.
2. **Deepen ≠ lengthen** — two of three got shorter. The most common deepening move is the cut.
3. **No expert names on the page** — experts appear only in the Receipt. The deepened prose reads as more honest, clearer, warmer, more specific, less defended — never as a technique stack.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Inverting the order — polishing sentences before confirming a spine.** Example 3's flat draft is the trap: its sentences are *grammatical*, so it's tempting to run Cole first. That yields beautiful lines about nothing. Recovery: always score deficit #1 first; if architecture is the weakest link, Hawley runs before any line work.
- **Over-deepening social.** Turning Example 1's 100-word post into a 400-word essay because the truth slot felt good. Recovery: cap social at 1–2 deficits, preserve hook and scannability, and re-check word count against the original — if it grew, you over-deepened.
- **Sacrificing conversion for "literary" feel.** Softening Example 2's CTA or burying the bullets under a beautiful Sunday-night scene. Recovery: every copy move must answer "does this make the reader more likely to believe, then act?" If not, cut it; clarity-to-action wins.
- **Manufacturing the load-bearing detail.** Inventing Example 3's hand-washing scene for a real person's memoir, or fabricating Example 2's "9 minutes" stat. Recovery: flag every invented specific in the Remaining-risk line of the Receipt; in real work it must be true, sourced, or honestly labeled before shipping.
- **Re-implementing craft instead of composing it.** Writing fresh compression rules into the deepened draft instead of calling Cole's owner workflow. Recovery: the conductor COMPOSES owners; it never duplicates the craft an owner already does. Technical sentence-craft knowledge lives only in the lamott-allen-really-real-writing technical-craft module — orchestrate it, don't copy it.
