# Daniel Priestley — SLL Engine Genius Context

Source: "Make $1,500 an Hour With This 3 Step System" (ScoreApp, 12:57) — full captions + watched whiteboard frames (`extractions/daniel-priestley-sll-engine/`). Everything below is from that source; no training-memory fill.

## The System in One Line

**Short-form (daily) → Long-form (monthly) → Lead-form (new offer quarterly)**, built to ride the AI recommendation engine: every post carries its own targeting signal, every layer points to the next, and the lead form hands a hand-raised buyer to the sales process (LAPS: Leads → Appointments → Presentations → Sales).

## The Physics (why the system works — argue from this, never from "best practice")

Four platform eras, drawn as a timeline (pre-2015 → 2025+):

1. **Chronological** (pre-2015): 90% of viewers were existing followers you met in the real world. Posting frequency ≈ visibility.
2. **Engagement sorting** (2015-2020): follower graph still ruled, but likes/watch-time/comments sorted the feed.
3. **Hyper-targeted / AI sorting** (2020-2025): TikTok-style — no audience needed; trending-topic matching pushed content to interested strangers.
4. **Recommendation engine** (2025+): AI matchmakes every piece of content with the person who should see it — "someone in Brisbane could make content absolutely perfect for someone in Boston." Priestley's ranking: "the recommendation engine is the most powerful AI on the planet, and large language models are second most powerful" — because it's invisible while it directs traffic, attention, politics, and consumer behavior.

**The Magical Telephone reframe**: the engine is a phone that, when you pick it up, connects you with someone who wants to buy from you. Posting = picking up the phone. How often would you pick up? That's the cadence argument.

## Genius Patterns

### 1. Ride the Invisible Rails
- **Unconscious behavior**: designs marketing around content-level matchmaking, not audience building. Followers are a legacy metric; every post is a cold-audience audition.
- **Executable**: write each post in the named customer's own pain/prize/problem language so the engine can classify who it's for. "When you're talking about your pain, your prize, your problem, and the news, it will figure out who's interested."
- **Success metric**: strangers (non-followers) in reach + lead-form fills, not likes.

### 2. Era-Map Before Tactics
- Sells the strategy by mapping platform history first — he draws the 2015 / 2020 / 2025 timeline on the pad ("Chrono → Engagement → HT/AI + Trending Topics → Recommendation Engine", [00:49]-[02:36], frames 0026-0042) before naming a single tactic, citing TikTok as the hyper-targeting break point [02:02]. The tactic then lands as inevitable, not hyped. Use the timeline whenever installing SLL for a skeptical client.

### 3. Three-Box Compression (SHORT → LONG → LEAD)
- Every marketing activity collapses into three boxes drawn on one page (SHORT / LONG / LEAD, frame 0073, [04:47]), each with an owned cadence: "the cadence is short form daily, long form monthly, new offer every quarter" [11:18]. If an activity doesn't live in a box, it doesn't happen.

### 4. Four-Lane Short-Form: Pain, Prize, Problem, +News
- **Pain** — what they experience NOW without your product ("revenues are flat... sitting around waiting for a referral that never comes").
- **Prize** — the other side; "the joyful celebration if everything worked because they bought from you."
- **Problem** — what has STOPPED them getting the result (the obstacle; distinct from pain).
- **+News** — a MULTIPLIER, not a fourth co-equal lane (drawn as `+News` on the pad): "pain that's in the news, prize that's in the news, problem that's in the news, or just news stories that relate to what you do" — "the recommendation engine is always looking for trending news... a turbo charge."
- Finite lanes kill the blank page. Worked triad (health/fitness): pain = life without the health you want; prize = what it would feel like to have it; problem = what's stopping you.

### 5. 11×90 Recognition Math
- "People see you for the first time when they see you for the 11th time." "Humans consume 200 billion short-form videos per day." "11 times within 90 days to even recognize that you exist." → Daily short-form is DERIVED arithmetic, not discipline-porn. Below-daily = mathematically invisible.

### 6. Proof → Principles → Process (long-form spine, in this order)
- **Proof story**: "a story that proves you know what you're talking about" — or a statistic/research. Exemplars: "I helped someone lose 20 kilos of body fat"; "I helped one of my clients go from 2 million to 5 million of revenue in under 2 years."
- **Principles**: the 3-5 things that MADE the proof story possible (health: diet, exercise, mindset).
- **Process**: how you engage clients (coaching / accountability / software / etc.).
- Order matters: credibility → why → how. Then CTA to the lead form.

### 7. Gap-Closing Explainer Doctrine
- "Your customers have gaps in their awareness, gaps in their understanding, and if you can close those gaps, they're more likely to buy. Don't assume they're mind readers — create long-form explainer content that explains step-by-step how something is done." Teaching sells when it closes a NAMED gap, not when it dumps everything.

### 8. Lead Form = Signal Capture (two layers)
- **What you offer** (pick ≥1, refresh quarterly):
  - **Special Offer** — discount / limited-time / launch campaign.
  - **Product for Prospects (P4P)** — "an easy first step to working with you": intro workshop, online assessment, waitlist, discussion group.
  - **Promotion** — time-boxed happening with an END DATE.
- **Page anatomy** (drawn below a divider in the LEAD box): **Hook** (explains what's going on) → **Value Prop restated** ("if I do this, I will get this") → **Credibility** (testimonials / qualifications / linked research) → **CTA** (fill in this form to get started).
- The form's job is a hand-raise: "signal that they want to do business with you." It "sparks the sales process" → LAPS.

### 9. Bridge Mechanics (short→long)
- Two named bridges only [07:50]-[07:58]: (a) "you might ask people to comment a word and you will send them the long-form content", (b) "direct them to check out a piece of long-form content that is pinned to the top of your channel." Every short post carries one.

## Hidden Knowledge (tacit, made explicit)

1. **Content IS the targeting data.** You never configure targeting; specific pain/prize/problem language is the targeting. Vague content = untargetable content.
2. **Monthly long-form is sufficient.** One strong explainer/month; short-form is the moving surface, long-form the stable converter (often pinned). Volume anxiety belongs in short-form only.
3. **Quarterly offer refresh fights offer fatigue.** "Once your lead form converts" the system is pure cadence maintenance — but the offer must be NEW at least every 90 days.
4. **The demo is the method.** This video is itself SLL long-form (proof: $1,500/hr + 7 startups → principles: era map → process: SLL) with two lead forms (live workshop, ScoreApp). Model deliverables on it.
5. **Recognition precedes trust.** The 11×90 threshold is about being RECOGNIZED at all — before any persuasion math applies.
6. **Sell the connection, not the platform.** He never names a platform strategy per-platform; the unit of strategy is the buyer connection the engine brokers.

## Anti-Patterns (source-anchored — what Priestley's system rules out)

1. **Engagement-era playbook on recommendation-era rails** — posting for existing followers and sorting-by-likes logic. Source: the era map itself [00:49]-[02:36]: "before 2015... 90% of the people who would see the posts... were your existing followers" — that world is gone; content now auditions to strangers.
2. **Below-daily posting** — Source [07:19]-[07:24]: "if you're posting anything less than daily, you're not being seen enough for people to recognize who you are and what you do." The 11×90 arithmetic makes sporadic cadence mathematically invisible.
3. **Assuming mind readers** — content that showcases instead of explains. Source [09:25]: "don't assume that they're mind readers, create long-form explainer content that explains step-by-step how something is done."
4. **Orphan content** — a short post with no bridge, a long piece with no form CTA. Source [04:49]: the strategy is defined as a chain — "short-form content that leads into long-form content that then directs people to fill in a form" — an unlinked piece is outside the system by definition.
5. **Stale offer** — the same lead-form offer running past 90 days. Source [11:15]: "at least a new offer every single 90 days"; promotions must have "an end date" [10:12].
6. **Form without anatomy** — a bare opt-in with no hook/value-prop/credibility. Source [10:20]-[10:45]: "make sure that the top of the lead form has a hook that explains what's going on. It restates your value proposition... states your credibility... and it has a clear call to action."

## Recognition Test

Before shipping any SLL deliverable: **would Priestley recognize this as his system — would he draw it on his pad?** Three boxes, four lanes, proof-first long-form, two-layer lead form, dated cadences. If the output could have come from a generic "content strategy" prompt — no lanes, no cadence arithmetic, no hand-raise capture — it fails, regardless of polish.

## Quality Rubric (what Priestley would check)

1. **Lane purity**: each short post sits in exactly one lane (or lane × News), written in the customer's own words — could a stranger tell who it's for?
2. **Cadence honesty**: daily short / monthly long / quarterly new offer — actually scheduled, not aspirational.
3. **Long-form order**: proof → principles → process, closes ONE named gap, ends with a lead-form CTA.
4. **Lead-form anatomy complete**: hook + restated value prop + credibility + single CTA; offers Special Offer, P4P, or Promotion with a reason to act now (end date for promotions).
5. **No orphan content**: every short points to a long; every long points to a form; every form feeds LAPS.
6. **Claims quarantine**: Priestley's stats (11×90, 200B/day, $1,500/hr) are HIS claims — usable as his framework's rationale, never restated as verified external facts.
