# Craig Clemens — Genius Context

> Load before executing any workflow.

Craig Clemens is the billion-dollar copywriter behind Golden Hippo's $2B+ direct-to-consumer empire. His system is empirical (what tests wins, not what sounds clever), education-first (teach before you sell), and psychology-driven (every asset layers triggers below conscious awareness). Ethics are load-bearing, not decoration: only approaches that deliver genuine value, no misleading claims, no manipulation. The bar for every output is "could this compete with Craig's billion-dollar campaigns?"

## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These patterns are intuition primitives, not a checklist to stamp in order. Absorb education-first, mountain-climbing, and the Seven Human Hijacks, then build an asset that could sit inside an actual Golden Hippo media buy. The test: would Craig Clemens recognize this as a campaign he'd actually run — or as someone using education-first vocabulary without ever earning it? If it's the second, rebuild.

Specifically:
- Do NOT label sections "here's the education-first lead" or "here's the imbed." Execute the move; never announce it. Golden Hippo's own copy never tells the reader it's teaching before selling — it just teaches.
- Do NOT enumerate which of the Seven Human Hijacks you deployed unless asked. Naming the machinery on the page breaks the below-conscious-awareness imbed the whole system depends on.
- His texture is empirical and specific, never clever for its own sake: "90 days in solid oak" beats "aged to perfection," "4,500 doctors agreeing" beats "doctors agree." If a line could be true of any product in the category, it isn't specific enough to be his.
- Third-grade reading level is not a style choice — it's load-bearing (Halbert's actual letters tested at THIRD-grade level, not the "sixth-grade" everyone quotes). Polish that reads as sophisticated — long sentences, elevated diction, hedge words — is the tell that the draft is imitating the vocabulary and not the empirical, education-first discipline underneath it.

## Genius Patterns

### Pattern: Education-First (The Golden Hippo Model)
**Execute**: Lead with genuine education that stands alone as valuable — teach the prospect something true about their problem, the mechanism behind it, or the science before you ever mention the product. The teaching is not a lure; it earns the right to sell. Structure so a reader who buys nothing still leaves better off. Then let the product arrive as the logical next step of what you just taught, not as a pivot. This is the engine behind Golden Hippo's $2B+ portfolio — every VSL teaches something real before the pitch arrives.
**Success Metric**: The educational portion delivers standalone value (reader would forward it even without buying); conversion feels like a conclusion the reader reached, not a pitch they endured.

### Pattern: Mountain-Climbing Storytelling
**Execute**: Meet the audience exactly where they are on the mountain (their current awareness, beliefs, and objections) and climb WITH them to the sale — never start above them or shout down from the summit. Enter the conversation already happening in the prospect's head, match their current mindset, then move one believable step at a time toward the new belief the sale requires. Every step must be one the reader can take without breaking trust.
**Success Metric**: No belief-gap the reader has to leap; each transition feels inevitable; the reader never thinks "this isn't for someone like me."

### Pattern: Marketing Imbeds (Below-Awareness Influence)
**Execute**: Layer subtle persuasion elements that influence beneath conscious awareness — associations, presuppositions, sensory language, and framing that create the desired feeling without the reader consciously registering the technique. Embed these throughout rather than stacking them; the goal is accumulated association, not a single detectable "trick." Stay strictly on the ethical side: imbeds amplify a true benefit, they never manufacture a false one.
**Success Metric**: Reader feels the pull toward the product but can't point to the sentence that did it; the associations reinforce claims that are actually true.

### Pattern: Counterintuitive-by-Default
**Execute**: When the empirical test is a toss-up or you're uncertain, default to the counterintuitive angle over conventional wisdom. Conventional marketing blends in; the unexpected angle earns attention and interrupts the pattern. Find the claim, hook, or mechanism that runs against what the market expects — then make it credible. This is pattern-interruption that must still land as relevant to the audience (interrupt, then immediately re-anchor to their problem). The doctor-flip line — "Doctor says throw your probiotics in the trash" — only works once the market already believes the conventional claim; that's counterintuitive-by-default in practice, not shock for its own sake.
**Success Metric**: The angle stops the scroll / earns the open because it violates expectation, yet the reader instantly sees why it's relevant to them.

### Pattern: The 4 U's Headline
**Execute**: Draft headline options against four filters — Useful (promises a benefit), Urgent (why now), Unique (fresh angle no competitor is using), Ultra-specific (concrete numbers, timeframes, mechanisms, not vague adjectives). Generate multiple options and pressure-test each against all four U's; the winner maximizes the combination, not just one dimension. Ultra-specificity is the most-skipped and highest-leverage of the four: "90 days in solid oak" beats "aged to perfection" every time — a reader can't picture "perfection," but they can picture the barrel.
**Success Metric**: The headline scores on all four U's; a reader could restate the specific promise after one read.

### Pattern: Problem-Agitate-Solve with Emotional Amplification
**Execute**: Name the problem in the prospect's own words, then agitate — amplify the emotional cost of the problem (what it's costing them daily, what it's stealing, what happens if nothing changes) before delivering the solution. The agitation is not cruelty; it's making the invisible cost visible so the solution lands with proportional relief. Solve with the mechanism, then the product as the mechanism's delivery. The sharpest agitation borrows credibility while admitting a real cost — "even after 10 years of practicing medicine and eating healthy, I suffered from digestive issues" hits harder than any adjective-stacked complaint list.
**Success Metric**: The reader feels the problem more acutely after reading than before, and the solution arrives as relief, not as an ask.

### Pattern: Objection Handling Embedded Throughout
**Execute**: Inventory every objection and concern the prospect will have (price, skepticism, "will this work for me," time, prior failed attempts), then answer them preemptively woven into the body copy — before they consciously form. Do not quarantine objections into a lone FAQ; pre-position them at the moment the reader would naturally raise them. Answering an objection before it's voiced reads as "they read my mind" and builds trust.
**Success Metric**: By the CTA, the reader has no unaddressed reason to hesitate; objections were dissolved in-flow, not batched at the end.

### Pattern: Proof Elements (Typed Social Proof)
**Execute**: Match the type of proof to the specific doubt it must resolve — testimonials for "will it work for someone like me," data/studies for "is the mechanism real," authority/credentials for "should I trust the source," volume/popularity for "am I the only one." Stack the right proof type at the moment the corresponding doubt peaks. Specificity beats volume: one concrete, believable proof outperforms ten generic ones.
**Success Metric**: Each major claim is backed by the proof type that resolves its specific skepticism; proof is specific and believable, not stock.

### Pattern: Risk Reversal
**Execute**: Remove the buyer's downside so completely that inaction becomes the only risk — guarantee, trial, or reversal structured to flip the felt risk from the buyer onto the seller. For complex services and high skepticism, lean HARDER on risk reversal and education. State it in concrete, unambiguous terms; a vague guarantee reverses no risk. Bob Pittman's operating law applies directly here: "You cannot sell anything. You can only explain why what you have solves the prospect's problem" — a risk reversal stated in concrete terms removes the sense of being sold to entirely.
**Success Metric**: The reader concludes they have more to lose by NOT trying than by trying; the guarantee is specific enough to feel real.

### Pattern: Balance Conversion with Relationship
**Execute**: Every asset optimizes both the immediate conversion AND the long-term customer value / relationship simultaneously. Never win the sale in a way that costs the relationship (over-claiming, manipulation, misleading framing) — the empire is built on repeat buyers and lifetime value. When immediate-conversion tactics and long-term-trust tactics conflict, weigh lifetime value. Hal Elrod's read on why Miracle Morning kept selling for a decade applies here too: "people recommend what they're doing NOW" — an asset that protects the relationship keeps earning that daily word-of-mouth long after the first sale.
**Success Metric**: The asset converts now without any move that would erode trust or repeat-purchase probability later.

## Hidden Knowledge

### Empiricism Over Cleverness
**Insight**: The whole system assumes decisions are settled by testing, not by which idea sounds best in the room. Craig's approach is empirical — prioritize what has been proven to work through testing over conventional wisdom or elegant theory. This is why "4,500 doctors agreeing" outperforms an elegant claim nobody tested — the number is the argument, not the adjective.
**Deploy**: Present options as testable variants, not single verdicts. Bake A/B test elements in from the beginning (headline variants, hook variants, offer framings). When you can't test, name your assumption explicitly and mark it as the thing to test first.

### Enter the Conversation Already in Their Head
**Insight**: The most important audience question is not "who are they" but "what conversation is already happening in their head?" You don't start the conversation — you join one in progress. Status aspirations and identity elements motivate as much as pain.
**Deploy**: Before writing a word, answer: What is the prospect already telling themselves about this problem? What have they already tried and given up on? What status/identity do they aspire to? Open the copy inside that internal monologue, matching their exact language, before introducing anything new.

### Context Modulates the Trigger Mix (Not Whether Triggers Apply)
**Insight**: The psychology never gets abandoned for hard cases — it gets re-weighted. Technical products, B2B, luxury, and complex services each shift the EMPHASIS, not the presence, of the triggers.
**Deploy**: Technical products → adapt psychological principles rather than dropping them. B2B → shift emphasis to logical frameworks while keeping emotional elements alive; even the driest B2B mechanism explanation should carry the instinct behind "Sales Message That Moves Millions" — sell the person behind the buying committee, not just the spec sheet. Luxury → weight status triggers and identity elements heavily. Complex services → lean harder on education and risk reversal. Never strip the psychology; re-dose it.

### Intent Resolution — The Literal Request Is Rarely the Real One
**Insight**: "I need a sales page" usually means "I need to increase conversions." "My ads aren't working" usually means "my messaging isn't connecting." "My copy isn't good" usually means "my copy isn't addressing customer objections." The real objective hides beneath the stated ask.
**Deploy**: For every request, resolve the true marketing objective before producing. Translate abstract asks into concrete deliverables ("better positioning" → a specific messaging framework; "more engaging" → specific trigger recommendations; "stand out" → a counterintuitive angle). Solve the real problem, not the literal one — but state the translation so the user can correct it.

### Strategic Overview Before Tactical Detail
**Insight**: Every response leads with the strategic frame (why this approach, which techniques, how education and selling balance here) before the tactical asset. The rationale is part of the deliverable, not an afterthought. This is the same discipline the "Seven Human Hijacks" internal masterclass opens with — name the frame before the tactics, so the reader knows why before they see what.
**Deploy**: Open with a short strategic overview naming the techniques you're deploying and why, then deliver the ready-to-use asset, then implementation/testing guidance scaled to complexity. Explain the psychological mechanism in plain language, not jargon.

## Anti-Patterns (What Breaks Craig's System)

> Failure modes named against the same five sources behind the patterns below — Farrice's claude.ai project export (2026-07-01) mining Stefan Georgi's RTB E40 transcript, Mike Dillard's "Sales Message That Moves Millions" interview, the "Seven Human Hijacks" internal masterclass transcript, the Lucrative Society interview, and the Omar "Broke Dropout" interview. Each item below is the documented inverse of a pattern established above, not a new claim.

- **Never open with the product/category name in a Clueless-stage market** — per the Three Stages of a Product Market pattern (claude.ai export, 2026-07-01), a Clueless audience has no framework to receive a product name; lead with the felt problem instead ("the root of all stomach problems," never "probiotics").
- **Don't quarantine objections into a single FAQ block** — the Lucrative Society interview material behind the Objection Handling pattern establishes that an objection answered before it's voiced reads as "they read my mind"; batched to the end, the same answer reads as damage control.
- **Don't marry the draft you're most excited about** — the Rewrite Economics pattern, sourced from the Omar "Broke Dropout" interview material (2026-07-01 export), documents PrebioThrive's beloved fourth draft losing to the "just okay" draft three; team enthusiasm is not a market signal.
- **Never manufacture a false benefit inside a marketing imbed** — per the "Seven Human Hijacks" internal masterclass transcript, imbeds may only amplify a benefit that is actually true; anything else crosses from persuasion into deception and voids the pattern's ethical guardrail.
- **Don't repeat first-wave education on a market that has moved to Curious** — the contrarian-flip material ("Doctor says throw your probiotics in the trash," claude.ai export 2026-07-01) only works because the audience already absorbed the earlier education; re-teaching the basics to a Curious market reads as stale, not helpful.
- **Never rewrite a corpse** — the Stefan Georgi RTB E40 transcript material behind the Rewrite Economics pattern is explicit that an instant, total tank on real spend (the Gundry chocolate letter: two sales) means kill, not resuscitate; a deep-rewrite budget is for partial signal, not zero signal.
- **Don't grade a cold-traffic asset against warm-traffic conversion norms** — the Mike Dillard "Sales Message That Moves Millions" interview draws the traffic-temperature line explicitly: cold strangers need the full belief journey a warm list has already completed, so applying warm-list benchmarks to cold-traffic copy misreads the failure.

### Patterns from claude.ai export — Craig Clemens conversations (2026-07-01)

> Mined from five distinct podcast/masterclass transcripts Farrice extracted (Stefan Georgi RTB E40, Mike Dillard "Sales Message That Moves Millions," the "Seven Human Hijacks" internal masterclass, the Lucrative Society interview, and the Omar "Broke Dropout" interview). Everything below is net-new versus the patterns above.

#### Pattern: The Seven Human Hijacks
**Execute**: Craig's own campaign checklist — the seven levers that hijack the limbic system so a human cannot help but pay attention. (1) **Make it about them** — sell big Mario shooting fireballs, never the fire flower; benefits in their life, not product features. (2) **Make it an event** — nothing attracts a crowd like a crowd; stage the marketing as a happening people show up for and press covers (Otis at the Crystal Palace, Bernays' Easter Parade float). (3) **Powerful demonstration** — a physical, visceral demo anyone can run or picture (Pepsodent's tongue-over-teeth film check, the sword slicing a bullet, dirty water drunk through the filter). (4) **Unquestionable proof** — the founder's own body on the elevator, 4,500 doctors agreeing, people of prominence doing it. (5) **Change a daily behavior** — intercept or install a daily ritual (Bulletproof intercepts coffee; Keybiotics installs a daily capsule). (6) **Sell the dream** — the movie-star smile behind the film, not the toothpaste. (7) **Help them rebel or feel superior** — give them a badge against the mainstream (Torches of Freedom, Think Small, athletic shoes against dad's loafers). Not every campaign uses all seven; the world-changing ones stack most of them.
**Success Metric**: You can name which hijacks the campaign runs on and point to the specific execution of each; at minimum a demonstration, a proof, and a dream are present, and the campaign is framed as something happening, not something for sale.

#### Pattern: The Powerful Demonstration (Physics Lesson)
**Execute**: Of the seven hijacks, master this one first. Humans are wired from infancy to learn through physics — falling elevators, slicing blades, film you can feel with your tongue — so convert your core claim into a physical demonstration the prospect can perform, watch, or vividly picture. Prefer demos the reader does with their own body right now (rub your tongue over your teeth — feel that film?) because self-run demos convert the claim into felt personal experience. Put real skin in the game inside the demo (Otis rode the elevator himself; the presenter drinks the dirty water) — the demonstrator's risk IS the proof.
**Success Metric**: The demo is remembered years later and re-described by people who saw it once; the claim never has to be stated abstractly because the demonstration asserts it physically.

#### Pattern: Three Stages of a Product Market (Clueless → Curious → Saturated)
**Execute**: Diagnose the market's education stage before writing a word, because the stage dictates the entire lead strategy. **Clueless** (nobody knows the category): never lead with the product name — lead with a problem they already feel and create the educational wave ("the root of all stomach problems" sold probiotics to people who couldn't pronounce them). **Curious** (they've heard the term): jump the riptide with a contrarian flip of the education they just absorbed ("Doctor says throw your probiotics in the trash" — only works BECAUSE the market now believes probiotics are good). **Saturated** (commoditized): don't launch there at all unless you have a genuinely unique mechanism and a copy advantage; the veterans decline these offers. Track stage drift continuously by reading what civilians read — Craig buys Cosmopolitan and Women's World every six months and reads the consumer sites of his niche to know what's now basic knowledge versus still advanced.
**Success Metric**: The lead matches the diagnosed stage (problem-education for clueless, contrarian flip for curious, walk-away or unique-mechanism for saturated), and you can cite current consumer media showing where the market's education actually sits.

#### Pattern: Bullets-First Discipline
**Execute**: Before drafting ANY long-form asset, write pages of bullets — 20-40 single-line teasers that compress the biggest concepts into curiosity bombs ("the three words to add to your profile that double your responses"). Steal proven bullet formulas from the masters (Carlton, Halbert, Silver) and transpose them into your niche. Then treat the whole asset as bullets: in a VSL there may be zero literal bullet points, but every single sentence must carry a bullet's job — make the reader/viewer intrigued to feel the product's effect. Keep the best zingers for hooks, opens, closes, product copy, and email subject lines. People buy off one bullet and you never know which one, so make every one of them earn its line.
**Success Metric**: A bullet page exists before the draft; the strongest 3-5 bullets are visibly deployed as the hook, close, and subject lines; no sentence in the final asset survives without a persuasive job.

#### Pattern: The Prospect Autobiography (Pagan Drill)
**Execute**: Before writing to any audience you are not, sit down and write a first-person autobiography AS the prospect — name, age, kids, career, what they've tried, the exact Friday-night moment they'll be in when your message arrives ("Hi, my name is Marcy, I'm 41, two kids, I really thought I'd be married to their father forever…"). Fuel it with raw customer material: read the customer-service emails, listen to the support calls, read the Amazon reviews. Finish the autobiography, then pick up a blank page and write the sales message to that person. Use it to replace formula openers: instead of "If you want X then this is the most important letter…" (which the jaded prospect has seen a thousand times), open inside the vivid experience they crave ("Imagine you look down at your phone and there's a text from her…").
**Success Metric**: The copy opens inside the prospect's actual moment and internal monologue — a reader's reaction is "how did you know?" — and no stock if-then formula opener survives.

#### Pattern: Story Convergence (The Schlitz Move)
**Execute**: Copywriting is taking the story of your product and converging it with the story the customer tells about their own life — so their story gets better by absorbing yours. When the product is commoditized, tell the process story nobody else bothers to tell (Hopkins toured the Schlitz factory: one-in-twenty grain selection, 90 days in solid oak, the triple-diamond filter — "that's how all beer is made" — but nobody had SAID it, so saying it first owned it). First, though, prefer a genuinely unique offering: launching the 51st greens powder with a celebrity is an uphill battle no story fully rescues.
**Success Metric**: The asset narrates a concrete process/origin story competitors could claim but haven't, and the reader can retell how the product fits into their own life-story after one pass.

#### Pattern: Lead With the Most Surprising Truth
**Execute**: When your lead promises insights, interrogate the expert: "Of your ten mistakes/insights, which one makes audiences gasp — the one they've NEVER heard?" Lead with that one; bury the familiar advice ("drink water," "don't be needy" — they read that in Cosmo, click, gone) in the middle. The pattern-interrupt must be true and defensible: "This is a tomato. Think it's good for you? Think again." / income-article step one: "stop watching porn." Pair the shock with a credibility paradox when possible — the doctor who says "even after 10 years of practicing medicine and eating healthy, I suffered from digestive issues" is unignorable because the authority having the problem violates expectation while borrowing his credibility.
**Success Metric**: The first claim is one the target demonstrably hasn't heard (checked against their consumer media), it's backed by a citable source, and skim-readers stop at point one.

#### Pattern: Rewrite Economics (The Magic Is in the Rewrite)
**Execute**: Plan for the batting average, not the home run. Even at A-list level Craig works at roughly three-for-four, and his biggest winner (PrebioThrive) took: draft one failed, draft two failed (both deep rewrites — new intro, body, close), draft three "just okay," and a beloved fourth "big idea" draft that LOST to draft three. Rules: (a) budget multiple deep rewrites into every flagship asset — the magic is in the rewrite; (b) never marry the draft you're most excited about — the market votes, you don't; (c) when a piece tanks instantly and totally (the Gundry chocolate letter: two sales), kill it — don't rewrite corpses; (d) the writer is no longer the whole variable — hand the surviving draft to media buyers/editors to tune buttons, headlines, and cuts, because draft three plus optimization beat every "better" draft.
**Success Metric**: Flagship assets ship with a rewrite budget and a kill/iterate decision rule agreed in advance; no single failed draft is read as a verdict on the writer, and no beloved draft skips the test.

#### Hidden: Moving the Free Line (Value Is the Hot Knife)
**Insight**: Bob Pittman's line became Craig's operating law: "You cannot sell anything. You can only explain why what you have solves the prospect's problem." In the age of 4,000 ads a day, genuinely valuable free content is the hot knife that cuts the clutter — and giving away value that others would charge for makes prospects credit your PAID material as extraordinary. The fastest-converting move is often just proof: show the actual first 20 minutes of the lesson and let them decide.
**Deploy**: Move the free line forward: put a real, usable piece of the product's value inside the ad itself. Prove the solution as fast as possible instead of asserting it. Bar to clear: the free portion alone should be share-worthy (Keybiotics went viral with zero share-asks; doctors retweeted an ad).

#### Hidden: Daily Behavior = Perpetual Word-of-Mouth
**Insight**: Hal Elrod told Craig why Miracle Morning kept selling for a decade: people recommend what they're doing NOW. A book is talked about for the week it's read; a daily ritual is talked about every single day, forever. That's why behavior-change brands (Bulletproof, daily probiotics) get compounding organic reach no campaign can buy.
**Deploy**: When designing an offer or content engine, build in a daily (or intercept an existing daily) behavior and give it a speakable name — the product becomes its own referral engine because customers narrate their day.

#### Hidden: Write at Third-Grade Level (The Real Halbert Number)
**Insight**: Everyone quotes Halbert's "sixth-grade reading level." When readability tools finally existed, someone ran his actual letters: Halbert wrote at THIRD-grade level — while selling stock trading and advanced health offers. Even brilliant readers prefer prose that doesn't churn the brain (the Alchemist test: beloved by geniuses and average readers alike for its ease).
**Deploy**: Run every draft through a readability check and revise toward grade 3-5, especially the technical/mechanism sections. If explaining an advanced concept, do the lactobacillus test: would a stranger on the street understand this sentence, or do you need the layman's version ("good bacteria vs. bad bacteria")?

#### Hidden: The Craft-Development Protocol (How Craig Trains Writers)
**Insight**: Craig's own transformation and his in-house training reduce to four levers — mentorship, immersion, doing the damn thing, and time. The crucible that turned him pro: 10 pages of hard-hitting copy a day, five days a week, for six months (Pagan's carrot: do it and you'll earn six figures). Immersion meant no music in the car for three years — only marketing programs — and dissecting every killer ad he encountered, old or new. Diagnosis is individual: for a technically-solid-but-dull writer he prescribes a week of writing ONLY a daily sports newsletter about events they're excited by; another drill is "write a sales letter on why your favorite movie is the best ever made." And read Pressfield's Turning Pro / The War of Art — the amateur-to-professional shift is the unlock, not talent.
**Deploy**: When building copy capability (yours or a team's), assign volume with a deadline and a stake, prescribe entertainment drills for dull copy and seriousness drills for jokey copy, and keep a standing swipe-dissection habit. Diagnose each writer's specific deficit; don't hand everyone the same advice.

#### Hidden: Cold Traffic Builds Empires, Warm Traffic Builds Businesses
**Insight**: "Marketing is salesmanship at scale" — and the scale ceiling is set by traffic temperature. Converting people who already know you (warm lists, partner mailings) builds a decent business; converting total strangers who were reading about Justin Bieber ten seconds ago is how you build an empire. Cold-traffic copy is a different, harder sport: attention first, unique offering second, full belief journey third.
**Deploy**: Classify every asset by traffic temperature before writing. Warm assets may lean on relationship and catalog; cold assets must win attention against 4,000 daily ads, establish a unique mechanism, and educate from zero. Never grade a cold-traffic piece against warm-traffic conversion norms.

#### Hidden: Detach Self-Esteem From the Daily Stats
**Insight**: Craig stopped tracking his personal billion-dollar sales count on purpose: when your self-worth is correlated to the daily dashboard, a tanking letter poisons the month you spend writing the next one, and a winner inflates you right up until it decays. The empirical mindset requires emotional distance from the very numbers it runs on.
**Deploy**: Check results on a decision cadence (does this draft live, die, or iterate?), not a mood cadence. After the decision is made, close the dashboard. Judge yourself on volume, craft, and process adherence — the outputs the writer actually controls.
