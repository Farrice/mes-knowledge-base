## A. THE PAIN MATRIX (10 orthogonal dimensions, each 1–10)

For each: score 1–10, then write the **marketing consequence** (the score is input, the consequence is output).

| # | Dimension (Low→High) | What it measures | Low-end play | High-end play |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Source: Physiological→Psychological** | mind vs. body root | (physiological) emphasize tangible markers, science, expert proof, mechanism | (psychological) relatable stories, tackle limiting beliefs/self-talk head-on |
| 2 | **Causal Clarity: Low→High** | market consensus on root cause | (low) investigate the "why," simplify cause to a single point | (high) contradict mainstream to create curiosity; focus on solutions, not cause |
| 3 | **Visibility: Low→High** | how visible to others | (low) validate the invisible struggle, don't make it "all in their head" | (high) fast visible results, before/after, social/confidence benefits |
| 4 | **Urgency: Low→High** | time-pressure felt | (low) drive into pain state via story, collapse timelines of inaction, social proof | (high) emotional triggers, instant relief, scarcity |
| 5 | **Social Stigma: Low→High** | shame/judgment attached | (low) direct approach | (high) empathy + non-judgment, make the problem feel *shared*, safe/confidential space |
| 6 | **Measurability: Low→High** | can progress be quantified | (low) vivid concrete imagery of solved life | (high) concrete measurable results, clear timelines |
| 7 | **Frequency: Low→High** | how often experienced | (low) honor the rare-but-significant event, guide through transformation | (high) immediate relief + long-term benefit |
| 8 | **Locus of Control: Internal→External** | who controls outcome | (internal) "not your fault," externalize blame onto other solutions, product creates the change | (external) empathize w/ victimization, open unexpected new pathways for control |
| 9 | **Emotional Intensity: Low→High** | depth of feeling | (low) use stories to generate emotion, connect to broader life consequences, direct solutions | (high) tread carefully, never invalidate the pain while offering the solution |
| 10 | **First Person: Self→Other** | who's primarily affected | (self) focus on their pain, give "permission to be selfish" as selfless ripple | (other: pet/child) highlight the bond, reciprocity ("imagine what your dog would do for you") |

**Benefit Matrix** = the same 10 axes plotted for the *desired/solved* state. The gap between Pain plot and Benefit plot = the transformation you sell.

## B. THE 9 ARCHETYPES OF SUFFERING

Type the market to set tone/approach. (Derived from positions on stigma, control, urgency, measurability, emotional intensity, causal clarity.)

1. **The Shadow** — hidden, high-stigma pain (private medical, financial shame). Inner turmoil + shame.
2. **The Warrior** — believes they can control/overcome; some causal understanding (saving a marriage via communication; optimizing a funnel).
3. **The Guardian** — urgency to protect others; powerless alone; wants measurable results (parent w/ a child's allergy; disaster repair).
4. **The Phoenix** — emotionally intense + stigma; seeking to rise & measure progress (battling depression/addiction).
5. **The Explorer** — complex, poorly-understood, low-visibility problems; moderate urgency (autoimmune; mastering complex software).
6. **The Sisyphus** — recurring problem, moderate intensity, believes they have control but can't sustain progress (workout consistency, procrastination).
7. **The Olympian** — measurable goal, strong personal control, moderate-high urgency (lose weight for an event, raise GPA).
8. **The Alchemist** — emotionally charged + stigma; believes in self-mastery transformation (overcoming limiting beliefs, inner peace).
9. **The Seeker** — unclear causes, moderate-high urgency, some control but no direction (conflicting diet info, career path).

## C. CORE WOUND + ONTOLOGICAL RESOURCES

**Core Wound** = the universal fear of total annihilation / abandonment / death. Every market refracts it differently based on which **Ontological Resources** it leans on to feel safe.

**12 (False) Ontological Resources:** physical appearance/attractiveness · financial wealth/possessions · social status/influence · professional titles/achievements · fame/celebrity · youth/vitality · relationships/emotional dependencies · intellect/knowledge · cultural/societal validation · sensory pleasure/indulgence · independence · power/control over others.

**Ontological Resource Matrix** — for each relevant resource:
`Resource | Inherent or Earned | Intensity (1–10) | Fear-of-Loss vs Desire-to-Gain | Description`

Then write the **Core Wound prediction** (1 paragraph) + a **"rub salt" message** that vividly threatens the high-intensity fear-of-loss resources.
> Inherent resources lost (youth, attractiveness) cut deeper than earned ones. Pain lives in two states: **Desire to Have** + **Fear of Loss.**

## D. THE RESONANCE HIERARCHY (full)

**Front:** Experiences ⇒ Beliefs ⇒ Values ⇒ Identity (formed bottom-up; sold top-down; hardens as it rises). Subsections:

**EXPERIENCES** — Past (frustrations) · Current (pains) · Future (fears)
**BELIEFS** — about themselves · about the problem · about the market · about the proposed solution · about specific experts/authorities · about what happens *after* they solve it ("If I could X, I'd feel Y")
**VALUES** — Primary Currency (market love language / what they value most) · Personal Standards (willing to do / not do) · External Standards (traits valued in others)
**IDENTITY** — Current Identity · Aspirational Identity · **Dysmorphic Avatars** (the identity *constraints* — the feared self, e.g. "the Washed-Up Has-Been") · Natural Allies / Enemies

**Market Love Languages (sample currencies):** financial security · status/prestige · power/control · freedom/independence · belonging · love/intimacy · growth/self-actualization · health · comfort · adventure · creativity · knowledge · meaning · tradition · legacy · exclusivity · etc. (cross-reference Maslow, Schwartz, Big Five, Self-Determination Theory, Hofstede when useful.)

**Rule:** if the lead conflicts with Identity, the prospect never gives you the chance — autopilot rejection. Appease Identity → avoid clashing with Values → re-frame Experiences → shift Beliefs → soften Identity.

### RH Constraints (6 types) — the objections, organized by tier
- **Identity Constraint** — "I'm just not the type of person who…"
- **Values Constraint** — "It's wrong to…" / "A real man shouldn't…"
- **Belief Constraint (Internal)** — "My problem is too ingrained to fix."
- **Belief Constraint (External)** — "You need to be rich/handsome/connected for this to work."
- **Resource Constraint** — no time / money / access / support.
- **Experience Constraint** — "I've tried X before and it failed."

## E. EPIPHANY THRESHOLD

Spectrum: Obvious (0) ────[Epiphany Threshold ≈7]──[Goldilocks 7–9]──[BS Limit 9]──── Outlandish (10).
Process: generate **10 below-threshold (obvious)** + **10 above-BS (outlandish)** + **10 Goldilocks (7–9, with reasoning).** Push toward outlandish.

**4 categories of challenging conventional wisdom:**
1. **Inversion & Extremism** — flip it entirely or push to extreme ("Over-moisturizing ages skin").
2. **Specific Exceptions & Hidden Factors** — general truth holds, but a hidden factor/exception flips outcomes ("Boring her on the first date makes her chase you").
3. **Reframing the Goal** — question the goal itself ("Copywriters should aim to confuse, not clarify").
4. **False Dichotomy & Oversimplification** — stark black/white contrast ("Women are attracted to jerks, not nice guys").

**Believability guardrails:** Plausibility (challenge wisdom, don't contradict hard science) · Implications (don't imply harm) · Specificity (focus, not broad generalization) · Familiarity-vs-Novelty (surprising but grounded).

## F. MAZE METAPHOR / HOOK THEORY

Hook types: **What** (problem/situation) · **Who** (entities) · **How** (process). Dimensions: Time · Specificity · Scale · Conflict · Insight · Center/Edge · Accepted/Not-Accepted · Status · Narrative · Number · Perspective. ~32 significant combinations (see `source-prompts/maze-hook-theory-prompt.md`).
**Bootleg Hook Theory:** Problem + {What / Who / How}.
- **What:** "The Truth About Dating in 2024" · "Why AI Means Most Men Will End Up Alone"
- **Who:** "What Andrew Tate Gets Wrong About Women" · "Why Jordan Peterson Hates Tinder"
- **How:** "3 Words That Make Any Woman Chase You"

**Curiosity generators:** authority-vs-authority conflict · "popular theory is wrong" · live > past · extreme edge > center · genuine insight.

## G. ANTI-HERO'S JOURNEY

**Levels of Consciousness** (3 zones, 2 inflection points): Victim → [accept some accountability] → Hybrid → [accept full accountability] → Accountability. Up = narrower market, better customers, higher prices. The **Energetic Changeling**: substitute the product/mechanism for the "epiphany."

**Part 1 (emotional backstory):**
1. **Garden of Eden** — vivid ideal state = reverse of the Core Wound (nostalgia, sensory, relatable, hints at transformation).
2. **Slow Descent & False Idols** — gradual worsening + failed solutions ("False Idols"); emotional + interpersonal consequences; cliffhanger.
3. **Fall of Man (PIG Story)** — the humiliating public collapse. Guidelines: add Stakes (public, important event) · add Delusion (top of the world, then the rug pulled) · reflect the Core Wound directly · make people *real*, not cartoon assholes.
4. **Dark Night of the Soul** — explicit confrontation with the Core Wound; isolation, mounting desperation, open-ended ache.

**Part 2:** A New Hope → Guru (self/other) → Epiphany/Insight/Mechanism → Initial→Final Results → A World Redeemed. (In a sales letter Part 1 opens; Part 2 follows the mechanism.)

## H. THE 10 DISSOLUTION FORMULAS (AWE delivery vehicles)

Each = Agree → Wedge → Elaborate, with a different Wedge vehicle:
1. **Scientific Evidence** — "a study from [institution] found [contradictory evidence]…"
2. **News & Current Events** — "did you hear about [recent event]…"
3. **Historical Examples** — "[historical figure] did X despite Y…"
4. **Storytelling** — "I heard about [character] who faced [constraint]…"
5. **Analogy** — "it's like how we used to believe [analogous belief]…"
6. **Thought Experiment** — "imagine for a second that [constraint] wasn't set in stone…"
7. **Personal Challenge/Experiment** — "I challenged myself with [experiment] and discovered…"
8. **Consequences** — "if we keep believing [constraint], we risk [negative consequence]…"
9. **Counterintuitive Wisdom** — "[respected figure] said '[counterintuitive quote]'…"
10. **Paradox** — "[paradoxical example that contradicts the belief]…"

For each constraint: state the **bundled assumptions**, target the load-bearing one, pick the vehicle, write the dissolution, note the reasoning.

## I. SUPPORTING MECHANISMS (the verbal/hidden layer)

- **Interoceptive Mechanisms** — pick the mechanism whose sensory word-picture is congruent with how the body experiences the problem (joint "hydration" > pain "switch"). Mismatch = unbelievable or an ejection trigger.
- **Ejection Triggers** — phrases that cause autopilot shutdown (pain market: "it's all in your head"). Map per market before writing.
- **Market Addictions** — some segments are addicted to *exploring the cause*, not just relief. Sell exploration to the curious, relief to the suffering.
- **Concentric Circles of Concern** — what's immediately top-of-mind; marry new ideas to already-proven static ideas to borrow credibility.
- **Desire Daisy-Chain** — benefit → feeling → relationship → identity, each link pulling toward the aspirational identity.
