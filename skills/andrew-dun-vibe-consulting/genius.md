# Andrew Dun — Genius Patterns & Hidden Knowledge

> Load this file before executing any workflow in this skill domain.

## Core Philosophy
"Prescription without diagnosis is malpractice." Never discuss solutions until the problem is fully mapped and quantified. The value isn't in building workflows — it's in knowing WHICH workflows to build and proving the ROI before touching a single tool.

## The 6-Step Framework
1. **Problem Identification** → Interview executives (destination) AND operators (road)
2. **Process Mapping** → Document every click, tab switch, manual handoff
3. **ROI Quantification** → `Time Wasted × People × Days/Year × Hourly Cost = Annual Waste`
4. **Prescriptive Solution** → Opportunity matrix: quick wins first, big swings second
5. **Implementation** → Self, partner (10-15% referral), or white-label (50% margin)
6. **Maintenance & Advisory** → Recurring retainer ($3-5K/mo), MSP-style lock-in

## Genius Patterns

### 1. The Lamborghini Chassis Principle
Companies bolting AI onto broken processes create "a Toyota Corolla with Lamborghini parts." Before ANY implementation, build the centralized knowledge base (the "chassis"). Ask: "Where does all your data live?" If the answer involves 5+ disconnected systems, the first recommendation is ALWAYS a unified data layer.

### 2. The "Then What?" Interview Cascade
Sit with operators (not executives). Make them narrate their ENTIRE day chronologically. Probe every click. They will intuitively skip inefficiencies because they're habituated — that's where the gold is. "Walk me through exactly what you did yesterday morning, from the moment you sat down."

### 3. Operators Know the Road, Executives Know the Destination
ALWAYS run dual-track interviews. CEOs are "too far away" from core problems. The delta between what executives THINK happens and what ACTUALLY happens is where the most expensive problems hide. This gap typically reveals 2-3 major waste areas the CEO didn't know existed.

### 4. Quick Wins as Springboard
Build a 2×2 opportunity matrix (value × difficulty). Always start with "quick wins" (high value, low cost). First deliverable produces measurable ROI within 30 days. Use that proven ROI as the springboard to sell the larger implementation contract. Never lead with the big swing.

### 5. The Change Management Gate
Strategy + implementation without adoption = failure. Before accepting ANY implementation: verify a senior-level AI Champion exists with org-chart authority. If not, prescribe change management and training FIRST. The #1 predictor of project failure is lack of internal championship.

### 6. Bottom-Line Focus (Profit Delta)
Frame every proposal in profit terms, not revenue. "You're at $10M ARR with $1.5M profit. We'll raise that to $2.2M." Quantify savings (reduced labor waste, reduced tool waste, increased efficiency) rather than growth promises. Business owners buy certainty, not projections.

### 7. Non-Technical Positioning as Advantage
Never lead with technical complexity. "I struggle to turn on my computer" becomes a trust signal — you're not selling tools, you're selling clarity. The moment you explain N8N flows, you've lost the executive. Translate every technical solution into profit/time language.

### 8. The ROI Quantification Formula
For EVERY identified inefficiency: `Time Wasted × People × Days/Year × Loaded Hourly Cost = Annual Waste`. Multiple bottlenecks aggregate. Present the total annual waste figure BEFORE discussing any solution. Frame against 12-month payback benchmark — enterprise considers 10-year acceptable, so under 12 months is a "home run."

### 9. The 4-Question AI Suitability Filter
Before recommending AI for any process, it must pass all four:
1. Structured input? (Not ambiguous data)
2. Rule-based decisions? (Clear logic, not judgment calls)
3. Predictable output? (Not creative/subjective)
4. Repeated often? (Not one-off tasks)

## Hidden Knowledge

### The 9-to-5 Narration Technique
Employees will skip over inefficiencies because they're normalized. The trick: make them narrate their ENTIRE day chronologically, not ask about specific processes. "Show me every click of your mouse" surfaces waste that targeted questions miss.

### The Assumption Decomposition
"We follow up with leads" sounds like one task. It's actually 7: open CRM → filter leads → check ICP match → read submission → draft response → send email → set reminder. EVERY "one task" is 4-10 sub-tasks. Decompose every assumption.

### The Backend Deal (First Clients)
For your first engagement: charge nothing upfront. "If you're satisfied, it'll be a couple thousand. If not, just give me a case study, testimonial, and reference." Eliminates buyer risk. Guarantees the three assets you actually need (social proof > cash at this stage).

### Technical Partnership Leverage Model
| Level | Description | Your Margin |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| **Referral** | Pass implementation to partner, earn commission | 10-15% |
| **White-Label** | Partner does technical work, you own the client | 50% |
| **Full-Stack** | You implement directly | 100% (higher skill req) |

### The Recurring Maintenance Lock-In
AI evolves so fast that implementations need updating within 3 months. This creates natural recurring revenue ($3-5K/mo advisory retainers). Mirrors the IT MSP model — clients stay for years because switching costs are prohibitive. Once you've built their AI infrastructure, you own maintenance.

### The 12-Month Payback Home Run
Enterprise companies consider 10-year payback acceptable. AI consulting typically delivers under 12 months. Frame every ROI calculation against this benchmark — it makes even $100K+ engagements feel conservative.

## Anti-Patterns (Never Do This)

- ❌ Prescribe solutions before completing diagnosis
- ❌ Interview only executives (skip operators)
- ❌ Quote "time savings" without dollar figures
- ❌ Lead with technical tool names in client conversations
- ❌ Skip the change management gate before implementation
- ❌ Try to close on implementation before proving ROI with an audit
- ❌ Accept an engagement without an identified AI Champion

Math: 2hrs/day × 8 SDRs × 260 days × $40/hr = **$166,400/year wasted on ONE process**. This single finding justified the entire engagement cost multiple times over. This is the power of diagnosis.
