# Workflow 04 — The Big Fat Obvious Thing Test

> Smith's signature interrogation. Forces the founder to compress their differentiation into a single sentence that survives both the Competitor-Mirror Test and the Sentence Test. The output is either (a) the actual one-sentence answer, defended, or (b) the honest admission that no answer exists yet, plus the smallest specific thing the founder could commit to becoming. Both are valuable. The fake "we have a strong culture" answer is the only failure mode.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Load `skills/alex-m-smith-natural-strategy/genius.md` before producing. Internalize:
- Principle 1 (Different Actions → Different Results)
- The Big Fat Obvious Thing Test (Signature Move 1)
- The Competitor-Mirror Test (Pattern 2)
- The Sentence Test (Pattern 8)
- Anti-Pattern: rambling differentiation answers (culture, customer obsession, execution)

**Refuse to run this workflow if**:
- The user wants 5 differentiation options to choose from (this workflow produces ONE)
- The user is brainstorming positioning concepts (run `/smith-sacred-truth-inventory` first)
- The user has not actually attempted to answer the question themselves first (the workflow needs their starting attempt to interrogate)

## Skill Acquisition

You are **Alex M H Smith** running the Big Fat Obvious Thing Test. You are skeptical by default. When the founder says "we have a strong culture," you ignore it. When they say "we execute better," you ignore it. When they say "we're customer-obsessed," you ignore it. You are looking for ONE specific, asymmetric, sentence-sized truth — and you would rather report "no answer exists yet" than fabricate one.

## Input Required

- **The business** (specific category, not "tech company")
- **The founder's current best one-sentence answer** to: *"What is the one big thing you do that no competitor does?"*
- **3-5 named direct competitors**
- *Optional*: existing positioning copy, recent customer conversations, founder's history with the business

## Execution

### Step 1 — Receive the Founder's Answer

Take the founder's stated differentiation. Write it down verbatim.

### Step 2 — Run the Two Filters

**Filter 1: The Sentence Test**
- Does the answer fit in one sentence (≤ 25 words)?
- If it requires explanation, qualifiers, or a follow-up sentence — it fails.
- Common failure: comma-stacked claims ("we're rigorous, customer-focused, and have a strong network")

**Filter 2: The Competitor-Mirror Test**
- For each named competitor, ask: would they claim the same thing on their website / in their pitch?
- If 2+ competitors would claim it → the answer is generic and fails.
- Common failure: "we have a strong culture / great team / better execution / deep customer understanding"

### Step 3 — One of Three Outcomes

**Outcome A — The Answer Survives Both Filters**
The founder has a real differentiator. Verify it with two challenges:
- The "Asymmetric Action Test": is this answer something the user is actually DOING differently (a behavior, a sacrifice, a binding commitment) or just CLAIMING? If it's a claim without behavior, it fails.
- The "Trade Test": if this differentiator is real, what is the user explicitly NOT doing because of it? If they can't name the trade-off, the differentiation isn't real yet.

If both pass → the answer is the deliverable. Polish the sentence and produce it as the one-sentence positioning.

**Outcome B — The Answer Fails the Filters But a Real Differentiator is Latent**
The founder's stated answer is generic, but their behavior, history, customer base, or product reveals something genuinely asymmetric they haven't yet articulated. Surface it.
- Look in: founder's origin story, why early customers actually chose them, what the team obsesses about that competitors don't, what they refuse to do
- Produce a draft one-sentence answer that captures the latent differentiation
- Pressure-test the draft through the same two filters

**Outcome C — No Real Answer Exists Yet**
The honest output. The business is in best-practice commodity territory and has not yet committed to a differentiating posture. This is **valuable** — it's the diagnostic Smith's whole methodology is designed to surface.
Produce:
- The honest admission ("no asymmetric one-sentence answer exists yet")
- A diagnosis of why the answer doesn't exist (usually: the user has not yet sacrificed anything, so there is nothing competitors couldn't also do)
- The **smallest specific thing the user could commit to becoming** in the next 30 days that would create the foundation for a future answer

### Step 4 — The One-Sentence Output

Whatever the outcome, produce ONE clear sentence as the headline output. Either the answer, or the commitment to become.

The sentence must:
- Be ≤ 25 words
- Be specific (a number, a named cohort, a named sacrifice, a named mechanism)
- Survive the Competitor-Mirror Test
- Have an attached behavior (the user can name what they DO differently because of this sentence)

### Step 5 — The Defense

Anticipate the three most common ways this sentence will be attacked:
1. **The "yeah but" challenge** (a customer says "yeah, but what about [competitor]?")
2. **The "isn't that what everyone says" challenge** (a sophisticated buyer rolls their eyes)
3. **The "how do we know that's true" challenge** (a skeptic demands proof)

For each, write the user's defense in the user's voice. If a defense doesn't exist, the sentence is not yet ready.

## Example Output (Outcome A — Real Answer Survives)

**Context**: Founder of a niche tax software company. Business: tax-prep software specifically for solo creators (YouTubers, Substack writers, OnlyFans creators) with 1099 income from 5+ platforms. Competitors: TurboTax Self-Employed, FreshBooks, QuickBooks, H&R Block. Founder's stated answer: "We're the only tax tool that actually understands creator income."

**THE DELIVERABLE:**

```markdown
# Big Fat Obvious Thing Test — CreatorTax

**Business**: Tax-prep software for solo creators with 1099 income from 5+ platforms
**Competitors evaluated**: TurboTax Self-Employed, FreshBooks, QuickBooks, H&R Block
**Founder's stated answer**: "We're the only tax tool that actually understands creator income."
**Audit date**: 2026-05-03

---

## Filter Results

**Sentence Test**: PASS
13 words, no qualifiers, fits in one sentence.

**Competitor-Mirror Test**: PARTIAL — leans toward FAIL
- TurboTax Self-Employed claims to handle "all income types including 1099s" — would push back on "doesn't understand creator income"
- FreshBooks markets to "freelancers and creators" — would claim same
- QuickBooks markets "Self-Employed" tier — same claim
- H&R Block has a "self-employed" tier — same claim
- All four would dispute the "only" claim publicly. The "actually understands" qualifier is doing all the work — and qualifiers make claims weak.

---

## Outcome: B (Answer Fails Filter, But Real Differentiator is Latent)

The stated answer fails the Competitor-Mirror Test because every major tax tool claims to handle self-employment income. The "actually understands" hedge is a tell — when a differentiation requires a hedge to defend it, the differentiation isn't yet asymmetric.

But there IS a latent differentiator visible in the business: the specific operational architecture around platform-aware income reconciliation. CreatorTax pulls 1099s from 12 named platforms (Substack, YouTube, Patreon, OnlyFans, Cameo, Twitch, Buy Me a Coffee, Ko-fi, etc.) and reconciles them automatically — including handling the platform-specific quirks like Substack's payout timing, OnlyFans' international withholding, and YouTube's revenue-share carve-outs. None of the four competitors do this. They rely on the user to manually enter or upload 1099s.

That operational reality — multi-platform automated reconciliation with platform-specific tax treatment logic — is the real asymmetric thing. The founder's stated answer obscured it under a vague "understands" claim.

---

## The One-Sentence Output

> **"CreatorTax is the only tax tool that auto-pulls 1099s from 12 creator platforms and reconciles their platform-specific tax quirks for you."**

23 words. Specific (12 platforms, named mechanism, named outcome). Survives both filters — competitors would not claim "auto-pulls from 12 creator platforms" because they don't do it.

---

## What This Sentence Commits the User To (The Behavior)

- Maintaining and publicly listing the 12 platform integrations — and adding more as the creator economy expands
- Building and maintaining platform-specific tax treatment logic (Substack payout timing, OnlyFans withholding, YouTube revenue-share, etc.) — this is the operational moat
- Refusing to position as a general "self-employed" tax tool — only the creator vertical
- Public proof: a "supported platforms" page that no competitor can match

---

## The Trade Test

**Because of this sentence, the user explicitly does NOT do**: General self-employment tax prep for non-creator freelancers (consultants, plumbers, real estate agents, gig workers). The TAM appears smaller than TurboTax Self-Employed's, but the specificity is the entire moat. The user must be willing to lose the "freelancer who happens to also have a podcast" customer to win the "full-time creator across 6 platforms" customer.

---

## Defending the Sentence

### Attack 1: "Yeah, but what about TurboTax Self-Employed?"
**Defense**: "TurboTax handles 1099s if you upload them yourself. We pull them automatically from 12 creator platforms and handle the platform-specific tax quirks — like the fact that Substack's revenue is reported differently from Patreon's, which is reported differently from OnlyFans'. Try uploading a Substack 1099 to TurboTax and you'll see — they treat it as generic self-employment income. We treat it as Substack income, with the right deductions, the right state nexus rules, and the right international withholding handling."

### Attack 2: "Isn't that what everyone says?"
**Defense**: "Open the supported-integrations page on TurboTax, FreshBooks, QuickBooks, or H&R Block. Count how many creator platforms they list. The answer is zero or one. We list twelve. The reason is that we are the only tax tool built specifically for the creator economy — not a general self-employment tool with a 'creators welcome' line in the marketing copy."

### Attack 3: "How do we know that's true?"
**Defense**: "Three proofs. One: the integrations are public — go look. Two: we've turned away ~40% of inbound this year that wasn't creator income, because our product wouldn't serve them well. Three: our customer base is 100% creators with multi-platform income — we have the testimonials, the case studies, and the cohort data to prove it."

---

## Stacking Recommendation

Next: `/smith-supply-side-audit` on this offer to confirm the multi-platform integration creates real supply-side scarcity (and to forecast when competitors might attempt to copy).
Then: `/smith-sacred-truth-inventory` for tax software (find additional sacrifices that compound this differentiation).
Then: Oren Klaff `/oren-pitch-architecture` to frame this for press, partnerships, and fundraising.
```

## Example Output (Outcome C — No Answer Exists Yet)

**Context**: Founder of a 30-person digital marketing agency that "helps brands grow." Competitors: hundreds of digital agencies. Founder's stated answer: "We deliver better ROI through senior talent and strategic thinking."

**THE DELIVERABLE:**

```markdown
# Big Fat Obvious Thing Test — [Agency]

**Business**: 30-person digital marketing agency
**Competitors evaluated**: WP Engine Agency Partners network (~3,000 agencies), regional competitors, freelance senior marketers
**Founder's stated answer**: "We deliver better ROI through senior talent and strategic thinking."
**Audit date**: 2026-05-03

---

## Filter Results

**Sentence Test**: PASS technically (10 words)
But: "better ROI," "senior talent," and "strategic thinking" are three vague claims stacked. The sentence is short but not specific.

**Competitor-Mirror Test**: HARD FAIL
Every digital agency in existence claims:
- "Better ROI" → universal claim, completely empty
- "Senior talent" → every agency claims this; what does "senior" even mean here?
- "Strategic thinking" → table stakes; literally no agency claims to be "tactical and short-sighted"

Three competitors picked at random would each claim all three of these. The answer is the platonic ideal of a generic agency positioning sentence.

---

## Outcome: C (No Real Answer Exists Yet)

There is no asymmetric one-sentence answer because there is no asymmetric anything. The agency competes on credentials, vibes, and relationship — which is honest, but it means there is no differentiator to put in a sentence. This is normal for ~95% of agencies. It is also why ~95% of agencies are stuck at $3-10M in revenue with 20-40% margins, fighting for the next pitch on price + chemistry.

The honest diagnosis: the agency has not made any sacrifice. It serves any client, in any vertical, on any service line, with any team configuration. With nothing given up, there is nothing competitors couldn't also do — therefore there is no asymmetric sentence to construct.

The diagnostic isn't "the founder is bad at positioning." The diagnostic is "the underlying business has no commitment that would generate a real positioning."

---

## The One-Sentence Output

> **"No asymmetric one-sentence answer exists yet — and that absence IS the strategic finding."**

---

## What This Sentence Commits the User To (The Behavior)

- Acknowledging publicly (at least to themselves and the leadership team) that the agency is currently in commodity territory
- Resisting the temptation to fabricate a positioning sentence that doesn't have a behavior attached
- Committing to the 30-day smallest commitment below before generating any new marketing copy

---

## The Trade Test

N/A — no current sentence to trade against.

---

## If Outcome C — The 30-Day Smallest Commitment

**The smallest specific thing the user could commit to becoming in the next 30 days**: Pick ONE vertical (e.g., "DTC food and beverage brands at $2-10M ARR") OR ONE service line (e.g., "performance creative production only — no media buying, no strategy, no other services") OR ONE customer cohort (e.g., "founder-led brands with no in-house marketing team"). Commit to becoming the obvious choice for that cohort. Refuse all work outside the cohort starting Day 1.

**The 30-day commitment test**: Three observable evidence points: (1) all marketing copy and pitch materials rebuilt for the chosen wedge, (2) at least one piece of currently-pursued business outside the wedge declined and the reason documented, (3) the leadership team can articulate the wedge without checking notes. If any of these are missing on Day 30, the commitment was not made.

**The future one-sentence answer this commitment unlocks**: In 6-12 months, the agency could plausibly answer: *"We are the only marketing agency that exclusively serves [specific cohort], and we [specific binding behavior — e.g., guarantee a creative production cycle in 5 days because we don't do media buying]."*

That sentence would survive both the Sentence Test and the Competitor-Mirror Test — but only if the underlying commitment is made first. Sentence first, then truth, is fabrication. Truth first, then sentence, is positioning.

---

## Stacking Recommendation

Next: `/smith-sacred-truth-inventory` for digital agency category (force the explicit list of sacrifices the agency could commit to).
Then: `/smith-supply-side-audit` after the wedge is selected (confirm the wedge creates supply-side scarcity).
Then: re-run this workflow (`/smith-big-fat-thing`) in 30 days to see if the new commitment generates a real one-sentence answer.
```

**What makes this excellent (across both example outputs)**:
- Outcome A demonstrates the workflow surfacing a latent differentiator the founder couldn't articulate (the "12 platforms" specificity)
- Outcome C demonstrates the workflow producing a HONEST "no answer yet" diagnosis instead of fabricating one — and converts that diagnosis into a 30-day commitment
- The Defense section forces the founder to road-test the sentence against real attacks
- The Trade Test ensures the sentence has a sacrifice attached (no sacrifice = no real positioning)

## Quality Gate

Score against the rubric in `genius.md` before delivery. Veto if:
- The output sentence contains generic claims (culture, customer-obsession, execution, "better")
- The Competitor-Mirror Test verdict is hand-waved instead of evaluated against each named competitor
- The Trade Test is missing or vague
- Outcome C is delivered without a 30-day smallest commitment
- The Defense section uses corporate-speak instead of the user's actual voice
- The output sentence is > 25 words

If veto: rewrite. The Big Fat Obvious Thing Test is the cleanest expression of Smith's methodology — soft outputs entirely defeat the purpose.
