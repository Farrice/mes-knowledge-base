# Kunal Shah — Consumer Psychology & Delta-4 Opportunity Evaluation

Kunal Shah founded FreeCharge (sold to Snapdeal) and CRED, and is the only humanities-background founder among India's unicorns — a philosophy student who treats startups as applied evolutionary biology. His core move: never ask "is this product good?" — ask "does this ride a river of core human motivation, and does it improve the efficiency of a desired behavior by 4+ points?" Everything else (positioning, trust, talent, pricing) hangs off status, shame, and the human drive from inefficiency toward efficiency.

## Quick Reference

- **Delta-4 rule**: Score the old behavior and the new behavior on efficiency (0-10). If the delta >= 4, three things follow: (1) irreversible behavior — users cannot go back, (2) high tolerance — they'll hate the flaws but never return to the inefficient way, (3) UBP — a Unique Bragworthy Proposition; users can't stop telling others. Delta < 4 (online shirt-buying with sizing friction) = the behavior never fully migrates. Adding tech does not make something Delta-4; only raising the efficiency score of the *desired behavior* does.
- **Rivers vs dams**: Motivations are rivers (status, mating success, progeny success — the evolutionary core). Most founders build ever-fancier dams in deserts hoping rivers show up. Mediocre dams on real rivers beat brilliant dams with no water. Move the dam, don't decorate it.
- **Insight = the smallest unit of truth that is actionable.** Operate in the currency of insights; successful people almost always do.
- **Trust concentrates in low-trust societies**: super apps, conglomerates (Tata), nepotism, celebrity-skew — familiarity substitutes for institutional trust. In such markets, launch adjacencies under one trusted brand; in high-trust markets, specialists win.
- **Shame-to-pride conversion**: the fastest path from shame to normalcy is pride. Whatever people declare they're proud of is also the handle by which they can be moved.
- **Reputation risk**: you cannot build substance, net worth, or experience without repeatedly risking 30-40% of your reputation. Shame-trained people can't risk 1% — and stall.
- **High-slope people**: a company becomes "big" (in the bad sense) the day its concentration of high-slope people drops. Obsess over revenue-per-employee to protect talent density.
- **Wealth stores itself in information asymmetry**: rarely will you meet a wealthy person who cannot keep secrets. Be a trader of nuances, not a superconductor of memes.
