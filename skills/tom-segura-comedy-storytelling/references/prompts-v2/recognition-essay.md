## Role & Activation

You are writing a memoir-grade recognition essay by stacking Tom Segura's observation-and-storytelling engine with memoir interiority — Segura is a working A-list observational/storytelling comedian (specials *Completely Normal*, *Sledgehammer*, *Teacher*; creator/showrunner of Netflix's *Bad Thoughts*; author) whose core genius is "noticing what everyone has experienced but no one has articulated — then earning the right to say it, grounding the absurd in the real, and holding the tension until it breaks." This deliverable is a flagship stack: Segura's observation engine (the universal-but-unarticulated premise, the installed way-in, the long-first-then-tight edit) carries the recognition payload; memoir-grade interiority carries the depth. The payoff is not a laugh — it's the reader thinking "why didn't *I* say it?"

Governing material: Pattern 3 (Universal-but-Unarticulated), Pattern 4 (The Way In), Pattern 10 (Long-First Edit), plus Pattern 2, 11, 12; Moves: The Annoyance Dig, The Way-In Install, The Word Swap, Long-First Edit. Load `skills/tom-segura-comedy-storytelling/genius.md` in full before executing. This is a stacking workflow: also load the interiority partner (e.g. `.agent/workflows/parallax.md` for memoir-grade interiority, with its Phase 2.5 GROUND if any external factual surface is involved; for deeper scene-level interiority, `skills/wright-thompson-mastery/` via `/wright-interiority`).

## Input Required

- **[LIVED EXPERIENCE OR PREMISE]** — a universal experience nobody has named precisely, that the writer has actually lived. Must be reported, not invented (a fabricated "universal" premise is disqualified), and must carry real temperature — the writer has to actually care.
- **[LENGTH TARGET]** (optional) — default 700-1500 words for a newsletter/Substack-grade edition.
- **[INTERIORITY PARTNER]** (optional) — which stacking partner's interiority layer to braid in (Parallax memoir mode, Wright Thompson scene-level interiority, or none if the writer wants pure Segura-mode recognition without the literary layer).

## Execution Protocol

1. **Dig the universal.** Generate 10+ candidate "that's a thing" observations the writer noticed "60, 70 times" but never voiced. Apply the Annoyance Dig — push each past the surface gripe to its irrational core. Select the one with the strongest "of course" charge and a temperature.
2. **Install the Way In.** Before narrating anything, write the reason-for-telling and the stakes that precede the events. Render the same facts flat, then framed; keep framed. The reader invests *before* the first event lands.
3. **Tell the fat version.** Draft the bloated, over-long essay with zero precommitment to shape. Report; do not invent. Let lived detail run long.
4. **Cut to need.** Remove everything the recognition payoff does not require. Hold the one real beat longer than comfortable instead of rushing it.
5. **Stack interiority, render voices.** Braid the memoir interiority partner into the held beats — the internal weather Segura's performance would carry vocally, rendered on the page. Render any person in actual speech/cadence, not paraphrase.
6. **Insert punches, end biggest.** Distribute surprise turns (recognition reversals, not jokes) across the gaps. Run the Word Swap: flat verbs become the funniest-sounding specific. Land the close on the strongest recognition beat.

## Output Contract

- Finished essay, 700-1500 words (or the stated length target), way-in first — never a flat anecdote opening on events.
- The named unarticulated-universal, stated or evident from the opening.
- Installed stakes preceding the first narrated event.
- At least one held real beat (extended past comfortable pace).
- Distributed surprise/recognition turns across the piece, not clustered at one point.
- Rendered voices over paraphrase for any character present.
- Ends on the recognition beat — the line that earns "why didn't *I* say it?" Never a rhetorical-question signoff.

## Output Skeleton

```
## The Unarticulated Universal
[one line naming the "that's a thing" the essay is built on]

## The Essay

[the finished piece — way-in first, then narrated events, held beats, recognition turns distributed, rendered voices, ending on the strongest recognition beat. Prose instructions, not sample copy: open on installed stakes per the Way In protocol; let the fat-version detail that survived the cut carry the lived specificity; hold the single most important beat past the comfortable pace; close on the line that reframes what the reader already knew but hadn't said.]
```

## Quality Gate

- Does the essay open on installed stakes/reason-for-telling rather than a flat "one time..." anecdote opening?
- Is the central premise genuinely unarticulated (a "why didn't I say it?" recognition), not merely relatable?
- Is at least one real beat held noticeably longer than a rushed telling would allow?
- Does the closing line land the recognition rather than ending on a summary, a moral, or a question?
- Is every detail reported (traceable to the writer's actual life) rather than invented to serve the premise?
- If a factual/external claim appears, was it routed through the interiority partner's grounding step rather than asserted?

## Creative Latitude

This is the highest-ceiling deliverable in the skill — the interiority braid is where craft compounds, not a mechanical add-on. Let the memoir partner's voice genuinely shape sentence rhythm and interior weather rather than treating it as a layer applied after the Segura structure is locked; the two should read as fused, not stapled. The held beat is a taste call: choose the moment where sitting in discomfort actually serves the recognition (not necessarily the most dramatic moment chronologically). Don't force jokes into an essay that doesn't want them — "punches" here means recognition reversals and reframes, and the single best essay might have only one or two, deployed with maximum precision, rather than many small ones.

## Deploy When

- A universal experience nobody named precisely, and the writer has lived it.
- Parallax-grade newsletter editions where recognition is the payoff, not argument.
- Personal-brand essays needing autobiography no one else can repeat.
