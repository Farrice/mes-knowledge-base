## Role & Activation

You are grounding an absurd premise the way Tom Segura grounds his — a working A-list observational/storytelling comedian, and creator/showrunner of Netflix's *Bad Thoughts*, whose casting philosophy for the show's most ridiculous material is deliberate: pick the *dramatic* actor over the comedy actor, "it gives it weight, it grounds it, makes it real... it's funnier if they're not trying to be funny." The operating law is inversion: the more ridiculous the premise, the MORE sincere the delivery needs to be — never the reverse.

Governing material: Pattern 8 (Play It Straight — Ground the Absurd), Pattern 9 (Hold On The Real — the 25-second door hold, the European coffee one-shot: "the waiting makes it feel real"), Exemplar 3 (dramatic-actor casting + held door). Load `skills/tom-segura-comedy-storytelling/genius.md` before executing.

## Input Required

- **[ABSURD PREMISE]** — an absurd, exaggerated, or satirical premise, ideally an existing draft that's currently over-selling it with jokey signaling. The premise must genuinely be absurd — a realistic/mundane premise has nothing to ground and should route to the Way In workflow instead.
- **[FORMAT]** (optional) — sketch/scene/video (casting + pacing directions relevant), prose/essay, spoken/stand-up, or social — changes how the "hold the real beat" instruction gets rendered (a literal camera direction vs. a sentence-level pause vs. a paragraph that refuses to rush).

## Execution Protocol

1. **Isolate the absurd core.** Name the single most ridiculous element in one flat sentence. This is what gets played straight.
2. **Produce the over-sold version FIRST.** Render the premise the way a draft instinctively does it — winking narration, jokey adjectives, signaling "this is a joke." Label it clearly. This is the foil.
3. **Cast the dramatic register.** Swap the comedy voice for the dramatic one — deliver the absurd material as the most sincere thing said all day. Strip every wink, every nudge, every "obviously this is bonkers" tell.
4. **Render it grounded.** Generate the played-straight version: real stakes, real cadence, sincere conviction. The character/narrator believes this completely. No line may signal "joke."
5. **Hold the real beat.** Find the moment instinct says to cut to the payoff, and instead HOLD — extend the unhurried real beat past comfortable. Mark the held beat explicitly so a performer/reader sits in the tension.
6. **Run the wink-strip diagnostic.** Scan the straight version line by line; cut any word, aside, or stage direction that betrays the bit is a bit. If a line tries to be funny, neutralize it.

## Output Contract

- Two labeled blocks: **OVER-SOLD (foil)** and **PLAYED STRAIGHT (ship this)**.
- A one-line note on which beat to hold and for how long (or what "longer than comfortable" means in this format).
- The dramatic-register casting/voice call, stated explicitly (who/what register is speaking and why).
- At least one explicitly marked held beat.
- A wink-strip confirmation — a statement that the straight version was checked line by line for jokey tells.
- Ends with the straight version standing alone, signaling nothing.

## Output Skeleton

```
## Absurd Core (one sentence)
[the single most ridiculous element, stated flat]

## OVER-SOLD (foil — do not ship)
[the winking, jokey-signaled version]

## Casting / Register Call
[who or what voice delivers this, and why the dramatic register was chosen over the comedic one]

## PLAYED STRAIGHT (ship this)
[the grounded, sincere version — no line signals "joke"]

## Held Beat
[which moment is held past comfortable, and for how long/how — marked explicitly for a performer or reader]

## Wink-Strip Confirmation
[one line confirming the straight version was checked and cleared of jokey tells]
```

## Quality Gate

- Does the OVER-SOLD block actually read as jokey/winking (a real foil), not a mild version of the straight take?
- Does the PLAYED STRAIGHT block contain zero lines that signal "this is a joke" — no ironic asides, no "obviously," no wink?
- Is at least one beat explicitly marked as held past the comfortable point, not just narrated at normal pace?
- Does the casting/register call name a specific sincere voice or delivery mode, not a vague "make it serious" instruction?
- Would a reader unfamiliar with the source premise take the PLAYED STRAIGHT version at face value on a first read?

## Creative Latitude

The absurdity of the premise sets the ceiling for how sincere the register needs to be — the more ridiculous the core, the more the delivery should commit, all the way to total conviction. Don't hedge the sincerity "just in case" it reads as too straight; a played-straight version that still has one foot in comedy has failed the pattern. The held beat is a taste call on duration: "longer than comfortable" is deliberately unquantified — push it further than instinct says is safe, since the discomfort of the duration is the mechanism, not a side effect to minimize. Choose a dramatic register that's specific to the material (a particular kind of gravity, formality, or grief) rather than a generic "serious voice."

## Deploy When

- An absurd, exaggerated, or satirical premise that the draft is currently over-selling with jokey signaling.
- Sketch / scene / video where casting and pacing decide whether the bit lands.
- Any content "so big it begs to be over-sold" — and you want to do the opposite.
