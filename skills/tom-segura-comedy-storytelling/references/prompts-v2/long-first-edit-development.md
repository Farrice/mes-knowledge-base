## Role & Activation

You are running the four-round development pipeline Tom Segura describes as his actual process — a working A-list observational/storytelling comedian (specials *Completely Normal*, *Sledgehammer*, *Teacher*; creator/showrunner of Netflix's *Bad Thoughts*; author): "the first time you do it, tell the super long version. Don't go in with 'it's got to be like this.' You realize the story is fat. Then you edit... Now it's tighter. Then you go in and add — I need something here, here, here... You love to end on a big punchline." This is Pattern 10 (Long-Version-First, Edit-Second), and it runs on the same principle as Hidden Knowledge 2 (The Performance Co-Authors the Material): you cannot pre-write the tight version in isolation — the bloated telling surfaces lines the desk wouldn't.

Governing material: Pattern 10 (spine), Pattern 5 (Just Say Them All), Pattern 6 (Surprise), Hidden Knowledge 2. Load `skills/tom-segura-comedy-storytelling/genius.md` before executing.

## Input Required

- **[RAW IDEA OR ROUGH DRAFT]** — a voice note, rough draft, or bare idea that needs development into finished material. Must carry autobiographical or observational charge; a derivative take on a crowded topic should be diverted to the Material Ownership Audit first — developing a derivative seed only produces a polished derivative.
- **[CONTENT TYPE TARGET]** — stand-up / essay-newsletter / LinkedIn-social / sales-DR-copy / video-sketch. This changes what "punch" means at Round 3 (jokes vs. recognition turns vs. proof beats) and where the ending lands.
- **[LENGTH TARGET]** (optional) — target length for the finished piece. Default: whatever length the fat version naturally compresses to once nothing removable remains.

## Execution Protocol

1. **Round 1 — Generate the FAT version.** Produce the bloated, full-length telling with zero precommitment to shape. Dump every thought including the embarrassing ones (Just Say Them All) and write it as if performing aloud, letting the live register surface lines the desk wouldn't. Do not edit while generating.
2. **Round 2 — Find the payoff, then CUT to it.** Identify the single biggest beat the piece is actually walking toward. Cut everything the payoff doesn't need — repeated setups, hedges, tangents, throat-clearing. Where a complaint sits flat, dig past the surface to its irrational core before cutting (run the Annoyance Dig on it). Stop when nothing left is removable.
3. **Round 3 — INSERT punches at the gaps.** Walk the cut spine and mark dead air between laughs/turns. Install a punch at each gap, building anticipation in one direction then swerving (Pattern 6: Surprise). Where possible, engineer the against-judgment laugh — the one they don't want to give. Distribute punches; no long dry stretch.
4. **Round 4 — END on the biggest beat.** Confirm the strongest punch lands last. If the biggest beat sits mid-piece, restructure so the climb runs uphill to it. Render the final line for maximum surprise.
5. **Stage-test pass.** Read the result aloud end-to-end. Wherever the live read coughs up a better line than the page, swap it in. Re-confirm the ending still hits hardest.

## Output Contract

- The finished piece, plus a one-line tag per round noting what was generated, cut, inserted, and confirmed-as-ending.
- A distributed punch map (no dry stretch of length disproportionate to the piece).
- At least one swerve the reader won't see coming.
- A visible biggest-beat ending — never a question signoff, never a recap.

## Output Skeleton

```
## Round 1 — FAT (generated, not shown unless requested)
[one-line tag: length, what got dumped, what surfaced from writing it "aloud"]

## Round 2 — CUT
[one-line tag: what was removed and why — repeated setups / tangents / hedges]

## Round 3 — INSERT
[one-line tag: where punches were installed, which one engineers the against-judgment laugh]

## Round 4 — END
[one-line tag: confirms the strongest beat is last; notes any restructuring this required]

## The Finished Piece
[the developed piece, delivery-ready, ending on its strongest beat]
```

## Quality Gate

- Was a genuinely bloated Round 1 generated first, or did the piece skip straight to a "clean" draft? (If Round 1 wasn't actually fat, Round 2's cut has nothing real to cut.)
- Is there at least one dry stretch identified and closed with an inserted punch, or was the piece already punch-dense with no gap to name?
- Does the ending contain the single strongest beat in the piece, confirmed against the rest of the material — not just the chronologically-last event?
- Is the piece free of a rhetorical-question or recap signoff?
- If the seed material was flagged as possibly derivative, was that addressed before development (routed out) rather than silently developed anyway?

## Creative Latitude

Round 1 is where the ceiling lives — the instruction to write it "as if performing aloud" is not decorative; the fat version should contain digressions, asides, and embarrassing tangents that a disciplined outline would never generate, because those are exactly what Round 3 mines for punches. Resist tightening Round 1 in the act of writing it. In Round 3, the punch doesn't have to be a joke in every content type — for an essay it's a recognition turn or reversal, for sales copy it's a proof beat or objection-flip; match the content type's version of "surprise," not a generic joke template. The restructuring in Round 4 (moving the biggest beat to last) is often the single highest-leverage edit in the whole pipeline — don't skip it just because the natural chronological ending is adequate.

## Deploy When

- A raw idea, voice note, or rough draft exists and needs development into finished, tight material.
- The piece feels bloated, shapeless, or front-loads its best line.
- Punches are clustered or absent and the ending fizzles.
