# /segura-surprise — Surprise Architect

Takes a LINE or BEAT the reader can see coming and rebuilds it so the payoff turns somewhere they didn't expect. Deploys **Pattern 6: Surprise is the Core Mechanism** — build anticipation in one direction, turn elsewhere — and crests at **Hidden Knowledge 4: Reluctant Laughter** (the laugh they didn't want to give).

## When to Use
- A punchline, hook payoff, reveal, or headline lands flat or predictable.
- The reader could finish the sentence for you — the turn is the obvious one.
- You want the highest-rush option: the against-their-own-judgment laugh.

## When NOT to Use
- The beat is flat because it has no *stakes* or *reason-for-telling* — route to the Way In workflow (Pattern 4); surprise can't rescue a story nobody's invested in.
- The complaint hasn't been dug past the surface — route to the Annoyance Dig (Hidden 3); the swerve needs a real core to turn off of.

## Pre-Flight Gate
Load genius.md. This workflow is governed by **Pattern 6 (Surprise)**, **Hidden Knowledge 4 (Reluctant Laughter)**, and **Hidden Knowledge 5 (Provocation as Strategic Asset)**. Disqualify the input if the predictability is actually a *stakes* problem (-> Way In) or an *under-dug complaint* (-> Annoyance Dig). Surprise re-engineers a payoff that already has fuel beneath it.

## Skill Acquisition
- Load: skills/tom-segura-comedy-storytelling/genius.md — Patterns **6 (Surprise), 7 (Earned Provocation Arc), 13 (Ownership)**; Hidden Knowledge **4 (Reluctant Laughter), 5 (Provocation)**; Moves **Long-First Edit-Second, The Word Swap**.

## Execution
1. **Map the anticipation vector.** Read the input and write the single direction the reader is being pointed: the ending they're already drafting in their head. Name it in one line — "they expect X." That predicted X is the wall you turn off of (Pattern 6: "they're anticipating, and then you turn somewhere else").
2. **Generate three turn-targets, each off a different axis.** Produce one swerve that turns on *scale* (push the opinion to a disproportionate extreme — Pattern 2's temperature), one on *target* (redirect the indictment back onto the reader/complainer — the Dig logic, Hidden 3), one on *register* (play the absurd 100% straight so the turn arrives deadpan — Pattern 8).
3. **Dig one option into the reluctant laugh.** Take whichever swerve touches something the reader resists, and engineer the against-judgment version (Hidden 4): make them laugh at the thing they didn't want to find funny. Optionally name it in the line itself — "you guys actually do like this" — surfacing the held-back laugh.
4. **Install provocation backward where it earns more.** If any option carries a hot edge, deploy **Hidden 5 / Pattern 7**: state the unacceptable turn cold *first* so it can't read as your real position, then let the support follow. Resistance is the fuel; design backward from the line they'll resist.
5. **Swap for the funniest-sounding word.** On each finalist, run the Word Swap (Move): replace flat verbs with the sharper-sounding specific ("yanked" over "pulled"). Confirm the turn doesn't telegraph — cut any word that previews the swerve.
6. **Rank by surprise magnitude and render.** Order the three: #1 = largest gap between anticipated and delivered (usually the reluctant laugh), descending. For each, label the axis it turns on and the predicted reaction.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Content Type | How this shifts |
|---|---|
| Stand-up / spoken | Land the turn on the strongest word; let the held beat (Pattern 9) sit before the swerve. |
| Essay / newsletter (Parallax) | The anticipation vector is the paragraph's logical drift; turn at the line break. Reluctant laugh = the reader's own assumption indicted. |
| LinkedIn / social | Hook sets the expected lesson; payoff swerves off it. Reluctant laugh works on consensus takes the feed agrees with. |
| Sales / DR copy | Reader anticipates the pitch; turn the angle (Hidden 5) — state the thing they resist about the category first, then support it. |
| Video / sketch script | Play the swerve straight (Pattern 8); cast/deliver the turn sincerely so it never signals "joke." |

## Output Requirements
- **Format**: The original line, the named anticipation vector, then 3 ranked swerve options — each tagged with its turn-axis (scale / target / register) and predicted reaction.
- **Must include**: At least one against-judgment ("reluctant") laugh option per Hidden 4, with the optional name-it line offered.
- **Ends with**: The #1 pick rendered clean, in final word-swapped form, ready to drop in.

## Quality Gate
Score against the genius.md rubric (name the anchor for any score >=8; the **Surprise** row savant anchor is "a swerve they didn't see — or the laugh they didn't *want* to give"). Reject if any of these anti-patterns are present: **Stopping at the obvious** (Hidden 3 — the turn is still the predictable one); **Comprehensive but dead** (clear but no surprise); **Over-selling the absurd** (winking instead of the straight-faced turn); **The neutral take** (a swerve with no temperature).
