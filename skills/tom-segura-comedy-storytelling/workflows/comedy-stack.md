# /segura-stack — Full Comedy Stack

Produces a finished comedic piece by chaining two experts with a hard boundary: Segura owns **WHAT to say and the story-craft** (antenna → dig → way-in → long-first edit); Mack owns the **JOKE-MECHANICS polish** (skew, tension-relief, vivid specificity, word-pivot). Core principle: Segura is where the bit comes from; Mack engineers the laugh it earns. They stack, they do not overlap.

## When to Use
- A piece needs both upstream craft (originality, stakes, structure) **and** downstream laugh-engineering on a single pass.
- Spoken bit, recognition essay, sketch, or DR copy where flat-funny-enough isn't enough — it must land mechanically.
- You have a true experience or annoyance worth developing, not just a punchline to file off.

## When NOT to Use
- One-shot punchline / caption with no story underneath → Mack alone (`/segura-stack` is overkill).
- Pure memoir interiority with zero comedic surface → route to `/parallax` or Wright/Roth narrative.
- Idea capture only (no development needed) → `skills/tom-segura-comedy-storytelling/workflows/antenna-engine.md`.

## Pre-Flight Gate
Load genius.md. **Phase A is governed by** Pattern 1 (Antenna), Pattern 3 (Universal-but-Unarticulated), Pattern 4 (Way In), Pattern 10 (Long-First, Edit-Second), Hidden 3 (the Dig). **Disqualify the input** if it has no ownership (derivative, not autobiographical, Pattern 13) or no temperature (neutral take, Pattern 2): fix at Phase A or kill it. Do not enter Phase B until a developed-but-not-yet-mechanically-optimized piece exists. Mack polishes a built thing, never a fragment.

## Skill Acquisition
- Load: `skills/tom-segura-comedy-storytelling/genius.md` — Patterns **1, 2, 3, 4, 10, 11, 13**; Moves **The "That's a Thing" Flag, The Annoyance Dig, The "Way In" Install, Long-First Edit-Second, The Word Swap**.
- Also load (Phase B hand-off): `skills/robert-mack-comedy-writing/genius.md` — Patterns **1 (Skewed Perspective), 3 (Tension-Relief), 5 (Vivid Specificity), 9 (Word Selection)**; plus its `workflows/` for the mechanics passes. Mack's rubric scores Phase B.

## Execution

**PHASE A — SEGURA (owns WHAT + story-craft).**

1. **Antenna + ownership.** Pull the raw fragment from capture or freewrite; flag the line with charge (Pattern 1). Confirm it's *yours* — autobiography only, report don't invent (Pattern 13). Confirm it has temperature — push the opinion to a vivid extreme; reject any neutral take (Pattern 2).
2. **Dig past the obvious.** Do not state the surface complaint. Ask "what *exactly* upsets me / why does this matter?" and keep expanding until the irrational core or the unarticulated-universal surfaces (Hidden 3 + Pattern 3). The dig is the angle.
3. **Install the Way In.** Before narrating any event, write the reason-for-telling and the stakes that precede it (Pattern 4). Audience must be invested before the first beat lands.
4. **Long-first, then edit to shape.** Render the bloated full version with zero precommitment → cut everything the payoff doesn't need → end on the strongest beat (Pattern 10, rounds 1–2 only). **Output of Phase A: the developed piece — structurally sound, owned, framed, but NOT yet mechanically optimized for laughs. Hand off here.**

**PHASE B — MACK (owns JOKE-MECHANICS polish). Do not touch WHAT, stakes, or structure — only engineer the laughs inside the built piece.**

5. **Skew + tension-relief pass.** At each developed beat, install Mack's mechanics: take the truth and find the alternate valid reading (Skewed Perspective), build a beat to the edge then release benign (Tension-Relief). Distribute punches into the gaps Phase A left.
6. **Vivid-specificity pass.** Replace every generic image with a maximally picturable one (Vivid Specificity) — the reader must SEE it with eyes closed.
7. **Word-pivot polish.** Final jeweler's pass: swap each flat word for the funniest-sounding specific that bridges both meanings (Mack Word Selection × Segura's Word Swap). End on the biggest mechanically-engineered punch (Pattern 10, round 4 confirm).

## Content Type Adaptations
| Content Type | How this shifts |
|---|---|
| Stand-up / spoken | Phase A renders voices/accents (Move: render, don't paraphrase); Phase B tunes word-sound for the ear and holds the real beat. |
| Essay / newsletter (Parallax) | Way In becomes the cold open; the Dig becomes the through-line; Phase B punches stay sparse, recognition-driven. |
| LinkedIn / social | Compress Phase A to one stakes-loaded frame; Phase B leans on skew + a single named concept, not a punch density. |
| Sales / DR copy | Dig surfaces the buyer's irrational annoyance; Phase B uses tension-relief to carry the offer, word-pivot on the CTA. |
| Video / sketch script | Play absurd premises 100% straight (Pattern 8); hold beats long (Pattern 9); Phase B engineers the turn at the cut. |

## Output Requirements
- **Format**: The finished piece, with a one-line **HAND-OFF MARKER** between Phase A output and Phase B output so the boundary is auditable.
- **Must include**: an installed Way In; a visible Dig (past the surface); distributed mechanically-engineered punches; at least one vivid, picturable image; the funniest-sounding word at every pivot.
- **Ends with**: the single biggest beat — Segura's structural ending fused with Mack's mechanical punch.

## Quality Gate
Score Phase A against the Segura genius.md rubric and Phase B against the Mack genius.md rubric; **name the anchor for any score ≥8.** Reject if any present: **the neutral take** (no temperature), **the flat anecdote** (no Way In), **stopping at the obvious** (no Dig), **over-selling the absurd** (winking instead of straight), or **comprehensive but dead** (everything said plainly, no surprise/stakes/ownership). Boundary violation — Mack rewriting WHAT or Segura engineering word-mechanics — is itself a reject. Retry the weakest element once before delivery.
