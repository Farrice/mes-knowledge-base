# /segura-dig — The Annoyance Dig

Takes a COMPLAINT or IRRITATION and produces the joke hiding inside it by digging past the obvious gripe to the absurd core of your own reaction. Deploys **Hidden Knowledge 3: The Obvious Isn't the Joke — The Dig Is** and **Pattern 2: Complaining is the Engine** via the **Move: The Annoyance Dig**.

## When to Use
- A piece has a flat, neutral, or balanced take and needs a temperature.
- You have a genuine irritation and want the bit, not the complaint.
- A hook or angle states the obvious gripe and stops there.

## When NOT to Use
- The irritation is fake or borrowed. Indifference is death (Pattern 2) — route back to a topic you actually have an opinion on.
- You need a story with stakes, not a dig. Route to **/segura-way-in**.

## Pre-Flight Gate
Load genius.md. This workflow is governed by **Hidden Knowledge 3** and **Pattern 2**, executed through the **Annoyance Dig**. **Disqualify** any input with no real feeling behind it — "it's fine, I don't care" cannot be dug (Anti-Pattern 1). If the input is already a developed story rather than a gripe, route to **/segura-way-in** instead.

## Skill Acquisition
- Load: skills/tom-segura-comedy-storytelling/genius.md — Patterns [2, 6]; Hidden Knowledge [3]; Moves [The Annoyance Dig, The Word Swap].

## Execution
1. **Capture the surface complaint verbatim.** Write the gripe exactly as a person blurts it ("concert openers suck"). Label it **THE PATH, not the payoff** (Hidden Knowledge 3): the obvious complaint is where the joke starts, never where it ends.
2. **Refuse the neutral take and set the temperature** (Pattern 2). Push the opinion to a vivid extreme. If the draft reads balanced, kill the balance — the disproportion is the fuel.
3. **Dig with the core question, repeatedly.** Ask **"what EXACTLY upsets me about this?"** Answer, then ask it of the answer, 3-4 rounds, until the reaction stops being reasonable. Concert-opener exemplar: surface = "openers suck"; dig = "you're annoyed by *15 minutes*." Stop when the absurdity is YOUR overreaction, not the original offense.
4. **Turn the annoyance back on the complainer.** Produce the **self-indictment** — make the joke the irrationality of caring this much, not the thing itself. Render it as a vivid analogy if one surfaces ("like yelling at the trailer before the movie — bro, just eat some popcorn").
5. **Generate 3-5 candidate digs.** Vary the absurd specific each exposes (the unit of time, the comparison, the misplaced stakes). Include the embarrassing ones.
6. **Word-swap the winner** (Move: The Word Swap). Replace flat verbs with the funniest-sounding specific. Swap each back to the boring option to confirm the line deflates without it.
7. **Select the single sharpest dig.** Pick the one where the swerve is biggest (Pattern 6: Surprise) and the absurd specific most exposed. Deliver it as the payoff; list runners-up beneath.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Content Type | How this shifts |
|---|---|
| Stand-up / spoken | Dig out loud across reps; let the rhythm of the overreaction build before the self-indictment lands. |
| Essay / newsletter (Parallax) | The dig becomes the interior turn — the narrator catching their own absurdity on the page. |
| LinkedIn / social | Open on the surface gripe as the hook, then snap to the self-indictment as the payoff line. |
| Sales / DR copy | Dig the prospect's irritation, expose the disproportion, position the offer as what ends the overreaction. |
| Video / sketch script | Hold the dig longer than comfortable (Pattern 9); the duration of the overreaction is the comedy. |

## Output Requirements
- **Format**: surface complaint (labeled PATH) → 3-5 candidate digs → the single sharpest dig.
- **Must include**: the explicit "what EXACTLY upsets me?" chain showing each dig round; the self-indictment that turns the annoyance back on the complainer; the exposed absurd specific.
- **Ends with**: the one sharpest dig, word-swapped, standing alone as the payoff.
