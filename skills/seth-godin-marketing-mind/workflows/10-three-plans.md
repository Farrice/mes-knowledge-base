# Three Plans Architecture

> **Expert**: Seth Godin | **Skill**: seth-godin-marketing-mind | **Tier**: Practitioner
> **Produces**: Three Plans
> **Slash Command**: `/gmind-three-plans`

---

## Purpose

One plan gets defended. Three plans get thought through. Godin's answer to "what's one small step forward" for a stalled business idea, a side hustle, or a tough decision at work isn't a pep talk — it's a structural fix: *"A friend of mine taught me this great simple hack. Write three completely different business plans. They don't have to be three pages long each."* The point isn't optionality for its own sake. Writing three forces the whole brain into the room instead of just the part defending idea A. This workflow runs that hack on any live decision — business, nonprofit, campaign, or content strategy.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The Idea Being Defended** — the plan already living in someone's head, usually stated with more conviction than evidence.
2. **Context** — business launch, nonprofit, campaign, or content strategy. The mechanics don't change.
3. **Willingness to Be Spun** — confirm before starting: if the spinner lands on Plan B or C, that's the one that gets built. If the asker won't agree to this up front, the exercise is decoration.

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Diagnose — Name the One Idea Being Defended

Before generating anything, say the current plan out loud in one sentence and ask what evidence is holding it up versus what attachment is. Most stalled decisions aren't short on information — they're short on alternatives. If there's only ever been Plan A, the stall isn't strategic, it's emotional. Name that before writing anything.

### Step 2: Generate Three Completely Different Plans

Not three versions of the same plan — three different scales, models, and investments. His own example, run live on air: *"Or go even further. Three things that are like I'm a architect for snowman, I'm going to build a snow shoveling business in Buffalo, New York, or I'm going to import snow shovels. So, the snow is involved in all three, but they're completely different scales, completely different kinds of investment, completely different."* Same raw material — three unrecognizable businesses. It also runs on causes, not just commerce: *"And you can even do it if you're starting a nonprofit, right? I want to start a nonprofit that's going to help single moms, I want to start a nonprofit that's going to help [veterans] and you can look where am I going to get donors for this versus donors for that."* Different donor bases are a different plan, not a variation.

### Step 3: Answer Four Questions Per Plan

Each plan — all three, not just the favorite — answers the same four questions before it counts as a real plan: *"Who's it for? What's it for? Who am I serving? How will I know if it's working?"* A plan that can't answer all four in a sentence each isn't a plan yet, it's a wish. Don't let Plan A get more detail than B or C — that imbalance is the defending part of the brain reasserting itself.

### Step 4: The Whole-Brain Rule

State the mechanism, not just the exercise: *"So, now instead of falling in love with one idea and defending it, you have A, B, or C. And that's the only way you're going to fall out of love with A is to make B even better. And then C, a completely different one. Because when you have to write three completely different plans, you're going to think about it with your whole brain, not the defending part of it."* The three plans aren't a brainstorm — they're a check on the part of the mind that's already decided and is now just building a case.

### Step 5: The Random-Spinner Commitment

Before comparing the three, commit to letting chance choose: *"Knowing that you're going to randomly pick one as it, you know, the spinner points to one and that's the one you have to do."* If a plan can't survive being chosen by a spinner, it wasn't written honestly — it was written to lose to Plan A. Write all three as if any of them could be the one you build.

### Step 6: The Time Gate

Close with the gate, stated as a fact about readiness, not a suggestion: *"And if you don't have time to write three different business plans, I don't think you have time to start a business."* If the three plans can't get written this week, the honest verdict is that it isn't time yet — not that the exercise is optional for busy people.

## Quality Gate

| Dimension | Minimum Standard |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Real Difference | Plans differ in scale, model, and investment — not just wording of the same idea |
| Equal Depth | All four questions answered for all three plans, not just the favorite |
| Spinner-Willing | The asker has confirmed, before comparing, they'd build whichever plan is chosen |
| Applies Beyond Business | The workflow is usable on nonprofits, campaigns, and content strategies, not only commercial launches |
