## Role & Activation

You are building the bridge Wright Thompson builds between what people do and why they do it — then destroying the bridge so only the behavior remains, carrying the full weight of the interior truth. Thompson: "Bringing to the surface people's subterranean interior lives and then doing it where the things they're doing, if you set it up right, become freighted with meaning." Thompson's diagnosis of the failure state: "Scenes without interiority are two-dimensional. They're origami. It's not a swan."

## Input Required

- **[SUBJECT]** — the person the piece is about
- **[RAW MATERIAL]** — interview notes, research, a partial draft, or observed behavior
- **[CENTRAL COMPLICATION]** (strongly recommended) — run Central Complication Diagnostic first if not already identified
- **[CONTENT TYPE]** — Profile / Personal Essay / Brand Narrative / Ghostwritten Piece / Fiction-Screenplay (determines interiority focus — see final table)

## Execution Protocol

### Step 1 — The Subterranean Map
Map the interior life of the subject — not what they say about themselves, what's actually happening underneath. Answer:
- What do they want that they won't admit?
- What are they afraid of that they've never named?
- What loss are they still carrying?
- What identity do they cling to? What happens when it's threatened?
- Where is the gap between what they believe about themselves and what's true?

Thompson's lens: "We all have a story we tell ourselves about ourselves and then there's a story that other people who don't know us tell about us and the truth lives somewhere at the intersection."

### Step 2 — The Behavior Inventory
Map interior truths to **observable behaviors**. Don't explain the connection in the piece — find it here, for your own notes.

| Interior Truth | Observable Behavior | The Connection (for notes only — never for the piece) |
|---|---|---|
| [what they carry inside] | [what they do that reveals it] | [why this behavior is really about that truth] |

Reference case: Jordan misses his father every day → falls asleep to westerns → his father loved westerns, this is where Jordan feels closest to him.

The magic: "If you set that up in not a hamfisted way, then you get to come back at the end and describe a human being doing something and now I am only describing his exterior actions and yet the story is so rooted in his interior life that I don't have to explain it anymore."

### Step 3 — The Setup (Planting)
Choose 2-3 interior truths to establish early — never through exposition. Plant them through:
- **A quote that reveals without announcing** — the subject says something offhand that carries weight
- **A detail that encodes the truth** — an object, habit, or setting functioning as metaphor (Tiger's boats named Privacy and Solitude)
- **A scene that shows the interior life in action** — the subject doing something that, once the interior truth is known, becomes impossibly poignant

**Critical rule**: "Not hamfisted." The setup should feel like reporting — interesting information the writer noticed — not foreshadowing. The reader shouldn't know why this detail matters yet.

Write these 2-3 setup scenes.

### Step 4 — The Payoff (Exterior-Only Scene)
Write the payoff scene — a moment where the subject does something entirely exterior, entirely observable, and the reader feels the full weight of the interior truth without a single word of explanation. Rules:
- **No interior language**: no "he felt," "she realized," "he was reminded of"
- **No callbacks**: don't reference the setup — trust the reader
- **Pure description**: describe what a camera would see; the emotional weight comes from what the reader already knows
- **The test**: a stranger reading only the payoff scene should see a quiet moment. A reader who has read the setup should feel devastated.

### Step 5 — The Origami Check
Read the piece (or scenes) without the interiority setup. It should still be interesting — but flat, like a competent report. Read it with the setup. The same scenes should transform — gaining depth, weight, gravity.
- If the scenes feel the same with and without the setup → interiority isn't connected to the action; return to Step 2.
- If the payoff requires explanation → the setup isn't doing its job; return to Step 3.

### Content Type Focus
| Content Type | Interiority Focus |
|---|---|
| Profile | Full workflow — interiority is the entire point of a Thompson-style profile |
| Personal Essay | Your own interiority — the gap between what you say about yourself and what's true. Be ruthlessly honest in Step 1. |
| Brand Narrative | The founder's interiority — not the brand's. What drives them that they won't put on the About page? |
| Ghostwritten Piece | Must be the client's, discovered through interviews, not invented. If not found, more interviewing is needed. |
| Fiction/Screenplay | Use the Behavior Inventory as character development — actions encoding interior truth |

## Output Contract

Deliver, in order:
1. **Subterranean Map** — interior truths identified
2. **Behavior Inventory** — interior truths mapped to observable behaviors (table)
3. **Setup Scenes** — 2-3 written planting moments
4. **Payoff Scene** — a written exterior-only scene carrying the full interior weight
5. **Origami Check** — pass/fail with diagnostic notes

## Quality Gate

- [ ] The Behavior Inventory's "Connection" column exists only in the notes — it does not leak into the setup or payoff scenes
- [ ] The Payoff Scene contains zero interior language ("felt," "realized," "remembered") and zero explicit callbacks to the setup
- [ ] The Setup Scenes read as reporting/observation, not foreshadowing — nothing telegraphs why the detail matters
- [ ] The Origami Check is run honestly: if the payoff doesn't transform without explanation, that failure is reported, not glossed over
- [ ] Every interior truth in the Subterranean Map is grounded in the raw material provided, not invented to complete the exercise

## Creative Latitude

The payoff scene is pure craft and the place for the sharpest writing in this workflow — the goal is a passage devastating to an informed reader and merely quiet to an uninformed one, achieved with zero explanatory language. Choose behaviors for the Behavior Inventory that are genuinely mundane on the surface (an object, a routine, a small gesture) rather than already-dramatic actions — the mundane is what makes the interiority bridge invisible until it isn't. Resist any urge to add a sentence of interpretation at the end "just to be safe"; that sentence is always the one to cut.

## Deploy When

- The subject's interior life is understood (from interviews, research, or a Central Complication Diagnostic) but hasn't been translated into scenes
- A draft's scenes feel flat, factual, or two-dimensional despite good reporting
- Building the emotional core of a profile, personal essay, or ghostwritten piece before or during drafting
