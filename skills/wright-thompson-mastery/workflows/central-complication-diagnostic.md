# Central Complication Diagnostic

Find the one thing. Gary Smith's formulation, adopted by Thompson as core method: "What is the central complication of someone's life and how on a daily basis do they go about solving it?" This workflow diagnoses that complication for any subject — a person, a brand, a company, a place.

## Execution

### Requirements

The user provides **information about a subject** — a person to profile, a brand to write about, research notes, interview transcripts, public information, or a brief description. The more raw material, the better the diagnosis.

### Step 1: The Ten Versions

Thompson: "10 different people who know [someone] from 10 different places in their life will have 10 different versions of them."

List as many "versions" of the subject as you can identify — the way different audiences, contexts, or time periods see them:

- How does the public see them?
- How does their family see them?
- How do their competitors see them?
- How do they see themselves?
- How did they see themselves 10 years ago?
- How will they see themselves in 10 years?

The central complication usually lives in the **gap** between two or more of these versions.

### Step 2: The Mask Test

Apply the Updike test Thompson uses: "The mask eats the face."

- What mask has the subject built (or had built for them)?
- Is the mask protecting them or consuming them?
- What's the distance between the mask and the person underneath?
- Is the subject aware of the distance? (Awareness vs. blindness is itself a complication.)

Tiger Woods: an introvert wearing an extrovert's marketing plan. Michael Jordan: a competitor whose competitive identity may be devouring his post-career self. Dale Murphy: the rare example of someone who killed the avatar on purpose.

### Step 3: The Complication Statement

Write the central complication as a single sentence following this structure:

**[Subject] is [specific tension] — the gap between [Version A] and [Version B] — and every day they [how they cope with or solve it].**

Test it:
- Is it **interior**, not exterior? (The complication should be about what's happening inside, even if it manifests externally.)
- Is it **irreducible** — can you compress it further? If so, compress.
- Is it **universal** — does it connect to something every human deals with, even in a less extreme form?
- Does it generate **sympathy**? Thompson: "These stories are a prayer for empathy."

### Step 4: The Daily Basis Test

The second half of Gary Smith's formula: "How on a daily basis do they go about solving it?"

Map the complication to observable behavior:
- What does the subject **do** every day that is really an attempt to manage this tension?
- What rituals, habits, or choices reflect the complication?
- What details in their environment carry the weight of the complication? (Tiger's boats named Privacy and Solitude. Jordan's security code name Yahweh.)

These observable behaviors become the scenes of the piece. If you can't map the complication to daily behavior, the complication may be too abstract — go deeper.

### Step 5: The Story Test

Does this complication generate a story? Check:

- [ ] Does it create a **driving question** the reader wants answered?
- [ ] Does it connect to a **moment in time** where the tension is heightened?
- [ ] Does it suggest an **ending** — either a resolution or an elliptical non-resolution that feels true?
- [ ] Is it something **only this subject** experiences at this intensity, while being something **everyone** recognizes?

If any box is unchecked, the complication needs refinement. Return to Step 1.

## Output Requirements

**Deliver**:
1. **Ten Versions Map** — the multiple ways the subject is seen
2. **Mask Analysis** — the distance between public identity and private reality
3. **Complication Statement** — one sentence, irreducible
4. **Daily Behavior Map** — observable actions that reflect the interior tension
5. **Story Test** — pass/fail with notes on any gaps
6. **Recommended Next Workflow** — which Thompson workflow to deploy next (typically Hammer-First Outliner or Interiority Bridge)

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content Type | Complication Focus |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Profile** | Full workflow — the complication IS the story |
| **Brand Story** | The complication is the founder's tension, not the company's mission statement |
| **Personal Essay** | The complication is your own — be honest about which version of yourself is in conflict with which |
| **Case Study** | The complication is the client's pre-transformation state — what they were wrestling with |
| **Ghostwriting** | The complication must emerge from the subject's life, not projected onto them |
