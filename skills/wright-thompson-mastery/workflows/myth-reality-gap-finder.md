# Myth/Reality Gap Finder

The most compelling subjects live in the tension between their public mythology and their private reality. Thompson (via Updike): "The mask eats the face." This workflow excavates the gap between who the world thinks someone is and who they actually are — then maps how that gap drives the story.

## Execution

### Requirements

The user provides **a subject** — a person (public figure, client, founder, character) with enough publicly available or researched information to identify a mythology. This workflow produces a gap map and story strategy.

### Step 1: Name the Mythology

What does the world believe about this person? The mythology is the public consensus — the story told by the brand, the media, the fanbase, the Wikipedia entry, the LinkedIn profile.

For each subject, map:

- **The Headline Version**: What would a stranger say about this person in one sentence?
- **The Brand Narrative**: What story has the subject (or their team) deliberately constructed?
- **The Frozen Image**: At what moment in their life is the subject permanently captured in public memory?

Thompson examples:
- Tiger Woods: Marketing plan built for an extrovert forced onto an introvert
- Michael Jordan: Code name Yahweh — "And it's not a compliment"
- Pat Riley: The perfectly composed exterior hiding "the world that built him"

**Output**: The mythology in 3-5 sentences. It should read like the version you'd tell someone at a dinner party.

### Step 2: Excavate the Reality

Now go beneath. The reality is the version hidden from the public — not necessarily dark, but private, complicated, contradictory.

**Excavation Questions** (answer all that apply):
- What does this person do when no one is watching?
- What is the tension between their stated values and their actual behavior?
- What fundamental question about themselves have they stopped asking (or never started)?
- What system, relationship, or structure designed to protect them is actually eroding them?
- What does the mythology cost them to maintain?

Thompson's key move: "10 different people who know [someone] from 10 different places will have 10 different versions of them. I don't think my job is to sort those out. I think my job is to present a fractal image of a complicated human being."

**Output**: The reality in 3-5 sentences — the version only an intimate friend would know.

### Step 3: Map the Gap

Now lay the mythology and reality side by side.

For each dimension of the gap, answer:

- **What does the world see?** → **What is actually happening?**
- **Where does the mask fit perfectly?** (These moments are boring — skip them.)
- **Where does the mask crack?** (These moments are your story.)
- **Where has the mask eaten the face?** (Where can the subject no longer tell the difference between the performance and the self?)

**Output**: A two-column gap map with at least 3 dimension rows. The widest gap is almost always the central complication (Pattern 2).

### Step 4: Find the Fractal Angle

Thompson doesn't sort out which version is "true." The writer's job is to present a fractal image.

For each version of the subject that emerges from the gap:
- **Name the version** (e.g., "Tiger the Introvert," "Tiger the Global Brand," "Tiger the Son")
- **Identify who sees this version** (family? Teammates? The public? The subject themselves?)
- **Mark where versions contradict each other**

The story lives at the intersection of these contradictions — not in resolving them.

### Step 5: The Story Strategy

Based on the gap map, determine:

1. **Entry Point**: What observable exterior moment reveals the gap without explaining it? (This is your lead — see Pattern 4, Interiority Bridge.)
2. **The Mask Moment**: What is the single most revealing instance where the mythology and reality collide? (This might be your hammer.)
3. **The Universal Question**: What does this person's specific gap illuminate about the human condition? (Tiger's gap → what happens when the infrastructure built to protect you is the thing destroying you? Jordan's gap → what do you do when the world worships a version of you that doesn't exist?)

## Output Requirements

**Deliver**:
1. **The Mythology** — 3-5 sentences, the public version
2. **The Reality** — 3-5 sentences, the private version
3. **Gap Map** — two-column table with at least 3 rows
4. **Fractal Image** — named versions with their audiences and contradictions
5. **Story Strategy** — entry point, mask moment, universal question
6. **Gap Width Score** — 1-10. Below 5 = the subject may not have enough tension for a compelling profile. Redirect to a different Thompson workflow.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content Type | Gap Adaptation |
|-------------|----------------|
| **Magazine Profile** | Full workflow applies. The gap IS the profile. |
| **Brand Audit** | Mythology = brand promise. Reality = customer experience. Gap = strategic vulnerability. |
| **Personal Branding** | Mythology = your curated online presence. Reality = your 3am truth. Gap = authenticity opportunity. |
| **Founder Story** | Mythology = the pitch deck narrative. Reality = the funding-is-running-out truth. Gap = the compelling founder arc. |
| **Ghostwriting** | Use the gap to find the client's real voice — not the voice they perform, but the one that leaks through when they stop performing. |
| **Case Study** | Before-state = mythology ("everything was fine"). After-state = reality acknowledged. Gap = the intervention story. |
