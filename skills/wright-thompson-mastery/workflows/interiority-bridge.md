# Interiority Bridge

Build the bridge between what people do and why they do it — then destroy the bridge so only the behavior remains, carrying the full weight of the interior truth. Thompson: "Bringing to the surface people's subterranean interior lives and then doing it where the things they're doing, if you set it up right, become freighted with meaning."

## Execution

### Requirements

The user provides **a subject and raw material** — interview notes, research, a partial draft, or a central complication already diagnosed. This workflow maps the interior life and builds scenes where exterior action carries interior weight.

### Step 1: The Subterranean Map

Map the interior life of the subject. Not what they say about themselves — what's actually happening underneath.

Answer these questions:
- **What do they want that they won't admit?**
- **What are they afraid of that they've never named?**
- **What loss are they still carrying?**
- **What identity do they cling to? What happens when it's threatened?**
- **Where is the gap between what they believe about themselves and what's true?**

Thompson's lens: "We all have a story we tell ourselves about ourselves and then there's a story that other people who don't know us tell about us and the truth lives somewhere at the intersection."

### Step 2: The Behavior Inventory

Now map interior truths to **observable behaviors**. Don't explain the connection — find it.

| Interior Truth | Observable Behavior | The Connection (for your notes — NOT for the piece) |
|---------------|---------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| [What they carry inside] | [What they do that reveals it] | [Why this behavior is really about that truth] |

Thompson's example:
| **Jordan misses his father every day** | **Falls asleep to westerns** | **His father loved westerns — this is where Jordan feels closest to him** |

The magic: "If you set that up in not a hamfisted way, then you get to come back at the end and describe a human being doing something and now I am only describing his exterior actions and yet the story is so rooted in his interior life that I don't have to explain it anymore."

### Step 3: The Setup (Planting)

Choose 2-3 interior truths to establish **early in the piece** — but not with exposition. Plant them through:

- **A quote that reveals without announcing** — the subject says something offhand that carries weight
- **A detail that encodes the truth** — an object, a habit, a setting that functions as metaphor (Tiger's boats named Privacy and Solitude)
- **A scene that shows the interior life in action** — the subject doing something that, once you know the interior truth, becomes impossibly poignant

**Critical Rule**: "Not hamfisted." The setup should feel like reporting — interesting information the writer noticed — not like foreshadowing. The reader shouldn't know why this detail matters yet.

### Step 4: The Payoff (Exterior-Only Scene)

Now write the payoff scene — a moment where the subject does something entirely exterior, entirely observable, and the reader feels the full weight of the interior truth **without a single word of explanation**.

Rules for the payoff:
- **No interior language**: No "he felt," "she realized," "he was reminded of"
- **No callbacks**: Don't reference the setup. Trust the reader.
- **Pure description**: Describe what a camera would see. The emotional weight comes from what the reader already knows.
- **The test**: A stranger reading only the payoff scene should see a quiet moment. A reader who has read the setup should feel devastated.

### Step 5: The Origami Check

Thompson: "Scenes without interiority are two-dimensional. They're origami. It's not a swan."

Read the piece (or scenes) without the interiority setup. Is it still interesting? It should be — but it should feel flat, like a competent report. Now read it with the setup. The same scenes should transform — gaining depth, weight, emotional gravity.

If the scenes feel the same with and without the setup → your interiority isn't connected to the action. Return to Step 2.
If the payoff requires explanation → your setup isn't doing its job. Return to Step 3.

## Output Requirements

**Deliver**:
1. **Subterranean Map** — interior truths identified
2. **Behavior Inventory** — interior truths mapped to observable behaviors (table format)
3. **Setup Scenes** — 2-3 planting moments, written
4. **Payoff Scene** — exterior-only scene carrying full interior weight
5. **Origami Check** — pass/fail with diagnostic notes

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content Type | Interiority Focus |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Profile** | Full workflow — interiority is the entire point of a Thompson-style profile |
| **Personal Essay** | Your own interiority — the gap between what you say about yourself and what's true. Be ruthlessly honest in Step 1. |
| **Brand Narrative** | The founder's interiority — not the brand's. What drives them that they won't put on the About page? |
| **Ghostwritten Piece** | The interiority must be the client's, discovered through interviews, not invented. If you haven't found it, you haven't interviewed enough. |
| **Fiction/Screenplay** | Use the Behavior Inventory as character development — actions that encode interior truth. |
