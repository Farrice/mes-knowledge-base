# Detail as Metaphor Engine

A great detail will do the work of 50 shitty sentences. Thompson doesn't choose details because they're interesting — he chooses them because they carry the weight of the theme. The boats named Privacy and Solitude. Michael Jordan's code name Yahweh. A widow dying alone. This workflow teaches you to see details as load-bearing structures, not decoration.

## Execution

### Requirements

The user provides **a piece in progress or raw material with details** — reporting notes with observed details, a draft with decorative description, or a subject they need to observe more thoughtfully. This workflow produces a detail hierarchy — what to keep, what to cut, what's missing.

### Step 1: Establish the Thematic Compass

Before evaluating any detail, you must know what the piece is about — not the subject, but the theme. The theme is the gravitational field (Pattern 9).

Answer:
- **What is the central complication of this piece?** (Pattern 2 — if unknown, run Central Complication Diagnostic first.)
- **What is the theme that should vibrate beneath the surface?** (State it in one sentence — but remember, this sentence will never appear in the piece.)
- **What is the emotional ending (the hammer)?** (Pattern 1 — if unknown, the detail hierarchy will be unreliable.)

### Step 2: Inventory All Details

List every concrete, specific detail in the raw material — physical objects, names, numbers, gestures, settings, dialogue, sensory observations. Don't filter. List them all.

For each detail, note:
- **The detail itself** (concrete description)
- **Where it appears** (or where it could appear)
- **What it literally describes** (surface level)
- **What it might carry** (thematic possibility — don't force this; if nothing comes, mark it "unknown")

### Step 3: The Weight Test

Run every detail through Thompson's three-part test:

**Test 1 — Thematic Resonance**: Does this detail vibrate with the theme? Not illustrate it. Not explain it. *Vibrate*.
- Tiger Woods's boats named Privacy and Solitude → resonates with the theme of forced extroversion on an introvert without ever stating it.
- A detail that resonates carries meaning the reader receives without being told.

**Test 2 — Central Complication Service**: Does this detail illuminate the central complication?
- The code name Yahweh → illuminates the gap between the person and the mythology others build around them.
- A detail that serves the complication makes the reader understand something about the subject they didn't know.

**Test 3 — Concrete Specificity**: Is this detail specific enough to photograph?
- "He was lonely" → fails (abstraction)
- "His widow died in the 80s and no one came to her funeral" → passes (you can see it)

**Scoring**:
- Passes all 3 → **Load-bearing detail**. Keep. This does the work of 50 sentences.
- Passes 2 of 3 → **Potential detail**. Keep if it can be sharpened on the failing test.
- Passes 1 or 0 → **Decorative detail**. Cut unless it serves a structural purpose (pacing, transition).

### Step 4: Find the Missing Details

The most important details are often the ones you don't have yet. This is where Pattern 3 (Reporting > Writing) intervenes.

For each load-bearing theme or complication moment WITHOUT a strong detail:
- **What would the perfect detail look like?** (Describe what you're looking for without inventing it.)
- **Where would you find it?** (Who would know? What archive? What physical space?)
- **What reporting question would uncover it?**

Thompson: "Every time I'm stuck, it's just that I haven't reported enough." Missing details are reporting prompts.

### Step 5: Detail Placement Architecture

Now place the surviving details in the piece:

- **The Opening Detail**: What single concrete detail drops the reader into the world? (This detail must set the thematic compass without the reader knowing it.)
- **The Recurring Detail**: Is there one detail that can return transformed? (Jordan falling asleep to westerns — first as behavior, then as grief.)
- **The Hammer Detail**: What detail carries the emotional payload of the ending?
- **The Invisible Chain**: When the reader finishes the piece, which details will they suddenly see were connected? (This is the "vibration" — it happens retrospectively.)

## Output Requirements

**Deliver**:
1. **Thematic Compass** — central complication, theme, hammer (one sentence each)
2. **Detail Inventory** — all details with literal description and thematic possibility
3. **Weight Test Results** — each detail scored with pass/fail on all 3 tests
4. **Detail Hierarchy** — load-bearing, potential, decorative categories
5. **Missing Detail Map** — gaps in detail coverage with reporting prompts
6. **Placement Architecture** — opening detail, recurring detail, hammer detail, invisible chain
7. **Detail Density Score** — 1-10 on the ratio of load-bearing details to decorative ones. Below 6 = more reporting needed.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content Type | Detail Adaptation |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Magazine Profile** | Full workflow applies. Details are the currency of profiles. A great profile is 80% detail selection. |
| **Personal Essay** | The details come from your own observation and memory. Step 4 (Missing Details) becomes "what do I need to go remember or re-observe?" |
| **Newsletter** | One load-bearing detail maximum. It must carry the entire piece's thematic weight. Rapid version — Steps 1, 3, 5 only. |
| **Brand Copy** | The detail is the proof point. One concrete detail (a stat, a customer moment, a product fact) that carries the whole argument. |
| **Ghostwriting** | The client's details — their specific language, their specific moments — are the only currency. Generic details = voice failure. |
| **Case Study** | The detail is the data point that makes the transformation real. "Revenue increased 40%" is a stat. "She cancelled her alarm for Monday because she no longer dreaded the morning" is a detail. |
