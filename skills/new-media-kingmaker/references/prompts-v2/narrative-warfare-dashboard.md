## Role & Activation

You are running **OODA-Powered Narrative Warfare** — the a16z OODA Loop Media Warfare pattern cross-pollinated with Luke Iha's Proof Ladder and the Flood-the-Zone crisis doctrine. The operating premise: a brand that always reacts to competitors has already lost. The goal of this deliverable is permanent narrative dominance — competitors respond to you; you never respond to them. This is the monthly maintenance layer of the Kingmaker methodology, run standalone once initial positioning (from the Kingmaker Sprint) is already in place.

## Input Required

```
[BRAND] — your brand/founder/organization
[COMPETITORS] — 2-5 key competitors with their public channels listed
[CURRENT_OODA_SPEED] — your current decision-to-publish cycle in hours, or "unknown"
[ACTIVE_NARRATIVE_BATTLES] — any live narrative conflicts right now, or "none"
[CHANNELS_AND_CAPACITY] — available publishing channels and team capacity
```

## Execution Protocol

### Step 1: Intelligence Gathering

**1a — Competitor OODA Assessment.** For each competitor, estimate: publish frequency (as a proxy for loop speed), response time in hours from an industry event to their post, which platforms they dominate (tag oral/written culture mode per platform), their crisis-response pattern (fast flood / slow defense / silent), and whether they speak through a brand account or the founder personally. Populate the table:

| Competitor | Est. OODA Speed | Dominant Platforms | Culture Modes | Crisis Pattern | Founder Go-Direct? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**1b — Narrative Map.** Identify the 5 dominant narratives active in the space right now. For each: who owns it (first-mover advantage)? Is it contested (multiple voices competing) or unowned (white space, no one has claimed it)? Unowned narratives are the priority targets for Step 4.

### Step 2: Speed Architecture

Design your own OODA loop for minimum cycle time at every stage:

- **Observe** (target: real-time) — define the monitoring setup: competitor content feeds, industry news, audience signals. The governing question: "How quickly do I know when the landscape shifts?"
- **Orient** (target: <1 hour) — build pre-set orientation templates for the 10 recurring scenario categories: competitor product launch, industry trend shift, regulation/policy change, viral moment in the space, direct competitor attack, customer win/loss, technology breakthrough, hiring/talent market shift, media/press coverage, cultural moment with an authentic tie-in. Each template follows: "This is happening → This is what it means for us → This is our position."
- **Decide** (target: <30 minutes) — name the decision authority (ideally the founder alone), and the decision filter ("does this affect our narrative?"). If yes, which template, which platform, which culture mode?
- **Act** (target: <2 hours from observation) — publishing protocol is draft → one review → publish. No multi-layer approval — that's old media. Written-culture responses (complex positions) go to Substack/essay; oral-culture responses (simple reactions) go to a tweet thread or short video.

Calculate the **Speed Dominance Ratio** per competitor (your loop ÷ their loop). Target: minimum 2:1 — you cycle at least twice as fast as your nearest competitor.

| Competitor | Their Loop | Your Loop | Ratio | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Status is one of: Dominant / Contested / Behind.

### Step 3: Proof-Armed Positions

For each of the brand's top 5 narrative positions, build:
- **Claim** — the position, stated clearly and without hedge.
- **Proof Stack** — 3+ proof types: personal proof (own experience/results), social proof (customer results, testimonials), third-party proof (industry data, research).
- **Platform Deployment Plan** — which culture mode gets which proof type: oral platforms get demonstration proof, before/after, social-proof clips; written platforms get statistical analysis, case studies, research citations.
- **Vicious Hooks** — 5 hooks per position using the Luke Iha methodology.

This builds a library of pre-armed positions deployable instantly when the OODA loop triggers — this is the arsenal, not a one-time list.

### Step 4: Offensive Disruption Calendar

Design a 4-week PROACTIVE disruption plan — the brand initiates, it does not wait to be provoked:

| Week | Disruption Play | Platform | Culture Mode | Proof Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contrarian take on industry consensus | Long-form first | Written | Research + personal |
| 2 | Exclusive data/insight reveal | All platforms, simultaneous | Cross-mode | Statistical |
| 3 | Founder vulnerability piece (why we almost failed) | LinkedIn + Substack | Hybrid/Written | Experiential |
| 4 | Industry prediction with evidence | X thread → YouTube deep dive | Oral → Written | Third-party + analysis |

Adapt the specific plays to the brand's actual narrative map from Step 1b, but preserve the shape: each week forces competitors into responding to this brand's narrative, never the reverse.

### Step 5: Defensive Flood Protocol

Pre-build the crisis arsenal:
- **Content Bank** — 10 "in the can" pieces ready for emergency publication: 3 data-driven insight pieces (evergreen-relevant), 3 founder story/vulnerability pieces, 2 customer success showcases, 2 industry analysis/prediction pieces.
- **One-Touch Templates** — pre-drafted contextual addresses for the top 5 attack vectors specific to this brand.
- **Ally Activation List** — 5-10 allies with a pre-coordinated rapid-response agreement.
- **Cascade Protocol** — a minute-by-minute flood deployment sequence.

### Step 6: Monthly Warfare Review Protocol

Define the recurring check-in: recalculate competitor OODA speeds (have they compressed?), update the narrative map (any newly unowned narratives?), refresh proof assets (new case studies, data, testimonials?), replenish the content bank (anything deployed last month needs a replacement), and score whether competitors are responding to this brand or the reverse.

## Output Contract

- Single dashboard document covering all six steps, each rendered with its specified table or list structure populated with real entries (not empty placeholder rows).
- The Pre-Armed Position Library (Step 3) must cover all 5 top narrative positions, each with a full proof stack and 5 hooks — partial coverage (e.g., 2 positions fully built, 3 stubbed) fails the contract.
- The Crisis Arsenal (Step 5) must specify all 10 content-bank pieces by concrete topic/angle, not generic category labels.
- Speed Dominance Ratios in Step 2 must be calculated against every competitor listed in Input, using the estimates gathered in Step 1a.
- Close with the Monthly Warfare Review Protocol as a standing recurring checklist, not a one-time note.

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Are Speed Dominance Ratios calculated for every competitor listed in Input, not a subset?
- [ ] Does the Pre-Armed Position Library cover all 5 positions fully (claim + proof stack + deployment plan + 5 hooks each)?
- [ ] Is every content-bank piece a specific topic/angle rather than a generic category placeholder?
- [ ] Is the Offensive Disruption Calendar proactive (brand-initiated) rather than reactive to a specific competitor move?
- [ ] Does every proof type/culture-mode pairing follow the oral-vs-written proof rules stated in Step 2/Step 3 (no statistical proof pushed to an oral-culture platform)?

## Creative Latitude

The Narrative Map (Step 1b) is where taste matters most — naming which of the 5 dominant narratives is genuinely unowned requires real judgment about the competitive landscape, not a mechanical scan. Push toward specificity: "no one is talking about X from an operator's POV" beats "there's white space in thought leadership." The disruption plays in Step 4 should be adapted to what's actually true and interesting about this brand's narrative map — the reference calendar is a shape, not a script to copy verbatim. Vicious hooks throughout should chase genuine cultural nerve per platform and per position; a hook interchangeable across two positions has failed to be genuinely proof-armed.

## Deploy When

- Competitive narrative battles are active or anticipated.
- Monthly maintenance check-in after an initial Kingmaker Sprint has established positioning.
- A brand needs to move from reactive (always responding to competitor moves) to proactive narrative control.
- Building a standing crisis-readiness arsenal before an attack happens, not during one.
