> **Produces**: Weekly/monthly content production sprint plan with district coverage balancing  
> **Used When**: Need to systematize ongoing content production, ensure balanced trust-stage coverage, or prevent "all attention, no trust" imbalances

## Step 1: Capacity Assessment

Before planning content, audit real capacity — the #1 reason content sprints fail is overcommitment.

**Prompt the user**:
> "How many hours PER WEEK can you ACTUALLY spend on content? Not aspirational. Not 'if I had a team.' Right now, this week, how many real hours?"

| Capacity Level | Hours/Week | Sprint Pace | Content Volume |
|---------------|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Minimal** | 3-5 hrs | Slow sprint — 2-3 pieces/week | 1 anchor + 1-2 derivatives |
| **Standard** | 6-10 hrs | Medium sprint — 4-6 pieces/week | 1 anchor + 2-3 derivatives + 1 experimental |
| **Full** | 11-20 hrs | Fast sprint — 7-12 pieces/week | 2 anchors + 3-5 derivatives + 1-2 experimental |
| **Team-supported** | 20+ hrs | Full media — 12+ pieces/week | 3+ anchors + daily derivatives + ongoing experimental |

**Quality gate**: Sprint pace must match REAL capacity. Over-planning kills consistency, and consistency is half the growth formula.

**Balance targets** (suggested):
- Attention: 25-30% of content volume (high frequency, low production cost)
- Discoverability: 15-20%
- Connection: 20-25%
- Trust: 20-25% (lower frequency, higher production investment)
- Conversion: 5-10% (only when trust threshold is met)

**Critical anti-pattern**: Most creators produce 60-80% Attention content and wonder why nobody buys. Rebalance toward Connection and Trust districts.

## Step 3: Sprint Calendar Design

Design the sprint calendar using Grace's Consistency × Experimentation formula:

### The 70/30 Split
- **70% Consistent** — formats your audience expects, trust-building staples
- **30% Experimental** — new formats, risky ideas, format swaps, collaborations

|------|-------|-------------|

| **Week 4** | Conversion Sprint | Revenue-connected content — launches, CTAs, proof stacking |

## Step 4: Content Batch Protocol

Design batching sessions for maximum efficiency — don't create one piece at a time:

### Batch Session Structure
1. **Ideation batch** (30 min): Generate 7-10 content ideas tagged by district + consistent/experimental
2. **Research batch** (30-60 min): Pull data, quotes, stats, examples for all pieces
3. **Production batch** (2-4 hrs): Create all pieces in one focused session
4. **Hook batch** (30 min): Write all hooks/headlines/subject lines in one pass (context-switching kills hook quality)
5. **Distribution batch** (30 min): Schedule, cross-post, write platform-native adaptations

### Batching Rules
- ✅ Same-format pieces batched together (all LinkedIn posts in one session)
- ✅ All hooks written in a single "hook sprint" session
- ✅ Research done once, used across multiple pieces
- ❌ NOT switching between formats mid-batch (YouTube editing → LinkedIn writing → email → kills focus)

### Sprint Health Metrics
| Metric | Target | Actual | Status |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Completion rate** | >80% of planned pieces published | [%] | ✅ / ❌ |
| **District balance** | No district with 0 pieces | [Report] | ✅ / ❌ |
| **70/30 split maintained** | 60-80% consistent, 20-40% experimental | [Ratio] | ✅ / ❌ |
| **Memorable minutes ratio** | >50% of production investment on memorable content | [%] | ✅ / ❌ |
| **Business outcome connection** | >80% of pieces traceable to a business outcome | [%] | ✅ / ❌ |

### Sprint Adjustment
Based on the review, adjust NEXT sprint:
- If completion rate <60%: Reduce volume, increase quality focus
- If one district is starving: Steal one slot from the most over-served district
- If experimental pieces outperform consistent ones: Consider promoting the format to consistent
- If nothing converts: Check trust pathway bridges (Workflow 02), not content volume
