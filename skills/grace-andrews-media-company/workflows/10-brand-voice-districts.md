# Brand Voice Districts

> **Produces**: Brand voice map with distinct registers for each trust stage, tone calibration per platform  
> **Used When**: Voice feels inconsistent across platforms, or content is one-note across all trust stages  
> **Time**: 60-90 minutes  
> **Genius Patterns**: Niche Precision Despite Breadth, Trust Pathway Sequencing, Emotional Residue Engineering

## Step 1: Voice Archaeology

Before designing voice districts, excavate the brand's NATURAL voice — the way they speak when they're not "performing content."

**Prompt the user**:
> "Send me 3 examples of your content where you felt most like YOURSELF — not your most viral, not your most polished. The pieces where you'd say 'that really sounded like me.' Also send me a voice memo, DM thread, or email where you're just talking normally."

**Quality gate**: If the Content Pattern is radically different from the Natural Pattern, the brand has a voice problem — they're performing rather than speaking. Flag this.

## Step 2: Trust-Stage Voice Map

Different trust stages require different voice registers — just like you speak differently to a stranger vs. a close friend:

### Attention District Voice (Talking to strangers)
| Element | Register | Rationale |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| **Energy** | High — confident, energetic, punchy | You have 3 seconds to prove you're worth attention |
| **Proof density** | Maximum — lead with credibility, results, social proof | Strangers don't trust you yet — show receipts |
| **Vulnerability** | Minimal — this is not the place for soft moments | Too much vulnerability from a stranger feels needy |
| **CTA style** | Soft — "follow for more" not "buy my thing" | The ask matches the relationship (none yet) |
| **Sentence rhythm** | Short, punchy, scannable | Attention is scarce — don't waste their time on long-form |

### Connection District Voice (Talking to acquaintances)
| Element | Register | Rationale |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| **Energy** | Moderate — still confident but warmer, more personal | They know you exist — now they want to know WHO you are |
| **Proof density** | Medium — stories > stats | They've seen the receipts — now show the person behind them |
| **Vulnerability** | Increasing — share struggles, failures, behind-the-scenes | Vulnerability at this stage builds genuine connection |
| **CTA style** | Relationship-building — "subscribe," "join the newsletter," "reply with your take" | Deepen the connection before monetizing it |
| **Sentence rhythm** | Mixed — longer when telling stories, short for insights | They'll give you more time now — earn it with depth |

### Trust District Voice (Talking to trusted associates)
| Element | Register | Rationale |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| **Energy** | Calm authority — teach, don't perform | They trust you — you don't need to prove yourself constantly |
| **Proof density** | Low-medium — reference track record casually, not desperately | Proof is assumed at this stage — too much looks insecure |
| **Vulnerability** | High — this is where real, specific vulnerability lands | They're invested enough for you to be genuinely honest |
| **CTA style** | Confident — "this is designed for you, here's how to join" | The relationship supports a direct ask |
| **Sentence rhythm** | Longer, more nuanced — teach in depth | They'll read/watch the whole thing |

### Conversion District Voice (Talking to community members)
| Element | Register | Rationale |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| **Energy** | Intimate — like a trusted advisor in a private room | These people are IN — speak with them, not at them |
| **Proof density** | Social proof from THEM — testimonials, results, community wins | The best proof is their own peers succeeding |
| **Vulnerability** | Selective — share specific operational details, admit what's hard | They can handle the real version — give it to them |
| **CTA style** | Exclusive — "this is for the inner circle," "as a member, you get..." | Reward their investment with exclusivity and access |
| **Sentence rhythm** | Natural conversation — match the medium (chat, DM, email) | Formality feels weird at this stage |

## Step 3: Platform Voice Adaptation

The same trust stage sounds different on different platforms. Map platform-native adaptations:

| Platform | Voice Adaptation | Example |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| **YouTube** | More conversational, uses pauses and emphasis. Can be longer. Visual cues (face, body language) carry tone. | "Look — I'm going to tell you something that most people in this space won't admit..." |
| **LinkedIn** | More professional, structured. Uses line breaks as rhythm. Authority-weighted. | "I've worked with 40+ brands on their content strategy. Here's the pattern I keep seeing:" |
| **Twitter/X** | Compressed. Sharp. Declarative. Contrarian works well. | "Your content strategy isn't broken. You just don't have one." |
| **Newsletter** | Most intimate of all written formats. First-person singular. Can be longer and more vulnerable. | "I've been thinking about something all week that I can't stop turning over..." |
| **Podcast** | Natural speech patterns. Thinking out loud. More tangents allowed. | "Actually — wait, let me back up. The real thing is..." |
| **Short-form** | Front-loaded hook. Visual-first. Energy dense. No warm-up. | "Stop. [pause] Everything you've been told about content strategy is designed to keep you posting, not earning." |

**Common voice mismatches**:
- Using Attention voice in Trust content (over-performing for people who already trust you)
- Using Trust voice in Attention content (being thoughtful/nuanced for people who haven't earned that yet — they scroll past)
- Same voice everywhere (the "one-note creator" trap — feels robotic)
