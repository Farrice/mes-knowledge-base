## Narrative-dosage decision

| Decision | Use when | Minimum evidence | Prohibited move |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FULL STORY` | Transformation, identity, connection, persuasion, or memory is central, and the supplied material contains real narrative movement | A specific want, a meaningful obstacle, an observable change, and enough facts to locate the turn | Inventing a scene, motive, dialogue, chronology, metric, result, or emotional state |
| `STORY FRAGMENT` | Evidence or direct explanation must remain primary, but one real moment, labeled analogy, frame, or micro-example can improve comprehension or recall | A sourced moment or a clearly labeled constructed analogy with an explicit mapping | Letting anecdote replace evidence or imply stronger causality |
| `NO STORY` | The main job is a decision, procedure, specification, calculation, incident update, risk statement, status, or direct instruction; or the facts cannot support a story | The direct communication spine and the facts needed for the actual decision or action | Adding a protagonist arc, dramatic scene, false stakes, or emotionalized risk |

## Truth-risk classes

| Class | Typical work | Story allowance | Required gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creative or personal | Clearly fictional work, supplied personal experience, internal ideation | Full story when the facts or fictional status are explicit | Label fiction, composites, and constructed examples |
| Ordinary real-world | Brand, founder, customer, product, content, sales, internal communication | Full story only from supplied or verified facts | Fact trace and uncertainty preservation |
| Evidence-sensitive | Health, finance, law, safety, research, policy, high-consequence operations | Evidence spine first; usually fragment or no story | Domain evidence and factual review; narrative cannot strengthen the claim |

## Story qualification

After `FULL STORY` is selected, Story Compass tests whether the candidate material actually contains:

1. a want;
2. tension or an obstacle;
3. change.

A failure means gather source material or downgrade the dosage. It never authorizes invention.

## Shared factual-integrity invariant

**SYSTEM GOVERNANCE:** Preserve supplied facts and uncertainty. Do not invent dialogue, chronology, metrics, outcomes, motives, emotions, or sensory detail as real. Label hypothetical examples, composites, and analogies. Use `[NEEDS SOURCE]` when a story beat requires missing evidence. In evidence-sensitive work, story may support comprehension or recall but cannot replace proof or strengthen causality.
