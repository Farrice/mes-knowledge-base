## The 22 judgment patterns

### Group A — Direction as a discipline (the spine)

**1. Prompting is a tactic; direction is a discipline.**
> *"The eye is already there. Prompting is a tactic. Direction is a discipline."* — S1, 2026-05-26

The order of those three sentences is the argument. Taste is assumed present, not taught. The gap
is never the eye — it is that a tactic is being used where a discipline is required. A tactic is
per-attempt and disposable; a discipline is a standing practice that governs every attempt.

**2. AI slop is a direction problem.**
> *"Grace Liu believes most 'AI slop' is fundamentally a direction problem."* — S1, 2026-05-26 (Forbes attribution, not direct quote)

The single highest-leverage claim in the skill, because of what it forecloses: if slop is a
direction problem, model-switching cannot fix it, reroll volume cannot fix it, and prompt-tuning
fixes only the sliver that was actually a translation failure.

**3. The three layers.**
> *"Her framework separates AI production into three layers: the Human Layer, where intention and
> creative vision originate; the Translation Layer, where intent becomes machine-readable; and the
> Machine Layer, where systems execute production."* — S1, 2026-05-26

Note precisely what each verb is doing: intention **originates**, intent **becomes machine-readable**,
systems **execute**. Nothing originates in the machine.

**4. The hierarchy never shifts.**
> *"The hierarchy never shifts. The human is always the director."* — S1, 2026-05-26

Stated as invariant, not aspiration. It is why she calls the framework one *"designed to preserve
human authorship inside AI workflows"* (S1) — the hierarchy is a thing that must be actively held,
because the tool's gravity runs the other way.

**5. Invisible decisions during planning are what make work authored.**
> *"Luxury creative has always been defined by invisible decisions during planning. The ones that
> make an image feel authored rather than mass-produced."* — S1, 2026-05-26

Three loads in one sentence: the decisions are **invisible** (the viewer never sees them), they
happen **during planning** (not during execution, not during selection), and the axis they produce
is **authored vs mass-produced** — which is not the same axis as good vs bad, or expensive vs cheap.
Note "has always been": she is claiming continuity, not an AI-era novelty.

**6. Creatives show; AI requires them to articulate.**
> *"As creatives, we show. But AI requires us to articulate — with the precision of a director
> leading a crew."* — S5, accessed 2026-08-02

The named precision bar is a **director briefing a crew** — not a person describing a mood, not a
moodboard handed over in silence. This is the clearest statement of what the Translation Layer
demands.

**7. The output should reflect your vision, not the model's defaults.**
> *"…so the output consistently reflects your vision, not the model's defaults."* — S5, accessed 2026-08-02

Names the actual adversary. Not bad models — **defaults**. Every element you did not decide was
decided for you by a system optimizing for the average of its training distribution, which is a
serviceable definition of slop. `[house implication]` Therefore any unspecified element is a
silent delegation, and slop is measurable as the count of silent delegations.

**8. Human direction first, tools follow.**
> *"The human direction comes first. Tools follow."* — S5, accessed 2026-08-02

The sequencing claim, stated flat. This is the same rule the house's craft-map reached independently
("no generation fires with a freehand prompt") — independent arrival is corroboration.

### Group B — Why the discipline is newly load-bearing

**9. AI collapses the distance between vision and execution — and exposes the gaps.**
> *"AI collapses the distance between vision and execution. And in doing so, it exposes any
> existing gaps in taste, medium fluency, or thinking."* — S2, 2026-04-15

Note the three named gaps and their order: **taste, medium fluency, thinking.** They map cleanly to
her tier ladder (pattern 14).

**10. AI removed the insulation layer.**
> *"AI removes the insulation layer that once separated taste from output. Meaning: you can no
> longer hide behind vendors, crews, budgets, or production value."* — S2, 2026-04-15

The most useful metaphor in her corpus. Production value historically **laundered** weak direction —
a competent crew and a real budget make almost any brief look intentional. Remove the crew and the
budget and the brief stands unclothed.

**11. The output becomes a direct reflection of your mind.**
> *"The output becomes a direct reflection of your mind."* — S2, 2026-04-15

The consequence of 10, and the harshest line she writes. It converts every output into evidence
about its author, which is why she treats direction as an identity-level practice rather than a
skill.

**12. AI is a multiplier — of expertise or of ignorance.**
> *"The risk is that because AI is so accessible, many people create without true medium knowledge
> (or knowledge of the tool either). AI is a multiplier. It can multiply expertise, or it can
> multiply ignorance."* — S2, 2026-04-15

Symmetrical and non-moralizing. Volume is not progress; volume is amplification of whatever
position you already held.

**13. AI is simultaneously a production tool and a medium — which requires dual fluency.**
> *"AI is simultaneously a production tool and a medium."* — S1, 2026-05-26
> *"To use it well, you need fluency in two distinct systems: the medium you want to create in;
> the production tool (aka intelligence model) translating your direction into form. This is no
> different from understanding painting and knowing how to use a brush, or understanding
> photography and knowing how to operate a camera. The difference with AI is scale."* — S2, 2026-04-15

The brush analogy is doing precise work: nobody confuses brush skill with painting, but AI's
interface invites exactly that confusion. She grants one genuine novelty — **scale** (one medium
spanning photography, film, 3D, graphic design, sound, sculpture, robotics-assisted physical
artmaking) — and grants nothing else: *"it still requires what every serious medium has always
required: taste, design principles, and deep visual literacy."*

### Group C — The tier ladder

**14. Three tiers of creatives using AI.**
> *"Tier 1 — Prompt Operators (Tool Knowledge): Capable of generating practical assets like product
> images with precision. Useful, but increasingly replaceable as tool literacy becomes standard."*
> *"Tier 2 — Aesthetic Stylists (Medium Taste + Tool Knowledge): Possess enough taste and technical
> control to make work feel elevated. Beautiful outputs, but often trend-sensitive and
> technique-driven."*
> *"Tier 3 — World Architects (Worldbuilders, on top of Medium Taste + Tool Knowledge): These
> creatives build coherent symbolic universes rooted in classical medium principles, yet expressed
> through AI."* — S2, 2026-04-15

Tier 3 is governed by three named things: *"Consistent visual language / Cultural and emotional
intelligence / Rich narrative and symbolic layers."* Her sharpest tier-2 diagnostic is
**"trend-sensitive and technique-driven"** — beautiful work with a short half-life, which is most
of what circulates as good AI creative.

**15. Creative mastery is tool-independent and era-independent.**
> *"What's fascinating is that this is the exact same path as becoming a strong artist, filmmaker,
> or auteur. Creative mastery remains creative mastery, regardless of tool or era"* — S2, 2026-04-15

The claim that makes the whole corpus durable rather than era-bound, and the reason this extraction
is a rubric other skills consume rather than a tool guide that expires.

**16. Execution is table stakes; worldbuilding is the differentiator.**
> *"In an AI-saturated world, anyone can generate a passable luxury-looking image. Execution
> becomes table stakes. The differentiator is worldbuilding — a coherent visual universe, anchored
> in cultural context, that sustains meaning across time. Tier 3 becomes the world brands want to
> enter."* — S2, 2026-04-15

"Sustains meaning across time" is the specific test that separates a world from a style: a style
is legible now, a world keeps paying out.

**17. Universe-by-universe, not campaign-by-campaign.**
> *"The strongest Creative Directors won't just work for brands. They will build parallel universes
> of their own — independent visual mythologies, recurring symbolic systems, original world IP…
> Brands won't simply hire them to 'execute a campaign.' They're hiring them for the emotional and
> cultural energy of their worlds."* — S2, 2026-04-15
> *"Less campaign-by-campaign. More universe-by-universe."* — S2, 2026-04-15

Her stated future CD is a hybrid of *"Artist / Film director / Cultural theorist / Systems
designer."* Named living examples: **Sybille de Saint Louvent, Benjamin Benichou.**

**18. AI is a proving ground before it is a deliverable.**
> *"AI also functions as a creative proving ground. Prototype your strongest ideas here,
> pressure-test them culturally, then translate into other mediums once resonance is proven. A short
> AI film becomes a live-action treatment. A generated image series becomes a gallery show. AI is
> the sketchbook, the pitch deck, and sometimes the first audience test."* — S2, 2026-04-15

The most immediately actionable pattern she has. It reframes generation as **cheap cultural
testing** rather than as production, and names the exit path in both directions.

### Group D — What makes work travel

**19. The tension between the references is the idea.**
> *"Primavera worked because it fused things that don't naturally belong together… None of those
> elements alone would have stopped the scroll. All of these unlikely elements combined is what did
> it… This is the thing about unlikely combinations: the tension between the references is the idea.
> You're not just making a pretty reinterpretation of a classic. You have to generate one that
> creates friction."* — S3, 2026-03-12

The strongest single line for grading concepts. If every element of a piece agrees with every other
element, there is no idea present — only a style. `[house implication]` This converges exactly with
the house's St. Pierre critique check ("what is in tension"), reached from a different direction.

**20. Meeting a cultural conversation feels inevitable; chasing a trend feels unoriginal.**
> *"There's a difference between work that chases a social trend, and work that meets a cultural
> conversation. The former can feel unoriginal, while the latter feels inevitable."* — S3, 2026-03-12

The felt-quality words are the test: **inevitable vs reactive.** Both can be timely; only one reads
as authored.

**21. The human crack in the marble.**
> *"Elevated fashion creative typically stays cold. It aspires, but doesn't invite. What made
> Primavera travel beyond its obvious audience was one small detail: Mercury's distraction in the
> storyline. His grin. The dropped fruit. It introduced warmth and humor without breaking the visual
> language — an emotional puncture that converted viewers into sharers. The most circulated work
> usually has one of these. A place where the marble cracks and something universally human shows
> through."* — S3, 2026-03-12

Two operative constraints, both easy to miss: the puncture must be **small and physical** (a grin, a
dropped fruit — not a concept), and it must **not break the visual language**. Aspiration without
invitation gets admired; invitation gets shared.

**22. Layer meanings to widen reach — never simplify.**
> *"Creatives caught the art history subversion… General fashion fans just saw a relatable comedy
> beat in a Gucci concept video. Both were rewarded. That's why it moved across different taste
> networks simultaneously. The work didn't simplify itself to reach a wider audience. It layered
> meanings so that different readers could find their own entry point."* — S3, 2026-03-12

An explicit rejection of the standard reach heuristic. Note "**Both were rewarded**" — layering
fails if the surface read is merely a consolation prize.

**Her own summary of D:**
> *"Your strongest creative work isn't a technical flex or even a consistent creative signature —
> it's a layered yet accessible cultural reinterpretation. It takes something that already lives in
> people's minds and repositioning it somewhere they didn't expect. The resonance lives in that
> repositioning. The medium used, AI or otherwise, is almost besides the point. The question is
> always: what are you reframing, and for whom?"* — S3, 2026-03-12

Worth flagging that she demotes **consistent creative signature** here — the thing most creatives
optimize for — below cultural reinterpretation. And that "already lives in people's minds" is a
hard constraint on the source material: you reposition the familiar, not the obscure.

## 7 signature moves (observed in her one documented piece + stated practice)

Only one piece is documented end-to-end: the Gucci #GucciPrimavera spec video (S3, 2026-03-12) —
9 seconds, 100K views, high share and save ratios. These are **observed moves**, not a taught method.

1. **Work from the designer's stated intent, not the images.** *"after watching Demna's polarizing
   first show for Gucci and reading his inspiration statement, I asked: Why not place the
   collection's bold looks and quirky characters within a 2026 reinterpretation of Botticelli's
   Renaissance classic, Primavera?"* — she reads the statement, then finds the analogue.
2. **Reach for the historical analogue, not the contemporary reference.** Botticelli (c. 1477–82)
   against a Fall 2026 collection. Her stated Tier 3 criterion — *"connect what is historically
   resonant with what feels urgent now"* — executed literally.
3. **Anchor on a word doing double duty.** *"Primavera as a word also carries seasonal weight — it
   felt current without being reactive."* The title carried the painting, the season, and Milan
   Fashion Week timing at once.
4. **Restage the archetype as a scene.** *"This is essentially a story old as time — intellect
   surrendering to beauty and desire — but restaged as a god getting distracted by women at the
   club."* Archetype supplies the resonance; the restaging supplies the friction.
5. **Build the emotional puncture as a small physical beat.** A grin, a dropped orange. Warmth
   inserted without breaking the visual register.
6. **Judge by audience breadth, not view count.** *"it was the type and breadth of audience engaging
   that made me stop in my tracks — notable fashion editors, underground cultural figures, fine
   artists and general fashion lovers alike from different corners of the globe."* Cross-network
   travel is the signal; volume is not.
7. **Let concept strength be the only variable.** *"It removed the traditional constraints between
   vision and output. The only thing that mattered was whether the concept was strong enough."*
   She treats collapsed production cost as a way to isolate the concept as the sole test variable.

## Her quality rubric — how she separates good from bad

Assembled from her own axes. Each is binary and checkable.

| Axis | Passing | Failing |
|---|---|---|
| **Authorship** | Authored — invisible planning decisions are nameable | Mass-produced |
| **Agency** | Reflects your vision | Reflects the model's defaults |
| **Circulation** | Travels across taste networks | Sits |
| **Tension** | Friction between references | Everything agrees; style without idea |
| **Timing** | Feels inevitable | Feels reactive |
| **Temperature** | Aspires *and* invites — the marble cracks | Cold; admired, not shared |
| **Depth** | Layered; multiple entry points, all rewarded | Simplified to widen reach |
| **Tier** | Tier 3 — consistent visual language, cultural/emotional intelligence, symbolic layers | Tier 1 competence asked to do Tier 3 work |
| **Durability** | Sustains meaning across time | Trend-sensitive, technique-driven |
| **Amplification** | Multiplies expertise | Multiplies ignorance |
| **Exposure** | Survives with the insulation stripped | Depends on production value |

Her explicit non-criteria — things she names and rejects as markers of quality: **technical flex**,
**consistent creative signature**, **view count**, **a polished portfolio of brand work**, and
**passable luxury-looking execution** (all S2 2026-04-15 and S3 2026-03-12).

## Voice profile

- **Declarative triplets with a demotion inside them.** *"The eye is already there. Prompting is a
  tactic. Direction is a discipline."* — she establishes what is not the problem, then ranks two
  things you thought were the same.
- **Names the axis, then names both poles.** authored/mass-produced · inevitable/reactive ·
  expertise/ignorance · sits/travels · aspires/invites. Almost every judgment in her corpus is
  delivered as a named pair.
- **Physical metaphors for abstract failures.** "the insulation layer," "the crack in the marble,"
  "an emotional puncture." Never abstract-on-abstract.
- **Historically continuous, not AI-triumphalist.** *"has always been defined by,"* *"no different
  from understanding painting,"* *"regardless of tool or era."* She consistently frames AI as
  removing a concealment rather than creating a discipline.
- **Concedes precisely, then holds the line.** She grants scale as genuinely unprecedented, then
  immediately: *"But it still requires what every serious medium has always required."*
- **Ends on a question that is a tool, not a rhetorical flourish.** *"What are you reframing, and
  for whom?"*
- **Anti-hype register.** No breathless futurism, no doom. Where the Forbes panel around her reaches
  for coinages, her contribution is the plainest language on the page.

## Anti-patterns — how to fail while sounding like her

- Reciting "prompting is a tactic, direction is a discipline" **and then giving a prompting fix.**
  The line is a routing instruction, not a slogan.
- Presenting the three layers as a **pipeline** to run through. She calls it a hierarchy that never
  shifts. Layers are locations for diagnosis, not stages of a process.
- Grading a **person** with the tier ladder. It grades the work; the same practitioner produces at
  different tiers on different days.
- Attributing **any** Machine Layer content to her — models, parameters, prompt syntax, lighting
  language, post workflow. Her public corpus contains none of it.
- Reconstructing "the Human Directed™ method." She published a doctrine. The method is taught live
  and privately, and is not public. See `source-notes.md`.
