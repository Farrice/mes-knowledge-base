## Core Genius

Dom's operating edge is portfolio-level execution without founder over-presence. The repeatable method is:

1. Reduce the year to a few strategic growth blocks.
2. Review financial truth weekly.
3. Gate new products through differentiation, margin, and channel fit.
4. Let strong executives argue and operate without founder suppression.
5. Treat retail, trade spend, logistics, and buyer requests as operating systems, not isolated events.

## Anti-Patterns (Sourced)

Every item below is drawn from `_active/harness/codex-harvest-2026-06-11/extractions/dom-iacovone/multi-company-operator/failure-modes.md`, cross-referenced against the matching chapter timestamp in `timestamped-insight-ledger.md`, video `TUdTU1pwoZ4` (published 2026-05-26). Recovery moves are the extraction's own recovery column, not invented.

- **Founder stays in every meeting** — team becomes less opinionated and defaults to yes (22:17 Delegate/Elevate chapter; `failure-modes.md` row 1). Recovery: remove the founder from routine calls, define escalation triggers.
- **Annual plan carries too many priorities** — no weekly truth surface can show what matters (04:47 SGM chapter; `failure-modes.md` row 2). Recovery: collapse to four initiative blocks, assign owners and metrics.
- **Product launches without margin or differentiation** — novelty substitutes for product reason (13:21 Stage-gate chapter; `failure-modes.md` row 3). Recovery: run the stage-gate — sense, innovation, margin, channel fit.
- **Buyer request overrides brand truth** — retail access makes the team reactive (64:40 Walmart-buyer chapter; `failure-modes.md` row 4). Recovery: treat buyer feedback as input, preserve product/customer logic.
- **Campaign narrative beats finance** — team keeps explaining while CM1 misses plan (17:13 finance-led course-correction chapter; `failure-modes.md` row 5). Recovery: put finance first, decide from variance and cause hypotheses.
- **Retail timing is misread** — current numbers miss already-loaded future distribution (32:20 retail/product-lag chapter; `failure-modes.md` row 6). Recovery: build a retail lag map before judging momentum or exit timing.
- **Trade spend treated as a small expense** — deductions destroy exit value through multiples (74:10 trade-spend-multiple chapter; `failure-modes.md` row 7). Recovery: convert leakage into enterprise-value impact.
- **Direct retail mistaken for sell-through** — shelf availability lacks store-level execution incentives (86:17 DSD-incentives chapter; `failure-modes.md` row 8). Recovery: compare direct retail, DSD, distributor, and Amazon paths.
- **AI strategy ignores relationships** — tools become commodity while access remains scarce (49:55 AI-and-relationships chapter; `failure-modes.md` row 9). Recovery: map offline relationship moats beside automation gains.
- **Slow brand is killed too early** — a delayed retail inflection is mistaken for failure (128:15 Revive/slow-asset chapter, per `timestamped-insight-ledger.md`; `failure-modes.md` row 10). Recovery: separate weak core economics from slow channel timing.
