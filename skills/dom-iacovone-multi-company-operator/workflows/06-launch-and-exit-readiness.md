# Workflow 06: Launch And Exit Readiness

Use this when a business is preparing a major launch, retail push, strategic partnership, or exit path.

## Inputs

- Business stage.
- Launch or exit objective.
- Current financial cleanup state.
- Partner/buyer/channel commitments.
- Forecast and lag assumptions.
- Risks and professional-review needs.

## Steps

1. Separate launch readiness from exit readiness.
2. Audit financial cleanup: audited financials, quality of earnings, CM1, gross-to-net, and owner accountability.
3. Map loaded future distribution and lag timing.
4. Identify strategic buyer or partner upside.
5. Decide whether the move is default-safe, evidence-supported exception, or premature.
6. Name professional review boundaries.

## Output Schema

- Launch readiness score: scored independently of exit readiness, with the evidence behind the score.
- Exit readiness score: scored independently of launch readiness, covering audited financials, quality of earnings, and owner accountability.
- Lag map: loaded future distribution kept separate from current revenue, with expected timing.
- Exception/risk verdict: exactly one of default-safe, evidence-supported exception, or premature, with the specific evidence for that category.
- Next operating move: the one action to take this week.
- Professional-review boundary: named whenever medical, legal, tax, investment, or valuation content appears.

## Quality Gate

- Are launch readiness and exit readiness scored separately, never merged into one composite number?
- Does the exception/risk verdict name specific evidence for its category (default-safe / evidence-supported exception / premature), not a generic gut call?
- Is loaded future distribution kept visibly separate from current revenue in the lag map, so momentum and plateau risk cannot be conflated?
- Is a professional-review boundary stated whenever medical, regulated-health, legal, tax, investment, or valuation content appears — is its absence on relevant content treated as a failed output?
- Does the strategic buyer/partner upside note preserve optionality language rather than asserting a guaranteed outcome?
