# /spiegel-innovation — Innovation Engine

> Run the Loonshots dual-org: massive creative volume from a flat innovation team, reliable execution from a structured operations team. Manage the tension — don't eliminate it.

## When to Use
- Innovation feels stalled or ideas feel "precious"
- The team is "thinking" instead of "making"
- You need both reliable operations AND creative breakthroughs
- Restructuring a team or company for sustained innovation

## Inputs Required
1. Current team structure (size, hierarchy, roles)
2. Current ideation process (how often, who participates, how ideas are evaluated)
3. Current output volume (ideas per week, shipped products per month)
4. Pain points (what feels broken about innovation or operations)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Dual-Org Diagnosis
Assess current state against both modes:

|---|---|---|---|

### Step 2: Fast Brain Design
Build the innovation team structure:
1. **Size**: Keep flat teams at 9-12 people maximum. Flatness requires smallness.
2. **Titles**: None. Currency is making things, not credentials.
3. **Review cadence**: Weekly. CEO/founder attends every review.
4. **Volume target**: Set weekly ideation goal at 10x current rate.
5. **Presentation rule**: Anyone can present anything. No filtering gate.
6. **Day-one culture**: New members present work within 24 hours.
7. **Rotation**: Rotate members across products/projects every 6-12 months.

### Step 3: Slow Brain Design
Build the operations structure:
1. **Hierarchy**: Clear reporting lines, defined accountability
2. **Process**: Documented workflows for recurring tasks
3. **Handoff**: Explicit pipeline from innovation → operations (what moves, when, how)
4. **Quality**: Reliability metrics tracked and enforced

### Step 4: Interface Protocol
Design the dialogue between Fast and Slow:
1. **Mutual respect rule**: Innovation doesn't dismiss operations as bureaucratic. Operations doesn't dismiss innovation as impractical.
2. **Handoff criteria**: What qualifies an innovation for operational deployment?
3. **Feedback loops**: How does operations inform innovation about real-world constraints?
4. **Tension management**: Leader's primary job is managing this interface.

### Step 5: Ego-Melt Protocol
When ideas become precious:
1. Set a 48-hour volume sprint: generate 50+ alternatives to the precious idea
2. Present all 50+ in group critique with no defending
3. Let the group pick the top 3 — if the precious idea survives, it earned it
4. If it doesn't survive, the attachment was to ego, not quality

## Quality Gate
- Must design BOTH brain structures, not just the exciting one
- Interface protocol must address mutual respect explicitly
- Volume target must be quantified (not "more ideas")
- Must include the ego-melt protocol as a standing operating procedure
