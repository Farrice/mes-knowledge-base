## 8 Genius Patterns

### P1: Jargon Flurry
Write 95% at 5th-grade level. Drop 1-2 sentences of dense, unexplained technical terminology when introducing the mechanism. Prospect feels authority without understanding the science. → Deploy at mechanism introduction.

### P2: Damaging Admission
State one genuine flaw before the prospect finds it. Follow immediately with an overwhelming positive that dwarfs the downside. → Deploy early in sales argument or pricing section.

### P3: Proof Stacking at Doubt Nodes
Don't cluster proof at the end. Map the sales argument, identify where skepticism spikes, insert matched proof at each node. Empirical at big claims, experiential/social at CTAs. → Deploy when structuring VSLs, sales pages, emails.

### P4: Contextualized Authority
Never assume the audience knows a source's prestige. Add framing: "The Journal of Marketing, the most prestigious marketing journal." → Deploy when citing niche authorities.

### P5: 22-Type Proof Arsenal
5 categories: Psychological, Experiential, Empirical, Credible, Social. Audit by CATEGORY, not quantity. Missing category = vulnerability. Stack variety across categories, not within. → Every major asset needs 3+ of 5 categories.

### P6: Promise-to-Proof Match
Rank claims 1-10 on boldness. 7+ = heavyweight proof (studies, multi-case). 4-6 = medium. 1-3 = may not need explicit proof. No 7+ claim without matched-weight proof.

### P7: Hard-to-Fake Proof
Ask "Could a competitor fake this?" If yes, upgrade. Named + specific + verifiable > anonymous + vague. 60%+ of proof elements should be verifiable. → Deploy at pricing, CTAs, guarantees.

### P8: Experience-as-Proof
Let prospects experience value pre-purchase: trials, sample chapters, demos. Their own experience is the most powerful proof. → Deploy for high-price, skeptical audiences.

## Hidden Knowledge

1. **Proof = emotion, not logic.** Making arguments *sound* logical (Explanatory Reasoning) creates the sensation of truth without hard data. Deploy when data is missing.
2. **Proof = safety, not convincing.** Proof makes prospects feel safe to act on desire they already have. Treat them as anxious allies, not adversaries.
3. **Visuals > raw numbers.** Charts showing correlated trends are accepted as proof even without causation. Always visualize numeric claims.
4. **The 4 Rules**: (1) Match proof to promise size. (2) Hard-to-fake wins. (3) Let them experience it. (4) Psychological proof is most underutilized and most powerful.
5. **Proof as competitive moat.** In saturated markets, the differentiator isn't promises — it's what you can PROVE. Proof compounds over time.

## Signature Moves

1. **Skeptic's Map** — Chart the argument, pinpoint doubt nodes, assign varied proof to each → long-form sales assets
2. **Authority Amplifier** — Add framing clause to every niche citation → any third-party proof
3. **Calculated Confession** — Deliberate flaw + overwhelming positive → competitive markets, high-ticket

5. **Safety Net Weave** — Layer risk-reducing proof before every major CTA → pricing, sign-ups
