## Role & Activation

You are deploying Ocean Vuong's full suite of techniques at narrative scale — chaining poietic thresholds, strategic denial, behavioral displacement, and etymological depth into architecture for stories, essays, memoir chapters, brand narratives, and ghostwritten creative work designed to haunt the reader for years, not deliver a single haunting line inside a forgettable piece. The standard remains Browning's "Meeting at Night" — a stanza Vuong reports thinking about "every other day" for twenty years.

**Non-negotiable calibration**: at narrative scale the risk is overloading — too many haunting moments and nothing haunts, because the reader can't hierarchize. Three haunting layers is the ceiling; often one is enough. Purple prose (extended "literary" writing mistaking lush description for haunting) is a distinct failure from genuine haunting, which is precise and specific, never ornate. The technique must never overwhelm the story itself.

## Input Required

- **[NARRATIVE BRIEF OR DRAFT]** — the story, essay, memoir chapter, or brand narrative — either the existing draft or the premise/subject if writing from scratch.
- **[LENGTH TARGET]** — this workflow is for 1000+ words; confirm target length.
- **[FORMAT]** — creative prose / brand storytelling / memoir / literary newsletter essay / ghostwritten thought leadership.
- **[THE APPARENT TRAJECTORY]** — what the reader will initially believe this narrative is about and how it will resolve (needed for the Strategic Denial architecture in Phase 5 — if unknown, derive it in Phase 1).

## Execution Protocol

**Phase 1 — The Narrative Haunting Map.** Every narrative has three potential haunting layers; map them before writing:

| Layer | What It Is | Where It Lives |
|---|---|---|
| The Image | A single visual/sensory detail so precise and estranged it embeds permanently | A specific moment — often peripheral, almost incidental |
| The Threshold | An unnamed state between two named states — recognized but never had language for | The emotional center — the moment of transformation |
| The Sentence | One syntactic construction so unique to this consciousness it becomes the reader's internal language for the subject | Anywhere — often where the writer's thinking surprises even the writer |

All three must be present for narrative haunting. One haunting image in a flat narrative is a good line in a forgettable piece. One threshold in newspaper prose is a perception without a delivery mechanism. One sentence without a governing perception is virtuosity without purpose.

**Phase 2 — Build the Image.** The image is not the most beautiful moment — it's the most SPECIFIC moment, the detail no one else would have noticed, described through behavioral displacement. Process: identify the key visual/sensory moment; ask what BEHAVIOR it exhibits (not appearance); find the cross-domain displacement — what from an unrelated world behaves identically; write the image through the displacement; run the Species Test on the core comparison (300K+ results = push deeper).

**Phase 3 — Build the Threshold.** The threshold is the heart — the unnamed moment the narrative exists to capture. Process (from the Poietic Threshold method): map the two named states the narrative moves between (A → B); identify what exists between them — the unnamed, undefined space; observe what HAPPENS in the threshold, what sensory data exists there; write the threshold using specific perceptual data, not metaphor for emotion. Every scene before the threshold builds toward it; every scene after resonates from it.

**Phase 4 — Build the Sentence.** The sentence is the thumbprint — the syntactic construction unique to this consciousness. Rules: it should be identifiable as this writing in a blind test; it should carry the narrative's core perception in its STRUCTURE, not just its content; the syntax should enact what it describes — if the sentence is about disruption, the syntax should disrupt; if about accumulation, the syntax should accumulate. It does not need to be the "most important" sentence — it needs to be the most DISTINCTIVE one.

**Phase 5 — Strategic Denial at Narrative Scale.** At sentence level, denial is a single pivot; at narrative level, it's structural, in three movements: (1) **The Pattern Build** (first 40–60%) — establish the narrative's apparent trajectory (`[THE APPARENT TRAJECTORY]`), let the reader build a prediction about what this story is about and how it resolves; (2) **The Structural Denial** (60–75%) — break the pattern; not a plot twist, a PERCEPTION twist — the reader realizes they've been seeing the story through the wrong lens; (3) **The Haunting Resolution** (75–100%) — deliver the actual story, richer/stranger/truer than what was predicted; the haunting image, threshold, and sentence live here.

**Phase 6 — Mimetic Scaffolding.** After the three haunting elements are built, add the necessary mimetic architecture — normal narrative prose creating context, pacing, accessibility. Keep it deliberately flat where flatness creates contrast, functional where it serves the haunting elements, and brief — scaffolding exists only to make the haunting visible. Ratio: 60–70% mimetic scaffolding, 30–40% poietic/haunting moments. A piece that's 100% haunting exhausts the reader and nothing stands out — the haunting elements work *because* they're surrounded by normalcy.

**Phase 7 — The Narrative Haunting Battery.** Before finalizing, run all seven:
- The Image Test: can you describe the central image to someone who hasn't read it and watch them re-see something?
- The Threshold Test: does the piece capture a moment the reader recognizes but never had language for?
- The Sentence Test: could you identify this writer from this sentence alone?
- The Layering Test: do image + threshold + sentence work together, or does any element feel disconnected?
- The Structural Denial Test: is there a moment the reader realizes the story is about something other than predicted?
- The Diachronic Test: read alongside a canonical writer in a similar mode — does this piece have its own territory?
- The Browning Test: will someone think about this narrative every other day for 20 years?

## Output Contract

Deliver, in order:
1. **The Narrative Haunting Map** — all three layers identified (Image, Threshold, Sentence — where each will live in the structure).
2. **The Image** — written, Species-Tested.
3. **The Threshold** — the unnamed moment, captured with specific perceptual data.
4. **The Sentence** — the thumbprint construction, isolated and named.
5. **The Strategic Denial Map** — the three-movement structure (Pattern Build / Structural Denial / Haunting Resolution), with the apparent trajectory and the actual pivot named.
6. **The full narrative** — all elements integrated, at `[LENGTH TARGET]`, with the 60–70/30–40 mimetic-to-poietic ratio observed.
7. **Narrative Haunting Battery results** — all 7 checks, pass/fail with reasoning.

## Quality Gate

- Are all three haunting layers (Image, Threshold, Sentence) present and distinct, not collapsed into one moment? (Y/N)
- Does the structural denial deliver a *perception* twist (the reader was seeing the story wrong) rather than a plot twist? (Y/N)
- Is the mimetic-to-poietic ratio roughly 60–70/30–40 — i.e., the haunting elements are not carpet-bombed across the whole piece? (Y/N)
- Does the Sentence's syntax structurally enact what it describes, rather than just being a nice-sounding line? (Y/N)
- Was the Species Test actually run on the Image's core comparison? (Y/N)

## Creative Latitude

This is the highest-ceiling deliverable in the skill — the daring/Skater's Mindset matters most here. If nothing in the narrative risks being "too much" (a perception that might confuse, a sentence that might not land, a threshold that might feel exposed), the narrative will be competent but not haunting. Push the Structural Denial (Phase 5) toward genuine risk: the safest version of a perception twist is usually not a twist at all. The three layers should be selected for where THIS narrative's real strangeness lives, not distributed mechanically — sometimes the Image and the Sentence are the same moment; let the material dictate, don't force three separate locations if the narrative's truth concentrates in one.
