# Haunted Copy Engine

Deploy Vuong's haunting principle to commercial writing. Most copy is designed to capture attention and create urgency: buy NOW, limited time, don't miss out. Haunted copy creates a recursive thought loop — the prospect keeps returning to the idea, the image, the reframing. The conversion happens because the copy has taken up permanent residence in their mind.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: This workflow is for copy that has SPACE to haunt — sales pages, VSLs, email sequences, brand manifestos, long-form ads. If the format is a 2-line Meta ad, the Haunting Engine's social workflow is more appropriate.

## Execution

### Phase 1: Diagnose the Copy's Current Mode

Read the existing copy (or brief) and classify:

| Element | Urgency Copy (Standard) | Haunted Copy (Target) |
|---------|------------------------|----------------------|
| **Hook** | "Are you tired of...?" / "What if I told you...?" | A perception the prospect has never had about their own problem |
| **Problem** | Pain agitation — make it hurt more | Problem *estrangement* — make them see their problem as if for the first time |
| **Mechanism** | Logical explanation of how it works | Behavioral metaphor — the mechanism described through a displacement that makes it unforgettable |
| **Proof** | Testimonials, stats, case studies (standard) | The ONE proof image that embeds — the specific detail from a case study they'll think about at 2am |
| **CTA** | "Click here now" / "Don't miss out" | The sentence they carry that makes NOT buying feel like voluntary forgetting |

### Phase 2: The Problem Estrangement

The prospect's problem has been described a thousand times. They've read the pain agitation. They're numb to it. Your job: make them RE-SEE their own problem.

**Process**:
1. What is the standard way this problem is described? (List the clichéd framings)
2. What BEHAVIOR does this problem exhibit? Not its symptoms — its behavior. How does it move through someone's life? How does it grow, shrink, hide, re-emerge?
3. What from a radically different domain behaves the same way? (This is the displacement that creates haunting)

**Example**:
- Standard: "Struggling to grow your business? You're not alone."
- Haunted: "The business grew like something in a petri dish — visible only under magnification until it wasn't, and by then the medium was exhausted. You kept feeding it the same substance. It kept splitting the same way."

The haunted version makes the prospect re-see their own situation through a lens they've never held.

### Phase 3: The Mechanism Haunting

Your product/service has a mechanism — a way it works. Most copy explains this mechanistically. Haunted copy makes the mechanism *unforgettable* through behavioral metaphor:

1. **Identify the core mechanism behavior**: What does your solution actually DO? Not features — the behavioral change it creates.
2. **Find the cross-domain displacement**: What unrelated process exhibits the same behavior?
3. **Write the mechanism through the displacement**: The reader should understand how it works AND carry the image permanently.

### Phase 4: The Proof Residue

Of all the proof points (testimonials, case studies, data), which ONE has haunting potential?

**Selection criteria**:
- The specific sensory detail from a testimonial ("She called me at 6am crying" — not "She was very happy")
- The number that doesn't compute ("From $400 to $47,000 in 11 weeks" — the specificity creates haunting)
- The behavioral change ("He stopped checking his phone at dinner" — a single behavioral detail that implies everything)

**Amplify ONE proof point** rather than listing twelve. The haunted proof point does more work than a dozen standard testimonials because it EMBEDS — the reader thinks about it when evaluating their own situation.

### Phase 5: The Recursive CTA

Standard CTAs create urgency. Haunted CTAs create the feeling that NOT acting means voluntarily releasing a perception you've just acquired:

- Standard: "Click here before the price goes up"
- Haunted: "You've seen something in the last three minutes that you haven't seen before. You can close this tab. It won't close in your mind."

The haunted CTA doesn't pressure. It states what has already happened — the copy has taken up residence — and makes the prospect decide whether they want to keep the new perception or try to forget it.

### Phase 6: The Commercial Haunting Test

- [ ] **The 3AM Test**: Would the prospect wake up at 3am still thinking about ONE line from this copy?
- [ ] **The Water Cooler Test**: Would they describe this copy to a friend — not the offer, but the WAY it was written?
- [ ] **The Competitive Immunity Test**: After reading this, would competitor copy feel like background noise by comparison?
- [ ] **The Recall Test**: Three days later, without re-reading, could the prospect quote at least one phrase?
- [ ] **The Conversion Without Urgency Test**: Would this copy convert even without scarcity, countdown timers, or limited-time offers?

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: The biggest risk is **literary pretension in commercial context**. Haunted copy is NOT "poetic copy." It's copy that uses the TECHNIQUES of perceptual writing — behavioral displacement, sensory anchoring, strategic denial — in service of commercial goals. If the copy sounds like it's trying to win a literary award rather than sell, the balance is wrong. The haunting should feel natural, inevitable, almost invisible — the reader doesn't notice the technique, they just can't forget the copy.
