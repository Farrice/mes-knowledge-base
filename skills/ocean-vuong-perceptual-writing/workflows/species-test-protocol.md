# Species Test Protocol

The ultimate originality audit. Line-by-line examination of key images and metaphors against Vuong's "Has the species had this sentence yet?" bar.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: Before executing, confirm the piece warrants this level of scrutiny. Deploy on premium content — brand essays, literary newsletter, ghostwritten thought leadership, creative prose. Not for transactional copy.

## Skill Acquisition

Load the following in order:

1. **Genius Context**: `skills/ocean-vuong-perceptual-writing/genius.md` — quality rubric + anti-patterns

## Execution

### Phase 1: Extract Key Lines

From the draft, identify:
- All metaphors and similes
- All descriptive passages (3+ words of description)
- All "landing" lines — the sentences that carry the piece's emotional or intellectual weight
- The opening sentence and closing sentence
- Any line you're particularly proud of (pride often signals either genuine novelty or comfortable familiarity — this audit distinguishes them)

### Phase 2: The Google Test

For each extracted line:

| Line | Google Results | Verdict | Action |
|------|---------------|---------|--------|
| [Your line] | 0-99 results | **PASS — potentially new** | Verify it's genuine novelty, not just awkwardness |
| [Your line] | 100-10K results | **BORDERLINE** | Check if your specific arrangement adds something |
| [Your line] | 10K-300K results | **COMMON** | Needs displacement or estrangement |
| [Your line] | 300K+ results | **FAIL — species has it** | Rewrite required per Phase 3 |

**Important**: Don't Google the full sentence — test the core image or comparison. "The sunset bled across the sky" → test "sunset bled."

### Phase 3: Rewrite Protocol for Failed Lines

For each FAIL or COMMON line:

1. **Identify the behavior**: What behavior does the subject exhibit? (Movement, rate of change, quality of presence)
2. **Cross-domain displacement**: What domain *outside* the subject's world exhibits the same behavior?
3. **Write 3 variations**: Each from a different displacement domain
4. **Re-test**: Run the Google test on each variation
5. **Select**: Choose the variation that most deeply alters perception of the subject

### Phase 4: The Diachronic Survival Test

Read the piece alongside an excerpt from a canonical writer in a similar mode:
- For lyric/reflective: Baldwin (*Notes of a Native Son*), Dillard (*Pilgrim at Tinker Creek*)
- For narrative: Morrison (*Song of Solomon*), McCarthy (*Blood Meridian*)
- For essayistic: Sontag (*Against Interpretation*), Didion (*The White Album*)

**Ask**: Does my piece feel like it belongs in this company — not in quality necessarily, but in *distinctiveness*? Does it have its own territory, or does it feel like a weaker version of what the canonical writer already did?

### Phase 5: The Thumbprint Verification

Read the piece aloud. Can you identify:
- A syntactic pattern unique to this writer's consciousness?
- At least one sentence that could only have been written by this specific mind?
- A perception that none of the canonical comparisons offered?

If all three are present, the piece has a thumbprint. If not, identify which element is missing and address it.

## Output Requirements

**Deliver**:
1. The complete Google Test table (all key lines scored)
2. 3 variations for each FAIL line
3. Diachronic comparison notes (which canonical writer, what was learned)
4. Thumbprint assessment — present/absent, with specific evidence
5. Final verdict: PASS (ready for publication) / REWRITE (specific lines flagged) / RETHINK (fundamental perception issues)

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: The biggest risk in this workflow is mistaking *obscurity* for *novelty*. A line that Google can't find may simply be poorly constructed, not genuinely new. Verify that novel lines still communicate — estrangement is not incomprehensibility. The reader should see *more*, not less.
