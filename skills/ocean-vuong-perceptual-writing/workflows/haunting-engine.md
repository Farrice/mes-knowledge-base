# Haunting Engine

Deploy Vuong's Operating Principle #4: **Haunting > Capturing**. Every other content framework optimizes for capture — hooks, engagement, clicks, opens. This engine optimizes for the line that stays with someone when they close their laptop, the image they think about in the shower three days later, the sentence they remember at their grandmother's funeral five years from now.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: Before executing, run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md` § Decision Framework. This workflow is for content that needs to *outlive its publication date*. If the content is purely transactional (event announcement, shipping notification), the haunting engine isn't the right tool.

## Execution

### Phase 1: The Capture Diagnosis

Read the piece. Diagnose its current operating mode:

| Signal | Operating in Capture Mode | Operating in Haunting Mode |
|--------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Opening** | Hook — pattern interrupt, curiosity gap, bold claim | Perception — a way of seeing the reader didn't have before |
| **Core images** | Decorative — add visual interest, support the argument | Behavioral — change how the reader perceives the subject permanently |
| **Syntax** | Newspaper — invisible, functional, could be anyone's | Thumbprint — identifiable as THIS writer's consciousness |
| **Closing** | CTA — "do this now," "share if you agree" | Residue — the sentence they'll carry out of the piece |
| **Structure** | Satisfy → satisfy → satisfy (deliver what was promised) | Satisfy → deny → deliver something they didn't know they wanted |

**Score**: Count how many signals operate in Capture vs. Haunting mode.
- 4-5 Capture: Full transformation needed
- 2-3 Capture: Partial — the haunting potential exists but isn't deployed
- 0-1 Capture: Piece is already haunting-forward — refine, don't transform

### Phase 2: Identify the Haunting Candidate

Every piece — no matter how commercial, functional, or short — has at least ONE moment with haunting potential. The line, image, idea, or perception that could embed permanently if given the right conditions.

**How to find it**:
1. Read the piece and notice which moment you can't stop thinking about. That's the candidate.
2. If no such moment exists, you haven't perceived deeply enough. Return to the subject and ask: *What have I noticed about this that I've never seen anyone articulate?*
3. The candidate is almost never the opening line. It's often buried in paragraph 3, or halfway through a supporting point, or in a throwaway observation.

**The Browning Test**: Robert Browning's "Meeting at Night" — Vuong says he thinks about one stanza from this poem "every other day for 20 years." YOUR haunting candidate should aspire to this level of persistent presence.

Write the candidate in one sentence: **"The line I want the reader to still be carrying when they close this:"**

### Phase 3: Apply the Thumbprint Standard

Take the haunting candidate and push it toward syntactic uniqueness:

1. **Strip newspaper syntax**: Remove every construction that any competent writer could produce. Subject-verb-object flatness kills haunting.
2. **Inject the thumbprint**: How does YOUR consciousness arrange words? Not "good writing" — YOUR writing. The syntax that could be identified as yours in a blind test.
3. **Run the Species Test**: Google the core image. If 300K+ results, the species already has it. Push the displacement deeper.
4. **Behavioral correspondence check**: Is the core metaphor based on visual resemblance (image correspondence = weak) or shared behavior between unrelated domains (behavioral correspondence = strong)?

### Phase 4: Strategic Denial Architecture

The haunting moment should arrive through denied expectation — not where the reader expected it:

1. **Build a pattern** in the first half of the piece that the reader begins to predict
2. **Deny the pattern** at the moment of maximum prediction confidence
3. **Deliver the haunting line** in the space the denial creates — the reader is open because their prediction machinery failed
4. **The exhilaration of denial**: The haunting moment should feel like a gift the reader didn't know they wanted, arriving precisely when they expected something lesser

### Phase 5: Residue Engineering

The difference between a good line and a haunting line is **sensory specificity + behavioral precision**:

| Technique | What It Does | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Behavioral verbs** | Replace "was/is" with what the subject DOES | Not "the light was warm" → "the light pressed against the wall like a palm" |
| **Sensory anchoring** | Attach the intellectual insight to a physical sensation | Not "change is hard" → "change has the texture of wet clay under fingernails" |
| **Etymological depth** | Use a word at its root meaning, not its dictionary-shallow definition | Not "comprehend" (understand) → "comprehend" (to grasp with hands, from Latin *comprehendere*) |
| **Threshold moments** | Capture the unnamed state between two named states | Not "she decided" → the moment BEFORE deciding, which has no name |
| **Mutual estrangement** | The comparison changes both elements, not just one | "Moss like applause" — now moss AND applause are re-seen |

### Phase 6: The Haunting Battery

Before finalizing, run all five tests:

- [ ] **The 20-Year Test**: Will someone think about a sentence from this piece every other day for 20 years? If not, which sentence comes closest — and can it be pushed?
- [ ] **The Thumbprint Test**: Could this piece be identified as yours in a blind test — from syntax, not topic?
- [ ] **The Diachronic Test**: Would this piece hold up read alongside the best thing you've ever read on this subject?
- [ ] **The Denial Test**: Is there at least one moment where the reader's expectation is strategically denied?
- [ ] **The Species Test**: Does at least one key image or metaphor fail to return 300K+ Google results?
- [ ] **The Involuntary Test**: Will the reader encounter the subject later that day and involuntarily think of YOUR sentence? (The strongest indicator of haunting)

## Output Requirements

**Deliver**:
1. The Capture Diagnosis table (Phase 1)
2. The Haunting Candidate — one sentence, identified and justified

4. The Strategic Denial Map — where expectations build and where they break

6. Haunting Battery results (all 6 checks)

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: Before delivering, review output against the **Anti-Patterns** in `genius.md` § Anti-Patterns. Key risks:
> - **"Literary" decoration** that adds atmosphere but doesn't haunt — if the image can be removed without loss, it's decoration, not haunting
> - **Forced profundity** — haunting is quiet, specific, and often arrives through small details, not grand declarations
> - **Hook disguised as haunting** — a pattern interrupt ("Did you know...?") is capture, not haunting. Haunting doesn't grab attention; it takes up permanent residence
> - **Aesthetic without perception** — beautiful sentences that don't change how the reader sees anything are still capture, just prettier capture
