# Cliché Rescue Protocol

Deploy Vuong's radical position: "There's no such thing as cliché" (Shklovsky). The problem is never the subject — it's the *idea* of the subject. Every familiar topic is a perception opportunity waiting for estrangement.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: This workflow fires when you need to write about a "done" subject. If the subject is novel or niche, the Estrangement Engine may be more appropriate.

## Execution

### Phase 1: Name the Idea (Not the Subject)

The subject isn't the problem. *The idea* of the subject is the problem.

| Subject | The Idea (What Makes It Cliché) | The Subject Itself (What's Actually There) |
|---------|--------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| Grandmother in kitchen | Warmth, nostalgia, flour on apron, homemade cookies | An actual human being in an actual room with specific objects, smells, light, sounds, history, politics, language |
| Sunset | Beauty, romance, endings, "golden hour" | A star's radiation filtered through atmosphere at an angle — violence, physics, optics |
| Coffee | Morning ritual, productivity, "fuel" | A specific temperature of liquid in a specific vessel at a specific time — the behavior of steam, the sound of ceramic on wood |
| Love | Warmth, connection, completion, "you complete me" | A neurological event that changes motor behavior, language patterns, perception of time, relationship to risk |

**The key move**: Separate the IDEA of the subject (the cultural overlay) from the SUBJECT ITSELF (what's actually there when you actually look).

### Phase 2: Context Displacement

Take the subject and place it in an unfamiliar behavioral context. The rose itself doesn't change — only where you put it:

**Template**: [Subject] removed from [Expected Context] → placed in [Unexpected Context]

| Operation | Result |
|-----------|--------|
| Rose + bride's hair | Cliché (expected context) |
| Rose + Mike Tyson's ear | Estranged (unexpected context — both rose and Tyson are re-seen) |
| Coffee + morning routine | Cliché |
| Coffee + midnight courtroom | Estranged |
| Sunset + romantic beach | Cliché |
| Sunset + factory parking lot | Estranged |

### Phase 3: Behavioral Re-perception

After displacement, don't just describe the subject in its new context — re-perceive its *behavior*:
1. What does this subject *do* that you've never articulated?
2. What is its rate of change? Its quality of presence?
3. What behaviors does it share with something from a radically different domain?
4. How does the new context change how the subject behaves?

### Phase 4: Write from Re-perception

Draft the passage starting from your behavioral observations, not from your knowledge of the subject. You're not writing about coffee — you're writing about the specific behavior of steam in this specific room at this specific time.

**Rules**:
- No adjectives that could apply to the subject's *idea* (no "warm" for grandmother, no "beautiful" for sunset, no "energizing" for coffee)
- Behavioral verbs only — what does the subject *do*, not what is it *like*?
- The displacement context should alter the subject's behavior in ways that surprise you

### Phase 5: The Permission Test

Before finalizing, verify:
- [ ] You did NOT exile the subject — you wrote about it, just from a new perceptual position
- [ ] The "idea" of the subject has been dissolved — the reader encounters the actual subject
- [ ] The new context creates genuine mutual estrangement (both elements re-seen)
- [ ] At least one behavioral observation surprises even you
- [ ] The piece could not be dismissed as "another [subject] piece"

## Output Requirements

**Deliver**:
1. The Idea vs. Subject table (Phase 1)
2. 3 context displacements (Phase 2)
3. Behavioral re-perception notes (Phase 3)
4. The written passage (minimum 150 words)
5. A one-sentence summary of the re-perception: "I used to see [subject] as [idea]. Now I see it as [new perception]."

**Quality Standard**: A reader who's "tired of hearing about [subject]" should finish the piece thinking: "I've never thought about it that way."

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: The biggest risk is *superficial displacement* — putting the subject in an unusual context but still describing it through the lens of its idea. A coffee in a courtroom described as "a warm comfort" hasn't been rescued. The displacement must change the *perception*, not just the setting.
