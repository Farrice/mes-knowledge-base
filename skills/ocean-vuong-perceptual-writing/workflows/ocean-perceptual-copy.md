# Perceptual Copy — Defamiliarized Conversion Copy That Escapes the Median Sentence (Ocean Vuong)

Open ten landing pages in a category and they are the same page. "Are you tired of struggling with X?" The pain agitation. The three-bullet promise. The countdown. Each one is *mimesis* — naming a problem that already has a name — and each one is instantly Google-returnable, which is the same as saying instantly invisible: the prospect has read this exact sentence on the last nine tabs and their perception has gone automatic. Vuong's diagnosis of "the newspaper sentence" — prose standardized for "brevity to keep room for advertising" — is now, literally, the prose *of* advertising, and the homogenizing pressure has only worsened: "before AI we've been homogenizing the sentence." This pass does not rebuild the page and does not add claims. It takes the four or five lines the conversion actually turns on — the problem, the mechanism, the proof, the close — and *re-sees* them through a behavioral displacement the prospect has never held, so the page stops reading like the category and starts reading like a single, unmistakable consciousness that "knows what they want better than they do." It is the tired-problem rose taken out of the bride's hair and put in Mike Tyson's ear. The subject never changes — the prospect still has the same problem, the offer still makes the same true promise — only the *automatized perception* of it is broken. Ocean tunes the perception; he never tunes the proof. The page differentiates without becoming literary and without losing a single claim.

This is **not** `haunted-copy.md`. That workflow is residue-first — it engineers the recursive thought loop so the prospect can't stop thinking about the page. This one is **differentiation-first**: the organizing question is not "will they think about this in two weeks?" but "could this page be confused with the median competitor page — could a reader paste any load-bearing line into Google and get a wall of results?" Residue is a byproduct here; escape from the median sentence is the job. Run `haunted-copy` when the page already differentiates and needs to *stick*; run this when the page is drowning in category sameness and needs to become un-confusable first.

- **Signature Moves → Behavioral Displacement (#2) and Cliché Rescue (#4)** — match *how the problem behaves* (moves, grows, hides, re-emerges), not how it looks; never avoid the overdone problem, put it in Mike Tyson's ear.

Specifically confirm **Q1** (the page genuinely needs to feel *new* — a re-seeing, not recycled angles with competent syntax), **Q2** (the prospect's problem is "overdone" — grief, fear, overwhelm, falling behind, wasted spend — exactly the subjects Vuong rescues from automatized perception; if the problem is hyper-technical and niche, the median sentence may not even exist and estrangement is unnecessary), and **Q4** vs. the honesty constraint below (the page can bear permanent residue, not just a 2-line hook needing raw capture; for a 2-line ad route to `haunted-social`).

A behavioral displacement that makes a *false* proof point feel inevitable is the worst failure in this skill — "the better the perception, the more convincing the lie." Before estranging any load-bearing line, confirm the claim under it is already true and substantiated. If a conversion turn needs a real proof point, a real mechanism, or a real offer that does not yet exist, **that routes back to `copy-engine` (proof/offer/mechanism architecture) or `luke-iha` (proof ladders), never to a beautiful sentence.** Devices re-see what is true; they cannot make a missing claim exist, and they must never be the reason an empty one *sounds* airtight. Ocean tunes the perception; copy-engine owns the proof.

## Input Required

- **The copy as it stands** — the actual ad / landing page / email / VSL section, whole, not a summary. We re-see specific real lines, so we need the real lines.
- **The format** — ad / landing page / email / VSL section (drives how much plain runway you have between estranged turns, and therefore how many lines you can re-see before the strangeness starts carpet-bombing).
- **The category's median sentences** — 3–5 of the actual lines the competition uses ("Tired of X?", "Imagine if…", "The #1 mistake…"). This is the *map of what to escape*; you cannot escape the median until you've named it.
- **The prospect's problem, in their own words** — not the marketer's framing. The lived texture: how the problem *behaves* in their day, when it hides, when it re-emerges. (This is the raw material for behavioral displacement — perception before syntax.)
- **The single conversion claim per turn** (honesty spine) — for each line we'll re-see (problem, mechanism, proof, CTA), the *true* thing it asserts, already substantiated. We estrange these; we do not invent them.
- **The one expected payoff** — the moment in the page where the reader can predict your next move (it'll be the line every competitor also delivers). This becomes the strategic denial.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Map the median; mark the conversion turns; do NOT carpet-bomb the page

A converting page has dozens of sentences and maybe four or five the sale actually turns on. Estrangement belongs only on those — and only where the *median* version is what's killing the line. Everything else stays mimetic on purpose: "Sometimes you do need sentences like 'he walked into the room.'" The plain lines are the floor that makes the re-seen ones audible; carpet-bombing every line with strangeness burns out the reader's wonder-detector and produces the *literary* failure (page reads like it's trying to win an award, not sell).

First, lay the page beside the category. For each conversion turn, write the median sentence next to your current line — if they're twins, that line is invisible and is a re-seeing candidate.

| Conversion turn | Where it lives | The median (what the category says) | What re-seeing must do | Principle the soil calls for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The problem** | the open / pain block | "Are you tired of struggling with X?" | make the prospect *re-see their own problem* through a displacement they've never held — un-confusable with tab 9 | **Behavioral Displacement** + Cliché Rescue |
| **The mechanism** | mid-body, after the promise | "Our 3-step system does X, Y, Z." | describe *how it works* through a behavioral metaphor so it can't be flattened into the category's feature list | **Behavioral Correspondence** (Principle 1) |
| **The proof** | the testimonial / data block | "Join 10,000 happy customers." | amplify the ONE true proof detail so it embeds as residue, not a wall of logos | **Poietic threshold** + Haunting Residue |
| **The expected payoff** | wherever the reader can predict you | the reveal every competitor also delivers | deny it once — "this director has thought ahead of me" | **Satisfy or Deny** (Principle 5) |
| **The CTA / close** | the button, the last line | "Click here now / Don't miss out." | the line that re-sees what the prospect would be *choosing to un-see* by leaving | **Strategic Denial** + Behavioral close |

> **The anti-carpet-bomb rule.** If a load-bearing line is *already* un-confusable with the median, leave it — re-seeing a line that already escapes is decoration, and decoration trains the reader's eye to skim exactly where you need it to stop. Mark **at most one re-seeing per turn**, and re-see only the turns where you and the competitor say the same sentence. A VSL section can bear three or four; a landing page usually two or three; a single email often *one*. More estranged lines than genuine median-collisions = the strangeness is the point, which means perception was skipped.

### Step 2 — Behavioral displacement on the problem (the rose in Mike Tyson's ear)

This is the engine of the whole pass. The prospect's problem has been named a thousand times; they are numb to its name. Do not *exile* the problem (don't avoid the overdone subject) and do not *agitate* it harder (pain-stacking is still mimesis, just louder). **Estrange the context** — put the same true problem into a behavioral environment the reader has never held it in, so they re-see it as if for the first time.

The non-negotiable order is **perception before syntax**. Before writing anything, answer from the prospect's lived texture (Input #4): *what does this problem actually DO?* Not its symptoms — its **behavior**. How does it move through their day, grow, hide, re-emerge? Only once you can name a behavior no competitor has articulated do you reach for the displacement.

| Move | What you do | Worked on "small business owner drowning in admin" |
|---|---|---|
| **Name the median** | the category's mimetic line | "Tired of spending hours on paperwork instead of growing your business?" |
| **Find the behavior** (perception) | how the problem *moves*, not how it looks | it doesn't *attack* — it *accretes*, silently, while you're doing the real work; you notice only the volume |
| **Find the cross-domain twin** | what unrelated thing behaves identically | **silt in a riverbed** — invisible per grain, then one day the channel won't carry the boat |
| **Write it through the displacement** | re-see the true problem, plainly | "The admin didn't arrive. It settled — a grain at a time, under everything you actually meant to do — until the week you couldn't push the work through anymore." |

Two hard guards from the genius:
- **Behavioral, not image-based (Anti-Pattern: image-based metaphor).** "The paperwork was a mountain" is visual correspondence — the reader *sees* the comparison and recognizes it; the moon is still a moon. "Moss grows along the tree like applause" is behavioral — it changes how moss *behaves* in the mind forever. Match the *rate*, the *accretion*, the *hiding-then-surfacing* of the problem — not its appearance. The test: does the displacement retroactively change how the prospect *experiences* the problem the next time it shows up? If it only gives them a prettier picture of it, it's an image metaphor in a costume.
- **The displacement re-sees what is true (honesty spine).** The silt metaphor is allowed only because the problem *genuinely* accretes silently — that's the prospect's real experience. If you reached for a displacement that implied the problem is, say, *fatal* when it's merely annoying, you've manufactured stakes the claim can't carry. Re-see the truth, sharper; don't inflate it.

### Step 3 — Behavioral correspondence on the mechanism (escape the feature-list)

The category describes mechanisms the same way: a numbered system, three pillars, a flywheel. All mimesis — naming a process that already has a name — and all flattenable into the same feature comparison the prospect runs across nine tabs. Re-see *how the solution actually behaves* through a cross-domain twin, so the mechanism becomes un-flattenable and the prospect understands it AND carries it.

1. **Name the median mechanism line** (the "3-step system" sentence).
2. **Name the mechanism's behavior** (perception): not features — the *behavioral change* the solution creates. What does it actually *do* to the prospect's situation? (e.g. it doesn't *add* a tool; it *removes the friction that compounds* — the situation stops accreting.)
3. **Find the behavioral twin and write it through** — the reader should grasp the working AND re-see it. (e.g. "It doesn't help you move the silt faster. It keeps the channel from filling — so the work flows through the same way it did before the river forgot how to carry you.")

> **Mechanism honesty (hard gate).** This is where false claims hide best, because a behavioral metaphor makes a mechanism *feel* inevitable and proven. Confirm the mechanism behaves the way your displacement says it does. If the displacement implies an outcome the product can't actually produce, the *better* the metaphor the more dangerous the lie — route the real mechanism claim back to `copy-engine` / `luke-iha` and re-see only what's substantiated.

### Step 4 — Poietic threshold on the proof (one detail that embeds, not a logo wall)

The category proves the same way: "Join 10,000 customers," five-star rows, logo grids. Mimesis again — recognition, not perception. Of all the *true* proof points, find the ONE with a poietic threshold — the specific, sensory, almost-uncomfortable detail that captures the *change* between two named states (skeptical → converted, drowning → free) rather than naming the after-state. Amplify that one; let the median proof recede to the floor.

| Median proof (recognition) | Poietic proof (perception — the threshold detail) |
|---|---|
| "She was very happy with the results." | "She emailed at 6:11am — not to thank me, to ask if the quiet was normal. She'd never had a Monday without the dread before." |
| "From $400 to $47,000 in 11 weeks." | keep the number (the specificity *is* the threshold) — but anchor it to the behavioral change: "the week he stopped checking the app before he was fully awake." |

The threshold detail does more work than a dozen testimonials because it's *poietic* — it captures the unnamed moment of crossing, which the reader can't help but place themselves inside. **Honesty spine: the detail must be real.** A fabricated sensory detail is the most seductive lie on the page. If you don't have a true threshold detail, that's a `luke-iha` proof-ladder problem — get a real one; do not invent the 6:11am email.

### Step 5 — Strategic denial on the expected payoff (satisfy, then deny, once)

Linear copy is a pattern-recognition game: the reader is constantly predicting your next move, and the moment they predict it, they stop paying *active* attention — they've read this page nine times. Find the one beat where the reader can predict the payoff (it'll be the exact reveal every competitor also delivers — "and that's when I discovered the secret…"). **Satisfy the pattern most of the page** — you need the mimetic scaffolding so the denial registers — then **deny that one expected payoff** and deliver something they didn't know they wanted. The reader feels "this director has thought ahead of me," and passive consumption flips to active engagement: they decide you know what they want better than they do.

| Step | Move | Example (a "discover the secret" page) |
|---|---|---|
| Find the predicted payoff | the line the reader can finish for you | reader expects: "…and here's the secret system that fixes it." |
| Satisfy up to the threshold | build the expectation honestly | "You came here for the trick. The shortcut. The thing the others won't tell you." |
| Deny — deliver the unexpected-but-true | the off-center thing they actually needed | "There isn't one. The silt was never the problem. You've just never had anything that kept the channel clear *while* you did the work." |
| The delight | the payoff they didn't know they wanted | the denial *is* the differentiation — no competitor denies their own hook; you just became the only honest page in the category |

> **Deny once, not constantly.** Strategic denial is a single drop, not a posture. A page that denies every expectation is just noise the reader can't pattern at all — and "if everything is a surprise, nothing is." One denial, placed where the median is most predictable. **Honesty spine bonus:** strategic denial is *natively* honest here — the most powerful denial is usually "the thing everyone promises isn't the thing" — which only works because it's true. Never deny the expected payoff with a *bigger false promise*; deny it with a *truer* one.

### Step 6 — Behavioral close (the line that re-sees what they'd un-see)

The category closes on urgency: "Click now / Don't miss out / Limited time." Mimesis, and the prospect is numb to it. The perceptual close doesn't pressure — it states what has already happened (the prospect now sees their problem differently than they did three minutes ago) and re-sees the *act of leaving* through the same behavioral lens you opened with, so the close braids back to the problem displacement. **This is where Ocean hands off:** the *do-it* punch — the short, hard, picture-able final word — is `ward-saxon-punch`'s job. Ocean supplies the re-seen *frame*; Ward (or `ward-copy-rhetoric`'s Saxon close) lands the button. Don't write a soft literary CTA; write the perceptual frame and let the punch close it.

- Median: "Sign up now before the price goes up."
- Perceptual frame (Ocean): "You've watched the silt for years without a word for it. You have one now. You can close this tab — the word won't close with it."
- The punch (hand to Ward): "Clear the channel." (button) — short, Saxon, picture-able, braided to the displacement.

> **Honesty at the close.** The close states what is *true* — that a perception was delivered — never a false consequence. "It won't close in your mind" is a claim about the prospect's experience, which is allowed; "you'll lose your business if you don't act now" is a manufactured stake, which is not. Re-see the leaving; don't fabricate the cost of it.

### Step 7 — The Species-Test gate + the final cold read (the line that separates re-seen copy from a strangeness demo)

Now run the two gates that decide whether this shipped or just got fancier.

**(a) The Species-Test, line by line (a real gate, not a vibe).** For every load-bearing line you re-saw, ask: *has the species had this sentence yet?* When the brief allows, actually check it — paste the line (or its core phrasing) into Google. "300,000 people beat you to it" is the standard Ben Lerner set. If a line returns a wall of results, it's still the median sentence wearing perfume — keep going. The whole point of the pass is escape from the Google-returnable; a line that fails the Species-Test failed the job, however nice it sounds.

**(b) The cold read-aloud, as a skeptical first-time buyer in a hurry.** Not the re-seen lines in isolation — how they sit in the flow:
- **The not-maximalist check.** Confirm the estranged lines are *spread*, with plain mimetic runway between them. Three re-seen lines back to back reads as literary pretension — the page announces its own cleverness and stops selling. The plain prose is the silence that makes the re-seeing audible.
- **The clarity-before-strangeness check (Anti-Pattern guard).** Read each re-seen line as a skeptic skimming. If they have to re-read to parse it, strangeness beat clarity — and a confused buyer doesn't buy. Estrangement is *idiosyncrasy*, not obscurity; the line must be instantly legible AND un-confusable.
- **The "show > tell the device" check.** Would the reader *notice* the technique, or just feel the page knows them? If they'd notice the displacement, bury it harder or cut it — "if they notice the technique, the technique failed." Never annotate the moves.
- **The honesty re-check (the kill-on-sight gate).** Read every re-seen line for whether it made a *false or unsubstantiated* claim *feel* inevitable. The better the perception, the more this matters. Any displacement gilding an empty claim — cut it and route the claim to `copy-engine`/`luke-iha`. The page must escape the median *and* stay true; escaping the median with a lie is a worse page than the median.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Social (LinkedIn / X → the link)** | One re-seeing only — at hook length the strangeness shows in a heartbeat, and there's no plain runway to hide it. Re-see the *problem* (the rose-in-Tyson's-ear displacement as the scroll-stopper), then hand the last line to `ward-saxon-punch`. Don't attempt mechanism + proof + denial in a post; that's the link's job. The hook re-sees, the page does the full pass. |
| **Marketing (ad / paid landing)** | Ads: usually one displacement (the problem) + the punch; the Species-Test matters *most* here because ad copy is the most homogenized surface on the internet. Paid landing pages: two or three re-seen turns (problem + mechanism, or problem + proof), with the strategic denial reserved for the page, not the ad. Keep plain runway between — paid traffic skims hard. |
| **Copy (VSL section / sales page)** | The format that can bear the full pass — long, with plenty of mimetic runway between turns. Re-see the problem (displacement), the mechanism (behavioral correspondence), the proof (poietic threshold), deny one expected payoff, close on the re-seen frame + Saxon punch. Spread the four across the section; never two re-seen lines in the same 30 seconds of read. This is the canonical use. |
| **Ghostwriting (founder's sales letter / authority page)** | The displacement must come from the *founder's* consciousness, not yours — find how *they* actually see the prospect's problem and amplify *its* strangeness (the Thumbprint). Imposing your own showpieces here is the homogenized-voice failure; the page should read as un-confusable with the category AND unmistakably *this founder*. Re-see through their lived noticing, not your vocabulary. |
| **Content (long-form that converts — newsletter-to-offer, essay-to-CTA)** | Lead with perception, not pitch: the whole piece is the problem-displacement extended, and the offer arrives as the *only* mechanism that fits the world the displacement built. Residue does heavy lifting here (the diachronic reader rewards it), so this is the one place `haunted-copy` and this workflow naturally stack — escape the median first, then engineer the recursion. |

### Worked example A — Landing page for a bookkeeping service (solo business owners)

**Category median:** problem → *"Drowning in receipts and spreadsheets? Get your time back."* · mechanism → *"Our 3-step onboarding handles your books so you can focus on growth."* · proof → *"Trusted by 2,000+ small businesses."*
**Honesty spine:** the service genuinely reconciles books weekly and genuinely removes the founder's reconciliation work — both substantiated. We estrange those truths; we invent nothing.

**1 · Behavioral displacement on the problem**
> MEDIAN: "Drowning in receipts and spreadsheets? Get your time back."
> BEHAVIOR (perception): the problem doesn't flood — it *accretes*, one ignored receipt at a time, invisible until the day the year won't reconcile.
> TWIN: silt settling in a channel.
> AFTER: "The books didn't fall behind. They settled — a receipt at a time, under everything you actually meant to do — until the quarter came and the numbers wouldn't move through anymore."
> *Why it escapes the median:* "drowning" is the category's image metaphor — every bookkeeping page uses it, instantly Google-returnable. *Settling, a receipt at a time, until the numbers wouldn't move* is behavioral — it re-sees the prospect's real experience (silent accretion, not a flood) and can't be confused with tab 9. The prospect re-sees their own shoebox of receipts differently.

**2 · Behavioral correspondence on the mechanism**
> MEDIAN: "Our 3-step onboarding handles your books so you can focus on growth."
> AFTER: "We don't help you shovel the silt faster. We keep the channel clear while you work — so the numbers move through the way they did before the year forgot how to carry you."
> *Honesty:* the service genuinely does continuous reconciliation (keeps the channel clear), not a one-time cleanup — the metaphor states the true mechanism, not a faster-shoveling promise the service can't keep.

**3 · Poietic threshold on the proof**
> MEDIAN: "Trusted by 2,000+ small businesses."
> AFTER: "One client told me she'd stopped opening the bank app on Sunday nights. Not because the money changed — because the not-knowing did."
> *Honesty:* a real client detail (the Sunday-night dread, the not-knowing) — the threshold between two named states (dread → quiet), not a fabricated number. Keep the 2,000 as plain floor below it; let the one detail carry the perception.

**5 · Re-seen close**
> MEDIAN: "Get started today."
> FRAME (Ocean): "You've watched the books settle for years without a word for it. You have one now. You can close this tab — the silt keeps settling either way."
> PUNCH (→ ward-saxon-punch): **"Clear the channel."** (button)
> *Why it converts:* the close braids back to the opening displacement (silt → channel), states a true consequence (it keeps settling — which it does), pressures nothing, and hands the *do-it* word to a short, hard, picture-able Saxon punch that *is* the metaphor resolved.

### Worked example B — Email (VSL-style) for a sleep-coaching program (chronically exhausted professionals)

**Category median:** problem → *"Tired of waking up tired? Discover the secret to deep, restorative sleep."* · expected payoff → *"…and that's when I found the one supplement / hack that changed everything."*
**Honesty spine:** the program genuinely rebuilds sleep *architecture* over weeks (no single hack); the "there is no hack" denial is the literal truth of the offer. We estrange the truth; the denial is honest by construction.

**1 · Behavioral displacement on the problem**
> MEDIAN: "Tired of waking up tired?"
> BEHAVIOR (perception): exhaustion doesn't *hit* in the morning — it *compounds* across nights, each short sleep borrowing against the next, so the debt is invisible until the body calls it in.
> AFTER: "You're not waking up tired. You've been borrowing sleep from the next night for so long the body finally stopped extending you credit."
> *Why it escapes the median:* "waking up tired" is the recognition line — the prospect has read it everywhere and gone numb. *Borrowing from the next night until the body stops extending credit* re-sees exhaustion as an accruing debt with behavior (compounding, then called in) — un-confusable, and it makes the prospect re-experience their own mornings.

**4 · Strategic denial on the expected payoff**
> PREDICTED PAYOFF: the reader, primed by every sleep email, expects "…and then I discovered the one hack/supplement that fixed it."
> AFTER: "You came here for the hack. The supplement, the trick, the thing the others gatekeep. There isn't one. You can't pay off compounding debt with a single payment — and you can't trick a body that's been keeping the books for twenty years. What you can do is stop borrowing. That takes weeks. It's the only thing that's ever worked."
> *Why it converts:* the page satisfies the pattern (names the hack the reader expects), then denies it — and the denial flips the reader into active engagement ("this is the only one not selling me a hack"). Crucially, the denial is *true*: the program really has no single hack, so the strategic denial and the honesty spine are the same move. No competitor denies their own hook; this email just became the only honest one in the inbox.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` (rows: **Image-based metaphor**, **The newspaper sentence**, **Hook addiction**, **The homogenized voice**) and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (rows: **1. Perceptual Novelty / Estrangement**, **6. Haunting Residue**). Flag and fix before delivering.

- **Re-seeing only where you collide with the median:** every estranged line sits on a conversion turn where your line and the competitor's line were twins. None on lines that already escape, none on connective filler. Remove a re-seeing and the line must get *more confusable with the category*; if it doesn't, it was decoration — cut it.
- **Behavioral, not image-based (Anti-Pattern):** each displacement matches how the problem/mechanism *behaves* (moves, grows, hides, compounds), not how it looks. "The problem was a mountain" fails; "it settled, a receipt at a time, until it wouldn't move through" passes. The displacement must retroactively change how the prospect *experiences* the subject, not just give them a prettier picture.
- **Species-Test passed, line by line:** every load-bearing re-seen line was checked (Googled where the brief allowed) and is *not* a wall-of-results sentence. A line that returns the median is the median in perfume — keep going. This is the gate the whole workflow exists to pass.
- **Not-maximalist; strangeness spread:** re-seen lines are distributed with plain mimetic runway between them. No three estranged lines back to back. The plain floor makes the re-seeing audible; carpet-bombing produces the literary-pretension failure (page sounds like it's chasing an award, not a sale).
- **Clarity before strangeness (Anti-Pattern guard):** every re-seen line parses on first read for a skeptic in a hurry. Estrangement is idiosyncrasy, not obscurity — un-confusable AND instantly legible. If it needs a re-read, rebuild plain, then re-add the displacement.
- **One strategic denial, true by construction:** the page denies the expected payoff *once*, and the thing it delivers instead is *truer* than the payoff it denied — never a bigger false promise. The most powerful denial is "the thing everyone promises isn't the thing," and it only works because it's true.
- **Residue present (Rubric row 6):** at least one re-seen line could survive in the prospect's mind after the tab closes — the byproduct of genuine perception, not the goal. (If residue is the *primary* need on an already-differentiated page, that's `haunted-copy`'s job.)
- **Honesty spine intact (the veto):** every re-seen line estranges something *true and substantiated*. No displacement is the reason an empty or unproven claim *sounds* inevitable — and the better the perception, the harder this is checked. Any line gilding a missing claim is cut on sight and the claim routed to `copy-engine` / `luke-iha`. Ocean re-sees the perception; he never manufactures the proof. **A page that escapes the median with a lie is worse than the median.**

## Common Pitfalls

- **Literary pretension in a commercial context (the #1 risk).** Reaching for the lush sentence to *sound* like Ocean Vuong instead of doing the perceptual work — so the page reads like it's auditioning for a poetry prize while the prospect, who came to solve a problem and buy, bounces. This is the exact failure the genius.md Opus Calibration warns about: "slot-filling lyrical vocabulary onto an unobserved subject." **Recovery:** run the cold read as a *skeptical buyer in a hurry*, not a literary reader. If any line would make them think "this is trying to be clever," cut it. Perception before syntax — go back and name the behavior you actually noticed; if you can't, no amount of beautiful language will rescue the line. Estrangement is *idiosyncrasy in service of selling*, never decoration.
- **Carpet-bombing strangeness onto every line.** Treating every sentence as a re-seeing candidate, so the whole page is estranged and the reader's wonder-detector burns out before the CTA — and the *actual* conversion turns drown in the noise. This is the not-maximalist failure: "I'm not arguing for maximalist sentences." **Recovery:** re-run Step 1. Re-see *only* the turns where you collide with the median; everything else stays deliberately mimetic. The plain prose is the silence that makes the re-seen lines land. Often the strongest page has *one* unforgettable displacement and otherwise clean, plain copy.
- **A displacement gilding an unproven claim (honesty-spine breach — the most dangerous failure).** A flawless behavioral metaphor that makes a benefit the offer can't deliver, or a proof detail that didn't happen, feel inevitable. The better the perception, the more convincing the lie — and on a commercial page, that's not just bad craft, it's a false claim someone will act on. **Recovery:** before re-seeing any line, confirm the claim under it is true and substantiated. If it isn't, that's a `copy-engine` / `luke-iha` problem — get a real proof point or a real mechanism — never a sentence problem. Re-see the truth, sharper; never inflate it into a stake the claim can't carry.
- **Image-metaphor in a behavioral costume (the silent failure).** Writing "the deadlines were a tidal wave crashing down" and believing you've estranged it — but it's still visual correspondence (the reader *sees* a wave, recognizes the comparison, moves on), just with more words. The median sentence survived; it just got longer. **Recovery:** apply the retroactive-change test from Step 2 — does the metaphor change how the prospect *experiences* the problem the next time it shows up, or only how they *picture* it this once? If only the picture changed, pivot from how it *looks* to how it *behaves* (rate, accretion, hiding, compounding) — and re-run the Species-Test, because image metaphors are exactly the lines that come back Google-returnable.
