# Poietic Threshold Writer

Deploy Vuong's distinction between mimesis (naming what has a name) and poiesis (capturing the threshold between named states). Write about transformation, change, and becoming — not as metaphor but as perceptual event.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: Before executing, confirm the subject involves transformation, change, in-between states, or processes. This workflow is ideal for: growth narratives, identity pieces, brand evolution stories, and any content about becoming.

## Execution

### Phase 1: Map the Named States

Every transformation has at least two named endpoints. Identify them:

| Named State A | Named State B | The Threshold Between |
|--------------|--------------|----------------------|
| Bud | Rose | The infinite moments when the rose tears open |
| Stranger | Friend | The precise instant recognition shifts to familiarity |
| Doubt | Decision | The tremor before commitment lands |
| Employee | Founder | The last morning commute before the leap |

**The threshold question** (from Heidegger): "When does the rose become a rose?" There is no answer — but the question opens the space between known states where poiesis lives.

### Phase 2: Perception Work

Before writing, spend time in pure observation mode:
1. **Have you witnessed this threshold?** Not the endpoints — the moment *between* them. The exact instant when the bud is no longer a bud but not yet a rose.
2. **What sensory details exist in the threshold?** Not metaphors — actual perceptual data. What does the light do? What is the quality of sound? What does the body feel?
3. **What behavior occurs in the threshold?** Things in transition behave differently from things in stable states. A person deciding is not the same as a person who has decided or a person who hasn't decided yet. The *behavior of deciding* is its own event.

### Phase 3: Write the Threshold

Do NOT describe the endpoints. The reader already knows what a bud looks like and what a rose looks like. Describe the tearing-open — the moments that "have no definition" because they exist in the space between definitions.

**Structure options**:
1. **Single threshold, sustained**: An entire piece about one moment of becoming (the instant before the leap, the breath between languages, the silence after the last argument)
2. **Multiple thresholds, cascading**: A series of threshold moments that accumulate into a pattern of becoming
3. **Threshold as lens**: Apply the threshold perception to a subject that's usually described mimetically (leadership as the threshold between following and deciding, creativity as the threshold between knowing and not knowing)

### Phase 4: Mimetic Scaffolding

After the poietic core is written, add minimal mimetic scaffolding — the named states that bracket and contextualize the threshold. These should be brief, functional, and deliberately flat:

> "She was a student. [mimetic — functional]
> Then the semester ended and the diploma was a piece of paper she held while something she couldn't name continued to tear open behind her sternum — the same tearing she'd felt in the third week of September when the reading list stopped being assignments and started being, she didn't know what, evidence maybe, or warnings, or just the sound of someone else's thinking pressing against the wall of hers — [poietic — the threshold]
> And then she was a graduate." [mimetic — functional]

The mimetic sentences exist only to make the poietic threshold visible by contrast.

### Phase 5: The Naming Test

Review every sentence. For each one, ask:
- [ ] Does this name something that already has a name? (mimetic — keep only if structurally necessary)
- [ ] Does this capture something between names? (poietic — protect and refine)
- [ ] Does this use a dictionary definition where etymological depth would serve better?
- [ ] Would the reader encounter a perception they've never had?

## Output Requirements

**Deliver**:
1. The Named States mapping table
2. Perception work notes (what you observed about the threshold)
3. The threshold prose (minimum 200 words of sustained poietic writing)
4. The full piece with mimetic scaffolding integrated
5. One alternate threshold (different sensory approach to the same moment of becoming)

**Quality Standard**: The reader should feel the threshold — the unnamed space between known states — as a genuine perceptual event. If the threshold reads as metaphor rather than direct perception, push deeper into the sensory data of the in-between.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: The biggest risk is poetic vagueness masquerading as poietic perception. True poiesis is *precise* about the unnamed — it uses specific sensory data, behavioral observation, and exactitude. Vagueness ("something shifted inside her") is not poiesis — it's lazy mimesis of emotion.
