# Recognition Workshop

Deploy Vuong's anti-workshop methodology: recognition before correction. Build the writer's self-knowledge by naming patterns and tendencies before imposing any editorial judgment.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: This workflow is for reviewing ANY writing — yours, client drafts, ghostwritten content. It reverses the standard critique model where correction = progress.

## Execution

### Phase 1: Pure Recognition (3 Passes — No Judgment)

**Pass 1 — Syntactic Tendencies**:
- Sentence length patterns (does the writer tend toward long or short? where do they shift?)
- Tense usage (present? past? where do they switch, and is it deliberate?)
- Clause structure (subordinate? compound? simple? where does complexity appear?)
- Enjambment (where do sentences carry momentum across paragraphs?)

**Pass 2 — Perceptual Tendencies**:
- Subject affinities (trees? bodies? weather? architecture? food?)
- Sense preferences (visual? tactile? auditory? olfactory?)
- Temporal orientation (past/memory? present/observation? future/anticipation?)
- Scale patterns (macro/landscape? micro/detail? does the writer zoom in or out?)

**Pass 3 — Behavioral Tendencies**:
- How does the writer handle transitions? (Hard cuts? Bridges? Dissolves?)
- Where do they show courage? (Which topics/images get the most energy?)
- Where do they self-censor? (Where does the prose suddenly flatten or generalize?)
- What's the relationship between idea and image? (Does thinking lead to seeing, or seeing lead to thinking?)

### Phase 2: Name the Tendencies

Present findings as observations, not judgments:

| Tendency | Observation | Example from Text |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Syntactic | "You shift from past tense to present tense in paragraph 5" | [specific quote] |
| Perceptual | "You're drawn to architectural metaphors" | [specific quote] |
| Behavioral | "Your prose gets most alive when describing physical sensation" | [specific quote] |
| Behavioral | "You tend to withdraw into abstraction right when the image gets most specific" | [specific quote] |

**Critical**: Frame everything as "interesting" or "notable," never as "wrong" or "needs fixing." The goal is to build the writer's *self-knowledge*.

### Phase 3: The Thumbprint Assessment

After naming tendencies, synthesize: What is this writer's unique syntactic fingerprint?

Answer:
1. **If I read this writer's sentences blind, what would I recognize?** (Name the identifiers)
2. **What perception does this consciousness produce that no other consciousness produces?** (The thumbprint)
3. **Where is the thumbprint strongest?** (Protect these moments)
4. **Where is the thumbprint weakest?** (Where the writer has defaulted to convention or imitation)

### Phase 4: Targeted Feedback (Only Now)

With the full recognition map complete, *now* offer feedback — but targeted to the writer's specific consciousness, not generic dogma:

- **Amplify strengths**: "Your architectural metaphors are your strongest perceptual tool — lean in harder. Where you see buildings, your sentences come alive."
- **Name retreats**: "You tend to retreat into abstraction in paragraph 7. Stay in the image. Trust your eyes."
- **Challenge comfort zones**: "You've used present tense for all your strongest moments but past tense as your default. What if you wrote the whole piece in present?"
- **Question dogma**: "You seem to be avoiding short sentences. Why? Is that a choice or a rule you picked up?"

### Phase 5: The Recognition Report

**Deliver to the writer**:

1. **Your Tendencies** (Phase 2 table)
2. **Your Thumbprint** (Phase 3 synthesis — what makes your consciousness unique in syntax)
3. **Your Strengths** (where the thumbprint is most visible)
4. **Your Retreats** (where convention overrides the thumbprint)
5. **3 Experiments** (specific exercises designed for THIS writer's consciousness, not generic prompts)

## Output Requirements

**Deliver**:
1. The 3-pass recognition map
2. The tendencies table with examples
3. The thumbprint assessment
4. Targeted feedback (maximum 5 items — each grounded in the recognition, not in dogma)
5. 3 writer-specific experiments

**Quality Standard**: The writer should feel *seen*, not corrected. They should learn something about their own consciousness they didn't know before reading the feedback. If the feedback could apply to any writer, it's too generic.

---

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: The biggest risk is disguised correction — feedback that *sounds* like recognition but smuggles in conformist editing. "You tend to use long sentences" is recognition; "You tend to use long sentences, which should be shortened" is correction in recognition's clothing. Stay in recognition until Phase 4.
