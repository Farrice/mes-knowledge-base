# Haunting Social Content

Deploy Vuong's haunting principle to social media — LinkedIn, X/Twitter, Instagram, Substack. Every social content system in the arsenal is hook-first: grab attention → deliver value → close. This inverts the architecture: **what sentence will the reader still be carrying when they close their laptop?** That sentence is the nucleus. Everything else orbits it.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: This workflow is for social content that needs to BUILD authority through permanent presence in the reader's mind. For growth/reach-optimized content, the standard content engines are more appropriate. Use this when the goal is depth of impact over breadth of distribution.

## Execution

### Phase 1: The Residue-First Inversion

Standard social content architecture:
```
HOOK → Body → CTA
(designed to capture → inform → convert)
```

Haunting social content architecture:
```
RESIDUE ← Body ← Opening
(design the haunting line FIRST → build toward it → open with perception)
```

**Start from the end**. Write the sentence you want the reader to carry. Then work backward:
1. What does the reader need to see/feel/understand before that sentence hits with full force?
2. What opening perception creates the conditions for the residue to land?
3. What structural denial makes the residue arrive as a surprise rather than a conclusion?

### Phase 2: The Haunting Line

Write the line. One sentence. This is the nucleus of the entire post.

**Criteria**:
- It reframes something the audience thinks about every day — but from a perceptual angle they've never held
- It uses behavioral metaphor, not decorative metaphor
- It could not have been written by ChatGPT, Grammarly, or "any competent writer"
- The reader will encounter the subject later that day and involuntarily recall THIS sentence

**The post exists to deliver this sentence.** Everything else is scaffolding.

### Phase 3: Build the Delivery Architecture

| Platform | Architecture | Haunting Deployment |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **LinkedIn** | 150-300 words. Open with perception, not hook. One deny/deliver cycle. The haunting line lives in the final 2-3 sentences — AFTER the "see more" fold. The reader earns it. | The haunting line should be separated by white space. It should feel like it drops — a sudden change in register or rhythm. |
| **X/Twitter** | Compress to perception anchor + one behavioral metaphor. The Species Test sentence IS the tweet. No thread — the haunting lives in density. | Every word must earn its place. The haunting is in the compression — meaning pressed into fewer words than seems possible. |
| **Instagram** | First slide or caption opening = perception. Final slide or caption close = haunting line. The visual supports the behavioral displacement. | The image should create the conditions for the haunting — atmospheric, not illustrative. The text does the haunting work. |
| **Substack** | Full perceptual treatment. 800-2000 words. Multiple deny/deliver cycles. The haunting line can live deeper — paragraph 6, 7, or even the closing sentence. | In long form, the haunting line works through accumulated pressure. The reader has been led through a specific sequence of perceptions, and the haunting line releases all that pressure at once. |

### Phase 4: The Perception Opening

Standard hooks grab attention through pattern interrupts: "Most people don't realize..." / "Unpopular opinion:" / "I spent 5 years..."

Haunting openings offer a **perception** — a way of seeing that the reader didn't have before reading:

| Hook (Capture Mode) | Perception (Haunting Mode) |
|---------------------|---------------------------|
| "Most people are doing X wrong" | "X behaves like [displacement] — not the thing we keep calling it" |
| "Here's what nobody tells you about Y" | "Y has a behavior I've never seen anyone name: [threshold]" |
| "I made $X in Y days" | "The money arrived the way [behavioral metaphor] — [specific sensory detail]" |
| "Stop doing X" | "I noticed something about X that changed how I see it entirely" |

The perception opening doesn't promise value — it delivers perception immediately. The reader continues because they've already received something they didn't have, and they want to see where this new way of seeing leads.

### Phase 5: The Involuntary Return Test

Before publishing:

- [ ] **The Later That Day Test**: Will the reader encounter this subject at work/home/commute and involuntarily think of my sentence?
- [ ] **The Screenshot Test**: Would someone screenshot this post and send it to a specific friend — not because it's "useful" but because it changed how they see something?
- [ ] **The Attribution Test**: If someone repeated this idea in conversation, would they credit you by name — "someone on LinkedIn said..."?
- [ ] **The Thumbprint Test**: Could this post be identified as yours without seeing the author name?
- [ ] **The Anti-Scroll Test**: Does this post create the desire to stop scrolling and SIT with it, rather than the desire to engage and keep scrolling?

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: Key risks:
> - **Cryptic ≠ haunting**: If the reader can't understand the post, it won't haunt them. Haunting requires comprehension followed by permanent re-seeing, not confusion.
> - **Hook in disguise**: A startling claim is still capture. If the opening creates curiosity rather than perception, it's a hook wearing haunting's clothes.
> - **Engagement bait inversion**: "I don't care about likes" is still engagement bait. True haunting content may or may not perform well by platform metrics — the metric is whether the reader carries the sentence.
