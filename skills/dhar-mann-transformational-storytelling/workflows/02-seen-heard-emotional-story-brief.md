# Workflow 02: Seen-Heard Emotional Story Brief

Use this to turn a raw thought, audience struggle, or lived observation into a story seed that earns recognition before it teaches.

## Inputs

- Raw thought or topic.
- One real lived scene, observed event, or source.
- Target audience.
- Starting feeling and belief.
- Intended after-state.
- Desired format and runtime.
- Creator mission and claim boundaries.

## Diagnose before treatment

Choose the earliest weak link:

- `NO SOURCE`: emotion is plausible but invented.
- `GENERIC AUDIENCE`: the struggle applies to everyone and therefore no one.
- `ABSTRACT CONFLICT`: the brief explains a pattern without a visible choice.
- `MISSING PRIVATE SENTENCE`: the viewer cannot recognize themselves.
- `UNEARNED SHIFT`: the lesson arrives by declaration.
- `PLOT-ONLY ENDING`: the character resolves the situation but the viewer receives no agency.

## Source-missing behavior

When the earliest weak link is `NO SOURCE`, or when `ABSTRACT CONFLICT` or
`UNEARNED SHIFT` is caused by absent reality rather than weak selection, search
available sources and mark the exact scene, bridge, or claim `MISSING`.

Continue with a source-labeled brief, outline, visible placeholder, or smaller
claim when useful. Do not construct a publishable lived scene or transformation
bridge from inference. If the missing source is the essential premise, return
an explicit authorship checkpoint.

Do not demand a cinematic epiphany. An accumulated hinge and its first
observable counterchoice are valid.

## Steps

1. **Anchor provenance.**
   - Identify the exact scene, event, observation, or source line.
   - Separate known detail from inference.
   - Mark unavailable detail instead of filling it.
   - If provenance is missing or causally incomplete, apply the source-missing
     behavior above before constructing a publishable private sentence,
     conflict, or shift.

2. **Write the viewer before-state.**
   - Situation: when they encounter the problem.
   - Felt state: what it feels like in the body or day.
   - Belief: what they currently think the problem means.
   - Private sentence: what they would admit only to themselves or a trusted friend.

3. **Find the embodied conflict.**
   - Identify one ordinary situation where the belief creates a choice.
   - Name what the person wants on the surface.
   - Name the unseen value, wound, or need underneath.
   - Define the consequence of continuing the current interpretation.

4. **Design the recognition moment.**
   - Let the audience witness an action, contrast, or detail that makes the private sentence visible.
   - Do not explain the moral yet.

5. **Earn the perspective shift.**
   - State belief-before and belief-after.
   - Name the witnessed choice or discovery that bridges them.
   - Reject a shift that depends on authority alone.

6. **Complete three transformations.**
   - Character: what changes in interpretation or choice.
   - Viewer: what becomes newly thinkable or usable.
   - Community: what response, share, conversation, or positive action the piece invites.

7. **Set the positive action.**
   - Make it small, safe, and voluntarily chosen.
   - Do not prescribe care, guarantee relief, or imply therapy.

8. **Write the brief.**
   - Preserve ambiguity and emotional truth.
   - Keep dialogue, shot design, and full script authorship out of scope unless the appropriate craft route is loaded.

## Output contract

Produce a Seen-Heard Story Brief containing:

- source/provenance status;
- viewer before-state;
- private sentence;
- surface desire and hidden need;
- embodied conflict;
- recognition moment;
- belief-before/belief-after;
- three transformations;
- safe positive action;
- source and craft gaps;
- next workflow.

## Adaptations

- **Recognition-you essay/video:** keep the creator mostly outside the body; route to workflow 06.
- **Testimony-I creator story:** preserve the creator's real scene; route to workflow 06.
- **Dramatized story:** use the brief as input to Stanton or a screenplay author; do not improvise a Dhar-style script.
- **Educational explainer:** route the shift to Kobi in workflow 07.

## Quality gate

- Is the source attributable?
- Is the private sentence specific enough to feel risky but common enough to recognize?
- Can the conflict be witnessed?
- Does the bridge cause the belief change?
- Are character, viewer, and community transformations distinct?
- Is the positive action safe and voluntary?
- Is the output free of therapy equivalence and fabricated detail?
