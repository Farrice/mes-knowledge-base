# Workflow 04: Accelerated YouTube Beat Map

Use this when a source-grounded story seed and package covenant exist, but the structure starts too early, explains too much, or loses escalation.

## Inputs

- Seen-Heard Story Brief.
- Packaging Promise Covenant.
- Intended format and runtime.
- Available real scenes or evidence.
- Selected transformation route: narrative or educational.
- Audience knowledge level.
- Any prior retention evidence.

## Diagnose before treatment

Choose the primary structural failure:

- `NOT STORY-READY`: no consequential choice or earned shift exists.
- `PRECONDITION OVERLOAD`: setup begins before the first meaningful consequence.
- `FALSE CLIMAX OPEN`: a dramatic fragment is shown without comprehension or fulfillment.
- `FLAT ESCALATION`: beats repeat information instead of increasing cost, contrast, or revelation.
- `LECTURE BRIDGE`: explanation replaces the moment of change.
- `RUSHED REPAIR`: the positive ending is attached rather than earned.
- `ANALYTICS GUESS`: retention tactics are prescribed without an actual drop point.

## Steps

1. **Confirm story readiness.**
   - Name the consequential question.
   - Name the change moment.
   - Confirm the source or mark missing proof.
   - Stop if the beat map would need invented scenes.

2. **Choose the entry point.**
   - Begin at the earliest beat where choice, consequence, or contradiction is already active.
   - Move required context after the audience has a reason to want it.
   - Keep only setup that changes interpretation.

3. **Fulfill the package.**
   - Place the clicked experience in the first beat.
   - Confirm full promise orientation by 30 seconds.

4. **Build accelerated beats.**
   - Consequence-first entry.
   - Minimal orientation.
   - Escalation one: cost or contradiction grows.
   - Escalation two: hidden need, unseen value, or belief becomes visible.
   - Recognition hinge.
   - Consequential choice.
   - Earned interpretation shift.
   - Positive action or repair.
   - Residue and community invitation.

5. **Mark clamp questions.**
   - For each transition, state what the viewer now needs to know.
   - Do not manufacture a question unrelated to the transformation.

6. **Mark evidence and craft gaps.**
   - Identify dialogue, scene, proof, production, or visual decisions the source does not authorize.
   - Route narrative structure to Stanton or educational change to Kobi when needed.

7. **Prepare retention diagnosis.**
   - Label the three highest-risk transitions.
   - After publication, inspect the actual graph before prescribing a fix.

## Output contract

Produce:

- structural diagnosis;
- entry-point decision;
- time-scaled beat map;
- promise-fulfillment marker;
- clamp question at every transition;
- recognition/change/action sequence;
- three retention-risk markers;
- source and craft gaps;
- post-publication diagnostic plan.

## Adaptations

- **Video essay:** the “character” may be the viewer or creator; choices can be cognitive or behavioral.
- **Dramatized narrative:** use this as a structure packet; full scene and dialogue writing needs a screenplay author.
- **Short-form:** compress to consequence, recognition, shift, action; do not add fake escalation.
- **Existing draft:** map current beats first, then cut or move only the earliest broken section.

## Quality gate

- Does the opening deliver the package rather than tease a different scene?
- Is all retained setup necessary for interpretation?
- Does each beat change cost, knowledge, contrast, or choice?
- Is the shift earned by a witnessed event?
- Are invented scenes absent?
- Are retention risks labeled as hypotheses until data exists?
