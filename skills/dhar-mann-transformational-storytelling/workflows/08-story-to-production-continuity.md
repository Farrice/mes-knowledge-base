# Workflow 08: Story-to-Production Continuity

Use this after a transformation contract and story structure exist, when the approved creative intent must cross into development and production without being lost between owners.

This workflow does not write dialogue, direct actors, design shots, or reconstruct Dhar Mann Studios' software. It creates the interface those specialists need.

## Inputs

- provenance-bearing incident, source, or approved premise;
- Viewer Transformation Contract;
- Packaging Promise Covenant;
- accelerated beat map, treatment, outline, or approved script;
- intended format and runtime;
- available production assets and known constraints;
- named production owners or departments;
- known permissions, safety, legal, or factual requirements;
- desired production-lock date.

## Diagnose before treatment

Choose the primary continuity failure:

- `SOURCE DRIFT`: the production concept no longer preserves the real incident or evidence.
- `CAPACITY BLIND`: the concept reached writing without a feasibility conversation.
- `OPAQUE UNIT`: the story cannot be divided into producible units with clear purpose.
- `HANDOFF AMBIGUITY`: owners cannot tell what they need, where, or when.
- `TRANSFORMATION LEAK`: recognition, consequence, shift, action, or mission disappears during simplification.
- `FRESHNESS DEBT`: the material, example, environment, or sequence feels recently repeated.
- `CRAFT GAP HIDDEN`: directing, editing, screenplay, safety, or another unsupported craft decision is being guessed inside the packet.

## Steps

1. **Lock the provenance-bearing development intake.**
   - Record the incident or source path.
   - Separate observed fact, interpretation, relatability hypothesis, and desired transformation.
   - Name the next development artifact and its owner.

2. **Run the concept-to-capacity checkpoint.**
   - Name the minimum environments, participants, evidence, permissions, and production dependencies.
   - Classify each as `AVAILABLE`, `ADAPTABLE`, `CONSTRAINED`, or `UNKNOWN`.
   - Decide `PROCEED`, `RESHAPE`, or `HOLD` before unnecessary writing expands.

3. **Break the approved structure into production units.**
   - A unit is the smallest coherent beat, scene, segment, or demonstration that can receive a production owner.
   - Do not invent missing script, direction, or edit decisions.

4. **Attach transformation invariants to every unit.**
   - Package promise served.
   - Recognition or information supplied.
   - Consequence or tension advanced.
   - Perspective shift prepared or earned.
   - Positive action supported.
   - Mission boundary protected.

5. **Build the department handoff matrix.**
   - Unit identifier and purpose.
   - Participants or characters.
   - Environment and continuity.
   - Props, wardrobe, evidence, graphics, permissions, and accessibility needs.
   - Timing or dependency.
   - Responsible owner.
   - Unresolved question.

6. **Run a human freshness and continuity checkpoint.**
   - Review at pre-production or table-read stage.
   - Flag recently repeated situations, examples, environments, wardrobe signals, or endings.
   - Confirm that production simplification has not broken the package-to-payoff covenant.

7. **Expose unsupported craft gaps.**
   - Route dialogue/screenplay, actor direction, blocking/coverage, editing/sound, casting, budget, legal, labor, safety, and clinical questions to grounded owners.
   - Never hide a missing specialist behind an operational checklist.

8. **Issue the production-lock verdict.**
   - `READY FOR SPECIALIST HANDOFF`
   - `RESHAPE FOR CAPACITY`
   - `HOLD FOR SOURCE OR PERMISSION`
   - `HOLD FOR CRAFT OWNER`

## Output contract

Produce:

- development intake record;
- concept-to-capacity checkpoint;
- production-unit map;
- transformation-invariant map;
- department handoff matrix;
- freshness and continuity review;
- unresolved craft-gap register;
- production-lock verdict.

## Adaptations

- **Creator-led essay:** units may be hook, scene, proof, analogy, belief bridge, and action rather than dramatized scenes.
- **Dramatized story:** a screenplay and directing owner remain required; this packet does not replace either.
- **Short-form:** use only the units that preserve promise, recognition, turn, and action.
- **Low-resource production:** reshape environments or delivery mode while preserving the transformation invariants.

## Quality gate

- Is the real incident or evidence traceable?
- Did capacity fit occur before avoidable writing or production expansion?
- Can every unit name its purpose and owner?
- Does every unit preserve at least one explicit transformation invariant?
- Are department needs concrete without inventing specialist craft?
- Did a human review freshness and continuity?
- Are unsupported directing, editing, screenplay, and operational gaps visible?
- Is the production-lock verdict justified?
