# Workflow 05: Audience Co-Greenlight Loop

Use this to let the community shape real pre-production decisions and to convert post-publication evidence into one next iteration.

## Inputs

- HEART Transformation Card.
- Seen-Heard Story Brief.
- Candidate package, theme, plot, pitch, or franchise decisions.
- Audience access and collection surface.
- Current audience evidence, if any.
- Production cost and cadence constraints.
- Intended discovery, retention, connection, and transformation signals.

## Diagnose before treatment

Choose the primary feedback failure:

- `NO DECISION`: the question cannot change production.
- `LEADING OPTIONS`: one answer is obviously preferred or mission-coded.
- `WRONG AUDIENCE`: respondents are not the people the piece is for.
- `VANITY SIGNAL`: likes or views are treated as transformation.
- `SAMPLE OVERCLAIM`: a small or biased response is treated as market truth.
- `FEEDBACK WITHOUT OWNER`: no one is responsible for the next decision.
- `SLOW LOOP`: production is too large to learn before assumptions become stale.

## Steps

1. **Name the decision.**
   - Choose one: title, theme, plot point, pitch, format, franchise, or next iteration.
   - State what will change for each plausible answer.

2. **Define the respondent.**
   - Describe the audience situation and relationship to the creator.
   - Separate existing community feedback from cold-market evidence.

3. **Build fair options.**
   - Offer three to five credible alternatives.
   - Keep quality, specificity, and mission safety roughly comparable.
   - Include an open-response field for language or stakes the creator missed.

4. **Write the greenlight instrument.**
   - Ask for preference.
   - Ask why.
   - Ask what they expect the piece to deliver.
   - Ask which private situation makes it relevant.
   - Do not ask for trauma disclosure or clinical information.

5. **Set the decision rule.**
   - Define how preference, qualitative language, mission, and production feasibility interact.
   - Audience preference informs authorship; it does not replace creator judgment.

6. **Set the smallest complete production.**
   - Preserve promise, recognition, shift, action, and feedback.
   - Use the source's 7-21 day window only as an example; set the actual cadence from constraints.

7. **Prepare the four scoreboards.**
   - Discovery.
   - Retention.
   - Connection.
   - Transformation.
   - State collection method and evidence limits for each.

8. **Create the next-iteration rule.**
   - Name one owner.
   - Name one decision date.
   - Change one primary variable unless the evidence reveals a broken mission or source assumption.

## Output contract

Produce:

- feedback diagnosis;
- decision-changing greenlight question;
- respondent definition;
- three to five options;
- survey/interview instrument;
- selection rule;
- smallest complete production unit;
- four-scoreboard plan;
- owner, decision date, and next-iteration rule.

## Adaptations

- **Small audience:** use five to ten structured conversations and label the result directional.
- **No audience yet:** use source-grounded VoC or a clearly labeled proxy panel; do not call it community validation.
- **Series/franchise:** ask about the next unresolved question, not only favorite characters.
- **Published piece:** start with actual drop points and substantive comments before asking broad preference questions.

## Quality gate

- Can the answer change a named production decision?
- Are the options genuinely viable?
- Is the respondent the intended viewer?
- Are sample and response-bias limits stated?
- Are the four scoreboards distinct?
- Is there one owner and decision date?
- Does the loop preserve creator judgment and mission?
