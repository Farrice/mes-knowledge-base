# Workflow 03: Packaging Promise Covenant

Use this when a viable story seed needs a title/thumbnail direction, concise pitch, and opening that earn attention without breaking the mission.

## Inputs

- Seen-Heard Story Brief or equivalent.
- Target audience.
- Intended platform and runtime.
- Creator/brand mission.
- Viewer after-state.
- Candidate titles, visuals, or opening ideas, if any.
- Claim and audience-safety boundaries.

## Diagnose before treatment

Find the first broken guardrail:

1. `PROMISE`: no clear reason to click.
2. `MISSION`: the strongest tension option changes what the brand stands for.
3. `PITCH`: the viewer value cannot be explained in 30 seconds.
4. `OPENING`: the clicked experience arrives late or in a different form.
5. `PAYOFF`: the story resolves plot but does not repay the emotional use of pain.
6. `CLAIM`: the package implies a result the evidence cannot support.

Treat the earliest broken guardrail. Do not add hook devices to compensate for a broken promise.

## Steps

1. **Lock the covenant.**
   - Literal promise: what the audience expects to see.
   - Emotional promise: what tension or unresolved question they expect to feel.
   - Positive repayment: what meaning, repair, or agency the content will earn.

2. **Generate three package directions.**
   - Each direction must use a genuinely different tension or curiosity frame.
   - Keep the promise truthful.
   - Reject rage bait, false certainty, therapeutic claims, humiliation, or sexualized/controversial framing that violates mission.

3. **Write the 30-second pitch.**
   - State the audience, situation, consequential question, and value.
   - The pitch must make the ending valuable without spoiling its form.

4. **Design opening fulfillment.**
   - First 10 seconds: prove the clicked experience is present.
   - By 30 seconds: establish the consequential question, character/viewer stake, and direction of value.
   - Remove greetings, creator biography, throat-clearing, and unrelated setup.

5. **Map the payoff.**
   - Literal fulfillment.
   - Perspective or moral shift.
   - Positive action.
   - Trust residue after the piece ends.

6. **Run mission and claim checks.**
   - State why the selected direction is permitted.
   - Name the highest-click option rejected and why.

7. **Issue a gate verdict.**
   - `PASS`: viable promise, pitch, opening, payoff.
   - `REVISE`: one guardrail can be repaired.
   - `STOP`: the only compelling package requires deception or mission betrayal.

## Output contract

Produce:

- first broken guardrail;
- tension-to-repair covenant;
- three package directions;
- selected direction and rejected high-click option;
- 30-second pitch;
- first-10-second and by-30-second opening beats;
- payoff map;
- mission/claim verdict.

## Adaptations

- **No thumbnail surface:** translate visual tension into headline/subhead or first-frame language.
- **Short-form:** fulfill the promise in the first beat; the 30-second boundary becomes the full arc ceiling.
- **Creator-led insight:** tension should point to the viewer's pattern, not inflate the creator's authority.
- **Brand activation:** the useful human act must be visible in the package without turning people into props.

## Quality gate

- Does every package direction promise something the piece contains?
- Is the selected direction mission-safe?
- Can the story be pitched clearly in 30 seconds?
- Does the opening fulfill the click within 10-30 seconds?
- Does the ending repay the emotional tension?
- Is the rejected option genuinely tempting and correctly rejected?
- Are guarantees and therapy claims absent?
