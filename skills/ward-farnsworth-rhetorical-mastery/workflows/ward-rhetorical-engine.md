# Ward Rhetorical Engine — Diagnose the Line, Deploy the Right Device (Ward Farnsworth)
Farnsworth's whole teaching is that a device works *because the reader never notices it* — "if they notice the technique, it's bad technique." So the skill is not "apply rhetoric"; it is **read the rhetorical soil, name what kind of thing you are trying to say, and grow the one device that belongs there.** This engine is the front door. It takes a line that matters, diagnoses what it is actually doing (or failing to do), routes to the correct device — backwards-thinking → chiasmus, must-linger → epistrophe, building-to-a-climax → anaphora, the-punch → Saxon close — and hands back three drafts plus the one-sentence reason for the choice. It picks **one**. The legacy `structural-wisdom-engine.md` stacked four devices into a single manifesto and that is now the AN-1 anti-pattern this workflow exists to replace.

Specifically, you must be able to answer Q1 (does this line carry the thesis, or is it ornament?) and Q2 (what am I actually trying to say — and what soil is that?) **before** you reach for any device. If the line carries no payload, there is no device to deploy — fix the line first or hand it back plain. If you cannot name *why this device here*, you are decorating, and the gate has stopped you correctly.

## Input Required
- **The line (or passage)** that matters — the hook, the thesis sentence, the closer, the tagline, the flat paragraph you want to make ring.
- **What you are trying to make this line do** — one phrase is fine: "I want them to feel they had it backwards," "I need this to linger after they stop reading," "this is the punch line of the whole post." If you do not know, that is fine — Step 1 diagnoses it for you.
- **The single word or idea you most want left in the air** (if you know it). This becomes the end-weight target. "I don't know" is an acceptable answer; Step 3 finds it.
- **Context**: format (social hook / headline / converting copy / tagline / essay line / speech), audience, and the native voice this has to sound like (yours, or the client's — never Churchill's).

## Workflow

### Step 1: Read the soil — diagnose what the line is *trying* to be (occasion-sensing)
This is the move the whole engine turns on, and the one most people skip: they admire devices in the abstract and never know when to reach for one. Do the opposite. **Diagnose the intent first, then pick the device the occasion calls for.** Hold the line up and ask which kind of thing it is trying to say. Most lines want exactly one of these. If the line matches two rows, it is two lines — split it (Pattern 10; AN-1).

| If the line is trying to say... | The tell in the draft | The soil grows... | Route into |
|---|---|---|---|
| "You have this **backwards** / and vice versa / it's a give-and-take" | The point is a reversal, a mismatch, a swapped cause-and-effect | **Chiasmus** (ABBA reversal) — the "wisdom illusion" loop | `/ward-farnsworth` chiasmus mode |
| "This one idea **must stay** in your head after you stop reading" | There is a single word/belief the whole piece must anchor on | **Epistrophe** (repeat at the *end* of 2–3 clauses) | `/ward-farnsworth` epistrophe mode |
| "I am **building to a climax** — lean in, the goal is coming" | A crescendo; the payoff lands on the line that *breaks* the pattern | **Anaphora** (repeat at the *start*, default three beats) | `/ward-farnsworth` anaphora mode |
| "This is **the punch** — the thesis / the closer / the CTA" | One sentence has to land like a physical hit and ring after | **Saxon close** (end on short, hard, Germanic words) | `memorable-line`, `depth-line` |
| "Validate the **head and the gut** in one breath" | The same idea needs a smart register and a visceral one | **Two Languages** (state it Latinate, restate it Saxon) | `copy-engine`, `visceral-persuasion-bridge` |
| "The line is **flat / inert** and I don't know why" | No payload, no end-weight, all one color | **Diagnosis, not device yet** → go to Step 2 | (fix the line, then re-route) |

State the diagnosis in one sentence before going further: *"This line is trying to say ___, which is [soil], so it grows [device]."* If you cannot finish that sentence, the line has no payload — that is not a rhetoric problem, and Step 2 handles it.

### Step 2: Run the anti-stacking gate — confirm the line earns ONE device, not four
Before you deploy, run the gate that makes this engine different from the manifesto it replaces. The legacy engine fired chiasmus *and* Two Languages *and* anaphora *and* epistrophe at one target; the result announces "look how I was constructed," which is the exact failure Farnsworth names ("if they notice the technique, it's bad technique"). Here you confirm the opposite.

| Gate question (from § Decision Framework) | Pass condition | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Q1 — Does this line carry the thesis, or is it ornament?** | The line carries real payload (thesis / hook / closer / anchor) | No payload → no device. Hand the line back plain; rhetoric on a trivial line is AN-2 |
| **Q3 — Where is the end?** | You can name the single last word the reader carries away | Decide the end-weight word now (Step 3 builds backward from it) |
| **Q4 — Is there a chord change available?** | There are Latinate/conceptual words to *set up* the Saxon payoff | If everything is already one color, the Saxon punch has nothing to land against — add conceptual setup or pick a different device (AN-4) |
| **Q5 — Am I stacking?** | Exactly **one** device is firing | More than one → cut to the device that does the most work. This is the headline rule of the workflow |
| **Q6 — Would Farnsworth notice the technique?** | The device will read as "that was well said," not as scaffolding | If the machinery shows, bury it deeper or cut it |

Only past this gate do you deploy. One line, one device. The other devices are not "saved for later in the same sentence" — they live in other sentences, or other workflows.

### Step 3: Start at the end — build the line backward from its last word
For *every* device, the end of the sentence is the most powerful real estate in English, and you engineer toward it (Pattern 8 — the meta-principle under the Saxon Punch and Epistrophe both). Decide the final word the reader will carry away — usually short, hard, picture-able, Saxon — then construct the line so that word arrives last and lands clean.

| Device chosen in Step 1 | What "build backward" means here |
|---|---|
| **Chiasmus** | The reversal's second half ends on the concept you want to win (A-B-B-**A**); JFK lands on *country*, not *you*. Decide which variable closes the loop, then crisscross toward it. |
| **Epistrophe** | The repeated end-word *is* the end-weight — pick the one word the piece must anchor on (Lincoln's *people*), then write 2–3 clauses that all resolve into it. Default to three (Pattern 7). |
| **Anaphora** | The repeated *opening* is the drumbeat; the climax lands on the line that **breaks** the pattern ("We shall never surrender"). Engineer the break-line's last word first, then build the three matching openings up to it. |
| **Saxon close** | Decide the stony monosyllable that ends the line ("so few," "the way," "to hate"), then set it up with a Latinate/conceptual first half so the chord change lands (Pattern 1; Pattern 8). |
| **Two Languages** | The Saxon restatement comes *second* and lands last — the head-version sets up the gut-version. End on the visceral word, not the conceptual one. |

If the strongest word is not last, rebuild until it is. Moving any other word to the end should weaken the line — that is the test that the end-weight is right.

### Step 4: Deploy the one device — write three drafts on a rising ladder
Write the line three ways with the *same* chosen device, escalating in confidence — never escalating by *adding* devices. The three drafts are: a clean direct execution, a sharper version with the chord change tightened, and the knockout. (For chiasmus: Direct → Asymmetrical → Knockout. For Saxon close: Plain → Tightened → Punch. For epistrophe/anaphora: 2-beat → 3-beat → 3-beat-with-the-break.) Aim for the *feeling* of the device, not geometric perfection — if a pure ABBA reads stilted in this voice, use the conceptual (imperfect) pattern (Pattern 9: invert the *ideas*, echo the same referent in different words) and let it ring structurally rather than literally.

Hold all three to the native voice (Opus Calibration): they must sound like *this* writer made them more eloquent, never like someone imitating Churchill or the King James Bible (AN-5). Influence, not pastiche.

### Step 5: State the one-sentence reason — and clear it against the Master Rule
Name *why this device, here*, in one sentence — the same sentence the Pre-Flight gate demanded ("This line had it backwards, so it grew a chiasmus; the loop makes the reversal feel inevitable"). If you broke a style rule to do it — passive voice, a repeated word, a long Latinate stretch — state the reason out loud in a second sentence (Pattern 11: Churchill's "so much owed by so many to so few" is passive *on purpose*, so "many" and "few" sit next to each other). No reason → revert to the rule. A device you cannot justify in one sentence is decoration, and it goes back to Step 1.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Which soil it usually grows · how the device deploys |
|--------|------|
| **Social hook** (LinkedIn / X) | Chiasmus ("you have it backwards" — feels wise enough to stop the scroll) or Saxon close (land the last word like a punch). **One device per post** — at this length the machinery shows in a single line, so the anti-stacking gate is non-negotiable. |
| **Headline** | Saxon close (end on the short, hard, concrete word) or Two Languages (run the concept once Latinate for the curiosity-click, once Saxon for the gut). Never both in one headline. |
| **Converting copy** (VSL / landing / email) | Chiasmus to shut an objection like "arguing against gravity"; epistrophe to hammer the *one* belief that must survive the close; Saxon close on the CTA line so it rings after they stop reading. Different lines get different devices — that is allowed; the gate forbids two *in one line*, not two in one page. |
| **Tagline / brand line** | Chiasmus or Saxon close — the closed-loop, quotable, admits-no-objection feel. The structure lends the "hue of wisdom" that makes a line feel inevitable rather than written. Short, hard words. |
| **Essay line** | Saxon close on the thesis sentence; anaphora across a few sentences *building toward* a section's payoff. The reader should finish having *felt* the device without being able to point to it. |
| **Speech / keynote / manifesto** | The native habitat and the only place the fuller toolkit earns its keep: build with anaphora, switch to epistrophe for the kicker (Pattern 6), land the climax on the broken pattern, close in Saxon. Still sparingly — the announcer gets loud near the goal, not for the whole game (AN-6). Even here, one device per *line*. |

**Worked example 1 — "the punch" soil → Saxon close**

```
LINE IN: "Our methodology enables organizations to achieve sustainable competitive differentiation."
WHAT IT'S TRYING TO DO: this is the thesis line of the deck — it has to land.

DIAGNOSIS: trying to say "this is the core claim, remember it" — that's the-punch soil, so it grows a
  SAXON CLOSE. (Not chiasmus — nothing is backwards here. Not anaphora — there's no series.)
END-WEIGHT TARGET: "win"

ANTI-STACKING GATE: one device [✓] · payload = the thesis [✓] · chord change = Latinate setup is
  already there ("methodology... competitive differentiation"), just needs a Saxon landing [✓] · hidden [✓]

THREE DRAFTS:
  1. (plain)       "Our method gives companies an edge they can keep."
  2. (tightened)   "The method builds an edge that lasts."
  3. (knockout)    "Strategy decides who plays. The method decides who wins."   ← recommended

THE ONE-SENTENCE REASON: this is the deck's thesis and it was dying in Latinate fog, so I kept the
  conceptual setup and ended on the one stony Saxon word — "wins" — that the room will carry out the door.
RULE BROKEN ON PURPOSE: none.
```

**Worked example 2 — "backwards" soil → chiasmus (conceptual / imperfect)**

```
LINE IN: "People think you need motivation before you can start doing the work."
WHAT IT'S TRYING TO DO: a scroll-stopping reframe for a LinkedIn hook — they have it backwards.

DIAGNOSIS: trying to say "you have cause and effect reversed" — backwards-thinking soil, so it grows a
  CHIASMUS. (Anytime what you mean is *and vice versa*, write it as a chiasmus.)
END-WEIGHT TARGET: "starts"

ANTI-STACKING GATE: one device [✓] · payload = the hook's whole reframe [✓] · chord change present [✓] ·
  hidden — reads as wisdom, not scaffolding [✓]

THREE DRAFTS:
  1. (direct)        "Motivation doesn't make you start. Starting makes you motivated."
  2. (asymmetrical)  "You don't start because you're motivated. You're motivated because you started."
  3. (knockout)      "Motivation doesn't start the work. The work starts the motivation."   ← recommended

THE ONE-SENTENCE REASON: the hook's only job is to reverse a cause-and-effect the reader holds
  backwards, and a chiasmus loop makes the reversal feel inevitable instead of merely argued.
RULE BROKEN ON PURPOSE: none — it's an imperfect/conceptual chiasmus (Pattern 9), echoing the same two
  ideas rather than two identical words, because the pure ABBA read stilted in this voice.
```

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: Review the output against `genius.md § Ward Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric before delivering. Flag and fix any violation.
- **One device, not a stack** (AN-1). Exactly one device fires in the recommended line. If a second device snuck in, cut to the one that does the most work. This is the workflow's reason to exist — a stacked output is an automatic fail.
- **The device sits on a line with payload** (AN-2). If you could delete the device and the line still did its job fine, it was decoration — cut it. Rhetoric belongs on the thesis, not the filler.
- **Clarity survived the music** (AN-3). Read the recommended draft cold. If a first-time reader has to re-read to parse it, sound beat clarity — rebuild plainly, then add the music back. Lesson one (clear) precedes lesson two (memorable).
- **The Latinate paid off in Saxon** (AN-4). If the line set up a conceptual stretch, it must land on short, hard, picture-able words. Tension with no release is not eloquence.
- **It sounds like this voice, not a costume** (AN-5). No pastiche of Churchill / Lincoln / the Bible. If a reader would think "this person is trying to sound like X," rewrite in the native voice.
- **The repetition isn't relentless** (AN-6). For epistrophe/anaphora: default to three beats, with relief-and-return if longer. No drumbeat that becomes a tic.
- **The one-sentence reason exists and holds.** Every delivery names *why this device here* in one sentence (gate Q2/Q6). If a rule was broken, the reason is stated (Pattern 11). No reason = revert to plain.
- **End-weight is correct** (Pattern 8). The recommended line's last word is the one the reader carries away; moving any other word to the end would weaken it.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Stacking devices to "make it stronger."** The instinct that built the legacy manifesto — chiasmus *and* Two Languages *and* anaphora *and* epistrophe at one target — reads as machinery announcing itself, the exact opposite of "that was well said." **Recovery**: run Step 2's Q5 honestly; if more than one device fires, cut to the single one the *soil* called for and let the others live in other sentences or other workflows.
- **Reaching for a device before diagnosing the soil.** Deciding "this needs a chiasmus" and then bending the line into ABBA when the line was never about a reversal. **Recovery**: go back to Step 1 and finish the sentence "this line is trying to say ___, which is [soil]." If the soil isn't backwards-thinking, chiasmus was the wrong tool no matter how clever the loop.
- **Decorating a trivial line.** Wrapping a beautiful structure around a sentence that carries no thesis — which trains the reader to ignore your emphasis (AN-2). **Recovery**: apply gate Q1. No payload → no device. Hand the line back plain and find the line that actually matters.
- **Costume rhetoric.** The drafts come out sounding like a Churchill impression or King James pastiche because the model leaned on famous cadences instead of the native voice (AN-5). **Recovery**: rewrite each draft in the writer's own register; the device should make *their* voice more eloquent, never replace it. Influence, not imitation.
