# Ward Tagline — Brand Lines With the Hue of Wisdom (Ward Farnsworth)

A great tagline does not *sound written*. It sounds remembered — as if it had always been true and someone finally said it out loud. Farnsworth names the mechanism plainly: an idea "beautifully compressed in a nice little jingle" takes on **the hue of wisdom**, and a sentence that maps to the math of the universe makes the brain assume the *claim* inside it is an undeniable truth too. That is the whole job here. You are not decorating a slogan; you are building the closed loop, the chord change, or the three-beat that lends a line its inevitability — and then you are burying the device so deep that the reader feels the wisdom and never sees the scaffold. This workflow does that on purpose, in three structural lanes, scored for the illusion and stripped the instant the machinery shows.

> **Cross-domain (Tier 3).** Ward supplies the *structural eloquence*; he does **not** supply positioning. Run this UNDER `/oren-brand` or `/name-framework` (and validate names/connotation through them). If you have no positioning yet — no enemy, no one true claim, no differentiated promise — stop and route there first. A perfectly balanced chiasmus wrapped around the wrong claim is just a beautifully phrased lie.

## Pre-Flight

Read before executing — load these `genius.md` sections verbatim (do not paraphrase from memory; the patterns are precise and the failure modes are subtle):

- **How to Use This Skill (Opus Calibration)** — restraint over saturation, one device per line, influence-not-imitation. A tagline is the *shortest* high-stakes line you will ever write, so the machinery shows fastest of all.
- **Hidden Knowledge — The "Wisdom" Illusion** — the engine of this entire workflow: balanced structure grants "the hue of wisdom," circumventing critical defense. This is *why* a brand line built this way feels inevitable rather than written.
- **Pattern 3 — The Chiasmus Reversal (ABBA)** — Lane A: the closed loop that admits no objection.
- **Pattern 7 — The Rule of Three** — Lane B: the tricolon that lands as "rhetorically perfect" — complete, balanced, nothing to add or cut.
- **Pattern 1 — The Saxon Punch (Chord Change)** — Lane C: end on the stony, picture-able word so the line lands like a struck bell.
- **Pattern 8 — Start at the End (End-Weight Engineering)** — the meta-principle under every lane: decide the last word the reader carries away, build backwards to it.
- **Pattern 9 — The Conceptual (Imperfect) Pattern** — the escape hatch when a literal ABBA reads archaic; invert *ideas*, not identical words.
- **Cross-Domain Applications** — the *Taglines / brand lines* row specifically: chiasmus · Saxon close · rule of three · the wisdom illusion.
- **Ward Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)** — load all six; **AN-1 (stacking)**, **AN-2 (rhetoric on a trivial line)**, and **AN-5 (imitation, costume rhetoric)** are the three that kill taglines.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` before executing. Four questions are load-bearing for a brand line: **Q1** — does this line carry the *one true claim* (the positioning), or is it ornament? A device on a generic claim trains the market to ignore you (AN-2). **Q2** — what soil is this, and therefore which lane? (Backwards belief → Lane A chiasmus · a three-part promise / values run → Lane B rule of three · a single hard payoff → Lane C Saxon close.) If you can't name *why this lane here*, you're decorating. **Q5** — is more than one device firing? At tagline length, two devices is almost always the machinery showing — cut to one. **Q8** — does this sound like *this brand* or like a Churchill/Bible costume? A tagline that wears borrowed eloquence (AN-5) reads as a brand trying to sound profound, which is the opposite of profound. **If positioning is unconfirmed, do not write a single line — return to `/oren-brand` or `/name-framework`.**

## Input Required

- **The one true claim** — the single thing this brand is, promises, or stands against, in *plain words*. One honest sentence. (From `/oren-brand` or `/name-framework`; if you don't have it, you are not ready — see the Gate.) This is the payload every line must carry.
- **The enemy / the backwards belief** (if any) — the thing the market has wrong that the brand corrects. If this exists, Lane A (chiasmus) is on the table.
- **Brand / product name** — so the line can be tested *beside* the name (a tagline lives glued to a logotype; it must scan with it, not fight it).
- **Voice + register** — luxury / challenger / warm / clinical / irreverent. Drives word choice and how much "hue of wisdom" the brand can carry before it tips into costume (AN-5).
- **Where it lives** — logo lockup, hero headline, ad sign-off, manifesto first line, packaging. (A line glued under a wordmark gets *less* runway than a manifesto opener.)
- **Felt verdict target** (optional) — the single feeling the line should leave: trust, defiance, calm, hunger, inevitability. Helps pick the lane and the last word.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Confirm the payload, then read the soil (which lane grows here)

A tagline is the line where AN-2 does the most damage: spend the architecture on a generic claim and you have trained the market to skim your most important sentence. So first, write the **one true claim** in plain words and confirm it is *the* claim, not a feature. Then diagnose the rhetorical soil — the soil decides the lane, not your preference. Most claims grow in exactly one.

| Soil (what you are really saying) | The tell | Lane | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The market has it backwards** ("they think X drives Y; really Y drives X") | What you mean is *and vice versa* | **A — Chiasmus** | The closed ABBA loop *feels* wise; arguing with it "feels like arguing against gravity." The reversal IS the positioning. |
| **A three-part promise, value-run, or escalation** | The claim naturally wants three beats — three things you do, three things you stand for, a small→big build | **B — Rule of Three** | Three is "rhetorically perfect" — complete, balanced. Two feels incomplete; four drags and announces itself. |
| **One hard, single payoff** ("this is the thing, full stop") | The claim is a single transformation that wants to *land*, not loop | **C — Saxon Close** | End on the short, stony, picture-able word and it rings like a struck bell after the eye leaves. The chord change lends inevitability. |

> Write the one sentence that names the soil: **"What this brand really means is _____."** If that sentence is a *vice-versa* → Lane A. If it has *three* honest parts → Lane B. If it's *one hard truth* → Lane C. If it fits none — if the claim is mushy or multi-part-but-not-three — the problem is the positioning, not the line. **Stop and re-route to `/oren-brand`.**

You may carry **one** claim into **at most two** lanes if it genuinely fits two soils (rare). Never force a claim into a lane it doesn't grow in — a forced chiasmus on a non-reversal claim is the single most common costume-rhetoric tell (AN-5).

### Step 2 — Decide the last word FIRST (Pattern 8, every lane)

Before drafting, choose the word the reader carries away. The end of the line is the most powerful real estate in English, and a tagline is *all* end. Strip the one true claim to the single hardest, most picture-able word that carries its truth, and audit it.

| The final word must be… | Why it matters double for a tagline | Pass / fail test |
|---|---|---|
| **One syllable** (or close) | A logo-line has no runway; the ear needs a *stop*, not a trail-off | Say it aloud — does it land like a door, or drift? |
| **Saxon / hard-consonant** | Stony words feel inevitable; Latinate words feel *marketed* (AN-4) | *Build* not *construct*; *win* not *prevail*; *stay* not *remain* |
| **Concrete / picture-able** | The market should *see* it, not decode it | Can you point at it or feel it in the body? |
| **The true end of the claim** | End-weight wasted on filler is the whole line wasted | Move any other word to the end — the line MUST get weaker |

```
ONE TRUE CLAIM (plain): __________
LANE (A/B/C, from the soil): __________
FINAL WORD (decided first): __________   ·   Felt verdict it should leave: __________
```

### Step 3 — Build the line in its lane

Build only the lane the soil chose. The mechanics differ; the goal is identical — the **hue of wisdom**: a line so structurally balanced the market assumes the claim inside it is true.

**Lane A — Chiasmus (the closed loop).** Name the two poles the market has tangled (A and B), state the relationship the wrong way (the forward frame, stated *fairly* — a strawman makes the reversal feel like a trick), then cross them so the second half mirrors the first in reverse.

| | Pole A | Pole B |
|---|---|---|
| **Their word for it** | (e.g. *price*) | (e.g. *quality*) |
| **What they believe** | A dictates B | — |
| **What's true** | — | B dictates A |

> Form: "We don't [A → B the wrong way]; we [B → A the true way]." Keep the grammar parallel so the ear hears the crossover *before* the mind parses the logic. If literal ABBA reads archaic in this voice, invert the *ideas* not identical words (Pattern 9) — the loop still rings.

**Lane B — Rule of Three (the tricolon).** Find the three honest beats — three verbs, three nouns, three promises — and run them in parallel grammar. Default to *exactly* three: two feels incomplete, four drags and starts to announce itself (AN-6). Put the **strongest, shortest, Saxon-est beat last** (Pattern 8) — the third beat is where the wisdom-illusion seals.

| Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 (the landing — shortest / hardest) |
|---|---|---|
| (parallel form) | (parallel form) | (the Saxon close of the trio) |

> Form: "[Beat], [Beat], [Beat]." Same part of speech, ascending weight, the last beat the one the reader keeps. If the third beat is the weakest, reorder — the tricolon lands on its last foot.

**Lane C — Saxon Close (the chord change).** Run a short conceptual / Latinate setup so the ear settles, then drop onto the stony Step-2 word. At tagline length the "setup" may be a single fancy word or phrase — but there must be a register *change*, or there's no chord to change. Say the claim twice (Two Languages, Pattern 4): once conceptual, once stripped to the gut. Keep the second; end on the final word.

> Form: "[one conceptual word/phrase] … [short Saxon payoff ending on the final word]." The Latinate exists *only* to make the Saxon land (AN-4 guard). If there's no setup to drop *from*, the line is just short — that's fine, but it isn't this lane.

### Step 4 — Score the wisdom-illusion (the move that separates a line from a slogan)

This is the step that makes the workflow Farnsworth and not a slogan generator. For each draft, score the **wisdom-illusion effect** — does the *structure* make the claim feel inevitable, or is it a claim wearing a costume? Score 1–5 on each axis; a brand line wants **≥4 on Balance and ≥4 on Inevitability**, with the device *invisible*.

| Axis | What you're testing | 1 (fail) | 5 (savant) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Balance** | Does the structure feel mathematically closed — ABBA loop, three-beat symmetry, or clean chord change? | Lopsided, the ear wants more or less | Perfectly closed; nothing to add or cut |
| **Inevitability** | Does it read as *remembered*, not written — as if it were always true? | Sounds composed, you can hear the writer | Feels discovered, like a proverb |
| **Invisibility** | Would Farnsworth *notice the technique*, or just say "that was well said"? | The device announces itself | The wisdom is felt; the scaffold is gone |
| **Truth-carry** | Does the line carry the *one true claim* (Step 1), or did the structure smuggle in a prettier-but-false idea? | Balanced but no longer true | The true claim, made inevitable |

> **Truth-carry is a veto.** The wisdom illusion is "the sword and shield of rhetoric" precisely *because* balanced structure makes a claim feel true whether or not it is. If a draft scores high on Balance and Inevitability but the structure bent the claim into something the brand can't honestly stand behind, kill it — that is the failure mode the illusion exists to seduce you into. Never let the loop choose the claim.

### Step 5 — Produce 3 candidates, strip every visible device, test beside the name

Deliver exactly three candidate lines. If the soil is single-lane, all three are variations within that lane (different poles, different third beat, different setup length). If the claim genuinely fits two lanes, you may show two lanes — but never stack two devices *in one line* (AN-1); at tagline length a chiasmus that's also a Saxon punch is the machinery showing in a heartbeat.

For each candidate, run the final strip:
- **Read it cold, aloud, beside the brand name.** A tagline never lives alone; it lives glued to a wordmark. Does it scan *with* the name or fight it?
- **The invisibility cut (AN-1 / AN-6):** if you can feel the device, it failed. One device, buried. No anaphora running four beats, no chiasmus + epistrophe in one breath.
- **The costume cut (AN-5):** would the market think "this brand is *trying* to sound profound"? Borrowed eloquence reads as insecurity. Rewrite in the brand's native register until the line sounds like *this brand* finally said the obvious.
- **The triviality cut (AN-2):** if you stripped the structure and the line still did its job fine, the device was decoration — and a decorated generic claim is worse than a plain one.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Tagline / logo lockup** | The *shortest* runway there is. Usually Lane A (loop) or Lane C (punch-only). End on the stony word; the hardness lends the hue of wisdom that makes it feel inevitable, not written. Test it literally typeset under the wordmark — it must scan in one breath. |
| **Positioning maxim / brand manifesto first line** | The most runway. A full chiasmus or a real Lane-C chord change earns its length here. This is the line the brand repeats internally and the market quotes back; it can carry the most structure before the machinery shows. |
| **Headline (hero / ad)** | Lane C (Saxon close) or Lane B (rule of three) usually win — end on the short hard concrete word, or land the third beat. One device. The conceptual front can be a single fancy noun-phrase the monosyllable shatters. |
| **Converting copy sign-off / CTA line** | Lane C — run the value conceptually so the logical brain signs off, then close in Saxon so the *do-it* word rings after the eye leaves. The line wants the closed-loop feel so the offer reads as inevitable, not pitched. |
| **Social hook / bio line** | One device, max — at this length the scaffold shows fastest. Lane A reframes ("you have it backwards") that *feel* wise enough to stop the scroll. Land the last word like a punch. |
| **Speech / keynote refrain** | A tagline meant to be *spoken and repeated*. Lane B (rule of three) and Lane A both shine aloud. Build it so a room could chant the third beat or the reversal back to you. Still sparingly — the line is the goal-line moment, not the whole game. |

### Worked example A — Lane A · Chiasmus (challenger studio, claim = "price should follow the work, not the reverse")

> **One true claim (plain):** we set the price from the standard of the work, not the work from the budget. · **Soil:** the market has it backwards (*and vice versa*) → Lane A. · **Final word:** *work*.
>
> **Poles:** A = *budget* · B = *work*. Forward frame (fair): "Your budget decides the work." Reversal: cross them.
>
> **Before (generic slogan):** "Premium craftsmanship at every price point." — rhetorically inert; it states a feature, loops nothing, and the market skims it.
> **After (chiasmus):** **"The work doesn't fit the budget. The budget fits the work."**
> *Why it lands:* the ABBA loop closes — *work / budget / budget / work* — so the claim feels like gravity, not a sales position. *Work* is the stony word left ringing. Balance 5 · Inevitability 5 · Invisibility 4 · Truth-carry 5. One device, no costume.

### Worked example B — Lane C · Saxon Close (recovery / sleep brand, claim = "rest is the foundation, not a luxury")

> **One true claim (plain):** rest isn't indulgence; it's the thing everything else is built on. · **Soil:** one hard payoff → Lane C. · **Final word:** *built*.
>
> **Two Languages:** *the cornerstone of sustained high performance* → **everything is built on rest**.
>
> **Before (Latinate, no chord change — AN-4):** "Optimize your recovery to maximize your performance potential." — pomp; never lands on a stony word; reads as *marketed*.
> **After (Saxon close):** **"You don't perform on willpower. You perform on rest."** — or compressed: **"Rest is the work."**
> *Why it lands:* the conceptual lull (*perform / willpower*) drops onto the one-syllable Saxon *rest*; the parallel makes it feel proverbial. *Rest* arrives last and hard. Balance 4 · Inevitability 5 · Invisibility 5 · Truth-carry 5.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review every candidate against `genius.md § Ward Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (rows: **Wisdom Resonance (Structure)**, **Impact Velocity (Saxon Punch)**, **Economy of Impact**). Flag and fix before delivering.

- **Carries the one true claim, not a feature (AN-2, Q1):** the line is built on the actual positioning. If you stripped the structure and a generic line remained that "did the job fine," the device was decoration on a claim that didn't deserve it — go back to Step 1 or back to `/oren-brand`.
- **Right lane for the soil (Q2):** chiasmus only where the claim is a *vice-versa* reversal; rule of three only where there are three *honest* beats; Saxon close only where there's a register to *change*. A forced lane is the loudest costume tell.
- **Wisdom-illusion scored, not assumed (Step 4):** every candidate carries Balance / Inevitability / Invisibility / Truth-carry scores. A line that scores high on Balance but low on Truth-carry is a beautiful lie — vetoed, no exceptions.
- **End-weight earned (Pattern 8):** the final word was decided *first* and the line built backwards to it. Move any other word to the end — the line must get weaker.
- **One device, invisible (AN-1, Invisibility ≥4):** exactly one device fires per line; the reader feels wisdom, not scaffold. If a reader would *notice the technique*, it failed — they should only think "that's just true."
- **Native voice, not costume (AN-5, Q8):** the line sounds like *this brand* finally saying the obvious, never like the brand cosplaying Churchill or scripture. Borrowed eloquence reads as insecurity.
- **Clarity before music (AN-3):** read cold beside the name, a first-time reader parses it instantly. A line that sounds wise but needs a re-read has failed lesson one and cannot be rescued by lesson two.
- **Three is three (AN-6):** any rule-of-three line runs *exactly* three beats — not two (incomplete), not four (drags and announces itself).

## Common Pitfalls

- **Letting the loop choose the claim (the wisdom-illusion's own trap).** The structure makes *any* claim feel true, so it's seductively easy to keep the prettier chiasmus even after it has quietly bent the positioning into something the brand can't stand behind. **Recovery:** Truth-carry is a veto. Re-state the one true claim in plain words from Step 1; if the balanced line no longer says it, the line is lying beautifully — rebuild from the real claim, even if the result is less symmetrical.
- **Forcing a claim into a lane it doesn't grow in.** Cramming a single-payoff claim into a chiasmus, or a reversal into a tricolon, produces the unmistakable costume-rhetoric tell — a line that sounds *structured* but hollow (AN-5). **Recovery:** re-read the soil (Step 1). If the claim is genuinely "one hard truth," it's Lane C; stop torturing it into a loop. The right lane is rarely the most ornate one.
- **Stacking devices because it's "just a tagline" (AN-1).** At the shortest length, two devices feels clever and reads as the machinery showing in one breath. **Recovery:** cut to the one device that carries the most weight; for a brand line that is almost always the loop *or* the punch, never both.
- **A balanced line that confuses on first read (AN-3).** Twisting word order for the prettier symmetry until the market has to re-read the line glued under your logo. **Recovery:** lesson one before lesson two — write the claim so it's unmistakable on first read beside the name, *then* layer the structure back. A tagline that needs decoding has already lost the only second of attention it gets.
- **Profundity costume (AN-5).** Reaching for gravitas the brand hasn't earned, so the line reads as "trying to sound wise" — which is the opposite of wise. **Recovery:** rewrite in the brand's actual register until the line sounds like the obvious thing this specific brand would say. Influence, never imitation; the goal is "that's just true," not "how eloquent."
