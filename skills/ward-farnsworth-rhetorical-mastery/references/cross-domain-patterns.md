# Cross-Domain Patterns — How Each Device Transfers

> The devices are domain-agnostic. They operate on the ear, not the subject — so the
> same eleven patterns that built the Gettysburg Address build a LinkedIn hook and a
> CTA button. This file is the *transfer map*: for each device, where it wants to live
> outside speeches and scripture, and a concrete **before → after** in that domain.
>
> **Read `genius.md` first.** Everything here obeys the same law: **one device per
> line** (AN-1), reserved for the line that **carries the payload** (AN-2), built
> backwards from the **last word** (Pattern 8), in **this** voice not a costume (AN-5).
> The shorter the format, the faster the machinery shows — so social/headline/tagline
> are the strictest one-device zones; converting copy and speeches may run *different*
> devices on *different* lines (never two in one line).

---

## The transfer law (read before the per-device maps)

A device does not change when the domain changes. What changes is **how much runway
you get** and **how visible the machinery becomes**:

| Domain | Runway | Machinery shows... | Hard rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social hook | One line | Fastest — a flourish is a third of the line | One device, opener or last-line only |
| Headline | A few words | Almost pure end-weight | One device; the device *is* the headline |
| Tagline / brand line | A phrase | A tagline that announces its device is dead on arrival | Shortest leash; usually chiasmus or Saxon close |
| Converting copy / VSL | A page | Slower — lines breathe | One device per *line*; different lines, different devices is fine |
| Email | A scroll | Medium | One controlling device per email; the P.S. and the CTA each earn their own |
| Speech / manifesto | Paragraphs | Slowest — the only home for the full toolkit | Build → switch → break → Saxon close; still sparingly |
| Ghostwriting | Whatever the client uses | Shows the instant it sounds like *you*, not them | Device must read as *theirs*; match their cadence, not your showpieces |

The "before" in every example below is a clear, correct, **rhetorically inert** line.
The "after" installs exactly one device and lands the end-weight. If you could delete
the device and the line still did its job, the device was decoration — cut it.

## 1. The Saxon Punch (Latinate setup → stony, one-syllable finish)

The chord change: lull the ear with conceptual words, then drop a short hard Saxon
word it can't ignore. Decide the last word first; build the sentence backwards to it.
**Workflow:** `/ward-saxon-punch`.

| Domain | Before (inert) | After (Saxon close) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** | "Consistency is ultimately what differentiates successful creators." | "Talent gets you noticed. Showing up gets you paid." |
| **Headline** | "How to Acquire More Qualified Customers Efficiently" | "Stop chasing leads. Make them come." |
| **Converting copy / VSL CTA** | "Click below to begin your transformation journey today." | "You've read enough. Now do the work." |
| **Tagline / brand line** | "Innovative solutions for modern operational challenges." | "Less noise. More built." |
| **Speech / manifesto** | "We must persevere through these difficult circumstances." | "They built the wall to last forever. We climbed it in a day." |
| **Ghostwriting** (founder voice) | "Our methodology enables sustainable competitive differentiation." | "Strategy decides who plays. The method decides who wins." |
| **Email** (sign-off line) | "I look forward to helping you achieve your goals." | "You know what to do. Hit reply." |

**Why it transfers:** the ear detects difference, not volume. Every domain has a
conceptual register the reader is numb to; the Saxon word is the one that wakes them.
The shorter the format, the shorter the Latinate runway — a hook gets one fancy word;
an essay line gets a full conceptual sentence. **Guard (AN-4):** the Latinate must
*resolve* into the stony word. A fancy run that never drops is pomp, not a punch.

---

## 2. Epistrophe (repeat the same word at the *end* of 2–3 clauses)

The anchor that must linger. Pick the one word the piece can't survive without, then
end 2–3 consecutive clauses on it. Default to three (Pattern 7).
**Workflow:** `/ward-parallelism` (epistrophe mode).

| Domain | Before (inert) | After (epistrophe) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** | "Most people blame the market, their boss, or bad luck." | "The market didn't fail you. Your boss didn't fail you. You stopped showing up — and that's on you." |
| **Headline** | "A System Built Around Your Actual Needs" | "Your pace. Your rules. Your win." |
| **Converting copy / VSL** | "There are many reasons people don't lose the weight, but it usually comes down to one." | "It's not the diet. It's not the gym. It's not the genes. It's the plan — and you've never had a plan." |
| **Tagline / brand line** | "We build for the people who do the real work." | "By the maker, for the maker, with the maker." |
| **Speech / manifesto** | "This effort belongs to all of us collectively." | "We started this. We funded this. We finished this." |
| **Ghostwriting** | "Ownership matters at every level of the company." | "The intern owns it. The lead owns it. I own it." |
| **Email** (the belief you must plant before the ask) | "You really need to commit to this if you want results." | "No one is coming to fix this for you. No one is coming to do it for you. No one is coming." |

**Why it transfers:** epistrophe is the workhorse of converting copy because it
hammers the *single belief that must survive the close*. In social it's the tricolon
that makes a claim feel inevitable. **Guard (AN-6):** three beats is the ceiling
unless you switch positions; relentless end-repeat becomes a tic.

## 3. Chiasmus (ABBA reversal — "you have it backwards")

The wisdom-illusion loop. Reach for it the instant what you mean is *and vice versa*.
Run the idea the way the audience holds it, then cross it. Use it **once**; hide the
hinge. **Workflow:** `/ward-chiasmus`.

| Domain | Before (inert) | After (chiasmus) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** | "You should start before you feel ready, not wait for motivation." | "Motivation doesn't start the work. The work starts the motivation." |
| **Headline** | "Why Working Harder Isn't the Same as Working Better" | "You're not too busy to plan. You're too unplanned to be free." |
| **Converting copy / VSL** (objection handler) | "People worry the program costs too much for what it offers." | "Don't ask what the program costs. Ask what staying stuck costs." |
| **Tagline / brand line** | "Design that puts function first." | "We don't design for the eye. We design what the eye missed." |
| **Speech / manifesto** | "We have a duty to give back to our community." | "Ask not what the work can do for you. Ask what you can do for the work." |
| **Ghostwriting** | "Hire people smarter than you and trust them." | "Hire people you'd work *for*, then work for them." |
| **Email** (reframe before the pitch) | "Stop treating content as a cost; it's an investment." | "You don't post because you've got an audience. You've got an audience because you post." |

**Why it transfers:** balanced structure grants the *hue of wisdom* — arguing against
it "feels like arguing against gravity." That makes it the king of objection-handling
in copy and the king of the scroll-stop reframe in social. **Guard (AN-3 / Pattern 9):**
if a pure ABBA reads stilted in the voice, use the *conceptual* chiasmus — invert the
ideas, echo the same referent in different words. Ring structurally, not literally.

## 4. The "Two Languages" Translation (say it Latinate, restate it Saxon)

Validate the head and the gut in one breath: state the idea conceptually so the
logical brain signs off, then restate it in visceral street-level words so the gut
buys. The Saxon restatement lands *second* and *last*.
**Workflows:** `/ward-saxon-punch` (mines the punch from it), `/ward-metaphor-argument`
(when the Saxon half is a *picture*), `visceral-persuasion-bridge`.

| Domain | Before (one register only) | After (Two Languages) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** | "Compounding makes early action disproportionately valuable." | "Small habits stack into outcomes you can't predict. Start ugly today; thank yourself in a year." |
| **Headline** | "Optimize Your Operational Workflow for Throughput" | "Stop optimizing the system. The system isn't the work. Do the work." |
| **Converting copy / VSL** | "Our framework reduces customer acquisition friction." | "It shortens the path from stranger to sale. Fewer hoops. They click, they buy." |
| **Tagline / brand line** | "Sustainable nutrition, scientifically formulated." | "Real food, engineered. Stuff your body actually knows." |
| **Speech / manifesto** | "I do not anticipate the institution will be dissolved." | "I do not expect the union to be dissolved — I expect it will cease to be divided." |
| **Ghostwriting** | "We prioritize psychological safety in our culture." | "We protect the right to be wrong out loud. You can say the dumb thing here." |
| **Email** | "This offer represents significant long-term value." | "The math works. You make it back in a month and keep it forever." |

**Why it transfers:** every audience has a smart half and a scared half, and they sit
in the same reader. The conceptual line earns the click from status; the Saxon line
closes the gut. **Guard:** the gut version must be *genuinely different words*, not
the same sentence with a synonym — and it lands last.

## 5. Anaphora (repeat the same word at the *start* of consecutive units)

The announcer effect: a repeated front-phrase primes the ear like a sports announcer
getting louder near the goal. The climax lands on the line that **breaks** the
pattern. Default to three beats. **Workflow:** `/ward-parallelism` (anaphora mode).

| Domain | Before (flat) | After (anaphora, climax on the break) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** | "I tried everything before this finally worked." | "I tried the courses. I tried the coaches. I tried the 5am routine. None of it worked until I tried telling the truth." |
| **Headline** | (rarely fits — usually too short; use a two-beat isocolon) | "More reps. More reach. More revenue." |
| **Converting copy / VSL** (build into the CTA) | "This works for all kinds of people in all situations." | "This is for the founder who's stuck. This is for the writer who's blocked. This is for the operator who's drowning. This is for you — start today." |
| **Tagline / brand line** | (use rule-of-three isocolon) | "Of the maker, by the maker, for the maker." |
| **Speech / manifesto** | "We will keep resisting no matter what comes." | "We shall fight in the courts. We shall fight in the streets. We shall fight in the press. We shall never quit." |
| **Ghostwriting** | "We stayed committed through every hard season." | "We stayed when the money ran out. We stayed when the press turned. We stayed when it stopped being fun. We stayed." |
| **Email** | "Here's everything you'll get inside." | "You'll get the templates. You'll get the scripts. You'll get the swipe file. And then you'll get out of your own way." |

**Why it transfers:** anaphora is **setup, not finish** — it builds the crescendo and
the *broken* line is the goal. That makes it ideal for the run *into* a CTA in copy
and the run *into* the payoff in a hook. **Guard (AN-6):** three openings, then break.
Sustained anaphora past three with no break feels like a tic — "lay off."

## 6. The Switch (anaphora ↔ epistrophe — relief and return)

Run the repetition at the start for a stretch, move it to the end (or vice versa),
then snap back. The ear gets the pattern, a release, and a satisfying return — which
is why people remember the line decades later. **Workflow:** `/ward-parallelism`
(the switch is the advanced mode; native to long form).

| Domain | Before (flat or relentless) | After (the switch) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** (rarely — needs room) | "You're not him. You'll never be him." | "I watched him win. I watched him quit. Winning was a friend of mine. You're no winner." |
| **Converting copy / VSL** (the kicker) | "Other programs overpromise and underdeliver every time." | "They promised fast. They promised easy. They promised free. Fast was a lie. Easy was a lie. And free? Free cost you a year." |
| **Speech / manifesto** (the takedown) | "Senator, you are not the equal of the man you invoke." | "I served with Jack Kennedy. I knew Jack Kennedy. Jack Kennedy was a friend of mine. Senator, you're no Jack Kennedy." |
| **Ghostwriting** (the memorable line) | "We gave it everything until the very end." | "Giving all, daring all, enduring all — to the utmost, to the end." |

**Why it transfers:** the switch is what keeps repetition from becoming a gimmick. It
lives almost entirely in long form (speeches, manifesto closers, the rhetorically
perfect kicker), because it needs room to set the pattern, release it, and return.
Don't attempt it at hook length — the machinery shows before the return can satisfy.

## 7. The Rule of Three (three beats — "rhetorically perfect")

The default count for any parallel run: two feels incomplete, four+ drags. Three is
the most pleasing rhythm. **Workflow:** applies inside `/ward-parallelism`,
`/ward-antithesis`, and `/ward-aphorism-forge` (the tricolon close).

| Domain | Before (wrong count) | After (rule of three) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** | "It's about consistency and patience." (two — incomplete) | "Show up. Shut up. Ship." |
| **Headline** | "Faster, Cheaper, Better, Safer, and More Scalable Workflows" (five — drags) | "Faster. Cheaper. Yours." |
| **Converting copy** | "It saves you time and money." | "Less time. Less cost. Less guessing." |
| **Tagline / brand line** | "Government that serves and represents everyone always, forever" | "Of the people, by the people, for the people." |
| **Speech / manifesto** | "Giving all and daring all and enduring all and risking all" (four — drags) | "Giving all, daring all, enduring all." |
| **Ghostwriting** | "We value honesty above most things" | "Tell the truth. Do the work. Keep the promise." |

**Why it transfers:** three is structural, not stylistic — it lands as *complete*,
nothing to add or cut. Any parallel series in any domain defaults to three unless
there's a reason to break it. **Guard:** if you have four good beats, the fourth is
usually the weakest — cut it, or you're padding the rhythm.

## 8. Start at the End / End-Weight (decide the last word, build backwards)

The meta-principle under every device. The end of a sentence is the most powerful
real estate in English. Decide the final word the reader carries away *first*, then
construct the line backwards to it. **Workflows:** `/ward-aphorism-forge`,
`/ward-cadence-audit` (Step 3 audits end-weight), and implicitly every other workflow.

| Domain | Before (strong word buried) | After (end-weight engineered) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** | "Failure, in the end, is really just data you can use." | "Failure isn't the opposite of success. It's the receipt." |
| **Headline** | "The Truth About Why Most Startups Eventually Fail" | "Most startups don't die. They quit." |
| **Converting copy / CTA** | "Sign up now to lock in your spot before it's gone." | "The door closes Friday. Walk through it." |
| **Tagline / brand line** | "We protect the freedom of even the speech we find objectionable." | "Freedom for the thought that we hate." |
| **Speech / manifesto** | "...so few people have ever been owed so much by so many." | "...so much owed by so many to so few." |
| **Ghostwriting** | "We always do the right thing even when it's hard for us." | "We do the hard thing. We do it anyway." |
| **Email** (subject line) | "An Update About Your Account That You Should Read" | "Your account closes Friday." |

**Why it transfers:** end-weight is format-blind — a subject line, a button, a thesis,
a manifesto closer all live or die on the last word. **The test:** moving any other
word to the end must *weaken* the line. If it doesn't, the end-weight is wrong.

## 9. Antithesis (set one idea hard against its opposite in parallel slots)

Not chiasmus (which *inverts* the same terms). Antithesis *juxtaposes* opposites in
matched slots — light/dark, much/few, was/never — so the gap between them rings.
Anything great is greater set off against what it isn't.
**Workflow:** `/ward-antithesis`.

| Domain | Before (one pole only) | After (antithesis) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** | "Everyone was busy optimizing the funnel." | "Everyone optimized the funnel. No one fixed the offer." |
| **Headline** | "Why Large Marketing Budgets Don't Guarantee Returns" | "Big budgets, small returns." |
| **Converting copy / VSL** (before/after) | "Imagine how much better your life will be after this." | "Today: guessing, stalling, hoping. Ninety days from now: a system that runs without you." |
| **Converting copy** (objection) | "Waiting to buy actually costs you more than buying now." | "It's not the cost of buying. It's the cost of waiting." |
| **Tagline / brand line** | "Premium quality at an accessible price point." | "Built like a tank. Priced like a tool." |
| **Speech / manifesto** | "So many people owe so much to such a small group." | "Never... was so much owed by so many to so few." |
| **Email** | "Most newsletters waste your time with fluff." | "Other newsletters fill your inbox. This one empties your to-do list." |

**Why it transfers:** the contrast does the persuading — the reader feels the truth
*because* they felt the opposite first. The before/after structure of every VSL is
antithesis at the architecture level; the pattern-interrupt of every great hook is
antithesis at the line level. **Guard:** keep both poles short and concrete, vary
only the hinge word, and end on the pole that matters (usually the Saxon one).

## 10. Aphorism / Compression (the one quotable line that survives the talk)

Compress a worthy claim buried in qualifiers into the line people repeat. Decide the
last word first, then dismantle everything that doesn't earn its place before it. Then
the honest move most "punch it up" tools skip: label whether the line is **TRUE** or
merely **beautifully said** (a STANCE dressed as truth). The balance lends *any* line
the hue of wisdom — that's the sword *and* the shield.
**Workflow:** `/ward-aphorism-forge`.

| Domain | Before (drowning in qualifiers) | After (aphorism + shield note) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook / closer** | "In most cases, it seems that the people who tend to win are usually just the ones who didn't quit when it got hard." | "Winners aren't the strongest. They're the last to quit." *(TRUE-ish — survivorship; flag the STANCE.)* |
| **Headline** | "Here Are Some Reasons Why Perfectionism Can Sometimes Hold People Back" | "Perfect is how good things die." *(STANCE dressed as TRUTH — ships well, flag it.)* |
| **Converting copy** (pull-quote) | "It's worth considering that the real risk might actually be in not taking action at all." | "The risk isn't moving. It's standing still." *(STANCE — the line that converts and gets quoted back at you.)* |
| **Tagline / brand line** | "We believe in doing fewer things but doing them really well." | "Make less. Make it last." *(STANCE by definition — a promise, not a proven fact.)* |
| **Essay line / maxim** | "Generally speaking, it tends to be true that we remember experiences more than we remember objects." | "We keep the trips. We forget the things." *(LIKELY — verify before shipping.)* |
| **Speech closer** | "I think the most important thing is to never give up on what you believe in." | "Beliefs are cheap. Standing for one when it costs you isn't." *(STANCE — the single loud line.)* |

**Why it transfers:** every domain has a "make it quotable" moment, and the danger is
identical everywhere — the *form* makes a false or unprovable claim feel inevitable.
The forge ships the line **and** the honest verdict, so the writer ships it knowingly.
**Guard:** an essay maxim must be TRUE/LIKELY (a reader who finds the underlined line
was merely well-said distrusts the whole essay). A tagline reading STANCE is correct,
not a failure — a promise isn't a proven fact.

## 11. Metaphor-as-Argument (the abstract claim restated as one concrete picture)

The Saxon/visceral half of a Two-Languages pair, taken all the way to an image. State
the mechanism conceptually so the head signs off; restate it as a picture so the gut
closes the case before the head reopens it. One controlling image — don't develop the
conceit over a paragraph. **Workflow:** `/ward-metaphor-argument`.

| Domain | Before (intellectually clear, emotionally inert) | After (one picturable image) |
|---|---|---|
| **Social hook** | "Unaddressed technical debt compounds and slows future delivery." | "You're not behind. You're mopping a floor while the tap's still running." |
| **Headline** | "The Union Was in a State of Internal Contradiction" | "A house divided against itself cannot stand." |
| **Converting copy / VSL** (the problem) | "Your funnel is leaking conversions at multiple stages." | "Your funnel is a bucket with three holes. We're not adding water. We're plugging holes." |
| **Tagline / brand line** | "We surface the insights hidden in your data." | "Your data's a haystack. We hand you the needle." |
| **Speech / manifesto** | "America has not honored its financial obligation to its citizens." | "...a promissory note... marked 'insufficient funds.'" |
| **Ghostwriting** | "Trust, once broken, is very difficult to rebuild." | "Trust is a mirror. Even glued, you see the crack." |
| **Email** | "Skipping the fundamentals will undermine your long-term progress." | "You're building a skyscraper on sand and wondering why it leans." |

**Why it transfers:** a true metaphor *is* the argument — the reader metabolizes it
instantly, the way they metabolize a Saxon word. It carries the *problem* in copy (so
the offer can be the fix) and the controlling *thesis* in a speech (so the room sees
it before they can dispute it). **Guard:** rank candidates by **persuasive load**, not
prettiness. If the image could sit on any brand or claim, it carries nothing — cut it.

**When in doubt about which device a line wants, don't guess — run
`/ward-rhetorical-engine`.** It reads the rhetorical soil, names what the line is
trying to say, and routes to the one device that grows there.
