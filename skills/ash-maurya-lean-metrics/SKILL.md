# Ash Maurya — Lean Metrics & Business Model Validation

Ash Maurya (author of *Running Lean*, creator of Lean Canvas, founder of LeanStack) treats the business model — not the product — as the true product of a startup. His system replaces build-first instinct with a validation engine: find the one traction metric that leads revenue, segment customers by switching triggers instead of personas, sell the demo before building the product, and pick one compounding unfair advantage instead of a feature arms race.

Load this skill for: startup/product validation, traction metrics and vanity-metric audits, ICP/segmentation grounded in causality, MVP scoping, moat strategy, vision statements, and elevator pitches for new ventures.

## Quick Reference

- **Traction defined**: the *rate* at which a business model captures monetizable value from its users. Rate, not cumulative. Leading, not lagging. Revenue and profit are after-effects.
- **One metric per archetype**: Direct model → key activity that drives purchase (Starbucks: time in store). Multi-sided → DAU + ARPU (Facebook reports only these two). Marketplace → transactions (Airbnb: guest nights booked).
- **Personas fail early-stage**: chase causality, not correlation. Good ICP = fewest distinguishing characteristics: switching trigger + desired outcome + chosen solution. Three trigger types: bad experience, change in circumstance, awareness event. Patterns emerge in 10–20 interviews.
- **MVP = Minimum VALUABLE Product** (Kano): one delighter as the UVP → minimum performance metric to be taken seriously → optional second axis of better → innovate *around* basic features (license, integrate, Wizard-of-Oz).
- **Demo-sell-build**: if you can't sell the demo, a working product won't help. 90-day timebox; traction (tangible commitments) is the goal that justifies going all-in.
- **Bigger context**: "Don't make a better X — make a better user of X." The customer is the hero; you are the guide; the product is the gift.
- **Vision in 3 steps**: Who is it for → their value-creating action → one North Star metric. Set minimum success criteria at 3 years, then 10x it for the stretch goal.
- **Elevator pitch = movie trailer** (3 acts): ordinary world + job to be done → switching trigger breaks existing alternatives → tease the better way. Never name or describe the product.
- **Unfair advantage**: anything worth copying will be copied. Pick ONE of the 7 powers matched to business model + stage; go all-in so moat-building becomes a side effect of product building.
- **AI-era rule (Vibe Startup)**: AI made building fast, not right. Compress PDCA validation cycles from 6–8 weeks to ~1 week; building is no longer the bottleneck — thinking is.
