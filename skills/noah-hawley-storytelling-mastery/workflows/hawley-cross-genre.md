# Cross-Genre Stack

A well-written scene with no spine is the most common failure in a multi-expert production, and it happens when the line-level talent runs before the architecture exists. This workflow fixes the order at the root: Hawley builds the question, the ending, and the tonal arc first, then the scene/detail/timing experts run *inside* that frame. The output is one stacked production plan — who owns what, and what runs when.

## Input Required
- The production goal and final form (screenplay, TV season, long-form essay/Substack run, brand campaign, short-form post series).
- The source or seed (original idea, IP being adapted, real event, brand to express).
- The format/medium and any hard constraints (length, episode count, channel, budget).
- The craft pressure you most fear getting wrong (flat prose, no reporting depth, no narrative drive, weak detail/timing).
- Which partner experts are available to deploy (Roth, Wright Thompson, Pressfield, Connelly, Segura).

## Workflow

### Step 1: Hawley Sets the Spine (never skip)
Architecture runs first, alone. Before any craft expert touches a line, lock three things — the question the work answers, the ending that gives it meaning, and the tonal arc that carries it. This is the frame every partner will work inside. If you cannot fill all three rows, stop and run `/hawley-theme-engine`, `/hawley-ending-first`, and `/hawley-tonal-arc` before proceeding.

| Spine element | Lock it as | Test |
|---------------|-----------|------|
| Theme-question | A question you genuinely can't answer yet (not a moral) | If you already know the answer, it's a lesson — rewrite it as a question |
| Ending | The fixed destination the whole work points at | Does it deepen the question rather than close it? |
| Tonal arc | The tension→release machine across the whole, and where the gray zone lives | Could a reader laugh and flinch in the same beat? |
| Assumption-as-tool | The medium/format/frame default converted into an instrument | What's being treated as fixed that could carry meaning? |

The spine is the contract. Partner experts may sharpen it; they may not overwrite it.

### Step 2: Diagnose the Craft Need and Route
With the spine locked, name where the production will live or die at the line. Match each craft pressure to exactly one partner. Route by pressure, not by prestige — pull in only the partners the work actually needs.

| Craft pressure | Route to | What they own inside the frame |
|----------------|----------|--------------------------------|
| Scene-level prose, character "wallets," off-center subtext, the line | **Eric Roth** | Writes the scene Hawley decided should exist |
| Reporting depth, interiority, the structural hammer (long-form non-fiction) | **Wright Thompson** | Excavates the *real* truth — note the myth/reality inversion: Thompson digs up real truth, Hawley can manufacture a "true story" frame; choose deliberately per piece |
| Narrative physics, gravitational drive, all-is-lost → epiphany (manifesto/sales) | **Steven Pressfield** | Gives the spine momentum and an inevitable pull |
| The single telling detail; comic timing on the build and the release (short-form) | **Connelly / Segura** | Sharpens the detail and the timing of Hawley's tension→release |

### Step 3: Sequence — Architecture First, Then Partner Inside the Frame
Set the run order explicitly. Hawley's output (theme + ending + tonal arc) is the brief the partner receives; the partner produces line work *answering to that brief*. Invert this and you get well-crafted scenes with no spine — the exact failure this stack exists to prevent.

```
SEQUENCE
1. HAWLEY  → theme-question, ending, tonal arc, assumption-as-tool   [the frame]
2. PARTNER → [routed expert] works the line inside the frame
            • Roth      → scene/edition prose, wallets, subtext
            • Wright    → reporting, interiority, structural hammer
            • Pressfield→ narrative physics, manifesto/sales drive
            • Connelly  → telling detail
            • Segura    → comic timing on the release
3. HAWLEY  → coherence cut: broom anything the partner added that serves
             craft but not the spine (service creep stays out)
```

For a multi-format mission (e.g. a season plus a campaign plus short-form), Hawley sets one spine, then each format gets its own partner pass inside the *same* frame — one architecture, many craft hands. This is also where a larger mission defers to `/supercomputer` for cross-phase anchor memory.

### Step 4: Integration Check — Spine AND Craft
A stacked production passes only when both layers are present and aligned. Run both checks; failing either sends you back, not forward.

| Check | Pass condition | If it fails |
|-------|---------------|-------------|
| Spine present | The theme-question organizes every choice invisibly; the ending feels inevitable | Return to Step 1 — craft can't rescue a missing frame |
| Craft present | Scenes/details/timing land at the line; nothing is flat or inert | Re-route Step 2 to the right partner; the spine was right, the hand was wrong |
| Spine ⟷ craft aligned | Every partner addition serves *this* story now | Coherence cut — broom the service creep, however well-crafted |

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| Screenplay / TV | Hawley breaks the season by theme and locks the finale; Roth writes the scenes inside it. The biscuit-scene logic governs climaxes — the partner crafts the line, Hawley decides whether the scene refuses its expected beat. |
| Long-form essay / Substack | Hawley sets the question-spine and the closing image; Wright Thompson supplies reporting depth and interiority. Honor the myth/reality choice — real excavation, or a deliberate "true story" frame, never both by accident. |
| Short-form (LinkedIn / X) | Hawley fixes the tension→release shape and the mirror; Connelly hands one telling detail, Segura times the release. Keep it tight — the spine is a single question, the craft is a single landed beat. |
| Brand / campaign | Hawley codifies one voice and one tonal arc across every maker; Pressfield gives the manifesto its physics. Many deliverables, one frame — diverge here and the campaign fractures. |

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review output against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` and the § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric. Flag and fix any violation before delivering.
- The spine ran first and is fully locked (question + ending + tonal arc) before any partner is named — no exceptions.
- Each partner is routed by an actual craft pressure, not added for coverage; unused partners stay out.
- The execution sequence puts architecture before craft everywhere; no partner produces line work before the frame exists.
- The integration check confirms *both* a spine and craft — a beautifully written piece with no organizing question is a FAIL, not a near-miss.
- The plan names no patterns on the page; the architecture stays invisible in any downstream output.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Running the partner first because the prose felt urgent.** You get polished scenes with nothing underneath, and Step 4 catches it too late. Recovery: discard the line work, lock the spine in Step 1, re-brief the partner.
- **Stacking every available expert for safety.** Coverage creep buries the spine under uncoordinated craft. Recovery: route only the partners whose pressure is real; the smallest stack that covers the line-level risk wins.
- **Letting the partner overwrite the spine.** A Pressfield pass that adds drive can quietly bend the ending; a Roth scene can drift the theme. Recovery: the coherence cut in Step 3 — the partner sharpens, never re-architects.
- **Choosing Wright Thompson's real-excavation and Hawley's manufactured frame in the same piece.** The myth/reality axis runs both directions; using both at once muddies the truth-claim. Recovery: decide one per piece before Step 2 routes.
