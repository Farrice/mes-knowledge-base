# Imagination Engine

A 42-minute show with 38 minutes of music tells the audience what to feel before they've had a chance to feel it. This pass goes through a piece and finds every place the work is doing the audience's job — explaining the thing, showing the threat, scoring the confession — and hands that work back. The soap dispenser only detonates because the confession before it was left dry.

## Pre-Flight
Read these files before executing:
1. `skills/noah-hawley-storytelling-mastery/genius.md`
2. `skills/noah-hawley-storytelling-mastery/references/genius-patterns.md` (Pattern 7: Imagination Over Explanation; Pattern 16: Score Where Music Doesn't Go)

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: Before executing, run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` — confirm you can answer the question the piece is exploring, where it ends, and what assumption it's taking for granted. You cannot decide what's safe to withhold until you know what the piece is *for*; cut the wrong thing and the spine goes with it.

## Input Required
- The piece or scene to pass over (full text, script pages, or storyboard).
- The medium and format (screenplay/TV, essay, short-form post, brand film, ad).
- The single feeling the piece is built to produce at its peak — its one detonation.
- Any moments the author already suspects are over-served (optional but useful).
- The destination: where the piece ends, so the withhold serves the arrival rather than starving it.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Find the over-served moments
Read the piece looking for where it does the audience's imagining for them. Three failure modes, each spending imagination that should have been triggered: **over-explained** (a line that states the meaning the scene already carries), **over-shown** (the threat, the violence, the monster put fully on screen when its absence would press harder), **over-scored** (emphasis laid on top of a moment — sad music before the sad beat, a flourish telling the reader this is the important part).

Mark every instance. Be greedy here; the pass is worthless if it finds two things. For each, note *what the audience is being handed* — the inference they're being denied.

| # | Moment (quote/locate it) | Type | What's handed to the audience |
|---|--------------------------|------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | | explained / shown / scored | |
| 2 | | | |
| 3 | | | |

A live test: read each moment and ask *what would the reader have figured out on their own?* If the answer is "exactly this," the line is paying for something already bought.

### Step 2: Design the withhold for each
For every marked moment, write the underwritten replacement. Not "delete" — *reduce until the audience leans in*. The move depends on the type:

- **Over-explained →** cut the explanation. Let the action, object, or silence carry the meaning. The reader assembling it themselves believes it more than being told.
- **Over-shown →** keep it offscreen or at the edge of frame. The thing in the basement scares more unseen; the violence implied lands harder than the violence staged.
- **Over-scored →** leave it dry. Pull the emphasis off this beat and bank it for the release later. *Where does music not go?* That's where you save it.

| # | Original | Underwritten replacement | What now does the work |
|---|----------|--------------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | | | the reader's inference / the offscreen / the dry beat |
| 2 | | | |
| 3 | | | |

Hold one rule: if cutting the explanation removes information the piece genuinely needs, you've found a thing to *relocate*, not delete. Move it earlier, lower, into an object.

### Step 3: Show vulnerability where you were showing threat
*Paranormal Activity* didn't scare his hard-to-scare daughter by trying to scare her — it showed her how vulnerable she is eight hours a day, asleep, while the room does whatever it does. The threat shown is finite. The vulnerability shown is a door the imagination walks through alone.

Take the over-shown moments from Step 1 and look for the inversion: instead of rendering the danger, render what the danger has access to. Not the intruder — the unlocked window, the kid asleep down the hall, the eight hours nobody is watching. Where the piece points its camera at the wolf, turn it toward the thing the wolf can reach.

For each over-shown moment, write the vulnerability cut:

```
Was showing:  [the threat / danger / monster]
Now showing:  [what's exposed to it — the unguarded thing]
The threat is now: [implied, off-frame, the reader's own to imagine]
```

### Step 4: Verify the elevated-stress payoff
The point of withholding isn't austerity — it's that *less shown raises the resting tension, so a small thing now spikes.* When stress is elevated you don't need much to happen: a drawer sliding in the corner of frame, a soap dispenser that finally works. Run the test on the underwritten piece.

Find the smallest movement near the piece's peak — the literal soap-dispenser equivalent. Ask: with the explanation cut, the threat offscreen, the earlier beats left dry, does this small movement now carry weight it couldn't have carried in the over-served version?

| Small movement at the peak | Weight it carried before | Weight it carries now | Spikes? |
|----------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------|---------|
| | | | yes / no |

If it doesn't spike, you withheld in the wrong place — you may have cut tension instead of banking it, or starved the spine. Return to Step 2 and check whether a withhold removed something the detonation was standing on. The withholds should make the ending *louder*, never quieter.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| Screenplay / TV | Score-where-music-doesn't-go literally. Mark the cues; pull the one over the confession and bank it for the release. Keep violence offscreen and let sound design imply it. |
| Long-form essay / Substack | Over-explanation is the stated thesis after a scene that already proved it. Cut the sentence that names the feeling; let the image be the argument. The reader's inference is the payoff. |
| Short-form (LinkedIn/X) | The over-served line is the moral tacked on the end. Delete it. The post should end on the concrete moment and let the lesson form in the reader's head — they'll trust what they concluded over what you announced. |
| Brand / campaign | Stop showing the product solving everything. Show what's at stake without it — the vulnerability, the gap. Withhold the tagline-as-explanation; let the spot end on the thing, not the claim. |
| Horror / thriller anything | Pure Pattern 7. Budget *is* the engine: the unseen costs nothing and presses hardest. Show the eight vulnerable hours, not the intruder. |

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review output against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` and the § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric. Flag and fix any violation before delivering.
- **Emotionality purged**: no moment now tells the audience what to feel before they feel it. Every "sad music before the sad beat" equivalent is gone or relocated.
- **Spine intact**: nothing withheld was load-bearing for the question or the ending — you cut explanation, not meaning.
- **The release got louder**: the small movement at the peak spikes *because* of the withholds, not in spite of them. If the ending went quiet, the pass failed.
- **Vulnerability over threat**: at least one over-shown danger was inverted into something exposed, not just trimmed.
- **No machinery on the page**: the replacements don't announce "this is left to your imagination" — they simply leave the space.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Cutting tension instead of banking it.** Withholding a beat that was *building* pressure, so the release has nothing to detonate against. Recovery: only withhold *emphasis* and *explanation*; protect the rungs of rising tension.
- **Austerity for its own sake.** Stripping everything until the piece is thin, not loaded. The goal is elevated resting tension, not emptiness. Recovery: run Step 4 — if the small movement doesn't spike, you over-cut.
- **Removing the thing the ending stands on.** A cut explanation that the detonation actually needed, leaving the peak confusing instead of charged. Recovery: relocate it earlier or into an object; don't delete information the arrival requires.
- **Replacing one explanation with a subtler explanation.** Trading "she felt betrayed" for "betrayal hung in the air" — still naming the feeling. Recovery: cut to the concrete (the unanswered text, the half-eaten plate left at the table) and let the reader name it themselves.
