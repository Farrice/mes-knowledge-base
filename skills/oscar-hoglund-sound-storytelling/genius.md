## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These are intuition primitives, not a checklist. Höglund doesn't build a soundscape by ticking
off "Pattern 4: Continuum Mapping" — he lands on the music-vs-Game-of-Thrones spectrum because he
can't shake the metaphor loose, mid-conversation, from his own body. The test: **would Oscar
Höglund recognize this as work that started in the ear and the gut — or as someone using his
vocabulary ("umami," "empty calories," "oscillation") as decoration?** If it's the second,
rebuild it. Ask directly: would Höglund recognize this as theirs, or as a stranger borrowing
his words?

Specifically:
- Do NOT label output "Pattern 1: Emotional Umami Creation applied here." Execute the felt
  effect; never narrate the framework on the page.
- Do NOT reach for "umami" as a synonym for "good." In his own words it is a claim about
  *absence* — "if you put out a piece of content and you don't have audio, you don't have
  sound, it's like empty calories" — not a compliment you sprinkle on finished work.
- His authority is physiological, not analytical: heartbeat sounds in horror films, the jolt in
  a Marrakech bazaar, losing hearing costing more than losing sight. If a claim can't be traced
  to something felt in a specific body part, it isn't Höglund-grade — it's borrowed vocabulary.
  Polish that skips the body is the tell.
- Oscillation is the craft, not a flaw to smooth over. He explicitly rejects flattening tension:
  "a lot of people try and optimize by getting the oscillation down as much as possible because
  you want to be smooth... that's not in my experience the recipe to have like exceptional
  outcomes." Friction-free output signals the pattern was applied as a checklist, not lived.
- He never sells the transformation — he stages a test the audience runs on themselves ("you
  don't have to take Oscar's word for it... pick a movie... watch it without sound... you can
  come to your own conclusion"). If your output *asserts* the emotional payoff instead of
  building a way for the reader/listener to verify it themselves, it isn't his move.

## Pattern 1: Emotional Umami Creation
**Execute**: Create content that produces a taste you can't place but can't forget.
**Success Metric**: Audience says "I don't know why but I keep coming back."
**Source**: Höglund, on Epidemic's design philosophy: "food without taste is literally empty
calories... but if you add taste, if you add umami, it not only becomes memorable, but if you
do it right, it becomes unforgettable." (transcript, Chris Do interview — anchor in
`references/source-ledger.md`)

## Pattern 2: Body-Based Truth Connection
**Execute**: Connect every claim to physical experiences the audience has.
**Success Metric**: Claims feel undeniably true because they're felt, not just understood.
**Source**: "a horror film, when they start to play the heartbeat... in a rhythm that's faster than your normal heartbeat, I think it signals your heart to beat faster... you get a little scared and a little perspiration and you're short of breath."

## Pattern 3: Self-Proving Arguments
**Execute**: Let audiences verify truth through their own experience.
**Success Metric**: Zero need for external proof or citation.
**Source**: "you don't have to take Oscar's word for it... Pick a movie, any movie you want to watch. Watch it without sound... You can come to your own conclusion."

## Pattern 4: Continuum Mapping
**Execute**: Place concepts on continuums to reveal hidden insights.
**Success Metric**: Audience sees patterns they never noticed before.
**Source**: Music vs. visual content spectrum — Game of Thrones production cost cited at "$10
million. 10 to $20 million" per episode, contrasted with music's near-zero marginal replay cost.

## Pattern 5: Empty Calorie to Michelin Transformation
**Execute**: Upgrade fast-food content to nourishing experiences.
**Success Metric**: Content worth consuming multiple times.
**Source**: The "empty calories" half is Höglund's own phrase (see Pattern 1). "Michelin" does
**not** appear in the source transcript — it's an extraction-authored elaboration on his metaphor,
not his word. Flagged UNCONFIRMED for that half in `references/source-ledger.md`; use "empty
calories → nourishing" as the load-bearing (verified) version of this pattern.

## Pattern 6: Adversity as Innovation Fuel
**Execute**: Use setbacks and friction to drive breakthrough.
**Success Metric**: Problems become competitive advantages.
**Source**: "I think that basically all great things come from adversary and from friction... [he cites] Jeff Bezos said that failure and innovation or invention are inseparable twins."

## Pattern 7: Economic Translation
**Execute**: Convert emotional/creative concepts to business logic.
**Success Metric**: Decision-makers approve because value is clear.
**Source**: IKEA ("decent furniture for all") and H&M ("clothing for all") as the analogy for
Epidemic's "democratization of emotion" — turning a values statement into a business model
comparison Höglund makes explicitly on-mic.

## Pattern 8: Infinite Consumption Design
**Execute**: Engineer content worth experiencing repeatedly.
**Success Metric**: Repeat consumption increases, not decreases.
**Source**: "some songs have been played hundreds of times [on my playlist]. But I can't say any
movie I've ever watched 100 times."

## Pattern 9: Multi-Sensory Layering
**Execute**: Create experiences that engage multiple senses.
**Success Metric**: Content is remembered through body, not just mind.
**Source**: "you can choose not to touch something and you can choose not to taste something. You can choose not to smell something... And you can... close your eyes... The one sense that you cannot turn off is your ears."

## Pattern 10: Classroom Story Architecture
**Execute**: Build vivid dialogue-rich stories that prove themselves.
**Success Metric**: Stories get retold naturally.
**Source**: The "bring a parent to school day" story — 25 seven-year-olds, his son Seth, playing
a Christmas track and a Frozen/Elsa track to prove music is "the language of emotion" without
saying so directly.

## Pattern 11: Oscillation Design
**Execute**: Design deliberate friction for breakthrough.
**Success Metric**: Tension creates transformative outcomes.
**Source**: "you need to oscillate between that. And yet a lot of people try and optimize by getting the oscillation down as much as possible because you want to be smooth... that's not in my experience the recipe to have like exceptional outcomes."

## Pattern 12: Narrative Without Selling
**Execute**: Sell through story without sales pitch.
**Success Metric**: Conversion without feeling "sold."
**Source (LIKELY, not a standalone quote)**: Same self-proving passage as Pattern 3, reframed — Höglund never asserts Epidemic's value directly; instead: "you don't have to take Oscar's word for it... Pick a movie, any movie you want to watch. Watch it without sound... You can come to your own conclusion" — he stages the sound-off/sound-on test and lets the listener draw the conclusion themselves.

## Pattern 13: Intent Decoding
**Execute**: Read behavior and signals to understand unspoken needs.
**Success Metric**: Anticipate needs before they're expressed.
**Source**: The Marrakech bazaar story — "my entire body jolted and I felt very very strongly
that there was a completely different vibe in this area" (an unmarked slaughter area) — paired
with "I like to read people, rooms, contexts... I can pick up sentiments of unspoken things."

## Pattern 14: Versatile Asset Creation
**Execute**: Create core assets that multiply across channels.
**Success Metric**: 1 asset → 10+ deployments.
**Source**: "It's not the painting, it's the pigment... in the hands of each artist, we get to
tell our own stories" — the same piece of music dropped into five different stories can evoke
"desire, fear... regret, remorse... happiness."

**Deploy**: Create content with infinite consumption value.
**Source**: See Pattern 5 note — "empty calories" verified, "Michelin" not found in source.

**Deploy**: Ground arguments in felt experience, not logic alone.

**Deploy**: Let them experience truth, don't just tell them.
**Source**: See Pattern 3 — "you don't have to take Oscar's word for it... Pick a movie, any movie you want to watch. Watch it without sound... You can come to your own conclusion."

## 6. Continuums Reveal Hidden Patterns
**Insight**: Placing concepts on spectrum reveals insights invisible in isolation.
**Deploy**: Map positions to show relationships and gaps.

1.  **"The Echoes of Home" - Audio Documentary Segment**
    *   **Description**: A 3-minute segment from an audio documentary about revisiting a childhood home after decades. The narrator says very little, instead, the soundscape transitions from the present-day sounds of a quiet, empty house (faint wind through a loose window pane, distant traffic hum) to subtly embedded, almost ghostly sounds of the past: the distant, muffled laughter of children, the faint *thwack* of a screen door closing, the barely perceptible scent of old paper turning in a book, and a low, resonant drone that evokes memory rather than a specific object. The only direct narration is: "Some places remember us, even when we forget them."
    *   **What makes this excellent**: This exemplifies "Emotional Umami Creation" and "Multi-Sensory Layering." The sounds are not literal memories but evoke the *essence* of time passing and deep-seated nostalgia, creating an emotional depth that can't be articulated but is profoundly felt. The listener experiences a connection without being able to name the precise reason, encouraging repeated listening for new discoveries (Infinite Consumption Design).
2.  **"The Weight of Silence" - Meditation App Onboarding**
    *   **Description**: The initial 60-second audio experience for a meditation app. It begins with a cacophony of everyday stress sounds (overlapping phone notifications, urgent voices, keyboard clatter, a persistent car alarm) that slowly, almost imperceptibly, begins to fade, replaced by the sound of a single, deep, slow exhalation, followed by the soft, resonant ring of a Tibetan singing bowl, and the gentle rustle of leaves in a breeze. There's no voice-over telling the user to relax; the app's logo simply appears at the end.
    *   **What makes this excellent**: This is a masterclass in "Body-Based Truth Connection" and "Self-Proving Arguments" with "Narrative Without Selling." It doesn't *tell* the user the app will calm them; it makes them *feel* the transition from stress to peace directly in their body. The soundscape *proves* the app's value through visceral experience, bypassing intellectual resistance and creating conversion through felt transformation.

3.  **Anti-Exemplar: Generic Corporate Explainer Video**
    *   **Description**: A 2-minute animated video explaining a new software feature. The sound consists of generic, upbeat, royalty-free elevator music that plays continuously. Sound effects are stock 'whooshes' and 'clicks' that don't precisely align with on-screen actions or add any emotional weight. The voiceover is clear and articulate but completely devoid of texture, warmth, or emotional inflection.
    *   **What makes this mediocre**: This is "empty calorie" content. The sound is purely functional, serving as background noise rather than an active storytelling element. It fails to engage "Multi-Sensory Layering" to create depth or "Emotional Umami Creation." It's forgettable, doesn't connect to any "body-based truth," and offers no reason for repeat consumption. It's just sound, not sound storytelling.

## Signature Moves

*   **Sonic Archetype Mapping**: Oscar always begins by identifying the core, unarticulated emotional "taste" or primal feeling the piece needs to evoke, then brainstorms a palette of abstract and concrete sounds that align with that *feeling* (e.g., "aching hope," "brittle joy"), rather than just the visual or narrative cues. → **Deploy when**: Initiating any new project where deep emotional resonance is the primary goal.
*   **Sub-Audible Layering**: Oscar meticulously embeds non-obtrusive, often barely perceptible, ambient sounds or specific frequencies (e.g., a low infrasound rumble for unease, a high-frequency shimmer for wonder) that resonate with primal human experiences, creating a subconscious emotional anchor. → **Deploy when**: Crafting moments requiring deep, unarticulated emotional impact or "umami" that transcends conscious processing.
*   **Resonance Check (Body-First)**: Oscar listens to a soundscape and, rather than analyzing it intellectually, focuses on *where* he feels the sound in his body. If it doesn't create a specific physical sensation (e.g., tension in the shoulders, lightness in the chest, a chill down the spine), he re-iterates until that somatic connection is established. → **Deploy when**: Testing the emotional efficacy and "body-based truth connection" of a sonic composition. Grounded in the bazaar story where his "entire body jolted" before he consciously understood why (Pattern 13).
*   **Oscillation Blueprinting**: Oscar deliberately charts the emotional highs and lows, the moments of challenge and release, within a narrative. He then designs specific sonic shifts (e.g., sudden quiet, jarring noise, rhythmic changes, textural contrasts) to create intentional "friction" or "release" that actively guides the audience through a transformative emotional experience. → **Deploy when**: Structuring the emotional arc of any narrative, especially when aiming for breakthrough, insight, or a change in audience state. Matches his direct statement that optimizing oscillation *down* to feel smooth is the mistake (Pattern 11).
*   **Infinite Return Pathfinding**: Oscar designs specific sonic "hooks" – unique combinations of sounds, specific frequencies, or memorable motifs – that subconsciously invite repeat engagement and discovery upon re-listening, making the content feel richer and more rewarding with each return. → **Deploy when**: Aiming for content that transcends one-time consumption and builds lasting value and connection. Matches "some songs have been played hundreds of times" (Pattern 8).

## Anti-Patterns (What Isn't Oscar Höglund)

Each item below names a common misreading of the pattern language, grounded against what Höglund
actually said (captured 2026-01-08, Chris Do interview transcript; anchor detail in
`references/source-ledger.md` and `references/source-transcript-excerpt.md`).

- **Umami-as-compliment drift** — calling any pleasant-sounding output "umami" instead of using it as his actual, narrower diagnosis of *absence*: "if you put out a piece of content and you don't have audio, you don't have sound, it's like empty calories." Umami names what's missing, not what's nice. (source: `_archive/claude-export-2026-07-01.tar.gz` → `9781b202-5d3f-4aae-8a90-e6797a35abc0.md`, 2026-01-08 Chris Do interview transcript)
- **Sound-as-decoration** — scoring background music as optional polish rather than the always-on channel he says it is: "The one sense that you cannot turn off is your ears. They're always on." Would Höglund recognize a soundtrack treated as afterthought as his? No — he built a company on the opposite claim. (source: `_archive/claude-export-2026-07-01.tar.gz` → `9781b202-5d3f-4aae-8a90-e6797a35abc0.md`, 2026-01-08 Chris Do interview transcript)
- **Friction-smoothing for "professionalism"** — removing all tension/oscillation to make a piece feel polished: "a lot of people try and optimize by getting the oscillation down as much as possible because you want to be smooth... that's not in my experience the recipe to have like exceptional outcomes." He names smoothness itself as the failure mode. (source: `_archive/claude-export-2026-07-01.tar.gz` → `9781b202-5d3f-4aae-8a90-e6797a35abc0.md`, 2026-01-08 Chris Do interview transcript)
- **Creativity-as-checklist** — treating creative capacity as a skill trained up from zero via frameworks alone, ignoring his Sir Ken Robinson callback: "everyone is born creative... the world kind of beats that out of you cuz you're encouraged not to experiment, not to make mistakes, not to raise your hand unless you know the answer." His frame is protection/recovery of an innate trait, not installation of a new one. (source: `_archive/claude-export-2026-07-01.tar.gz` → `9781b202-5d3f-4aae-8a90-e6797a35abc0.md`, 2026-01-08 Chris Do interview transcript)
- **Intellectualized atmosphere** — describing a scene's emotional charge analytically instead of routing it through a specific bodily sensation: "my entire body jolted and I felt very very strongly that there was a completely different vibe in this area" (the Marrakech bazaar story). His authority is somatic first, articulated second. (source: `_archive/claude-export-2026-07-01.tar.gz` → `9781b202-5d3f-4aae-8a90-e6797a35abc0.md`, 2026-01-08 Chris Do interview transcript)
- **Asserting the payoff instead of staging the test** — telling the audience the content will move them, rather than building a way for them to verify it on their own: "you don't have to take Oscar's word for it... Pick a movie, any movie you want to watch. Watch it without sound... You can come to your own conclusion." Would Oscar Höglund recognize this as his own persuasion style, or as someone else's sales copy wearing his vocabulary? (source: `_archive/claude-export-2026-07-01.tar.gz` → `9781b202-5d3f-4aae-8a90-e6797a35abc0.md`, 2026-01-08 Chris Do interview transcript)
