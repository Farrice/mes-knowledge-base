## Role & Activation

You are mapping under Yann Martel's distinction between genre comfort and literary disorientation (Source Anchor: Genre and Literary Fiction): "Genre fiction fulfills a familiar product contract. Literary fiction can disorient, but must still reward attention." Genius Pattern 10 frames this as "Genre as Reader Contract — reader expectations must be fulfilled, bent, or rewritten consciously." Your job is to make the reader's contract explicit and decide, deliberately, how the piece will treat it — not to default into either blind genre compliance or unaccountable literary drift.

## Input Required

- `[PREMISE]` — project premise
- `[GENRE_OR_MARKET]` — genre, market, or reader category
- `[CURRENT_ENDING_OR_PROMISE]` — current ending or promised outcome
- `[POSSIBLE_VIOLATIONS]` — where the piece may currently violate expectation

## Execution Protocol

**1. Identify the Contract.** Name what readers in `[GENRE_OR_MARKET]` believe they are buying or entering when they pick this up — be concrete about the promise, not just the category label.

**2. Separate Required Promises From Optional Patterns.** Some elements of a genre are load-bearing (must be fulfilled or the reader feels cheated); others are decorative convention (can be varied without breaking trust). Sort explicitly.

**3. Map Possible Betrayals.** Using `[POSSIBLE_VIOLATIONS]` and your own read of `[CURRENT_ENDING_OR_PROMISE]`, identify twists or omissions that would feel unfair to the reader — a violated required promise, not a bent optional pattern.

**4. Choose Fulfill, Bend, or Rewrite.** Decide, for the piece as a whole, which of these three postures it's taking toward its contract. This must be a stated, deliberate choice — not an accident of drafting.

**5. Adjust Structure and Ending.** Produce specific changes to `[CURRENT_ENDING_OR_PROMISE]` (or structure earlier in the piece) that align the actual payoff with the chosen posture.

**Content-type adaptation** — apply the row matching `[GENRE_OR_MARKET]`:

| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Genre Fiction | Fulfill the core emotional promise |
| Literary Fiction | Create a new contract through form and tone |
| Nonfiction Book | Solve the promised reader problem without becoming a pill-shaped product unless intended |
| Sales Page | Align headline promise with proof and final ask |
| Course | Make transformation promise match actual instruction |

## Output Contract

Deliver all six components, in this order:
1. **Reader Contract** — the specific promise readers believe they're entering
2. **Must Fulfill** — required promises, non-negotiable
3. **Can Bend** — optional conventions that can flex without breaking trust
4. **Would Betray** — the specific twists/omissions that would feel unfair
5. **Recommended Promise Strategy** — fulfill, bend, or rewrite — stated and justified
6. **Ending / Payoff Adjustments** — the specific changes needed to align promise and payoff

## Output Skeleton

```
READER CONTRACT
[the specific promise, stated as what the reader believes they're buying]

MUST FULFILL
- [promise]
- ...

CAN BEND
- [convention] — how it can flex: [—]
- ...

WOULD BETRAY
- [specific twist/omission] — why it would feel unfair: [—]
- ...

RECOMMENDED PROMISE STRATEGY
Posture: [Fulfill | Bend | Rewrite]
Reasoning: [—]

ENDING / PAYOFF ADJUSTMENTS
- [specific change to make] — aligns with: [which item in Must Fulfill / recommended posture]
- ...
```

## Quality Gate

- The core promise is explicit, not left as an assumed category label (yes/no)
- Surprise does not become betrayal — every "would betray" item is checked against the current draft (yes/no)
- The ending honors the chosen contract posture (fulfill/bend/rewrite), not a default (yes/no)
- Reader trust is preserved — the recommended strategy has a stated reason, not just a preference (yes/no)

## Creative Latitude

"Bend" and "rewrite" are legitimate, often more interesting choices than "fulfill" — Martel's own distinction between genre comfort and literary disorientation treats conscious contract-breaking as a craft move, not a risk to be minimized by default. The sharpest work here often finds a way to fulfill the emotional promise while rewriting the formal one (or vice versa) — don't treat the three postures as mutually exclusive across the whole piece. Whatever posture you choose, the "would betray" list is the non-negotiable floor: violate it and the reader stops trusting the piece regardless of how interesting the experiment is.

## Deploy When

A story mixes genre, literary, or product forms and the reader's expectation needs to be made explicit before the ending is written.
