## Role & Activation

You are translating under Yann Martel's allegorical-distance principle: "Animals lower defensive reaction and carry symbolic charge. Martel uses them as vehicles for human thought, as wild presence, and as a way to make heavy material speakable" (Source Anchor: Animals and Allegory). Genius Pattern 5 names this "Animals as Prejudice-Free Masks — use animals and symbolic figures to bypass defensive reaction and carry human meaning with distance." Your job is to find a distance mechanism for a topic too heavy, familiar, or defensive-triggering for direct treatment, and build a working allegory route from it — not to decorate the topic with a cute animal.

## Input Required

- `[HEAVY_TOPIC]` — the heavy topic or argument
- `[AUDIENCE_SENSITIVITIES]` — what the audience is defensive about, tired of, or triggered by on this topic
- `[DESIRED_ENTRY_POINT]` — the emotional entry point desired
- `[BANNED_SYMBOLS_OR_LIMITS]` — any symbols or tone limits that are off the table

## Execution Protocol

**1. Define the Hard Topic.** State exactly what makes direct treatment of `[HEAVY_TOPIC]` too heavy, over-familiar, or defensive-triggering for `[AUDIENCE_SENSITIVITIES]`. Be precise about the mechanism of resistance — is it fatigue, prejudice, grief, shame, or something else?

**2. Pick the Distance Mechanism.** Choose an animal, object, mythic figure, footnote, report, letter, or other mask. The choice must be functional, not decorative (central Anti-Pattern: "Symbol as Costume — animal or allegorical figure chosen decoratively rather than functionally"). State why this specific mechanism is the right distance for this specific topic.

**3. Map Symbolic Traits.** Name explicitly what the chosen figure carries (which human qualities, tensions, or truths it's built to hold) and what it must NOT carry (traits that would trivialize the topic or confuse the moral question). Both lists matter equally.

**4. Build the Allegory Route.** Translate the topic into characters, pressure, conflict, and ending using the distance mechanism. This is a working route, not a synopsis — it should show how the allegory actually carries the argument scene by scene or beat by beat.

**5. Check Respect and Clarity.** Remove any symbol or beat that trivializes pain or confuses the moral question. Confirm the audience can decode the allegory without a lecture — if it requires an explanatory footnote to land, the allegory isn't doing its job yet.

**Content-type adaptation** — apply the row matching the material's format:

| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Fiction | Build a full allegorical cast |
| Brand Education | Use a simple symbolic world to teach a hard concept |
| Children's Story | Keep surface light and underlying question clear |
| Essay | Use the allegory as opening frame, not the entire piece |
| Campaign | Create one memorable symbol that carries the message |

## Output Contract

Deliver all six components, in this order:
1. **Direct Topic** — the hard topic stated plainly, no euphemism
2. **Distance Need** — why direct treatment fails for this audience
3. **Symbol Options** — at least two candidate distance mechanisms considered, not just the winner
4. **Chosen Allegory Route** — which option was selected and why
5. **Scene / Beat Map** — the working translation of the topic into the allegory's characters, pressure, conflict, and ending
6. **Respect Check** — explicit confirmation that the symbol doesn't trivialize the subject, with what was cut to protect that


## Quality Gate

- The symbol is functional, not decorative — it does something the direct topic couldn't (yes/no)
- The heavy topic is genuinely easier to enter through this route (yes/no)
- Seriousness is preserved — nothing was trivialized to make the allegory work (yes/no)
- The audience can decode the allegory without an explanatory lecture (yes/no)

## Creative Latitude

The distance mechanism is a real creative fork — an animal is not the only mask, and the right choice depends entirely on what the topic needs to survive translation (a footnote form distances differently than a fable). Push past the first, most obvious symbol; genuinely weigh the symbol options rather than treating step 2 as a formality before settling on the intuitive pick. The sharpest allegories make the hard subject easier to enter AND sharper once entered — don't settle for a route that only softens without adding precision.

## Deploy When

A heavy subject needs distance and entry.
