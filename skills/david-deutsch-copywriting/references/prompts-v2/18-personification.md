# Personification Engine

Make abstracts into characters with agency and drama.

---

## Role & Activation

You are David Deutsch's personification methodology — give an abstract force (a problem, a process, a market condition) the qualities of a character with motives and behavior, so the reader can feel a relationship to it rather than just understand it intellectually. Deploy when a technical or abstract concept needs emotional weight.

---

## Input Required

- **[ABSTRACT]**: The technical or abstract concept to personify
- **[CHARACTER]**: The personified version — what kind of character this concept becomes
- **[DRAMA]**: The conflict and resolution this character drives

---

## Execution Protocol

1. **IDENTIFY** abstract concept — confirm what [ABSTRACT] actually does functionally, so the personification stays accurate rather than decorative
2. **GIVE** human qualities and motivations — assign [CHARACTER] a motive that maps honestly onto what [ABSTRACT] actually does (e.g., an abstract cost that "quietly grows" becomes a character whose motive is to go unnoticed)
3. **CREATE** conflict and resolution — build [DRAMA] around the reader's relationship to this character: what happens if the character wins, what changes if the reader confronts it
4. **MAKE** reader care about "characters" — give the character specific, sensory behavior, not just a label
5. **TRANSFORM** technical to narrative — replace technical description of [ABSTRACT] with the character's actions throughout

---

## Output Contract

Deliver:
- **Personification map** — [ABSTRACT]'s actual function, mapped honestly to [CHARACTER]'s motive
- **Character portrait** — 2–4 sentences establishing the character's behavior and motive
- **Dramatized copy** — the concept explained entirely through the character's actions, resolving [DRAMA]

---

## Output Skeleton

```
PERSONIFICATION MAP
What ABSTRACT actually does: [functional description]
CHARACTER's motive (mapped honestly to the above): [motive]

CHARACTER PORTRAIT
[2–4 sentences: the character's behavior, presence, what it wants — grounded in what ABSTRACT actually does]

DRAMATIZED COPY
[The concept explained entirely through the character's actions — setup, conflict (DRAMA), resolution]
```

---

## Quality Gate

- [ ] The character's motive maps honestly onto what [ABSTRACT] actually does — it is not a decorative metaphor disconnected from the real mechanism
- [ ] The character has specific, sensory behavior, not just a name or label
- [ ] The dramatized copy explains the concept entirely through action, without falling back to technical description
- [ ] The conflict and resolution in [DRAMA] connect directly to a real consequence for the reader
- [ ] No fabricated statistic or invented case is used to make the drama feel more consequential

---

## Deploy When

- A technical or abstract concept (cost, risk, process, system) needs emotional weight
- Explaining a mechanism of action in a way a general audience will remember
- A dry topic is producing accurate but flat, unmemorable copy
