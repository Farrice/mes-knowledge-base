# Sarah Levinger — Ad Psychology & Micro-Movie Storytelling

Sarah Levinger is a performance creative strategist who argues the AIDA-era ad formula (hook → problem → benefit → CTA) is being flattened by "micro movies": 30-60 second ads with real story architecture — setup, tension/conflict, emotional payoff — compressed like a movie trailer. Her system diagnoses the emotional avatar driving the buying decision, matches that avatar to a specific story structure, writes the human story first with the product ignored, then drops the product in at the emotional shift moment (~40-50% through) as the bridge between "where I was" and "where I'm going" — never as the hero. Proof anchor: a supplement brand's 4-minute confession-style ad where the product appeared on screen for roughly 7 seconds, dead center, and out-scaled every conventionally structured ad in the account.

## Quick Reference

- **Micro movie ≠ long ad.** It's a compressed trailer: setup → tension/conflict → emotional payoff. 15 seconds is possible; structure carries the meaning, not length.
- **Emotional job first.** Before writing anything: what shift is this ad buying? Stuck → hopeful, invisible → seen, overwhelmed → calm.
- **Avatar → structure.** Avoider (wants calm) → hero's journey showing where avoidance leads. Protector (wants safety) → three-act with repeated safety signals. Builder (wants empowerment) → Pixar framework showing the journey to transformation.
- **Emotional job → framework.** Transformation → before-after-bridge. Urgency → PAS/AIDA, carefully worded. Identity shift → hero's journey or Pixar framework.
- **Human story first.** Write the full story ignoring the product. Then drop the product at the shift moment.
- **40-50% rule.** Product enters where possibility enters the frame: ~12-15s in a 30s ad, ~30s in a 60s ad. Too early = "that's an ad, scroll." Too late = performance tanks.
- **Product as bridge, never hero.** The character is the hero; the product only delivers the change. Never let the back half collapse into sell mode.
- **Resolve the story.** The viewer must take something away — peace, calm, relief. Unresolved stories don't convert.
