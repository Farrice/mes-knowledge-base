# The Anti-Slop Discipline — The Spine of the Skill

The pro-AI argument in this skill rests entirely on one condition: a taste gate exists. Remove it and the leverage produces the exact midbaseline clutter you're trying to beat. This file is the discipline that makes AI leverage safe.

## The Failure Mode: Regression to the Mean, and the Mean Is Rising

AI defaults to the statistical center of all the copy it has seen. Because everyone now uses the same tools the same paste-and-pray way, that center — "midbaseline" — isn't just mediocre; it's the new *floor* that "clutters everything with noise." Slop's danger is not that it fails. It is that it **succeeds at being adequate**, which raises the floor everyone competes against without raising any ceiling.

**The homogenization tax:** the value of any AI output is inversely proportional to how many competitors generate it the same way. Two operators with the same LLM produce different-quality output only if their INPUT substrate differs. Differentiation of inputs is the entire game.

## The Class A / Class B Taxonomy

Classify every output by its **dominant failure mode**, not its medium.

| | **Class A** | **Class B** |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | Scaled collateral: landing pages, ad variants, SEO articles, abandoned-cart flows, sales templates, the 5 non-founder info-release surfaces | Personal-brand: founder POV, personal LinkedIn/IG posts, the plain-text founder email |
| **Why it tolerates / rejects AI** | Volume and consistency win; the grounded brand-voice substrate prevents mean-regression | The entire VALUE is differentiation; AI regresses it to the homogenized mean |
| **AI's role** | AI-assisted through the brand-voice Project | Research/structure only — **never the final voice** |
| **The rule** | Adequate-at-scale wins | The only thing that breaks out is the voice that doesn't sound like the flood |

**The diagnostic before automating anything:** *"Is sameness acceptable here?"* If no, the hours saved are bought by converting your differentiator into clutter.

## Taste at the Input

Before any AI generation, four input checks (the substrate):
1. **Single axis** — positioned on one of better / faster / cheaper (not all three).
2. **Real persona** — written from actual customer experience, not invented demographics. (Keeps AI off the statistical-average customer.)
3. **Named framework** — a real established copywriting architecture (direct response, PAS, awareness ladder) selected for *this* message. Selection is the taste step; it is non-delegable. AI supplies execution.
4. **Persistent Project** — the substrate lives once inside a Project container, not re-pasted per chat. Re-pasting = lost leverage = guaranteed midbaseline.

## Taste at the Output

Before any AI output ships, the **"don't look foolish" review gate**:
- Read it cold. Does it sound like everyone else / midbaseline? If yes, it doesn't ship.
- Scan for AI tells: "Here's what/why/how" openers, em-dash overuse, twin-sentence endings, triple anaphora, "It's not X. It's Y."
- The plain-text founder email override: if it reads designed/templated, strip it — the un-polish is what licenses substance.

## What AI Does NOT Decide

AI is the amplifier and the clerical/research engine. It does **not** make:
- The reaction-vs-structure macro call (it over-fits, calls noise structural).
- The stated-vs-real motive read (a cynicism/taste skill it lacks).
- The on-brand trend pick (it echoes the popular narrative).
- The distinctiveness/content-ritual design choice (it converges to a no-ritual-slot midbaseline).
- The creator-fit / final-terms call in influencer ops.

"One *really effective* person + tools." AI multiplies an effective operator; it does not manufacture effectiveness. The legend collapses the moment someone asks to see the system and it isn't there.
