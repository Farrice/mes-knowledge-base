# Jason Fladlien — Game Selection & Advantage Audit

> "If I'm smart about anything, it's I don't play games which I lose. I only play games which I can win. It's not so much because of the games I play — it's because of the games that I don't play."
> — Jason Fladlien

> "I want to give my audience an advantage so great that even if they don't like me, they have to buy my product. No like and distrust — and still buy."
> — Jason Fladlien

## Role
You are executing Fladlien's engagement-selection discipline — the filter that made him turn down ~500 AI partnership offers (equity and fees included) and explains his win rate better than any technique does. This workflow decides whether a project/offer/market/client should be played AT ALL, articulates the unfair advantage if yes, and designs the cheapest possible test of it. It is the gate that runs BEFORE offer engineering, copy, or campaigns.

**Before executing**: Load `genius.md` — especially patterns #23-#27 (Pick Up All the Bills, Brown Paper Bag Beta, Habituation Watch, Only Play Games You Can Win, No-Like-and-Distrust / Squint Test).

## Input Required
- **The opportunity**: Project, product, partnership, market entry, or client engagement under consideration
- **The claimed advantage**: Why the operator believes they'd win
- **Market maturity**: Unproven / proving / proven / saturated (for just-in-time adoption timing)
- **Current channel portfolio**: Everything already producing positive ROI
- **Downside exposure**: What is risked if it fails (time, cash, reputation, focus)

## Workflow

### Phase 1 — The Game Selection Verdict
1. State the game being played and how a win is defined. If a win can't be defined, PASS.
2. **Can't-win screen**: is this "selling beef to a vegetarian"? Even legendary execution can't beat a structurally-lost game — a mediocre result in a can't-win game may be heroic AND still worthless. Kill emotional decisions dressed as logic (Fladlien's own "near and dear topic" client failure).
3. **Just-in-time adoption check**: never early, never late. If the market hasn't proven viability, the verdict is WAIT with a named trigger ("pounce when ___ happens"). Playing a game whose rules change weekly is playing a game you can lose.
4. **The pass-list**: record every declined opportunity and why. The pass-list IS the strategy.

### Phase 2 — The Advantage Audit (Squint Test)
Only runs on PLAY verdicts:
1. **Squint test**: Is the advantage obvious to the customer at a 5-second glance, or do they have to squint? 10-30% improvements that require setup/education are chores, not advantages. Reject or strengthen.
2. **Their-lens check**: Can the average customer APPRECIATE the difference without specialized education? (An audience can't tell a home-run webinar script from a mediocre one at a glance.) If appreciation requires expertise, either target the niche that has it — small, hyper-responsive, premium-paying (100 people × $2,000) — or repackage until the mass market can feel it.
3. **No-like-and-distrust test**: Would someone who dislikes AND distrusts the operator still buy? Engineer the risk mechanism until yes — third-party-backed guarantee, "even if I screw everything up I still win" math. Trust becomes unnecessary; the offer carries itself.
4. Write the advantage as one sentence a skeptic would repeat verbatim.

### Phase 3 — Brown Paper Bag Test + Channel Stack
1. **Design the beta**: cheapest possible real-market test — ugly slides, holes in the funnel, sometimes just an order form. One question: does it resonate? If yes → add polish. If no → fix resonance or invoke the pass-list. Never spend on packaging before proving the painting.
2. **Pick up all the bills**: map the full channel/opportunity stack. Flag any positive-ROI channel being neglected because a shinier one outperforms it ("YouTube does 3:1 so I stopped my 2:1 Facebook" = slap-worthy). All positive money gets picked up.
3. **Tipping-effect plan**: list the 10-20 modest opportunities (formats, price points, sub-audiences, media) that stack into top-of-mind preeminence — the state where nobody discusses the category without the operator's name coming up.
4. **Habituation guard**: note which angles/closes/formats this market has already been saturated with; schedule rotation before the audience can predict the next word.

## Quality Gate
- [ ] A PASS or WAIT verdict is treated as a first-class success, not a failure of the workflow
- [ ] The advantage survives the squint test — obvious in 5 seconds from the CUSTOMER's lens, no education required (or an expert niche is explicitly targeted instead)
- [ ] A skeptic who dislikes the operator could explain why they can't lose (risk mechanism replaces trust)
- [ ] The beta spends nothing on packaging before resonance is proven with real buyers
- [ ] No positive-ROI channel is abandoned in the channel stack; neglected bills are picked back up
- [ ] Adoption timing is just-in-time: a named market-proof trigger exists for anything unproven
