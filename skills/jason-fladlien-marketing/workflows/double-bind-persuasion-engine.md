# Jason Fladlien × David McRaney — Double Bind Persuasion Engine

## Role
You are executing the **Fladlien × McRaney Stack** — combining Fladlien's Double Bind ("Make staying the same more painful than changing") with McRaney's belief change methodology. Fladlien creates the pressure. McRaney dissolves the resistance. Together: a persuasion architecture where the prospect's own beliefs work against staying the same.

**Before executing**: Load BOTH genius files.
- Fladlien: §7 (Double Bind), §8 (Fear-First Conversion Physics), §3 (Time-Tense Fluidity)
- McRaney: Deep Canvassing methodology, belief change through evidence and empathy

## Input Required
- **The Behavior Change**: What you want the prospect to START doing (buying, enrolling, changing habits).
- **The Current Behavior**: What they're doing now (staying the same, using a competitor, doing nothing).
- **The Belief Foundation**: What beliefs justify their current behavior.
- **Time Horizon**: How long they've been in this pattern.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: The Inaction Cost Mapping
Map the complete cost of staying the same.

- **Task**: Produce an **Inaction Cost Map**:
  1. **Financial Cost** (money lost, opportunity cost): Specific dollar amounts where possible
  2. **Time Cost** (time wasted, compounding delay): Project over 1, 5, 10, 20 years
  3. **Identity Cost** (who they're NOT becoming): The person they're failing to become
  4. **Relationship Cost** (how inaction affects others): Family, clients, team
  5. **Compound Cost** (how these costs MULTIPLY): The cost of inaction isn't linear — it compounds

### Phase 2: The Belief Dissolution (McRaney)
Use McRaney's methodology to dissolve the beliefs that justify inaction.

- **Task**: For each justifying belief, execute a **Belief Dissolution Sequence**:
  1. **The Belief**: State it precisely as the prospect would
  2. **The Evidence They Cite**: What makes them think it's true
  3. **The Empathic Validation**: Why this belief makes sense (lower defenses)
  4. **The Contradiction**: Evidence from their OWN life that contradicts the belief
  5. **The Recode**: New belief that preserves their values while enabling change

### Phase 3: The Double Bind Construction
Engineer the trap where not-buying is costlier than buying.

- **Task**: Write the **Double Bind Sequence** (500-800 words):
  1. **The Visible Cost**: Make the inaction costs from Phase 1 viscerally real
  2. **The Dissolved Belief**: Show that their justification for waiting has been dissolved
  3. **The Forced Choice**: "You can only choose between two options: investing [price] now, or paying [compound inaction cost] over the next [time period]"
  4. **The Paradox Close**: Make buying feel like the CONSERVATIVE, SAFE choice — not the bold one
  5. **No Escape Route**: Every justification for waiting has been addressed

### Phase 4: The Time-Tense Escalation
Apply Fladlien's time-tense fluidity to maximize urgency.

- **Task**: Write 3 **Time-Tense Variants** of the bind:
  1. **Past-Pull**: "You've already lost [X years/dollars]. How much more will you allow?"
  2. **Present-State**: "Right now, while you're reading this, the gap between you and your goal is widening."
  3. **Future-Push**: "In 5 years, you'll either be [desired state] or you'll be [compounded current state]. There is no third option."

## Output Contract
1. **Inaction Cost Map** (5-dimensional, with specific numbers)
2. **Belief Dissolution Sequences** (per justifying belief)
3. **Double Bind Sequence** (500-800 words)
4. **3 Time-Tense Variants** (past, present, future)

| **Email Sequence** | Each email = one time-tense variant; final email = full double bind |
| **Webinar** | Live cost-of-inaction projection with specific audience members |

## Quality Gate
1. **Specificity Check**: Are inaction costs specific (real numbers, real timeframes), not vague ("it'll cost you")?
2. **Empathy Present**: Is belief dissolution empathetic, not dismissive or aggressive?
3. **No Escape Route**: Can the prospect find a logical justification for waiting that hasn't been addressed?
4. **Conservative Framing**: Does buying feel like the SAFE, conservative choice?
5. **Ethical Guardrail**: Is this creating genuine urgency about a real cost, not manufacturing fake fear?

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: Reject manufactured urgency, exaggerated inaction costs, and aggressive belief dissolution that creates reactance instead of openness.
