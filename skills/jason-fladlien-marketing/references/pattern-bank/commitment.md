## Choose a Door  [Commitment]
**Summary**: Makes the decision you want to occur the most attractive.
**Structure**: `There are only {#} ways to {solve problem}. Which one do you see yourself taking?`
> ****

1. Hire someone internally.
1. Use a system like this.
Which option do you see yourself taking?

## Commitment Chain  [Commitment]
**Summary**: Creates agreement momentum by starting with smaller agreements first.
**Structure**: `Can you see {benefit}? → Would {benefit} be valuable? → If you were going to {benefit}, when would you start?`
> ****

##### Can you see {benefit}?
Would {benefit} be valuable? 
If you were going to {benefit}, when would you start?

##### Examples
Can you see how this could help generate more qualified leads?
Are you interested in generating more qualified leads?
If you were going to start generating more qualified leads, when would be the best time to start?
---
Can you see the value of having predictable lead flow?
Would predictable lead flow be useful?
If you were going to build predictable lead flow, when would be the best time to begin?
---
Can you see how clearer expectations would improve team performance?
Would improved performance be valuable?
If you were going to create clearer expectations, when would be the best time to start?

#### Why It Works

##### Agreement Ladder
The prospect walks up the staircase one step at a time instead of being asked to jump straight to a major decision.
Each agreement makes the next agreement easier.

##### Ownership of Benefits
The prospect must personally recognize the benefit before they are asked whether they want it.

##### Consistency Pressure
Once someone says:
> Yes, I can see that.
And:
> Yes, that would be valuable.
The third question creates internal pressure to remain consistent with those earlier answers.

##### Presupposition
The final question does not ask:
> Will you do it?
It asks:
> When would you do it?
The action is treated as the natural next step.
---

#### What It Accomplishes
- Creates momentum inside the conversation.
- Gets the prospect selling themselves.
- Moves the conversation from possibility to implementation.
- Creates consistency pressure without confrontation.
- Makes the final step feel natural.
- Reveals genuine interest.
- Identifies whether the prospect sees value before discussing solutions.
- Creates micro-commitments that support larger commitments.

##### Conversational Ladder Applications

| Stage | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Clarify | Can you see how the real issue might be follow-up rather than lead generation?  Would fixing follow-up be valuable if that turned out to be true?  If follow-up is the problem, when would you want to solve it? |
| Expand | Can you see how adding just one more salesperson without fixing the process could create even more chaos?  Would avoiding that chaos be important?  If you were going to fix the process first, when would be the best time to do it? |
| Commit | Can you see how having a written hiring process would help you avoid another bad hire?  Would avoiding another six-month hiring mistake be valuable?  If you were going to put that process in place, when would you begin? |
| Resolve | Can you see how the cost of doing nothing for another year is probably larger than the investment we're discussing?  Would avoiding that loss be worthwhile?  If you were going to stop absorbing that loss, when would be the best time to begin? |

## Decision Contract  [Commitment]
**Summary**: Creating the rules that get someone to say yes.
**Structure**: `If it's in your best interest, say yes. If not, say no. If unclear, let's discuss it. Fair enough?`
> ****

##### I’m going to {action}. Then I’m going to ask you to {decision}. 

If it's clearly in your best interest, I’ll ask you to say yes. 

If it's clearly not in your best interest, I'll ask you to say no. 

And if it's unclear, then let's discuss what's unclear until we determine whether it's a yes or a no. 

Fair enough?

##### Examples
I'm going to present the offer.
Then I'm going to ask you to make a decision.
If it's clearly in your best interest, I'll ask you to say yes.
If it's clearly not in your best interest, I'll ask you to say no.
And if it's unclear, then let's discuss what's unclear until we determine whether it's a yes or a no.
Fair enough?
---
At the end of this discussion, there are really only three possible outcomes.
We conclude this is a good fit.
We conclude it isn't.
Or we conclude we need more information.
I'm comfortable with any of those outcomes.
Are you?
---
Once you've heard the proposal, I'd like you to evaluate it on its merits.
If you believe it's the right move, support it.
If you believe it isn't, challenge it.
And if you're undecided, let's talk through your concerns.
Does that sound fair?
---
When we're finished, there are only three intellectually honest answers:
"Yes, this makes sense."
"No, this doesn't make sense."
Or
"I don't yet have enough information to know."
If it's yes, let's proceed.
If it's no, let's not.
If it's the third, let's determine exactly what information is missing.
Agreed?

#### Why It Works

##### Eliminates the "Maybe"
Most of us are afraid of commitment, even when it's in our best interest.
When we say yes, we give up the ability to say no.
When we say no, we close the door on the opportunity entirely.
As a result, we often drift toward maybe because it feels safer. Maybe allows us to keep all our options open. Maybe postpones the risk of being wrong. Maybe delays the responsibility of making a decision.
The problem is that maybe doesn’t serve us.
It's perfectly reasonable to be thoughtful. It's perfectly reasonable to gather information. But many times, "I need to think about it" is not actually a request for more information. It's a way to delay making a decision.
This pattern addresses that issue before it ever appears.

##### Creates a Closed Loop
The conversation now has a defined ending.
We're not talking just to talk.
We're talking to determine:
- yes
- no
- or what information is missing so we can get a yes or a no. 
That creates focus.

##### Aligns With Their Best Interest
The pattern explicitly gives permission to say no.
That dramatically lowers resistance.
We're not trying to get a yes.
We're trying to find the truth.
The prospect feels safer because we are positioned on the same side of the table.

##### Removes Hidden Agendas
Most sales conversations contain an unspoken expectation.
Everyone knows a decision is desired.
Nobody says it.
This pattern brings the expectation into the open.
That transparency creates trust.

##### Converts Commitment Into Evaluation
Many people fear commitment.
Few people fear evaluation.
The pattern reframes the conversation.
We are not committing.
We are evaluating.
The commitment only comes after the evaluation is complete.
That makes the process feel safer.

##### Protects Your Time
If a prospect can't even agree to this frame at the beginning of the conversation, they’ll never buy at at the end. 
Think about what you're asking them to agree to:
- If it's a fit, say yes.
- If it isn't a fit, say no.
- If it's unclear, let's discuss what's unclear.
That's a remarkably fair standard.
So when someone resists even that level of commitment, it often reveals that they aren't prepared to make a decision regardless of what you present.
This pattern surfaces that reality early.
Instead of spending forty-five minutes uncovering problems, discussing solutions, handling objections, and answering questions, only to discover they were never willing to make a decision in the first place, you find out within the first few minutes.
Then you can end the call immediately. You can be nice about it: 
> “Oh it seems like you're still very early on in the discovery stage of figuring out what it is you want and don't want. To save us both time on this conversation, is it okay if I send you some trainings that I've developed that may help assist you with gaining more clarity?”

#### What It Accomplishes
- Establishes a decision frame.
- Removes endless maybes.
- Creates trust.
- Lowers resistance.
- Makes “no” acceptable.
- Surfaces hidden objections.
- Creates a clear outcome for the conversation.
- Prevents wasted time.
- Positions you as objective.
- Creates expectation that a decision will be made.

## Hypothetical  [Commitment]
**Summary**: Use a condition to remove a limitation, excuse or constraint.
**Structure**: `If I could show you {desired outcome}, would you be willing to {commitment}?`
> ****

##### If I could show you {desired outcome}, would you be willing to {commitment}?

##### Examples
- If I could show you exactly how this could pay for itself within the first 90 days, would you be willing to make a yes decision today?
- If the investment turned out to be significantly smaller than the cost of the problem, would you be willing to proceed?
- If I could show you how clients in your exact situation recovered the cost in less than six months, would you be comfortable making a decision today?
- If we could show you a way to implement this without creating additional work for your team, would you be willing to move forward today?
- If I could demonstrate that getting started would take less than an hour of your time this week, would you feel comfortable saying yes?
- If we could show you a way to test this before making a larger commitment, would you be willing to proceed?
- If you became completely confident that we could deliver what we've discussed, would you be comfortable saying yes today?
- If you could reclaim ten or twenty hours a week without sacrificing growth, would you be comfortable saying yes?
- If we could eliminate that concern entirely, would there be any reason not to proceed?

##### Alternatives
- If it turned out that {desired outcome}, would you be willing to {commitment}?
- If we could prove {desired outcome}, would there be any reason not to {commitment}?
- If we could eliminate {objection}, would you be willing to {commitment}? 
- If we could accomplish {desired outcome} without {feared consequence}, would you be willing to {commitment}?
- If you discovered that {desired outcome} was actually achievable, would you be comfortable {commitment}?

#### Why It Works

##### Conditional Commitment
The prospect agrees to the decision before seeing the evidence.
Instead of arguing about whether they should buy, we establish that if the condition is met, the decision makes sense.
The conversation becomes about proving the condition rather than defending the offer.

##### Objection Isolation
The condition often reveals the real obstacle.
If they say no, we've learned that the stated concern wasn't actually the deciding factor.
If they say yes, we've identified a buying criterion.
Either way we gain clarity.

##### Future Pacing
We temporarily move them into a future where the desired outcome already exists.
That allows them to evaluate the decision from the perspective of success rather than uncertainty.

##### Decision Simplification
Most buying decisions contain dozens of variables.
This pattern reduces the entire decision to a single condition.
"If this is true, would you move forward?"
That creates a much easier question to answer.

##### Consistency Pressure
Once someone agrees that a condition would justify action, it becomes harder to reject the action after the condition has been satisfied.
The commitment is made before the evidence is presented.

##### Buying Criteria Discovery
The condition itself reveals what matters most.
Payback period.
Implementation speed.
Risk reduction.
Time investment.
Confidence.
The pattern helps uncover which variable is actually driving the decision.

#### What It Accomplishes
- Creates a conditional yes.
- Establishes buying criteria.
- Surfaces hidden objections.
- Simplifies complex decisions.
- Creates consistency pressure.
- Future paces the prospect into success.
- Tests whether an objection is genuine.

#### Conversational Ladder Applications

| Stage | Application |
|---|---|
| Connect | If I had a perfect solution for you by the end of our conversation, would it be okay to share it in the last few minutes?  |
| Discover | If there was a way to fix this problem permanently, would you want to know about it? |
| Clarify | If we could prove that hiring isn't actually the bottleneck, would you be open to looking elsewhere for the cause? |
| Expand | If you could get back the fifteen hours you're losing every week to this issue, what would that be worth to you? And if we could make that happen in a way that made sense to you, would you be willing to act on it? |
| Commit | If I could show you exactly how this pays for itself within 90 days, would you be willing to make a decision today? |
| Resolve | If we could completely eliminate the concern about implementation, would there be any reason left not to sign up right now? |

## Permission  [Commitment]
**Summary**: Gains consent before introducing claim, suggestion, challenge or reframe.
**Structure**: `Would it be okay if I {reframe/challenge/suggest something}?`
> ****

##### Would it be okay if I {reframe / challenge / suggest something uncomfortable}?

##### Examples
- Would it be okay if I were to suggest an alternative way of looking at this?
- If I were to show you {outcome} for free and it was the best lesson you ever had on the subject, then would you at least consider the offer that I’ll make at the end? 
- May I suggest to you to be open to the idea that maybe you’re wrong about your limitations?
- Is it ok if I ask you to be at least 1% open to the idea that sometimes the best way to put a bad deal behind you is to start a new good deal right after?

#### Why It Works

##### Consent Lowers Resistance
The moment we ask permission, resistance almost disappears.
When someone grants permission, they've agreed to stay open before they've heard what's coming.

##### Creates a Contract
Asking permission creates a small agreement.
When someone says "sure, go ahead," they've committed to listen. They've briefly lowered their defenses.
Now what follows gets received differently than if we'd just said it without asking.

##### Positions You as Respectful, Not Pushy
Direct challenges can feel aggressive. Direct reframes can feel manipulative.
Asking first communicates respect. The listener feels in control because they technically said yes before they heard the reframe.

##### Frames What Follows as Significant
"Would it be okay if I offered a different perspective?" signals that something meaningful is coming.
The listener prepares to receive it with more attention than they'd give a casual remark.

#### What It Accomplishes
- Reduces resistance before a reframe or challenge
- Creates consent before delivering uncomfortable truths
- Signals respect for the other person's perspective
- Increases the weight and attention given to what follows
- Makes pushback less likely
- Creates a small pre-commitment to remain open

#### Conversational Ladder Applications

| Stage | Example |
|---|---|
| Connect | "Would it be okay if I asked a few questions before we get started?" |
| Discover | “Is it fine if I ask you a lot of questions along the way to get the deepest understanding of your business possible?” |
| Clarify | "Would you be open to me suggesting a different cause for what you're describing?" |
| Expand | "Is it okay if I show you what this is actually costing per month?" |
| Commit | "Do I have your permission to show you what we can offer you?” |
| Decide | "Would it be okay if I asked you to sign up right now?” |
| Resolve | “May I suggest to you to be open to the idea that maybe you’re wrong about your limitations?” |

## Trial Close  [Commitment]
**Summary**: Measures interest, agreement and readiness to a bigger ask.
**Structure**: `How do you feel about {proposal/idea} so far?`
> ****

##### How do you feel about {proposal / idea / what we've covered} so far?

##### Examples
- "How do you feel about what we've discussed so far?”
- On a scale from 1 to 10, how well does this solve your current problem?”
- How do you see this fitting into your current workflow?
- Where do you see this having the biggest impact?
- Compared to how you're doing it today, how much improvement do you see?
- On a scale from 1-10, how much of a priority is solving this?
- What would you need to see to feel completely comfortable?
- Does this support the goals you've described?

#### Why It Works

##### Reveals Reality Before It's Too Late
Most salespeople wait until the close to find out whether the prospect is with them.
Trial closes check in throughout the conversation. This lets us course-correct in real time rather than hitting "let me think about it" at the very end.

##### The Answer Tells You Everything
A strong answer: "This is exactly what I've been looking for" tells you to keep moving with confidence.
A weak answer: "It sounds interesting" tells you there's more work to do.
A deflected answer: "I'm not sure yet" tells you there's something unspoken.
Each response gives you information you can use.

##### Creates Ownership
When someone evaluates how they feel about something, they stop being a passive listener and start forming a position.
Once they've expressed any level of investment, they have something at stake. That ownership makes the final close feel natural.

##### Trains for the Close
Trial closes are small rehearsals. Each time someone answers favorably, they practice saying yes.
By the time we ask for the real decision, the pattern feels familiar.

#### What It Accomplishes
- Reveals genuine interest level before the final close
- Creates checkpoints to catch problems early
- Encourages the prospect to begin forming and owning a position
- Makes the final close feel like a natural next step, not a sudden ask
- Gives permission to surface and handle concerns before they become deal-breakers

#### Conversational Ladder Applications

| Stage | Example |
|---|---|
| Connect | "What made you decide to take this call today?" |
| Discover | "Does any of what you've described feel like it points toward a specific root cause?" |
| Clarify | "How does that compare to how you thought about the problem coming in?" |
| Expand | "How much more painful does this feel now that we've put a number to it?" |
| Commit | "If we could deliver what we've talked about, how close to a yes would that get you?" |
| Resolve | "Now that we've addressed that, how are you feeling about moving forward?" |

## When Would NOW  [Commitment]
**Summary**: Makes the present the most attractive time to act
**Structure**: `When would now be a good time to {desired action}?`
> ****

##### When would now be a good time to {desired action}?

##### Examples
- When would now be a good time to start increasing revenue?
- When would now be a good time to start recovering that lost opportunity?
- When would now be a good time to build the business?
- When would now be a good time to create the future you want?
- When would now be a good time to hire the help you've already determined you need?
- When would now be a good time to begin saving money? After you've become financially free?
- When would now be a good time to start creating content? After you've built an audience?

#### Why It Works

##### "Now" Is Already in the Question
The structure presupposes that "now" is on the table.
We're not asking "should we do this?" We're asking "when would now be a good time?"

##### Buries the Real Question
The real question is: "Will you do this?"
"When would now be a good time?" sounds like a scheduling question. But it functions as a close.

##### Creates Urgency Without Pressure
Direct urgency feels like demand: "You need to decide today."
This question creates urgency through structure, not pressure.
The word "now" is there, but the question is still soft. The listener doesn't feel pushed but "now" has been planted.

#### What It Accomplishes
- Creates urgency without applying direct pressure
- Presupposes that action will occur
- Surfaces hidden objections about timing
- Buries the close inside a softer scheduling question
- Moves the conversation from "if" to "when"

#### Conversational Ladder Applications

| Stage | Example |
|---|---|
| Commit | "When would now be a good time to explore whether there's a solution worth investing in?" |
| Decide | "When would now be a good time to get started?" |
| Resolve | "When would now be a good time to move past the hesitation and make a real decision?" |
