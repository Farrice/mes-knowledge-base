# Fladlien Pattern Bank — Future Pacing (4 patterns)

---

## Begin → Find  [Future Pacing]
**Summary**: Creates a positive outcome associated with the action you want to occur. 
**Structure**: `As you begin {action}, you'll find yourself {beneficial realization/state}.`
> ****

#### As you begin {take action} you will find yourself {feeling a certain way} 

##### Examples
- As you begin publishing your ideas, you'll realize how much expertise you've been keeping to yourself.
- As you begin raising your prices, you'll attract clients who value outcomes over discounts.
- As you begin documenting your processes, you'll remove yourself as the bottleneck.
- As you begin addressing objections, you'll stop being surprised by them.
- As you begin publishing, you'll build confidence that can't be created through preparation alone.
- As you begin showing up, you'll remove much of the pressure you've been placing on yourself.
- As you begin focusing on the solution, you'll spend less time feeding the problem.
- As you begin considering the long-term impact, you'll place less emphasis on the short-term inconvenience.
- As you begin considering what's possible here, you'll notice how small today's obstacles look compared to tomorrow's opportunities.

#### Why It Works

##### 1. Presupposes Action
Rather than asking whether someone will take action, it assumes they already have. 
Compare:
- "Will you start publishing content?"
- "As you begin publishing content..."
---

##### 2. Uses Accepted Ideas to Smuggle In Bigger Ones
The mind naturally evaluates statements from left to right.
When the beginning of a statement feels believable, resistance often decreases toward what follows.
For example:
> As you begin publishing content...
Most people can imagine themselves doing that.
Then:
> ...you'll notice how easy and natural it feels...
Still fairly believable.
Then:
> ...which allows you to create the best content you've ever produced.
Now we're making a much larger claim.
But instead of presenting that claim directly, we've attached it to two ideas that were already accepted.
The larger outcome effectively arrives under the protection of the smaller ones.
This is why hypnotic language often starts with simple, observable, or easily accepted experiences before moving toward more ambitious suggestions.
The mind tends to follow a chain of agreement further than it will leap to a conclusion.
---

##### 3. Chunks Down Commitment
The word "begin" implies:
- Small
- Easy
- Temporary
- Low risk
- Reversible
Instead of asking someone to become an expert, transform their business, or change their life, you're only asking them to begin.
This dramatically lowers resistance.
---

##### 4. Shifts Attention from "Should I?" to "What Happens Next?"
The listener stops debating the action and starts mentally experiencing the future.
---

##### 5. Creates Future Pacing
The listener mentally rehearses a future experience before it occurs.
They imagine:
- Taking the action
- Experiencing the feeling
- Receiving the result
This makes the future feel familiar and believable.
People are naturally more comfortable moving toward experiences they have already mentally rehearsed.
---

##### 6. Creates Cause-and-Effect
Humans naturally look for causal relationships.
The structure:
> As you begin X, you'll notice Y.
automatically encourages the brain to connect the two.
Without explicitly proving the relationship, the mind starts treating the action as the cause and the result as the effect.
---

##### 7. Presents the Outcome as an Observation Rather Than a Claim
Direct claims invite argument.
For example:
> This program will make you confident.
By contrast:
> As you begin using this system, you'll notice confidence.
sounds like an observation someone discovers for themselves.
Observations generate far less resistance than assertions.
---

##### 8. Uses Experience Instead of Logic
Many versions of this pattern use experiential language:
- Notice
- Discover
- Realize
- Find yourself
- Become aware
These words direct attention toward experience rather than analysis.
---

##### 9. Becomes a Self-Fulfilling Prophecy
Once you tell someone what they are likely to notice, they begin looking for evidence of it.
---

##### 10. Creates Immediate Gratification
Many worthwhile outcomes are delayed.
This pattern inserts an immediate reward.
Instead of:
Action → Months of work → Reward
The pattern creates:
Action → Feeling → Reward
For example:
> As you begin publishing, you'll notice confidence...
The confidence arrives immediately rather than months later.
This increases motivation and reduces resistance.
---

##### 12. Feels Like Discovery Rather Than Instruction
People resist being told what to think.
They rarely resist discovering something for themselves.

## Looking Back  [Future Pacing]
**Summary**: Uses the future self to uncover insight, action and priorities in the present. 
**Structure**: `Looking back after {desired success}, what do you think made the biggest difference?`
> ****

##### Looking back after {desired success}, what do you think made the biggest difference?

##### Examples
- "Imagine it's two years from now and you've hit every goal we talked about. Looking back, what do you think the turning point was?"
- "If this problem is fully solved by next quarter, what do you think you'll say made the difference?"
- "Looking back after you've doubled the business, what do you think you'll say you had to stop doing to get there?"
- "If next year is the best year your company's ever had, what decision do you think you'll be most glad you made?"
- "When this is behind you, what do you think you'll wish you had done sooner?"

#### Why It Works

##### Uses the Future Self to Inform the Present
The pattern temporarily places the listener at a point after they've already succeeded.
From that vantage point, looking back makes the decision feel not just possible but inevitable.
It's no longer "will this work?" but "what made it work?"

##### Bypasses Resistance by Skipping the Decision
We assume success and ask about what caused it.
The listener has to engage with the future rather than argue with the decision.

##### Creates Insight About What's Required
When someone describes what they think "made the difference," they often name the very thing we're offering.
Or they name the qualities they need to show up with: commitment, speed, focus.
Either way, we learn how to help them succeed.

##### Activates the Feeling of Success
By asking the question, we cause the listener to briefly simulate having achieved the goal.
That simulated success is emotionally real. It creates desire for the actual outcome and provides a preview of what's possible.

#### What It Accomplishes
- Uses a future perspective to inform present action
- Bypasses current hesitation by assuming success
- Surfaces what the prospect believes is required to succeed
- Creates emotional preview of the desired outcome
- Reveals how the prospect thinks about their own success
- Makes the path to action feel natural and inevitable

#### Variations
- "Five years from now, what decision do you think will have mattered most?"
- "Imagine this is already solved. What do you think got you there?"
- "When you've beaten this problem, what would you tell someone who's where you are today?"

## Sooner or Later  [Future Pacing]
**Summary**: Assumes success within a choice of timelines 
**Structure**: `I don't know if {positive outcome soon} or {positive outcome later}`
> ****

##### I don't know if {positive outcome soon} or {positive outcome later}...

##### Examples
- I don’t know if you’ll see {success} right away or if it will take a bit longer…
- I don't know if the breakthrough comes after the first campaign or the fifth campaign, but most people are surprised by how much they learn once they start.
- I don't know if the biggest benefit will be the money, the time saved, or the stress removed
- I don't know if you'll notice the change immediately or if it sneaks up on you over time.
- I don't know if you'll trust yourself more after one success or after 3, 4 or 5 successes
- I don't know if you'll find your voice quickly or if you won’t even need to find it in order to win
- I don't know if you'll discover your best product intentionally or accidentally.
- I don't know whether the lesson arrives through repetition or experience.
- I don't know whether the old story fades all at once or piece by piece.

#### Why It Works

##### Both Outcomes Are Positive
The pattern removes the possibility of "no outcome."
The only question is when the positive outcome arrives - sooner or later.
The listener's mind stops evaluating "will this work?" and starts wondering "how quickly will it work?"

##### Admitted Uncertainty Builds Trust
When we say "I don't know if...", we signal honesty.
We're not overselling. We're acknowledging that timing varies.
That admission makes everything else we say more believable.  

##### Creates Curiosity About the Outcome
"I don't know if you'll see it in the first campaign or the fifth..." naturally makes the listener wonder: which will it be for me?
That curiosity keeps them engaged and personally invested.

#### What It Accomplishes
- Presupposes success within a range of timelines
- Builds trust through admitted uncertainty about timing
- Creates curiosity about which outcome the listener will experience
- Handles timing-based objections before they arise
- Keeps both fast and slow outcomes positive
- Makes future success feel inevitable rather than conditional

## When You Notice  [Future Pacing]
**Summary**: Directs attention to future evidence of progress or change.
**Structure**: `When you notice {future evidence of progress}, you'll {immediate realization}.`
> ****

##### When you notice {future evidence of progress}, you'll {immediate realization}.

##### Example
- When you notice how much more confident you've become, you'll probably struggle to remember why you doubted yourself so much.
- When you recognize how much trust you've developed in yourself, you'll appreciate how it was built one decision at a time.
- When you look back at today's challenges, you'll probably laugh at how big they once seemed.
- When you notice how much you've learned, you'll probably forget how confusing this once felt.
- When you realize you're operating from instinct rather than effort, you'll know mastery has begun.
- When you see yourself responding differently to situations that once controlled you, you'll recognize the change.
- When you look back at your early fears, you'll be amazed at how many never happened.
- When you notice that the old belief no longer has the same grip on you, you'll wonder why it felt so permanent.
- When you notice the results, you'll probably forget how much time you spent worrying about the decision.

#### Why It Works

##### Directs Attention to Evidence of Progress
The pattern plants a specific thing to look for.
Once we tell someone what to notice, they start looking for it. And when they find it, the pattern becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy.
The noticing creates the realization.

##### Shifts from Doubt to Discovery
Instead of "will this work?", the listener starts asking "when will I notice this?"
That's a completely different mental posture. One is defensive. The other is curious.
Curiosity is far more useful than doubt when we want someone to move forward.

##### Creates Future Confirmation
The pattern builds in its own proof.
When the listener eventually does notice what we mentioned, the experience confirms everything we said.
It makes us look prescient. It builds trust retroactively.

##### Links Small Evidence to Big Realizations
We pair something small and observable with something meaningful.
"When you notice how much you've learned, you'll probably forget how confusing this once felt.”
The small thing becomes evidence of the big thing. The listener doesn't have to take our word for it, they just have to notice.

#### What It Accomplishes
- Plants specific evidence to look for
- Shifts attention from "will this work?" to "when will I notice this?"
- Creates a self-fulfilling confirmation loop
- Builds future trust by predicting what the listener will experience
- Connects small observable evidence to larger meaningful realizations
- Makes positive change feel inevitable
