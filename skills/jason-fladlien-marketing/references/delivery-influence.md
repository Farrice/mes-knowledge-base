# Delivery influence: runtime reference

Source: `extractions/jason-fladlien/delivery-influence-2026-09-09/`. Read `source-ledger.md` for timestamped support and `uncertainty-report.md` for evidence limits. Full report stays cold.

Choose the medium before choosing technique. Diagnose one weak point: relevance, reception, demonstration, adaptation, or ending. Use only the mechanics needed to repair it. Preserve truthful claims, supplied examples, voice, price and reader agency.

- **D01 Earn relevance before biography** (0:59): Open with the receiver’s usable outcome; demonstrate competence before listing credentials.
- **D02 Invite participation with a real job** (2:54): Use a question that checks understanding or elicits evidence. In text, replace crowd response with a concrete self-check. Agreement is not purchase consent.
- **D03 Rehearse the portable sentence** (4:39): Build a precise, memorable sentence from the mechanism, then prove it. Spoken work may repeat; text gets one strongest version.
- **D04 Prepare receptivity before the payoff** (6:10): Establish the unresolved decision before giving the answer. Curiosity must pay off in a usable distinction, not a larger promise.
- **D05 Repeat through variation** (7:12): Change examples or delivery emphasis while preserving the core rule. Avoid anaphora as default written decoration.
- **D06 Renew relevance at transitions** (9:13): At a topic change, translate the lesson into the receiver’s actual task. State what varies and what survives.
- **D07 Set up each deliverable before the offer** (11:16): Map each offer component to a demonstrated need. Use actual cases or labeled hypotheticals; do not invent winners and losers.
- **D08 Prepare modules; adapt their placement** (11:43): Know the material in modules; adapt sequence to explicit questions. Do not fake spontaneity or claim a prerecorded aside arose live.
- **D09 Let the demonstration carry authority** (13:54): Show an actual transformation of the material. For copy, rewrite a sentence; for video, show the work; for sales, diagnose a supplied example.
- **D10 Challenge with audience-fit warmth** (16:56): Challenge a supported assumption with language appropriate to the receiver. Warmth can be plain acknowledgment; jokes and profanity are optional.
- **D11 Separate the room from its customers** (19:40): Distinguish the audience’s stated objective from end-customer evidence. Label any third-person scenario; never diagnose an unspoken fear.
- **D12 Teach before inviting paid help; shrink the promise** (22:39): Make the free step genuinely usable. Offer a bounded paid outcome whose scope and proof are visible.
- **D13 Replace conventional wisdom with a demonstrable possibility** (24:44): Name a real assumption, its limits, and the evidence for an alternative. Steady tone cannot upgrade uncertain claims.
- **D14 Use disruptions as live material** (26:17): Prepare recovery branches: acknowledge disruption, preserve the point, continue. In edited media use a genuine visible event, not fabricated live feedback.
- **D15 Build a concrete demonstration and ask permission to extend** (29:36): Choose a safe, inspectable demonstration relevant to the claim. Acknowledge useful work, offer an optional extension, respect a no.
- **D16 Remove friction before adding capability** (33:05): Reuse the smallest sufficient asset; make the benefit tangible before expanding the offer. Use callbacks only to events that actually occurred.
- **D17 Plan the ending before optional material** (34:38): Build a complete main ending and, if needed, a complete encore ending. Attach aspiration to one executable next action.

## Medium translation

| Medium | Preserve | Translate or omit |
|---|---|---|
| Written post/email/page | Relevance, concrete example, small supported promise, real action | Replace call-response with one useful self-check; no applause cues, fake room, forced repetition or invented spontaneity |
| Recorded video/podcast | Spoken rhythm, visible demonstration, a complete ending | Intentional pauses are suggestions, not measured source timings; viewer can pause but cannot answer live; no invented responses |
| Live presentation/sales | Permission, relevant questions, modular examples, recovery and optional encore | Use observable signals; silence means unknown, not fear; a no ends that invitation |
| Audit | Weak-link diagnosis, minimal repair, evidence limits | Do not rewrite everything or award causal effectiveness from craft checks |

## Output proof

Always return a finished artifact, then concise source IDs, what changed, what remains unsupported, and the smallest appropriate outcome test. Example scenario labels stay outside public copy. Do not add business promises or fabricate personal experience to improve the prose. Spoken excitement must match the user's register; quiet confidence is valid. The default is substance and clarity, not Jason impersonation.
