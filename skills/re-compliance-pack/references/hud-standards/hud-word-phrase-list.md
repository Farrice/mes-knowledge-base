# HUD Advertising Word/Phrase List (24 CFR §100.75)

**Authority**: 24 CFR §100.75 — Fair Housing Act Prohibited Bases in Advertising  
**Source**: HUD Fair Housing Act & Real Estate Advertising Guidance (2016); Fair Housing Center case law  
**Last Updated**: 2026-07-14  

---

## TIER 1: DIRECT VIOLATIONS (RED)

### Race & Color (Direct Bans)

- Segregated
- Integrated / integration
- Mixed
- [Race/ethnicity name] community
- [Race/ethnicity] neighborhood
- "Suitable for [race]"
- "Ethnic" (when used to exclude; if used positively in self-referential context, review)
- White / Caucasian (in neighborhood descriptor context)
- Colored / People of color (outdated, avoid in MLS)

### Religion (Direct Bans)

- [Denomination] community (Christian, Jewish, Muslim, Buddhist, Hindu, etc.)
- [Religion] neighborhood
- "Suitable for [religion]"
- "Perfect for [religious group]"
- "Kosher community" / "Halal area" (if implying religious exclusivity; factual amenity OK)

### National Origin & Language (Direct Bans)

- [Country/region] community
- [Nationality] neighborhood
- "English-speaking only" / "English preferred"
- "Bilingual required" / "Spanish-speaking area" (in exclusionary context)
- "[Accent] area" or "[accent] neighborhood" (e.g., "Latino accent")
- "No [nationality]" / "[Nationality]-free"

### Sex/Gender & Familial Status (Direct Bans) — HIGHEST VOLUME

**Age + Family targeting:**
- Perfect for young families
- Ideal for families
- "Great for families"
- Families welcome / Children welcome (implies others not welcome)
- Family community / Family neighborhood
- Young professional community
- Ideal for retirees
- Perfect for retirees
- Elderly community / Senior community (if exclusive)
- "Perfect for singles"
- Young couples preferred
- "No kids" / "Children not suitable"

**Explicit household-type language:**
- Young professionals only
- Newlyweds
- Couples (if excluding families)
- Bachelor / bachelorette (in community descriptor, avoid)

### Disability (Direct Bans)

- "Not suitable for [disability]"
- "Wheelchair inaccessible" (must disclose factually; avoid lifestyle language)
- "Not ADA compliant" (factual OK; phrasing matters)
- "Requires [physical ability]"
- "Physical ability required"
- "Not for the disabled"

### Sexual Orientation

- Straight-friendly (implies exclusion of LGBTQ+)
- "No gay" / "No LGBTQ+"
- (Rare in listings; flag if appears)

## TIER 2: CASE-LAW-DOCUMENTED CAUTIONS (YELLOW)

### Race & Color (Dog-Whistle Codes)

**Established case law: *Fair Housing Council v. 1734 East 82nd Street* (9th Cir. 2019)**

- **Safe neighborhood** (when used to differentiate from other neighborhoods; implies race-based concern)
  - Context matters: "Safe area with police presence" (OK, factual). "Safe compared to X neighborhood" (RED)
  - Flag YELLOW unless clearly factual/comparative

- **Quiet area** (case-law established as race-coded; also disability-coded per *Fair Housing Center v. Sears*)
  - "Quiet on street level" or "Quiet cul-de-sac" (OK, descriptive)
  - "Quiet neighborhood" in isolation (YELLOW, educate)
  - Paired with family language (RED)

- **Ethnic restaurants / Diverse dining** (when used as *sole* neighborhood descriptor)
  - "Fine dining and cultural events" (OK, neutral)
  - "Ethnic restaurants nearby" as only amenity (YELLOW, may imply race-based targeting)

- **Up-and-coming area** / **Emerging neighborhood** (gentrification language, risky if demographic-inflected)

- **Good investment** (risky if paired with demographic language; standalone usually OK)

- **Charming** (can code for "gentrified" or "where a certain demographic is moving out"; review context)

### Religion (Implicit Codes)

- **Spiritual** (if attached to amenity or community descriptor, YELLOW)
- **Conservative** (if used in moral/values context, risky; if political, dangerous)
- **Traditional** (context-dependent; if paired with exclusionary language, RED)

### National Origin (Implicit Codes)

- **International community** (if implying non-English speakers, YELLOW)
- **Bilingual nearby** (documents non-English speakers; factual, but YELLOW in deed-restriction context)
- **UNESCO World Heritage** / **Old World charm** (review context; usually OK if referencing actual aesthetics)

### Familial Status (Implicit Codes)

**Established case law: HUD Guidance (2016); *United States v. Newberry* (4th Cir. 1999)**

- **Walking distance to schools** (YELLOW; educational amenity OK, but flag if paired with family language)
  - Compliant rewrite: "Walking distance to Lincoln Elementary and Central MS" (specify schools)
  - Non-compliant context: "Walking distance to top schools — perfect for families" (RED)

- **Playground nearby** / **Parks for children** (YELLOW; "Parks nearby" alone is OK)

- **Daycare nearby** / **Preschool nearby** (YELLOW; educational descriptor OK, but implies family preference)

- **Sports fields** / **Recreation for kids** (YELLOW if specifically youth-focused; "Parks and recreation" OK)

- **Family-oriented community** (YELLOW; especially if paired with "where families thrive")
  - Compliant: standalone "family-oriented" with neutral features
  - Non-compliant: paired with age language or household-type exclusion

- **Neighborhood schools** (YELLOW if paired with family language; "Near schools" alone OK)

- **Backyard** (YELLOW if used to target families; "Large outdoor space" OK)

- **Quiet area** (YELLOW — case law establishes this as familial-status coded; families need quiet, disabled may not)

### Disability (Implicit Codes)

**Established case law: *Fair Housing Center v. Sears* (8th Cir. 2009)**

- **Active lifestyle community** (implies disabled not welcome)
  - Compliant: "Community recreation center" or "Amenities" (neutral)

- **Requires climbing stairs** (OK if factual; avoid lifestyle language like "requires active climbing")

- **No structural modifications allowed** (factual disclosure OK; may violate reasonable-accommodation duty if exclusionary)

- **Quiet area** (implies disabled persons' needs incompatible)

- **High-energy area** / **Fast-paced community** (implies disabled/elderly not welcome, YELLOW)

## TIER 3: BLUE IMPROVEMENTS (Optional Rewrites)

### Weak Descriptors (Improve Specificity & Credibility)

- "Beautiful" → "Recently renovated" or "Hardwood throughout"
- "Lovely" → "Charming" or specific feature (e.g., "built-in shelving")
- "Nice location" → "Walking distance to downtown" or "Cul-de-sac location"
- "Great schools" → "Lincoln Elementary (9.2/10), Central MS (8.8/10)"
- "Excellent schools" → Specific school names + ratings
- "Perfect neighborhood" → Specific features (parks, shopping, transit)
- "Safe area" → "Gated community," "Neighborhood watch," "Low crime per [source]" (if factual)

### Vague Amenities (Add Precision)

- "Close to everything" → "Walking distance to downtown, retail, dining"
- "Convenient location" → "5 min to highway, 10 min to shopping center"
- "Great for entertaining" → "Large deck" or "Open floor plan"
- "Community feel" → "Established neighborhood with parks and gathering spaces"
- "Peaceful setting" → "Tree-lined street" or "Cul-de-sac" (if true)

## COMPLIANCE DECISION TREE

### For Every Phrase, Ask:

1. **Does it name a protected class?** 
   - YES → RED violation
   - NO → Continue to #2

2. **Is it case-law documented as problematic (with citation)?**
   - YES → YELLOW (contextual review needed)
   - NO → Continue to #3

3. **Does it exclude or imply preference for a household type?**
   - YES → Likely RED (rewrite)
   - NO → Continue to #4

4. **Is it a factual statement (e.g., "stairs to entry")?**
   - YES → Usually OK; consider neutral reframing
   - NO → Continue to #5

5. **Is it a lifestyle assumption?**
   - YES → YELLOW or RED (context-dependent)
   - NO → Proceed to BLUE (optional improvement)

---

## Rewrite Heuristic: "The Inclusion Test"

**Rewrite question for every phrase:**
> "Would a [different household type] feel welcome in this property based on this language?"
>
> Examples:
> - "Perfect for young families" — Would a retiree feel welcome? NO → RED
> - "Safe neighborhood" — Would a person of color feel equally welcomed (or is this race-coded)? → YELLOW
> - "Walking distance to schools" — Would a childless adult or senior feel equally welcomed? Depends on context → YELLOW
> - "Spacious, multiple bedrooms" — Would all household types feel welcomed? YES → OK

If the phrase would *exclude* a protected class, it's a violation.
