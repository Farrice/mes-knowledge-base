# Expert Practice OS: Conductor Judgment

## Calibration

This is an orchestration spine, not a new expert persona. Its quality comes from refusing false universality and selecting one owner from explicit evidence. Do not narrate a council, blend lane philosophies, or write the offer inside the route receipt.

## Decision Patterns

### Universal Gates, Specific Practices

Packet completeness, claims safety, proof classes, capacity, stage, authorization, and handoff can be shared. Buyer language, positioning, protocol, delivery, evidence, and economics cannot.

**Decision rule:** reuse the gates; reload the practice.

### Achieved Stage Is Not Desired Stage

A revenue ambition does not change proof state. One completed founding engagement remains Stage 0 even if it permits Stage 1 work next.

**Decision rule:** `proof_stage` records what has happened. `next_stage` records what may be attempted.

### One Scalar Lane Owner

The route selects one lane owner. Capacity, POP, protocol IP, and revenue truth may have separate function owners, but they do not become co-owners of the lane.

**Decision rule:** one owner per function slot and one scalar `selected_lane_owner`.

### A Valid Hold Is Success

The conductor succeeds when it stops an unsafe or premature move for the right reason. `STOP_OR_HOLD` can pass verification when it preserves evidence truth and gives a bounded checkpoint.

**Decision rule:** never fabricate a full offer or delivery plan to make a hold look productive.

### Runtime Proof Is Not Market Proof

`RUNTIME_OBSERVED` can show that the system chose the correct route and output shape. It cannot show that a buyer wants the offer, the practitioner can deliver the result, or the economics repeat.

**Decision rule:** keep runtime, demand, delivery, outcome, and repeatability evidence separate.

### Cold Means No Authority Expansion

This folder is loadable by path, but it has no command, public workflow alias, registry entry, global mirror, or client authorization. A prompt catalog is an asset index, not permission to act.

**Decision rule:** fail validation if any route authority appears before approval.

## Decision Rubric

| Criterion | Pass | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| Practitioner | Relevant experience, credentials or credible scope, and exclusions are explicit | Authority or scope missing |
| Protocol | Named steps plausibly match the bounded outcome | Mechanism vague or promise mismatched |
| Buyer | One observable person, problem, present state, and desired state | Broad identity or unobservable problem |
| Offer | Paid unit, scope, price status, terms status, and paid-event gate exist | Free activity or undefined payment event |
| Proof | Evidence classes and actual counters are separated | Source, interest, future events, or permissions inflated |
| Acquisition | One primary path | Several active primaries |
| Capacity | Founder hours and per-unit load are explicit or honestly unknown | Forecast exceeds stated capacity |
| Route | One lane owner and exact rejected routes | Multiple owners or leaked payload |
| Economics | Locked until runtime, inputs, and approval | Model appears early |
| Authorization | Local output only | Outreach, payment, publishing, registration, or client action |

## Anti-Patterns

- **Expert soup:** Andrew, Luisa, Sunny, Dan, Growth, and protocol experts all loaded around one problem.
- **Payload leakage:** giving a life coach the Final 10 offer or an AI consultant a generic coaching cohort.
- **Proof laundering:** using source examples as practitioner or client outcomes.
- **Stage inflation:** calling one successful founding unit a repeatable practice.
- **Activity inflation:** treating interviews, proposals, or free work as collected cash.
- **Scale theater:** generating annual or monthly economics at Stage 0 or before runtime proof.
- **Unauthorized usefulness:** sending outreach, creating payment, publishing, or registering because the route looks ready.
