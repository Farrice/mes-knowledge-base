# The Organic Marketing Taxonomy

Source: "How to Sell More Books on Amazon KDP WITHOUT Paid Ads" (youtube.com/watch?v=CZgZmPd-ETY, 15:41). All view counts and handles frame-verified. Free book marketing has exactly one path worth walking: **high-volume short-form video, TikTok-first, faceless-preferred, run as a probabilistic volume game.** Everything else (podcasts, Substack, blogging) is "a complete waste of time."

## The 7-type video taxonomy

| # | Type | Composition spec | Effort | Evidence case + views | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ASMR-style (no-talk showcase)** | Background music + text overlay + book-cover showcase; film yourself OR behind camera; ~30 sec | Low | Sean's described default format | Faceless volume filler; calming niches |
| 2 | **Talking-head topic + book pitch** | Speak to camera on one concept from the book, then pitch it; text-overlay hook question | Medium (on-camera) | @theinnerwork — *"Why does my partner get mad when I try to explain why I did something?"* → 154.5K likes, 25.7K saves, 59.7K shares | Author is comfortable on camera / has a teaching angle |
| 3 | **Book-showcase behind camera** *(his stated best converter)* | Hold book to camera, show cover, talk about it; no editing | Very low | Jeffrey Mason "Mom/Dad, I Want to Hear Your Story," wood-stump shot → **10M views** | Always — highest-ROI format he's found |
| 4 | **Page flip-through / interior** | Turn camera on, flip pages, narrate; no exercises, no editing | Very low ("easiest video to create") | @heeryourstory unboxing → "millions" | Journals / prompt books; fastest to shoot |
| 5 | **One-page-read** | Point at / read ONE page aloud; hand + pen on interior | Very low | Shadow Work Journal inner-child wound page → **70M views** (871.6K likes, 152.9K saves, 96.3K shares) | Unlock the 1-page=1-video engine; best-selling journals |
| 6 | **Coloring / interior process** | Color a page on camera OR simpler flip-through | High (or outsource) | Coco Wyo "Only 14 Fridays Left til Halloween" → 28K–106K/video | Coloring-book niche |
| 7 | **AI image carousel** | Auto-scrolling Midjourney images + numbered text overlays; CTA "comment"; link in bio | Low, fully online | Hayden Fox Media @haydenfoxmedia "10 responsibilities… by age 10" → **34,000 saves** (113.9K likes) | Refuse to show yourself OR the book; children's / list books |

The counterintuitive verdict: the single best-*converting* format (type 3) is the *lowest-effort* one — an inversion of "more production = more results."

> "Literally just showing your book behind the camera. And for whatever reason, it converts really well."

## The volume-game doctrine

Individual video views are near-random — you don't predict winners, you buy lottery tickets in bulk and let the feed surface the hit. **Judge the account, never the video.** Post multiple videos every single day.

> "Most of your videos probably will not get a lot of views. So, it's a volume game… you just never know which video does well."

## Winner-repetition (clone the outlier 10×)

Once the algorithm reveals a hit format, the *format* — not luck — is the asset. Recreate it relentlessly (exploit) while still testing new ideas (explore). Jonathan's sitting-with-book format has "more than 10" versions, "many of them did really well."

> "Once one video does well, you want to kind of repeat the formula as much as possible, but you just want to keep testing new things."

## Faceless > face

The book, not the creator, is the product. Removing the face lowers friction AND (per his data) raises virality — so the highest-leverage format is also the easiest. Jonathan's viral hits are all faceless (4.7M, 1.4M, 861.4K); his face videos average hundreds.

> "Most videos that went viral are ones where he's not even showing his face… simple is best. You don't have to show your face."

## 1-page = 1-video = unlimited content math

Atomize the static product: a 100-page book = 100 videos; when exhausted, restart from page one with new framing → unlimited inventory. Solves the "I'll run out of ideas" fear. The Shadow Work Journal one-page-read hitting 70M is the proof.

> "One page is one video. That means you can create 100 videos from one book… you essentially got unlimited content."

## The repurpose waterfall (TikTok → IG → YouTube)

Marginal cost of reposting an existing clip is ~zero, so every additional platform is free incremental sales even at lower reach. **TikTok first** (highest reach) → repurpose to **Instagram** (fewer views) → repurpose to **YouTube**. Coco Wyo runs all three and "does really well on YouTube."

## The second-person wound-accusation hook

Every viral overlay is a second-person accusation naming a painful self-recognition — weaponizing shame-relief as the scroll-stop. Write text overlays as "you" statements that name a hidden wound. Verified examples:
- *"You say you love your family… but you snap at them, shut down, or overreact… and then hate yourself after"* → **1.4M**
- *"You're not broken. That's your inner child still carrying pain no one helped you process"* → **1.4M**
- *"What if you stopped trying to change people and instead changed how you react?"* → **4.7M**

The overlay text *is* the product — the visuals behind it (sky b-roll or a plain held book) barely matter.

## The monetization ladder

Sequenced by earned proof — graduate only once you have proven video templates delivering consistent views:
1. **Viral spillover / no link needed** — a viral book video creates its own Amazon search demand; viewers hunt the title down. "I've seen this happen multiple times."
2. **Link-in-bio** — once you have followers, add a link to Amazon/website.
3. **TikTok Shop** — once views are reliable; "Amazon KDP goes hand in hand with it."

## The governing trade

Organic is not free — it's a resource swap: you save money by investing a lot more time. Ads (the paid lane) work at **$3–5/day** and are faster; if scaling seriously, run both. Outsourcing the effort (hire a colorist, buy Ohuhu markers, ship pages to an illustrator who films) exits the free-strategy frame — it costs money.

> "You're saving money by investing a lot more time… Or would you rather save time and just pay money to run ads… So, which do you want?"
