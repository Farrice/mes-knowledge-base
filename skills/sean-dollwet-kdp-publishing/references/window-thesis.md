# KDP Window Thesis — Source-Reported Hypothesis

Source: [Sean Dollwet, `NyfGjgs5Q7w`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NyfGjgs5Q7w). The video argues that easier AI production, growing reader demand, and declining creator hype create a temporary KDP opportunity. Its 12–18-month deadline, demand figures, competition conclusion, review effects, income examples, and portfolio cadence are `SOURCE_REPORTED` unless independently verified.

## What survives as an operating method

- Separate creator attention from buyer demand; they need not move together.
- Look for current demand plus a specific execution weakness.
- Compare the whole reader experience: cover, title, sample, content, price, freshness, and reviews.
- Act decisively once evidence is sufficient, but keep quality and policy gates fixed.
- Record current observations instead of treating an old “window” as still open.

## What does not become doctrine

- A countdown or guaranteed closing date.
- AI making world-class books “in minutes for pennies.”
- Declining YouTube coverage proving less marketplace competition.
- A review-count threshold or algorithmic door-close effect.
- Fixed per-book income, catalog size, niche count, or exit value.
- Urgency overriding rights, disclosure, reader quality, or approval.

## Current window scan

For any proposed niche, record:

`captured_at, marketplace, format, query, current sellers, visible rank/review/price, publication freshness, cover/sample/content gaps, reader-problem corroboration, rights/risk, source, uncertainty`

Verdict:

- `OPEN`: current demand and a defensible gap exist.
- `HOLD`: evidence or qualified review is missing.
- `CLOSED/NO-GO`: no credible demand/gap or unacceptable risk.

This verdict applies only to the observed niche and date. It does not authorize a portfolio.

## Book One rule

The first book tests one niche, one reader, and one operating loop. Factory and client lanes remain parked until `NET_COLLECTED` and a learning receipt exist.
