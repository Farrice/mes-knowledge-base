# Book Blueprint — Evidence, Architecture, and Approval

## Pre-Flight Gate

Run only after workflow 01 returns `GO` and the niche checkpoint is approved. Title and metadata changes are constrained after publication, so front-load the decision—but do not describe the title as universally irreversible.

Load `genius.md`, `references/prompt-chain.md`, `references/kdp-policy-and-evidence-boundary.md`, and `references/prompts-v2/book-production-package.md`.

## Execution

### 1. Lock the reader promise

Name one reader, one costly problem, the useful outcome, what the book will not claim, and why this operator can serve the reader honestly. Identify every credential, expert review, interview, or lived-story gap.

### 2. Build the source and claim plan

Create an authoritative source dossier and provisional claim ledger before prose. Prefer primary sources for factual claims. Mark claims `VERIFIED`, `LIKELY`, `UNCONFIRMED`, or remove them. Record access date, source, allowed use, and whether quotation permission is needed.

### 3. Abstract competitor intelligence

Study samples, contents, descriptions, and reader reviews only to identify coverage themes, complaint patterns, category conventions, and unmet reader needs. Do not copy chapter names, wording, examples, stories, distinctive sequencing, screenshots, or substantial expression.

### 4. Architect the book

Build the transformation arc, chapters, sections, claim/source needs, exercises/tools, story/example slots, and word ranges. Size the book by the problem solved. The 10–15k and 30k figures are source-reported planning heuristics, not quality targets.

### 5. Engineer and clear title/metadata

Produce clear, accurate options grounded in real search language and the reader promise. Keep title plus subtitle under 200 characters, match cover/interior metadata, avoid keyword stuffing and unauthorized names/marks, and document Amazon/web/trademark clearance. Capture explicit sign-off.

### 6. Specify human authorship and AI assets

For every planned text, cover, interior, translation, and metadata asset, define creation mode, human contribution, source inputs, disclosure duty, rights basis, and evidence path. Do not promise copyright from an edit pass.

### 7. Create the gold-chapter and cover briefs

Choose a representative chapter that tests voice, factual density, explanation depth, stories/examples, and edit burden. Build a cover brief from category fit and conversion gaps, including thumbnail hierarchy, rights, AI disclosure, wrap geometry, trim, bleed, safe zones, resolution, and metadata match.

Checkpoint: Farrice approves the reader promise and outline before the gold chapter begins.
