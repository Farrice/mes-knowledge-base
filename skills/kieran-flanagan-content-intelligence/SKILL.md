## THE CORE CONVICTION — read this before routing anything

Kieran Flanagan's system works because of **where he places the firewall between AI and human.**

Everything **upstream** of the firewall is *recoverable from the world* — audience psychology, format performance, market signal, research, precedent. It can be retrieved, so it can be delegated.

Everything **downstream** is *unrecoverable* — sentence rhythm, which analogy gets reached for, what is funny, what someone will stake their reputation on.

**The seam sits at outline-to-draft.** AI hands over a fully-loaded outline. The human writes the prose. Always.

> *"I do not use my system to create finished pieces of content. I do not copy and paste from AI."*

**Never draft finished prose on the user's behalf when a capability's output specification says outline.** When AI-assisted output reads as generated, the firewall has drifted downstream. Hold it.

## OPERATING PRINCIPLES — apply to every capability

**1 · Patterns, not topics.** Map ideas to the structural shape that has worked, never to subject matter. Topics decay in weeks; patterns compound for years.

**2 · Triangulate before spending craft.** An idea is worth writing when three signals converge — **proven** (a format that works for this operator), **trending** (live market demand with a real analog), **owned** (a genuine, defensible position). Score all three, show the components, never just the total.

**3 · Audience profile ≠ ICP.** An ICP optimizes for qualification. An audience profile optimizes for *reaction*. Feeding an ICP into a content system is the most common root cause of generic output.

**4 · Evidence currency before argument strength.** Establish what proof-type this audience accepts, then convert claims into it. A weaker argument in the accepted currency beats a stronger one in a discounted currency. Most content is built entirely from the weakest tier — general statistics.

**5 · Saturated ≠ slop.** Separate quality from distribution. A device that is effective but ubiquitous gets three options — use it where this audience isn't saturated, **bend** it (keep the mechanic, shed the fingerprint), or **own** it deliberately. Only genuinely bad devices get cut.

**6 · Label every inference. Never invent a number.** When context is missing, build a working version inline, mark it `INFERRED`, and cap its confidence. Never present a recalled figure as observed, an unverifiable source as verified, or a target as a research finding. **A statistic you cannot source is worse than no statistic**, because it discredits everything around it.

**7 · Grade confidence, including downgrades.** Every context artifact carries a confidence note. Refresh cycles must record what got *less* certain, not only what improved.

**8 · Assistant-agnostic, always.** Context lives in portable plain-markdown artifacts, never in proprietary memory. This hedges platform risk and enables running one context through multiple engines.

**9 · Overstock, then prune hard.** Ideas are cheap. Selection under surplus beats generation under scarcity. Kill without deliberation.

**10 · The corpus is the moat; the prompt is commodity.** Everyone has the same models. Almost nobody has a structured year of their own performance data. Architecture that does not accumulate a corpus does not compound.

## THE 30-DAY PATH

**Day 1** — `/profile` → `/patterns` → `/signals` → `/ideas`. Three context assets and ten grounded ideas in 90 minutes.
**Week 1** — `/queue`, then `/dossier` on your two strongest. **Write them yourself.** Publish. Start the analogy vault.
**Weeks 2–3** — Publish from the queue, never from a blank page. `/pov` once. `/transpose` your best idea across every surface.
**Day 30** — `/refresh`. **This is where the system starts compounding.** Then `/architect` to design what comes next.

**Honest note**: week one is *worse* than ad-hoc prompting — setup costs time and the assets are thin. Roughly break-even by week three. Materially better by month three. Not comparable by month twelve. Anyone unwilling to accept a worse week one should not build this.

## AUTOMATION

Schedule `/signals` weekly — a fresh, saturation-scored market report waiting every Monday is the single highest-value automation available here, because it means you never start a week from nothing.

Schedule `/refresh` monthly. It is the least gratifying step and the one with the highest compounding return, which is exactly why it gets skipped. Put it on a clock rather than relying on memory.

**Automate up to the firewall aggressively. Never across it.**
