# Copy Blocks v2.0 — Deployment Guide (How to Actually Use This)

> For Farrice. Load this before any copy-heavy session. It's not a feature list — it's how to think, ranked by where the leverage actually is for *your* surfaces (your offers, LinkedIn/Parallax, client work, and the way you already use bots).

---

## The one-sentence reframe

**You no longer "write copy." You assemble and tune blocks** — and more importantly, you now have a *shared vocabulary* you can hand to an AI to steer it, and a *diagnostic* to catch where it (or you) went flat. The blocks are the grammar; the bots are the typist; **you are the editor with X-ray vision.**

That's the whole game Luke is playing: the bots are built on copy blocks, so the person who *knows the blocks* writes better copy with AI than anyone writing solo or prompting blind.

---

## The 3 mental shifts that make it work

**1. Stop thinking "is this good?" Start thinking "which blocks are here, and which is weakest?"**
"Good/bad" is a feeling you can't act on. The Two-Move Diagnostic is: *(a) does it have all 6 blocks? add the missing ones. (b) which present block is weakest? strengthen it with CRAVES.* That converts a vague "this feels off" into a specific fix in seconds. This is literally how Luke reviews $2,500/mo client copy.

**2. The architecture must stay invisible.** The reader feels velocity; they never see blocks. The moment your output reads like "Pain → Promise → Proof," you've failed. Throw away the ladder — these frameworks are scaffolding you climb and kick away. If copy looks like it's enumerating a checklist, rewrite it.

**3. Clarity is King, and it beats everything.** Every other rule (velocity, density, power words, idea caricature) bows to comprehension. A confused reader does not buy. When in doubt, strip it back until it just makes sense.

## Highest-leverage plays (ranked for YOU)

### 🥇 #1 — The AI Steering + QA Layer (your biggest unlock)
You already write with bots. The blocks are what let you *steer* them and *course-correct* their output. Two moves:
- **Steer:** give the bot the vocabulary up front — "open with a curiosity block tied to the constraint, hit the core wound indirectly, keep the proof braided." It produces dramatically better drafts when it knows the grammar you know.
- **QA:** run `/copy-block-audit` on any AI draft (yours or a client's). The bot *will* drop the curiosity block or over-proof — the audit catches exactly where, and hands you the rewritten line. **This is the difference between "AI slop" and "AI-leveraged at the top 1%."**
> Make this a reflex: nothing AI-generated ships until it's passed the Two-Move Diagnostic.

### 🥈 #2 — Building & sharpening your OWN offers ($2–5K urgency)
This is where copy blocks touches revenue most directly. Before writing a single sales line:
- Run `/copy-equation` on the offer itself — `(Promise × Proof × Curiosity) ÷ Constraints × Conditions`. It tells you the *one* limiting factor. Usually it's not "needs more proof" — it's a **value/identity constraint** in the denominator you didn't see.
- Use the **Little Big Idea** (genius.md / constraint-dissolution.md) to name the offer: what they want + what they won't do + what's unique. "Lunch Break Millionaire" energy. A great name does the heavy lifting before the body copy exists.
- `/promise-engineering` to pitch the promise to your market's **identity ceiling**, not the belief ceiling — the #1 reason offers feel "salesy" is promising past identity.

### 🥉 #3 — LinkedIn / Authority Flywheel content
- **Hooks**: `/curiosity-engine` → the Curiosity Quadrant gives you four reliable angles for every topic (*your problem-belief is wrong / the real problem / why everyone's solutions are BS / the one that works*). You will never stare at a blank hook again.
- **The body**: copy velocity. LinkedIn dies on slow blocks — interleave, no block for 4+ lines, fresh angle each reappearance.
- **The whole post**: `/craves-polish` on your hook line before posting. Specific + Visual is where 80% of LinkedIn hooks lose.
> Note the boundary with Parallax: Parallax is memoir-grade interiority — use copy blocks there *lightly* (hooks + curiosity loops only). Don't block-engineer a personal essay; it'll kill the heartbeat. Copy blocks is for *persuasion* surfaces, not literary ones.

## How to think about the blocks (the deep model)

- **Pain & Promise are static** — you don't control them, you *present* them. Move fast here. (But present pain at full depth via the Pain Chain, and remember: psychological pain must be re-triggered with story; physiological pain just needs acknowledging.)
- **Proof, Constraints, Curiosity are where the marketing actually happens** — your flexibility, your differentiation. Spend your art budget here. **Curiosity is the single most important and most-botched block.** If you only get great at one thing, get great at the Curiosity Quadrant + Evocative Naming.
- **Conditions is the multiplier** — but naked conditions sound like everyone else. Always bake curiosity into them.
- **The Belief Bank Account** is the mental model for proof: every claim is a withdrawal, every proof a deposit. Don't drain the account.
- **Identity Runway < Belief Runway** is the master calibration for promises. Memorize this one.

## Anti-patterns (how to NOT waste it)

- ❌ **Running it on Parallax memoir.** Persuasion surfaces only. Personal essays get hooks + curiosity at most.
- ❌ **Visible architecture** — block labels, "CASH," CRAVES surfacing in the actual copy.
- ❌ **Sequential blocks** (Pain→Promise→Proof→CTA in rigid order). Interleave.
- ❌ **Calling the core wound directly** ("Do you feel invisible?"). Weave it, soften it.
- ❌ **Challenging identity/values head-on** in copy. Work around them.
- ❌ **Promising to the edge of belief instead of identity.** Trips the BS detector.
- ❌ **Generic mechanism names** ("hidden root"). Run the portability test.
- ❌ **Reporting a "copy velocity score" to anyone.** It's a fake internal thinking tool. Never client-facing.
- ❌ **Naked critique.** Never say "needs more curiosity" — show the rewritten line.
- ❌ **Boosting the wrong block.** Don't add proof when a value constraint is the real denominator killer. Run `/copy-equation` first.

## Your first week with it

1. **Day 1 — Calibrate your eye.** Take 3 of your *best-performing* past posts/emails and run `/copy-block-audit` on each. You'll see *why* they worked (which blocks, what velocity). This trains the intuition faster than anything.
2. **Day 2 — Run the QA reflex.** Next time you generate copy with a bot, audit the output before touching it. Notice what the bot always drops.
3. **Day 3 — Build one thing from scratch** with `/copy-from-scratch` (a LinkedIn post or a client hook). Watch how the blank page disappears.
4. **Day 4 — Score your own offer** with `/copy-equation`. Find the limiting factor. It probably isn't what you think.
5. **Day 5 — Name something** with `/curiosity-engine` (a mechanism, a framework, an offer). Practice the Little Big Idea.

After a week, stop consciously running the frameworks. **Throw away the ladder.** The goal is that you see the missing curiosity block the way you see a typo — instantly, without thinking "let me check the blocks."

Here's the fixed line."* That skill alone puts you in the top 1% of copywriters — and it's the steering wheel for every bot you'll ever use.
