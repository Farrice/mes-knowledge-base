# The Six Copy Blocks — Deep Reference

## 1. PAIN

You don't control the pain — you present it. Move quickly through pain in the structure, but present it at full depth.

### The Pain Chain (escalation ladder)
1. **General pain** — the overarching description of what's bothering them.
2. **Specific pain** — 3 concrete aspects of that pain.
3. **Cinematic pain** — dimensionalize it: a movie of what it looks like in their actual life.
4. **Emotional pain** — what they *feel* (shame, fear, frustration, dread).
5. **Core Wound** — brief touch on the existential bottom (see below).

> Luke's default VSL sequence: overarching description → 3 specifics → cinematic life-picture → the emotion → brief core-wound touch.

> Hormozi: "The point of good writing is for the reader to understand. The point of good persuasion is for the prospect to feel **understood**." Accurately described pain is cathartic.

### The Pain Matrix (10 dimensions — calibration, not a checklist)
The dimensions shape how a market experiences pain *and* what it responds to. The most load-bearing:
- **Psychological vs Physiological** — the master split. Physiological pain (burning joints) is felt *now*; do NOT over-remind it — they just need to believe you have a new solution that works. Psychological pain (feeling like a social outcast, feeling overweight) is *intermittent* — only felt "at the party." You must **re-trigger it through story and imagination** while they read, because people don't act unless they're currently feeling the pain.
- **Measurable vs not** — money is dollars-and-cents measurable; skincare is not (so always connect a stat like "+25% elasticity" to a concrete *feeling*, or it falls flat).
- **Social stigma** — informs how directly you can approach it.
- (+ others; full set in the AI Avatar Machine training.)

**Strategic use:** the Matrix tells you whether a market you're *swiping from* matches the market you're *writing to*. Swiping a physiological structure (drama/horror story open) into a psychological market — or vice versa — falls flat. It keeps you in the ballpark and rules out bad swipes.

### The Core Wound
The one deep existential fear at a market's heart — its expression of **death, annihilation, abandonment, total loss**. There's usually *one* dominant theme (sometimes two).
- Skincare → **invisibility** (women go from over-seen to unseen; profound sense of loss).
- Joint pain → **loss of freedom** (trapped in your own body, dependent on others).

**Deployment rules (it's nuclear):**
- Deploy **sparingly and strategically**.
- **Indirect, never direct** — never "Do you feel invisible?" Weave it into stories and themes.
- **Warm up to it** — tap lightly, don't stick a finger in the wound.
- **Softening / hedging language** — "I don't know if you've felt this, but…", "maybe you feel…" — gives non-resonators a graceful exit and lets resonators attach. People resent being told you know exactly how they feel.
- Make it **marketing DNA** — a recurring subtle theme woven into the fabric, not a callout.

## 2. PROMISE

The promise block = the proof block, but the *good* part. It's the foundation of all direct response — the reason anyone reads. Feature it **prominently and early** (don't bury it pages deep building up). Curiosity amplifies *how* you deliver it; proof amplifies *why* they should believe you can.

### The Promise Ladder (mirror of the Pain Chain)
*General → Specific → Cinematic → Emotional → Core Transformation.* Always one **Core Transformation** the market most wants — make it the dominant theme (often an *identity* transformation: "the person you become"). Self-observe: when *you* respond to copy about an identity you'd become, the market does too.

**It's hard to overdo promises.** Most copywriters drastically under-promise / hedge. Err toward too much. (Chris Haddad: "make your promises have balls.")

### Promises With Balls (≠ a bigger number)
A promise has balls when it sits at the far edge of what the prospect believes is possible **for them** *and* is painted with **specificity + emotion + conviction**. The same "$10k/month" can be a loose pencil sketch or an 8K hidden-camera video of their poolside life. Paint it as if there is zero doubt in your mind they'll achieve it.

### The Identity Runway < The Belief Runway (master calibration)
When you make a promise, the prospect's **identity** ("am I someone who could do this?") runs out *before* their **belief** that it's possible. Even with all the proof in the world, "$1M business" exceeds most people's identity. So pitch to the **edge of identity constraints**, not believability.
- Copywriter market identity ceiling ≈ **$10–20k/mo** — promise there even if you can deliver $50k.
- Proof is the *other* lever: stronger proof lets you push the promise further.

### Gradualization
Anchor a believable core promise first, then build a *ladder* for the ambitious segment:
> "This Amazon store takes 2–3 hrs/week and brings an extra $2–3k/mo. Some people, when they see how simple it is, double down and scale to $10k, $30k, even $50k/mo." — opens the pathway without confronting them with the BS-triggering number up front. (Requires real proof of the higher tier.)

## 3. PROOF (compact — deep-dive: `luke-iha-proof-mechanisms`, `luke-iha-proof-ladder`)

**Proof creates the FEELING of truth/trust — not a logical airtight victory.** After pain+promise land, the prospect *wants* it true ("it's your game to lose"). Give just enough to relax and move forward. **Over-proofing reads as trying too hard and lowers trust.**

### The Proof Braid ("Siamese twin")
Every claim carries an intertwined proof element, until the proof is nearly **invisible**.
- **Belief Bank Account**: claim = withdrawal, proof = deposit. Drain it and they disengage without knowing why.
- **Proof-as-Promise**: show a testimonial of someone who already got the result instead of stating "you will" — an implied promise immune to "BS."
- **Never make your claim bigger than your proof** (BS card-game): back claims consistently and they stop second-guessing, buying latitude for later.
- **Proof Balance Scale**: big promise → big proof; small promise → small proof. (Don't cite meteorologists for "the sky is blue.")

### The 5 Categories / 22 Types (compact)
| Category | Types | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Psychological** | technical-jargon drop · candid/damaging admission · explanatory reasoning ("because") · logical *frame* (even broken logic) · compliance · guarantees | **Most underutilized; best pound-for-pound.** |
| **Experiential** | demonstration · testable proof · trials/samples · challenges/contests · comparisons · before/afters | **Hardest to get; most convincing.** Always use when available. |
| **Empirical** | scientific studies | Science-as-truth paradigm. |
| **Credible** | certifications · expert endorsements · media/press · awards · track record | Borrows existing status structure. |
| **Social** | testimonials · case studies · reviews · metrics | What others say. |

**Rules of thumb:** match proof to promise level · harder-to-fake = stronger · experiential is most convincing · psychological is the hidden edge · place heaviest proof at the biggest doubt-node, don't cluster it all at the end.

## 4. CONSTRAINTS

Whatever **blocks the market from moving forward** — not objections you fight. They *want* the solution; clear the path. (Marketers/offer owners can solve constraints at the *offer* level — Hormozi's exercise: list every constraint, solve + prioritize each → irresistible offer.)

### The Big Three (always present)
**Money · Time · Effort.** "Can't afford it / no time / too hard." Ratios vary by market (money-rich+time-poor vs reverse). Address them in the copy *and* the mechanism name.

### The Resonance Hierarchy (how constraints form, bottom-up)
**Experiences → Beliefs → Values → Identity.** Things *crystallize* (harder to change) as you rise.
- *Experience*: dated a girl, she cheated with a tall guy.
- *Belief*: "women love tall guys / I'm not enough."
- *Value*: loyalty, control.
- *Identity*: "I'm someone who can only date soft sevens."

**Rule: don't challenge identity or values in a sales letter — work around them.** You *can* play with **interpretation of experiences** (the soft base). Map the market holistically: their experiences, beliefs, values, identities. (Dating example: marketing "date supermodels" triggers identity constraints for most men — "I'm not good enough" — and handicaps a system that would work for any woman.)

### Dissolving Constraints — the AWE Framework
When you *must* address (not work around) a constraint, dissolve it by working *backward* down the hierarchy to experience-interpretation:
- **A — Acknowledge**: validate the belief, lower resistance. "Most people think X, and I totally understand why — I used to too." (Don't make them feel stupid.)
- **W — Wedge** *(the most important part)*: drive apart the bundled hidden assumptions with a **counter-example** (scientific evidence, anecdote, analogy, historical example) or a **paradoxical question**. People live boxed in either/or realities ("eye bags are genetics," "you have to be a good writer"); the wedge opens a *new option*. *(Bundled assumption example: "eye bags are genetic" = (a) genetics play a role + (b) genetics is the ONLY thing that matters. The wedge separates (a) from (b).)*
- **E — Elaborate**: expand the new option into a full new belief that fits your message.

> **Paradoxical questions are nuclear** for the wedge — they dissolve belief AND generate curiosity simultaneously.

### Curiosity Dissolves Constraints (the ideal)
Bake constraint-relief into the curiosity mechanism / name:
- "**whisper** command stops dog barking" → value-safe (I don't want to yell), gentle, easy.
- "**lazy** mom's 10-minute kitchen trick" → effort + time safe.
- "works **while you sleep**" → effort + time safe.
- "without expensive gym memberships / strict dieting" → money + effort safe.

## 6. CONDITIONS (the 6th / bonus block — "the infinity stones")

The most powerful block, but requires a real offer to deliver. In a Hormozi-saturated market everyone's conditions sound the same → **blend curiosity INTO the conditions** to differentiate.

> "Get 10–20 agency clients or you don't pay." → "**Former VC partner reveals lead-cloning method** that brings you 10–20 agency clients/month on autopilot — guaranteed, or you don't pay." Same offer, curiosity baked in.

### The 5 Types
1. **Qualifications** — call-out language + criteria. "Attention former athletes who've watched their bodies weaken…", "For ambitious agency owners drowning in $10k/mo client work."
2. **Conversion Triggers** — urgency, scarcity ("9 spots remaining," "price doubles at midnight").
3. **Risk Reversal** — guarantees, money-back, "you don't pay" clauses.
4. **Value Adds** — bonuses, discounts, free offers, rewards.
5. **Terms & Structures** — payment options, financing clauses.

Copywriters often don't control these; offer owners should use as many as the offer allows. Conditions always attack **time, money, risk**.
