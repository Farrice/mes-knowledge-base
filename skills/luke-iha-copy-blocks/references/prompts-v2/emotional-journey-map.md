## Role & Activation

You are mapping the reader's emotional arc across an ENTIRE piece, not one block. The Pain Chain gets a single block emotionally deep; this workflow gets the whole piece sequenced as a felt journey — the ordered list of feelings from first line to CTA, and the specific trigger that fires each shift. Copy that actually lands isn't a stack of blocks; it's a felt journey where each emotion sets up the believability of the next. This is the missing bridge between "I have all 6 blocks present" and "the reader feels pulled through, not informed at."

## Input Required

- **[MARKET/AVATAR]**
- **[ASSET TYPE + LENGTH]**
- **[CORE WOUND]** — the low point of the arc
- **[CORE TRANSFORMATION]** — the high point of the arc
- **[MECHANISM]** — the Curiosity element that pivots the arc from fear to hope
- **[VOICE-OF-CUSTOMER FEELING-LANGUAGE]** (optional) — real emotional vocabulary from the market; build the arc from their words, not generic sentiment labels
- **[DOMINANT EMOTION]** — required; psychological markets need this re-triggered through story/imagination, so it must be known before mapping

## Execution Protocol

**Map the arc — ordered feelings with named triggers.** Lay out the emotional sequence the reader travels, using the standard high-conversion shape as the default (adapt to the specific market — not every piece runs all six beats):
1. **Recognition** ("this is written for me") — trigger: a callout or mirrored pain specific. Producing block: Pain (general/specific).
2. **Descent** (the ache sharpens) — trigger: cinematic dimensionalizing plus the softened core-wound tap. Producing block: Pain (cinematic → emotional → wound).
3. **Curiosity/Hope-crack** (the pivot — "wait, there's a way?") — trigger: the wedge, a paradoxical question, or the evocative-named mechanism. Producing block: Curiosity (plus Constraint dissolution if relevant).
4. **Relief/Belief** ("I could actually trust this") — trigger: proof-as-promise, the feeling of truth. Producing block: Proof.
5. **Desire/Identity-fit** ("this is who I'd become") — trigger: the Promise Ladder pitched to the identity edge. Producing block: Promise.
6. **Resolve/Urgency** ("act now — it'd be dumb not to") — trigger: curiosity-baked conditions. Producing block: Conditions.

For every beat used, name the SPECIFIC trigger explicitly — a feeling with no named trigger is a wish, not a design.

**Assign and check continuity.** Confirm every emotional beat maps to a producing block, with no orphans (a felt shift with no block causing it) and no emotionally-silent blocks (a block present but producing zero feeling — dead weight in the piece). Run the continuity check on every adjacent pair: does beat N genuinely make beat N+1 believable? You cannot jump from Descent straight to Desire without the Curiosity/Proof bridge — skip it and the reader won't feel the turn, only notice the topic changed. Mark the pivot point (fear → hope) explicitly — it is the single most important transition in the arc and it usually carries the mechanism reveal.

**Write along the arc.** Draft so each block is written to PRODUCE its assigned feeling, not merely convey information: Descent uses heavy Expressive + Visual CRAVES (the full charge); Relief deliberately drops intensity so Proof reads calm and credible rather than desperate. Keep opening velocity high (3-4 blocks in the first stretch) while the emotional through-line itself stays single and clean — structural velocity and emotional clarity are not the same axis and both must hold. Pre-CTA: slow the rhythm so Resolve lands with weight rather than a rushed pitch.

## Output Contract

An internal emotional-arc map (beat → feeling → trigger → producing block, in order, with the pivot point marked), a continuity check confirming every adjacent pair earns the next beat, the finished copy written to produce the mapped arc with zero visible labels, and build notes naming how the dominant emotion was carried and where/how the Core Wound was tapped and softened.


## Quality Gate

- Does every used beat have a specifically named trigger, not a generic "this creates X feeling" restatement?
- Does the continuity check explicitly address EVERY adjacent beat pair, confirming or fixing the earn-the-next-beat logic?
- Is the fear→hope pivot point named explicitly and tied to a real mechanism, not left implicit?
- Are there zero orphaned feelings (felt shift with no causing block) and zero emotionally-silent blocks (present, producing nothing) in the final map?
- Does Relief/Proof read calm rather than desperate — intensity deliberately dropped there, not carried over from Descent?

## Creative Latitude

The six-beat shape is a default, not a requirement — a short ad might run three beats, a long VSL might insert an extra Descent wave before the pivot. What must survive any adaptation is the continuity discipline: every beat earns the next. The real craft is in the trigger language for Descent and the pivot — reach for the market's own voice-of-customer feeling-words rather than generic emotional vocabulary, and let the pivot line carry genuine surprise, not just a topic change signaled by a transition word.

## Deploy When

A piece has all 6 copy blocks present (verified by `copy-block-audit`) but still doesn't "pull" the reader through — that's an emotional-continuity problem, not a block problem. Before drafting any long-form asset (VSL, landing page) where the whole-piece feeling arc matters more than any single block's depth. As the whole-piece companion to the single-block `pain-chain-builder`.
