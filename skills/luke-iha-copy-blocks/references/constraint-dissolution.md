# Constraint Dissolution — Resonance Hierarchy & the AWE Framework

> Deep reference for the Constraints block. Most copywriters can't even *identify* constraints, let alone dissolve them — this is a top-1% edge. Cross-links: belief-dissolution skills (`belief-dissolve-copy`, `drk-belief-dissolve`), `luke-iha-unaware-ads` (awareness levels).

---

## Constraints are minefields, not battles

A constraint is **anything blocking the market from moving forward** — money, time, effort, beliefs, values, identity. The prospect already *wants* your solution; you're not fighting objections, you're clearing the path so they can move toward what they already want. **Default posture: work AROUND constraints, leave people alone, don't trigger them** — especially identity and values. Only dissolve directly when you must.

(Offer owners: solve constraints at the *offer* level — Hormozi's exercise: list every constraint/objection, design a solution for each, prioritize by importance × ease → irresistible offer. Copywriters usually inherit the offer and must dissolve in *copy*.)

---

## The Big Three (always operating)
**Money · Time · Effort.** "I can't afford it / I don't have time / this is too hard." Every market has all three in some ratio (money-rich+time-poor vs. time-rich+money-poor). Address them in copy and — ideally — bake the relief into the **mechanism name** (see "Curiosity dissolves constraints").

## The Resonance Hierarchy (how constraints form)

Bottom-up. We start as blank slates (tabula rasa); life builds the stack:

```
        IDENTITY      ← "I am someone who…"  (hardest to change)
         VALUES       ← "what's right/good for me"
        BELIEFS       ← "what's true"
       EXPERIENCES    ← raw events  (softest, easiest to reinterpret)
```

**Worked formation example:**
- *Experience:* dated a girl → she cheated with a tall guy.
- *Belief:* "women cheat / women love tall guys / I'm not good enough."
- *Value:* loyalty, not being superficial, wanting control.
- *Identity:* "I'm someone who can only date docile, soft sevens."

**Crystallization rule:** the higher you go, the harder things are to change. **Never try to change someone's identity or values in a sales letter** — suicide mission. Map them so you can *work around* them. You *can* safely play with their **interpretation of experiences** (the soft base layer) — that's where dissolution happens.

**Why it matters (dating case):** existing marketing sold "date supermodels." For most men that triggers identity constraints ("I'm not even good enough to date models") — so the system gets handicapped even though it would work for *any* woman. Meeting people at their identity level (not above it) is the move.

**Research implication:** avatar research must surface, per market, the dominant **experiences, beliefs, values, and identities** — past/current/future experiences, beliefs about self / the problem / the market, and identity "I am" statements.

## The AWE Framework (dissolving a constraint when you must)

To dissolve, work *backward* down the hierarchy to the interpretation of experiences. Three moves:

### A — Acknowledge (lower resistance)
Validate the belief first. People don't respond well to having beliefs attacked, even loosely-held ones. Don't make them feel stupid.
> "It's definitely true that genetics play a role…" · "Most people think X, and I totally understand why — I used to believe the same thing."

### W — Wedge (the most important part)
People live boxed inside **either/or realities** ("women don't like short guys," "eye bags are just genetics," "you have to be a great writer"). The wedge drives apart the **bundled hidden assumptions** and opens a *new option*. Tools:
- **Counter-examples** — "Casanova was ~5'4″, short even for his time." "John Caples, considered one of the greatest copywriters ever, called himself a bad writer his whole life."
- **Scientific evidence / studies** — the identical-twins eye-bag study.
- **Anecdotes, analogies, historical examples.**
- **Paradoxical questions** ⭐ — nuclear: dissolve the belief AND spike curiosity at once.

> **Assumption-splitting (the core mechanic):** "eye bags are genetic" bundles (a) *genetics play a role* + (b) *genetics is the ONLY thing that matters.* The wedge concedes (a) and detonates (b): "two twins, same genetics, same diet/exercise/sun — look at the difference in their eye bags. Turns out scientists found one thing that made the difference, and it wasn't skincare, genetics, or lifestyle…"

### E — Elaborate
Once the wedge has opened a gap, expand the new interpretation into a full new belief that fits your message — then hand off to the mechanism/solution.
> "…so it's something else, more important than raw writing skill. What's that other thing? [my mechanism]."

## Curiosity Dissolves Constraints (the ideal end-state)

The best move isn't dissolving constraints in a separate passage — it's **baking the constraint-relief into the curiosity mechanism / name** so a single line does double duty:
- "**whisper** command stops dog barking" → value-safe (no yelling) + gentle + easy.
- "**lazy** mom's 10-minute kitchen trick that melts fat **without** expensive gym memberships or strict dieting" → effort + time + money safe, all in the name + clause.
- "works **while you sleep**" → effort + time safe.

When curiosity and constraints fuse like this, you've hit the sweet science.

---

## The Little Big Idea (constraints in the core concept)

Fuse the three forces into one idea: **what they want** (promise) + **what they won't do** (constraints) + **what's unique** (curiosity/mechanism).
- *"Lunch Break Millionaire"* — trade in 10–20 min (mechanism/curiosity), without giving up the day job or screen-staring (constraints), to make money (promise).
- *"Wild Card Copywriter"* — make $15–20k/mo without a lot of work (constraints) via a versatile method (mechanism) for the freedom they want (promise).
A great Little Big Idea does enormous heavy lifting before a single body sentence is written.
