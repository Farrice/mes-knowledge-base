# Authority Density Companion: Behavior Proof

## Proof status

This is a frozen synthetic cold-start case. It proves that the companion changes consequential strategy decisions and preserves negative controls. It does not prove market demand, content performance, or current Farrice offer economics.

Source fixture: `references/fixtures/authority-density-health-performance.json`

## Input tested

A health-performance advisor wants funded health and performance brands to buy a synthetic $5,000 Evidence-to-Campaign Sprint. The current position is “science-backed health content.” The existing content mix rewards whatever reaches the most people.

The frozen batch contains:

- four broad myth/trend posts averaging 77,750 views, but only two target-account saves and no qualified replies, calls, proposals, or deposits;
- four Proof-to-Promise posts averaging 7,625 views, with 39 target-account saves, eight qualified replies, three calls, and one proposal;
- four Campaign Decision Room posts averaging 4,950 views, with 35 target-account saves, six qualified replies, three calls, one proposal, and one synthetic deposit;
- two Claim Memo chaos posts with strong early buyer signals but only half the minimum evidence floor.

## Weakness diagnosed

The baseline strategy would crown the broad bucket because it generates roughly ten to sixteen times more views. That would optimize for consumer attention while suppressing the content producing every commercial-quality signal in the fixture.

The baseline position is also interchangeable. “Science-backed” describes a hygiene claim shared by many health creators. It does not identify the buyer's costly decision or the advisor's distinctive judgment.

## Source mechanics used

- authority density over follower volume;
- capability-backed positioning contrast;
- one broad and two narrow content jobs;
- one controlled-chaos slot;
- four fair reps as the minimum evidence floor;
- distinct scorecards for acquisition and authority;
- `KEEP / MODIFY / KILL / INCONCLUSIVE` decisions.

## Output produced

### Authority job

Become the trusted name among funded health and performance growth teams for deciding **what the evidence permits the campaign to promise**, evidenced by target-account saves, qualified replies, buyer calls, proposals, and deposits.

### Decisive positioning contrast

**Category default:** explain health research or label claims “science-backed.”

**Controlling contrast:** turn evidence into the strongest commercially useful promise a brand can responsibly ship.

**Position:**

> The claim boundary is the creative brief. We turn the sentence a study can actually support into the strongest promise a health brand can responsibly take to market.

**Why it is defensible:** the position depends on source judgment, claim-boundary discipline, commercial translation, and the ability to show the decision trail. It cannot be copied by adopting a visual style.

**Supporting contrast:** show the decision, not just the conclusion. Every piece resolves to approve, rewrite, kill, or need more proof, with the evidence boundary visible.

The other five source lenses remain research notes. They are not surfaced as a completed positioning grid because they do not change the buyer's choice in this case.

### Authority portfolio

| Job | Series | What it proves | Primary measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad acquisition | **Claim Court** | Can identify an expensive misconception in language the category recognizes | qualified target-account reach and follows |
| Narrow authority A | **Proof to Promise** | Can translate a study into permitted promise, forbidden leap, and usable angle | saves, qualified replies, referrals |
| Narrow authority B | **Campaign Decision Room** | Can make the approval decision a founder or growth lead faces before launch | calls, proposals, deposits, attributed action |
| Chaos | **Claim Memo** | Imports the investment-memo mechanism: bull case, bear case, proof boundary, next evidence | entry into a four-rep core-bucket trial |

### Four-rep decisions

- **Broad: modify.** Reach is real; authority evidence is absent. Keep the category-recognition job, but rewrite the CTA, examples, and framing toward brand operators. Do not scale the current version.
- **Proof to Promise: keep.** It clears the evidence floor and generates qualified trust plus a proposal despite lower reach.
- **Campaign Decision Room: keep.** It produces every synthetic deposit in the fixture and exposes the commercial decision directly.
- **Claim Memo: inconclusive; continue to four reps.** Early signals are promising, but two posts cannot earn promotion under the approved floor.

### Next batch change

1. Rebuild the broad series around a brand-side consequence: lost claim, delayed launch, unusable angle, or evidence gap.
2. Keep both narrow series and test two new opening mechanisms without changing their job.
3. Run two more Claim Memo experiments before any promotion decision.
4. Instrument target-account follows, qualified replies, calls, proposals, deposits, and attributed revenue separately from views.

## Behavior delta

| Decision | Baseline | Companion |
|---|---|---|
| Winner | Highest-view broad posts | Narrow posts that produce qualified trust and commercial action |
| Positioning | Generic “science-backed” claim | “The claim boundary is the creative brief” with visible approve/rewrite/kill judgment |
| Broad bucket | Scale because reach is high | Modify because fit and authority evidence are missing |
| Narrow buckets | At risk of being killed for lower views | Kept because they perform their declared authority and commercial jobs |
| Chaos slot | Promote after two promising posts | Continue until the four-rep floor is met |
| Scoreboard | Engagement blended together | Reach, fit, trust, and commercial action remain separate |

## Validation

- The fixture contains four reps for every core bucket and only two for chaos.
- The expected decisions reject the obvious vanity-metric false positive.
- The position uses one controlling and one supporting contrast, not a seven-axis checklist.
- The synthetic price, deposit, and scenario are labeled as proof-case inputs rather than market facts.

## Remaining risk

Human taste has not yet rated this revised proof. The position may still be too consultant-shaped, the series names may not fit Farrice's voice, and the synthetic signals do not establish real buyer demand. Live promotion proves reusable behavior; market proof still requires real exposures, qualified replies, proposals, deposits, or payments.
