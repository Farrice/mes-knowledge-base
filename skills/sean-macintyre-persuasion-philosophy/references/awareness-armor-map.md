# Awareness-Armor Map — Audience State Taxonomy + Decision Tree

The single most important diagnostic in Sean's framework. Before any copy is written, the audience's state must be classified. The state determines which tools work and which fail. Mismatched copy fails not because the writer is bad — it fails because the *tool was wrong*.

This reference is the lookup table for Workflow 01 (`/sean-armor-diagnose`) and the decision input for nearly every other workflow.

---

## The Three Armor States

### State 1: Problem-Aware (No Armor)

**The reader wakes up wanting the thing.**

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Recognition signal** | The reader can articulate the problem in their own words before encountering your copy. They Google solutions. They have a budget. They're shopping. |
| **Sean's example** | *"I take dick pills every day. But you know, I didn't wake up out of bed being like, man, I wish my dick worked better. And that is what motivated me to get them. So it didn't take a whole lot of copy to get me there. It was my own inability to satisfy people as a man that got me there. I was very problem-aware is what I'm trying to say."* |
| **Schwartz mapping** | Awareness levels 4-5 (product-aware → most-aware); sophistication levels 1-2 |
| **What works** | Direct value propositions. Hormozi-style value equation. Money models. Problem-Agitate-Solution at low sophistication. Specific benefit claims. |
| **What fails** | Sophisticated mechanism leads (overshooting). Long story-driven setups (the reader wants the thing already). Contrarian frames (no need — they're not defended). |
| **Length appetite** | Short to medium. Reader is shopping; long copy may be friction. |
| **Proof appetite** | Moderate. Some social proof, guarantee, risk-reversal. Doesn't need to overwhelm skepticism. |
| **Workflow recommendations** | Hormozi-style direct value, Cardinal Mason short-form copy, voice/tonality skills. Sean is overkill here. |

---

### State 2: Defended (Armor of Defense)

**The reader is on the cusp of recognizing the problem; wary of being sold.**

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Recognition signal** | The reader has *suspicion* of the problem but resists naming it because acknowledging it implies they need to act, change, or admit fault. Has been burned by previous solutions. |
| **Sean's example** | A copywriter making $5K/mo who suspects they could be making $20K but doesn't want to admit their current path is a local maximum, because admitting it means leaving the plateau. |
| **Schwartz mapping** | Awareness levels 2-3 (problem-aware → solution-aware); sophistication levels 3-4 |
| **What works** | Mechanism leads (provides intellectual permission to engage without admitting need). Contrarian frames ("the lie everyone else is telling you"). Belief-dissolve copy. Identity-respecting reframes. Pattern interrupts that bypass the defensive crouch. |
| **What fails** | Direct urgency (triggers defenses). Scarcity tricks (read as manipulation). Hard CTAs without sufficient earned trust. PAS in any obvious form. |
| **Length appetite** | Medium to long. Reader needs intellectual satisfaction before emotional commitment. |
| **Proof appetite** | High. Reader is looking for reasons to dismiss; proof must withstand digging. |
| **Workflow recommendations** | Sean's mechanism-test (Workflow 02), genealogy attack (Workflow 04), three-vector idea forge (Workflow 13). Stack with Luke Iha proof ladders. |

---

### State 3: Apathetic (Armor of Apathy)

**The reader walks past every billboard. Has built emotional callus to advertising.**

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Recognition signal** | The reader treats advertising as background noise. Pop-ups dismissed reflexively. Email subject lines pattern-matched to spam in 0.3 seconds. They're not defending — they're not even *listening*. |
| **Sean's example** | *"Most people walk through life with an armor of apathy, ignoring the advertisements around them."* — and Sean's *They Live* reference: people walking past billboards that, if visible, would say "OBEY" / "CONSUME" / "NO INDEPENDENT THOUGHT." |
| **Schwartz mapping** | Sophistication level 5 (market completely jaded; previous frames exhausted) |
| **What works** | Story-driven leads with three-vector idea (emotional + intellectual + personal ignition). Identity-disruption ("This is for the person who…"). Classical rhetoric (litotes, antithesis — patterns that bypass the apathy filter because they don't sound like ads). Pattern-violations (unexpected framing that breaks scrolling rhythm). |
| **What fails** | Almost every standard direct-response technique. Direct value props. Mechanism leads (reader doesn't care enough to evaluate). Even Hormozi-style fails — the value-equation logic assumes a reader who's evaluating, not one who's tuning out. |
| **Length appetite** | Variable. Either *very* short (a single line that breaks the pattern) or *very* long (story that earns continued attention). Medium-length copy dies — too long to be ignored, too short to earn engagement. |
| **Proof appetite** | Highest. But proof must be *narratively integrated* — proof dumps fail because the reader has tuned out before reaching them. |
| **Workflow recommendations** | Sean's three-vector idea forge (Workflow 13), classical rhetoric deploy (Workflow 14), post-hook architecture (Workflow 15). Stack with Wright residue, Roth visual prose for narrative integration. |

## The Diagnostic Decision Tree

Use this when you're not sure which state applies:

```
START
  │
  ├── Did the audience Google "[product category] reviews" or "best [product type]" recently?
  │     ├── YES → State 1 (Problem-Aware)
  │     └── NO  → continue
  │
  ├── Has the audience tried 2+ solutions to this problem and been disappointed?
  │     ├── YES → State 2 (Defended)
  │     └── NO  → continue
  │
  ├── Does the audience encounter your category of advertising daily but can't name a specific brand?
  │     ├── YES → State 3 (Apathetic)
  │     └── NO  → continue
  │
  ├── Would the audience be surprised to learn the problem applies to them?
  │     ├── YES → State 3 (Apathetic; needs ignition)
  │     └── NO  → State 2 (Defended; needs permission)
```

**If the tree returns ambiguous results**: lean toward the more defended state. The cost of treating a problem-aware audience as defended is *slight overshoot* (some unnecessary mechanism setup). The cost of treating a defended audience as problem-aware is *complete bounce-off*.

## Sub-State Variations

For higher-resolution diagnostics, sub-categorize within each state:

### State 2 sub-states (Defended)

| Sub-state | Distinguishing trait | Best tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Burned-defended** | Lost money/time on previous solutions | Mechanism-substance proof + risk reversal heavy |
| **Identity-defended** | Acknowledging the problem threatens self-concept | Identity-respecting reframe; "the lie X told you" reveal |
| **Sophistication-defended** | Has heard every claim in this market | Contrarian frame + classical rhetoric |
| **Status-defended** | Solution would imply they're not as advanced as they think | Pattern of "for people who already know X, here's what they're missing" |

### State 3 sub-states (Apathetic)

| Sub-state | Distinguishing trait | Best tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Saturation-apathetic** | Drowning in ads in this category | Three-vector idea + narrative integration |
| **Category-apathetic** | Doesn't believe the category solves real problems | Genealogy attack — show the category's depth |
| **Trust-apathetic** | Has decided the category is full of scams | Anti-guru positioning; ethical-fork transparency |
| **Energy-apathetic** | Tired of evaluating; just wants someone trusted | Authority/track-record signal + simple mechanism |

## Common Diagnostic Errors

### Error 1: Confusing self-described intelligence with armor state.

A reader who says *"I'm a smart consumer; I see through marketing"* is **probably in State 2 or 3**, not problem-aware. The self-description is itself a defense pattern.

Sean's quote on this recursion: *"Everybody nods their head in the info space or the marketing space and they all say, 'We see the matrix. We're the 1% that sees the matrix.' [...] It's the same 99% / 1% in our space [...] It's the same thing constantly. Even though you know the trick, you know that the shiny object is a magic trick — you still want the trick."*

### Error 2: Assuming defended = sophisticated.

A reader can be defended (burned by previous solutions) without being sophisticated (knowing the market deeply). Defended-but-naive readers are common in info products and supplements. They need: mechanism + proof, but in *simple* language. Don't load the copy with industry jargon.

### Error 3: Treating apathy as resistance.

Apathy is *not* resistance — resistance requires engagement. Apathetic readers don't fight your copy; they don't see it. Treating them as resisters and using "objection-handling" copy fails because there's no objection to handle. The first job is *ignition*, not refutation.

### Error 4: Mixing armor states in long-form copy.

A 70-page promo's reader may *enter* in State 3 (apathetic — "yet another VSL"), shift to State 2 (defended — "okay, this is interesting but I'm wary"), and exit in State 1 (problem-aware — "I want this thing"). The copy must architect that progression. Workflow 15 (post-hook architecture) maps the state-transition explicitly.

## How To Use This Reference

1. Before invoking any Sean workflow, classify the audience using the decision tree.
2. If classification is ambiguous, run Workflow 01 (`/sean-armor-diagnose`) to get a structured diagnostic.
3. Pass the classified state into downstream workflows as a constraint: *"This is a State 2 (Defended, identity-sub-state) audience; choose tools accordingly."*
4. For long-form copy, plan the state progression (where in the document the reader transitions from State 3 → State 2 → State 1) and architect the corresponding shifts in tone, density, and mechanism.

**The single most common copywriting failure mode** is using State-1 tools on State-2/3 audiences. This reference is the prevention.
