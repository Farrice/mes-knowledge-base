# Sean Macintyre × Wright — Cross-Domain Residue

## Role

You are running a stack: Sean's cross-pollination engine (David Deutsch model) feeds Wright's residue engineering (memorable-line construction). Each tool alone produces partial output:
- **Sean alone**: cross-domain insight that may decorate copy without producing memorability.
- **Wright alone**: memorable line construction that draws from copywriting's standard repertoire.

The stack: mine a *distant field* for an analogy → render the analogy through Wright's residue protocol → produce a line that sounds like nothing else in the copy and stays in the reader's head days later.

Sean's reference quote applied here: *"What can I learn from music / set theory / history / quantum computing that I can apply to copy to make it better?"* + Wright's residue principle: *the line that lingers is the line that earned the right to be remembered through the rest of the piece.*

**Before executing**:
1. Read `genius.md` § "Genius Pattern 4: Cross-Pollination" + § "Genius Pattern 12: Classical Rhetoric Doctrine"
2. Load Wright residue skill (`skills/wright-residue` and adjacent Wright skills)
3. The piece must already be drafted (Wright residue operates on existing material, not from scratch)

## Input Required

1. **The Existing Piece**: Drafted copy where the residue moment will sit. (Sales page, essay, VSL, post.)
2. **The Theme**: Wright's theme concept — the underlying claim the piece is making (often unstated).
3. **The Audience State**: From Workflow 01.
4. **3-5 Candidate Cross-Domain Fields**: Where you'd consider mining. If unsure, default to: music, military history, set theory, evolutionary biology, theology.
5. **The Stuck Spot**: Where in the piece the line needs to land — usually a section close, a bridge, a callback, or the final beat.

> **Pre-Flight Gate**: Wright residue requires the surrounding piece to *earn* the moment. If the surrounding copy is weak, the residue line will feel detached. Strengthen the surroundings first.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Theme Extraction (Wright layer)

Apply Wright's theme-extraction protocol on the existing piece. The theme is usually:
- A claim the piece is making but hasn't said outright
- A truth the piece is dramatizing
- An idea that, if compressed to one sentence, is the *spine* of the piece

Reference Wright's theme skill for full protocol. Output: the theme as a single sentence.

### Phase 2: Field Selection — Specific to the Theme (Sean layer)

Run Workflow 03 (cross-pollinate) on the theme as the "stuck problem." The output: a single field whose structural similarity maps to the theme.

Examples:
- Theme: *"Real authority is built quietly across years"* → Field: viticulture (wine aging — the years-of-quiet-fermentation analogue)
- Theme: *"Most marketing claims collapse under pressure"* → Field: structural engineering (load-bearing vs. decorative)
- Theme: *"What you tune out, the few who don't tune out exploit"* → Field: signal processing (signal-to-noise discrimination)
- Theme: *"The desire for more never ends — but its direction can be changed"* → Field: evolutionary biology (adaptive vs. maladaptive selection pressure)

### Phase 3: Source-Text Mining

Mine 1-2 specific texts in the chosen field for *images, metaphors, technical vocabulary, or structural concepts* that compress the theme.

Sean's protocol: *"Pick a book at random — go into it with the frame of like 'I have this copywriting project in mind. What can this teach me at the level of the word, the level of the sentence choice, the level of structure, the level of the way the ideas are presented or even the ideas themselves?'"*

Output: 5-10 raw images / phrases / concepts from the field that have *structural* resonance with the theme.

### Phase 4: Wright Residue Forging

Take the raw images and render them through Wright's residue protocol:

**Wright residue criteria** (from his standard skill):
- **Earned**: the line earns its placement — surrounding copy has built the substrate the line lands on.
- **Compressed**: short. The line is denser per word than the surrounding text.
- **Specific**: concrete imagery; no abstractions if they can be replaced with specifics.
- **Auditory**: the line is *heard* as well as read. Rhythm, sound, mouth-feel.
- **Inevitable on second read, surprising on first**.

For each raw image from Phase 3, draft 2-3 candidate residue lines. Apply Wright's compression / specificity / auditory tests.

**Example — theme**: *"Real authority is built quietly across years"*
- Raw image (from viticulture): *vintage years*, *fermentation in oak*, *dust on the bottle*
- Candidate lines:
  1. "The vintage worth opening was alone in the cellar for thirty years before anyone asked for it."
  2. "Authority is what's left after twenty years in the dark."
  3. "There's a reason the oldest bottles are the dustiest."

Wright criteria check on candidate 2: earned (yes, if the piece built up to this), compressed (8 words), specific (the dark = specific image), auditory (rhythmic — three two-beat phrases), inevitable-yet-surprising (yes — common idea, novel framing).

### Phase 5: Placement + Surroundings

Determine where in the piece the residue line goes. Usually:
- **Section close** (final beat of a section)
- **Bridge** (transition from one section to the next, where the line carries the reader across)
- **Callback** (mid-piece reference back to a planted earlier line)
- **Final beat** (last line of the entire piece — the piece's *resonance*)

Then verify the surrounding copy *earns* the line. If it doesn't, edit the surroundings rather than the line.

### Phase 6: Three-Vector Verification + Wright Self-Check

Score:
- **Three vectors**: emotional / intellectual / personal — same as all Sean workflows
- **Wright criteria**: earned / compressed / specific / auditory / inevitable

Both must pass. Vector scores below 7 mean the line resonates only in one register. Wright criteria below pass mean the line decorates rather than residues.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Long-form essay / Substack** | Residue line goes in the final beat. Plant the surrounding image earlier (3-5 paragraphs before) for callback resonance. |
| **Sales page** | Residue line at the end of the lead OR as the framing for the close. State 2/3 audiences benefit most. |
| **VSL** | Residue line at the moment of mechanism reveal. Audio rendering — must work spoken aloud. |
| **Twitter / LinkedIn post** | The whole post is residue. Cross-domain image → 280-1200 char post structured around the image. |
| **Newsletter** | Residue line as the issue's tagline OR the email's PS. The PS placement weaponizes Wright's "final beat" placement at email scale. |
| **Speech / talk** | Residue line is the closing — the line the audience tweets afterward. |

## Quality Gate

- **Criterion 5: Cross-Domain Application** — must be 9+ (the cross-domain insight must produce a specific copy decision, the residue line)
- **Criterion 3: Three-Vector Resonance** — must be 7+ on all three
- Wright self-check — all 5 criteria must pass

Kill-test: 24 hours after reading the piece, can the reader recall the residue line unprompted? If they recall the *idea* but not the *line*, Wright criteria failed. If they recall neither, the field selection was wrong.
