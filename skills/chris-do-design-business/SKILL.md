# Chris Do — Design Business, Premium Positioning & The Kind Way to Sell

Chris Do is an Emmy award-winning designer, founder of The Futur (millions of followers across platforms), and the leading teacher of business skills to creatives — pricing, negotiation, sales, positioning — and creative skills to business people ("left-brainers who want to be creative"). His career pivot came from one business coach (Keir McLaren) whose three sessions took his firm from $2.2M to $3.9M in a year and his close rate from ~20% to ~75%, by replacing "minor league" selling with problem-centered diagnosis. His signature moves: reframe everything (a brand is not what you say it is, it's what *they* say it is), position against the dominant player by turning their strength into a weakness, let price itself do the positioning, and sell by asking questions instead of pitching. This engine deploys that thinking to price premium, win against bigger competitors, close expertise-based work kindly, and build personal brands on authentic self-expression.

## Quick Reference

**Brand & branding (the definitions everything hangs on):**
- A brand is **a person's gut feeling** about a product, service, or organization (Marty Neumeier). It's not what you say it is — **it's what they say it is**. If enough people share the same gut feeling, you have a brand.
- Branding = **impression management**: you can't control feelings, only influence them — through how you show up, speak, behave, and the consistency of it. (Bezos: "brand is what people say about you when you're not in the room.")
- The logo is a **container for meaning**, not the brand. Products are made in factories; brands are made in the hearts and minds of people, at every touchpoint.

**Positioning (Ries & Trout, weaponized):**
- Positioning = the space you occupy in the hearts and minds of customers **relative to your competitors**. It is the art of **reframing**: paint spilled on canvas is worthless; framed on a gallery wall behind velvet rope, it's art. Move the frame, change the meaning.
- The guerrilla move: **make the dominant competitor's strength a weakness.** Scope vs. Listerine ("no more medicine breath" → 0 to ~33% share), 7UP "The Uncola," Apple's "I'm a Mac / I'm a PC," Dunkin' "friends don't let friends drink Starbucks."
- Three steps: (1) identify who you're *really* competing against (who else is the buyer actually considering — don't get aspirational), (2) name their top 3 known traits, (3) **be the anti** — force a choice: the big faceless thing, or the one who stays up worrying about your payroll?
- Purpose of positioning: **reduce or outright eliminate competition.** A company of one doesn't compete on price — it commands a premium. Don't make it easier to work with you; make it *harder* (scarcity signals "I'm worth it").

**Pricing (price communicates position):**
- Chris's ladder: $250 → $500 → $750 → $1,500 → $5,000/hr — each raise solved an overload problem AND repositioned everything below it ("at $1,500/hr the $10K course looks cheap"). Price is a filter: high price attracts believers who act on your advice, repels non-believers.
- **The Friday-6pm-phone-call test:** charge enough that when the client calls at 6pm Friday, you're *happy* to pick up. Charge so much that scope requests don't bother you — measure outcomes, not hours.
- Not worth the number yet? **Close the gap** (upskill, better service, more leniency) — but decide the number first. "Decide and you will be."
- Confidence is really **permission + validation**: "Who's the *they* who said you can't? You see the they in the mirror." Give yourself the permission slip.

**Marketing (Seth Godin, operationalized):**
- Marketing = the generous act of helping people get what *they* want. Sell to people who want to hear from you; don't interrupt people who don't.
- Permission marketing test: is it **anticipated, personal, relevant**? Build a **marketing curriculum** like an educator — broad exciting material first, deeper and higher-value as the relationship deepens; the selling happens throughout.
- Customer journey: **Awareness → Interest → Desire → Conversion → Delight** — most people stop at conversion; Delight is what makes clients evangelize ("you HAVE to work with Mary").
- Value prop = the bridge: **current state → obstacle → desired future state**. Sell benefits (emotional: "sleep better at night," "get your spouse off your back"), not features ("24-hour access"). Psychographics (beliefs, values, opinions) over demographics.

**Sales (the kind way):**
- **Sales is change management:** someone wants something and can't get it. Two questions: what do they want, and what's getting in the way?
- People know money makes problems go away; **spend is proportionate to problem size — relative to the buyer.** Hunt bigger problems, not better pitches.
- Inbound prospect = **already qualified.** You start with goodwill; everything after either builds or erodes trust.
- The prospect has three options: hire you, hire someone else, do nothing. Your job is clarity so they make the best decision for themselves — **align or decline.** Sometimes the best answer is "I don't think you need to do anything at all."
- **SALES = Serve, Ask, Listen, Empathize, Summarize.** Whoever asks more questions is in control of the relationship. Shift: self-centered → client-centered, advising → asking, talking → listening, selling → summarizing.
- The Keir McLaren lesson: above ~$200K, clients can hire anyone in the world — **what got you here won't get you there**; a minor-league strategy in the major leagues is why the win rate collapses.

**Personal brand & creativity:**
- Foundation: **know yourself → accept yourself → express yourself.** You can't perform authenticity with dried-up paint; heal the wound first (therapy is a legitimate business tool).
- Be **bilingual** — speak the language of art AND business (Todd McFarlane). The intersection is the moat.
- Creativity = **remix**: take what's known, make one small tweak (Apple: "No pain. All gain."; Danger Mouse's Grey Album). "Don't reinvent the wheel — reinvent how it's used." You don't learn creativity; you *remember* it (Ken Robinson: we grow out of it).
- **Lean into the weakness** (asymmetric warfare): Johnny Cupcakes' "Short stories from a short guy about short-sleeve T-shirts" only works because he's short. Make your weakness your strength and your competitor's strength their liability.
- "**Your words shape your world**" — self-labels ("I'm not creative," "I'm not funny") become reality; the camera transmits discomfort. Seek progress over perfection; asking "what's the next step?" is usually a stalling tactic — just do the first step.
- Story transcends algorithms: a compelling story is the foundation of every post; hacks are layers on top.

**Story & narrative (with Michael Margolis on The Futur):**
- People never buy the product — they buy the story attached to it, *and the story they tell themselves about what it means*. (The Zippo ladder: 2022 Zippo $15 → WWII Zippo $200 → General Marshall's D-Day Zippo = priceless. Same hunk of metal.)
- **Story vs. narrative:** a story is a closed-loop event (beginning/middle/end); a narrative is an abstract shared belief. The narrative is the Christmas tree; stories are the ornaments.
- **Data is a story of the past; disruption is a story about the future.** Start future-first, then use data to legitimize it. Conviction is the currency — put a stake in the ground (thesis), *then* research to test it. Leading with research is outsourcing conviction.

**Strategy (with Mark Pollard on The Futur):**
- Strategy = **an informed opinion about how to win.** Opinion without information is reckless; information without opinion is a report.
- Find the **organizing idea** — two topics colliding (the Knicks as "anger therapy") that all tactics and design hang from. Four points: problem (the barrier to buying), insight, advantage, strategy.
- Divergent thinking is the cheap part; **the people who get paid the most are good at convergent thinking** — simplify. If your strategy can't survive being said in three words of street language, it isn't clear yet. A clear strategy is a sandbox: small from outside, enormous inside.
