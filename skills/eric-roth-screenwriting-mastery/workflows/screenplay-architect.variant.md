# Screenplay Architect + Subconscious Accumulation

Build a complete screenplay structure using Eric Roth's methodology — theme-first, beginning-end locked, one-word middle, visual prose scene descriptions.

> **Variant**: Adds Step 5.5 (Subconscious Accumulation Architecture) between Scene-Level Architecture (Step 5) and Theme Pressure Test (Step 6). All other steps unchanged.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Theme Discovery (Pattern 2 — Theme-First Compass)

Before any character or plot:

> **What is this movie literally about?** Not the plot — the *theme.*
> Articulate it in one sentence under 12 words.
> Examples from Roth:
> - *Forrest Gump*: "An innocent man witnesses the loss of American innocence"
> - *The Insider*: "The cost of telling the truth in a system that punishes it"
> - *Munich*: "Vengeance corrodes the avenger more than the enemy"
> - *Benjamin Button*: "Time is the one thing no love can defeat"

**Deliverable**: One-sentence theme statement that will serve as your compass for every scene.

---

### Step 2: Beginning-End Lock (Pattern 3 — Beginning-End Architecture)

Write your **first scene** and **last scene** before touching anything else.

For the **opening** (Pattern 12 — The Home Invitation):
- Make audiences feel they've "found a home" worth living in for 2 hours
- Establish sensory immersion — weather, light, ambient sound, time of day
- Introduce the emotional register without explaining it
- Ask: "Would someone want to keep reading past page 2?"

For the **ending**:
- Ask: "What emotional truth does this character arrive at?"
- The ending must feel like the *inevitable destination* of the opening
- Plant a seed in the opening that blooms in the ending

**Deliverable**: Opening scene (2-3 pages of visual prose) + Closing scene (1-2 pages).

---

### Step 3: One-Word Forward Outline (Pattern 14 — One-Word Outlining)

Outline the middle with single words or short phrases. No detailed scene descriptions. Know the next 5-8 beats maximum.

Format:
```
ACT ONE (Setup):
- [opening scene — already written]
- meeting
- first glimpse
- the proposition
- refusal
- catalyst

ACT TWO (Confrontation):
- threshold
- new world
- first test
- the ally
- midpoint reversal
- darkest hour
- decision

ACT THREE (Resolution):
- preparation
- final confrontation
- aftermath
- [closing scene — already written]
```

**Deliverable**: One-word outline spanning three acts (15-25 beats).

---

### Step 4: Character Wallet Construction (Pattern 4)

For every major character, build a psychology wallet:

| Wallet Item | Details |
|------------|---------|
| **Name & Age** | |
| **What they love** (2-3 things) | |
| **What they fear** (1-2 things) | |
| **Neuroses** | Specific behavioral quirks that reveal psychology |
| **Anger triggers** | What makes them disproportionately angry |
| **Giddiness** | What makes them childishly happy |
| **Speech pattern** | Verbal tics, vocabulary level, rhythm |
| **Wallet contents** | Literal: what's in their pocket/purse/phone? |
| **Backstory seed** | One formative event that shaped who they are |
| **Theme connection** | How do they embody or challenge the theme? |

**Deliverable**: Character wallets for 3-5 major characters.

---

### Step 5: Scene-Level Architecture

Expand each one-word beat into a scene description using Visual Prose Cinematography (Pattern 7):

For each scene, write:
1. **Time of day** (chosen for emotional register — see HK-8)
2. **Weather/atmosphere** (sensory immersion)
3. **What happens** (action, not dialogue)
4. **Subtext** (what's really being communicated beneath the surface)
5. **Theme application** (how this scene relates to the compass)

**Deliverable**: 15-25 scene descriptions, each 3-5 sentences of visual prose.

---

### Step 5.5: Subconscious Accumulation Architecture [NEW]

> **Principle**: The audience should never SEE the pressure building. They should only FEEL the ending arrive as if it was always the only destination. This is Roth's invisible craft — why "My momma always said..." lands like a freight train on the 40th repetition, why the feather returns and the audience weeps without knowing why. The skill is not in the payoff. The skill is in the 30 deposits the audience never consciously registered.

After the scene outline is built, run the **Accumulation Pass** — a layer that engineers invisible emotional pressure across the full scene sequence.

**The 5 Accumulation Mechanics:**

1. **Echo Planting** — Identify 2-3 sensory details, gestures, or phrases in the opening scene. These become your echo seeds. Reintroduce them in mutated form across Acts 1-2 (different context, slightly different meaning each time). By Act 3, when the echo returns in its final form, the audience's body recognizes it before their mind does. *Example*: The feather in Forrest Gump. The rattle of a specific car engine. A character's habit of touching their collar when they lie — which they stop doing in the final scene.

2. **Absence Architecture** — Choose one thing the protagonist needs but never asks for directly. Map how that unspoken need appears as a HOLE in each scene — not as presence but as the shape of what's missing. Scenes where a chair sits empty. Conversations where one name is never spoken. Silences that last half a beat too long. The accumulation of absence becomes louder than any declaration. *Test*: If you removed the final scene where the need is either met or permanently denied, would the audience feel something was structurally incomplete?

3. **Behavioral Drift Mapping** — Track one specific behavior of the protagonist across all scenes. It should shift incrementally — so gradually that no single scene contains a "change moment." A character who speaks in full sentences in Scene 1 answers in fragments by Scene 12. A character who holds eye contact begins looking away. The drift is the arc, made invisible. *Rule*: No scene should contain a noticeable shift. The change only becomes visible in retrospect.

4. **Thematic Pressure Points** — In each act, identify the one scene where the theme presses hardest against the character. Not the most dramatic scene — the one where the theme's question becomes most personally unbearable. These three scenes (one per act) form the invisible spine. Everything else is connective tissue. Mark them in the outline. *Test*: If you read only these three scenes in sequence, do they tell the complete emotional story?

5. **Convergence Tightening** — In Acts 1-2, allow characters, storylines, and emotional threads to run in parallel without intersection. In Act 3, begin converging them — not through plot mechanics, but through thematic resonance. Two characters who never met start making the same gesture. Separate storylines begin rhyming. The convergence should feel gravitational, not engineered. *Roth method*: "You start with everything spread out, and by the end, everything has to feel like it was always heading to the same point."

**Accumulation Map Format:**

| Mechanic | Seeds (Act 1) | Mutations (Act 2) | Payoff (Act 3) |
|----------|--------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Echo 1: [detail] | Scene X: [first appearance] | Scene Y: [mutated form] | Scene Z: [final echo] |
| Echo 2: [detail] | ... | ... | ... |
| Absence: [the unspoken need] | Scenes where absence appears | Scenes where absence intensifies | Resolution or permanent denial |
| Drift: [the behavior] | Baseline state | Mid-shift markers | Final state (visible only in retrospect) |
| Pressure Point 1 | [Scene + how theme presses] | — | — |
| Pressure Point 2 | — | [Scene + how theme presses] | — |
| Pressure Point 3 | — | — | [Scene + how theme presses] |
| Convergence threads | [Thread A], [Thread B] running parallel | First subtle rhyme | Full convergence |

**Accumulation Integration Rule**: After completing the map, revisit each scene description from Step 5 and embed ONE accumulation element per scene. Never more than one — the whole point is that each deposit is too small to notice. If a scene already serves subtext, theme, AND accumulation, it's overloaded. Strip the most explicit layer and let accumulation carry it silently.

**Deliverable**: Accumulation Map + revised scene descriptions with embedded micro-deposits.

---

### Step 6: Theme Pressure Test

Review every scene against the theme compass:
- Does this scene apply to the theme? Even obliquely?
- If not — can it be reworked to connect, or should it be cut?
- Are there three consecutive scenes with the same emotional register? Insert contrast.

**Deliverable**: Revised scene outline with theme annotations.

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Theme stated in under 12 words
- [ ] Opening creates a "home" — someone wants to live in this world
- [ ] Ending feels like the inevitable destination of the opening
- [ ] Every scene connects to the theme (even obliquely)
- [ ] Each major character has a distinct voice and full wallet
- [ ] Visual prose present in scene descriptions (not just slug lines)
- [ ] Middle was *discovered*, not over-planned
- [ ] **Accumulation echoes planted in Act 1 and paid off in Act 3** [NEW]
- [ ] **At least one absence thread runs the full screenplay** [NEW]
- [ ] **Behavioral drift is invisible scene-to-scene but unmistakable start-to-finish** [NEW]
