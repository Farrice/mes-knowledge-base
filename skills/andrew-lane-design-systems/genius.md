# Andrew Lane — Genius Context

## Genius Patterns

### Pattern: Vibe Before Specs
Every good brand starts with the feeling it should convey, not the fonts, colors, or imagery. Lane's first deliverable is always a Brand Vibe Report — written instructions establishing the feel — because when the AI (or a designer, or the founder) has emotional clarity, every downstream visual decision becomes coherent instead of arbitrary.
**Execute**: Before touching any visual detail, write 1-2 paragraphs answering "what is the feeling I'm trying to capture, and what should the target customer feel when they encounter this brand?" Iterate the language with the client/AI until the words alone evoke the brand. Only then generate visuals.
**Success Metric**: A stranger reading the vibe report can predict what the brand looks like before seeing a single image.

### Pattern: The Blind-Designer Guidelines Test
After the mood board is approved, Lane has the AI analyze the image and write detailed visual brand guidelines "as if writing an email to my design and marketing team" — with the explicit assumption that the designers will never see any visual reference. This constraint forces self-contained, unambiguous written descriptions of the style.
**Execute**: Prompt: "Analyze this mood board image and write detailed visual brand guidelines as an email to my design and marketing team so they can create on-brand content. Assume they cannot see this or any other visual — the description must be clear and easy to follow with no visual inspiration available."
**Success Metric**: Fresh AI session given only the written guidelines (no images) generates output recognizably in the same style.

### Pattern: One-Graphic Vibe Capture, Unlimited Variations
The mood board's job is to capture the whole vibe in ONE visual graphic. If it doesn't feel right, generate as many variations as needed — the cost of iteration is near zero, so never settle on a first draft. Works for physical products, courses, services, coaching offers, anything.
**Execute**: Feed the vibe report and ask for a complete mood board image (typography, palette, textures, imagery style, product presentation together). Generate 3-5+ variations; react to each in feeling language ("warmer," "less corporate," "more premium") rather than spec language, until one lands.
**Success Metric**: Client/founder reaction is "wow, this is how my brand could look" — immediate recognition, not polite approval.

### Pattern: Opposite-Extremes Probe
When exploring direction, Lane deliberately asks the AI to go "completely opposite" — e.g., after a warm, naturally-premium doodle-grooming direction, he asked for a bold, playful, high-energy luxury-pet version. Both could win; they'd attract different customers. Seeing the extremes reveals the spectrum and makes the actual choice conscious instead of default.
**Execute**: After the first credible direction, prompt "Now go in a completely different direction — show me the opposite style of brand for this." Present both extremes and ask: which customer do we actually want? Then position on the spectrum between them.
**Success Metric**: The founder can articulate WHY they chose their direction and who it deliberately excludes.

### Pattern: Persistent Brand Project (Train Once, Create Forever)
Lane uploads the vibe report + mood board + written guidelines into a persistent AI project/workspace. Every future session opens pre-trained on the brand — no retraining, no re-explaining. Then any request (thumbnails, sales pages, podcast concepts, social feeds) produces on-brand written concepts AND visual mockups in minutes.
**Execute**: After Workflow 01, save the three foundation documents into a dedicated project (ChatGPT project, Claude project, or repo folder). Start every brand task by loading that foundation, then state what you want to create.
**Success Metric**: A branded deliverable concept + mockup produced within one conversation, zero re-explanation of the brand.

### Pattern: Layered Asset Architecture
The mood board is not the deliverable — its extracted LAYERS are. Lane pulls background images, textures, and graphic elements out of the mood board as standalone assets usable across websites, slides, and social graphics. Every element should be reusable; consistency emerges from the component library, and value compounds because future work builds on past extraction.
**Execute**: Prompt the AI to analyze the approved mood board and generate standalone background images/textures from the parts you like (screenshot a region and say "make more background images like this"). File layers by type. Combine with icon libraries (e.g., Noun Project) for fast real-asset assembly.
**Success Metric**: Next marketing task assembled primarily from existing layers — measured in minutes, not design days.

### Pattern: One-Paragraph Photographic Style
Instead of hunting generic stock photos, Lane writes a single-paragraph description of the brand's photographic style. Then any image = one line describing the subject + that paragraph. Result: unlimited custom, on-brand "stock" photos for campaigns, sales pages, and course content.
**Execute**: From the mood board/guidelines, have the AI write one paragraph defining the photographic style (lighting, mood, color grade, composition, subject treatment). Save it in the brand project. Generate images with: "[one-line subject]. Style: [the paragraph]."
**Success Metric**: Every generated photo sits naturally next to the others; no image reads as generic stock.

### Pattern: Business Decisions vs Branding Decisions
Two decision types every founder must make. Business decisions = the brain, logical: what you offer, how you price it, how you deliver it. Branding decisions = the heart, emotional: how you package and sell it — style, voice, energy, personality, the relationship and feelings people associate with you. Same underlying business with different branding attracts entirely different customers. Confusing the two is why "brand work" gets stuck.
**Execute**: Before any brand work, split the founder's existing decisions into two columns. Confirm the business column is settled (offer, price, delivery, story). Then run branding decisions as their own explicit pass: who are we targeting, how do we want to show up, what should people feel?
**Success Metric**: Founder can state their business decisions and branding decisions as separate lists without mixing them.

### Pattern: The Two Question Sets (Escaping the Race to the Bottom)
Without branding, customers ask only commodity questions: best price? features? how fast? return policy? cheaper elsewhere? With branding, they ask emotional questions: do I trust them more than others? does this brand get my values? do I feel good buying? would I tell friends? would I pay more for their version? Branding decisions are what move a buyer from the first list to the second — that shift is where the margin lives.
**Execute**: Audit the brand's current touchpoints against both question lists. Any touchpoint that only answers commodity questions is a race-to-the-bottom liability — rework it to answer at least one emotional question (story, values, founder, feeling).
**Success Metric**: Each major touchpoint (site, packaging, ads, emails) visibly answers at least one heart-question, not just price/feature questions.

### Pattern: AI Mocks Up, Human Executes
Lane explicitly does NOT let AI produce final assets end-to-end — "it just doesn't come out right and I want to have more control." AI's job is concepts, direction, mockups, and layers; the human's job is final messaging, layout, and assembly. The upfront AI work compresses weeks of research/back-and-forth into ~20 minutes, but the craft stays human.
**Execute**: Draw the line per project: AI generates vibe, mockups, variations, and extracted layers; human assembles final pages, refines copy, and controls layout. Never ship a raw AI mockup as the finished asset.
**Success Metric**: Final deliverables show the mockup's direction but exceed its polish; no unedited AI composite in production.

## Hidden Knowledge

### Insight: Written Guidelines Are the Real Brand Asset
**Insight**: The breakthrough in Lane's system isn't image generation — it's that AI excels at deconstructing an image into written instructions for recreating the style. The mood board is a means; the self-contained written description is the durable, portable, tool-agnostic brand asset that makes every future generation consistent.
**Deploy**: Treat the written guidelines as the source of truth and the images as evidence. When brand drift appears in outputs, fix the words, not the pictures. Port the guidelines document to any new tool before generating anything there.

### Insight: Mockups Are Conversation, Not Product
**Insight**: The client value of instant mockups is decision velocity — clients decide in one meeting when looking at pictures what would take weeks of verbal back-and-forth. Mockups are conversation starters that surface which direction the client actually wants; their disposability is the feature.
**Deploy**: In client sessions, generate directions live and let the client react to visuals instead of describing preferences in the abstract. Ask "which of these feels like you?" — never "what fonts do you like?"

### Insight: The Bottleneck Moves From Production to Direction
**Insight**: Once creation is near-free, the scarce skill becomes creative direction — knowing what to make and whether it's right. Lane's framing: the entrepreneur becomes "the creative director, the CEO," working with AI rather than being replaced by it. Getting past the first level of creative thinking unlocks a flood of "I could do this, I could test that."
**Deploy**: Budget time for direction (vibe, spectrum choice, taste calls) as the primary work; treat generation as cheap fill. When output disappoints, diagnose direction clarity before blaming the tool.

### Insight: The Late-Night Library
**Insight**: Lane batch-generates brand asset libraries during low-stakes off-hours ("those late nights when you're bored") so that real working sessions start with assets ready instead of a blank canvas. Separating playful generation time from focused assembly time protects creative energy — every design decision has an energy cost, and low-value hunting drains high-value creativity.
**Deploy**: Schedule deliberate low-stakes generation sessions per brand: fill the layer library, stock-photo bank, and concept folder. Working sessions then pull from the library and never open a generator cold.

### Insight: AI Convergence as Validation (the Dude Woof effect)
**Insight**: Given only the business decisions and founder story of Dude Woof, the AI's first premium direction closely matched the (unpublished, unseen) professional rebrand the founder had spent months on — founder-focused, warm, down-to-earth, naturally premium. Well-grounded inputs make AI converge on what a professional process reaches, which means it can also work in reverse as a cheap validation check.
**Deploy**: When a brand direction already exists, run the method blind from business decisions + story and compare. Convergence = confidence; divergence = a specific, discussable gap in either the direction or the story inputs.
