# Brand Direction Decision Spine

Use this reference when a moodboard must guide a client decision and become a repeatable visual system. It extends Andrew Lane's feeling-first method with source-grounded decision traceability; it does not replace the Brand Vibe Report, the opposite-extremes probe, or the blind-designer test.

## The spine

`discovery evidence -> design challenge -> internal exploration -> client direction set -> selection and borrowing -> proving surface -> reusable rule -> applied touchpoints`

The chain is only as strong as its weakest handoff. If a direction cannot name the evidence or explicit taste decision behind it, it is decoration. If it cannot survive a real application, it is not yet a system.

## 1. Separate exploration from commitment

### Internal exploration board

The internal board is a working research surface. It may be broad, messy, and highly categorized, but it is not indiscriminate.

Categories may include:

- photography and imagery;
- typography;
- color and light;
- texture and material;
- composition and spatial rhythm;
- graphic devices;
- effects, finishing, or motion behavior;
- cultural or historical references.

For each retained reference, complete the Reference Role Card below. Remove anything kept only because it is attractive.

### Client direction board / stylescape

The client board is a commitment surface: sparse, coherent, legible in seconds, and built to support a decision. It should show only the references needed to make one strategic argument. Recoloring, cropping, and resizing are editorial tools. Visual size communicates decision weight.

Do not send the internal board as the client board. Research volume and decision clarity are different jobs.

## 2. Reference Role Card

For every dominant client-facing reference, record:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Role | Is this proving imagery, type, color, material, composition, device, effect, or cultural context? |
| Borrowed quality | What exact quality is useful: lighting, crop, density, rhythm, texture, contrast, historical signal, or something else? |
| Design challenge | Which evidence-backed challenge does this help answer? |
| Decision weight | Is this a dominant commitment, supporting cue, or contextual footnote? |
| Implication | What must change in design, production, photography, motion, or copy if this is chosen? |
| Cost / rights | Does it imply a shoot, custom fabrication, illustration, licensing, or another material expense? |
| Rejection test | What would make this reference misleading or unusable? |

A caption should protect intent: it says what to notice and what not to copy. Never imply that the whole reference should be imitated.

## 3. Build genuinely distinct directions

Create three or more client directions that take different positions on the design challenges. Changing only fonts and colors is not a different direction.

Each direction card states:

1. the design challenge(s) it answers;
2. its strategic argument in one sentence;
3. the emotional and functional consequence;
4. three to seven dominant Reference Role Cards;
5. what the direction deliberately excludes;
6. its likely production or cost implication;
7. the first touchpoint that can prove or disprove it.

Include an opposite-extreme direction when the decision space is narrow or the client is defaulting to the familiar.

## 4. Record selection and borrowing

Approval is not merely "we like board B." Record:

- the selected direction and why it best answers the challenges;
- elements borrowed from rejected directions;
- elements explicitly rejected and why;
- unresolved taste choices owned by the client or founder;
- any factual, legal, production, or budget risk still open.

This makes the next designer's job a continuation of a decision, not a reinterpretation of taste.

## 5. Prove before systematizing

Apply the chosen direction to the highest-consequence first surface: a homepage, hero module, flagship package, campaign key visual, physical space, or equivalent. Choose the surface named in discovery, not the easiest mockup.

Test:

- Does the audience understand the intended position without explanation?
- Does the brand mechanic remain visible under real content constraints?
- Do typography, imagery, and composition behave as one system?
- Which reference-derived choices collapse in application?
- What must be revised before becoming a rule?

Only after this test should tokens and guidelines be locked.

## 6. Direction Decision Ledger

Use one row per material decision:

| Evidence / taste owner | Design challenge | Direction | Reference role + borrowed quality | Selected / borrowed / rejected | Proving-surface result | Reusable rule | Applied touchpoints | Open risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The ledger preserves two kinds of truth without confusing them:

- **Evidence-backed**: grounded in research, business constraints, or observed audience behavior.
- **Taste-owned**: an explicit founder/client creative judgment that cannot be proven by research alone.

## Failure modes

- One giant Pinterest board is used for research, approval, and production.
- References are selected by attraction rather than named function.
- Three boards are cosmetic variants of one idea.
- Captions describe an image instead of protecting the intended borrowing.
- Reference scale is treated as decoration rather than emphasis.
- Expensive art-direction implications are hidden until production.
- A direction is turned into tokens before being tested in context.
- Guidelines erase the reasoning trail, leaving future makers to reverse-engineer taste.
