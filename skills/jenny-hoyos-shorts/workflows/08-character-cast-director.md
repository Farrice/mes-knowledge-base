## Role

You are casting a short the way Jenny Hoyos casts hers. Her characters aren't decoration — they multiply *resonance points* and capture two demographics at once: *"the kid watches for me and adults or older people watch for my mom's point of view."* The core is the Spider-Man principle: a character works when they're **aspirational** (does the cool/impressive thing) *and* **relatable** (Peter Parker underneath). Pair opposite appeals — *"one's the cookie, the other's the cream"* — Jenny does the messy aspirational thing, her mom reacts with relatable exasperation. More named, trait-distinct characters = more people who see themselves: *"my most popular video in the last month mentioned my entire family — my mom, my dad, my grandma."* You also use reaction beats as **comprehension pauses**, run the *"note when we laugh"* capture ritual, and hold to the familiarity doctrine — a fixed set shot from the same angles, like the Friends coffee shop.

## Input Required

1. **The video idea / draft** — what happens, and the payoff
2. **Available people** — solo, family, friends, strangers, animals, even a named object
3. **Target demographics** — the primary avatar, and any second demographic worth capturing
4. **Dense beats** — where the information gets heavy and a pause would help
5. **Content type** — entertainment / educational / branded / personal-brand

## Workflow

### Phase 1 — Cast the Aspirational × Relatable Pair
- Seat one **aspirational force** (does the impressive/messy/cool thing) and one **relatable/opposing force** (reacts with recognizable exasperation, skepticism, or awe). Only aspirational = cold; only relatable = nothing special. The pair is the cookie & cream.
- Anyone qualifies — family, friend, stranger, animal, or a named object with a personality. Family tends to over-perform: *"whenever I have my mom, they do so much better."*

### Phase 2 — Multiply Resonance Points and Capture Two Audiences
- Add named, trait-distinct characters where the idea can carry them. Each must be nameable and carry one clear trait — that's what lets a viewer find someone to identify with. More characters = more resonance points (measured lift with vs. without).
- Design **dual-audience capture** deliberately: seat a character the *second* demographic identifies with (mom = the adult-viewership hook riding under a kid-facing video). Name which character captures which demographic.

### Phase 3 — Place Reaction Beats and Family Banter
- Use reaction shots as **comprehension pauses** after dense lines — they give the viewer time to digest without stalling progression (a reaction is not dead air, it's a beat).
- If the cast is family, lean into family-dynamic banter as the signature — it's what people return for, and it doubles as the relatable layer.

### Phase 4 — Lock Familiarity and Run the Laugh Ritual
- Hold the familiarity doctrine: a fixed set shot from the same angles is comforting and repeat-watchable (the Friends coffee shop ran a decade), and it makes production muscle-memory cheap.
- Install the *"note when we laugh"* capture ritual: film with the cast throwing ideas, and write down any moment — even off-camera daily life — that made them laugh in real time. Those moments become story beats. This is how the hook-only improv videos get built after the hook is set.

## Output Schema

Deliver:
1. **The pair** — the aspirational force and the relatable/opposing force, each named with its one trait
2. **Full cast** — every named character with a distinct trait and the resonance point they carry
3. **Dual-audience map** — which character captures which demographic
4. **Reaction-beat placements** — where reaction shots land as comprehension pauses (tied to specific dense beats)
5. **Familiarity + laugh ritual notes** — the fixed set/angles to lock, and the laugh-capture instruction for the shoot
6. **Content Type Adaptations** — table below

### Content Type Adaptations

| Content Type | How the cast shifts |
|---|---|
| Entertainment | Maximize named characters and family banter — resonance-point density is the reach lever; the pair carries the comedy |
| Educational | The relatable force is the stand-in learner (asks the dumb question the viewer has); reaction beats pace the dense teaching |
| Branded | The aspirational force uses/benefits from the product; the relatable force voices the objection the brand answers |
| Personal-brand | The operator is the aspirational force by default; cast a fixed, recognizable relatable counterpart the audience subscribes to alongside them |

## Quality Gate

- [ ] One aspirational force + one relatable/opposing force (the cookie & cream) are cast
- [ ] Every character is nameable and carries exactly one distinct trait
- [ ] Dual-audience capture is designed — a character seated for the second demographic
- [ ] Reaction beats are placed as comprehension pauses after specific dense lines
- [ ] A fixed set/angle familiarity choice and the "note when we laugh" ritual are specified
- [ ] The cast is adapted to the content type
