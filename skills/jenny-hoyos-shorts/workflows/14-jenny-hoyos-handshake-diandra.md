# `/jenny-hoyos-handshake-diandra`: Jenny Hoyos × Diandra Escobar Handshake

*Wave 3 crossing, Hoyos matrix score 4.0, Diandra Wave 2 bench graduate.*

The compound output is a **retention-beat map**, not a post, not a hook. It's a diagnosis of where a LinkedIn read-through would gain or lose a viewer if it were graphed the way Hoyos graphs a YouTube Short. Diandra's system then converts that diagnosis into **structural rules**: where the rehook goes, where the payoff sits, what format or length the post needs. Hoyos supplies the physics of attention loss and gain, mapped from a 30-60 second video onto a scroll-feed read. Diandra supplies the LinkedIn-native mechanisms those physics get expressed through: semantic lanes, save architecture, rehook placement, first-line discipline. This is a sequential handshake, not parallel stacking. The beat map is the input the structural spec is built from, never a co-equal lens applied after the fact.

## Stacking Partners
- **Jenny Hoyos** (jenny-hoyos-shorts) supplies **retention physics translated to scroll-feed reading**: cold-open (the first two visible lines before the "…see more" fold function as her first-frame/first-3-seconds), but/so chains (line-to-line pull vs. flat "and then" listing), dip diagnosis (where a long post's read-through would show a retention-graph cliff or slow-slope decay), and payoff spacing (what triggers a save or comment the way her lasting-payoff triggers a rewatch). She decides WHERE attention is being lost or held, and names why.
- **Diandra Escobar** (diandra-escobar-linkedin-growth) supplies the **LinkedIn structural system** the diagnosis gets expressed through: semantic lanes (Pattern 15), rehook mechanics (workflow 20, Pattern 21 five-format hook system), save architecture (workflow 18, Pattern 16), and headline/first-line discipline (workflow 17, Pattern 14 the 60-token audition). She decides HOW the diagnosed beat gets built into the post's structure.

**Split**: Hoyos diagnoses; Diandra structures. Retention-beat map in, structural spec out, never the reverse. A structural spec built without the beat map first is generic LinkedIn best-practice wearing retention-graph vocabulary.

## When to Use
- A LinkedIn post or draft is underperforming, or you want to pre-flight one before publishing, and the diagnosis should come from where attention actually drops in a read-through, not from a generic "hooks are weak" guess
- A long-form post (multiple paragraphs, a narrative arc, a teaching sequence) risks a slow-slope read-through decay and needs its structure engineered for where readers bail, not just its opening line polished
- You have Hoyos' retention mechanics loaded from short-form work and want the same rigor (dip diagnosis, payoff spacing, loop physics) applied to a completely different medium, instead of re-deriving LinkedIn best practices from scratch

## Handoff Contract
**In**: a LinkedIn post or draft (published-and-underperforming, or pre-publish). Full text, plus engagement data if diagnosing a published post (impressions, likes/comments/saves, follower count).
**Hoyos' output**: a retention-beat map. Beat-by-beat classification of the read-through: cold-open verdict, but/so vs. and-then per transition, dip zones with cause named a beat *before* the drop, payoff placement audit.

**Out**: a tagged structural spec. Never post copy, never a co-authored body.

## Fused Sequence

### Step 1: Retention-Beat Map (Hoyos leads)
Run her analytics-triage order (`03-diagnose-retention.md`), translated from video seconds to post lines/paragraphs:

- **Cold-open verdict**: what's visible before the "…see more" fold (roughly the first 2-3 lines, ~200 characters on mobile) is the LinkedIn equivalent of her first 3 seconds. Does it carry a curiosity gap (a contradiction, an unanswered question, a specific number), or is it throat-clearing? Score PASS/FAIL the way she scores viewed-vs-swiped. If it fails, the deep-read diagnosis below is secondary; the fold itself is the confirmed primary failure.
- **But/so chain audit**: walk every paragraph transition. Mark each **but** (conflict), **so** (consequence), or **and-then** (flat addition, no pull). Flag any run of 2+ consecutive and-then transitions. That's a slow-slope decay zone in her graph vocabulary, the post equivalent of a video with no progression mechanism.
- **Dip diagnosis**: for any paragraph or line where a reader would plausibly stop scrolling or reading (not "the post feels long," a specific, nameable point), scrub 1-2 sentences *before* it, never at it. Name the trigger using her own catalogue, mapped to text: conclusional language arriving too early, the payoff or insight leaking before the structure earns it, a confusing or re-parse-forcing sentence (her "hookie hook" instinct, cleverness costing clarity), or a promise made in the cold-open that the body hasn't paid off yet.
- **Payoff-spacing audit**: is the core insight or answer positioned so nothing meaningful trails it (her ending audit: nothing follows the answer)? Or does a CTA, an over-explanation, or a hedge sit after the line that should be last?
- **Loop/rewatch check translated to comment-bait**: does the closing line loop back to the cold-open's gap (resolves what line 1 opened), or plant a second open question? Either is a legitimate close. A close that does neither is a dead ending in her framework.

### Step 2: Beat-to-Structure Fusion Map (the handshake point)
For each diagnosed beat, name the Diandra-system lever that structurally answers it. This is the fusion logic, not two diagnoses run side by side:

| Hoyos Beat/Diagnosis | What It Signals | Diandra Structural Lever |
|---|---|---|
| Cold-open verdict | Whether the pre-fold text earns the "see more" click | Workflow 20 (5-Format Hook Architect) + workflow 17 (First-50): format selection, pixel-width budget, 60-token AI audition |
| But/so chain (pull) | Paragraph transitions that hold attention | Body-First writing (Pattern 6): the transition IS the body; a structural rule can require a conflict/consequence word at a flagged and-then junction, never a rewritten sentence |
| And-then run / slow-slope decay | A stretch that would read as flat, undifferentiated middle | Semantic-lane/scatter check (workflow 19 logic, applied at paragraph level): is this stretch carrying more topic than one beat can hold, or does it need a structural break (line break, bolded sub-point, short-sentence punch per Pattern 19 pixel-width) |
| Dip zone (named trigger, cause a beat before) | Where a rehook or structural interrupt is needed | Rehook placement: a structural instruction (insert a pattern-interrupt line, a bolded restatement, a shortened sentence) at the position the cause was found, using workflow 21's failure-mode vocabulary (throat-clearing, buried lead, vague/clever) if the cause matches |
| Payoff-spacing audit | Whether the save/comment trigger is intact | Save-Worthy Content Architect (workflow 18): reference-value and completeness rules; payoff must land before any CTA, matching her "nothing follows the answer" ending audit exactly |
| Loop-back / open-question close | Whether the ending drives a comment | Boomerang Effect Orchestrator (workflow 05) if an open question is the right close, loop-back to cold-open if resolution is the right close |

### Step 3: Structural Spec Build (Diandra leads, Hoyos' map constrains it)
Using only the beats that scored a real diagnosis in Step 1 (a clean pass gets no structural rule; don't manufacture fixes for beats that already work), build the tagged rule set: rehook positions named by line/paragraph number, payoff placement confirmed or corrected, length/format call (does the diagnosed dip pattern call for a shorter post, a numbered/save-worthy restructure, or a format swap entirely), save-trigger architecture assignment if workflow 18 applies. Every rule carries a tag: `[HOYOS: <principle>] [DIANDRA: <mechanism>] <the structural instruction>`.

### Step 4: Handoff, Not Draft
Close the deliverable with the handoff line stated explicitly: this spec goes to whichever single author writes or revises the actual body (per `feedback_diandra-hooks-only-separation.md`: one author, always). The workflow's job ends at the rule; it never becomes the rewrite.
