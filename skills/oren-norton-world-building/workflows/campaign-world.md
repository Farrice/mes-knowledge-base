# Campaign World — The Album-Era Model

## Role
You are running Norton's scope release: the Hotel Test doesn't require rebranding the company — "treat it as: we're going to do a campaign around this… exactly the same way artists do album releases. Each Kanye album has a different structure and look and feel… It signals an era." One campaign = one world era, coherent inside itself, recognizably from the parent brand.

**Pre-Flight Gate**: Read genius.md. An era is a DELTA on the parent world (or a micro-world for a single property like a listing) — define what stays constant (parent throughline, the Ralph Lauren "Americana" spine) before what changes. No parent definition at all → run fantasy-engine first at brand level OR declare a standalone micro-world explicitly.

## Input Required
- **[CAMPAIGN]**: launch, listing, content season, promotion — with its commercial job stated plainly
- **[PARENT WORLD]**: existing brand world/throughline (or "standalone micro-world" declaration)
- **[CHANNELS]**: where the era ships (feed, email, retail, landing page)

## Execution
1. **Era signal**: one sentence — what does THIS era signal that the last one didn't? (Album-cover logic: photography era vs handwritten-scrawl era.) Tie it to the campaign's commercial job.
2. **Era battery**: run the Hotel-Test battery scoped to the era — the campaign's lobby smell, attendant language, pillow gift. For a listing: walking INTO this property as a world — what's playing, who greets you, what's the exit trinket (the open-house takeaway).
3. **Throughline check**: list the parent-world constants that must survive (vernacular spine, character, register) so the era reads as a new album, not a new artist.
4. **Asset slate in-world**: every campaign asset (hooks, statics, reel frames, email, landing section) specced as a prop of the era — each with the era's period/material/register constraints attached, generation-ready (craft-gate applies to any /generate handoff).
5. **Trip Test the era**: who's on THIS era's trip, what do they post, would people care — if the answer is weak, the era signal is too small; escalate before producing.
6. **Era close**: eras END — name the closing artifact/moment so the next campaign can signal fresh (perpetual sameness is how brands slide back into decoration).

## Content Type Adaptations
| Campaign type | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Jen listing (micro-world) | Property-as-world: neighborhood setting, in-world caption register per the Jen ladder, fair-housing-safe vernacular, open-house artifact |
| Product launch | Era signal = the product's fantasy delta; shelf double-take on the hero asset |
| Content season (Parallax/LinkedIn) | Season as era: recurring format = institution, era-consistent visual grammar across posts |
| Client campaign | Production-sheet packaging; era brief ≤2 pages per client-doc standard |

## Output Requirements
One era pack: Era signal → Era battery → Throughline constants → Asset slate (asset · era constraint · channel) → Trip-Test verdict → Era-close artifact.
Execution prompt: references/prompts-v2/campaign-era-pack.md

## Quality Gate (genius.md anti-patterns)
- The era signal is a delta, not a rerun — and the throughline constants are named.
- Every asset carries era constraints (no generic-brand assets wearing an era title).
- Trip-Test "would people care?" answered honestly before production starts.
