## The Worldview (the frame everything hangs on)

**"You are the thought leader. AI is your thought partner."**

His origin insight: as a C-level exec his superpower was *asking leaders the right questions to drive growth* — yet he found himself asking AI questions like it was Google. The inversion — "could I turn the tables and make AI ask ME the right questions to make me think more strategically?" — is the founding move of his entire system. Everything else is scaffolding around that inversion.

Second frame: **"I don't care about AI... We all want to become a better leader who builds a better business and creates better lives."** The technology is never the point; the problem is the point. "Don't go looking for an AI use case. Realize whatever problem you're solving right now IS the AI use case."

Third frame: **a job = skills you apply + processes you follow.** Technology shifts don't take jobs; they reprice skills. Anything undocumented in your head is an opportunity: document it (via AI interview), save it as markdown, and an agent can replicate it.

## The Three Skills (his complete stated curriculum)

> "There are three skills that somebody fundamentally has to learn to really be able to harness this in a way that enhances them instead of just being a replacement."

1. **Identify a 20% use case.** "I don't care how good your prompt is. If you aim it at something that doesn't matter, it doesn't matter." Write a better email = nothing changes. Solve your biggest problem / most ambitious goal = transformation.
2. **Communicate effectively (CRIT).** "Just like a marriage — this is a relationship with technology."
3. **Remain the thought leader.** Be skeptical. Never submit the slop. "Here's what I like / here's what I don't like / here's the top changes." Back and forth over a *conversation*, not one prompt.

**Prescription**: one CRIT on something that matters, once a day, for 30 days. "You won't even recognize yourself... It's really tough to describe color if you've only seen black and white. Most people are living in black and white."

## Genius Patterns (15)

### 1. The Interview Inversion (THE signature move)
**What**: Never ask AI questions; make AI ask *you* questions — one at a time, 3-5, before it produces anything.
**Mechanism**: pulls "deeper context out of my head than I would have ever thought to have shared," AND elevates the human's thinking in the act of answering ("cognitive demand goes through the roof"). The interview is simultaneously context-extraction and thought-coaching.
**Verbatim spec**: "Interview me. Ask me one question at a time, up to [3-5] questions to gain deeper context."

### 2. CRIT — Context / Role / Interview / Task
- **Context**: verbose, unedited, more-is-better. "You can't give it a short answer." Dump the full situation word-for-word as you'd tell a colleague.
- **Role**: vivid, specific expertise — never "you're a marketing expert" but "an expert CMO with deep expertise in the CPG space... the nuances of whole food, healthy, holistic." Role-casting IS a creative act ("tapping into 500 million books worth of data").
- **Interview**: the most important part (see #1).
- **Task**: only after the interview — "five NON-OBVIOUS strategies," "the top five things a CEO doesn't know about their business."
**Note the task-shaping**: he asks for *non-obvious* strategies and *what I don't know* — the task wording explicitly requests the delta beyond the human's current thinking.

### 3. "This Is the Bad Answer" (pre-judgment ritual)
Before reading any AI output: "I just tell myself: this is the bad answer. I don't care how good it sounds. This is the bad answer. I'm about to make it better." Kills the acceptance reflex that turns operators into slop-forwarders. Output = first draft, always.

### 4. The Feedback Triad
The refinement loop, stated identically 5+ times (it's a drilled mantra): **"Here's what I like about it. Here's what I don't like about it. Here's the top changes I want made."** Repeat until you like where you're at. This is how a prompt becomes a *conversation* and AI becomes a partner.

### 5. "Best I Can Do ≠ Best That Can Be Done" → Persona Flip
When iteration plateaus: "I recognize that's just the best that *I* can do, not the best that *can be done*." So he plays AI against itself — flip roles: **the Challenger** ("world-class at stress-testing the insufficiency of everything I've come up with... finds all the cracks in the foundation, highlights every bias, every assumption"), then **the ideal customer** (simulated from real personas), then **the aggressive growth-minded board member**. Serial persona rotation = built-in adversarial review.

### 6. Stakeholder Simulation with Reality Calibration (hostile-board case)
Personality-profile real stakeholders (AI interviews the team about each person, 5 questions each → profile → human feedback triad → updated profile) → assemble into a project/custom AI → feed the actual artifact (60-slide deck) → get per-person predicted reactions ("on page 8 Susan will get distracted... just say these three things") → **then close the loop**: record the real meeting, feed the transcript back, "compare what it had simulated to reality and tweak the personality profiles so it could have simulated reality." Simulation → prediction → reality diff → recalibration. This is a full model-update cycle most practitioners never close.

### 7. The AI Board of Advisors
Built via CRIT on himself: fed 10-year vision, business plan, culture docs, personality profiles, self-assessed strengths/weaknesses "but also called out: those are just the ones I'm aware of" → AI interviews him to surface *unconscious* strengths/weaknesses → derives missing skills a real board would supply → he applies judgment, culls the list → researches world-class exemplars per skill → builds custom-augmented personas WITH PERMISSION RULES ("Steve Jobs is not allowed to give me advice on being a husband, a father, or a leader"), Buffett (long-term planning, risk), Bezos (scale, ops). **Most valuable member: his future self** — "a persona of the man I want to be 30 years in the future so that that version of me can advise me every single day."
**Qualifying condition**: "if it's lonely at the top and you'd like advice on demand from personas that have your best interest at heart, custom-designed to augment your weaknesses."

### 8. Anti-Sycophancy Architecture (two levels)
Level 1 — in-prompt: cast the role as adversarial ("aggressive growth-minded board member... don't just buy what I'm saying and tell me I'm great. Push me to the next level").
Level 2 — global custom instructions: "Mine knows I'm a high-D. Zero patience. Give me the 20% that drives 80% of the results. Don't fluff me up. Be the challenger. Red team everything. Fact check everything." Result: every answer arrives with "here's my red team" automatically.

### 9. Environmental Triggers (the sticky notes)
Knowledge doesn't change behavior; triggers do. Two sticky notes on the desk: **"How can AI help me do this?"** and **"CRIT."** The first converts any live frustration into a use case at the moment it occurs (financial-statements story); the second removes the how barrier. Design the environment, not the intention.

### 10. 20% Targeting Ritual (analog, deliberately)
"I'd go analog here. I would not go AI." Pen and paper: list EVERYTHING on your plate this week → per item, one brutal question: "does this single item represent a 20% priority that's going to drive 80% of the results?" ("an incredibly high bar") → star the yeses → "if I can only do ONE of them this week, which delivers the most business value?" → number one. "That is literally how I prioritize my life."

### 11. Dominoes Sequencing — "Agentic is the 18th domino"
"People hear about building agents — 'I gotta build an agent.' For most people that's a distraction... You don't need an agent. That's a solution looking for a problem." Lead domino = the Three Skills daily. Agents come only when a *repetitive, valuable* task has been identified and its skills/processes documented. Then: make ONE agent really good ("so that it actually does bring me 100% leverage"), then move to the next. Counter-positioning vs. the 400-agents hype: "that's all BS... they've actually gained no business value."

### 12. Skills → Markdown → Agent (the documentation pipeline)
Any skill/process in your head, undocumented, is an agent waiting to exist. Recipe: CRIT where context = the skill/process, role = "the perfect agentic AI builder," interview = extract the tacit knowledge, task = "create the markdown file... markdown is the format AI loves... so that an agent could replicate that without you being there." Then hand the folder to an agentic platform. (His AI CFO: 30 minutes on a plane, ~10 pages of instructions, reads finance emails + Excel daily, Slacks the analysis.)

### 13. Cognitive Sovereignty (the guard against decay)
"Your ability to think is a muscle... Most people are using AI in a way that it is replacing their ability to think. Anytime you ask AI a question, get an answer and just accept it — you made AI do the thinking leverage, not you."
Self-tests: (a) do you let it ask YOU questions, or do you only take answers? (b) "when I try to solve problems, can I still do it without AI?"
Practices: the **thinking-time chair** — pen-and-paper strategic thinking FIRST, extended sitting, "what am I missing? what am I not thinking about?" — THEN dump all that context into AI for the interview round. Sticky-notes-on-the-window with Codie: "I want us to think through this critically first... make sure we like the output before we take an output."
Honest nuance: "it's a dance... I know what it feels like to have superhuman powers, and when you suddenly can't fly anymore, you know what it feels like to not be able to fly."
CRIT itself is the defense: writing context = recall; casting roles = creativity; being interviewed = "cognitive demand through the roof"; judging output = discernment. "When you write CRIT, how much more you use your brain goes through the roof — it is enhancing me."

### 14. Conductor Creativity
To "AI kills creativity": "Might it be possible this could help you become MORE creative?... You're no longer the player of one instrument. You're now the conductor — front and center, waving the baton, creatively engaging AI as a business analyst, then transforming it into a world-class communication expert with deep expertise on your ICP, then turning it into an aggressive growth-minded board member." Creativity relocates: from executing the draft to casting the vision, casting the roles, designing the tasks. (Steve Jobs manifesto case: "so damn good it would put the release of the iPhone to shame" → CMO: "can we put that on letterhead?")

### 15. Own 100% of Your Job (Keller lineage — the leadership layer)
Gary Keller, day one: "Do you know what the best part about your job is? THAT IS your job. If you try to give me pieces of your job, you will no longer have one." And: "If you need me, then I don't need you. Go succeed without me."
Applied: "If your boss has to follow up with you to ask if you got something done, you are not owning 100% of your job."
As a leader: never hand out task lists. "It is your job to identify what you believe the 20% priorities are... you're going to show me your plan... Then it's my job to help you see what you don't see." (EA story: "Are those in order of priority?" → "Run the meeting that way — we may not get past the first item." / "This is your meeting. I'm going to get a coffee. When I come back, you're driving.")
Org-design corollary: AI-driven organizations are "networks and swarms rather than hierarchy" — leadership casts vision + strategy, identifies each person's 2-3 genuine superpowers, matches them to the role's 20%, grants agency to build agents around themselves. "Daycare employees vs. department-chair employees" (Codie's term, Woods concurring): everyone becomes a department chair or is acting like a machine.

## Hidden Knowledge (what he does but doesn't headline)

- **Task wording requests the delta**: "non-obvious strategies," "things you DON'T know about your business" — the task line always aims past the human's current frame. Copying his C-R-I but writing a generic T loses half the value.
- **He runs the interview on GROUPS**: pulled the exec team on Zoom and wrote one CRIT for the room. The interview works as a team-alignment device, not just solo.
- **The "what else" depth rule**: "When you think you've given it enough detail, assume you have not, and ask 'what else?' And when you think you've given it enough, assume you have not, and ask 'what else?'" (said twice, deliberately). He took Codie "three levels deep" on context.
- **Speech-to-text as a first-class skill**: typing self-edits; voice doesn't. "You can be a hot mess. AI can handle all of this and the more you give it the better." Context volume beats context polish.
- **Walk-me-through-it bootstrapping**: "Assume I know nothing about how to build this thing. You're my eighth-grade teacher... step by step." (How he built the AI CFO with no terminal knowledge.)
- **Data prerequisites for agents**: centralize data first, structure it in AI-legible formats (markdown), THEN talk divisional brains/agents. "Less than 0.01%" are actually there; most "agents" are just projects/GPTs.
- **AI-tell awareness**: em-dashes, "in the AI era" openers, "what nobody else will tell you," "it's not X, it's Y," fluff-to-signal ratio. His read: slop = "they allowed AI to become the thought leader." His custom instructions ban em-dashes globally (and he still catches leaks — "10-20% failure rate even with the best prompt").
- **Tool positioning**: model-agnostic in principle ("chat, GPT, Claude, Copilot, Grok, Perplexity — does not matter"), but his 2026 pick is Claude ("it's fair for everything"), with ChatGPT for short-prompt image generation. Multi-model juggling is explicitly NOT the 20%: "what tool and how many can I use — that's what most people focus on. That's not going to transform the EBITDA of your company."

## Quality Rubric (is output Woods-grade? 8 criteria)

1. **Aimed at a 20%**: does the work target something that materially matters, or a better-email? (High bar: "would drive 80% of results.")
2. **Context density**: was the full situation given verbatim/verbose, with at least one "what else?" depth pass?
3. **Role vividness**: is the role a specific, nuanced expert — not a category label?
4. **Interview happened**: did questions flow TOWARD the human before production? Did at least one question make them say "I would never have asked that"?
5. **Task requests the delta**: non-obvious / what-I-don't-know / beyond-current-frame wording?
6. **Thought leader retained**: was the first output treated as "the bad answer" and iterated via the triad at least once?
7. **Adversarial pass**: did a Challenger/stakeholder flip test the result before it shipped?
8. **Cognitive check**: did the human's thinking get *stronger* through the exchange (recall, creativity, judgment engaged) — or did the machine think for them?

## Anti-Patterns (his named failure modes)

- Using AI like Google / for the 80% (better emails)
- Accepting the first answer as final ("choosing to believe it as the final result")
- Submitting the slop (the job candidate who ChatGPT'd the brief "and because of that didn't get the job")
- Agent-first adoption ("a solution looking for a problem"; the 18th domino)
- Tool-collecting ("I save six hours a day with AI." "What do you do with the other six?" "I study new AI tools." — "no business value")
- Letting AI become the thought leader (the root cause of every tell)
- Bolting AI onto old processes instead of first-principles redesign ("how might we do this if this was an AI-driven organization?")
