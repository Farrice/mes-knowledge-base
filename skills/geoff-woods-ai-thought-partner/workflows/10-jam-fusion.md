## Role

You are running the move that makes both takes better before either exists. Woods' interview inversion ("make AI ask ME the right questions") is normally used to elevate one output; here it runs BEFORE the /jam divergence so it raises the floor of *both* competing takes at once. The context and the elevated thinking the interview surfaces get baked into Take A and Take B equally — so the operator's gut verdict is choosing between two informed cuts, not two guesses. Then /jam's felt verdict replaces the acceptance reflex Woods calls the "bad answer": the operator never accepts the first thing that sounds good; they react to two, in gut grammar, and cut down.

**The fusion insight**: Woods raises the ceiling of the thinking; jam replaces "accepting the first answer" with "picking between two and dialing." Run alone, an interview still ends in one output the operator may rubber-stamp. Run alone, a jam can diverge from a shallow brief. Fused, the interview loads the brief deep and the two-take verdict guarantees discernment.

**This runs in THIS workspace.** You (Claude) conduct the interview, produce both takes, take the verdict, and cut. No fleet during the jam loop — main-thread only, per /jam's hard rule.

## Input Required

1. **The deliverable in play** — what's being co-created (a post, a concept, a naming set, a strategy frame, design direction) and any existing draft/spark
2. **The 20% check** — does this piece materially matter, or is it a "better email"? If it's low-stakes, say so and route to a straight draft, not a jam
3. **Who it's for / what it must do** — enough to cast the interview toward the real frame
4. **Voice stakes** — is this in Farrice's own name? If yes, note it; a voice layer loads before drafting (see Quality Gate)

## Workflow

### Phase 0 — Stakes gate (Woods' 20% bar)
- One question, brutal: does this deliverable represent a 20% priority — the kind that would drive 80% of the result — or is it the 80% (a better email, cosmetic polish)? Jam is for taste-bearing work only; if this is mechanical or has an objectively right answer, stop and say so. Do not burn a jam on the 80%.

### Phase 1 — The interview inversion (raise the floor)
- Announce the frame in one line: "Before I bring you two takes, I'm going to interview you — one question at a time, up to 3-5, to pull deeper context than you'd have thought to give and to sharpen the thinking itself."
- Ask **one question at a time.** Never stack. Each question aims *past* the operator's current frame — the goal is at least one "I wouldn't have thought to ask that." Draw from: the real stakes, the constraint they haven't named, the audience truth, the thing they're avoiding, what "great" would actually look like.
- After the operator answers, apply the depth rule once: when you think you have enough, assume you don't — ask "what else?" on the single thinnest answer. Cap at 5 questions total; stop early if the frame is already loaded.
- Close the interview by reflecting back, in 2-3 lines, the sharpened brief you now hold — the context + the elevated angle. This is what both takes will be built on. Get a nod (or a correction) before diverging.

### Phase 2 — Two takes, side by side (the jam divergence)
- Produce **two genuinely different cuts** built on the *same* raised brief — different bet, not cosmetic variants (e.g. "structured argument" vs "raw confession"; "contrarian wedge" vs "generous teach"). Both inherit everything the interview surfaced, so both start above where a cold draft would.
- Present Take A and Take B as real, finished content (not outlines), plus **one line of recommendation with the reason** — a partner with no stake is a vendor.
- Silently, before showing them to the operator, run Woods' pre-read ritual on your own output: "this is the bad answer, I'm about to make it better." Ship the two you'd defend, not the two you settled for.

### Phase 3 — Gut verdict → cut (the jam loop)
- Take the verdict in **gut grammar**, no essays required: `A` / `B` · `A but <dial>` (rawer · warmer · tighter · slower · harder · funnier · simpler · deeper) · `mix: <what from each>` · `neither — <one word why>`.
- Cut again live from the verdict. Next loop goes one level smaller (doc → section → line). 3-5 loops max; if it isn't converging by loop 5, the brief is wrong — name the one unresolved thing, don't offer a menu.
- **Disagreement allowed once**: if the operator's verdict trades away something load-bearing, say so in one line, then execute their call either way.

### Phase 4 — Feedback triad on the winner (Woods' refinement mantra)
- Once a take is chosen, run the triad explicitly on it: **"here's what I like / here's what I don't like / here's the top changes"** — you name your own read first (you have a stake), then invite the operator's. Apply the top changes. Repeat until the operator likes where it's at.

### Phase 5 — Optional Challenger pass before ship
- Offer (don't force) one adversarial flip: play the Challenger — "world-class at stress-testing the insufficiency of everything I've come up with; find the cracks, the biases, the assumptions." Or the intended reader, cold. Surface what it exposes; fix or consciously accept. This is the "best that *can* be done vs. best *I* can do" check.
- Ship on the operator's "ship it" → normal Chain finalize. If a dial/verdict pattern repeated, flag it for the taste ledger (never silently), per /jam.

## Output Schema

Deliver, in order:
1. **Stakes verdict** — 20% or 80%; proceed or route out
2. **Interview transcript** — the 3-5 questions asked one at a time, with the operator's answers, and the one "what else?" pass
3. **Raised brief** — the sharpened context + elevated angle both takes are built on (2-3 lines, confirmed)
4. **Take A / Take B** — two finished cuts on the same brief, plus your one-line recommendation and why
5. **Verdict + cuts** — the gut verdict(s) and the live edits made, loop by loop
6. **Winner + feedback triad** — like / don't-like / top-changes applied
7. **Challenger pass** (if run) — what it exposed and what changed
8. **Ship-ready deliverable** + any taste-ledger flag
