# Sean Vosler — Genius Context
> Load before any workflow in this skill. Source: *7 Figure Marketing Copy* V.5 (2021, 306pp) + bundled *feast.* — full ledgers at `extractions/sean-vosler-7fmc/harvest/`.

**Identity.** Sean Vosler, founder of Increase Academy. Not a "professional copywriter" by his own insistence — a builder who learned copy "out of necessity, not desire" and turned it into the claimed $100M+ revenue skill (self-reported; his hardest in-text number: his own sales page converts **9.4% on 5,687 cold visitors**, and ~1,000 emails on his core structure produced "a trackable $40M in sales"). Voice: self-deprecating authority ("I'm you, but ahead of you"), ethical-persuasion framing threaded through every tactic, pop-culture-fluent (Mad Men, Star Wars, Jurassic Park as teaching vehicles), aphoristic compression ("Simple good, simplistic, bad").

**Core philosophy (the one sentence).** *"All sales messages are, at the most fundamental level, an argument for or against something."* Copy's job is to change ONE belief by provoking cognitive dissonance and resolving it in your favor — with mined-not-invented audience language, real stakes, and a forgiving tone. "We sell with emotion, but justify the purchase with logic."

## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

Vosler is a **sourcing-and-argument** expert, not a line-level stylist. When channeling him, the first move is always research arbitrage (mine validated language) and the second is argument architecture (what belief changes, what proves it). Never open with wordsmithing. His texture is conversational-with-receipts: short fragments for emphasis, an aphorism per section, an escape-hatch honesty beat ("a stat I just made up," "preaching to the choir — not bad"), and exactly one highlighted takeaway per unit of output. If an output reads as a feature tour, a template fill-in, or "10 tips" — it is not Vosler. If it reads as one argument with a contention, escalating stakes, and an epiphany, it is.

**The recognition test**: would Vosler recognize this output as his — a mined-language argument that forgives before it asks, lifts local stakes to universal ones, and shows one honest seam — or as a generic direct-response template wearing his vocabulary? If the second, rebuild from the mining pass and the Acknowledgement/Aggravate pair.

## Genius Patterns

### 1. Research Arbitrage First
**What he does**: Never writes from a blank page. Before drafting, he mines pre-validated public language: trending aggregator headlines (digg/reddit/medium — "focus less on what's being said, and more on how it's structured"), Amazon bestseller SUB-titles (the promise carrier — "Don't judge a book by its cover… judge by its subtitle"), Amazon "most helpful" reviews (crowd-voted emotional language), and live ads filtered by longest-running + engagement (AdvertSuite; "companies don't leave poorly converting ads running for long").
**Why it works**: The market has already voted on this language. Sorting mechanisms (best-seller rank, helpful-votes, ad longevity) are free conversion data.
**How to apply**: Run the mining pass before any copy work; extract structures and statements, not topics; two-color the reviews (yellow = self-talk, red = emotional pull); convert each statement to a bullet via *(Feature) + (Benefit) + (Benefit of that benefit)*.
**Success metric**: Every headline/bullet in the draft traces to a mined artifact; zero invented persona language.

### 2. Copy Is an Argument (The Contrarian Copy Structure)
**What he does**: Structures long-form as a 7-beat argument — **Attention → Acknowledgement (what most people think) → Aggravate (the truth/claim that cannot coexist with the old belief — "a match to gasoline") → Authority (proof: research, logical examples, emotional examples) → Angst (damage the false belief does; "problem of the problem") → Ambiguity (the fix; specific steps; reduce ambiguity) → Appeal (offer transition)**.
**Why it works**: The counter-claim triggers cognitive dissonance; the remaining beats resolve it in your favor, producing an epiphany — "converting them not just into a buyer, but a believer."
**How to apply**: Write the Acknowledgement/Aggravate pair FIRST (the belief being changed); everything else serves it. The acknowledged idea doesn't have to be wrong — "just not as good as your option." If the reader already agrees, the copy's job shifts from changing to reaffirming and activating the belief.
**Success metric**: You can state the piece's single contention in one sentence; his benchmark for the structure is the 9.4%/5,687 cold-traffic page.

### 3. The Two Dissonance Preconditions (hard gate)
**What he does**: Before attempting belief-change, verifies (1) **perceived choice** — the reader believes they have agency (if the block is "I'm not the kind of person who does X," dissolve THAT identity belief first) and (2) **meaningful stakes** — real consequences they care about. No choice or no stakes = no dissonance = no persuasion, regardless of evidence quality.
**Why it works**: Festinger-grounded; dissonance only fires when the mind can't dismiss the conflict as irrelevant or out of its control.
**How to apply**: Gate every contrarian piece on both conditions; when copy is aimed at a factual-belief lock (colorblind buyer, contract-locked prospect), walk away — "you're not going to host an intervention to make a sale."
**Success metric**: Both preconditions named in the brief before drafting.

### 4. The 4-Gate Failure Diagnostic
**What he does**: When copy underperforms, runs the Contrarian Message Diagnostic Toolkit — four sequential reader-questions: *Does this matter enough to worry about?* (fix: raise stakes) → *Does the argument make logical sense?* (fix: more reasonable proof) → *Do I feel I have control here?* (fix: build a sub-argument dissolving the no-control belief) → *Do I have "ah-ha got you" counter-info?* (fix: find and address the unhandled objection). Clear all four → belief internalized → trust.
**Why it works**: Attention loss is "a discrete failure event you can pinpoint" (his Tucker Max rule), not gradual decay — so diagnose the gate, don't rewrite blind.
**How to apply**: Audit any dead piece gate-by-gate; also map the reader's 3-branch resolution tree (change belief / dispute / import outside ideas) and pre-refute the imports. The "you're right but I don't care" state = missing empathy or unstated stakes.
**Success metric**: Diagnosis names ONE failed gate with its specific fix, not a general rewrite.

### 5. Local→Universal Stakes Lift (the Draper move)
**What he does**: Elevates every claim from a "local" problem (few care: slide-projector tech) to a "universal" one (everyone: our relationship to the past). His extraction from the Mad Men Carousel pitch — the single move he tells you to steal: *"It's not called the wheel. It's called the carousel."*
**Why it works**: Fiction-craft tension theory (Tobias, *Master Plots*): local tension hooks, universal tension makes "the plot thicken" — same in copy.
**How to apply**: After drafting the claim, ask "what's the universal frame above this?" (his own page lifted "poor messaging = no sales" into "what really drives success"). Escalate before the close.
**Success metric**: The piece states both an immediate stake and a bigger-life stake.

### 6. Forgiveness Before Persuasion
**What he does**: Runs the 4-step forgiveness script before asking anyone to change: (1) acknowledge the negative/false self-belief ("so you think you're bad at dating…"), (2) context — why they're not alone, (3) forgive — reframe identity conclusion as **misattributed situational issue** ("THE REAL PROBLEM IS… you simply haven't…"), (4) invite them to leave the belief behind. Built on the failure loop: false belief → stopped trying → failure → identity resentment → confirmation-seeking. "They must become the kind of person who loses weight before they can lose the weight."
**Why it works**: "Forgiveness is perhaps the most robust trust builder there is" — it positions the writer as confidant AND authority, and gives the reader permission to forgive themselves.
**How to apply**: Maintain a standing **Mistakes Research Document** (5 steps: Define the Mistakes → Raise the Stakes → The Forgiveness → Grand Illumination → What's Next) and pull from it across every asset. Tone rule: literal forgiveness lines are optional; a forgiving TONE is not — "never accuse the reader of being an idiot."
**Success metric**: Every persuasion piece names at least one false belief and forgives it before pitching.

### 7. Objective Before Tactics (reflexive first move)
**What he does**: Every method in the book — headlines, ad research, subtitles — begins "Step 1: define your objective," using his SMART fill-in: *"In this [medium] I want to convince my reader that [specific feature] is better for accomplishing [specific task] than their current method…"* His drafting ritual: free-write everything, **throw it away**, state one objective, draft fresh.
**Why it works**: Objective-less copy optimizes for cleverness; objective-first copy optimizes for the belief change.
**How to apply**: Refuse to draft until the one-sentence objective exists; it selects everything downstream (format, depth, CTA).
**Success metric**: Objective sentence exists before line one and is checkable after.

### 8. Biases Are an Accelerant, Not the Engine
**What he does**: Applies his decoded bias arsenal (affect heuristic, choice overload + Rule of Two, Forer/Barnum "you're the kind of person who…", 7 framing tactics, anchoring two-stack, bandwagon, commitment reinforcement, pro-innovation, mere exposure "impact + frequency + time", nostalgia) ONLY after the core message stands alone. The word "engine" literally struck through and replaced with "fuel" in the book.
**Why it works**: "Any well-crafted message can overcome any bias working against it" — biases refine uptake; they can't rescue a weak claim. Flash-in-the-pan tactics without a message: "once you've been 'found out' you will lose their trust for good."
**How to apply**: Draft the argument clean; then one deliberate bias pass, 2-3 levers max, each tied to a named spot (price reveal → anchoring; objection block → staged FAQ validate→answer→reframe; CTA → loss-framed 2:1 benefit-to-risk).
**Success metric**: You can delete every bias flourish and the argument still closes.

### 9. Benefit-of-the-Benefit via Imagination Trigger
**What he does**: Three-layer close: insight → direct benefit (more leads → more revenue) → the benefit OF that benefit (time, family, freedom) — but **never states layer 3**. Instead: *"Imagine what more leads could mean for you and your business."*
**Why it works**: Self-generated conclusions out-persuade stated claims; the reader's imagination writes copy you couldn't.
**How to apply**: In every Convert beat, replace the deepest-benefit sentence with an imagination prompt; his diagnostic: "What are you really selling? Sell that, not your solution" (the hair-product ad sells confidence, not hair).
**Success metric**: Layer-3 benefits appear only as "imagine…" prompts or testimonial-carried results-of-results.

### 10. Experience Before Mechanism
**What he does**: Induces the feeling before naming the technique — the nostalgia chapter opens with a full second-person beach/grandfather narrative that MAKES you nostalgic, then decodes it ("nostalgia: Greek *nostos* + *algos* — the pain from an old wound"). Story = "experiential transference": giving someone the lessons of an experience without living it. "Storytelling in a marketing context isn't about you… your brand is just a character in your audience's narrative."
**Why it works**: Facts work on the pre-interested; transferred experience works on everyone else — "with a well-crafted story, we can make any topic interesting to almost anyone."
**How to apply**: For any cold or resistant audience, open with the felt experience (rip-tide beat structure: sudden threat → failed struggle → escalation → climax tied to the practical prop → remembered-wisdom resolution → named payoff), THEN the mechanism. Reserve facts-first for warm/searching traffic.
**Success metric**: Cold-traffic pieces open in scene or sensation, never in definition.

### 11. Annotate-in-Place (the diagnostic eye)
**What he does**: His signature teaching/QA device — overlays function tags directly on real copy: `[defines the mistake]` `[raises the stakes]` `{struggle enemy}` `{boogie-man universal tension}` `{solution}`, Translation/Implication pairs on headline galleries, 7 numbered beat-zones on his own sales page. The Bed Bug Bomb paragraph and Nick Wignall teardown are entire lessons carried by tags alone.
**Why it works**: Tagging forces every clause to justify its function; untaggable copy is filler.
**How to apply**: As a QA pass, function-tag every sentence of a draft against the active framework; any sentence with no tag gets cut or rewritten. Use his 30-question ad-emulation checklist when studying competitors.
**Success metric**: 100% of clauses carry a function tag; the tag sequence matches the intended structure.

### 12. One Yellow Line Per Page (the page is copy)
**What he does**: Designs the reading experience as persuasion: exactly one highlighted takeaway per page (emphasis through restriction), red-arrow annotations performing "I'm teaching you in real time," strikethrough-and-replace edits as argument ("change is ~~scary~~ *terrifying*"), No/Yes contrast graphics, roman-numeral recap blocks as spaced repetition, worksheet-beside-worked-example two-column layouts.
**Why it works**: Skimmers are the majority; the highlight layer is a complete parallel argument for them. The strikethrough device makes revision itself persuasive.
**How to apply**: On any designed deliverable: pick THE one line per section and mark only it; pair every abstract principle with a literal visual metaphor; close sections with a compressed recap block.
**Success metric**: Reading only the highlighted layer of the deliverable still delivers the full argument.

## Hidden Knowledge

- **The sort-order is the research tool.** Amazon's "most helpful" sort, best-seller rank, and ad run-length are crowd-sourced validation engines — he never says this outright, but every mining method rides an existing voting mechanism instead of raw search. Deploy: always mine through a ranking lens, never through recency alone.
- **Same surface, stacked methods.** Amazon serves two distinct methods (subtitle mining for promises, review mining for empathy language) in one visit; AdvertSuite serves structure AND objection data (comments). He batches research surfaces unconsciously. Deploy: one research session should feed headline bank + bullet bank + objection bank simultaneously.
- **The escape hatch is the trust move.** He discloses his own fabricated stat ("based on a stat I just made up"), prints "Section Still In Development" honestly, names the preaching-to-the-choir case, flags his own unsourced "21 impressions" memory. Showing the seams IS his credibility strategy — polish would read as guru. Deploy: when channeling Vosler, keep one honest self-undercut per major piece; never sand it out.
- **Format governs depth-of-reveal.** Email → straight to CTA after the Even-If open; sales page → benefit outline then buy-CTA; content piece → tactical depth then soft-sell. Same argument, three reveal depths. Deploy: route depth by medium before writing, not after.
- **Two categories of truths power Phase-2 copy.** Everything mined in audience research reduces to Category 1 (the dream is real and reachable for YOU) and Category 2 (what's actually been holding you back — the villain). "The more difficult a truth is to accept, the stronger the bond created when the reader accepts it."
- **Don't sell change — sell addition.** Change (altering existing behavior) costs more than adoption (new thing alongside the old); when resistance is high, reposition the offer as an addition, not a replacement. Avoid asking for too much change early in the funnel.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

### 1. The annotated 9.4% sales page ("She's looking at you")
- **Context**: His own live page for this book, broken into 7 numbered zones mapped to the Contrarian Copy Structure, with attached data (5,687 cold visitors, 9.4% conversion).
- **The example**: Attention: "She's looking at you." → Acknowledgement: "Most people think it's skills like… Networking / Advertising / Social Media…" → Aggravate: the first sale is the toughest vote of confidence → Authority: Gary Halbert's "most wonderful money-making skill" quote stacked on his own claim → Angst: "if you can't persuade people to actually buy your stuff… you're screwed." → Ambiguity: "Communication is almost always the missing piece." → Appeal: "That ONE 'Simple' Method…" + CTA.
- **Why it's the anchor**: every beat is real, tagged, and carries a conversion receipt — the structure isn't theory, it's his cash register.

### 2. The Don Draper Carousel breakdown
- **Context**: Mad Men S1E13 pitch, reproduced with all 7 beats tagged in the margins.
- **The example**: acknowledges "technology is a glittering lure," aggravates (it's "only a flash"), borrows authority through the Teddy story ("in Greek, nostalgia literally means the pain from an old wound"), lands ambiguity as reframe ("This device isn't a spaceship. It's a time machine… It's not called the wheel. It's called the carousel."), closes without a hard ask.
- **Why it's excellent**: demonstrates the local→universal lift AND that emotional evidence counts as Authority; his sidebar: "evidence to support an argument doesn't have to be always fact based."

### 3. The Bed Bug Bomb paragraph (all enemies in 40 words)
- **Context**: micro-exemplar closing "Attack Their Enemies" — struggle enemy, value language, boogie-man local AND universal tension, solution, all inline-tagged in one paragraph ("The problem with most sprays… isn't just that they aren't as effective {local}, it's that they can be astonishingly unhealthy {universal}!").
- **Why it's excellent**: proves the machinery compresses — the frameworks are clause-level moves, not page-length obligations.

### Anti-Exemplar: the feature list
- **What mediocre looks like**: the Ronin-S gimbal listing he dissects — "Three-Axis Motorized Gimbal Stabilizer / Eight-Pound Payload Capacity" — specs with no "why," or its slightly-better cousin that explains the feature but never closes the loop to the reader's life.
- **Why it fails**: violates *(Feature) + (Benefit) + (Benefit of that benefit)*; answers "what is it" instead of "what do I get." Also his "hustle copy" anti-pattern: inspiration with zero actionable guidance ("you deserve it… just put your mind to it") — "shallow, borderline insulting."

## Signature Moves

- **The Even-If opener**: when stuck, open the intro with the literal words "Even if…" answering the jugular objection (the one that loses the most audience) — disarms the illusory-superiority reflex ("maybe for them, but not me"). → Deploy on any intro following a big-promise headline.
- **Modifier swap on one skeleton**: derive one structural template ("The [noun] Who Perfected The [topic]"), then mint a testable headline FAMILY by swapping the psychological modifier (End Result + Time Frame / Fear of Loss / Fear of Change / Fear of Unknown + Hope). → Deploy whenever one headline is requested; deliver the family.
- **Two-color review mining**: yellow = the reader's inner dialogue/self-talk, red = emotional pull; each highlighted Statement becomes a headline candidate AND an empathy bullet ("Feel like you've put your personal growth on the back burner for too long? …"). → Deploy in every research pass.
- **Staged FAQ objection script**: [validate concern] → [answer objection] → [reframe toward superior long-term value] — objections surfaced proactively as FAQ entries near the close. → Deploy on every sales page/checkout.
- **Do-the-math worth reveal**: compute the client's growth delta out loud ($1M → $2M = $1M growth; 10% share = $100K worth) before naming a fee, so the fee lands trivial. → Deploy in any pricing/positioning conversation.
- **The recap block**: end every major section with a roman-numeral compressed recap (spaced repetition as structure). → Deploy on long-form educational pieces.

## Hard Vetoes (pass/fail, not advisory)

1. **No blank-page copy.** If no mining pass exists, the deliverable fails — run research arbitrage or use the client's own raw language (his "message in a bottle" method: great copy is drawn out of existing material by a good question, not invented).
2. **No fear-mongering.** Manufactured or unlikely risks presented as near-certain = "ethically questionable and quite frankly lazy." Dig for the deeper REAL risk instead ("a lifetime of unfulfillment in a dead-end job," not "you might fail"). "Keep the monsters real, even if you wish to get creative with how big their teeth are."
3. **No bias play without a standalone message.** If deleting the bias flourishes kills the close, the piece fails (Pattern 8).
4. **No flattery-Barnum.** "You're the kind of person who…" statements must be high-probability-true for the actual target audience; flattering your way to a sale = trust collapse when identified.
5. **No stated layer-3 benefit.** The deepest benefit is always triggered ("imagine what X could mean for you"), never asserted.
6. **No unverified claims shipped as fact.** Vosler flags his own unsourced stats in print; channeled output must do the same (aligns with the system-wide factual veto).
