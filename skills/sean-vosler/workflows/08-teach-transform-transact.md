# 08 — Teach, Transform, Transact

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. **You must know more than they do.** "If your audience knows more than you on the subject, you lose all leverage." A research pass on product, industry, and audience exists before drafting — Hard Veto 1 in its Teach-shaped form.
2. **Objective before tactics (Pattern 7).** The SMART sentence exists and names what belief changes, not what topic gets covered.
3. **The reframe exists before the outline.** State in one sentence what the prospect *thinks* they need and what they *actually* need. Vosler's: *"You don't need a website, you need an online lead generation funnel."* No reframe = no Transform, and TTT collapses into a tutorial.
4. **Two dissonance preconditions (Pattern 3).** Perceived choice + meaningful stakes. Teaching a reader who believes they have no agency produces comprehension, not conversion.

## Execution

**1 — The premise, so you build correctly.** Neuroplasticity means the brain rewires from what it's exposed to, and the *emotional weight* of an experience determines how strong and lasting the new connection is (Rubin's parallel: systematic desensitization for fear of heights). You can't run that protocol on a customer, so **education is the practical substitute** — to get someone to act you must often first help them *unlearn* a defective belief and replace it with an accurate one. Chain: teach → rewire belief → remove fear → earn trust → transact. Build for emotional weight, not coverage.

**2 — Diagnose the audience on Bloom's Taxonomy.** Six ascending rungs: **Knowledge** (recall terminology, facts, dates) → **Comprehension** (derive meaning by organizing, comparing, interpreting) → **Application** (use it in a new context to solve a problem) → **Analysis** (break the whole into parts, see how parts relate to the whole) → **Synthesis** (recombine into a new whole) → **Evaluation** (make educated judgments, propose new solutions). Name where the target sits today — a buyer who has shopped three vendors enters at Analysis and is insulted by a Knowledge-rung opener.

**3 — One deliverable-outcome sentence per rung, ascending. That is the outline.** Not topics — outcomes, phrased as what the reader will possess. Vosler's worked template (a copywriting book sold to business owners who dislike marketing):
- *Knowledge:* "By the end of this section your reader will have a clear picture of what copywriting is and why their personal wealth has a DIRECT correlation with their ability to write quality copy."
- *Comprehension:* "…clear understanding that there are different methods/schools of thought to copywriting and why they were established."
- *Application:* "…a useful, action-oriented, clearly understood strategy they can apply to their marketing right now to increase results."
- *Synthesis:* "…a clear picture of the difference between a successful piece of marketing copy vs. one that doesn't accomplish its goal."
Start at the diagnosed rung, not the bottom by default. Each sentence becomes one section.

**4 — TEACH.** For cold or resistant readers, open in the felt experience before naming the mechanism (Pattern 10) — facts serve the pre-interested. Every claim carries at least one convincing piece of logic or fact; bigger claims demand more evidence. Bring them to a conclusion, don't hand them one.

**5 — TRANSFORM: the challenger reframe.** Conventional wisdom says relationship-builders sell best; the best reps instead **challenge the prospect's framing of their own problem** and offer an alternative problem plus solution.
1. **Replace the stated spec with the real need.** The prospect asks for a website with home/about/contact; every other vendor quotes that spec. The challenger says *"You don't need a website, you need an online lead generation funnel"* — then teaches what one is.
2. **Quantify it.** Not "more leads" — the lead lift, the revenue lift, the number. Do the math out loud: baseline → growth delta → what a share of that delta is worth. A reframe without math is an opinion.
3. **Be the last vendor** — the one who challenges what they think they need and sells the right solution to the real problem.
4. Self-check before shipping: *"am I challenging my customer to think differently about their problem?"*

**6 — Engineer the eureka moment.** It lands when the reader assembles the conclusion themselves: teach the alternative path → let the contradiction sit → state the reframe in one quotable line. *"Never underestimate people's ability to change their minds if you guide them to their 'promise land.'"* The close follows the eureka, not the pitch. A reframe arriving before the teaching reads as a sales tactic.

**7 — TRANSACT: three layers, layer 3 never stated.**
1. Teach the alternative path to what's actually needed (the insight).
2. State the direct benefit — more leads → more revenue.
3. Go one layer deeper — what that revenue buys emotionally: time, security, freedom — and **do not state it.** Trigger the imagination instead. Vosler's exact mechanic: not "imagine having more leads so you could have more time with family," but **"Imagine what more leads could mean for you and your business."** Self-generated conclusions land harder than stated claims. The blunt illustration: a hair-growth infomercial selling "stop getting rejected at the bar" is selling confidence, not hair.

**8 — The "what are you really selling?" diagnostic.** Ask it of the finished piece: *"What are you really selling? Sell that, not your solution."* Website buyers are really buying leads and sales; ad-service buyers are really buying eyeballs to sell more; health-coaching buyers are really buying confidence in themselves. Universal form: **"Consider: what are they actually buying?"** — everything traces to increasing sales or reducing risk. If the answer differs from what Transact sells, rewrite Transact.

**9 — Lift local to universal (Pattern 5).** Before the close: what is the universal frame above this claim? The piece states an immediate stake and a bigger-life stake. Draper's move — "It's not called the wheel. It's called the carousel."

**10 — Evaluate the ascent.** Re-read against step 3's sentences: did the piece move the reader up each rung, or skip, or stall? Stalling at Knowledge produces a tutorial; skipping to Evaluation produces a piece only insiders can read. Fix by rung, not by rewrite. Then apply yellow-line discipline: one highlighted takeaway per section, each closing with a compressed roman-numeral recap.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content type | Bloom entry | Reframe lands | Transact shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cornerstone guide | Knowledge / Comprehension | After two teaching sections | Soft-sell; trigger, no price |
| Sales page | Comprehension | Right after Acknowledgement | Canonical close + layer-3 trigger |
| Webinar / live teach | Knowledge → Application | Pivot between teach and pitch | Eureka → offer; do-the-math aloud |
| Sales call / pitch deck | Analysis (they've shopped) | Opening move — replace their spec | Quantified delta → fee lands trivial |
| Email series | One rung per email | The Challenge email (workflow 07) | Triggers seeded across sends |
| Client proposal | Analysis / Synthesis | The scope section | Growth-partner; sales-up or risk-down |

## Output Requirements

1. SMART objective and diagnosed Bloom rung, with reasoning.
2. **The rung-by-rung outline** — one deliverable-outcome sentence per level, ascending, each a named section.
3. The challenger reframe as one quotable line, plus its quantification (baseline → delta → worth).
4. The drafted asset with phases marked: TEACH / TRANSFORM / TRANSACT.
5. The eureka line, isolated.
6. Layer-3 benefits in working notes only; in copy, **only** as imagination triggers or testimonial-carried results-of-results.
7. The "what are you really selling?" answer in one sentence, confirmed against Transact.
8. Local and universal stakes; one highlighted line per section; a compressed recap per section.

## Quality Gate

- **Hard Veto 5 (no stated layer-3 benefit):** the deepest benefit is triggered, never asserted. Any "so you can finally have more time with your family" fails Transact.
- **Reframe present and quantified:** replaces the stated problem with the real one AND puts a number on it. Reframe without math is an opinion; number without reframe is a spec sheet.
- **Ascent verified (Bloom evaluate step):** the draft moves through the outlined rungs; stalling or skipping fails and is fixed by rung.
- **Hard Veto 1 (no blank-page copy):** teaching traces to a real research pass. Anti-exemplars: the feature list, and "hustle copy" — inspiration with zero actionable guidance.
- **Rubric row "Stakes":** local AND universal stakes present; the "problem of the problem" named.
- **Hard Veto 6:** every quantification, study reference, and result claim labeled VERIFIED / LIKELY / UNCONFIRMED; do-the-math figures use the client's real baseline, never an illustrative one presented as theirs.
