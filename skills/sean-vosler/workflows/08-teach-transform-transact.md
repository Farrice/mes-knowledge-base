# 08 — Teach, Transform, Transact

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. **You must know more than they do (research gate).** "If your audience knows more than you on the subject, you lose all leverage." Teaching is how a marketer earns the right to persuade — so a research pass on the product, the industry, and the audience exists before drafting. This is Hard Veto 1 in its Teach-shaped form: no blank-page copy, and no blank-page teaching either.
2. **Objective before tactics (Pattern 7).** The SMART objective sentence exists in writing, and it names what belief changes — not what topic gets covered.
3. **The reframe exists before the outline.** You can state, in one sentence, what the prospect *thinks* they need and what they *actually* need. Vosler's: *"You don't need a website, you need an online lead generation funnel."* No reframe = there is no Transform, and TTT collapses into a tutorial.
4. **Two dissonance preconditions (Pattern 3).** Perceived choice + meaningful stakes. Teaching a reader who believes they have no agency produces comprehension, not conversion.

## Execution

**Step 1 — The neuroscience premise, stated so you build correctly.** Neuroplasticity means the brain rewires from what it's exposed to, and the *emotional weight* of an experience determines how strong and lasting the new connection is. Rubin's clinical parallel is systematic desensitization for a fear of heights: controlled, repeated exposure retrains the reaction. You can't run a desensitization protocol on a customer, so **education is the practical substitute** — to get someone to act, you often must first help them *unlearn* a defective belief and replace it with an accurate one. The causal chain the whole framework rests on: teach → rewire belief → remove fear → earn trust → transact. Build for emotional weight, not coverage.

**Step 2 — Diagnose the audience level on Bloom's Taxonomy.** Six ascending rungs: **Knowledge** (recall terminology, facts, dates; answer basic questions) → **Comprehension** (derive meaning by organizing, comparing, interpreting) → **Application** (use the knowledge in a new context to solve a problem) → **Analysis** (break the whole into parts, see how parts relate to the whole) → **Synthesis** (recombine elements into a new whole) → **Evaluation** (make educated judgments, propose new solutions). Name where your target actually sits today. Total beginners start at Knowledge; a sophisticated buyer who has already tried three vendors may enter at Analysis and be insulted by a Knowledge-rung opener.

**Step 3 — Write one deliverable-outcome sentence per rung, ascending. This is the outline.** Not topics — outcomes, phrased as what the reader will possess at the end of that section. Vosler's own worked template (a copywriting book sold to business owners who dislike marketing):
- *Knowledge:* "By the end of this section your reader will have a clear picture of what copywriting is and why their personal wealth has a DIRECT correlation with their ability to write quality copy."
- *Comprehension:* "…clear understanding that there are different methods/schools of thought to copywriting and why they were established."
- *Application:* "…a useful, action-oriented, clearly understood strategy they can apply to their marketing right now to increase results."
- *Synthesis:* "…a clear picture of the difference between a successful piece of marketing copy vs. one that doesn't accomplish its goal."
Start at the diagnosed rung, not at the bottom by default. Each sentence becomes one section. Use the ladder to gauge sophistication and calibrate how complex the content should get.

**Step 4 — TEACH: build the sections, cold-audience rules applied.** For cold or resistant readers, open in the felt experience before naming the mechanism (Pattern 10) — facts serve the pre-interested. Every claim carries at least one convincing piece of logic or fact; bigger claims demand more evidence. Bring them to a conclusion, don't hand them one.

**Step 5 — TRANSFORM: the challenger reframe.** The HBR finding underneath this: conventional wisdom says relationship-builders sell best; the actual best reps **challenge the prospect's framing of their own problem** and offer an alternative problem plus solution. The mechanic, in order:
1. **Replace the stated spec with the real need.** The prospect asks for a website with home/about/contact. Every other vendor quotes on that spec. The challenger says *"You don't need a website, you need an online lead generation funnel"* — and then teaches what one is.
2. **Quantify it.** Not "more leads" — the lead lift, the revenue lift, the specific number. Do the math out loud: baseline → growth delta → what a share of that delta is worth. The reframe without the math is an opinion.
3. **Be the last vendor** — the one who challenges what the prospect thinks they need and sells the right solution to the real problem.
4. Self-check before shipping any TTT asset: *"am I challenging my customer to think differently about their problem?"*

**Step 6 — Engineer the eureka moment.** The reframe lands as a eureka when the reader assembles the conclusion themselves rather than reading it. Sequence: teach the alternative path → let the contradiction with their current belief sit → state the reframe in one quotable line. Vosler: *"Never underestimate people's ability to change their minds if you guide them to their 'promise land.'"* A closed deal almost always follows the eureka, not the pitch. If the reframe arrives before the teaching, it reads as a sales tactic and gets rejected.

**Step 7 — TRANSACT: benefit-of-the-benefit, three layers, layer 3 never stated.**
1. Teach the alternative path to what's actually needed (the insight).
2. State the direct benefit — more leads → more revenue.
3. Go one layer deeper — what that revenue actually buys emotionally: time, security, freedom — and **do not state it.** Trigger the reader's imagination instead. Vosler's exact mechanic: not "imagine having more leads so you could have more time with family," but **"Imagine what more leads could mean for you and your business."** The reader's imagination writes copy you couldn't, and self-generated conclusions land harder than stated claims.
The blunt illustration: a hair-growth infomercial selling "stop getting rejected at the bar" is selling confidence, not hair.

**Step 8 — Run the "what are you really selling?" diagnostic.** Ask it of the finished piece: *"What are you really selling? Sell that, not your solution."* The positioning twins from FEAST: website buyers are really buying leads and sales; ad-service buyers are really buying eyeballs to sell more; health-coaching buyers are really buying confidence in themselves. Universal form: **"Consider: what are they actually buying?"** — and everything traces back to increasing sales or reducing risk. If the answer to the diagnostic differs from what the Transact section actually sells, rewrite the Transact section.

**Step 9 — Lift local to universal (Pattern 5).** Before the close, escalate: what is the universal frame above this claim? The piece states both an immediate stake and a bigger-life stake. Draper's move — "It's not called the wheel. It's called the carousel."

**Step 10 — Evaluate the ascent.** Re-read against Step 3's sentences: did the piece actually move the reader up each rung, or does it skip levels, or stall on one? Stalling at Knowledge produces a tutorial. Skipping to Evaluation produces a piece only insiders can read. Fix by rung, not by rewrite. Then apply the yellow-line discipline: one highlighted takeaway per section, and close each with a compressed roman-numeral recap.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content type | Bloom entry rung | Where the reframe lands | Transact shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cornerstone content / long guide | Knowledge or Comprehension | Mid-piece, after two teaching sections | Soft-sell CTA; imagination trigger, no price |
| Sales page | Comprehension | Immediately after the Acknowledgement beat | Full canonical close + layer-3 trigger |
| Webinar / live teach | Knowledge → Application on stage | The pivot beat between teach and pitch | Eureka → offer; do-the-math out loud |
| Sales call / pitch deck | Analysis (they've shopped vendors) | Opening move — replace their spec on slide two | Quantified delta → fee lands trivial |
| Email series | One rung per email, ascending | The Challenge email (see workflow 07) | Imagination triggers seeded across all sends |
| Client proposal | Analysis / Synthesis | The scope section — reframe the deliverable | Growth-partner framing; sales-up or risk-down |

## Output Requirements

1. The SMART objective and the diagnosed Bloom rung, stated up top with the reasoning.
2. **The rung-by-rung outline** — one deliverable-outcome sentence per level, ascending, each becoming a named section.
3. The challenger reframe written as one quotable line, plus the quantification behind it (baseline → delta → what it's worth).
4. The drafted asset with its three phases marked: TEACH / TRANSFORM / TRANSACT.
5. The eureka line, isolated and identified.
6. Layer-3 benefits listed separately in the working notes, and appearing in the copy **only** as imagination triggers or testimonial-carried results-of-results.
7. The "what are you really selling?" diagnostic answered in one sentence, with confirmation the Transact section sells that.
8. Local and universal stakes both stated; one highlighted line per section; a compressed recap closing each major section.

## Quality Gate

- **PASS/FAIL — Hard Veto 5 (no stated layer-3 benefit):** the deepest benefit is triggered, never asserted. Any "so you can finally have more time with your family" sentence fails the Transact phase.
- **PASS/FAIL — Reframe present and quantified:** the piece replaces the reader's stated problem with the real one AND puts a number on it. A reframe with no math is an opinion; a number with no reframe is a spec sheet. Both fail.
- **PASS/FAIL — Ascent verified (Bloom evaluate step):** the draft actually moves through the outlined rungs. Stalling on one rung or skipping a rung fails and is fixed by rung.
- **PASS/FAIL — Hard Veto 1 (no blank-page copy):** the teaching content traces to a real research pass — product, industry, audience — not to general knowledge. Anti-exemplar to avoid: the feature list, and "hustle copy" (inspiration with zero actionable guidance).
- **PASS/FAIL — Rubric row "Stakes":** local AND universal stakes present; the "problem of the problem" is named, not just the problem.
- **PASS/FAIL — Hard Veto 6 (no unverified claims shipped as fact):** every quantification, study reference, and result claim is labeled VERIFIED / LIKELY / UNCONFIRMED, with unverified numbers either sourced or cut. The do-the-math figures use the client's real baseline, never an illustrative one presented as theirs.
