# Contrarian Sales Argument

"All sales messages are, at the most fundamental level, an argument for or against something." Long-form is not length — it is one argument carried to the point where the reader must resolve it.

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. **Mining pass exists** (Hard Veto 1 — No blank-page copy). A language bank from `02-research-arbitrage.md`, a client raw-language dump, or verbatim customer material is on disk. If none exists, run 02 first. Persona language you invented does not count.
2. **Both dissonance preconditions pass** (Pattern 3, hard gate, p192). Name in the brief: (a) **perceived choice** — does the reader believe they have agency here? If the block is a *layered misplaced belief* ("I'm not the kind of person who does X"), that identity belief becomes the argument's real target and gets dissolved first. If it's a **factual belief lock** (contract-locked, physically unable), stop — "you're not going to host an intervention to make a sale." (b) **Meaningful stakes** — consequences they already care about. Missing either = no dissonance = no persuasion, regardless of evidence quality.
3. **SMART objective written** (Pattern 7, p42): "In this [medium] I want to convince my reader that [specific feature] is better for accomplishing [specific task] than their current method. To do this I will show specific evidence, share specific case studies, and appeal to [xyz] emotions."
4. **Format routed before writing** (Hidden Knowledge: format governs depth-of-reveal). Email → Even-If open → straight CTA. Sales page → benefit outline → buy CTA. Content/cornerstone → tactical depth → soft-sell. Same argument, three reveal depths.

## Execution

**1. Write the Acknowledgement/Aggravate pair FIRST.** Everything downstream serves it.
- *Acknowledgement* = what most people think. It does not have to be wrong — "just not as good as your option." Source it from the mined bank (review self-talk, live-ad framings), not from guessing.
- *Aggravate* = the truth that cannot coexist with that belief. Vosler's image: a match to gasoline.
- Write the contention as one sentence. If you can't, you have a topic, not an argument. If the reader already agrees, the job shifts to reaffirming and *activating* the belief rather than changing it.

**2. Attention.** Claim + hook. Position the problem and why it matters. Use an allegory or metaphor to get inside the reader's head and make them feel understood. His own page: "She's looking at you." If a big-promise headline precedes this, open the intro literally with **"Even if…"** answering the jugular objection — the one that loses the most audience — before illusory superiority ("maybe for them, but not me") fires.

**3. Authority — why it's true.** Open the beat with the **borrowed-authority object**: one external, independently checkable artifact (a named magazine cover, a quoted authority, a specific study, a named story) — mined buyer language corroborates behind it, never carries the beat alone (self-sourced authority is assertion). Three evidence classes, all legitimate: scientific research, logical examples, **emotional examples**. Standard: *if you make a claim, you need at least one convincing piece of logic or fact*; the bigger the claim, the more evidence. Don't over-elaborate methodology — link out for the curious, keep the piece lean. Borrowed authority stacks (his Gary Halbert quote under his own claim). Flag anything unverified as unverified (Hard Veto 6).

**4. Angst — the damage of the false belief.** Three sub-beats: what the damage has already done in the reader's life; where continued belief leads; the **"problem of the problem"** list. Keep the monsters real — manufactured or improbable risk sold as near-certain is fear-mongering and fails the deliverable (Hard Veto 2). Dig for the deeper real risk ("a lifetime of unfulfillment in a dead-end job"), not "you might fail."

**5. Lift local → universal (the Draper move).** Before Ambiguity closes the loop, escalate: the immediate stake is local tension; the philosophical stake makes the plot thicken. Ask "what's the universal frame above this claim?" His page lifted "poor messaging = no sales" into "what really drives success." Draper lifted a slide projector: *"It's not called the wheel. It's called the carousel."* The finished piece states BOTH an immediate stake and a bigger-life stake.

**6. Ambiguity — the fix.** What to do now; the specific steps (reduce ambiguity, do not perform mystery); which beliefs have changed; how to self-remind when it gets hard. This is where the epiphany lands: acknowledge current perspective → introduce deeper perspective → belief disharmony → realization → lasting trust.

**7. Pre-refute the imports; stage the FAQ near the close.** The reader has three resolutions: change belief, dispute, or **import outside ideas** to rationalize against you. Handle imports by naming and refuting the likely alternatives inside the copy ("admission of an alternative path"). Then run the staged FAQ objection script on every surfaced objection: **[validate concern] → [answer objection] → [reframe toward superior long-term value]**. Cover the 7 objection types (empathy, authority, logical, urgency, ego, risk, value).

**8. Appeal — the offer transition, on the canonical 8-step close:**
**Here's What You Get → Establish Value → Conditional Bonus → Price → Trivialize Price → Guarantee → Risk Reversal → Scarcity.**
Bullets run *(Feature) + (Benefit) + (Benefit of that benefit)* — and layer 3 is never stated. Replace the deepest-benefit sentence with an imagination trigger: "Imagine what more leads could mean for you and your business" (Hard Veto 5). Ask "what am I really selling?" and sell that.

**9. Bias pass, once, after the argument stands.** 2–3 levers max, each pinned to a named spot: price reveal → anchoring two-stack; objection block → staged FAQ; CTA → loss-framing at a 2:1 benefit-to-risk evidence ratio. Delete every flourish; if the close dies, the piece fails (Hard Veto 3).

**10. Forgiveness and self-undercut pass.** Name at least one false belief and forgive it before any ask (see `04-forgiveness-engine.md`). Keep exactly one honest self-undercut — a flagged unsourced stat, a named limitation. Showing the seams is the credibility strategy; sanding them out reads as guru.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Type | Beat weighting | Reveal depth | Close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales page | All 7 at full length; Angst + Ambiguity carry the most words; 7 zones mappable on the layout | Benefit outline → buy CTA | Full 8-step offer-close + staged FAQ |
| Email | Attention/Acknowledgement/Aggravate compressed to 3–5 lines; Even-If open | Straight to CTA | Single CTA; defer proof to the next asset (~1,000 emails on this structure) |
| Social long-form / cornerstone | Authority + Angst expanded into teaching; genuine tactical value given away | Tactical depth → soft-sell | Soft CTA; "plant value, reap the rewards" |
| Webinar / VSL | "A sales page with interaction"; Ambiguity becomes the taught content block | Full; Appeal held until the pitch turn | 8-step close, 3 CTAs (desire → logic → FOMO) |
| Ad | Contention + one Authority beat + one stake; longer than "buy now" is fine | Minimal | Click as the ask, not the purchase |

## Quality Gate

1. **Blank-page check (Hard Veto 1).** Every headline and bullet traces to a mined artifact. Any invented persona language = FAIL.
2. **Preconditions named (Pattern 3).** Both stated in the brief before line one; factual-belief-lock cases walked away from rather than written around.
3. **Contention in one sentence.** If the piece requires a paragraph to state what it argues, it's a feature tour — FAIL.
4. **Stakes ladder.** Local AND universal both present; "problem of the problem" list exists; no manufactured risk (Hard Veto 2).
5. **Bias-deletion test (Hard Veto 3).** Strip every bias flourish — the argument still closes, or FAIL.
6. **Layer-3 discipline (Hard Veto 5).** No deepest-benefit sentence asserted; every one appears as an imagination trigger or testimonial-carried result.
