# 12 — Growth Positioning (feast. · Coal Calling · Value Ascension)

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. **Do you actually move their number?** Everything traces to one axis: your work either **increases sales or reduces risk**. If you can't name which and roughly how much, the math won't hold — fix the offer first.
2. **Real numbers only.** The do-the-math method runs on **past-client-derived baselines**, never invented projections. His caveat is explicit: results aren't guaranteed; the math depends on the right strategy and on being good at the work.
3. **Product-quality floor accepted.** "Great marketing applied to a crappy product is just adding saffron to a turd."
4. **Objective stated** (Pattern 7): who you're repositioning to, in what medium, and what belief changes.

## Execution

**1 — Vendor→growth-partner reframe.** *"Good clients don't care what you do, they care about how 'what you do' affects their bottom line."* Every service resolves to increasing sales or reducing risk, so the diagnostic isn't "what do I sell?" but **"what are they actually buying?"** The freelancer isn't selling a website; he's selling a share of the growth it produces. Write one sentence — *"You're not buying [deliverable]. You're buying [the growth or risk reduction it produces]"* — then lift it once (Pattern 5): not "more leads," but what the business becomes when leads stop being the constraint.

**2 — Do the math, out loud.**
1. **Baseline** — a real or past-client-derived figure: the client does $1M/year.
2. **Growth delta** — project the tangible outcome: doubling to $2M = **$1M in growth**.
3. **Reframe the ask** — you're selling a share of $1M in growth, not a website.
4. **Share of the delta** — a modest gross-share benchmark on **just the growth**, not total revenue. Vosler uses ~**10%** → **$100,000 of worth**.
5. **Anchor the fee** — *"Would you be comfortable charging $20,000+ for your service if you knew it had a potential $1M upside for the customer?"* Against $100K of worth, $20K lands trivial.
6. **Equity variant** — structured as a profit- or revenue-share from the outset, that $100K becomes the literal entitled payout (give or take gross vs. profit basis).

State the caveats in the same breath: not guaranteed, dependent on strategy and execution, built from past client results. The three effects he names, worth including because they're what the *client* gets from the structure: the client becomes an engaged partner who moves mountains because the stakes are legible; competitors sell solutions and you sell growth, so comparison collapses; more per client means fewer clients and your time back.

**3 — Coal Calling qualification, before pursuit.** *"Sell the right thing to the right person and you won't have to sell at all."* He disclaims pitchman skill — the close rate comes from targeting.
1. **Don't wait for inbound.** Identify the exact client type that benefits *enough* to justify premium or equity fees. A qualifying filter, not a demographic profile.
2. **Math-makes-sense test first.** If your work can't move the needle enough for Step 2's arithmetic, it's a bad-fit lead out the gate. Easier to justify $20K taking a $1M company to $1.2M than doubling a $5K/month company — the **absolute-dollar delta** matters, not the percentage.
3. **Position, don't pitch.** Understand the "right thing" — your offer reframed as growth — and position it *before* the conversation. A sales call is not where positioning gets invented.
4. **The six diagnostic questions**: Where do I find clients who need my help to grow? · Inbound, or systematically hunt specific client types? · How do I optimize offer and positioning to sell "growth"? · How do I position without sounding salesy? · Can I structure equity deals inside my existing model? · Does the growth math work for this prospect?
5. **Four-point recap**: I — redefine your worth via positioning; II — sell only where you've pre-identified you can help them grow, and pursue only those; III — don't sell a service, position as a partner and deliver; IV — selling is trust plus honesty about what the lead actually needs, not what they want.

**4 — Value Ascension: two products.** **P1 — Step-by-Step Tactical Guide, $24–$49**: solves ONE narrow problem; book or mini-course; low-friction entry. **P2 — Time-Framed Strategic Guide, $199–$499**: solves multiple related problems *and* presents a path to a bigger transformation, something "new" for the buyer; step-by-step video course; the backend upsell to P1 buyers.

**5 — Fill in the $20K/mo table.** His brief: objective = launch a sustainable info product; goal = $20K/month within 30 days; measure = 80 P1 sales/month minimum with a 20% take-rate on P2. Replace each input:

| Input | His default | Yours |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors (weekly) | 1,000 | |
| P1 price / conversion | $24 / 8.00% | |
| P1 units / revenue | 80 / $1,920 | |
| P2 price / conversion of P1 buyers | $199 / 20.00% | |
| P2 units / revenue | 16 / $3,184 | |
| **Revenue / week** | **$5,104** | |
| **Revenue / month** | **$20,416** | |
| Share P1 / P2 | 62.38% / 37.62% | |

Arithmetic: Visitors × P1 conv = P1 units × P1 price = P1 revenue; P1 units × P2 conv = P2 units × P2 price = P2 revenue; sum × ~4 = monthly. Ship his reality-check with it: traffic and conversion competence isn't assumed, you build it alongside the product. And reframe lump-sum fantasy math ("1,000 sales at $1,000!") into monthly targets — 80 sales a week is a number a person can picture.

The four steps carrying the build: **Compass** — unflinching commitment to quality, refined as you build, without tipping into perfectionism. **Map** — a working, reusable list of assets, quality standards, and faster ways of doing things across business model, product structure, and marketing; adapting a working structure beats inventing one. **Vessel** — everything around the product: credibility, strategic partners, voice and brand, distribution; "as complex as a battle ship, or as simple as a rowboat." **Putting It All Together** — this framework gives strategy; execution tactics live outside it, so route them from your own tactical library.

**6 — Reverse Product Development.** Write the marketing **before** building the product. Mapping and rewriting copy for a hypothetical product surfaces where you need more features or benefits, who it's actually for, and better stories with deeper empathy. The loop: identify a problem → map a solution → develop your message → optimize your solution → test your messaging — message development sits *before and alongside* solution optimization, never after. It prevents building from "people would love it if they could do X faster" on the assumption the idea sells itself. Floor stands: saffron on a turd.

**7 — Dung-beetle resource matching.** *"The Right Strategy Can Beat The Biggest Advantages."* Two morphs: Chad invests in horns and fights rivals; Winston invests elsewhere, tunnels past the fight, and out-reproduces him. Don't out-Chad Chad. Name the axis you actually have — **capital**, **distribution/audience access**, or **time and experience** — and match strategy to it. Advantage without strategy: Facebook Phone, Amazon Fire Phone, Blackberry, Google+, Google Glass, Microsoft Zune.

## Quality Gate

- **Numbers derived, not invented** — every figure traces to a past client result or a stated assumption. Unlabeled projections = **Hard Veto 6, No unverified claims shipped as fact** — fail.
- **Real stakes** — the risk of inaction is a genuine business consequence, not a manufactured near-certainty. Violation = **Hard Veto 2, No fear-mongering** — fail.
- **Provenance** — the client's own words for their bottleneck appear in the positioning, not your paraphrase. Violation = **Hard Veto 1, No blank-page copy** — fail.
- **Argument survives ornament removal** — delete anchoring, trivialization, and scarcity from the fee reveal and the worth math still justifies the price. Otherwise = **Hard Veto 3** — fail.
- **Layer-3 discipline** — time back, freedom, fewer clients are triggered ("imagine what fewer, better clients would mean"), never asserted. Violation = **Hard Veto 5** — fail.
- **Qualification precedes pursuit** — the math-makes-sense test is documented before any outreach exists. Pitching unqualified = fail (Coal Calling recap II).
- **Product floor honored** — one line stating why the underlying product survives its own marketing. Missing = fail.
