# 07 — KLTCC Sequence (Know · Like · Trust · Challenge · Convert)

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. **Hard Veto 1 — no blank-page copy.** Trust and Like run on real biography; Challenge on a real customer problem. The operator's actual low point, origin, and mined audience language are on the page before drafting. Invented vulnerability is the worst thing you can put in the Trust slot.
2. **Two dissonance preconditions (Pattern 3).** Challenge attempts a belief change: verify **perceived choice** (if the block is "I'm not the kind of person who does X," dissolve that identity belief first) and **meaningful stakes** before writing email 4.
3. **Objective before tactics (Pattern 7).** One SMART objective for the sequence, plus the named paid offer email 5 lands on.
4. **Forgiveness before persuasion (Pattern 6).** One false self-belief named with its situational reframe ready — "THE REAL PROBLEM IS… you simply haven't…" A forgiving tone is mandatory; literal forgiveness lines are optional.

## Execution

Every email runs the book's two-column shape: **answer the worksheet questions in writing first, then draft from the answers.** The worksheet is source material, not a warm-up.

**Email #1 — KNOW.**
*Worksheet:* Who are they getting to know (you / brand / product / themselves — usually all)? Why should they be excited to know you, framed as benefits of knowing you rather than accomplishments? What will you help them accomplish (case studies of help given, or mistakes you've solved)? When did you get started? What events led you to start — what were you tired of, what got you excited, what drove you here?
*Beats:* welcome and thank them for opting in → disarm expectations with humor → promise a multi-email teaching arc → humanize with real specifics (name, family, pets, "just a normal guy") → state the stakes (why most attempts in this category fail) → **value-first CTA to a short teaching asset, not a pitch** → soft engagement ask → **PS/PPS stack: one soft CTA, one hard cliffhanger naming tomorrow's topic**, plus a whitelist request.
*Principle:* people don't buy on facts and figures alone — emotion and price both scale with story.

**Email #2 — LIKE.**
*Worksheet:* What people and organizations do you like to associate with? What principles and values do you want associated with you? Where did you grow up and how did it shape your view of business? What do you enjoy most about your business, in the context of your customers?
*Beats:* recap the prior takeaway → name a **universal problem** no one in the category escapes → a **vulnerability beat** (lost, frozen, before finding focus) → reframe into what you now love about fixing it → urgency stakes → **explicitly withhold the solution for tomorrow** → soft value add with a community or pricing tease in the PS.
*Principle:* humor as a bonding mechanism, not decoration; stories woven into the teaching.

**Email #3 — TRUST.**
*Worksheet:* What personal details would readers find genuinely interesting? Who do you associate with in the industry? How have you helped others succeed? How has your income let you live more fulfilled?
*Beats:* **raw low-point story** (Vosler's own: sleeping on a tennis court after financial hardship) → **hard contrast cut to the present** → **mindset shift stated plainly** ("money is a TOOL, nothing else") → **engagement CTA** asking readers to share their own view, driving them to a comment section → cliffhanger naming tomorrow's protagonist.
*Principle:* don't be shy about successes; if first-person claims feel uncomfortable, video testimonials carry the trust instead.

**Email #4 — CHALLENGE.**
*Worksheet:* What industry assumptions have you proven wrong? What drove your success contrary to popular belief? What's the #1 problem facing your customers' businesses? How did you find the answer? What unrecognized needs do they have? What is the REAL problem they don't know they have? How will you guide them to the solution?
*Beats:* **persona parable** (Vosler's "Sarah": skilled freelance web designer, quit her job, became her own accountant/support/sales/marketing/dev, traded a 9-5 for a 5am-9pm, sales declined with no lead-gen system) → **explicit bridge line** ("earlier we focused on sales; the systems *after* the sale need to be solid so you can scale WITHOUT burning yourself out") → **40,000-feet challenge prompt** with a **self-diagnostic multiple-choice list** (boost traffic? optimize lead funnels? find the right lead sources? sift the countless methods for the right ones?) → **systematize reframe** — the instinct is to power through; what's required is to systematize, "this is what 100% of successful businesses do" → cliffhanger + soft webinar CTA in the PS.
*Principle:* be the last vendor. Self-check: *"am I challenging my customer to think differently about their problem?"*

**Email #5 — CONVERT.**
*Worksheet:* What is the REAL solution? How did it feel finding it? What measurable effects will your method produce? What's the #1 reason to act now?
*Beats:* **resolve the cliffhanger** — no simple solution, but a smarter one → **smarter-solution triplet** ("the smarter solution is to learn from others who've solved it… to use the tools your customers already use… to learn from the experts") → **social-proof stack** establishing category credibility → name the paid offer → **trivialize the price** (Vosler's "water bottle investment" of $1) → **scarcity** (trial price and seat count both finite) → **bonus stack** (recurring group access + a lifetime discount for acting today) → **explicit "#1 Reason You Should Sign Up Today" block** (most people won't invest in themselves; this is the first step) → **risk reversal restated** (cancel anytime, no further charge).
*Principle:* close on benefit-of-the-benefit — insight → direct benefit → benefit OF that benefit, layer 3 never stated: *"Imagine what more leads could mean for you and your business."* The sequence should be "riddled with subtle imagination triggers," direct or testimonial-carried.

**Cross-email pass — the cliffhanger chain.** Read all five end to end: #1's PS names #2's topic → #2 withholds the solution → #3 names #4's protagonist → #4 defers the parable → #5 resolves it and pivots to the offer. A break is a structural defect, not a stylistic one.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content type | KLTCC mapping | Compresses | Never cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5-email onboarding (canonical) | One per letter, daily | Nothing — the reference build | Cliffhanger chain, layer-3 trigger |
| Long-form sales page | Ascending page sections | Know/Like fuse into one origin block | Challenge section, canonical close |
| Webinar / VSL | Know+Like intro, Trust story, Challenge teach, Convert pitch | Worksheets become speaker notes | Eureka reframe, trivialized price |
| Nurture newsletter | Five issues, weeks apart | Scarcity, bonus stack | Challenge — the reframe is the value |
| Onboarding DM sequence | Know+Like / Trust+Challenge / Convert | The PS stack | Vulnerability beat, real-problem reframe |
| Cold ad-to-email funnel | Experience-before-mechanism in #1 | Industry framing in #2 | Opening in scene, never definition |

## Output Requirements

1. **Per email:** answered worksheet questions first (visible), then the drafted email.
2. Subject line, body with beats labeled in the margin, CTA, PS/PPS stack where the beat map calls for one.
3. **Cliffhanger ledger** — five rows: email → loop opened → email that closes it.
4. The named paid offer with its full canonical close on email #5.
5. The false belief and forgiveness reframe used, cited to its email.
6. One honest self-undercut in the sequence. Never sanded out.

## Quality Gate

- **Hard Veto 5 (no stated layer-3 benefit):** the deepest benefit appears only as an imagination trigger or testimonial-carried results-of-results. One "so you can spend more time with your family" fails email 5.
- **Hard Veto 1 (no blank-page copy):** the Trust low point and Challenge parable derive from real material. Fabricated vulnerability fails outright.
- **Cliffhanger chain intact:** every email but the last opens a loop the next closes; the ledger proves it.
- **Rubric row "Tone" (forgiving):** a false belief named and forgiven before any ask; no email accuses the reader of being an idiot; one honest self-undercut present.
- **Challenge actually challenges:** #4 replaces the reader's stated problem with the real one and carries a self-diagnostic choice set. A #4 that relates rather than reframes fails.
- **Hard Vetoes 2 and 6:** scarcity and urgency in #5 are real (the deadline exists); every stat, name, and result claim is VERIFIED or removed.
