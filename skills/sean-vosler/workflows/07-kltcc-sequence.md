# 07 — KLTCC Sequence (Know · Like · Trust · Challenge · Convert)

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. **Hard Veto 1 — no blank-page copy.** The Trust and Like emails run on real biography and the Challenge email runs on a real customer problem. Before drafting: the operator's actual low-point story, actual origin, and mined audience language (review mining, community arbitrage, or the client's own raw material) are on the page. Invented vulnerability is the worst possible thing to put in the Trust slot.
2. **Two dissonance preconditions (Pattern 3).** The Challenge email attempts a belief change — verify **perceived choice** (the reader believes they have agency; if the block is "I'm not the kind of person who does X," dissolve that identity belief first) and **meaningful stakes** before writing beat 4.
3. **Objective before tactics (Pattern 7).** One SMART objective for the sequence as a whole, plus the named paid offer the Convert email lands on. No named offer = the sequence has nowhere to go.
4. **Forgiveness before persuasion (Pattern 6).** At least one false belief the reader holds about themselves is named, with the situational reframe ready — "THE REAL PROBLEM IS… you simply haven't…" A forgiving tone is mandatory across all five; literal forgiveness lines are optional.

## Execution

Every email runs the same two-move shape from the book's two-column pages: **answer the worksheet questions in writing first, then draft the email from the answers.** The worksheet is not a warm-up; it is the source material.

**Email #1 — KNOW.**
*Worksheet:* Who are they getting to know (you / your brand / your product / themselves — usually all)? Why should they be excited to know you — framed as benefits of knowing you, not a list of accomplishments? What will you help them accomplish (case studies of help given, or mistakes you've solved for yourself and others)? When did you get started in the industry? What events led you to start — what were you tired of, what got you excited, what drove you here?
*Beats:* welcome and thank them for opting in → disarm expectations with humor → promise a multi-email teaching arc → humanize with real specifics (name, family, pets, "just a normal guy" framing) → state the stakes (why most attempts in this category fail) → **value-first CTA to a short teaching asset, not a pitch** → soft engagement ask → **PS/PPS stack: one soft CTA, one hard cliffhanger naming tomorrow's topic**, plus a whitelist request.
*Governing principle:* people don't buy on facts and figures alone; emotion and price both scale with story.

**Email #2 — LIKE.**
*Worksheet:* What people and organizations do you like to associate with? What principles and values do you want associated with you? Where did you grow up and how did it shape your view of business? What do you enjoy most about your business, in the context of your customers?
*Beats:* recap the prior email's takeaway → name a **universal problem** (something no one in the category escapes) → a **vulnerability beat** (felt lost, frozen, before finding focus) → reframe into what you now love doing about it → urgency stakes → **deliberately withhold the solution, explicitly, for tomorrow** → soft value add (a video, a resource) with a community or pricing tease in the PS.
*Governing principle:* humor as a bonding mechanism, not decoration; stories woven into the teaching, not bolted on.

**Email #3 — TRUST.**
*Worksheet:* What personal details would readers find genuinely interesting? Who do you associate with in the industry? How have you helped others succeed? How has your income let you live a more fulfilled life?
*Beats:* **raw low-point story** (Vosler's own: sleeping on a tennis court after financial hardship) → **hard contrast cut to the present** → **mindset shift stated plainly** ("money is a TOOL, nothing else") → **engagement CTA** that asks readers to share their own view and drives them to a comment section → cliffhanger naming tomorrow's protagonist by name.
*Governing principle:* don't be shy about successes; if first-person claims feel uncomfortable, let video testimonials carry the trust instead. Note what the book is doing here — it practices Know-Like-Trust on you while teaching it.

**Email #4 — CHALLENGE.**
*Worksheet:* What industry assumptions have you proven wrong? What drove your success contrary to popular belief? What's the #1 problem facing your customers' businesses? How did you find the answer? What unrecognized needs do your customers have? What is the REAL problem they don't know they have? How will you guide them to the solution?
*Beats:* **persona parable** (Vosler's "Sarah": skilled freelance web designer, quit her job, became her own accountant/support/sales/marketing/dev, traded a 9-5 for a 5am-9pm, sales declined because no lead-gen system existed) → **explicit bridge line** connecting the earlier emails' theme to the new one ("earlier we focused on sales; the systems *after* the sale need to be solid so you can scale WITHOUT burning yourself out") → **40,000-feet challenge prompt** with a **self-diagnostic multiple-choice list** (boost traffic? optimize lead funnels? find the right lead sources? sift the countless methods for the right ones?) → **systematize reframe** — the instinct is to power through; what's actually required is to systematize, "this is what 100% of successful businesses do" → cliffhanger (the parable resolves tomorrow) + a soft webinar/asset CTA in the PS.
*Governing principle:* be the last vendor — challenge what they think they need. Self-check before sending: *"am I challenging my customer to think differently about their problem?"*

**Email #5 — CONVERT.**
*Worksheet:* What is the REAL solution to the problem? How did it feel finding the solution? What measurable effects will your method produce for customers? What's the #1 reason they should take action now?
*Beats:* **resolve the cliffhanger** — there is no simple solution, but there is a smarter one → **smarter-solution triplet** in rhetorical repetition ("the smarter solution is to learn from others who've solved it… to use the tools your customers already use… to learn from the experts") → **social-proof stack** establishing category credibility → name the actual paid offer → **trivialize the price** (Vosler's "water bottle investment" of $1) → **scarcity** (the trial price and the seat count both finite) → **bonus stack** (recurring group access + a lifetime discount for acting today) → **explicit "#1 Reason You Should Sign Up Today" block** (most people won't invest in themselves; this is the first step) → **risk reversal restated** (cancel anytime, no further charge).
*Governing principle:* Convert closes on **benefit-of-the-benefit** — insight → direct benefit → the benefit OF that benefit, with layer 3 never stated. *"Imagine what more leads could mean for you and your business."* The sequence should be "riddled with subtle imagination triggers," delivered either directly or through testimonials that share results-of-results.

**Cross-email pass — the cliffhanger chain.** After all five drafts exist, read the sequence end to end and verify each email opens a loop that the next one closes: #1's PS names #2's topic → #2 explicitly withholds the solution → #3 names #4's protagonist → #4 defers the parable's resolution → #5 resolves it and pivots into the offer. A break anywhere in the chain is a structural defect, not a stylistic one — fix it before shipping.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content type | KLTCC mapping | What compresses | What must not be cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5-email onboarding (canonical) | One email per letter, daily | Nothing — this is the reference build | Cliffhanger chain, layer-3 imagination trigger |
| Single long-form sales page | KLTCC as ascending page sections | Know/Like fuse into one origin block | Challenge section and the full canonical offer-close |
| Webinar / VSL | Know+Like in the intro, Trust in the story, Challenge in the teach, Convert in the pitch | Worksheet answers become the speaker notes | The eureka reframe and the trivialized-price beat |
| Content / nurture newsletter | KLTCC over five issues, weeks apart | Scarcity and bonus stack drop out | Challenge (the reframe is the value) |
| Client onboarding / DM sequence | KLTCC compressed to 3 messages: Know+Like / Trust+Challenge / Convert | The PS stack | Vulnerability beat and the real-problem reframe |
| Cold-audience ad-to-email funnel | Front-load Experience Before Mechanism (Pattern 10) in #1 | Industry framing in #2 | Opening in scene rather than definition |

## Output Requirements

1. **Per email:** the answered worksheet questions first (visible in the deliverable, not discarded), then the drafted email.
2. Each email carries: subject line, body with beats labeled in the margin, CTA, and the PS/PPS stack where the beat map calls for one.
3. **Cliffhanger ledger** — a five-row table: email → loop opened → email that closes it. Any unclosed loop flagged.
4. The named paid offer and its full canonical close on Email #5: Here's What You Get → Establish Value → Conditional Bonus → Price → Trivialize Price → Guarantee → Risk Reversal → Scarcity.
5. The false belief named and the forgiveness reframe used, cited to the email it appears in.
6. One honest self-undercut somewhere in the sequence (Hidden Knowledge — the escape hatch is the trust move). Never sanded out.

## Quality Gate

- **PASS/FAIL — Hard Veto 5 (no stated layer-3 benefit):** the deepest benefit appears only as an imagination trigger or as a testimonial carrying results-of-results. One sentence that states "so you can spend more time with your family" fails the Convert email.
- **PASS/FAIL — Hard Veto 1 (no blank-page copy):** the Trust low-point and the Challenge parable derive from real material — the operator's own history and mined audience language. Fabricated vulnerability fails outright.
- **PASS/FAIL — Cliffhanger chain intact:** every email but the last opens a loop the next one closes. The ledger proves it.
- **PASS/FAIL — Rubric row "Tone" (forgiving):** a false belief is named and forgiven before any ask, and no email accuses the reader of being an idiot. One honest self-undercut present.
- **PASS/FAIL — Challenge actually challenges (Pattern: be the last vendor):** Email #4 replaces the reader's stated problem with the real one and contains a self-diagnostic choice set. A #4 that merely relates, rather than reframes, fails.
- **PASS/FAIL — Hard Vetoes 2 and 6:** scarcity and urgency in #5 are real and honorable (the deadline actually exists); every stat, name, and result claim is VERIFIED or removed.
