# 06 — Bias Stack (Cognitive Manipulation, Applied Late)

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. **Clean argument first (Pattern 8 / Hard Veto 3).** A finished draft exists whose contention states in one sentence and which closes with zero bias flourishes present. Not closing yet? Back to the Contrarian Copy Structure. In the book "engine" is literally struck through and replaced with "fuel."
2. **Ethical gate, verbatim (p70):** *"If, and only if, you believe in your offer's real value should you consider influencing your reader with these strategies."*
3. **Preconditions hold (Pattern 3):** perceived choice + meaningful stakes. Biases manufacture neither.
4. **Lever budget set before selection:** 2-3 for the whole piece, each pre-assigned to one location. More is a tell — once "found out," trust is gone for good.

## Execution

**1 — Baseline.** Read the clean draft; write one sentence on why it closes. That is the control every lever must survive deletion against.

**2 — Map locations.** Mark the attachable spots: **opener**, **evidence block**, **price reveal**, **objection block**, **CTA**, **post-sale**. A lever without a location is decoration, not a selection.

**3 — Select 2-3 levers.** The arsenal, mechanism → natural location:

- **Affect Heuristic** — swing valence in and out, land net-positive immediately before the CTA (when risk gets calculated). Negatives attach to competition, other approaches, inaction; positives to your offer. Net shape: "a Hollywood blockbuster hero film with just enough drama, excitement, peril, and uplifting twists." Subtle > overt. *Opener + pre-CTA.*
- **Choice Overload + Rule of Two** — ≤3 tiers, common options first with the rest behind "load more," minimum form fields, options shown visually. **Never one option** (they just leave), never many; frame the conscious binary: "Sign up today" vs. "No, I'm not interested in…" — "a conscious decision is always better than an unconscious one." *Pricing, opt-in, CTA.*
- **Forer/Barnum + quiz segmentation** — "you're the kind of person who…" assumes shared identity then predicts their emotional reaction; non-targets self-select out, which is fine. Segmentation: 1-10 quiz → 3 tiers → tailored validate-then-pitch per tier. *Opener or pre-CTA identity beat.*
- **Framing — the 7 tactics.** (1) *Contextual Savings* ("for less than the price of a cup of coffee per day"; or an expensive comparator to inflate value). (2) *Picking the Right Number* — good → big frame ("200% growth"), bad → small frame ("only 1% of the total"); two valid readings → take the positive ("80% fat-free"). (3) *Savings or Penalty?* — the saving AND the risk of not acting at once. (4) *Good vs. Evil* — brand as the tool for customer-as-hero; enemy internal (mindset, habits) or external (competitors, time). (5) *Us vs. Everyone Else*. (6) *Unique in a Sea of the Same* — new way better, old way toward disaster. (7) *"Because I'm Worth It"* — entitlement to the benefit. Governing question: "am I presenting my information in the most attractive way?" *Price reveal, positioning.*
- **Loss aversion + staged FAQ + 2:1 ratio.** Losses ≈ 2x gains; primary emphasis on losses of inaction, secondary on gains of acting. Tactics: kill the coupon field if you offer no coupon; loss-frame bonuses and their window; live scarcity ("Only 1 Room Left," expiring timers); order bumps; strike-through features in the lower tier; loss-flavored rewrites. **2:1 benefit-to-risk ratio** — purchase is perceived as risk by default; evidence must outweigh it twofold. **Staged F.A.Q.:** `Q: [likely miscalculated objection]. A: [validate concern] → [answer objection] → [reframe toward superior long-term value].` *Objection block, checkout, CTA.*
- **Anchoring two-stack** — expensive familiar comparator → "even at that price it's justified" → reveal. *Price reveal, exactly once.*
- **Bandwagon** — "Thousands Have Figured Out…", testimonials distributed not pooled, live social proof. *Pre-CTA.*
- **Commitment / choice-supportive** — testimonials immediately pre-CTA, post-sale affirmation ("Great Choice!"), unannounced bonus near the refund deadline. *CTA, post-purchase.*
- **Pro-Innovation** — `[Feature] + [Benefit] + [Innovation framing]`, calibrated to the audience's frame of "new," not the market's. *Mechanism block.*
- **Mere Exposure — IMPACT + FREQUENCY + TIME** — one singular big idea repeated, retargeting, tolerated frequency; devices **Anaphora** (repetition at clause start — MLK / Dickens / Churchill models) and **Epistrophe** (at clause end). Works when the brand is new; repetition won't flip an existing negative. *Campaign level + one anaphoric run in-piece.*
- **Nostalgia ("pain from an old wound")** — three levels: *company branding* (narrow-cohort heritage brands only), *product branding* (vintage treatment; the buyer needn't have lived the era), *campaign level* (visuals/music + the **Invoking Shared Nostalgic Values** 6-line skeleton: "A lot has changed, but, like you, we still believe…" → "that ___ is still true" → "It may seem old fashioned…" → "Remember when…?" → "Wouldn't it be great if…?" → "Well that's why we…"). Deep craft, not retro-font pandering. *Opener or campaign wrapper.*

**4 — Write each lever into its location.** One lever, one location, one edit. A lever smeared across the piece is the flash-in-the-pan tactic that gets you found out.

**5 — Annotate-in-place QA (Pattern 11).** Function-tag every insertion: `{affect: land positive pre-CTA}`, `{anchor: comparator}`, `{staged FAQ: validate}`. Untagged insertions come out.

**6 — The delete test (hard gate).** Strip every tagged insertion, re-read. Still closes on the step-1 control sentence? If not, the argument never stood and the pass is masking a weak claim — revert everything, return to the Contrarian Copy Structure.

**7 — Ethical sweep.** (a) you believe in the offer's real value; (b) **raising the stakes without fear-mongering** — no manufactured or improbable risk sold as near-certain, dig for the deeper real one; (c) every Barnum statement is high-probability-true of the actual target.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content type | Levers that fit | Avoid | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-form sales page | Anchoring, staged FAQ, affect landing | All 7 frames stacked | Anchor once at price; FAQ pre-CTA |
| Email sequence | Mere exposure, loss-framed bonus window | Anchoring, quiz segmentation | One lever per email |
| Paid ad / short-form | Affect, one frame, loss rewrite | Staged FAQ, bonus stacks | First two lines |
| Checkout / pricing | Rule of Two, tier cap, order bump, strike-through | Nostalgia, Barnum | Binary CTA; ≤3 tiers |
| Content / editorial | Anaphora, pro-innovation, community frame | Scarcity tech, trivialized price | Invisible or it reads as a pitch |
| Brand / campaign | Nostalgia 3-level map, mere exposure | Loss-aversion tactics | Campaign frequency, not in-piece |

## Output Requirements

1. The step-1 control sentence.
2. **Lever table:** lever · named location · exact inserted copy · mechanism in one line.
3. The revised draft, insertions function-tagged in place.
4. **Delete-test result stated explicitly** — still closes / doesn't, with reasoning.
5. Ethical sweep sign-off: offer-value belief, real risk named, Barnum truth check.
6. Levers rejected and why.

## Quality Gate

- **Hard Veto 3 (no bias play without a standalone message):** delete every flourish; if the close dies, the piece fails. Run literally, report the result.
- **Lever budget:** 2-3 total, each pinned to one named location. Four or more, or an unlocated lever, fails.
- **Hard Veto 2 (no fear-mongering):** every risk invoked is real and likely. "Keep the monsters real, even if you wish to get creative with how big their teeth are."
- **Hard Veto 4 (no flattery-Barnum):** every "you're the kind of person who…" is high-probability-true of the actual target.
- **Rubric "Objection handling":** loss aversion selected → staged FAQ runs full validate → answer → reframe, and meets the 2:1 ratio.
- **Rubric "Tone" + Pattern 11:** forgiving tone survived; 100% of insertions carry a function tag.
