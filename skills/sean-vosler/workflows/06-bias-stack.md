# 06 — Bias Stack (Cognitive Manipulation, Applied Late)

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. **Clean argument first (Pattern 8 / Hard Veto 3).** A finished draft exists whose contention can be stated in one sentence, and which closes with zero bias flourishes present. If the draft is not yet closing on its own, this workflow does not run — go back to the Contrarian Copy Structure. In the book the word "engine" is literally struck through and replaced with "fuel." Biases are fuel.
2. **Ethical gate, verbatim (p70).** *"If, and only if, you believe in your offer's real value should you consider influencing your reader with these strategies."* Persuasion = everyone wins, reader decides fairly. Manipulation = control unfairly, only your interest. If the offer's value is in doubt, stop and say so.
3. **Two dissonance preconditions still hold (Pattern 3).** Perceived choice + meaningful stakes. Biases do not manufacture either. No agency or no stakes = no amount of anchoring rescues the piece.
4. **Lever budget set before selection.** 2-3 levers maximum for the whole piece, each pre-assigned to one named location (price reveal, objection block, CTA, opener). More than three is a tell, and the reader who is "found out" is lost for good.

## Execution

**Step 1 — Baseline read: delete-test the draft in advance.** Read the clean draft and write down, in one sentence, why it closes. That sentence is the control. Every lever added from here must survive deletion against it.

**Step 2 — Map the locations.** Mark the named spots the levers can attach to: **opener** (affect valence, Barnum), **evidence block**, **price reveal** (anchoring, framing), **objection block** (staged FAQ, loss aversion), **CTA** (affect landing, Rule of Two, loss framing), **post-sale** (commitment reinforcement). A lever without a location is not selected — it's decoration.

**Step 3 — Select 2-3 levers from the arsenal.** The decoded set, with its mechanism and its natural location:

- **Affect Heuristic** — swing valence in and out, land net-positive immediately before the CTA (that's when risk gets calculated). Two associative rules: negatives attach to the competition, other approaches, and inaction; positives attach to your offer. Net shape: "a Hollywood blockbuster hero film with just enough drama, excitement, peril, and uplifting twists." Subtle > overt — overplayed emotion reads as manipulation. **Location: opener + the beat before CTA.**
- **Choice Overload + the Rule of Two** — ≤3 tiers per configuration, common options first with the rest behind "load more," minimum form fields, options presented visually. **Never one option** (the reader just leaves) and never many; frame the conscious binary: "Sign up today" vs. "No, I'm not interested in…" — "a conscious decision is always better than an unconscious one." **Location: pricing table, opt-in, CTA.**
- **Forer/Barnum + quiz segmentation** — "you're the kind of person who…" statements that assume a shared identity and then predict the reader's emotional reaction; non-targets self-select out, which is fine. Segmentation variant: a 1-10 scale quiz → 3 tiers → a tailored validate-then-pitch message per tier. **Location: opener, or the pre-CTA identity beat.**
- **Framing — the 7 tactics.** (1) *Contextual Savings* ("for less than the price of a cup of coffee per day"; or an expensive comparator to inflate value). (2) *Picking the Right Number* — good number → big frame ("200% growth"), bad number → small frame ("only 1% of the total"); when a number has two valid readings, take the positive one ("80% fat-free"). (3) *Savings or Penalty?* — show the saving AND the risk of not acting at once. (4) *Good vs. Evil* — brand as the tool for the customer-as-hero; enemy internal (mindset, habits) or external (competitors, time). (5) *Us vs. Everyone Else* community frame. (6) *Unique in a Sea of the Same* — new way better, old way toward disaster. (7) *"Because I'm Worth It"* — entitlement to the benefit. Governing question: "am I presenting my information in the most attractive way?" **Location: price reveal, positioning block.**
- **Loss aversion tactics + staged FAQ + 2:1 evidence ratio.** Losses register at ~2x gains. Primary emphasis on the losses of inaction; secondary on the gains of acting. Tactical checklist: remove the coupon field if you offer no coupon; loss-frame bonuses and their window; live scarcity ("Only 1 Room Left," expiring timers); order bumps at checkout; strike-through features in the lower tier; loss-flavored rewrites. **2:1 benefit-to-risk ratio** — every purchase is perceived as risk by default; supply evidence weighing more than double it. **Staged F.A.Q.**: `Q: [likely miscalculated objection]. A: [validate concern] → [answer objection] → [reframe toward superior long-term value].` **Location: objection block, checkout, CTA.**
- **Anchoring two-stack** — expensive familiar comparator → "even at that price it's justified" → reveal the real price. **Location: price reveal, exactly once.**
- **Bandwagon** — phrase bank ("Thousands Have Figured Out…"), testimonials distributed rather than pooled, live social proof. **Location: pre-CTA.**
- **Commitment / choice-supportive** — testimonials immediately before the CTA, post-sale affirmation ("Great Choice!"), an unannounced bonus near the refund deadline. **Location: CTA and post-purchase.**
- **Pro-Innovation** — `[Feature] + [Benefit] + [Innovation framing]`, calibrated to the audience's frame of "new," not the market's. **Location: mechanism block.**
- **Mere Exposure — IMPACT + FREQUENCY + TIME** — one singular big idea repeated, retargeting tolerated at higher frequency, and the rhetorical devices: **Anaphora** (repetition at clause start — MLK, Dickens, Churchill models) / **Epistrophe** (repetition at clause end). Works best when the brand is genuinely new; repetition does not flip an existing negative association. **Location: campaign level + one anaphoric run inside the piece.**
- **Nostalgia ("pain from an old wound")** — three levels: *company branding* (narrow-cohort only, heritage brands), *product branding* (vintage treatment; the buyer needn't have lived the era), *campaign level* (visuals/music plus the **Invoking Shared Nostalgic Values** 6-line skeleton: "A lot has changed, but, like you, we still believe…" → "that ___ is still true" → "It may seem old fashioned…" → "Remember when…?" → "Wouldn't it be great if…?" → "Well that's why we…"). Handle with respect — deep craft, not retro-font pandering. **Location: opener or campaign wrapper.**

**Step 4 — Write each lever into its location.** One lever, one location, one edit. Do not spread a lever across the piece; a lever smeared everywhere is the flash-in-the-pan tactic that gets you "found out."

**Step 5 — Annotate-in-place QA (Pattern 11).** Function-tag every inserted clause: `{affect: land positive pre-CTA}`, `{anchor: comparator}`, `{staged FAQ: validate}`. Any inserted clause with no tag comes out.

**Step 6 — The delete test (hard gate).** Strip every tagged insertion. Re-read. Does the piece still close on the Step-1 control sentence? If not, the argument was never standing and the bias pass is masking a weak claim — revert everything and go back to the Contrarian Copy Structure. This is Hard Veto 3, not advice.

**Step 7 — Ethical sweep.** Three checks, verbatim from the source: (a) you believe in the offer's real value; (b) **raising the stakes without fear-mongering** — no manufactured or improbable risk presented as near-certain; dig for the deeper REAL risk instead; (c) every Barnum statement is high-probability-true of the actual target. Flattery-to-sale collapses trust the moment it's identified.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content type | Levers that fit | Levers to avoid | Location notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-form sales page | Anchoring two-stack, staged FAQ, affect landing | Stacking all 7 framing tactics | Anchor once at price reveal; FAQ block sits before the final CTA |
| Email sequence | Mere exposure (one big idea repeated), loss-framed bonus window | Anchoring (no room), quiz segmentation | One lever per email, not per paragraph |
| Paid ad / short-form | Affect heuristic, one framing tactic, loss-flavored rewrite | Staged FAQ, bonus stacks | Everything lands in the first two lines |
| Checkout / pricing page | Rule of Two, choice overload cap, order bump, strike-through | Nostalgia, Barnum | Binary CTA; ≤3 tiers; drop the unused coupon field |
| Content / editorial piece | Anaphora, pro-innovation framing, community frame | Scarcity tech, trivialized price | Soft-sell only; bias must be invisible or it reads as a pitch |
| Brand / campaign wrapper | Nostalgia 3-level map, mere exposure, us-vs-everyone-else | Loss aversion tactics at brand level | Campaign-level frequency, not in-piece repetition |

## Output Requirements

1. The Step-1 control sentence: why the clean draft closes, before any lever.
2. A **lever table**: lever name · named location in the piece · the exact inserted copy · the mechanism it exploits (one line).
3. The revised draft with insertions function-tagged in place.
4. **Delete-test result**, stated explicitly: the stripped draft still closes / does not close, with the reasoning.
5. Ethical sweep sign-off: offer-value belief, fear-mongering check with the real risk named, Barnum truth check.
6. Anything rejected during selection and why (lever budget discipline is part of the deliverable).

## Quality Gate

- **PASS/FAIL — Hard Veto 3 (no bias play without a standalone message):** delete every flourish; if the close dies, the piece fails. Non-negotiable, run literally, result reported.
- **PASS/FAIL — Lever budget:** 2-3 levers total, each pinned to exactly one named location. Four or more, or a lever with no location, fails.
- **PASS/FAIL — Hard Veto 2 (no fear-mongering):** every risk invoked is real and likely; the deeper real risk is named rather than an inflated surface one. "Keep the monsters real, even if you wish to get creative with how big their teeth are."
- **PASS/FAIL — Hard Veto 4 (no flattery-Barnum):** every "you're the kind of person who…" is high-probability-true of the actual target audience, and non-targets self-selecting out is acknowledged as acceptable.
- **PASS/FAIL — Rubric row "Objection handling":** if loss aversion was selected, the staged FAQ runs the full validate → answer → reframe script and the 2:1 benefit-to-risk evidence ratio is met.
- **PASS/FAIL — Rubric row "Tone" + Pattern 11:** forgiving tone survived the bias pass (no clause accuses the reader), and 100% of inserted clauses carry a function tag.
