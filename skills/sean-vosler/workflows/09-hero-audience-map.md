# 09 — Hero Audience Map (The Unlikely Hero, Phases 1–2)

## Pre-Flight Gate

1. **Is there a real product you'd defend?** Vosler's Phase-1 close: if you don't believe in the product/service, refine it or find another — a copywriter taking any paying client degrades output and burns out. Stop here if the answer is no.
2. **Depth calibration run?** You cannot start until the three variables are scored (Step 1). Skipping straight to the question bank produces $20K-coaching research for a $12 thermos, or vice versa.
3. **Dissonance preconditions checkable?** Genius Pattern 3: the map must be able to name *perceived choice* and *meaningful stakes*. If the audience is locked by a factual constraint (contract, physiology), walk away — "you're not going to host an intervention to make a sale."
4. **Mining source identified.** Hard Veto 1 (No blank-page copy): the six questions are answered from customer interviews, reviews, community posts, or client raw language — best-guess-with-empathy is the *fallback*, disclosed as such, never the default.

## Execution

**Step 1 — Depth calibration (do this first, in writing).** Score the offer on Vosler's three variables (iceberg model, p.232):
- *Familiarity* — how well does this audience already know this category?
- *Active search* — are they hunting for a solution right now, or unaware?
- *Bottom-line impact* — how much does this decision move their money/health/identity?

High familiarity + actively searching + low impact = **shallow map** (skip Step 4's narrative, one page total). Low familiarity + not searching + high future impact = **deep map** (full run, plus values work). His anchor: a $12 coffee thermos needs nothing like the world-building a $20,000 coaching program needs. Broad audience × high price = proportionally more nurturing. Record the score — every downstream workflow reads it to route reveal-depth.

**Step 2 — Frame the two worlds.** Reject the generic "day-to-day" reading of the Ordinary World. The real axis is **their accepted world vs. their dream world**: "if bliss is reaching a dream world, most people are lost in the opposite: a world of distraction and denial." Write one line for each, in their words, before the question bank.

**Step 3 — Run the 6-question bank** (Ordinary World Worksheet, `link.sean.co/hj-1`). Answer each with mined language, sourced:
1. What dream worlds could the hero be harboring? (better health / wealth / happiness; give more to community or family; more time with loved ones — *how much* more?)
2. Why are they putting off pursuit? (waiting for "the right time"? believe they lack resources? waiting on a trigger event — age, money, mindset? afraid of others' judgment? unaware the possibility exists for people "like them"?)
3. What emotional fears hold them back? (too shy / too outgoing? lacking patience, knowledge, experience? scarred by past overconfidence-then-failure? told their whole life they don't have what it takes?)
4. What deep hopes do they hold unconsciously — or that you'd wish for them? (what possibilities do you wish they knew were attainable? who could they become? how would they experience the world differently?)
5–6. What past positive experiences have they likely had related to your industry / product / service? *(printed twice in the source; treat as one question and note the duplication rather than inventing a sixth.)*

Twin goal: (a) the reader understands where they are vs. where they want to be; (b) they're woken to possibilities — including ones they didn't know existed.

**Step 4 — Character Sketch + Day-in-a-Life** (~30 min, `link.sean.co/hj-2`). Write 2–3 paragraphs of narrative, not bullets, answering: what was life like **BEFORE** the product/service · what is it like **AFTER** · what was the **IN-BETWEEN** — the buying experience itself, hypothetically. Prose forces empathy that a grid hides.

**Step 5 — Values pass** (deep maps only). Heroes pursue higher-level values they often haven't named (Accountability, Beauty, Boldness… ~50-term list, `link.sean.co/100-values`). Pick the 3 the audience actually holds and state explicitly how the offer aligns. This is the seed for The Value Battle enemy in §3.

**Step 6 — Extract the truths (Phase 2, "stoke the nagging voice of change").** Marketing joins a conversation already running in the reader's head. Sort truths into exactly two categories, **drawn from the Step 3–4 answers, not researched fresh**:
- **Category 1 — the dream is real and reachable for YOU**, even if they don't believe it yet.
- **Category 2 — what has actually been holding you back**, internal and external: the villain.
Rule: the more difficult a truth is to accept, the stronger the bond when the reader accepts it. Keep the monsters real (Hard Veto 2).

**Step 7 — Apply the change-psychology rules to every truth.** Sell **addition over change** — adding one food costs far less than changing a diet; when resistance is high, reposition the offer as an addition, not a replacement. **Hope > fear** as the primary driver ("both drive change, but hope is what we want to win"). **Don't ask for too much change early in the funnel**; if the reader refuses the call by rationalizing "that's not for me," re-examine the core argument — don't repeat the ask louder. Trust mechanic: give them a usable truth; win the heart with emotion, the mind with logic, the sale with both.

**Step 8 — Extend with the master per-phase template (p.239).** For any of the 12 phases beyond 1–2, work the same four items: (i) the audience's internal and external world at that phase; (ii) your role as guide there; (iii) which emotions to encourage and which to mitigate; (iv) which tactical assets to build and where they enter the funnel. Segments sit at different phases simultaneously — tag each segment with its phase. Guardrail (p.235): not every page needs the whole journey; weave hero attributes into the writing rather than staging a full myth each time.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Deliverable | Depth score to expect | What the map must carry | Phase emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold sales page / VSL | Deep (low familiarity, high impact) | Full 6-question bank + Day-in-a-Life + both truth categories + values | Phase 1 heavy, Phase 2 as the argument spine |
| Email nurture sequence | Medium | Category 1/2 truths, one per email; addition-framing per ask | Phase 2, escalating one small ask at a time |
| Social / content marketing | Shallow–medium | Accepted-vs-dream world lines + question 3 fears, verbatim | Phase 1 recognition beats |
| Ecommerce / low-ticket product page | Shallow | Dream world + past positive experiences only | Phase 1 compressed to one paragraph |
| High-ticket coaching or consulting | Deepest | Everything, plus values pass and segment-by-phase tagging | All 12 phases via the master template |
| Client discovery / positioning brief | Medium | Depth score, both truth categories, guide-role definition | Phase 1 + guide role from p.239 |

## Output Requirements

Deliver one **Audience Hero Map** file containing, in order:
1. **Depth score** — the three variables rated, with the resulting map depth stated.
2. **Accepted World / Dream World** — one line each, in mined language.
3. **The six answers** — each with its source tagged (interview, review, community post, client raw text, or `EMPATHY-GUESS` where no source exists).
4. **Character Sketch + Day-in-a-Life** — 2–3 paragraphs, before / after / in-between.
5. **Values shortlist** (deep maps) — 3 values plus the alignment sentence.
6. **Truth Bank** — Category 1 and Category 2 truths, each traced back to the question that produced it.
7. **Change-psychology notes** — the addition-framing for the offer, the hope frame, and the size of the first ask.
8. **Per-phase extension table** (optional) — the 4-item template for any additional phases, with segments tagged.
9. **Open questions** — what only a real customer interview can answer.

## Quality Gate

Pass/fail, from the genius.md rubric and Hard Vetoes:

- **Language provenance (rubric: Language provenance = 10).** Every truth and every dream-world line traces to a mined artifact. Unsourced lines are labeled `EMPATHY-GUESS`. Violation = **Hard Veto 1, No blank-page copy** — fail.
- **Stakes (rubric: Stakes).** Category 2 names a real villain with real consequences, not a manufactured or inflated risk. Violation = **Hard Veto 2, No fear-mongering** — fail.
- **Barnum check.** Any "you're the kind of person who…" line in the map must be high-probability-true for the actual target, letting non-targets self-select out. Violation = **Hard Veto 4, No flattery-Barnum** — fail.
- **Layer-3 discipline.** Deep-benefit outcomes (freedom, time, family) appear as material for imagination triggers, never as asserted claims in downstream copy. Violation = **Hard Veto 5, No stated layer-3 benefit** — fail.
- **Preconditions named.** Perceived choice and meaningful stakes are each stated in one sentence in the brief. Missing = fail (Pattern 3 is a hard gate).
- **Claim hygiene.** Any statistic or third-party fact carried into the map is labeled VERIFIED / LIKELY / UNCONFIRMED. Violation = **Hard Veto 6, No unverified claims shipped as fact** — fail.
- **Ask-size sanity.** The first ask requested of the reader is an addition, not a wholesale change, or the map explains why the exception holds. Fail if unexamined.
