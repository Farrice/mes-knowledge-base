# Eugene Teo — Minimalist Strength Training

Eugene Teo (strength coach and educator, 20-year competitive-bodybuilding background) rebuilt his method around a single move: stop chasing the 1% marginal gains, master the 99% foundations. His minimalism is not "light weights, little equipment" — it is efficiency: **remove every redundancy and wasted effort, then get the most out of what's left.** One exercise per movement pattern instead of four. One effortful set instead of three token ones. Rest periods spent on mobility instead of your phone. Timers instead of open-ended sessions. The result develops five qualities at once (hypertrophy, strength, power, mobility, endurance) in 30-40 minutes, 2-5 days a week — "suboptimal on any single axis, but still highly effective, and you cover all your bases."

## Quick Reference
- **Genius Context**: [genius.md](genius.md) — load before any workflow
- **Five qualities**: hypertrophy · strength · power · mobility · endurance — every program ticks all five
- **Movement patterns** (tick ≥1 each, no gaps): squat · hinge · lunge/single-leg · horizontal push · horizontal pull · vertical push · vertical pull (+ tweaks: shoulder raise, elbow bend)
- **Session skeleton**: Warm-up (tissue prep for today's lifts) → Main Work (heavy, E2MOM) → Swollen Flexi (mobility + hypertrophy) → Accessories (weak-link finishers)
- **Effort law**: as sets drop, intensity must rise — most people leave ~10 reps in reserve while believing they're at failure
- **Health floor**: mortality-risk benefits of resistance training tap out near 60 min/week — the minimum effective dose is far lower than the culture assumes
