# Oren — Brand Social Media Archetypes: Genius Context

> Load this file before executing any workflow in this skill domain. These patterns are the operating system — the workflows are the applications.

---

## Pattern 1: The Resource-Reality Gate

**The archetype is selected FROM the resource audit, not the other way around.**

Before any content strategy discussion, run a 4-question inventory:
1. **Camera talent**: Who can be on camera? (Expert/founder vs. hired creator vs. nobody)
2. **Design capability**: Who can produce? (Internal designer, agency, budget for freelancer?)
3. **Showcasable assets**: What can you actually showcase? (Physical product, process, space, knowledge?)
4. **Acquirable resources**: What can you GET that you don't have?

**Anti-pattern**: Selecting an archetype because it's "cool" then discovering you don't have the people, budget, or access to execute it. The Oracle requires a genuine expert on camera. The Performer requires creative talent willing to take risks. The World Builder requires budget and risk tolerance. No resources = no archetype.

---

## Pattern 2: The Single-Archetype Discipline

**One archetype per brand. The constraint IS the strategy.**

"Stop trying to be everything to everybody and lock in on one archetype." This is the opposite of content pillar thinking, which distributes attention across 4-5 categories. The archetype is a COMMITMENT — every piece of content should be unmistakably from the same archetype.

**Diagnostic signal**: If a brand's feed shows educational content one day, entertainment skits the next, motivational quotes the third — that's strategic incoherence. Prescribe archetype selection.

## Pattern 3: The 5 Archetypes — Decision Architecture

### The Oracle
- **Best for**: Brands with deep expertise to share
- **Requires**: A core expert or team of experts who can storytell
- **Qualifying question**: "What does our customer need to understand about the world we inhabit that nobody else is explaining properly?"
- **Content types**: Historical explainers, category comparisons ("Is X or Y better for Z?"), "why things are the way they are," predictions for the future
- **Funnel mechanic**: Education → trust → product credibility → purchase
- **Exemplars**: Ken Sakata/Front Office (workwear), Blam Motorworks (Porsche restoration), Kreiss (interiors/founder account), Rarify (collectible furniture)

### The Performer
- **Best for**: Brands where entertainment drives affinity
- **Requires**: Creative talent, visual product, willingness to take risks
- **Qualifying question**: "If our brand had a show, what would it be? Would people watch it without knowing it was ours?"
- **Content types**: Skits, character series, compilations, entertainment-first narratives with OMNIPRESENT product (product is visible, never pitched)
- **Funnel mechanic**: Entertainment → brand affinity → omnipresent recognition → purchase
- **Exemplars**: Duer Shoots (fashion skits — clothes always on), Mohawk Chevrolet (The Office spinoff at dealership), LC Sign (skit-to-promotion pipeline)
- **Key principle**: The product is scenery, not subject. Omnipresence, not promotion.

### The World Builder
- **Best for**: Teams/founders willing to take creative moonshots
- **Requires**: Budget, creative director or creative team, risk tolerance
- **Qualifying question**: "Can we make something creative that people will love so much they love the brand right next to it?"
- **Content types**: Short films, fictional character series, immersive intellectual content, cultural curation
- **Funnel mechanic**: Cultural relevance → "they get us" affinity → organic brand love → purchase
- **Exemplars**: Death to Stock (visual culture commentary for stock photo platform), Cluey (narrative-driven office series), Fern (fragrance short films)
- **Risk hierarchy**: HIGHEST risk archetype. "Is this an easy strategy? No. Is it one that makes them by far the best content of anyone they're competing with? Yes."

### The Catalyst
- **Best for**: Brands helping people become better versions of themselves
- **Requires**: Educational capacity (doesn't require a deep expert — can use multiple staff)
- **Qualifying question**: "How can our content be the bridge between who our customer is and who they want to be?"
- **Content types**: Training/process documentation, inspirational content, challenges, community highlights and curation
- **Funnel mechanic**: Aspiration → community belonging → brand as enabler → purchase
- **Exemplars**: Crease Group (manufacturing catalyst), Artifaxing (community curation at scale), Base Living (lifestyle curation account)
- **Distinguisher**: Content is ASPIRATIONAL — shared because it makes people FEEL like they're becoming better.

### The Helper
- **Best for**: Brands offering practical value without deep expertise requirements
- **Requires**: Relatable creator (doesn't need to be an expert — more "on the journey with you")
- **Qualifying question**: "What are little helpful things we can do that our customer will appreciate?"
- **Content types**: Visual tutorials, carousel tips, story vlogs, interviews, curated comparisons
- **Funnel mechanic**: Practical value → ambient recognition → paid ad performance lift → purchase
- **Exemplars**: Kiyoko Beauty (Korean skincare comparisons with multiple creators), HashiHome/Certigrad (relatable host content), Marpipe (informational marketing content for catalog ad software)
- **Distinguisher**: Content is PRACTICAL — shared because it solves a specific problem. Key difference from Catalyst: Helper is realistic, Catalyst is aspirational.

## Pattern 4: The Two-Account Method (Oracle Variant)

When the Oracle brand has a charismatic founder:
- **Brand account** → curated, polished, product-focused
- **Founder account** → top-of-funnel expert content, trends, opinions, the real driver
- The founder account drives awareness; the brand account captures intent
- "In this two-account method, you're able to post your typical content on main... but then his social media is the real driver."

**Exemplar**: Kreiss — brand showroom content on main, founder doing trend analysis and landscaping hacks on personal.

---

## Pattern 5: The "Nothing Is Too Boring" Diagnostic

When a client says "our industry is boring," treat this as a DIAGNOSTIC SIGNAL, not a legitimate objection. Deploy the Interest Excavation Protocol:
1. What does an insider find fascinating that outsiders don't know?
2. What arguments happen between experts in this space?
3. What's the history behind something everyone takes for granted?
4. What process would blow someone's mind if they saw it?

"I don't think anything is too boring. There's a story behind every screw, every bolt, every welding piece."

---

## Pattern 6: The Omnipresence Principle

For Performer and World Builder archetypes, the product is EMBEDDED, not promoted. The product appears in every frame without being discussed. Duer Shoots never talks about the clothes. Mohawk Chevrolet skits happen at the dealership. The product is architecture, not advertisement.

**The test**: A viewer watches 10 posts and knows what the brand sells without ever being explicitly told.

---

## Pattern 7: The Expert-Adjacency Funnel (Oracle Architecture)

3-layer content funnel for Oracle brands:
1. **Top**: Wide-interest educational (history, trends, "why things are the way they are")
2. **Mid**: Process and development (what you're working on, how you think)
3. **Bottom**: Product releases, launches, carousels

This is how Ken Sakata turns salvage denim history → development process → Front Office product releases. The audience is EDUCATED into wanting the product.

---

## Pattern 8: The Niche Audience Math

Small audience ≠ failure. "Maybe there's not 100,000 people a video to watch it, but say there's 2,000. If they're nerding out about welded parts, you're probably adjacent to an industry that's going to be procuring them."

For B2B and niche brands: calculate audience VALUE, not audience SIZE. Use this math to justify Oracle and Helper strategies to leadership that only understands vanity metrics.

---

## Pattern 9: The Exercise-as-Deliverable Model

Content strategy is presented as a FACILITATED EXERCISE, not a delivered document:
1. Present archetype options
2. Run the resource audit together with the client
3. Cross-reference resources against archetypes (eliminate the impossible)
4. Generate content ideas from selected archetype (the client generates ideas, not you)
5. End with a content roadmap the client helped build → ownership → execution

This format "makes you look more professional" and "helps establish buy-in." It also solves the younger-employee-convincing-leadership problem because archetype language maps to concepts "CMOs and executives are familiar with."

---

## Pattern 10: The Organic-to-Paid Bridge (Helper Mechanic)

For technical or complex products (SaaS, manufacturing): informational helper content makes paid ads perform better through ambient brand recognition. The ROI of "unglamorous" helper content isn't in organic metrics — it's in paid ad CPA reduction.

"Just being that entry point, helping reduce the money or the barrier to entry it requires when you're advertising is a massive value add."

---

## Pattern 11: The Risk Hierarchy

Archetypes have an implicit risk gradient:
1. **Oracle** — safest (just share what you know)
2. **Helper** — low risk (be practically useful)
3. **Catalyst** — medium (requires aspirational framing)
4. **Performer** — higher (requires entertainment skill)
5. **World Builder** — highest ("this is best for teams and founders willing to take a risk")

Match risk tolerance to archetype. Don't prescribe World Builder to a conservative brand.

---

## Pattern 12: The Content Roadmap Exit Condition

Every archetype session MUST end with:
1. ✅ Selected archetype (one)
2. ✅ Resource inventory (what you have, don't have, can get)
3. ✅ 15-20 content ideas from the archetype's content types
4. ✅ Buy-in confirmation (people are "excited about" the ideas)

If the session ends without all four, it was strategy theater.
