# /lynch-perception-campaign — Perception-Engineered Campaign (Lynch × Rory Sutherland)

> Stack Ron Lynch's identity-first campaigns with Rory Sutherland's perception engineering. Change what the product MEANS, not what it DOES. Reframe perceived value without changing the product.

## When to Use
- When the product is good but perceived as a commodity
- When competitors sell similar products and price is the only differentiator
- When the category has "meaning decay" (everyone says the same thing)
- When you need to charge premium prices for functionally similar products

## Inputs Required
1. Product/service description (what it does)
2. Current market perception (how it's seen now)
3. Desired perception (how you want it seen)
4. Competitive perceptions (how alternatives are positioned)
5. Identity word (if available from `/lynch-identity-campaign`)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Perception Audit (Sutherland)
Map current vs. desired perception:
- How does the market CURRENTLY describe this product?
- What Sutherland "psycho-logical" frame applies?
- What MEANING has been drained from the category?
- What's the "Red Bull test"? (functionally identical products, 10x price difference through meaning)

### Step 2: Reframe Architecture (Sutherland)
Design the perception shift:
- **What to add**: What new meaning elevates the product?
- **What to remove**: What category baggage must be shed?
- **What to reframe**: What existing attribute can be repositioned?
- **The Sutherland inversion**: What happens if we do the OPPOSITE of category convention?

### Step 3: Identity Layer (Lynch)
Build the identity cascade around the NEW perception:
- The identity word must reflect the reframed perception, not the old one
- If the perception shift changes the identity word → update it
- The customer's SELF-IMAGE must align with the new perception

### Step 4: Campaign Execution
Build campaign elements that INSTALL the new perception:
- Creative that demonstrates the new meaning (not the old category)
- Social proof that reinforces the new perception
- Pricing that signals the new value (Sutherland: price IS the product)
- Distribution that matches the new perception (where do premium products appear?)

### Step 5: Perception Moat
Design defenses against perception erosion:
- How do competitors respond to this reframe?
- What makes this perception DIFFICULT to copy?
- How does the identity-first architecture protect the perception?
- What ongoing signals reinforce the perception over time?

## Quality Gate
- Perception audit must show a SPECIFIC gap between current and desired perception
- Reframe must NOT change the product — only the meaning
- Identity word must be updated if the perception shift demands it
- Perception moat must address competitive response
- Both expert contributions explicitly documented
- Score ≥7 on both Identity Clarity AND Perception Engineering
