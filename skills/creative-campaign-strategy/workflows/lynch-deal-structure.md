# /lynch-deal-structure — Creative Strategy Deal Architecture

> Structure the cost-plus-royalty deal that transforms a creative strategist from a flat-fee freelancer into an equity-holding partner. Ron Lynch's financial architecture — "I'll do it for this and a little royalty" — where the client thinks it's cheap and you build wealth.

## When to Use
- After a creative brief passes the audit (`/lynch-brief-audit` → PRODUCTION-READY)
- When pricing any creative strategy engagement
- When transitioning from project-based to royalty-based income
- When a client asks "what do you charge?"
- When structuring an incubation deal (you found the product, not the client)

## Inputs Required
1. Completed creative brief with business economics section
2. Product unit economics (COGS, margin, projected volume)
3. Client's budget and growth stage
4. Your current career level (from `/lynch-strategist-career`)
5. Competitive landscape for creative strategy services in this category

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Engagement Type Classification

Determine which model applies:

| Type | Who Found Whom | Risk Distribution | Best Deal Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Client-Initiated** | Client brings product to you | Low risk — product exists, some traction | Fee + moderate royalty (1-3%) |
| **Strategist-Initiated** | You pitch strategy to a brand | Medium risk — your idea, their product | Lower fee + higher royalty (3-5%) |
| **Incubation** | You scout the product AND bring strategy | High risk — you're the catalyst | Minimal fee + equity or high royalty (5%+) |

### Step 2: Fee Architecture

Build the fee structure in layers:

**Layer 1: Strategy Fee (The Creative Brief)**
- The 14-20 page brief IS the deliverable
- Pricing range by career level:
  - Level 2-3 (Creative/Director): $5,000-$15,000
  - Level 4 (Strategist): $15,000-$35,000
  - Level 5 (CMO/Founder): $25,000-$50,000+
- Frame as: "This brief IS your marketing MBA for this product. Other agencies charge $100K+ for this level of strategic thinking and give you a 2-page deck."

**Layer 2: Production Costs (Pass-Through)**
- All production expenses billed at cost + 15-20% management fee
- Video production (crew, talent, equipment, editing)
- Test weekend media buy
- Call center / fulfillment setup
- Total test weekend: $30-40K (Lynch standard)

**Layer 3: Royalty Income (The Wealth Layer)**
- Negotiate as % of attributed net sales (after returns)
- Rate ranges:

| Engagement Type | Royalty Range | Typical Sweet Spot |
|---|---|---|
| Client-initiated | 1-3% | 2% of net sales |
| Strategist-initiated | 3-5% | 4% of net sales |
| Incubation | 5-10% | 5% + equity option |

- The Lynch frame: "I'll do it for [base fee] and a little royalty." 
- Why clients accept: The base fee looks CHEAP compared to agency retainers. The royalty only kicks in if the campaign WORKS — aligned incentives.

### Step 3: Contract Architecture

Key terms to negotiate:

**Attribution & Reporting**
- Define "attributed sales" precisely (direct response = trackable, brand = harder)
- Monthly reporting cadence with access to sales dashboards
- Audit rights: Right to review sales data quarterly
- Attribution window: How long after campaign launch do royalties apply?

**Term & Territory**
- Contract term: 12-24 months initial, auto-renewal
- Territory: Which markets/channels are covered?
- Exclusivity: Are you exclusive to this category, or can you work with competitors?
- Exit clause: What happens if the product is discontinued or sold?

**Creative Control**
- Ron's rule: The strategist must control the creative. "Approval committees" kill campaigns.
- Define approval workflow: Strategist presents → client has one round of feedback → strategist makes final creative call
- Revisions: 2 rounds included, additional rounds billed at hourly

**IP Ownership**
- Strategy and brief: Shared IP (client can use, strategist retains methodology)
- Creative assets: Client owns produced assets
- Campaign architecture: Strategist retains the strategic framework for portfolio

### Step 4: Proposal Framing

Structure the proposal to make the deal feel inevitable:

**The Comparison Frame**
"Traditional agencies charge $150-300K for strategy + production with no performance alignment. I'm offering the same caliber strategy for [base fee] with production at cost. The royalty means I only make real money if YOU make real money. Our incentives are perfectly aligned."

**The Risk Inversion Frame**
"You're not risking $300K on an agency retainer. You're investing $[base + test weekend] to get market data. If the test weekend works, we scale together with aligned incentives. If it doesn't, you've spent the cost of a bad dinner party, not a bad marriage."

**The Portfolio Frame** (for Levels 4-5)
"I currently manage creative strategy for [X] brands generating $[total attributed revenue]. My campaigns have a [win rate]% success rate on test weekends. Here's what those campaigns look like: [portfolio pieces]."

### Step 5: Negotiation Decision Tree

| Client Says | You Respond | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| "The base fee is too high" | Lower base, increase royalty rate | You make MORE if it works |
| "We don't do royalties" | Increase base fee 3-5x | They're paying you fairly either way |
| "Can we do equity instead?" | Only for incubation deals where YOU found the product | Equity > royalty for high-growth |
| "We want to approve all creative" | "My track record depends on creative control. I'll present options, but final creative call is mine." | Walk if they insist on committee approval |
| "We want a lower royalty rate" | Accept, but add performance bonuses at volume thresholds | Upside at scale compensates |

## Quality Gate
- Engagement type must be correctly classified with rationale
- All three fee layers must be defined with specific dollar amounts or ranges
- Royalty rate must be within defined ranges for the engagement type
- Contract terms must address: attribution, term, territory, creative control, audit rights
- At least ONE proposal framing must be written out in full
- Projected economics must show both success and failure scenarios
- The "walk-away" conditions must be identified (what terms are non-negotiable)
- Must include at least 3 negotiation responses from the decision tree
