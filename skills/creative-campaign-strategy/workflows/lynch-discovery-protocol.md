# /lynch-discovery-protocol — George Foreman Discovery Protocol

> Launch with your best hypothesis audience, monitor where ACTUAL sales come from, pivot creative to match the real buyer. The market tells you who the customer is — your job is to listen.

## When to Use
- Launching a new product where the buyer is hypothesized but unproven
- When initial campaign results don't match expectations
- When sales are coming from unexpected segments
- After `/lynch-identity-campaign` when you need market validation
- Pivoting a campaign mid-flight based on actual buyer data
- When the "obvious" target customer isn't converting

## Inputs Required
1. Product/service description
2. Hypothesized target customer (who you THINK will buy)
3. Current campaign creative and channels
4. Sales data (if available) — who has ACTUALLY purchased
5. Analytics access (traffic sources, demographics, behavior data)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Hypothesis Documentation
Before any data analysis, lock the hypothesis:
- **Hypothesized buyer**: [Demographics, psychographics, identity]
- **Why we think they'll buy**: [Logic, research, assumptions]
- **Campaign creative built for**: [What audience the current creative addresses]
- **Expected conversion profile**: [What the "ideal" sale looks like]

Write this down BEFORE looking at data. Ron Lynch's lesson: George Foreman Grill was designed for families. Actual buyers were single people and elderly who didn't want to turn on a stove. The hypothesis was wrong — and the DATA told the truth.

### Step 2: Data Collection Framework
Set up tracking to identify the REAL buyer:

| Data Point | Source | What It Tells You |
|---|---|---|
| Who buys | Sales/CRM data | Actual buyer demographics |
| Who engages | Social analytics | Which content resonates with whom |
| Who converts | Funnel analytics | Where in the journey they commit |
| Who shares | UGC monitoring | Who becomes customer-as-media |
| Who returns | Retention data | Who the product ACTUALLY serves |
| Who searches | SEO/query data | What language real buyers use |

### Step 3: Reality-Hypothesis Comparison
Compare the hypothesis to the data:

| Dimension | Hypothesis | Reality | Match? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age range | [expected] | [actual] | ✅/❌ |
| Gender split | [expected] | [actual] | ✅/❌ |
| Use case | [expected] | [actual] | ✅/❌ |
| Purchase motivation | [expected] | [actual] | ✅/❌ |
| Discovery channel | [expected] | [actual] | ✅/❌ |
| Price sensitivity | [expected] | [actual] | ✅/❌ |
| Identity signal | [expected] | [actual] | ✅/❌ |

Score the match: 7/7 = Hypothesis confirmed → scale. ≤4/7 = Hypothesis wrong → pivot.

### Step 4: Pivot Decision Framework
Based on the comparison, choose a path:

**Path A: Hypothesis Confirmed (5-7 match)**
- Double down on current creative
- Scale the channels that are working
- Refine the identity word (it's correct, sharpen it)

**Path B: Partial Match (3-4 match)**
- Keep the identity word but adjust the EXPRESSION
- The who is right but the how is wrong (or vice versa)
- Split test: original creative vs. reality-matched creative

**Path C: Hypothesis Wrong (0-2 match)**
- Full creative pivot to match the REAL buyer
- Redesign the identity word around the actual buyer's aspiration
- Don't fight the market — the George Foreman lesson is to FOLLOW the data
- The product didn't change — the AUDIENCE did

### Step 5: Pivot Execution (If Path B or C)
If the real buyer is different from the hypothesized buyer:

1. **New Identity Audit**: Run `/lynch-identity-campaign` for the REAL buyer
2. **New Voice Calibration**: What is the REAL buyer's internal monologue?
3. **New Media Match**: Where does the REAL buyer consume content?
4. **New Counter-Identity**: What does the REAL buyer aspire to?
5. **Retain what works**: Keep any creative elements that the REAL buyer responds to

### Step 6: Feedback Loop Design
Build ongoing discovery into the campaign:
- **Weekly check**: Are buyers still matching the pivot target?
- **Monthly audit**: Has a NEW unexpected segment emerged?
- **Quarterly review**: Does the identity word still fit?
- **Never assume the current buyer is the FINAL buyer** — markets shift

## Quality Gate
- Hypothesis must be documented BEFORE data analysis (no retroactive justification)
- Data must include at least 4 of the 6 data points (buyers, engagement, conversion, sharing, retention, search)
- Reality-hypothesis comparison must be specific (not "close enough")
- Pivot recommendation must cite specific data, not intuition
- If Path C, the new identity word must be different from the original
- Feedback loop must have specific check intervals and metrics
- Score ≥7 on Campaign Ecosystem health in the Ron Lynch Quality Rubric
