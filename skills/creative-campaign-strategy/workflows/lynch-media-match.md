# /lynch-media-match — Media-Matched Creative Strategy

> Design segment-specific creative where the AUDIENCE sees themselves in the content. Not one ad for everyone — custom stories for each identity segment.

## When to Use
- Scaling a campaign across multiple channels or audience segments
- When a single creative isn't resonating across all audiences
- Entering new markets or demographics with an existing product
- After `/lynch-identity-campaign` when you need execution-level creative direction

## Inputs Required
1. Identity word (from `/lynch-identity-campaign`)
2. Full channel/media plan (where the ads will run)
3. Audience data (demographics, psychographics, behavioral segments)
4. Product capabilities that map to different use cases
5. Budget allocation across channels

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Segment Identification
Identify 5-10 distinct identity segments within the target audience:
- What different STORIES do different users tell with this product?
- Each segment = a different version of the identity transformation
- Key question: If 10 different people use this product, what are 10 different stories?

### Step 2: Media-Activity Matching (GP-4)
For each segment, map the specific activity/story to the channel:

| Segment | Story/Activity | Best Channel | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Example: Surfers | Wave riding footage | YouTube/Instagram Reels | Visual spectacle |
| Example: Parents | First steps capture | Facebook/TikTok | Emotional sharing |

### Step 3: Creative Brief Per Segment
For each segment, write a mini creative brief:
- **Identity angle**: How does this segment express the one-word identity?
- **Visual signature**: What imagery is unique to this segment?
- **Emotional trigger**: What feeling drives this segment's engagement?
- **Proof type**: What social proof resonates with this segment?
- **CTA framing**: What action feels natural for this segment?

### Step 4: Cross-Segment Coherence
Verify all segments reinforce the master identity:
- Remove any segment whose creative contradicts the identity word
- Ensure visual language has a consistent thread across all segments
- Confirm the product positioning is the SAME — only the story changes

### Step 5: Production Prioritization
Rank segments by impact-to-production-cost ratio:
- Which 3 segments reach the most people at the lowest cost?
- Which segment's creative can be customer-generated (reducing production cost)?
- What's the minimum viable number of segments to launch with?

## Output Format
```
## MEDIA-MATCHED CREATIVE STRATEGY — [Product/Brand]

### Master Identity: [One Word]

### Segment Map
[5-10 segments with story, channel, and rationale]

### Creative Briefs
[Mini brief for each segment: identity angle, visual, emotion, proof, CTA]

### Cross-Segment Coherence Check
[How all segments reinforce the master identity]

### Production Priority Ranking
[Top 3 segments to produce first + cost estimates]

### Testing Framework
[How to measure which segments outperform and reallocate]
```

## Quality Gate
- Minimum 5 distinct segments identified
- Each segment must have a unique story AND a specific channel match
- Cross-segment coherence must be verified (all reinforce the identity word)
- Production priorities must be ranked by impact-to-cost ratio
- Score ≥7 on Media Matching in the Ron Lynch Quality Rubric
