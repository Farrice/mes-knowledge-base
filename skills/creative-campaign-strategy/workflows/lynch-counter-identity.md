# /lynch-counter-identity — Counter-Identity Advertising

> Audit a product's REAL user behavior, then design the aspirational identity that advertising should sell. The gap between reality and aspiration = the sale. The Coca-Cola principle.

## When to Use
- When current advertising reflects the ACTUAL customer (unglamorous) instead of the ASPIRATIONAL customer
- When brand perception has calcified around the median user
- When competitors own the aspirational space and you're stuck with "accuracy"
- Before any identity campaign to pressure-test whether the identity is aspirational enough
- When "authentic" marketing has made the brand boring

## Inputs Required
1. Product/service description
2. Current customer data: who ACTUALLY buys this? What do they look like?
3. Current advertising creative (for audit)
4. Competitor positioning (who owns the aspirational space?)
5. Identity word (if exists from `/lynch-identity-campaign`)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Reality Audit — The Uncomfortable Truth
Map the ACTUAL user profile with radical honesty:
- **Median user behavior**: What does the typical customer actually DO with this product?
- **Usage context**: WHERE and WHEN do they really use it? (Not the fantasy scenario)
- **Social display**: Do they SHOW this product publicly? Or hide it?
- **Identity signal**: When someone sees this customer, what do they ACTUALLY think?

Score the reality-aspiration gap:
- **10/10 gap**: Product used lazily but could enable heroism (Coca-Cola: couch → Olympic torch)
- **7/10 gap**: Product used practically but could enable artistry (cooking appliance → chef identity)
- **4/10 gap**: Product used aspirationally already but by wrong segment (GoPro: existing in surf shops)
- **1/10 gap**: Product already sold on accurate identity (rare, usually luxury brands)

### Step 2: Aspirational Mining — The Top 5%
Find the MOST aspirational users:
- Who are the top 5% of users by behavior (not spend)?
- What do THEY do with this product that nobody else does?
- What would the BEST possible use case look like?
- What story would make someone say "I want to be THAT person"?

The Ron Lynch principle: You don't sell what 95% of users do. You sell what 5% of users do. The 95% buy because they ASPIRE to the 5%.

### Step 3: Counter-Identity Design
Build the aspirational identity that advertising will sell:

| Dimension | Reality (95%) | Aspiration (5%) | Advertising Shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Activity | [what they really do] | [what the best do] | [aspiration only] |
| Setting | [where they really use it] | [where the best use it] | [aspiration only] |
| Social context | [who they're really with] | [who the best are with] | [aspiration only] |
| Emotion | [what they really feel] | [what the best feel] | [aspiration only] |
| Identity label | [who they really are] | [who the best are] | [aspiration only] |

### Step 4: Voice Engineering (GP-6 + Counter-Identity)
Write the customer's INTERNAL MONOLOGUE at two levels:

**The Reality Voice** (what they actually think):
> "I use this thing on weekends when I have time. It's fine. It works."

**The Aspirational Voice** (what they WANT to think):
> "This is who I am now. I'm the person who does THIS."

The advertising copy should sound like the ASPIRATIONAL voice — not the brand's voice, not the reality voice. The customer should hear their best self speaking.

### Step 5: Competitive Aspiration Mapping
Map where competitors sit on the reality-aspiration spectrum:

```
REALITY ←————————————————→ ASPIRATION
[Competitor A]        [You are HERE]        [Competitor B]
(accurate but boring)  (stuck in middle)  (aspirational but fake)
```

Position the counter-identity to be MORE aspirational than Competitor A but MORE credible than Competitor B. The sweet spot: aspirational enough to inspire, grounded enough that the customer believes they could become it.

### Step 6: Campaign Application
Translate the counter-identity into campaign elements:
- **Imagery**: Only show the top 5% use case
- **Testimonials**: Feature aspirational users, not average ones
- **Copy**: Write in the aspirational voice (Step 4)
- **CTA**: Frame the purchase as JOINING the 5%, not buying a product
- **Social proof**: Display the aspirational community, not the total customer base

## Quality Gate
- Reality audit must be HONEST — not flattering to the brand
- Top 5% users must be identified with specific behaviors, not generic "power users"
- Counter-identity table must show clear separation between reality and aspiration
- Voice engineering must produce actual monologue samples (not descriptions)
- Competitive mapping must position against at least 2 competitors
- The counter-identity must be ASPIRATIONAL but BELIEVABLE — not fantasy
- Score ≥7 on Identity Clarity in the Ron Lynch Quality Rubric
