# /lynch-creative-brief — The 14-20 Page Creative Brief

> Ron Lynch doesn't write 1-2 page agency briefs. He writes 14-20 page documents that function as business plans — if you can't fill 14 pages, you don't understand the product well enough to sell it. This workflow produces the production-grade creative brief that IS the campaign strategy.

## When to Use
- After product pre-qualification passes (`/lynch-product-prequalify` → GREEN LIGHT)
- After customer voice mining is complete (`/lynch-customer-voice-mine` → Voice Card ready)
- When starting any serious campaign engagement
- When a client says "we need a creative strategy" — THIS is the deliverable
- When transitioning from "copywriter for hire" to "creative strategist"

## Inputs Required (ALL mandatory)
1. Product Pre-Qualification results (from `/lynch-product-prequalify`)
2. Customer Voice Card (from `/lynch-customer-voice-mine`)
3. Product deep dive: features, specs, manufacturing, supply chain basics
4. Pricing strategy: retail, wholesale, COGS, margin targets
5. Competitive landscape: top 3-5 competitors with positioning analysis
6. Available assets: existing creative, testimonials, product photography/video
7. Budget parameters: test weekend budget, production budget, media budget
8. Client business context: revenue goals, growth stage, team structure

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Product Intelligence (Pages 1-3)

**Product Deep Dive**
- What is it? (In the customer's language, not the inventor's language)
- What does it DO that nothing else does? (The USP in one sentence)
- The "wow" demonstration: What's the 60-second demo that makes people say "I need that"?
- Manufacturing/quality story: What's the craftsmanship detail that builds trust?
- Product evolution: How did this get better than previous versions?

**Competitive Landscape**
- Top 3-5 direct competitors with positioning, pricing, and weakness analysis
- Indirect competitors (what else solves this problem, even imperfectly?)
- Category perception: What does the average person THINK about this category?
- White space: What is NO ONE in this category saying or doing?

### Step 2: Customer Intelligence (Pages 4-6)

Import and expand the Customer Voice Card:

**Primary Customer Profile**
- Character wallet (from Voice Card)
- Internal monologue (from Voice Card)
- Voice DNA — pain, desire, skepticism vocabularies

**Customer Journey Map**
- Trigger: What event makes them START looking for a solution?
- Research: Where do they go? What do they search? Who do they ask?
- Evaluation: How do they compare options? What criteria matter?
- Objections: What stops them from buying? (Price? Trust? Timing?)
- Decision: What tips them over the edge?
- Post-purchase: What do they DO after buying? (Customer-as-Media opportunity)

**Identity Architecture (GP-1)**
- The one-word identity: [from `/lynch-identity-campaign` or defined here]
- The 5-6 product identity stack: Where does this product fit?
- Counter-identity analysis: Real behavior vs. aspirational identity (GP-11)

### Step 3: Campaign Strategy (Pages 7-10)

**The Transformation Argument**
- Opening hook: How do you grab attention in the first 5 seconds?
- The problem articulation: (In the customer's voice — NOT the brand's voice)
- The "wouldn't it be cool if" reframe (GP-7)
- The transformation reveal: What does the customer BECOME?
- The product discovery: "You need THIS to get THAT" (GP-5)
- The proof cascade: Demo, testimonials, data, social proof
- The offer architecture: Price, bonuses, guarantee, urgency

**Sales Argument Perspective Outlines**
Write 3-5 different angles of attack:

| Angle | Hook | Transformation | Proof Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity angle | "Be the person who..." | Identity shift | Social proof / UGC |
| Problem-solution angle | "Tired of...?" | Relief / elimination | Demo + testimonials |
| Aspiration angle | "Imagine if..." | Lifestyle upgrade | Before/after + data |
| Fear angle | "What happens if you don't?" | Loss prevention | Expert authority + stats |
| Social proof angle | "Join 10,000+ people who..." | Belonging | Numbers + testimonials |

Select the primary angle and 2 supporting angles for the campaign.

**Offer Architecture**
- Price point analysis: Where does this sit vs. competition? Why?
- Bonus structure: What bonuses increase perceived value without increasing COGS?
- Guarantee: What risk reversal makes "not buying" feel riskier than buying?
- Urgency mechanism: What creates authentic (not fake) time pressure?
- Upsell/cross-sell: What's the natural next purchase?

### Step 4: Creative Direction (Pages 11-14)

**The Two-Column Script Framework (GP-10)**
- Long-form script concept: 20-30 minute format outline (copy + visuals)
- Key demonstration sequences: 3-5 demo moments that sell
- Testimonial integration: Where testimonials go and what they prove
- CTA placements: 2-3 CTAs within the long-form (the infomercial model)
- Modular extraction: How this long-form becomes 5-12 standalone assets (GP-10 → `/lynch-modular-script`)

**Visual Language**
- Color palette that reinforces the identity
- Imagery direction: What the viewer SEES
- Talent/pitchman direction: Who delivers the message? How?
- Setting/environment: Where does this take place?
- Production quality level: Polished studio vs. authentic handheld?

**Media-to-Market Matching (GP-4)**
- Channel identification: 5-10 media channels matched to audience segments
- Segment-specific creative variations: What changes per channel?
- Budget allocation: Where does the media spend concentrate?

### Step 5: Campaign Ecosystem (Pages 15-17)

**Customer-as-Media Infrastructure (GP-2)**
- Shareable moment design: What will customers broadcast?
- Friction removal: What tools/templates do customers need to share?
- UGC incentive: Contest, giveaway, or social reward structure
- Hashtag/handle strategy: Brand-owned social infrastructure

**Contest Funnel Architecture (GP-3)**
- Giveaway mechanic: What product, how often, at what cost?
- Data capture: What information is collected at entry?
- Post-entry exposure: How does the entrant see the full product range?
- Conversion funnel: Entry → Browse → "I'll never win" → Purchase

**Email/Nurture Architecture**
- Welcome sequence: 5-7 emails for contest entrants
- Buyer sequence: Post-purchase onboarding → UGC encouragement
- Win-back sequence: For browsers who didn't convert

### Step 6: Business Economics (Pages 18-20)

**Unit Economics Model**
- Revenue per sale (average order value)
- Cost of goods sold
- Gross margin
- Target customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- Projected customer lifetime value (LTV)
- Payback period
- Break-even on test weekend

**Test Weekend Budget**
- Media spend: $___
- Production costs: $___
- Call center / fulfillment setup: $___
- Total test budget: $30-40K (Lynch standard)
- Success criteria: What metrics determine go/no-go at scale?

**Scaling Projections**
- If test weekend succeeds: Month 1-3 projected revenue
- Media spend scaling plan: From test → $100K/mo → $500K/mo
- Fulfillment scaling requirements: What breaks first?

**Deal Structure**
- Creative strategy fee: $___
- Production costs (passed through): $___
- Royalty rate: ___% of attributed sales
- Audit rights and reporting cadence
- Contract term and renewal conditions

## Output Format
```
## CREATIVE BRIEF — [Product/Brand Name]
## Prepared by [Strategist Name] | [Date]
## [Page Count]: ___/14 minimum

### SECTION 1: PRODUCT INTELLIGENCE (pp. 1-3)
[Product deep dive, competitive landscape, USP, demo concept]

### SECTION 2: CUSTOMER INTELLIGENCE (pp. 4-6)
[Voice Card integration, journey map, identity architecture]

### SECTION 3: CAMPAIGN STRATEGY (pp. 7-10)
[Transformation argument, sales angle outlines, offer architecture]

### SECTION 4: CREATIVE DIRECTION (pp. 11-14)
[Two-column script framework, visual language, media matching]

### SECTION 5: CAMPAIGN ECOSYSTEM (pp. 15-17)
[Customer-as-media, contest funnel, email architecture]

### SECTION 6: BUSINESS ECONOMICS (pp. 18-20)
[Unit economics, test weekend budget, scaling plan, deal structure]

### APPENDIX
- Customer Voice Card (full)
- Pre-Qualification Results
- Competitive screenshots/examples
- Reference creative (inspirations)
```

## Quality Gate
- Brief MUST be minimum 14 pages — if it's shorter, you don't understand the product
- Customer Voice Card must be integrated (not just referenced) — the customer's actual language must appear throughout
- All 12 Genius Patterns must be addressed or explicitly noted as "not applicable with rationale"
- Sales argument perspective outlines must include minimum 3 angles with hooks
- Two-column script framework must include BOTH copy AND visual direction
- Offer architecture must include price, bonuses, guarantee, and urgency mechanism
- Unit economics must be numerically specific — no "TBD" on critical financial assumptions
- Test weekend budget must total $30-40K with itemized line items
- Score ≥7 on ALL 7 dimensions of the Ron Lynch Quality Rubric
- Must chain to `/lynch-brief-audit` before production begins
