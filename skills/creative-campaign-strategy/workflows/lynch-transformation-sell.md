# /lynch-transformation-sell — Transformation-First Sales Creative

> Write sales creative that leads with the OUTPUT (the food, the adventure, the result) and positions the product as the "oh, you need this" discovery. Never sell features.

## When to Use
- Writing any sales copy, VSL, landing page, or product demonstration
- When current creative leads with product features or specifications
- When the product is a tool/platform/service (not an end in itself)
- When prospects aren't converting because they don't WANT the product — they want the result

## Inputs Required
1. Product/service description
2. The transformation it enables (what does the customer GET?)
3. Current sales copy or creative (for audit)
4. Target customer profile
5. Identity word (if available from `/lynch-identity-campaign`)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Transformation Identification
Apply Ron Lynch's "Food-First" principle:
- What is the FOOD (the delicious output)?
- What is the APPLIANCE (the product that makes it)?
- What is the COOKING SHOW (the demonstration of the transformation)?

Map it:
| Element | Your Product |
|---|---|
| The Food (what they want) | |
| The Appliance (what you sell) | |
| The Cooking Show (how you demonstrate) | |

### Step 2: Current Copy Audit
Score existing creative on the Feature ↔ Transformation spectrum:
- **1**: All features, specs, technology ("12MP sensor, waterproof to 30m")
- **4**: Features with transformation hints ("Capture your adventures in stunning detail")
- **7**: Transformation-led with product reveal ("Live boldly — here's how")
- **10**: Pure transformation — product is discovered ("You want to make THIS? Oh, you need...")

### Step 3: Reverse Sell Architecture
Structure the creative using Ron Lynch's reverse sell:
1. **Open with the transformation**: Show the food, the adventure, the result
2. **Create desire for the output**: Make the viewer WANT the transformation
3. **Reveal the product as the means**: "Oh, you need this to do that"
4. **Never reverse the order**: NEVER start with "Buy this product to get that result"

### Step 4: Voice Calibration (GP-6)
Write from inside the CUSTOMER'S head, not the brand's:
- What is the customer's internal monologue when they see the transformation?
- What metaphors do THEY use (not industry jargon)?
- How does a chess player talk to a checkers player without intimidation?
- The copy should sound like the customer's THOUGHTS, not the brand's pitch

### Step 5: Creative Execution
Write the full creative piece:
- Headline that names the transformation (not the product)
- Opening that demonstrates the output in vivid sensory detail
- Body that deepens desire for the transformation
- Product reveal positioned as the "oh, you need this" moment
- CTA framed as joining the transformation (not purchasing the product)

## Output Format
```
## TRANSFORMATION-FIRST CREATIVE — [Product]

### Transformation Map
- The Food: [what they want]
- The Appliance: [what you sell]
- The Cooking Show: [how you demonstrate]

### Current Copy Score: [1-10]
[Specific examples of feature-first language to eliminate]

### Reverse Sell Creative
[Full creative piece — headline, opening, body, reveal, CTA]

### Voice Notes
[Customer monologue samples, metaphor palette, sophistication level]

### A/B Test Recommendations
[Feature-first version vs. transformation-first version comparison]
```

## Quality Gate
- Product must NEVER appear before the transformation in the creative
- Copy must be written in customer's voice, not brand's voice
- The "oh, you need this" moment must feel like a DISCOVERY, not a pitch
- Must include specific feature-first language to eliminate from current copy
- Score ≥7 on Transformation-First in the Ron Lynch Quality Rubric
