# /lynch-identity-posture — Identity-Validated Campaign (Lynch × Dai Media)

> Identity campaign brief validated against Dai Media's Consumer Posture framework. Ron's execution layer + Dai's psychological profiling = identity-validated campaigns that sell to who the customer IS, not who you wish they were.

## When to Use
- When the identity word from `/lynch-identity-campaign` needs psychological validation
- When previous campaigns have underperformed due to wrong audience assumptions
- When building a campaign for a new market with limited customer data
- When "identity-first" feels right but you need the PSYCHOLOGICAL proof
- When the gap between brand identity and consumer self-image is unclear

## Inputs Required
1. Identity word and cascade (from `/lynch-identity-campaign`)
2. Product/service description
3. Target audience (broad definition)
4. Any existing customer data, surveys, or feedback
5. Competitor positioning (who else targets this audience?)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Consumer Posture Profile (Dai Media)
Build the 3-dimensional consumer posture:
- **Occupation dimension**: What they do for work and how it shapes their identity
- **Activity dimension**: What they do for leisure and how it signals who they are
- **Thought Process dimension**: How they make decisions and what they value

Apply the Kristen Stewart Test: Name 5-6 products this person buys that DEFINE them on social media. If your product can't earn a slot in those 5-6, the identity campaign will fail.

### Step 2: Identity Validation Cross-Reference
Cross-reference the Lynch identity word against the Dai Media posture:

| Test | Question | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspiration Fit** | Does the identity word match what the consumer ALREADY aspires to? | ✅/❌ |
| **Posture Alignment** | Does the identity word reinforce or contradict their projected image? | ✅/❌ |
| **Activity Resonance** | Does the identity show up in their leisure activities? | ✅/❌ |
| **Occupation Compatibility** | Does the identity conflict with their professional self-image? | ✅/❌ |
| **Decision Style** | Does the identity appeal to their thought process (analytical/emotional/social)? | ✅/❌ |

Score: 5/5 = Validated. ≤3/5 = Revise the identity word to match the posture.

### Step 3: Counter-Identity Mapping (GP-11 + Dai Media)
Combine Lynch's counter-identity with Dai Media's radical individual:
- **The real person** (Dai Media): What does the typical customer actually DO?
- **The posture** (Dai Media): What image do they PROJECT to their social network?
- **The aspiration** (Lynch): What identity GAP exists between posture and dream?
- **The campaign opportunity**: The gap between projected posture and deeper aspiration

### Step 4: World Design (Dai Media)
Build the emotional world around the validated identity:
- What WORLD does this product invite the customer into?
- Is this world designed around emotional OUTCOME, not problem-solving?
- Does the world feel like a place the customer ALREADY wants to inhabit?
- Apply the No Phone Policy: Would this customer stop scrolling to BE in this world?

### Step 5: Campaign Build (Lynch — with Validated Identity)
With validated identity and designed world, execute:
- Identity cascade using the VALIDATED identity word (may differ from original)
- Media-matched creative using the consumer posture segments
- Customer-as-media tools designed for the WORLD (not just the product)
- Contest/incentive layer aligned with posture-appropriate motivations
- Voice calibration: Write from inside the POSTURE-VALIDATED customer's head

## Quality Gate
- Consumer posture MUST use all 3 Dai Media dimensions (occupation, activity, thought process)
- Kristen Stewart Test must be applied with 5-6 specific products named
- Identity word must be VALIDATED against posture data (not assumed)
- If validation score ≤3/5, identity word MUST be revised
- World design must be emotion-first (not problem-solution)
- Voice must be from inside the POSTURE-VALIDATED customer's head
- Both expert contributions must be explicitly documented
- Score ≥7 on both Identity Clarity AND Voice Accuracy in the Ron Lynch Quality Rubric
