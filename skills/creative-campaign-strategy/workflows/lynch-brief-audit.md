# /lynch-brief-audit — Creative Brief Quality Audit

> Score any creative brief against Ron Lynch's 14-20 page standard. This is the quality gate between strategy and production — a mediocre brief produces a mediocre campaign. No brief goes to production without passing this audit.

## When to Use
- After completing `/lynch-creative-brief` (MANDATORY next step)
- When reviewing a client's existing creative brief before accepting the engagement
- When auditing why a campaign underperformed — the brief is usually the root cause
- When training a junior strategist's brief-writing ability
- Before any test weekend commitment ($30-40K is real money)

## Inputs Required
1. The completed creative brief (must be the full document, not a summary)
2. Customer Voice Card (for voice accuracy validation)
3. Pre-qualification results (for economics validation)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Structural Integrity Check

The brief MUST contain all 6 sections. Score each:

| Section | Required | Present? | Page Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Intelligence | pp. 1-3 | ✅/❌ | ___/3 |
| Customer Intelligence | pp. 4-6 | ✅/❌ | ___/3 |
| Campaign Strategy | pp. 7-10 | ✅/❌ | ___/4 |
| Creative Direction | pp. 11-14 | ✅/❌ | ___/4 |
| Campaign Ecosystem | pp. 15-17 | ✅/❌ | ___/3 |
| Business Economics | pp. 18-20 | ✅/❌ | ___/3 |
| **Total** | **14-20 pages** | | **___/20** |

**Hard rule**: If total pages < 14, the brief is NOT READY. Return to `/lynch-creative-brief`.

### Step 2: The 7-Dimension Lynch Quality Rubric

Score each dimension 1-10:

**1. Identity Clarity**
- Is the identity named in ONE word? (Not a phrase, not a tagline)
- Does the identity permeate every section — product framing, creative direction, CTA, social proof?
- Would a stranger reading only the identity word understand the campaign's emotional core?
- Score: ___/10

**2. Customer-as-Media Design**
- Is there a specific mechanism for customers to broadcast their experience?
- Are tools, templates, or social infrastructure provided?
- Is the shareable moment designed into the product experience, not bolted on?
- Does the brief project that customer media can eventually EXCEED paid media?
- Score: ___/10

**3. Transformation-First Architecture**
- Does the brief lead with the transformation (the food), not the product (the appliance)?
- Is the product revealed as the "oh, you need THIS" discovery moment?
- Could you remove the product name and the brief would still be compelling?
- Score: ___/10

**4. Voice Accuracy**
- Pull 5 random sentences from the campaign strategy section. Are they written in the CUSTOMER'S voice?
- Compare against the Customer Voice Card — do the metaphors, vocabulary, and sophistication level match?
- Would the target customer say "it's like they were reading my mind"? Or would they say "that's impressive"?
- "Reading my mind" = voice is working. "That's impressive" = you're in the wrong voice (HK-3)
- Score: ___/10

**5. Media-Creative Match**
- Are there segment-specific creative variations, or one ad for everyone?
- Does each creative variation match the identity of its target channel?
- Does the viewer see THEMSELVES in the ad before they see the product?
- Score: ___/10

**6. Campaign Ecosystem Coherence**
- Do all elements feed each other? (Identity → contest → purchase → UGC → new customers)
- Is there a self-reinforcing loop, or just a linear funnel?
- Would removing any ONE element weaken the entire system?
- Score: ___/10

**7. Business Economics Rigor**
- Are unit economics numerically specific? (Not "TBD" or "competitive")
- Does the test weekend budget have itemized line items?
- Is the royalty rate defined with a specific range?
- Does the scaling projection address what breaks first at volume?
- Score: ___/10

### Step 3: The "One Sentence" Test

Write the entire campaign strategy in ONE sentence:

"[Product] sells [identity word] to [specific customer] by [transformation mechanism] through [campaign ecosystem], acquiring customers for $[CAC] against $[LTV]."

If you can't write this sentence cleanly, the brief lacks coherence.

### Step 4: The Adversarial Stress Test

Challenge the brief from 3 hostile perspectives:

**The Skeptical Customer**: "Why should I believe this is different from [competitor]?"
- Does the brief contain a clear answer? Where?

**The CFO**: "Show me the math. When do I break even?"
- Does the business economics section survive scrutiny?

**The Competitor**: "We can easily copy this positioning."
- Is the identity defensible? What makes it uncopyable?

### Step 5: Final Verdict

| Composite Score | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **≥63/70 (avg ≥9)** | 🟢 **Production-Ready** | → Proceed to test weekend |
| **49-62/70 (avg 7-8)** | 🟡 **Revise & Resubmit** | Specific sections flagged for improvement |
| **<49/70 (avg <7)** | 🔴 **Major Rework** | Return to `/lynch-creative-brief` — fundamental gaps |


## Quality Gate
- All 7 dimensions must be independently scored with evidence
- No dimension may be "N/A" — every brief must address all 7
- If any single dimension scores ≤4, verdict is automatically MAJOR REWORK regardless of composite
- The One Sentence Test must be attempted — if it fails, that failure must be diagnosed
- All 3 adversarial perspectives must be tested
- Revision notes must be specific enough to act on (not "make it better")
- Must recommend next workflow in chain
