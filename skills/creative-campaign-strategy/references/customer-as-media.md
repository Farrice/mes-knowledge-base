# Customer-as-Media Engineering — Deep Reference

## The Core Principle

The goal is not to OPTIMIZE customer acquisition cost — it's to make acquisition cost approach **ZERO**. The mechanism: engineer the customer's natural post-purchase behavior into advertising.

Ron Lynch's question: "How do I make my customers readvertise and become my media channel for free?"

---

## The Customer-as-Media Framework

### Step 1: Identify the Shareable Moment
Ask: "What will the customer DO with this product that other people will want to see?"

- GoPro: Film extreme sports from first-person POV → inherently shareable
- Cooking appliance: Make incredible food → photograph-worthy
- Vacation: Experience something beautiful → Instagram-ready

**Rule**: If the product's usage isn't inherently visible/shareable, you must DESIGN the shareable moment.

### Step 2: Remove Broadcast Friction
Every barrier between "I just did something cool" and "everyone can see it" must be eliminated:

| Friction Point | GoPro Solution |
|---|---|
| "I don't have editing skills" | Downloadable commercial templates |
| "I don't have music" | Licensed music library provided free |
| "I don't know how to make it look pro" | Handles of professional GoPro commercials to remix/reference |
| "Where do I post?" | Branded hashtags + submission mechanism |
| "Why would I bother?" | Contest entry + identity reinforcement ("I'm a Hero") |

### Step 3: Provide the Tools
The brand must provide everything the customer needs to become a media producer:
- Templates (visual/editing templates they can customize)
- Audio (licensed music they can use legally)
- Reference material (professional examples to model)
- Distribution channels (branded hashtags, submission pages, featured slots)
- Recognition (featuring top UGC as official brand content)

### Step 4: Let the Flywheel Compound
```
Customer purchases → Uses product → Creates content → Posts content
     ↑                                                        |
     └── New customer sees content → Wants the identity ──────┘
```

This loop is self-reinforcing. Each new customer potentially creates a new piece of advertising. As volume grows, the ratio of paid-to-organic media shifts dramatically toward organic.

## The GoPro Case Study (Full Decomposition)

### Infrastructure Provided
1. **Licensed music library** — Customers could legally use high-quality music in their GoPro videos
2. **Downloadable commercial templates** — Pre-built editing templates that made amateur footage look professional
3. **Professional reference handles** — Links to official GoPro commercials that users could study and remix
4. **Branded submission mechanism** — Easy way to submit UGC for potential feature
5. **Daily contest** — Incentive to create and share (beyond intrinsic identity motivation)

### Results
- **100,000 unpaid "commercials"** created by users
- **Cost**: 365 cameras given away at cost of goods per year (contest)
- **Paid media cost**: Minimal — users became the media channel
- **Revenue trajectory**: $600K → $6M → $16M in 2 years

### Why It Worked
The identity (bravery) + the product (camera) + the usage (extreme sports) created a NATURAL shareable moment. Ron didn't force sharing — he removed friction from an activity customers already wanted to do.

## Advertising vs. Marketing (Ron's Distinction)

| | Advertising | Marketing |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | What YOU say to the audience | What makes THEM talk about you to each other |
| **Control** | Full control | Mechanism design, not message control |
| **Scale** | Linear (spend → reach) | Exponential (each customer recruits more) |
| **Cost** | Ongoing media spend | Upfront tool investment, then near-zero |
| **Example** | GoPro TV spots | 100,000 user-generated commercials |

**Ron's insight**: "When your customers are talking about you to OTHER customers, that's marketing. When you're talking TO customers, that's advertising." Layer 2 (marketing) is where the real scale lives.

## Applicability Assessment

### High Fit (natural shareable moments)
- Physical products with visible usage (cameras, apparel, tools)
- Experiential services (travel, fitness, food)
- Transformation products (before/after results)
- Community-driven brands (lifestyle, identity tribes)

### Medium Fit (designed shareable moments)
- Software/SaaS (share outputs, not the tool)
- Education (share transformations, certificates, results)
- Professional services (share case studies, wins)

### Low Fit (requires significant engineering)
- Invisible/private products (healthcare, finance)
- B2B infrastructure (no natural consumer visibility)
- Commodities without identity attachment
