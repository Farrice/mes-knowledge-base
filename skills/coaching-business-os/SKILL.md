# Coaching Business OS — Conductor

The Coaching Business OS is the front door for every business-of-coaching problem: pricing and offer design, positioning and belief-shifting messaging, fitness-content monetization, health-coaching client acquisition, and the transformation psychology that makes premium prices defensible. It never does lane work itself — it diagnoses which layer of the business is actually broken (the presenting problem is usually downstream of a different layer), routes to the owning lane, and composes multiple lanes when the mission spans layers. Every route stays inside this hub's own lanes; it is a self-sufficient system, not a pipeline into other hubs.

## Routing Map

| Business Need | Lane | What It Owns |
|---|---|---|
| Offer economics, pricing psychology, wealth identity, scaling ceilings | `skills/taylor-welch-wealthy-consultant/` | Identity/belief rewiring fused with consulting mechanics — when a revenue problem is an interior problem |
| Positioning, differentiation, belief-shifting message architecture | `skills/jay-hiette-coaching-positioning/` | Reality Matrix: contrarian messaging, symptom-language copy, VSSL deal flow — when the coach blends into the sea of competitors |

| Health-coaching business build (paid traffic → masterclass → application → call) | `skills/yuri-elkaim-health-coaching-business/` | Perfect Client Pipeline + 30-day Social Authority roadmap + High-Converting Offer blocks |
| Client transformation: flow, focus, peak performance | `skills/steven-kotler-flow-performance/` | 22 flow triggers, challenge-skills balance, motivation stack — the mechanism behind the promised transformation |
| Client adherence: habits, distraction, program follow-through | `skills/nir-eyal-habit-design/` | Internal triggers, timeboxing, pacts, Hooked-loop program design — clients actually do the program |
| Client mindset: values, meaning, self-sabotage | `skills/mark-manson-values-psychology/` | Value discovery and change, three-layer happiness stack — why change sticks or doesn't |

Routing rule: the first three transformation lanes (Kotler, Eyal, Manson) are the DELIVERY side — they make the offer's promise true. The first four lanes are the BUSINESS side — they make the promise sell. A premium offer needs at least one of each; workflow 02 does that composition.

## Quick Reference

- **Diagnose before routing.** "I need more clients" can be a positioning problem (Hiette), a pipeline problem (Elkaim), a content problem (Novotny), or a pricing/identity problem (Welch). Workflow 01 exists because the presenting symptom is usually the wrong layer.
- **Business side sells the promise; transformation side keeps it.** Never design an offer with acquisition lanes alone — the mechanism (Kotler/Eyal/Manson) is what justifies $2K-$10K pricing and survives the client's third week.
- **Ads guarantee visibility, not conversions** (Elkaim). If the offer or positioning is weak, routing to traffic burns money — fix Welch/Hiette layers first.
- **One pipeline before many channels.** Elkaim grew to multiple seven figures with one webinar and no social presence; Novotny did it with organic IG only. Pick one acquisition lane per mission, not all of them.
- **Field guide:** `references/field-guide.md` — marginal experts from the source harvest (Jonathan Mast's AI training-encyclopedia system, James Smith's revenue-backwards content ideation, Yuri Elkaim's pipeline talk) that inform the hub's diagnostic instincts.
- Peers like `strength-conditioning-os` or existing lanes (`luisa-zhou-coaching`, `dan-bolton-coaching-offers`, `greg-hickman`) compose freely with this hub when a mission needs both — an option, never a pipeline step.
