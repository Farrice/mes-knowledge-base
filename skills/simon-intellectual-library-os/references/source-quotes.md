# Source Quotes — Simon (Better Creating / Systems Made Better)

Verbatim quote bank from the two source videos. Factual grounding labels at bottom.

**Sources**: V1 = "Turn Books Into AI Business Advisors (Full Notion Demo)" (youtube.com/watch?v=OAgU6sOmih0) · V2 = "Build A Claude Knowledge Base That Self-Improves!" (youtube.com/watch?v=ib74sLgjIBM)

## On the core inversion
- "The problem with something like Notion or Obsidian to manage a second brain... is that they kind of ask you to be the librarian. You organize things yourself... What Karpathy has figured out with this approach using LLMs is that **the AI becomes the librarian**. You dump information in, Claude organizes and links it, summarizes it and indexes it." (V2)
- "We find something brilliant, we save it, and then we lose it." (V2)
- "Don't make this pretty. The point is it's a folder for capture. **The organization is the AI's job.**" (V2)

## On grounding
- "Not a chatbot that makes things up — a specialist collaborator backed by sources you choose." (V1)
- "The only reason they're any good is because they're grounded in real curated knowledge, not generic AI training data." (V1)
- "Without the knowledge base, I found that the AI tends to just be still a bit generic but with a very clear process that it is following." (V1)
- "It literally gets told: this is your purpose and your north star, and this is your knowledge base." (V1)
- Empty-KB test: "I've searched it. There's nothing in there. I don't think I can help you. **There is proof why this system is so powerful.**" (V1)
- "This is a specialist, not a chatbot giving you the same generic advice it gives everyone else... and it gets better every time you refine that knowledge base or add a new skill." (V1)

## On the schema
- "You'll see that we'll need for that knowledge base a topic, category, key insight, when to apply, confidence level, source. These are going to really be properties in the knowledge base." (V1)
- "Categories let the agent search by area... We've got confidence. This means it can prioritize proven stuff over untested ideas. If you're adding new material in, that will go in as low until something is validated." (V1)
- "What it's doing is atomizing. It's taking the key ideas in the book and making them very easy to read for an AI agent." (V1)
- "Daniel Priestley says something similar. So I've linked it to Daniel's ideas as well." (V1)

## On architecture
- "Three folders and one file... a CLAUDE.md at the top which is the schema... Raw — think of raw as your junk drawer... the wiki where AI writes the organized version. **You never edit this by hand.** And then outputs... those then get fed back in." (V2)
- "No database, no Obsidian, no vault setup, just folders and text files on your computer... no RAG embedding or any vector store. Karpathy's own knowledge base is around 100 articles and 400,000 words. And the LLM handles it fine, maintaining an index and reading what it needs." (V2)
- "A skill is a clear process or set of actions that you define, whereas a knowledge base holds the principles and ideas that inform the agent's decision making." (V1)
- "Think of it as the job description you'd write for the new hire... a training manual on one page." (V1)

## On process discipline
- "The key is not to just let AI do it all. You need to take some control of taste and choice about how it works." (V1)
- "Before we now begin the ingest, please update your instructions thoughtfully and without creating too much token bloat... that's better in the underlying instruction that we'll always use." (V1)
- "Please could you now review the instructions for token efficiency... keeping the quality... ensuring the steps are followed, but reducing the amount of content." → "55% shorter." (V1)
- "AI struggles with PDFs any longer than say 15 pages and honestly just pasting the content in is so much better." (V1)
- "You got to be careful because sometimes it can get lost in its process or you can overload the context window." (V1)
- "Look up AI writing style on Wikipedia, paste that into Claude and say create yourself instructions to never do any of this." (V2)

## On loops
- "Every time you ask the agent a question you like the answer to, you can then save that back... and the system gets smarter the more you use it. **Each question makes the next answer better.**" (V2)
- "The AI will sometimes write something slightly wrong. You'll save it back and the next answer quietly builds on a mistake." → monthly health check. (V2)
- Health check stages: "contradictions, broken backlinks and orphaned references, source provenance, coverage that the raw files have, stale articles... older than 90 days, and suggested new articles." (V2)
- "Suggested new articles. **This is probably where the real value is.**" (V2)
- "Review the knowledge base, flag anything outdated, suggest three new entries based on the gaps and it will get sharper every cycle." (V1)

## On economics
- "A lot of us won't want to be spending credits like this... go and make yourself a skill that does exactly that and then you can just trigger it with personal agent once a month... it's a hell of a lot cheaper." (V1)
- "Notion AI runs on different models underneath. So you're not locked into one provider." (V1)
- "You could go into Gemini and ask it and it would use the same instructions." (V1)

## On the payoff
- "Day one... your knowledge base is pretty basic, but day 100, it's a company asset that nobody else has. Your perspective, your sources, your judgment in one place." (V2)
- "It's that kind of asset that's nearly impossible to replicate because nobody else has read what you've read or saved what you've saved." (V2)
- The Godin advisor: "Position against AI productivity, not within it... it's crowded 'do more faster.' Yours is 'less but better.'... This does genuinely feel like I've asked Seth Godin how to approach marketing it." (V1)
