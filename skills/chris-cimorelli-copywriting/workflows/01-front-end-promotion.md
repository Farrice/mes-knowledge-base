## Workflow

### Phase 1: Big Idea Distillation
Take the product edge and compress it into a single sentence. This is the DNA of the promo — everything radiates from this.

Complete the sentence: *"This promo exists because [ONE insight] means [ONE outcome] for [the reader]."*

Test the Big Idea against:
- **Curiosity**: Does it make you want to know more? (Not just "that's interesting" — but "I need to see this")
- **Specificity**: Does it name something concrete, not abstract?
- **Skeptic-proof**: Can a jaded reader dismiss it in one sentence? If yes, sharpen.

### Phase 2: CLF Lead Construction
Build the 4-part opener:

1. **Shock Hook**: Stop the scroll. One sentence that creates a knowledge gap. Write 10+ variants, score each on Curiosity × Specificity × Credibility (1-10 each). Present the top 3.
2. **Personal Tie**: A brief "behind the curtain" moment — confiding, not bragging. Why YOU discovered this.
3. **Big Idea Drop**: The reveal. Present the Big Idea in 2-3 sentences that make the reader feel like they've glimpsed something valuable.
4. **Cliffhanger CTA**: Force the next action. Make NOT clicking feel like leaving money on the table.

### Phase 3: Email Hook Sequence (3 emails)
Build the acquisition sequence:

- **Email 1 — The Teaser**: Subject line = Shock Hook variant. Body = condensed CLF (200 words max). End with cliffhanger link.
- **Email 2 — The Story**: Personal Tie expanded. One anecdote that makes the Big Idea feel inevitable. Proof drip (1 data point, not a proof dump).
- **Email 3 — The Reveal + Urgency**: Big Idea fully unveiled. Risk reversal front and center. Scarcity element (limited spots, time-bound, or capacity cap).

### Phase 4: Sales Page Shell
Produce the structural skeleton:

- **Headline**: Best Shock Hook variant
- **Sub-headline**: Big Idea in one sentence
- **Opening section**: CLF lead (from Phase 2)
- **Body structure**: Story beats every 200 words, CTAs every 500 words, micro-commitments throughout
- **Proof section**: Proof Pyramid — broad market data → case studies → personal proof
- **Objection matrix**: Top 5 objections preempted in body copy
- **Close**: Asymmetry Principle — make the math undeniable

## Example Output

**Scenario**: A fintech newsletter ($49/year) that uses AI to scan SEC filings and flag unusual insider trading patterns before the market reacts. Target audience: self-directed retail investors, 35-55, who've been burned by "guru" stock picks but still believe in data-driven edges.

**Result**:

### Big Idea
*"This promo exists because a pattern in SEC filings — visible 72 hours before price moves — means retail investors can front-run institutional money for the first time in market history."*

### CLF Lead

**Shock Hook** (Top 3):
1. "A former SEC analyst just leaked the exact filing pattern that precedes 83% of major stock moves." *(C: 9 | S: 9 | Cr: 7)*
2. "Wall Street spent $2.3B building algorithms to read SEC filings. This $49 tool does the same thing." *(C: 7 | S: 9 | Cr: 8)*
3. "The last time this SEC pattern triggered, early investors made 4.7x in 11 days." *(C: 8 | S: 10 | Cr: 7)*

**Personal Tie**: "I've spent 14 years writing about financial edges. Most of them are marketing. This one isn't — I found it because a compliance officer at a mid-tier broker said something he shouldn't have during a conference happy hour."

**Big Idea Drop**: "There's a specific filing pattern — it involves Form 4 amendments filed within 48 hours of earnings — that correlates with significant price moves 83% of the time over the last 8 years. The institutional desks already scan for it. Now an AI does it for $49/year."

**Cliffhanger CTA**: "I've put the full breakdown — including the three tickers currently flagged — behind this link. Fair warning: the data is time-sensitive."

### Email 1 — The Teaser

**Subject**: The SEC filing pattern Wall Street doesn't want you scanning

You know what's funny about "insider trading"?

The legal version is hiding in plain sight.

Every time a corporate insider buys or sells shares, they file a Form 4 with the SEC. Public record. Anyone can read it.

But here's what nobody tells you: it's not the filing that matters. It's the *amendment* — filed within 48 hours of earnings.

That pattern has preceded 83% of significant price moves over the last 8 years.

Wall Street built $2.3B in algorithms to catch it. Now an AI does the same scan for $49/year.

[Three tickers are currently flagged → See them here]

*(200 words. Cliffhanger. No proof dump.)*

**What makes this excellent**: The lead doesn't explain the product — it sells the insight. The Shock Hook creates a knowledge gap ("SEC filing pattern") that can't be dismissed because it's grounded in public data. The proof is light and specific (83%, $2.3B) — enough to intrigue, not enough to satisfy. The cliffhanger makes the click feel urgent without hype.

## Quality Gate
1. **80/20 Check**: Is this 80% story and curiosity, 20% proof? If the proof outweighs the story, you've written a back-end piece.
2. **Skeptic Scan**: Read the Big Idea aloud to someone cynical. If they say "yeah right" — the proof isn't specific enough.
3. **CLF Integrity**: Can each CLF component stand alone? Shock Hook = subject line. Personal Tie = anecdote. Big Idea Drop = tweet. Cliffhanger = ad CTA.
4. **Momentum Audit**: Story beat every 200 words? CTA every 500? If any stretch goes flat, inject urgency.
5. **Asymmetry Visible**: Is the risk/reward math obvious to the reader without them having to calculate it?
