# Emotional Angle Library — Reference

Vince's emotional marketing principle: "An angle in our case is multiple emotions. It can be a good emotion or it can be fear, it can be loss, it can be confidence, it can be convenience."

The angle IS the emotion. The product is the vehicle for the emotion.

This library categorizes emotions Vince treats as primary ad-engineering targets, with seed examples and triggering mechanics.

## The 8 Primary Emotions (Vince Standard)

### 1. Fear
**Ad mechanism**: Show the consequence of NOT acting. "If you don't, X happens."

**Seed examples**:
- Fear of judgment: "I stopped going to the pool because of how my hair looked after"
- Fear of missing out: "Everyone in my friend group has one — I felt like the only person who hadn't tried it"
- Fear of failure: "I'd been trying everything for 6 months — nothing worked until..."
- Fear of decline: "I noticed it getting worse every month and I knew I had to do something"

**Visual triggers**: contrast (with/without), scenario showing avoidance behavior, before/after but emphasizing the BEFORE

**Watch out**: Fear-based ads can trip platform policy. Avoid medical claims, age claims, body shame. Frame fear as relatable not shameful.

### 2. Loss
**Ad mechanism**: Reference what's already been lost. "I used to have X, then I lost it."

**Seed examples**:
- Lost time: "I used to spend 2 hours every morning on this — now it takes 5 minutes"
- Lost money: "I'd spent $400 on stuff that didn't work before I found this"
- Lost confidence: "I used to walk into rooms feeling on top of my game — until..."
- Lost connection: "I stopped going on dates because of how I felt about myself"

**Visual triggers**: nostalgic frame ("remember when..."), the "old you" reference, montage of failed attempts

**Watch out**: Loss must feel relatable not pity-bait. The tone is "we've all been here," not "you poor thing."

### 3. Confidence
**Ad mechanism**: Show the after-state of someone in command. "Now I walk in and..."

**Seed examples**:
- Social confidence: "I caught myself smiling in photos for the first time in years"
- Performance confidence: "I went from shaky to closing without thinking about it"
- Body confidence: "I wore a sleeveless top to a wedding and didn't think about my arms once"
- Decision confidence: "I stopped second-guessing every meal choice"

**Visual triggers**: posture change (slouched → upright), eye contact direct to camera, slow walk-in, confident speech cadence

**Watch out**: Confidence can read as arrogance. Pair with vulnerability (the journey to confidence) or it lands hollow.

### 4. Convenience
**Ad mechanism**: Show the friction REMOVED. "I used to have to do X, now I just..."

**Seed examples**:
- Time convenience: "I make breakfast for 4 in the time it used to take me to make one"
- Cognitive convenience: "I don't have to think about it anymore — it just happens"
- Logistical convenience: "I don't have to go to 5 different stores anymore"
- Decision convenience: "I stopped having to choose between [A] and [B] — this does both"

**Visual triggers**: time-lapse, side-by-side (then vs. now), "watch how easy this is" demonstrations

**Watch out**: Convenience without proof = mid. Show the actual time saved with timer overlay, the actual steps removed.

### 5. Belonging
**Ad mechanism**: Show the community that "gets it." "Finally, people who understand."

**Seed examples**:
- Identity belonging: "Other [identity] people will know what I mean when I say..."
- Niche belonging: "I found my people — they all have this"
- Generational belonging: "Anyone else from [era/place/experience] remember when..."
- Pain belonging: "If you've ever dealt with X, you'll understand why this matters"

**Visual triggers**: group shots of "people like you," shared knowing nod, in-joke reference, community comments overlay

**Watch out**: Belonging requires real authenticity to a community. AI-generated belonging ads read as performative — middle-of-funnel real creators win this category.

### 6. Status
**Ad mechanism**: Show external recognition. "People started asking me where I got it."

**Seed examples**:
- Recognition status: "Three people stopped me on the way to my car"
- Aspirational status: "I felt like the version of myself I'd been waiting to be"
- Insider status: "Most people don't know about this — but the [relevant insiders] all do"
- Quality status: "I can finally afford the version of this that actually works"

**Visual triggers**: third-party reactions (other people's faces noticing), envy frames, "the look" close-ups

**Watch out**: Status ads can feel cringe. Anchor in specific micro-moments ("the cashier asked"), not generic aspiration.

### 7. Relief
**Ad mechanism**: Show the moment the pain stopped. "Finally."

**Seed examples**:
- Physical relief: "First time in 6 months I slept through the night"
- Mental relief: "I stopped checking my phone obsessively because I knew the [system] was handling it"
- Financial relief: "I stopped getting that pit in my stomach when I checked my account"
- Emotional relief: "I cried the first time I [outcome] — I didn't realize how much I'd been carrying"

**Visual triggers**: exhale moments, shoulders dropping, sleep scenes, the "finally" facial expression

**Watch out**: Relief works when the pain is named SPECIFICALLY. Generic relief ("life is easier now") doesn't trigger.

### 8. Curiosity
**Ad mechanism**: Open a loop. "Wait, you can do that?"

**Seed examples**:
- Mechanism curiosity: "I had no idea this was even possible until I saw..."
- Outcome curiosity: "I want to see if it actually works the way they say"
- Story curiosity: "She did WHAT to get this result?"
- Method curiosity: "I can't believe how simple it is — but here's why no one talks about it"

**Visual triggers**: pattern interrupt visual (unexpected frame), "wait what" facial cue, mid-scene start (in media res), surprising before/after

**Watch out**: Curiosity-led ads must DELIVER on the loop. Open-loop without payoff = clickbait, kills retention and brand trust.

## Mapping Emotions to Funnel Stage

| Funnel Stage | Primary Emotions | Format Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Top of funnel** (cold) | Curiosity, Belonging, Loss | Story-first, scenario open, AI-led production OK |
| **Middle of funnel** (consideration) | Confidence, Status, Convenience | Real creators, comparison, demonstration |
| **Bottom of funnel** (decision) | Relief, Fear, Confidence | Customer testimonials, UGC, specific outcomes |
| **Retention** (post-purchase) | Belonging, Status, Confidence | Community, identity reinforcement, advocacy nudges |

## The Emotion-Naming Pre-Brief Discipline

Before any concept brief is written, the strategist names ONE primary emotion for the ad. The brief then builds around that emotion:

```
PRIMARY EMOTION: Relief
DATA BANK QUOTE: "First time in 6 months I slept through the night..."
FUNNEL STAGE: Bottom (decision)
PRODUCTION TYPE: Real creator UGC
HOOK CANDIDATE: "I haven't slept through a full night in months — until [product]"
VISUAL OPEN: Creator in bed, exhale, slow open eyes, soft morning light
SCRIPT BEAT 1: Pain context (15 sec — "I'd tried everything")
SCRIPT BEAT 2: Discovery (10 sec — "Then I found...")
SCRIPT BEAT 3: First experience (15 sec — "First night I used it...")
SCRIPT BEAT 4: Outcome + invitation (10 sec — "I'd never go back")
```

The emotion is the spine. Everything else is in service of triggering that one emotion.

## Multi-Emotion Stacking (Advanced)

For VSSLs (3-5 min), you can stack multiple emotions in sequence:

- Beat 1-2: **Loss** (the pain you've been carrying)
- Beat 3-4: **Curiosity** (wait, there's a way)
- Beat 5-6: **Relief** (it actually worked)
- Beat 7-8: **Confidence** (now I'm the version of me I wanted to be)

But ONE primary emotion still anchors. The others are the journey TO that emotion.

## Anti-Patterns

- ❌ Naming "compelling" or "emotional" without specifying which emotion
- ❌ Stacking 4 emotions in a 30-second static (overload, none land)
- ❌ Writing the script first, then trying to "make it emotional" (backwards)
- ❌ Using fear-based emotion in regulated categories (health, finance, age) — platform risk
- ❌ Generic emotional copy that could fit any product ("life-changing," "transformative")
- ❌ Not anchoring emotion to a real customer voice quote from the data bank
