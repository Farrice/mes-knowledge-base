# /velocity-identity-partnership — Danny Yeung × Greg Hoffman: Identity-Engineered Partnerships

> Chain Danny Yeung's equity partnership model (GP-8) with Greg Hoffman's emotional value engineering to position celebrity deals as identity transformation — not endorsement. Yeung handles the DEAL STRUCTURE. Hoffman handles the STORY the partnership tells.

## When to Use
- Structuring a celebrity/athlete/influencer partnership from scratch
- Have a signed partner but the marketing around them feels transactional
- Need to pitch a celebrity where reputational risk is the primary objection
- Want to build brand identity that survives beyond any single partner
- Transitioning from paid endorsements to equity-based partnerships

## Prerequisites
- Load: `skills/velocity-scaling/SKILL.md` (Danny Yeung — equity partnerships, HK-2 reputational risk)
- Load: Greg Hoffman genius patterns (emotional value engineering, identity-based branding)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: The Partnership Identity Map
Before approaching any partner, define what the partnership MEANS — not what it promotes:

**Hoffman's Identity Questions**:
- What does this partnership say about who our CUSTOMER is?
- What identity does the customer try on when they see this partnership?
- What emotional shift does the partnership create? (Not "excitement" — deeper)
- If the partner left tomorrow, would the brand identity survive?

**Yeung's Structural Questions**:
- Is this person an authentic daily user of the product?
- Will they commit to 3+ years with equity alignment?
- Does their personal brand complement (not overshadow) the product?
- Can the product stand alone beyond this partnership?

### Step 2: The Reputational Risk Solution (Yeung HK-2 × Hoffman)
Danny Yeung's hidden knowledge: "No one's going after Danny. Everyone's going after David."

The celebrity's real objection isn't money — it's reputation. Solve it with Hoffman's framework:

**The Safety Architecture**:
1. **Scientific credibility** — Independent testing, clinical data, peer-reviewed backing
2. **Brand values alignment** — Show the brand stands for what the celebrity stands for
3. **Control provisions** — Give the celebrity product-quality veto power
4. **Exit dignity** — Contractual exit clause if product quality drops
5. **Peer validation** — Other credible figures associated with the brand

**Pitch pivot**: Don't sell "here's how much money you'll make." 
Sell: "Here's why your reputation is SAFER with us than without us."

### Step 3: The Emotional Value Narrative Design
Use Hoffman to engineer the story that surrounds the partnership:

**Three Narrative Layers**:

| Layer | Function | Example (IM8 × Beckham) |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface** | What the public sees | "Beckham invests in wellness brand" |
| **Identity** | What the consumer feels | "If Beckham trusts this for his performance, it validates MY choice" |
| **Aspirational** | What the consumer becomes | "I'm the kind of person who takes world-class care of themselves" |

**Hoffman's emotional value engineering applied**:
- The product isn't supplements → it's a performance PROTOCOL
- The partnership isn't endorsement → it's shared CONVICTION
- The consumer isn't buying → they're JOINING something

### Step 4: Partnership Content Architecture
Design the content strategy that makes the partnership feel authentic — not negotiated:

**Content Categories** (authenticity-descending order):

| Priority | Content Type | Authenticity Score | Production |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Partner using product in daily life (organic) | 10/10 | Captured, not produced |
| 2 | Behind-the-scenes partner involvement in product R&D | 9/10 | Documentary-style |
| 3 | Partner-hosted expert sessions (digital access layer) | 8/10 | Live, interactive |
| 4 | Co-created content (partner's ideas, brand production) | 7/10 | Collaborative |
| 5 | Produced campaign creative featuring partner | 5/10 | Professional production |
| 6 | Partner reads scripted ad copy | 2/10 | **AVOID** |

**Yeung's mandate**: "People just see right through" transactional endorsements. If the content could be mistaken for a paid ad read → kill it.

### Step 5: The "Beyond the Celebrity" Brand Identity Test
Yeung's structural firewall: "The brand has to live beyond him."

**Hoffman's Brand Survival Audit**:
1. Remove all partner imagery from the website. Does it still convert? (Test this)
2. Read the brand story without mentioning the partner. Is it compelling?
3. Show product to 10 people who don't know the partner. Do they still want it?
4. If the partner had a public controversy tomorrow, would the brand survive?

**Passing score**: 3 of 4 = proceed. Below 3 = the brand is dependent, not partnered. Fix the identity architecture before scaling.

### Step 6: Deal Structure (Yeung's Non-Negotiables)
Lock in the structure that creates genuine alignment:

| Term | Standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Duration** | 3-year minimum | Enough time for authentic integration |
| **Equity** | Meaningful stake (not token) | Aligns incentives with brand growth |
| **Usage** | Verified daily product use | Prevents hollow endorsement |
| **Involvement** | Weekly calls, product input | Partner becomes co-creator |
| **Independence** | Brand survives without them | Structural firewall |
| **Content rights** | Co-owned, perpetual license | Protects investment in produced content |

## Quality Gate
- Partnership must solve for reputational risk BEFORE pitching economics
- Emotional value narrative must articulate all 3 layers (surface, identity, aspirational)
- Content strategy must rank authenticity — NO scripted ad reads as primary content
- Brand independence audit must pass 3 of 4 tests
- Deal structure must include all 6 Yeung non-negotiables
- If the partnership couldn't survive the celebrity leaving → redesign before launching
