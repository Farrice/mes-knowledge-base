# /velocity-constraint — Danny Yeung × Dan Martell: Buyback Velocity Delegation

> Chain Dan Martell's "Buyback Principle" (diagnose WHICH tasks to delegate) with Danny Yeung's velocity filter (screen WHO to delegate to). Martell identifies the bottleneck. Yeung ensures the replacement moves at founder speed.

## When to Use
- Founder is the bottleneck and knows it, but delegation attempts keep failing
- Hired people but they move slower than the founder — creating frustration
- Need to figure out what to let go of first (Martell) and who to hand it to (Yeung)
- Scaling from solo/2-person to 5-10 person team
- Decision cycles are lengthening as the team grows

## Prerequisites
- Load: `skills/velocity-scaling/SKILL.md` (Danny Yeung — team velocity filter, founder-in-the-weeds)
- Load: Dan Martell genius patterns (buyback principle, constraint diagnosis, operational leverage)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: The Buyback Audit (Martell Framework)
Map every task the founder currently does against delegation value:

**Martell's Quadrant**:

| | Low Skill Required | High Skill Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Low Energy (drains you)** | **DELEGATE FIRST** — admin, scheduling, data entry, invoicing | **DELEGATE SECOND** — reporting, customer support, operations |
| **High Energy (fuels you)** | **DELEGATE THIRD** — social media posting, email management | **NEVER DELEGATE** — vision, key partnerships, product decisions |

**Action**: List every task from the last 2 weeks. Place each in a quadrant. Everything in "Delegate First" gets handed off within 7 days.

### Step 2: The Velocity Filter (Yeung Framework)
Once you know WHAT to delegate, screen WHO gets it:

**Yeung's Binary Hiring Screen**:
1. "I will text you at midnight. Will you respond?" (Not a metaphor — an actual test)
2. "I need this done by tomorrow, not next week. Is that your default?"
3. "I'll give you full context and authority. Will you run with it or ask for permission?"

**Velocity Interview Protocol**:

| Question | Green Flag | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| "Tell me about a time you moved faster than expected" | Specific story with compressed timeline | Vague answer about "working hard" |
| "How do you handle ambiguity?" | "I ship something and iterate" | "I research thoroughly first" |
| "What's your response time expectation?" | "Same day, often same hour" | "Within 24-48 hours" |
| "When was the last time you disagreed with a process and changed it?" | Last week | Can't remember |

**Kill rule**: One slow person contaminates the entire velocity chain. Don't hope they'll speed up. They won't.

### Step 3: The Context-Rich Handoff Protocol
Merge Martell's delegation structure with Yeung's GP-2 (Founder-in-the-Weeds):

**Yeung's mandate**: "I never tell someone 'just do this.' I always give the reason why."

**Handoff Template** (for every delegated task):
```
WHAT: [Specific deliverable]
WHY: [Business context — not just "because I said so"]
BY WHEN: [Deadline in hours, not days]
SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE: [Measurable outcome]
DON'T DO: [Explicit anti-patterns to avoid]
AUTHORITY: [What they CAN decide without checking with me]
ESCALATE IF: [Only escalate if these specific conditions arise]
```

**Yeung's acceleration effect**: When the person has full context, they make decisions in minutes. Without context, they schedule a meeting to ask questions → 48 hours lost per decision.

### Step 4: The Decision Velocity Architecture
Build the system that preserves speed as the team grows:

**Martell's Leverage Levels**:
1. **Replacement** ($10/hr tasks) → Delegate to anyone passing the velocity filter
2. **Growth** ($100/hr tasks) → Delegate to A-players with domain expertise
3. **Leverage** ($1,000/hr tasks) → Delegate only to people you'd trust with your checkbook
4. **Vision** ($10,000/hr tasks) → NEVER delegate — this is the founder's job

**Yeung's Decision Matrix**:

| Decision Type | Who Decides | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Tactical (ad copy, email subject lines) | Team member with context | Minutes |
| Operational (pricing changes, vendor selection) | Department lead with founder context | Same day |
| Strategic (new market entry, partnership terms) | Founder directly | Same day |
| Existential (pivot, fundraise, shutdown) | Founder with advisor input | 24-48 hours max |

### Step 5: The "One Slow Person" Intervention Protocol
When a team member becomes a velocity bottleneck:

**Diagnosis** (before firing):
1. Did they receive full context? (If no → founder's fault, fix the handoff)
2. Do they have the skill? (If no → wrong role, not wrong person)
3. Do they have the ambition? (If no → velocity mismatch, must exit)
4. Is the system creating friction? (If yes → fix the system, not the person)

**Yeung's compassion principle** (HK-5): "Letting someone free as early as possible to find the next thing is way better than to keep them on the ship."

**Timeline**: Diagnose in 1 day. Decide in 1 day. Act in 1 day. Never let a velocity mismatch linger.

## Quality Gate
- Buyback audit must identify 5+ tasks for immediate delegation
- Every hire must pass the velocity filter (no exceptions for skill alone)
- Every handoff must include full context (Yeung mandate — no "just do this")
- Decision matrix must show sub-24-hour cycles for all non-existential decisions
- Bottleneck intervention must complete diagnosis → action in ≤3 days
- Martell's leverage levels must map to Yeung's decision authority
