# /velocity-raise — Investor Pitch With Operator Credibility + Unit Economics

> Build investor presentations that lead with operator credibility, battle-tested unit economics, and the Danny Yeung "jockey, not the horse" conviction signal.

## When to Use
- Raising capital (seed, Series A-C, or pre-IPO)
- Pitching to angel investors or strategic partners
- Need to differentiate from all other founders pitching the same investors
- Want to lead with unit economics and operator credibility, not just vision

## Inputs Required
1. Business stage and current metrics (revenue, CAC, LTV, growth rate)
2. Capital needed and what it funds
3. Founder's personal story and track record
4. Target investor profiles
5. Competitive landscape

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: The Operator Credibility Stack
Danny Yeung doesn't pitch ideas — he pitches HIMSELF as the operator. Build yours:

**Credibility Layers** (stack as many as possible):
1. **The Number** — One metric that proves you can execute (revenue, growth rate, users)
2. **The Pivot** — Show you've already adapted when conditions changed
3. **The Speed** — "We built X in Y weeks" (not months)
4. **The Sacrifice** — What you've personally risked or endured
5. **The Conviction** — "I haven't sold a single share" / "I took no salary for X months"

**Danny's stack**:
- Built 5 companies across 3 industries
- Pivoted from genomics → COVID testing in 3 weeks
- Personally went door-to-door in COVID zones for 8 hours
- Never sold a share through $1B → $40M crash
- IPO'd on NASDAQ

### Step 2: Unit Economics Presentation
Lead with the math, not the story:

**Slide Architecture** (Danny Yeung order):
1. **The Problem** — Market size (TAM) + customer pain
2. **The Unit Economics** — CAC, LTV, payback period, LTV:CAC ratio
3. **The Subscription Model** — 3-month subscription play with retention data
4. **The Creative Machine** — Ad volume, testing cadence, scale potential
5. **The Partnership Strategy** — Celebrity/expert partnerships with equity alignment
6. **The Operator** — Your credibility stack
7. **The Ask** — Capital needed, use of funds, milestones it unlocks

### Step 3: The "Jockey" Signal
Investors who matter bet on the jockey, not the horse. Signal you're the right jockey:

**Conviction signals**:
- Investment in your own company (personal capital, not just sweat equity)
- Speed of execution (not plans — shipped products)
- Resilience through adversity (specific examples, not generic "it was hard")
- Network effects (who believes in you enough to invest time/equity?)
- AI-native operation (shows forward-thinking, not legacy mindset)

**Danny's paradox**: "Once you do due diligence, it doesn't work." The best investors know that financial analysis on early-stage companies is theater. They're betting on YOU. Make sure you're the signal, not the spreadsheet.

### Step 4: The "Cash > Market Cap" Patient Play
If applicable, deploy the asymmetric value narrative:

- "We have $X cash on hand"
- "Our market cap is $Y (less than cash)"
- "This means you're getting the entire business — team, technology, partnerships, revenue — for free"
- "We haven't sold a single share through this compression"
- "Patient capital wins. Impatient capital misses this opportunity."

### Step 5: The IPO Timing Strategy
For later-stage companies considering public listing:

**Danny's principle**: Time the listing as a strategic weapon.
- List when the market is hot (capture maximum capital)
- Use the capital to fund the NEXT pivot (not to sustain current operations)
- Build the war chest before the downturn hits
- "Create your own destiny" — don't let market timing create yours

## Output Format
```
## INVESTOR PITCH — [Company]

### Operator Credibility Stack
[5-layer credibility hierarchy with evidence]

### Unit Economics Dashboard
[CAC, LTV, payback, ratio — with trajectory]

### Pitch Deck Outline
[7-slide architecture with key talking points]

### "Jockey" Conviction Signals
[Specific actions that prove operator quality]

### Ask + Milestones
[Capital needed → specific milestones it unlocks]
```

## Quality Gate
- Must lead with unit economics, not just vision/story
- Operator credibility must include at least 3 verifiable proof points
- Pitch deck must follow Danny Yeung's math-first slide order
- Must include specific "jockey signals" (not generic "I'm passionate")
- The ask must tie to specific milestones (not general "growth")
