# /velocity-loss-leader — Guerrilla Loss-Leader Customer Acquisition

> Acquire customers and build brand credibility when you have zero brand recognition — using Danny Yeung's "buy Starbucks at full price, sell at half" guerrilla economics.

## When to Use
- Zero brand recognition in a new market
- Major brands won't partner with you yet
- Need to acquire first 1,000 customers with minimal budget
- Creating a marketplace and need supply-side credibility
- Traditional advertising isn't working because no one knows who you are

## Inputs Required
1. Target market and customer definition
2. Current brand recognition level (0-10)
3. Available budget for customer acquisition
4. Target CAC and timeline
5. Competitors or aspirational brands customers already trust

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Brand Association Mapping
Identify brands your target customer already trusts:

| Brand | Trust Level (1-10) | Your Customer Overlap | Partnership Likelihood |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Brand A] | | % | Low/Med/High |
| [Brand B] | | % | Low/Med/High |
| [Brand C] | | % | Low/Med/High |

**Key insight**: You don't need a partnership. You need the *association*.

### Step 2: The Starbucks Play Design
Danny Yeung's guerrilla formula:

1. **Buy** the trusted brand's product at full retail price
2. **Resell** on your platform at 50% discount (absorb the loss)
3. **Customers see** the trusted brand on your platform → instant credibility
4. **Customers return** for more deals → now they know your platform
5. **Real partners** see the traction → now they're willing to talk

**Economics**:
```
Investment: [Brand product cost] × [Units purchased]
Loss per unit: [Purchase price] - [Resale price]
Total CAC: Loss per unit + [distribution costs]
Value: Customer acquired + brand association + PR value
```

**Legal consideration**: "As long as you're a small company... beg for forgiveness later." This works when you're small. Once you scale, legitimate partnerships replace the guerrilla play.

### Step 3: The Cold-Call Bypass (for legitimate partnerships)
When the guerrilla play creates enough traction to pursue real partnerships:

1. **Research** the actual owner/founder of target partner brand
2. **Bypass** the marketing director (they'll say no reflexively)
3. **Call** the office directly and ask for the owner by name
4. **Lead with passion** — your story, your mission, your traction
5. **Show data** — "We've already brought X customers to your brand"
6. **Ask for a trial** — "Give us 30 days to prove we can drive sales for you"

**Danny's example**: Marketing director hung up on him. He Googled the restaurant group owner (Sandeep), called the office, told his story with passion. Sandeep gave him 20 restaurants. "Just because you're so passionate about your business, I'll work with you."

### Step 4: Loss-Leader-to-LTV Conversion
The loss-leader only works if you convert acquired customers to profitable LTV:

| Phase | Timeline | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | Days 1-7 | Customer buys discounted brand product |
| Recognition | Days 7-14 | Customer explores your platform/other products |
| Conversion | Days 14-30 | Customer purchases YOUR product at full margin |
| Retention | Days 30-90 | Customer subscribes or repeats at positive LTV |

**Conversion triggers**:
- First-purchase discount on your own products
- Bundle deals (trusted brand + your product)
- Email/SMS nurture with your product's value proposition
- Social proof from other customers who made the switch

### Step 5: Scale and Transition
Phase out the guerrilla play as brand recognition grows:

| Phase | Brand Recognition | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Guerrilla** (0-3/10) | Nobody knows you | Loss-leader + association plays |
| **Traction** (4-6/10) | Some awareness | Mix of guerrilla + legitimate partnerships |
| **Credibility** (7-10/10) | Established brand | All legitimate partnerships, products stand alone |

**Transition trigger**: When legitimate partners start approaching YOU → the guerrilla phase is over.

## Quality Gate
- Must calculate exact CAC through the loss-leader play (not "we'll lose some money")
- Must show the LTV conversion path (loss-leader isn't charity)
- Cold-call bypass must identify SPECIFIC decision-makers
- Transition plan must define clear milestones for phase-out
- Legal risk assessment must be included (even if "beg for forgiveness" is the strategy)
