# /velocity-retention-story — Danny Yeung × Kallaway Addiction Loop: Subscription Retention Engineering

> Chain Danny Yeung's 3-month subscription economics with Kallaway's 4-step Addiction Loop (Stakes → Big Question → Head Fake → Rehook) to engineer content that keeps subscribers psychologically committed past the critical Month 3 renewal point.

## When to Use
- Subscription churn spikes at month 3-4 (the renewal cliff)
- Product retention is fine but emotional retention is weak
- Need a content strategy specifically designed to prevent cancellation
- Want to apply storytelling psychology to subscription lifecycle marketing
- Building a retention content engine (not just a drip sequence)

## Prerequisites
- Load: `skills/velocity-scaling/SKILL.md` (Danny Yeung — 3-month subscription, digital access layer)
- Load: Kallaway addiction loop patterns (Stakes, Big Question, Head Fake, Rehook)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Map the Subscription Emotion Arc
Every 90-day subscription cycle follows a predictable emotional pattern. Map it:

```
Week 1-2:   EXCITEMENT  ████████████  (New purchase, discovery phase)
Week 3-4:   CURIOSITY   ██████████   (Exploring features, early results)
Week 5-6:   PLATEAU     ████████     (Novelty wears off, "is this working?")
Week 7-8:   DOUBT       ██████       (Comparing alternatives, questioning value)
Week 9-10:  CRITICAL    ████         (Pre-renewal decision zone — DANGER)
Week 11-12: DECISION    ██           (Renew or cancel — determined by Week 9-10)
```

**The insight**: Most retention strategies target Week 11-12 with desperate "please stay" offers. Yeung × Kallaway targets Week 5-8 — BEFORE doubt crystallizes.

### Step 2: The Addiction Loop Applied to Subscription Content
Deploy Kallaway's 4-step loop at each emotional phase:

**Phase 1: STAKES (Weeks 1-4)** — Make them feel what they'll LOSE
- Content type: "What happens when you stop [product benefit]"
- Yeung's framework: Show the specific metrics that decline without the product
- Kallaway's stakes: Make the COST OF STOPPING vivid and personal
- Example: "Your [metric] improved 23% in 30 days. Here's what happens if you stop now..."

**Phase 2: BIG QUESTION (Weeks 5-6)** — Ask the question that keeps them hooked
- Content type: "What would happen if you did this for 6 months instead of 3?"
- Yeung's framework: Share data from customers who stayed past the first cycle
- Kallaway's big question: Plant a curiosity gap that REQUIRES staying subscribed to answer
- Example: "We're tracking something in your data that gets interesting around month 4..."

**Phase 3: HEAD FAKE (Weeks 7-8)** — Subvert their expectation
- Content type: Introduce a NEW benefit they didn't sign up for
- Yeung's digital access layer: Drop an exclusive expert masterclass or community event
- Kallaway's head fake: They expected a product → they got a transformation community
- Example: "This month's surprise: A live session with [expert] exclusively for members"

**Phase 4: REHOOK (Weeks 9-10)** — Set up the NEXT cycle's addiction loop
- Content type: Preview what's coming in the next 90 days
- Yeung's framework: Reveal the next product drop, expert partnership, or feature
- Kallaway's rehook: End the current cycle with a cliffhanger that REQUIRES renewal
- Example: "In Q2, we're launching [exciting thing]. Members get first access."

### Step 4: The "Anti-Churn" Content Rules

**Kallaway's addiction principles applied to retention**:

1. **Never resolve tension completely** — Always leave one open question per cycle
2. **Reward consistency, not just results** — "You've been a member for 60 days" matters even without dramatic outcomes
3. **Surprise > Discount** — A surprise bonus (expert call, free product sample) beats a 20% renewal discount every time
4. **Show them their own data** — People don't cancel things they can SEE working
5. **Create social proof of STAYING** — "12,000 members just renewed for their 3rd cycle" is more powerful than any testimonial

### Step 5: The Renewal Cliff Intervention (Emergency Protocol)
If churn is already spiking, deploy these in order:

| Priority | Intervention | Cost | Expected Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personal video from founder: "I noticed you've been with us for 90 days..." | Time only | 10-15% churn reduction |
| 2 | Surprise bonus product or expert session | Product cost | 15-20% churn reduction |
| 3 | "Skip a month" option instead of cancel | Revenue delay | 30-40% save rate |
| 4 | Downgrade to lower tier (keep relationship) | Reduced revenue | 25-35% save rate |
| 5 | Loyalty discount for 6-month commitment | Margin hit | 20-30% save rate |

**Never**: Beg. Guilt-trip. Make cancellation difficult. These destroy brand trust and Yeung's DTC relationship forever.

## Quality Gate
- Addiction loop must deploy ALL 4 phases across the 90-day cycle
- Content must target Weeks 5-8 (pre-doubt), not just Weeks 11-12 (post-doubt)
- Head Fake content must introduce genuine NEW value (not repackaged existing content)
- Rehook must contain a specific cliffhanger requiring renewal to resolve
- Emergency protocol must be ordered by priority, not applied randomly
- No desperate discounting before trying surprise/value interventions first
