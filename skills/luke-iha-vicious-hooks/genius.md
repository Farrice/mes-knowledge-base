# Luke Iha — Vicious Hook Mastery: Genius File

## Core Philosophy

A polite hook is a dead hook. A comfortable hook is a dead hook. A hook that doesn't make someone flinch, gasp, or feel personally called out is a dead hook. You spend 70-80% of your writing time on the hook because if you can't get someone to open, to see more, to want to consume — your ad is dead on arrival. Most copywriters start their real ad 3-4 sentences in. They hedge. They warm up. They dribble the ball. A vicious hook goes right for the gut in the absolute first line. No hesitation, no breathing room, no throat-clearing.

## The 8 Vicious Hook Principles

### Principle 1: Relevant in the First Line
Must connect to something the reader already cares about within the first 10-20 words. Three relevance categories:
- **Pain/Condition Callout**: Call out a pain point or named condition they already understand ("cortisol" works if they already believe it's bad; "electrical imbalance" doesn't if they've never heard of it)
- **Belief Callout**: Take a stance for or against something the audience already debates — myths, sacred cows, community arguments
- **General Openness**: Pure transgressive intrigue — gossip, confession, voyeuristic pull. Hardest to write. Highest failure rate. Grand slam when they win.

**Key Insight**: Relevance is NOT always a pain callout. The "I went to sleep as a black woman" hook has zero product relevance — it works on pure human fascination.

### Principle 2: Emotionally Charged Language
Use visceral, earthy, concrete language — words with Germanic/Anglo-Saxon roots over Latin/French equivalents.

| Latinate/French (weak) | Germanic/Anglo-Saxon (vicious) |
|---|---|
| perspire | sweat |
| fabric | cloth |
| excessive | too much |
| insomnia | sleeplessness |
| severe | bad |
| synthetic | man-made |

**Power Words** (inject 1-2 per hook): banned, sexless, stink, panties, lying, die, naked, killing, terrifying, mortal, beg, murder, stole, rotting, bleeding, screaming, crawling

**The Rule**: Every word must earn its place. Lower grade-level = less friction = more visceral impact.

### Principle 3: Natural Intrigue & Open Loops
Don't give away the answer. The reader must be unable to make a confident prediction about what comes next.

**The Prediction Test**: If someone reads your hook and can guess what the ad will say → the loop leaks → rewrite.

**What a leaky loop looks like**: "The #1 thing to avoid with AI tools" — they can guess (hallucinations, privacy, etc.)
**What a tight loop looks like**: "When I cleaned out my father's guest bedroom, I realized I inherited his sexless marriage" — no prediction possible

### Principle 4: Stakes & Rubbernecking
Something heavy must be at risk — death, identity, marriage, health, money, reputation. The reader should feel the compulsion to look, like passing a car wreck.

- Implicit stakes: "My doctor accused me of lying" (credibility + health at risk)
- Explicit stakes: "...and it nearly killed me" (death)

If nothing real is at stake, the hook has no gravitational pull.

### Principle 5: Specificity as Spice
Add concrete details — but treat them like seasoning, not the main course.

- ✅ "after 26 years of marriage" (specific, grounds the story in reality)
- ✅ "my father's guest bedroom" (the detail "guest bedroom" makes it feel true)
- ❌ Overloading with 5 specific facts in one sentence (cluttered, kills rhythm)

### Principle 6: Doesn't Read Like an Ad
The hook should read like a confession, a piece of gossip, a story, a breaking revelation, or a piece of information promising new insight. Never like marketing material.

**Test**: Cover the brand name. Can you tell it's an ad? If yes → rewrite.

### Principle 7: Caveman Language
Write at the simplest possible reading level. Short words. Short sentences. Earthy. Raw. Rooted. This reinforces Principle 2 — Germanic word roots are naturally simpler.

After drafting, read it to a 12-year-old. If they can't understand every word → simplify.

### Principle 8: Consequence First, Mechanism Later
Lead with the consequence they already care about. Save your mechanism for the body.

- ❌ "This weird morning habit causes electrical imbalance" (they don't know/care about electrical imbalance)
- ✅ "This weird morning habit spikes cortisol" (they already know cortisol = bad)
- ❌ "Silver releases ions that neutralize bacteria" (mechanism-first, irrelevant to most)
- ✅ "The water you're drinking is feeding the bacteria slowly killing you" (consequence-first)

**Rule**: Only use mechanism in the hook if it's already widely known AND believed.

## Hidden Knowledge

### HK1: The 3-Sentence Hedge Diagnostic
Most writers start their real ad 3-4 sentences in. **Diagnostic**: Delete your first 3 sentences. If the 4th sentence is a stronger opener → those lines were throat-clearing. This is the #1 mistake Luke sees across hundreds of coached writers.

### HK2: The 70/80 Rule
Luke spends 70-80% of his writing time on the hook. The hook is not "the first part you write" — it's the entire creative exercise. Body copy is execution. The hook is strategy.

### HK3: The Prediction Test (Quantified)
Show the hook to 3 people. Ask "What do you think this is about?" If 2+ make the same guess → the loop leaks. Rewrite until predictions diverge completely.

### HK4: Relevance ≠ Pain Callout
Most people hear "relevant" and think "call out the pain in line one." Luke distinguishes three architectures (pain, belief, openness) and notes that the highest-performing ads often use general openness — which has no product relevance at all.

### HK5: AI as Word-Level Upgrade Tool
Luke explicitly uses AI to upgrade individual word choices — not to write hooks but to find "the most vicious possible words" for a concept. Paste copy → ask for Germanic equivalents and emotionally charged synonyms → swap in.

### HK6: The Transgressive Edge
The best hooks feel "borderline dangerous, like you're not supposed to see this story." This isn't recklessness — it's calibrated boundary-pressing. Topics others avoid are attention magnets.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

### The Gay Husband Hook
> "My husband came out as gay after 26 years of marriage and it nearly killed me."

**Principles activated**: All 8. Vicious first line (GP1). Emotionally charged — "gay," "killed" (GP2). Open loop — what happened? (GP3). Stakes — death and marriage destroyed (GP4). Specificity — "26 years" (GP5). Anti-ad — reads as confession (GP6). Caveman language (GP7). Consequence first (GP8).

### The Identity Wake-Up Hook
> "I went to sleep as a black woman and I woke up as a white woman."

**Why it's savage**: Pure general openness relevance. Zero product connection. Transgressive. Unbelievable. You MUST know the story. Grand slam open loop.

### The Sexless Marriage Hook
> "When I cleaned out my father's guest bedroom, I realized I inherited his sexless marriage."

**Why it's savage**: "Guest bedroom" is the telling detail. "Sexless" is the charged word. "Inherited" creates the open loop. The stakes are generational — inherited dysfunction.

### Anti-Exemplar: The Mechanism-First Mistake
> "This weird morning habit causes electrical imbalance in your body"

**Why it fails**: Leads with a mechanism ("electrical imbalance") nobody knows. Not vicious — it's polite and informational. No stakes. No charged language. Zero emotional pull. The reader scrolls past because nothing in this sentence connects to something they already care about.

## Signature Moves

1. **The Hedge Execution**: Delete the first 3 sentences of any draft. If sentence 4 is stronger → those lines were filler. → **Deploy when**: Reviewing any first draft
2. **The Flinch Test**: Read the hook aloud. Does it make you slightly uncomfortable? If not → rewrite. → **Deploy when**: Final hook validation
3. **The Germanic Swap**: Post-draft, find every Latinate/French word in the hook. Swap for Anglo-Saxon equivalents. → **Deploy when**: Word-level polish phase
4. **The Prediction Block**: Show hook to a stranger. Ask "What do you think comes next?" If they guess → loop leaks → rewrite. → **Deploy when**: Testing open loop integrity
5. **The Consequence Flip**: If any hook leads with a mechanism → flip it to lead with the consequence the reader already fears/desires. → **Deploy when**: Mechanism-based offers

## Decision Framework

Before writing any hook, answer these diagnostic questions:

1. **What relevance type am I using?** (Pain/Condition | Belief | General Openness)
2. **What is the reader's existing awareness level?** (Unaware → Most Aware)
3. **What are the highest stakes I can legitimately invoke?** (death > identity > health > money > inconvenience)
4. **Does my audience already know/believe the mechanism?** If no → consequence first. If yes → mechanism OK.
5. **What is the brand's transgressive tolerance?** (Conservative → Edgy → Boundary-pressing)

## Anti-Patterns

### AP1: The Warm-Up Intro
**What it looks like**: "Hey guys, so I've been thinking about this topic and I wanted to share something with you..."
**Why it kills**: 3-4 sentences of nothing. The real hook is buried. Dead on arrival.

### AP2: The Polished Professional
**What it looks like**: Clean, grammatically perfect, LinkedIn-appropriate language
**Why it kills**: Reads like an ad. No flinch factor. No charge. Sanitized = invisible.

### AP3: The Mechanism Lecture
**What it looks like**: "Our proprietary bio-cell restoration matrix uses ionized silver to..."
**Why it kills**: Leads with a mechanism they don't understand. Zero relevance. Zero stakes.

### AP4: The Loose Loop
**What it looks like**: "The one thing every entrepreneur needs to stop doing with AI"
**Why it kills**: They can guess (hallucination, over-reliance, etc.). If they can predict it, they won't read it.

### AP5: The Word Snobbery
**What it looks like**: Using words like "exacerbate," "facilitate," "paradigm," "leverage"
**Why it kills**: Latinate, abstract, high-grade-level. Creates friction. Kills visceral impact. Sweat > perspire. Always.

### AP6: The Generic Stakes
**What it looks like**: "This could hurt your business" or "You might be missing out"
**Why it kills**: Abstract, low-emotion stakes. No rubbernecking. Compare to: "and it nearly killed me."

## Voice DNA

- **Tone**: Direct, aggressive, urgent — uses combat/violence metaphors ("go for the throat," "foot on the neck," "right for the kill")
- **Pacing**: Short declarative sentences. Rapid-fire. No hedging.
- **Signature phrases**: "dead on arrival," "vicious," "go right for the gut," "doesn't let up for one second," "every word must earn its place"
- **Energy**: High-intensity coach energy — not theoretical, not gentle. Like a fight trainer before a bout.
- **Critical distinction**: The violence is in the METAPHORS about how seriously to take hooks — the actual hooks themselves are crafted, precise, and psychologically sophisticated.

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | Score 4 (Acceptable) | Score 7 (Good) | Score 10 (Savant) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First-Line Compulsion** | Hook is relevant but feels like a strong headline rather than something you MUST read further. | Hook stops the scroll and creates genuine curiosity, but the pull weakens after the first sentence. | Hook is physically impossible to scroll past — the reader's thumb freezes, their brain demands the next line before conscious choice kicks in. |
| **Emotional Charge** | Uses some emotionally weighted words but the overall tone stays safe and professional. | Language is visceral and earthy with 1-2 power words, but occasional Latinate/abstract words dilute the charge. | Every word is Germanic, guttural, concrete — the hook hits like a body blow. Zero abstraction. The reader FEELS it before they think about it. |
| **Open Loop Architecture** | Hook implies a topic but the reader can predict the general direction of the content. | Loop is tight enough that casual readers can't guess the answer, but sophisticated readers might. | Loop is airtight — no human on earth can confidently predict what comes next. The Prediction Test yields zero consensus across 3+ people. |
| **Stakes Communication** | Something is mildly at risk (convenience, minor frustration) but nothing that triggers rubbernecking. | Real stakes are present (health, money, identity) but communicated indirectly or abstractly. | Stakes are visceral, immediate, and undeniable — death, marriage, identity destruction. The reader feels the gravitational pull of consequence. |
| **Anti-Ad Authenticity** | Hook reads like good marketing copy — polished, purposeful, clearly selling something. | Hook could pass as organic content in most contexts, but a cynical reader might suspect an ad. | Indistinguishable from a confession, piece of gossip, or breaking personal revelation. Cover the brand name and nobody can tell it's an ad. |
| **Caveman Language** | Most words are simple but 2-3 multi-syllable or Latinate words create friction. | Consistently simple and earthy, readable by a 12-year-old, with rare exceptions. | A caveman could grunt-translate every word. Monosyllabic where possible. Anglo-Saxon roots throughout. Zero cognitive friction. |
| **Specificity** | Hook uses general claims or round numbers that feel manufactured. | Includes 1-2 concrete details that ground the hook in reality ("26 years," "guest bedroom"). | Specific details are the TELLING details — each one does double duty as both grounding AND emotional charge, making the story feel undeniably true. |
