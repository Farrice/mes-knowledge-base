## Phase 0: Revenue-Stage Diagnostic (NEW)

Before positioning, diagnose WHERE the writer is. Revenue stage determines which actions compound and which are premature diversification.

### The 5 Revenue Stages

| Stage | Monthly Revenue | Core Bottleneck | What to Build | What to DEFER |
|-------|----------------|----------------|---------------|---------------|
| **Stage 0: Pre-Revenue** | $0 | No proof of value | ONE door opener package + 3 case studies (even free/discounted) | Retainers, performance bonuses, adjacent services, personal brand content |
| **Stage 1: Survival** | $1-3K | Inconsistent deal flow | Repeatable client acquisition system + standardized delivery process | Performance kickers, multiple service tiers, content marketing |
| **Stage 2: Foundation** | $3-6K | Pricing ceiling (still trading time for money) | Consultant Frame shift — outcome-based pricing + first retainer conversion | Product development, audience building, team hiring |
| **Stage 3: Leverage** | $6-10K | Capacity ceiling (can't take more clients at current delivery model) | Adjacent services bundle (irreplaceability) + performance bonus architecture | IP products, courses, community building, speaking |
| **Stage 4: Scale** | $10-15K+ | Growth ceiling (need systems, not more hours) | Messaging Source Document as premium offer + systematized delivery + selective performance deals | Aggressive scaling without delivery systems, equity deals without track record |

### Stage Diagnosis Protocol

**Input Required**: Writer's current monthly revenue + number of active clients + primary service + biggest frustration.

**Execute**:

1. **Classify the stage** — use the revenue ranges above, but weight the BOTTLENECK description more than the dollar amount. A writer making $4K/month who has never converted a retainer is functionally Stage 1 (inconsistent flow), not Stage 2.

2. **Identify the ONE bottleneck** — name it in a single sentence. "Your bottleneck is [X], which means [consequence]. Everything else you could build right now is premature until this is solved."

3. **Premature Diversification Check** — list 2-3 things the writer is probably TEMPTED to do at this stage that would dilute focus. Explain WHY they're premature (not wrong, just wrong-order).

4. **Stage-Specific Success Metric** — define the ONE number that signals "you've graduated from this stage." Not revenue alone — the metric that proves the bottleneck is solved.
   - Stage 0: 3 completed projects with measurable results documented
   - Stage 1: 2+ months of consistent $3K+ without cold outreach panic
   - Stage 2: First retainer client at $3K+/month (not project-based)
   - Stage 3: Average client lifetime value exceeds $15K (irreplaceability proven)
   - Stage 4: Revenue maintained during a 2-week vacation (systems work without you)

**This diagnosis gates all subsequent phases.** Steps 1-5 below are executed through the lens of the diagnosed stage — not as a generic positioning exercise.

## Phase 2: Execution (Stage-Gated)

### Step 1 — Career Path Assessment (Stage-Filtered)

**Input Required**: The writer's target medium + current positioning + existing skills/experience + **diagnosed revenue stage from Phase 0**.

**Execute**:

Map the writer against Joanna's 7-career taxonomy:

| Career Path | Revenue Model | Ceiling Breaker | Client Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Channel Strategist | Retainer + performance bonus | Revenue share above milestone | Long-term optimization partner |
| Messaging Strategist | Intensive workshop + retainer | Value of consistent messaging | Strategic advisor |
| Brand Strategist | Retainer + brand system | Irreplaceable voice ownership | Creative partner |
| Copywriter | Project → ongoing → performance | Measurable conversion lift | Revenue architect |
| Content Writer | In-house or retainer | Pipeline value attribution | Invisible sales engineer |
| Ghost Writer | Project + adjacent services | Entire problem ownership | Irreplaceable partner |
| Newsletter Writer | Retainer + list growth + sponsors | Platform mastery bundle | Growth partner |

**Stage-Specific Filter**: At Stages 0-1, recommend ONE career path only. At Stage 2, allow a primary + secondary. At Stages 3-4, map the overlap between paths that create irreplaceability.

### Step 2 — Consultant Frame Construction (Stage-Calibrated)

**Execute**:

Transform the writer's self-concept from "service provider" to "consultant who solves business problems." **Calibrate language to the diagnosed stage** — a Stage 0 writer claiming "I consult on enterprise messaging architecture" sounds delusional. Frame must be credible at current proof level.

1. **Problem Definition**: What specific, measurable business problem does this writer solve?
   - **Stage 0-1**: Frame around the specific problem you CAN solve with evidence — even from free/discounted work. "I help [specific type of business] fix [specific broken thing] — here's what happened when I did it for [client]."
   - **Stage 2+**: Frame around the category of problems. "I optimize [channel/system] for [industry] — which means [measurable outcome]."

2. **Outcome Language**: Translate all service descriptions from effort-based to outcome-based.
   - **Stage 0-1**: Outcome claims must be backed by specific project results. No aspirational outcome language without receipts.
   - **Stage 2+**: Pattern-based outcome claims ("clients typically see X") become credible.

3. **Identity Vocabulary**: Build consultant-level language calibrated to proof level:
   - Stage 0: "I specialize in [specific outcome] — here's a recent example"
   - Stage 1: "I help [type of business] achieve [outcome] through [method]"
   - Stage 2: "I consult on [channel] optimization for [industry]"
   - Stage 3+: "I build [systems] that produce [outcomes] — my clients [proof]"

4. **Credibility Architecture**: Map what gives this writer authority — calibrated to stage. Stage 0 writers have PROCESS credibility (methodology, thinking, approach) even without OUTCOME credibility (case studies, revenue numbers). Don't fake outcome credibility. Lead with process credibility and convert early clients into outcome proof.

### Step 3 — Revenue Architecture (Stage-Sequenced)

**Execute**:

Design pricing and offer structure **for the current stage + the next stage**. Not the full 5-stage architecture — just what's relevant now and what to prepare for next.

**Stage 0 Revenue Architecture**:
1. **Door Opener ONLY**: A tightly scoped project priced to be accessible ($500-2,000). Purpose is proof-of-concept, not profit. Must produce measurable result within 2-4 weeks.
2. **Document everything**: This project IS your future sales material. Screenshot before/after. Track metrics. Get a testimonial.
3. **Next-stage prep**: While delivering, identify the 2-3 adjacent problems. These become your pitch for ongoing work when you hit Stage 1.

**Stage 1 Revenue Architecture**:
1. **Standardized Door Opener**: Now priced at $2-5K. Repeatable scope, predictable delivery timeline.
2. **Upsell Path**: Design the natural "what's next?" conversation that converts project clients to ongoing clients.
3. **Next-stage prep**: Track your effective hourly rate. When it exceeds $150/hour consistently, you're ready to break the time-for-money ceiling.

**Stage 2 Revenue Architecture**:
1. **First Retainer Conversion**: Design a $3-5K/month retainer offer. Base fee + clear deliverables + performance metric tracking.
2. **Outcome-Based Pricing Introduction**: Shift at least ONE element of pricing from effort to outcome.
3. **Next-stage prep**: Identify which adjacent services would make you irreplaceable. Don't add them yet — document them for Stage 3.

**Stage 3 Revenue Architecture**:
1. **Adjacent Services Bundle**: Add 2-3 services that surround the core offering. These create switching costs.
2. **Performance Kicker**: Revenue share or bonus tied to specific milestones. Only kicks in when client is already winning.
3. **Next-stage prep**: Start documenting your methodology as a system. This becomes the foundation for scaling.

**Stage 4 Revenue Architecture**:
1. **Premium Positioning**: Messaging Source Document ($30-50K) or equivalent high-value strategic engagement.
2. **Systematized Delivery**: Can you deliver results without being personally involved in every step?
3. **Performance Deals**: Selective revenue-share arrangements with ideal clients.

### Step 4 — Financial Foundation Mapping (Stage-Aware)

**Execute**:

If the writer has a "dream" career goal, map how the CURRENT STAGE feeds the dream:

1. Which of the 7 career paths is the **financial foundation** right now?
2. What does that foundation fund at Stage 4+?
3. **Stage-specific timeline**: How many months at current stage before graduation? (Use the success metric from Phase 0)
4. How does each stage **compound** into the next? The ghostwriting at Stage 1 builds voice capture skill → becomes the methodology moat at Stage 2 → becomes the irreplaceability architecture at Stage 3 → becomes the premium positioning at Stage 4.

### Step 5 — Competitive Differentiation (Stage-Appropriate)

**Execute**:

Apply Joanna's Liquid Death principle, calibrated to proof level:

1. **Stage 0-1 Methodology Moat**: Your moat is HOW you work, not results (yet). What's your unique process? Even early-stage writers have a distinctive approach to research, ideation, or delivery.
2. **Stage 2-3 Results Moat**: Now you have data. Build differentiation around measurable outcomes. "My clients see X because I do Y differently."
3. **Stage 4 System Moat**: Your moat is the SYSTEM — the methodology, the delivery process, the adjacent services bundle. Competitors can copy individual pieces but not the integrated system.

**Positioning Statement** (stage-calibrated):
- Stage 0: "I [specific outcome] for [specific audience] using [unique process]"
- Stage 1-2: "I [specific outcome] for [specific audience] — my last 3 clients saw [results]"
- Stage 3-4: "I build [systems] that [outcome] for [audience] — which means [measurable result they can expect]"

**Anti-Positioning**: What you are NOT. What you don't do. What clients are wrong for you. This gets sharper at each stage as you learn who your best clients actually are.
