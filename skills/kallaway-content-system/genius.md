## Core Genius

Kallaway's hidden move is not "use AI for content." It is **workflow partitioning**: separate the work into stages, assign AI to stages where data and repetition dominate, then protect the human-only moments where content earns originality.

His production unit is a six-stage rep:

1. **Topic** — choose what already has demand.
2. **Format** — choose the structural container that already performs.
3. **Substance** — create the contrarian take and evidence.
4. **Hook** — coordinate spoken, text, and visual openings.
5. **Script** — write through a proven voice/rhythm profile.
6. **Edit** — choose the right production path for the creator's leverage.

Then the rep becomes a batch. Ten videos get posted back to back. Performance is judged by the highest-value available metric: conversions, then followers, then views. A 5x winner informs three of the next ten. A 10x winner gets the same topic and format rerun.

## What Makes This Different

Most content systems over-index on calendars, prompt banks, or generic ideation. Kallaway builds around **validated creative constraints**. He wants the creator's freedom to happen inside a high-signal lane:

- data picks the arena,
- format picks the rules,
- human substance creates the edge,
- hooks package the edge,
- scripts preserve voice,
- batches turn performance into compounding selection.

The system is anti-slop because it does not ask AI to invent the creator's point of view. AI clears the path so the point of view has more room to exist. Kallaway names his own philosophy for this directly: "It's not that AI is going to remove human creativity. It's that it frees up human creativity" (transcript.txt:830-832, 2026-05-07) — the whole architecture, from the 8-to-15-bucket topic cluster down to the 10-video batch, exists to protect that one sentence.

## Genius Patterns

### 1. The Six-Stage Rep

**Unconscious behavior:** He never treats "make a video" as one task. He breaks it into topic, format, substance, hook, script, and edit so each stage can be improved separately.

**Executable behavior:** Diagnose any content bottleneck by stage. Do not rewrite a script when the real problem is a losing topic. Do not change the hook when the format cannot carry the take.

**Success metric:** Every video brief identifies the weakest stage before production begins.

### 2. Signal Before Creativity

**Unconscious behavior:** He refuses to spend creative energy on unvalidated topics and formats.

**Executable behavior:** Build a watch list of 20-30 micro, small, and medium creators in the niche. Sort recent videos by outlier score, filter for meaningful engagement, deep-analyze the strongest set, export the structured report, and ask Claude to rank specific topics and topic buckets.

**Success metric:** No sprint begins without a ranked topic and format list.

### 3. Specific Topic, Not Category

**Unconscious behavior:** He distinguishes broad categories from workable topics. "Hooks" is too broad. "Text hook matters more than spoken hook" can be reacted to.

**Executable behavior:** Reduce every idea to a 3-5 word specific framing plus a one-sentence pitch for why it fits the creator's authority statement.

**Success metric:** The creator can immediately answer, "What do I believe here that most people miss?"

### 4. Format As Creative Constraint

**Unconscious behavior:** He locks format before substance because restrictive formats change which takes are possible.

**Executable behavior:** Decide whether the format is restrictive, moderate, or loose. Use restrictive formats like tier lists, A/B, clone, rating, or ranking when the creator needs rails. Use breakdown, case study, or scenario when the creator has a strong freestyle point of view. His own example: choosing an "S tier rank format" for a hooks video forced the contrarian take to live inside the S-A-B-C-D-F ranking itself instead of anywhere he wanted (transcript.txt:504-512, 2026-05-07) — as he puts it, "Constraints breed creativity is like a common frame" (transcript.txt:516, 2026-05-07).

**Success metric:** The take and evidence fit the chosen format without forcing.

### 5. Substance Is The Sauce Layer

**Unconscious behavior:** He treats substance as the place where originality lives: contrarian take plus evidence.

**Executable behavior:** For each validated topic, generate the creator's non-obvious belief, then build evidence from proof, A/B contrast, examples, stories, metaphors, case studies, and psychology.

**Success metric:** The video can be summarized as "Most people think X, but actually Y, and here is why."

### 6. AI As Evidence Partner, Not Take Creator

**Unconscious behavior:** He knows AI is poor at first-principles novelty but useful for supporting a human claim.

**Executable behavior:** Do not ask AI for the final opinion. Ask it: "Given my topic, format, and contrarian take, what evidence would make this claim believable?"

**Success metric:** The creator owns the take; AI supplies research directions and proof options.

### 7. Hook Triad Coordination

**Unconscious behavior:** He separates the hook into visual hook, text hook, and spoken hook, then studies proven examples by format.

**Executable behavior:** Generate spoken hooks from format-matched hook patterns. Then manually inspect outlier videos for text and visual execution because the eyes can read opening design faster than a visual model can describe it. Kallaway's own proof case: "one video flopping and one crushing in the exact same hook, but one has title text and one doesn't" (transcript.txt:452, 2026-05-07) — the spoken hook was identical; the text hook decided the outcome.

**Success metric:** Text hook adds instant visual comprehension; spoken hook opens the loop; visual hook prevents scroll.

### 8. Script Profile Over Generic AI Writing

**Unconscious behavior:** He does not ask AI to "write a script" cold. He trains a profile from 10-20 winning transcripts from one voice.

**Executable behavior:** Build a script model from one creator's strongest relevant videos, then feed it topic, format, contrarian take, evidence, and hook.

**Success metric:** Draft lands 90-95% of the way there because it is writing from prepared bones and a focused rhythm model.

### 9. Edit Path Is A Leverage Decision

**Unconscious behavior:** He does not romanticize editing. If content is a business engine, the creator should not live in low-leverage editing tasks.

**Executable behavior:** Choose one of four edit paths: low-edit format, DIY brute force, emerging AI/Remotion automation, or outsourced editor. The choice must match resources, quality needs, and creator leverage. Kallaway states this as a hard boundary, not a preference: "You cannot be editing. It's too low leverage of a task for you as the creator to be doing it" (transcript.txt:804-806, 2026-05-07).

**Success metric:** Editing supports the format and business model without stealing the creator's creative time.

### 10. The 10x Batch System

**Unconscious behavior:** He treats ten posts as one learning unit. Performance is judged relative to the batch, not by isolated feelings.

**Executable behavior:** Publish 10 videos back to back. Calculate average conversions, followers, or views. Any 5x winner influences three of the next ten. Any 10x winner gets the same topic and format rerun.

**Success metric:** Three differentiated winning combinations emerge and become the default production base.

## Hidden Knowledge

- **The content system is really an attention allocation system.** AI is valuable because it moves attention away from research and toward the take.
- **Data validation increases creative courage.** When topic and format are already proven, the creator can push harder on the non-obvious point.
- **Format can generate insight.** A restrictive format forces choices and often reveals the contrarian take.
- **A generic script is usually an upstream failure.** Bad AI scripts often happen because the user did not provide topic, format, take, evidence, hook, and voice model.
- **Views are the fallback metric.** The real hierarchy is conversions per view, then followers per view, then views.
- **A winner is not a command to clone everything.** A 5x winner gets three slots in the next batch, not all ten, to avoid audience fatigue.

## Hall Of Fame Exemplars

### Exemplar 1: The Full Rep Unit

**Context:** Kallaway defines the repeatable process for one premium short-form video.

**Example:** "To make one video, I go through the same six stages every single time" (transcript.txt:44, 2026-05-07): topic, format, substance, hook, script, edit.

**Why it matters:** The simplicity is deceptive. Each stage has a different decision owner. Topic and format are data-heavy. Substance is human-heavy. Hook and script are hybrid. Edit is a leverage call.

### Exemplar 2: Text Hook Insight

**Context:** While explaining contrarian takes, Kallaway gives the hook example.

**Example:** "One video flopping and one crushing in the exact same hook, but one has title text and one doesn't" (transcript.txt:452, 2026-05-07). The most valuable hook insight may have nothing to do with the spoken first sentence — it may be that the on-screen text is where the viewer's eyes go first.

**Why it matters:** This shows how he thinks: start with the obvious arena, then look for the overlooked mechanism that actually changes behavior.

### Exemplar 3: The 10x Carry-Forward Rule

**Context:** Kallaway explains how a batch becomes a system.

**Example:** If one video in a 10-video batch performs 5x to 10x over average, three of the next ten should follow that topic and format. If it clears 10x, rerun the same topic and format.

**Why it matters:** This prevents both chaos and overfitting. The system follows winners without letting one signal hijack the whole calendar.

### Anti-Exemplar: Prompt-And-Post AI Content

**What mediocre looks like:** Asking AI to generate ten content ideas, scripts, and captions with no outlier evidence, no human take, no format lock, and no batch review — the exact failure mode Kallaway names when he says AI is "good at regurgitating what it's been trained on and what it's heard" and "not great at coming up with novel things outside the box" (transcript.txt:472-474, 2026-05-07).

**Why it fails:** It automates the one thing that should remain human and skips the boring work AI is best at.

## Signature Moves

- **Lock The Arena First:** Validate topic and format before working on substance. Deploy when a creator wants to "brainstorm."
- **Ask For The Shock Belief:** Force the creator to state what they believe that others would initially doubt. Deploy when a topic feels flat.
- **Evidence Like A Lawyer:** Treat every claim as something that must be proven under time pressure. Deploy after the contrarian take is chosen.
- **Inspect Hooks By Format:** Study hooks from the same format before writing your own. Deploy before scripting.
- **Model One Voice:** Build script profiles from one focused creator or one creator's own winners. Deploy when AI drafts sound generic.
- **Carry Winners Forward:** Let 5x and 10x batch signals decide the next batch mix. Deploy after every ten posts.

## Quality Rubric

| Criterion | Score 4 | Score 7 | Score 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Topic validation | Topic is plausible | Topic appears in outlier data | Topic is ranked, specific, and mapped to authority statement |
| Format fit | Format is named | Format matches platform norms | Format constrains and strengthens the take |
| Substance | Take is useful | Take is non-obvious | Take is shocking at first and obvious after proof |
| Evidence | One support point | Multiple proof types | Proof stack includes contrast, example, psychology, and visual proof |
| Hook triad | Spoken hook only | Spoken and text hook align | Visual, text, and spoken hooks each do distinct work |
| Script profile | Generic style prompt | Based on examples | Based on 10-20 top transcripts from one focused voice |
| Edit path | Editing is assumed | Editing path is chosen | Editing path matches leverage, format, and quality bar |
| Batch feedback | Reviews views | Reviews relative winners | Uses conversions, followers, or views with 5x/10x carry-forward rules |

## Anti-Patterns

- Choosing topics because they feel interesting but have no evidence — Kallaway's own rule is the opposite: "my process is if I don't have an original idea I want to make, I'm always starting with this list [the outlier-ranked topic report]" (transcript.txt:222, 2026-05-07).
- Treating categories as topics — "the topic should not be at the category level" and a category like "hooks or storytelling or Nike or Apple... that's way too broad" (transcript.txt:204, 372, 2026-05-07).
- Moving to scripting before format is locked.
- Asking AI to invent the creator's belief — Kallaway is direct that AI "is good at regurgitating what it's been trained on and what it's heard" and "not great at coming up with novel things outside the box" (transcript.txt:472-474, 2026-05-07).
- Repeating the same message across visual, text, and spoken hook.
- Combining many creator voices into one script profile — "I don't recommend combining multiple creators here because oftentimes... speaking patterns are like fingerprints. If you combine examples from all three, it'll confuse the writer and make it generic" (transcript.txt:688-692, 2026-05-07).
- Editing everything personally when the goal is business scale — "you cannot be editing. It's too low leverage of a task for you as the creator to be doing it" (transcript.txt:804-806, 2026-05-07).
- Declaring a single good post a new strategy without batch comparison — carried winners get three of the next ten, not all ten, because running them all "is going to get way too repetitive for you and the viewer" (transcript.txt:952, 2026-05-07).
