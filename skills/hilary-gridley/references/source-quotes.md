# Hilary Gridley — Anchor Quotes (verbatim, from source transcript + slide)

Calibration anchors. When a workflow output drifts abstract, return here — this is her register: concrete, story-first, stakes-aware, laughing but precise.

## The bar
- "You have to make it super clear for people what good looks like... If you are not setting that quality bar as a leader, you can't be surprised when that quality starts slipping."
- "When people find out that there's an easy button that they can press to do their job for them... you notice it starts kind of seeping into more and more things."
- "You kind of have this feeling that you're just talking to a person who's just an intermediary for Claude — I could go talk to Claude if I wanted to."

## The three roots
- "Everybody's kind of doing this in their own way... completely decentralized." / "They're all pulling from different context... some outdated, some conflicting. There's no canon, no central brain." / "Nobody is really articulating what good looks like here."

## The pipeline (crown jewel, full verbatim)
- "I literally had a document and in one column I had draft emails that people had sent me and then in the other column I had my revisions... I said what is the difference between column A and column B?... what are the edits that I make over and over and over again and can you help me spot the patterns? AI is very good at spotting patterns."
- "Turn those rules into criteria — give me five criteria — and then write out in plain English what does passing versus failing that criteria look like? And then you have a rubric and you can run anything against a rubric."
- "Write that rubric as a prompt that I can paste into a custom GPT or into a skill... It's just English... paste that in the system prompt and then anyone can use it. Anyone has access to your brain."
- Criteria she surfaced: "does it lead with the message in the very first sentence, is it actionable, is the tone right, is every single word adding clarity rather than ambiguity."

## Tool scoping
- "I've seen people who make these second brains for their team... I was NOT like 'I'm going to make a second Hilary.' I made this as specific as: an editor for emails that you are sending to an executive that you need to get a yes on. I've made dozens of these and they're all that specific."
- "This is different than just saying 'hey, can you run your emails through Claude before you send them' — there's no me in that equation."
- "On-demand access to my own thinking models rather than just saying 'yeah, I don't know, go talk to Claude.'"

## AI-native redesign
- "We're past the point of starting from where we are today and incrementally adding some AI here and there."
- "What if all your dreams come true and your team is totally AI-native — what does a day in their life look like? Can you show that picture to them?"
- "If nothing was a surprise and if the AI could always go one step further."
- "Very concrete to the point of: what are the data sources this is pulling from, what is the exact next action the AI is doing, and what is the role of the human in all of this."
- "It reduces the fear, uncertainty and doubt... gives them confidence in you because it seems like you know what you're doing... and gives them something to work toward."

## Accountability
- "The job has never really been about the work — it's always been about accountability for the work."
- "In theory you could have contracted your work out to some third party surreptitiously... the only reason anyone would ever know is if the work wasn't good... now anyone has that third party and it doesn't have to be a secret. But you're still accountable."
- "'I told my team to use AI and now they're making all this bad stuff' — that's too focused on HOW to do the work... have the conversation about what you're accountable for and what good looks like."

## Feedback culture
- "This is a good first start but it still feels too AI-generated, so keep going."
- "It's not 'you made slop, you're a bad person.' It's 'okay, this is a first take — it doesn't seem like you have put a ton of thought into it, so I'm not going to put a ton of thought into it. Why don't you take another pass and put your spin on this, and then I'll sit down and put my spin on this.'"

## Layers, loops, and the split
- "I built these 10 applications that nobody's ever going to see or use... because I thought it was cool and only I thought it was cool."
- "Teams that get better make the systems get better, which makes the people get better — a virtuous cycle. As opposed to cognitive rot... people get worse, systems get worse — the slop doom loop."
- "The cognitive energy it takes to get from 0 to 80% is a lot and it's not that differentiated. The differentiation comes in exercising your judgment and taste to get it from good to great. If good lands on your desk and your job becomes getting everything to great — that's a lot of fun."
- "Even if we had no AI, if you took the time to assemble something like that and disseminated it, everything would improve immediately."
- "I laugh with all this AI stuff because it's just good leadership and management."
- Context: "the information that a person or an AI agent needs in order to do a job well... If I say 'here's every A/B test we have ever run, knock yourself out' — that's not helpful. Likewise it is not helpful to give them no information."
