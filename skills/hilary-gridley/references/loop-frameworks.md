# Loop Frameworks — Kipp Bodnar (host-side material, attributed)

These frameworks belong to **Kipp Bodnar** (HubSpot CMO) and **Kieran Flanagan**, from their book *Loop* (announced for September release in the interview). They are captured separately from Hilary Gridley's system — one author per body. Cite them as Bodnar/Flanagan, never as Gridley.

## 1. The Taste Profile (the concept Farrice is productizing — see `taste-profile-spec.md`)

Verbatim essence: "All your context that you need to build your marketing... all of the stuff that ISN'T in an ideal customer profile — non-demographic data. What that customer **believes**, what they **feel**, what **pushes them too far**... that is actually the instructions that AI needs. They need a deep emotional understanding of the customer. And the other part of it is all the **stories you as a brand are trying to tell** — the core product narrative, the core emotional brand story, the thing you want people to feel."

Payoff claim: "If everybody on your team has access to that same taste profile, the quality of work automatically goes up. The consistency automatically goes up... the judgment making by both AI and the people gets a lot more focused."

Gridley's convergent endorsement (bridge between the two systems): "Even if we had no AI, if you as a marketing leader took the time to assemble something like that and disseminated it, everything would improve immediately... in the new world you actually need more depth. **Slop is a function of a lack of depth.**" *(the "slop = lack of depth" line is Kipp's, seconded by her)*

## 2. Craftspeople vs Context Carriers

Two pre-AI manager types: **deep craftspeople** (great email marketer, creative director, UX designer) and **context carriers** — "a human API... glue that could move context around" with no deep craft. AI obsoletes the second group's default mode: "AI makes it really easy to move information around. And that's what creates all the slop — information regurgitation on an endless cycle."

Gridley's amendment: context carriers with genuine context-genius still have a role — keeping canon real and updated is hard — but "if you're not really sure what value you bring, that is going to be made very apparent in the AI era."

Craftspeople's opportunity: "shape these tools so they are making my team better at what they do **even if you took the tools away tomorrow**" (= her kick-the-crutch test, converged).

## 3. Months-to-Minutes Constraint

"Things have moved from months to minutes." Use timeline compression as a forcing function: "You need to get your CEO a prototype which he or she can review **within 30 minutes** — and it can't be slop, because I'm not going to send a slop landing page to my CEO. It's a really good constraint and forcing function. If you can do something like that, you could probably do almost anything slop-free." Pair with Gridley's redesign time badges (~half a day → ~15 min).

## 4. Remarkability Premium

"In a world where AI slop is so prevalent, the remarkable — the OMG decisions and content and products — get way more rewarded. And they only come from people willing to make hard choices and having a real clear vision of what they're trying to achieve." (Godin-adjacent; stacks with /godin-remarkability-engine.)

## 5. Constraint-and-Focus (attribution UNCONFIRMED)

Kipp cites "Inside the Box by David Epstein" with a Pixar/Toy Story constraints story. **Attribution likely garbled** (David Epstein wrote *Range*; *Inside the Box* is Boyd/Goldenberg; the Pixar story resembles *Creativity, Inc.*). Use the principle — leaders set constraints and focus; teams go deep on 1-3 highest-impact things, not 100 — but never cite the book/author from this source.
