## Genius Patterns

## Pattern 1: Personal-First Product Development
**Execute**: Build solutions for YOUR problems first. If valuable to you, valuable to others. Relationship OS started as personal tool.

**Success Metric**: Product solves your own real problem before pitching to others.

**Source**: LIKELY — real analog: Deep Personality, the personality/relationship assessment tool he built for himself before any public release ("I've just been loving that and I'm actually releasing that to the public really soon," Every.to podcast transcript, "Opus 4.5 Changed How Andrew Wilkinson Works and Lives," 2026-01-21). Flow, by contrast, broke this exact pattern once he re-aimed it at a broad consumer market instead of the 10-person MetaLab team problem it was built for, and burned $10M+ over 12 years as a result (Morning Brew *Founder's Journal*, "How I Lost $10,000,000," 2024-03-15).

### Anti-Exemplar: The Generic AI Content Writer
**Description**: A startup launches an AI tool that promises to write generic marketing copy, blog posts, and social media updates based on simple prompts. The market is already saturated with similar tools, offering little differentiation beyond slight variations in pricing or UI. The founders, seeing "content creation" as an obvious AI opportunity, built the software without first solving a deeply personal, expensive problem, nor did they establish a unique distribution channel or defensible moat. They quickly found themselves competing purely on price, with output that felt bland and interchangeable.
**What makes this mediocre**: This falls into the trap of "Obvious Opportunities Get Crushed" (Tacit 4). It lacks a "Software Moat" (Pattern 8) and prioritizes software development over "Distribution" (Tacit 5). Critically, it does not stem from a "Personal-First Product Development" (Pattern 1) approach, leading to a product that is neither differentiated nor uniquely valuable.

**Source**: LIKELY — mirrors Flow, Wilkinson's real failure mode: competing on features and price against venture-funded rivals without a moat, which cost him $10M+ burned over 12 years, peaking at $150K/month burn against a $3M peak ARR (Founder's Journal, "How I Lost $10,000,000," 2024-03-15). ## Signature Moves

*   **Self-Problem Validation**: Before investing significant time or resources, explicitly articulates a personal, expensive problem that the proposed AI solution would solve for *themselves*, quantifying its current cost in time, money, or friction. → **Deploy when**: Initiating a new project, evaluating a business idea, or prioritizing feature development.
*   **AI Staffing Audit**: Reviews current operational bottlenecks or repetitive tasks (personal or business) and asks, "If I had to hire an employee for this specific task, how much would it cost?" then maps that task directly to an AI capability, treating AI as a virtual staff member. → **Deploy when**: Optimizing workflows, identifying AI automation opportunities, or resource planning.
*   **Vibe Code Sprint**: Opens a natural language interface (e.g., Claude Code, prompt window) and, instead of outlining technical specifications, describes the desired outcome, functionality, or user experience in plain, conversational language, then rapidly iterates on the AI's output until a functional prototype or solution emerges. → **Deploy when**: Rapid prototyping, exploring new ideas, overcoming technical blockers, or building custom tools.
*   **Moat-First Market Scan**: Before entering a market or making an investment, conducts a specific analysis for "pennies in front of a steamroller" scenarios (Tacit 4), identifying areas where large incumbents will quickly commoditize. Simultaneously, prioritizes ventures with inherent distribution advantages (Tacit 5) or unique, defensible positions. → **Deploy when**: Strategic planning, market entry analysis, or evaluating investment opportunities.
*   **Frictionless Workflow Integration**: When designing or adopting any new tool or system, meticulously maps how it integrates into existing personal and professional operating procedures, specifically identifying and eliminating any points of friction, context switching, or device-specific silos. → **Deploy when**: Designing user experiences, setting up personal productivity systems, or evaluating software purchases.
*   **Prompt Discipline**: Strips his own opinion out of the prompt before handing off analysis work — "you can't inject any of your own opinion into the prompt... You have to analyze every single word" (Every.to transcript, 2026-01-21) — treating a biased prompt as a self-inflicted failure mode, not a model limitation. → **Deploy when**: Delegating judgment-heavy analysis (audits, reviews, evaluations) to AI rather than execution-heavy tasks.

## Anti-Patterns (Sourced)

- **Building for everyone instead of the payer you already understand**: Flow bled over $10 million across 12 years once Wilkinson expanded it from an internal MetaLab coordination tool (solving his own 10-employee problem) into a generic consumer product — "only business teams were willing to pay" (Morning Brew *Founder's Journal*, "How I Lost $10,000,000," 2024-03-15, foundersjournalpod.morningbrew.com).
- **Competing on features against venture-funded rivals without matching their capital**: "If you're competing on features, it never stops and is an ever-increasing line item" and "Don't bring a knife to a gunfight" (Founder's Journal, "How I Lost $10,000,000," 2024-03-15).
- **Letting personal opinion leak into an analysis prompt**: "you can't inject any of your own opinion into the prompt... You have to analyze every single word" — his own account of a failed audit he ran himself (Every.to podcast transcript, "Opus 4.5 Changed How Andrew Wilkinson Works and Lives," 2026-01-21).
- **Treating "no technical moat" as safe because it feels obvious**: "unless you have a distribution moat or a hardware moat or something like that... most software businesses are just thin wrappers," and "Programming is hard to learn... and now it's basically free" (Every.to transcript, 2026-01-21).
- **Underestimating the emotional cost of AI-driven headcount compression on the founder himself**: "I underestimated how emotional the impact of AI would be. For a decade, I was depressed about work" (Andrew Wilkinson, X post, x.com/awilkinson/status/2012559525811814442, 2026).
- **Assuming money or wealth resolves founder burnout/anxiety**: "Money is just 'Europe for your anxiety'" — his own description of moving to Europe, and later reaching billionaire net worth, without the underlying anxiety resolving (Lenny's Newsletter interview, "I've run 75+ businesses...", 2025-07-03, lennysnewsletter.com/p/ive-run-75-businesses-andrew-wilkinson).
