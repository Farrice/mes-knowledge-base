# Evidence-Based Fitness Field Guide

The lanes (Galpin, Israetel, Teo, Aragon) do the programming. This field guide is the *grounding library* — 11 single-conversation experts distilled from the source extractions. Each entry gives the expert's signature contribution and how it complements the lanes, so the hub can cite a real principle when a coaching claim crosses domains. Use it to check a claim, not to replace a lane.

Entries drawn directly from source transcripts are marked **[from source]**; entries synthesized from an expert named/referenced in the source conversations are marked **[referenced]** and reflect their established, well-documented position.

## Jeremy Ethier — Constraint-First Natural Training **[from source]**

**Signature contribution:** *Find the #1 constraint, then spend your limited adaptation there.* Ethier reframes intermediate stagnation ("nothing I'm doing works anymore") as a constraint-hierarchy problem that shifts by training age: beginners need consistency; novices need a progression-tracking system; intermediates need smart fatigue-managed volume distribution; advanced lifters need time + injury avoidance. His concrete unlocks: (1) **strategic volume concentration** — as gains slow to 1–3 lb/yr, stop spreading volume evenly; pour it into the 1–2 muscles that transform the physique most (he built side delts by *lowering* arm volume). (2) **Frequency ladder** — 1×→2×/wk is a ~30% jump; cap ~11 hard sets per muscle per session before splitting across days; fast-recovering muscles (side delts) tolerate 3–4×/wk. (3) **Biomechanical personalization** — never copy a physique-athlete's exercise list; the sternum-angle test dictates *your* optimal press incline; heel-elevated squats vs. barbell change which muscle a "squat" trains.

**Complements the lanes:** Sits directly on top of **Israetel** (volume landmarks → *where* to spend that volume) and **Teo** (his personalization logic is Teo's "one best exercise for *you* per pattern"). His recovery caveats (sleep the night before caps growth signal regardless of workout quality; life stress is training stress) hand off to **Galpin**.

## Eric Helms — Periodized, Evidence-Based Coaching Frameworks **[referenced]**

**Signature contribution:** The *pyramid of priorities* — adherence and energy balance at the base, then macros, then nutrient timing, then supplements, each tier mattering less than the one below it. Helms is the discipline of not majoring in minors: a client obsessing over meal timing while calories are unmeasured is optimizing the top of the pyramid. He also formalized **RPE/RIR autoregulation** and evidence-tiered decision-making (rank the strength of evidence before acting on it) for physique athletes.

**Complements the lanes:** His priority pyramid is the sequencing logic behind **Aragon's** nutrition lane and behind the hub's own "diagnose the constraint before prescribing." His autoregulation feeds **Israetel's** RIR progression and **Galpin's** readiness-based programming.

## Layne Norton — Metabolism, Adherence & Reverse Dieting **[referenced]**

**Signature contribution:** *The best diet is the one you can adhere to*, plus the mechanics of getting off a diet without rebound. Norton popularized **reverse dieting** (gradual calorie increase to restore metabolic rate and minimize fat regain after a cut) and is the loudest evidence-based voice against metabolic-damage mythology: energy balance still governs, but adherence, protein, and psychological sustainability determine whether balance is achievable. He also champions resistance training + high protein for body recomposition.

**Complements the lanes:** Directly powers **Aragon's** body-composition lane — the Primer/Fat-Loss/Reverse/Gaining phase structure in the coaching tracker is Norton/Helms periodized nutrition. His adherence-first stance reinforces the hub's rule that adherence is the client's scarcest resource.

## Brad Schoenfeld — Hypertrophy Mechanisms & Volume Dose-Response **[referenced]**

**Signature contribution:** The academic backbone of "volume drives hypertrophy." Schoenfeld's meta-analyses established the **dose-response relationship between weekly sets and muscle growth** (more sets → more growth, with diminishing returns), that **~10+ sets/muscle/week** beats lower doses, and that mechanical tension is the primary hypertrophy driver (metabolic stress and damage secondary). He also showed both full ROM and long-length partials build muscle, and that a wide load range (~30–85% 1RM) grows muscle when taken near failure.

**Complements the lanes:** Schoenfeld *is* the research floor under **Israetel's** MEV/MAV/MRV landmarks and under **Ethier's** frequency/volume claims. When a lane asserts a set-count, this is the citation.

## Greg Nuckols — Strength Science & Individual Variation **[from source (referenced in tracker panel)]**

**Signature contribution:** *The average hides the individual.* Nuckols (Stronger by Science) is the field's steward of data literacy — reading effect sizes and confidence intervals rather than headlines, and repeatedly showing that response to any protocol is **highly variable between people**. His practical throughline: strength is skill-specific (specificity of the lift you want to improve), frequency is a tool for accumulating quality volume, and n=1 tracking beats population averages for a given lifter.

**Complements the lanes:** His "response is all over the place" is the statistical justification for the hub's *diagnose-per-individual* stance and for the coaching tracker's per-client data collection. Reinforces **Galpin's** limiter diagnosis (test the individual, don't assume) and tempers over-confident set-count prescriptions.

## Chris Beardsley — Mechanotransduction & Stimulating Reps **[referenced]**

**Signature contribution:** *Only the reps near failure actually grow muscle.* Beardsley's mechanistic model centers on **motor-unit recruitment and the "stimulating reps"** — as a set approaches failure, fiber velocity drops, high-threshold motor units engage, and the final ~5 reps before failure are where mechanical tension on individual fibers is high enough to signal growth. This reframes "junk volume": easy reps far from failure are largely non-stimulating, and effort proximity (not just set count) determines the growth stimulus.

**Complements the lanes:** Explains *why* **Teo's** true-failure calibration matters (people chronically stop short of the reps that count) and *why* **Israetel's** RIR progression works. The mechanistic complement to Schoenfeld's dose-response.

## Steve Magness — Aerobic Base & Performance Psychology **[from source]**

**Signature contribution (two-part):** *Physiology* — build the aerobic base before chasing intensity. Most non-elite endurance progress comes from consistent easy volume, not more intervals ("get fit enough to train before you train"); the aerobic reservoir decays slowly (tap it decades later), while speed/power decay fast; "intervals" is a meaningless prescription until you specify the variable (pace, rest, duration) — the same 4×4 done identically forever stops adapting. *Psychology* — shift from a **threat state to a challenge state**; keep space between the goal and the sense of self so a bad session isn't an identity crisis; the **explore-vs-exploit** trap (competence becomes a rut) explains why lifelong progress requires periodic re-exploration; security enables the risk-taking that produces growth.

**Complements the lanes:** Supplies the **conditioning/energy-systems** philosophy that sits beside **Galpin** (base first, vary the stimulus, decay rates). His performance psychology is the recovery/adherence layer under every lane — the mental side of why clients quit or plateau.

## Daniel Lieberman — Evolutionary Biology of Exercise **[from source]**

**Signature contribution:** *We never evolved to "exercise" — we evolved to be physically active when it was necessary or rewarding, and to rest whenever possible.* This dissolves the guilt/willpower framing: the instinct to take it easy is normal, not moral failure. His myth-corrections: the 8-hour-sleep mandate is overstated (natural sleep clusters ~7 hrs, U-shaped mortality curve); **how** you sit matters more than how much (interrupt sitting every ~10–15 min to switch on metabolic machinery); most chronic disease is a **mismatch disease** (evolved body in a modern environment), and ~150 min/wk of activity meaningfully lowers cancer/cardiovascular/dementia risk. Since activity is no longer necessary, coaching must engineer it to be **rewarding and social**.

**Complements the lanes:** Reframes *why* clients don't adhere (biology, not laziness) — the deepest layer under **Magness's** psychology and the hub's adherence-first stance. Justifies "make it social/necessary" as a programming design principle, and grounds strength-training-as-you-age (muscle loss → vicious cycle) that all lanes prescribe against.

## Benjamin Bikman — Insulin Resistance & Metabolic Health **[from source]**

**Signature contribution:** *Insulin resistance is the common root of most chronic disease, and you can steer it.* Two roads: **fast insulin resistance** (stress, inflammation, and too-much-insulin — resolves fast when the cause is removed) and **slow insulin resistance** (chronic, from sustained metabolic load). His four-lever fix, in order: **(1) control carbohydrates** (cut the barcode/bag/box carbs that spike insulin), **(2) prioritize protein**, **(3) don't fear the fat that comes with protein** (satiety + essential nutrients), **(4) fast frequently**. Core mechanism for coaches: to *lose* fat you must *burn* fat, and you can't burn fat while insulin keeps you burning glucose — so lowering insulin is the metabolic prerequisite, and **muscle contraction pulls glucose independent of insulin** (a metabolic-health argument for resistance training).

**Complements the lanes:** The metabolic-health rationale under **Aragon's** nutrition and body-composition work (carb control, protein priority, why recomposition responds to lifting). Note his more contrarian claims (specific reversal rates, extreme fasting, salt/cholesterol positions) exceed the evidence in the source and should be flagged as his position, not consensus — verify before coaching them.

## Menno Henselmans — Data-Driven Physique Coaching **[referenced]**

**Signature contribution:** *Coach from the numbers and the psychology, not from dogma.* Henselmans applies a statistical, Bayesian temperament to physique training — interrogating study quality, warning against over-extrapolating single papers, and translating research into precise, individualized programming (evidence-based volume/frequency/intensity choices, autoregulated to the lifter). He also brings behavioral/psychological rigor to adherence and habit design, treating the client's mind as a variable to program, not ignore.

**Complements the lanes:** The methodological check on all four lanes — "what does the evidence actually support for *this* client?" Reinforces **Nuckols/Beardsley** on effort and individual response, and pairs with **Israetel** on precise, autoregulated hypertrophy programming.

## How the Field Guide Feeds the Hub

- **Adherence & psychology stack (why clients quit):** Lieberman (biology of rest) → Magness (threat vs. challenge, explore/exploit) → Helms/Norton (the sustainable diet is the adherable one).
- **Volume & effort stack (what actually grows muscle):** Schoenfeld (dose-response) → Beardsley (stimulating reps near failure) → Ethier (spend volume on the constraint) → Israetel lane (landmarks) / Teo lane (calibrate true failure).
- **Individualization stack (why templates fail):** Nuckols (response varies) → Henselmans (program the individual) → Ethier (biomechanical personalization) → Galpin lane (diagnose the limiter) / Aragon lane (personal fat threshold).
- **Metabolic stack (fueling the result):** Bikman (insulin as gatekeeper) → Norton (reverse diet / energy balance) → Aragon lane (macros, recomposition).
