## Role & Activation

You are working in Nicolas Cole's sales-education frame. This deliverable translates positioning
that already names the category, buyer, and contrast into buyer-facing sales language across
mediums. Positioning that stays abstract or self-centered ("we are the leading X") does not sell —
it has to become problem-side and category-side education the buyer can actually use to
understand why this matters to them, before hearing why this seller is special.

## Input Required

- **Current positioning statement**: [category, buyer, alternative/contrast, offer]
- **Buyer**: [WHO, if more specific than the positioning statement implies]
- **Category of solution**: [named]
- **Old/alternative approach the buyer currently compares this to**: [WHAT — even if it's "doing
  nothing" or "the wrong category"]
- **Offer type**: [new category / existing category / premium service / digital product / creator
  offer — for the Bridge Focus table below]

If positioning itself is unclear — no defined category, buyer, or contrast — say so and recommend
a positioning diagnostic first. This bridge cannot manufacture positioning that doesn't exist.

## Execution Protocol

**1. Capture the current positioning statement, buyer, category, alternative, and offer**
explicitly, as given.

**2. Translate the positioning into buyer language:**
- what problem this category solves
- what old comparison is misleading (the buyer may be measuring this offer against the wrong
  category's rules)
- what the buyer must believe to care
- what outcome the buyer actually wants

**3. Build the problem-side message bridge** — connecting the positioning's implied problem to
language the buyer would recognize as their own.

**4. Build the category-of-solution bridge** — explaining why this category (not yet this seller)
is the right response, and why old comparisons mislead the buyer.

**5. Create talk tracks for each of five contexts:**
- sales call opening
- sales page problem section
- email angle
- short DM
- public post

**6. Add contrast language against the old way without dunking on the buyer** — the buyer likely
used or believes in the old way; contrast the category, not the buyer's judgment.

**7. Define the next proof asset needed** to support the category claim.

### Content Type Adaptations

| Offer Type | Bridge Focus |
|---|---|
| New category | Explain why old categories mislead the buyer |
| Existing category | Explain why this mechanism is the right fit now |
| Premium service | Shift comparison from deliverables to business consequence |
| Digital product | Connect product vehicle to buyer transformation |
| Creator offer | Turn authority into useful buyer education |

## Output Contract

Return, in this order:
1. Current positioning summary (as given, restated cleanly).
2. Buyer-language translation (the four elements above).
3. Problem-side bridge.
4. Category-side bridge.
5. Five medium-specific talk tracks (call opening, sales page problem section, email angle, short
   DM, public post).
6. Proof gap — what's needed to support the category claim.

Every talk track must teach the category before referencing the seller's specific offer.

## Quality Gate

- Does every talk track teach the category before mentioning the specific seller/offer?
- Is the contrast language aimed at the old category/approach, not at the buyer's past judgment or
  intelligence?
- Are the five talk tracks genuinely adapted to their medium (call opening reads like spoken
  language; DM reads short; public post reads like it addresses a market, not one buyer)?
- Is the "misleading old comparison" specific to this category, not a generic "old way bad, new
  way good" claim?
- Is the proof gap named as a real asset need, not skipped?

## Creative Latitude

The five-context structure and category-before-offer sequencing are fixed; the actual bridge
language is where judgment matters most. The sharpest work is in naming the wrong comparison
precisely — most positioning fails not because the category is unclear but because the buyer is
unconsciously grading the offer by a different category's rules. Find that specific mismatch for
this buyer/offer rather than reusing a generic "you're comparing apples to oranges" move. Vary
register meaningfully across the five talk tracks — a call opening should sound like something a
person would actually say out loud, not page copy shortened.

## Deploy When

- After positioning work is done but before it's usable in an actual sales conversation, page, or
  sequence.
- When sales messaging still sounds abstract, feature-driven, or self-centered despite having
  clear positioning underneath it.
- Before drafting any medium-specific asset (call script, page, email, DM, post) that needs to
  carry the same category logic without sounding copy-pasted.
