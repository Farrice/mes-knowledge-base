# Hidden Knowledge — Nate B Jones Manager Loop

Tacit insights the video carries without stating them as rules. Anchors in `source-quotes.md`.

- **T1 The confidence gap, not the capability gap, is the bottleneck.** "The agent is hungry for a task. And therein lies the gap" (08:52). The card is a confidence device first — it exists so a tired human will actually hand the job over.
- **T2 Nobody writes the spec at the moment of need.** "Nobody wants to sit down before a household move and write an approval matrix and a 14-part spec" (08:34). Cards must be pre-written, matched, and editable, or the job runs as a chat.
- **T3 Flagship models are wasted on task-shaped work.** Cheaper models finish contained tasks (04:09); the flagship earns its keep on entangled jobs. Triage protects budget as much as quality.
- **T4 Five capacities changed, not intelligence.** Breadth, focus duration, tool span, re-planning under change, high-fidelity memory (14:28). A manager loop is the only shape that uses all five at once.
- **T5 Drudgery is an ordering problem.** People postpone jobs because of a "chain of little decisions… hundreds or thousands long" they "can't order" (15:12). Lanes with dependencies ARE the product.
- **T6 Agent supervision is the responsible model at scale** (10:02). A manager checking workers is how trust scales past one human's attention; the human stays at the choice level.
- **T7 The felt test is "me but more"** (26:04). A loop that finishes the job but makes the human feel managed has failed; a loop that makes them feel extended has won.
- **T8 Undefined problems want a different model than defined ones** (16:52). Shape first, then hand to the executor — and keep the handoff cheap (a packet, not a re-explanation).
- **T9 Watch the hand-back.** The old relationship is "the model will come back with an output and I will evaluate it and I'll ask again" (05:42). Any turn that ends with runnable work and no decision owed is that relationship returning.
