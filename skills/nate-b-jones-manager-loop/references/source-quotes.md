## Recipe cards
- [19:39] "I don't think a master prompt is the answer."
- [19:49] "you cannot construct the scale of job we're talking about in one beautiful paragraph and have it have enough instructions to to handle a job that takes a week."
- [20:03] "we need something postprompt. We need something that goes beyond what a prompt does, but isn't so long we can't manage it."
- [20:17] "I'm calling these recipe cards. I like to cook and it just makes sense to me."
- [20:20] "A recipe card names a real job. It shows all of the jobs inside it, at least as a sketch. It tells the agent what to ask you for, what the agent can handle, when the agent ought to come back, and which actions need your approval."
- [20:37] "You can copy it into Astra or to any manager agent. It can answer the questions for your situation and ask you and interview you and make sure it understands the problem and then it can get moving."
- [21:02] "most of us don't really naturally look at tasks and like break them out like that. That's very hard, right?… our brains just kind of like phase over. We're tired. The static comes."
- [21:16] "A recipe card puts those things all together and says, 'This is a kind of work you can give an agent that is pre-broken out and you can adjust it as needed and get going on this massive task.'"
- [22:55] "It shows the manager agent how to divide out the task… how to remove the parts that don't apply, how to run independent work at the same time. It names the information and access the agents will probably need. It says what the agent can do without interrupting me and where it has to stop. It says what should come back and it says what to do when the work like breaks"
- [23:19] "If a login fails, if a document is missing, if sources disagree or a website blocks an agent, the card, the recipe card, whatever you want to call it, tells the agents how to keep going and when to ask for help. Essentially, it's a map to the work."

## The short card (verbatim, 21:34–22:24)
"I need to move my household to X city by Y date. I don't want to lose a week of my life to forms or two weeks. Please start this task by showing me which parts of the move you can handle completely. Ask me about who's moving, about the budget, about the kind of home that I'm going to move to, about schools, doctors, vehicles, pets, utilities, anything else that might change that job. And then I want you agent to manage the work. You handle the research, the comparison, the appointments, the form prep, and you use other agents when that helps you get the job done. Your job is to keep working on parts that aren't blocked. Your job is to bring me the choices or approvals that still need me. Do not make me manage the step by step. You are the central point of contact for this task."
