# Revenue Mechanics Floor

This reference raises the default output floor for the Suzuki OS. Use it whenever the system builds or repairs a post, funnel, launch sprint, or DM path.

## Preservation Lock

Keep:
- Source grounding from the two captured Suzuki videos.
- No guaranteed-results language.
- Buyer-quality filtering over broad attention.
- Comment-to-DM or reply-to-DM funnel design.
- Proof gaps and claim uncertainty.

Change:
- The free asset must move a buyer toward a decision, not only make them more cautious.
- The paid step must feel like the next tool in a plan, not a random link after education.
- The DM path must separate curiosity from purchase intent.

Do not disturb:
- Existing command names.
- Skill identity.
- Source package separation.
- Creator-claim labeling.

Gate:
- No workflow passes unless it produces a sellable path and a post-purchase or first-use plan.

## Buyer Readiness Diagnosis

Every run must classify the buyer into one of four lanes:

| Lane | Meaning | Best Next Step |
|---|---|---|
| Cold | Interested in the topic, not yet considering purchase | Send decision asset, ask one context question, do not push link |
| Warm | Has the problem and wants a plan | Send decision asset plus first-use path, invite reply with score/context |
| Ready | Already considering the product or asking for link/details | Run fit check, disclose incentives, then offer direct link or application |
| Wrong fit | Wants shortcut, guaranteed result, or urgent rescue | Give a safer alternative and do not sell hard |

The lanes can use any labels in public, but the output must route these behaviors differently.

## Purchase-Adjacent Free Asset

A free asset passes only if it does at least three of these:

- Names the buying situation.
- Helps the buyer diagnose fit.
- Shows a sample of the paid method.
- Gives the first 7 days, first use, or first implementation path.
- Answers one objection that would otherwise block purchase.
- Creates a reason to ask for the link, checkout, application, or next conversation.

Examples:
- Readiness score plus first 7-day plan.
- Sample page plus one worked example.
- Decision map plus buyer lane.
- Mini teardown plus checklist.
- Proof bundle: before/after, FAQ, and first-use path.

Fails:
- Question list only.
- Generic checklist unrelated to the paid product.
- Free education that fully satisfies curiosity but gives no next step.
- Link CTA with no proof, fit, or first-use path.

## Direct Or Conversation Link Lane

Each output needs two lanes by default:

| Lane | CTA Style | DM Job |
|---|---|---|
| Value lane | `Comment FIT/MAP/SAMPLE/PLAN` | Deliver the asset and ask for score, context, or biggest blocker |
| Purchase lane | `Comment LINK/READY/APPLY` | Disclose incentive, run quick fit check, then send link or application by permission |

For low-ticket products, the purchase lane can point to checkout after the fit check. For high-ticket products, it should point to application, diagnostic questions, call, or a trust container.

## First-Use Path

Every paid-product output must include what the buyer does after purchase:

- Day 1 orientation or setup.
- Day 2 proof/sample/action.
- Day 3 first public action or private implementation.
- Day 4 objection or support question.
- Day 5 DM/email/sales asset.
- Day 6 proof collection or iteration.
- Day 7 decision, recap, or next step.

For smaller products, compress this into "first 30 minutes", "first 24 hours", or "first 3 uses."

## Proof Ladder

Use the strongest available proof without making unsupported claims:

1. Product proof: pages, modules, templates, examples, demos, sample outputs.
2. Process proof: exact steps, time to complete, before/after, checklist.
3. User proof: beta feedback, testimonials, screenshots, comments, sales logs if verified.
4. Authority proof: expertise, lived experience, prior results, partner details.
5. Risk proof: refund rule, fit note, who should not buy, support boundary.

If proof is missing, the output must name what to collect before publishing or selling harder.

## Suzuki Output Rubric

Minimum pass criteria:

| Criterion | Pass Standard |
|---|---|
| Buyer self-recognition | The right buyer can say "that is me" in the first 2 lines or first frame. |
| Decision motion | The free asset helps the buyer pick a lane or take the first step. |
| Paid step fit | The paid offer feels like the next tool in the plan. |
| CTA separation | Curiosity and purchase intent have different keywords, replies, or paths. |
| Intent routing | Cold, warm, ready, and wrong-fit buyers receive different replies or DMs. |
| Price path | Checkout, application, call, or trust container matches price and risk. |
| Proof clarity | Proof is concrete, sourced, or explicitly missing. |
| First-use plan | Buyer sees what happens after purchase. |
| Replay plan | Launch includes reusable templates or daily prompts, not only one post. |

Fail and rerun Launch Debugger if:
- The asset only asks more questions.
- The buyer is left with insight but no next move.
- CTA is only "comment for the guide" with no buyer routing.
- There is no path for someone already ready to buy.
- The answer resembles a neutral report more than a guided selling system.

## Regression Fixtures

These fixtures test whether the OS transfers across offer types. They are not source roots and must not override the Suzuki transcript mechanics.

Use these prompts to test the floor:

1. `$200 ebook`: "Sell a $200 ebook for independent consultants that turns market signals into posts, emails, and sales call prompts."
2. Affiliate course like DWA: "Sell an affiliate digital marketing course with no income claims, faceless X/Threads/Instagram paths, and clear disclosure."
3. High-ticket coaching/community: "Sell a $2,000 coaching/community offer for operators who need help building a digital product revenue system."

Each fixture must produce:
- Post or thread.
- Purchase-adjacent free asset.
- Value lane and purchase-intent routing.
- DM scripts.
- Checkout, application, or conversation path.
- 7-day launch/replay sprint.
- Proof/risk ledger.
