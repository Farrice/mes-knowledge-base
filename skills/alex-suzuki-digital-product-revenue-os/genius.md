# Genius Patterns

## Pattern 1 - One Post Is A Funnel Event
The post is not judged alone. It is judged by whether it sends qualified people into a next step: comment, DM, free asset, community, email, checkout, or application.

## Pattern 2 - Specificity Carries Trust
The strongest hooks use numbers, time frames, buyer type, and outcome. When proof is thin, the system switches to owned facts: page count, lesson count, sample, workflow count, test result, turnaround time, or buyer problem.

## Pattern 3 - Buyer Quality Beats Attention
High impressions can be worthless when the audience is wrong. The system scores whether the post attracts likely buyers, peers, spectators, or curiosity-only readers.

## Pattern 4 - CTA Means Micro-Commitment
The comment keyword is a buyer filter. It turns passive interest into a public action that starts the follow-up path.

## Pattern 5 - Free Asset Is A Proof Bridge
The freebie should preview the paid result and remove one objection. It is not a random lead magnet.

## Pattern 6 - Direct Checkout Depends On Price And Trust
Low-ticket digital products can move to checkout quickly. Higher-ticket products need proof, community, application, conversation, or a call.

## Pattern 7 - Early Engagement Is Operational
The first minutes after posting are a work block. Reply to comments, prompt DMs, answer objections, and keep the post alive with useful responses.

## Pattern 8 - Repeat The Format, Change The Proof
Winning post shapes are reused with new proof, buyer situation, and free asset. The system does not chase novelty when a format is already converting.

## Pattern 9 - Platform Transfer Requires Native Behavior
The X mechanism transfers as structure, not wording. Instagram may need carousel or Story DM triggers. LinkedIn may need a credibility-first narrative. Email may need reply-based intent. Substack may need an essay plus offer box.

## Pattern 10 - Claim Safety Protects Trust
Revenue figures from the source are treated as creator claims. Client/user outputs should use verified internal proof or label assumptions.

## Pattern 11 - Decision Asset Beats Question Asset
A free asset should not leave the buyer parked in analysis. It should help them choose a lane, see the first-use path, and understand why the paid product is or is not the next step.

## Pattern 12 - Two-Lane CTA Separates Curiosity From Purchase Intent
Use a value keyword for people who want the free asset and a purchase keyword for people already considering the paid step. Both lanes need disclosure, fit checks, and useful replies, but they should not receive the same DM.

## Pattern 13 - First-Use Path Creates Purchase Motion
The buyer should know what happens after purchase: first 30 minutes, first 24 hours, first 7 days, or first implementation sequence. This turns the paid product from "more content" into the next tool in a plan.

## Pattern 14 - Replay The Winner
One strong post is not the whole launch. The system repeats the winning frame through proof posts, objection posts, first-use posts, platform adaptations, and final reminders.

## Pattern 15 - Borrowed Proof Beats No Proof
You do not need your own results. Partner with a proven operator who has authority but no time; you run the content and split the revenue. Half his five-figure students reportedly start with zero experience (creator claim). The proof must be the partner's real proof, disclosed.

## Pattern 16 - Proven Product Removes Guesswork
Selling an already-validated product (affiliate) skips creation and demand-testing. Pick what already sells; add distribution. Disclose the affiliate relationship.

## Pattern 17 - The Account Is The Scaling Unit
Scale by adding accounts (niche × proof source), not only posts. Dozens of accounts across niches; each is its own funnel.

## Pattern 18 - Manufacture First Distribution
The cold-start blocker is views, not content. A retweet/boost network gives a new account instant reach so the first post can go viral. Treat first-distribution as a required input, not luck.

## Pattern 19 - Separate Create, Schedule, Convert
Automation splits into three jobs: batch-generate posts, schedule them, and auto-DM the opt-in free asset/link. Volume + autopilot + opt-in conversion — never cold mass-DM.

## Pattern 20 - Qualified Comments, Not Views
Each like/comment is a lead and a permissioned DM. Optimize qualified comments × price, not impressions. Low-view posts can out-earn viral reach.

## Pattern 21 - Free To Application For High-Ticket
$3K-$10K offers route through free value → application → call → close, not self-serve checkout. The free layer warms, the application qualifies, the founder closes.

## Pattern 22 - Aligned Incentives Self-Select
A profit-split (you win when they win) attracts committed buyers, raises success rate, and justifies selectivity and a conditional guarantee. The offer structure is itself a filter.

## Pattern 23 - The Mirrorboard Sells High-Ticket
A 6-section visual board — story, proof, how, tools, results, offer — is a reusable VSL-as-board for any high-ticket offer.

## Pattern 24 - Simplicity Enables Scale
Posts are deliberately simple (a 15-second header + body + CTA) so they can be generated and scheduled by the hundred. Simplicity is an operating choice, not a lack of craft.

## Signature Output Shape
Every strong output follows this stack:

1. Buyer and product in one line.
2. Specific result hook.
3. Proof or case detail.
4. Concrete deliverables in bullets.
5. Purchase-adjacent free asset with comment or reply trigger.
6. Value lane and purchase lane.
7. Intent-routed DM path.
8. First-use or first-7-day plan.
9. Funnel path after the comment.
10. Proof gaps and next action.

## Cold-Start Layer (no proof, no product)
When the operator has nothing yet, the system still runs:
1. Pick a path — Partnership (borrowed proof, 30-50% split) or Affiliate (proven product).
2. Manufacture first distribution (retweet/boost network).
3. Generate simple posts at volume; schedule; auto-DM on opt-in.
4. Route to the partner's or affiliate's checkout/application.
See `references/scaling-mechanics.md`. Orchestrated by `/suzuki`.

## Scaling Layer
The account is the unit. Add accounts (niche × proof source), each with its own automated funnel. Separate create / schedule / convert. Optimize qualified comments × price, not views. See `/suzuki-scale`, `/suzuki-automate`.

## High-Ticket Offer Layer
Free community + courses → application → founder call → close. Structure the offer with an aligned profit-split, selectivity, and a conditional guarantee. Sell it with a 6-section mirrorboard. See `references/scaling-mechanics.md`.

## Compliance Layer (non-negotiable)
Every revenue figure labeled a creator claim; borrowed proof real and disclosed; affiliate disclosed; auto-DM opt-in only; multi-account distinct; real value delivered. See `references/compliance-gate.md`.
