# Genius Patterns

## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These 24 patterns and the 9 signature moves (`references/voice-and-texture.md`) are intuition primitives, not a checklist to march through. Internalize them, then build the post live — a draft that visibly ticks boxes ("Move 1: Transformation Headline ✓, Move 2: Enemy Line ✓…") has already failed before it ships. The test: would Alex Suzuki recognize this as *one of his own $30K–$100K/post case studies* — or as someone using his vocabulary (mechanism, freebie, CTA) without his grip? If it's the second, rebuild against `references/real-post-teardowns.md`, not this file.

Specifically:
- Do NOT label the finished post "Headline (transformation)," "Enemy line," "Freebie stack" on the page. Execute the moves; never narrate them. His real tweets (`references/real-post-teardowns.md`) never announce their own architecture — the reader only feels the pull toward the comment.
- Do NOT default to a "free guide" or a topic-headline. Both are named failure conditions in the specificity gate (`references/voice-and-texture.md`) — regenerate instead of shipping a draft that trips it.
- His texture is blunt and number-drenched, not polished marketing copy — "69% of the operation was DRAG," not "a significant portion of the work was inefficient." A smoothed-out draft that trades numbers for adjectives is generic, not Suzuki. **Polish that removes numbers is the tell-class failure here.**
- Where the source is thin (a workflow with no direct citation, an income-adjacent niche under compliance restriction), do not invent a number to fill the gap. Mark it UNCONFIRMED per `references/source-ledger.md` and substitute an owned fact (page count, sample, turnaround time, build count) instead of a guessed one.

## Pattern 1 - One Post Is A Funnel Event
The post is not judged alone. It is judged by whether it sends qualified people into a next step: comment, DM, free asset, community, email, checkout, or application. Suzuki walks the full post → freebie → sale chain on-camera (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` 00:03:25-00:05:00).

## Pattern 2 - Specificity Carries Trust
The strongest hooks use numbers, time frames, buyer type, and outcome. When proof is thin, the system switches to owned facts: page count, lesson count, sample, workflow count, test result, turnaround time, or buyer problem. Source: the specific-number hook architecture Suzuki breaks down line-by-line (`PGeAozxJyho` 00:05:19-00:06:05).

## Pattern 3 - Buyer Quality Beats Attention
High impressions can be worthless when the audience is wrong. The system scores whether the post attracts likely buyers, peers, spectators, or curiosity-only readers. Suzuki names this directly: a low-quality audience "don't know how to monetize their audience at all... don't know how to sell" (`PGeAozxJyho` 00:22:32-00:24:48).

## Pattern 4 - CTA Means Micro-Commitment
The comment keyword is a buyer filter. It turns passive interest into a public action that starts the follow-up path. Source: the comment-keyword mechanic (`PGeAozxJyho` 00:06:53-00:12:00).

## Pattern 5 - Free Asset Is A Proof Bridge
The freebie should preview the paid result and remove one objection. It is not a random lead magnet. Source: the multi-freebie CTA breakdown (`PGeAozxJyho` 00:06:53-00:08:16).

## Pattern 6 - Direct Checkout Depends On Price And Trust
Low-ticket digital products can move to checkout quickly. Higher-ticket products need proof, community, application, conversation, or a call. Source: low-ticket-checkout vs. high-ticket-conversation contrast (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` 00:03:25-00:05:00 and 00:51:36-00:56:18).

## Pattern 7 - Early Engagement Is Operational
The first minutes after posting are a work block. Reply to comments, prompt DMs, answer objections, and keep the post alive with useful responses. Source: the 10-minute reply-sprint rule (`PGeAozxJyho` 00:10:18-00:10:41).

## Pattern 8 - Repeat The Format, Change The Proof
Winning post shapes are reused with new proof, buyer situation, and free asset. The system does not chase novelty when a format is already converting. Source: repeat-the-winning-template guidance (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` 00:14:04-00:16:05).

## Pattern 9 - Platform Transfer Requires Native Behavior
The X mechanism transfers as structure, not wording. Instagram may need carousel or Story DM triggers. LinkedIn may need a credibility-first narrative. Email may need reply-based intent. Substack may need an essay plus offer box. Source: cross-niche/cross-platform transfer walkthrough (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` 00:58:29-01:09:37).

## Pattern 10 - Claim Safety Protects Trust
Revenue figures from the source are treated as creator claims. Client/user outputs should use verified internal proof or label assumptions. Suzuki himself draws this line on-camera: "I don't encourage any of you guys to lie or make up any numbers" (`PGeAozxJyho` ~00:21:35-00:21:40).

## Pattern 11 - Decision Asset Beats Question Asset
A free asset should not leave the buyer parked in analysis. It should help them choose a lane, see the first-use path, and understand why the paid product is or is not the next step. Source: the trust-container/free-material walkthrough (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` 00:24:47-00:26:09).

## Pattern 12 - Two-Lane CTA Separates Curiosity From Purchase Intent
Use a value keyword for people who want the free asset and a purchase keyword for people already considering the paid step. Both lanes need disclosure, fit checks, and useful replies, but they should not receive the same DM. Source: the same comment/CTA segment as Pattern 4 (`PGeAozxJyho` 00:06:53-00:08:16).

## Pattern 13 - First-Use Path Creates Purchase Motion
The buyer should know what happens after purchase: first 30 minutes, first 24 hours, first 7 days, or first implementation sequence. This turns the paid product from "more content" into the next tool in a plan.

## Pattern 14 - Replay The Winner
One strong post is not the whole launch. The system repeats the winning frame through proof posts, objection posts, first-use posts, platform adaptations, and final reminders. Same source segment as Pattern 8 (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` 00:14:04-00:16:05).

## Pattern 15 - Borrowed Proof Beats No Proof
You do not need your own results. Partner with a proven operator who has authority but no time; you run the content and split the revenue (30-50%, per `references/scaling-mechanics.md`). Half his five-figure students reportedly start with zero experience (creator claim). The proof must be the partner's real proof, disclosed — Suzuki is explicit that the reason to partner at all is "you don't want to post fake stuff... you don't want to lie" (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` 00:47:49-00:47:57). Source: the partnership/borrowed-proof segment (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:16:06).

## Pattern 16 - Proven Product Removes Guesswork
Selling an already-validated product (affiliate) skips creation and demand-testing. Pick what already sells; add distribution. Disclose the affiliate relationship. Source: the affiliate model and the $700, 270-page ebook example (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:11:56 and ~00:19:12).

## Pattern 17 - The Account Is The Scaling Unit
Scale by adding accounts (niche × proof source), not only posts. Dozens of accounts across niches; each is its own funnel. Source: the "dozens of accounts" claim (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:03:42).

## Pattern 18 - Manufacture First Distribution
The cold-start blocker is views, not content. A retweet/boost network gives a new account instant reach so the first post can go viral. Treat first-distribution as a required input, not luck. Source: the retweet-boost-network walkthrough (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:14:57).

## Pattern 19 - Separate Create, Schedule, Convert
Automation splits into three jobs: batch-generate posts, schedule them, and auto-DM the opt-in free asset/link. Volume + autopilot + opt-in conversion — never cold mass-DM. Source: the Tweet Hunter automation demo, named on-screen (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:19:59).

## Pattern 20 - Qualified Comments, Not Views
Each like/comment is a lead and a permissioned DM. Optimize qualified comments × price, not impressions. Low-view posts can out-earn viral reach. Source: "comment = a lead" (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:04:54).

## Pattern 21 - Free To Application For High-Ticket
$3K-$10K offers route through free value → application → call → close, not self-serve checkout. The free layer warms, the application qualifies, the founder closes. Source: the Parker application-funnel math (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:08:10).

## Pattern 22 - Aligned Incentives Self-Select
A profit-split (you win when they win) attracts committed buyers, raises success rate, and justifies selectivity and a conditional guarantee. The offer structure is itself a filter. Source: the profit-split offer-model segment (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:25:25); the 90-day guarantee condition (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:36:25).

## Pattern 23 - The Mirrorboard Sells High-Ticket
A 6-section visual board — story, proof, how, tools, results, offer — is a reusable VSL-as-board for any high-ticket offer. Source: the mirrorboard walkthrough that opens the video (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:00:02).

## Pattern 24 - Simplicity Enables Scale
Posts are deliberately simple (a 15-second header + body + CTA) so they can be generated and scheduled by the hundred. Simplicity is an operating choice, not a lack of craft.

## Signature Output Shape
Every strong output follows this stack:

1. Buyer and product in one line.
2. Specific result hook.
3. Proof or case detail.
4. Concrete deliverables in bullets.
5. Purchase-adjacent free asset with comment or reply trigger.
6. Value lane and purchase lane.
7. Intent-routed DM path.
8. First-use or first-7-day plan.
9. Funnel path after the comment.
10. Proof gaps and next action.

This 10-step shape is the same skeleton visible in the exemplar teardown — headline, proof, bulleted before/strip/after, 5-part freebie, mechanical CTA (`references/real-post-teardowns.md`, the "Michael Davidson" / `@mike_research` post, `PGeAozxJyho` 00:05:19-00:08:16).

## Cold-Start Layer (no proof, no product)
When the operator has nothing yet, the system still runs:
1. Pick a path — Partnership (borrowed proof, 30-50% split) or Affiliate (proven product).
2. Manufacture first distribution (retweet/boost network).
3. Generate simple posts at volume; schedule; auto-DM on opt-in.
4. Route to the partner's or affiliate's checkout/application.
See `references/scaling-mechanics.md`. Orchestrated by `/suzuki`.

## Scaling Layer
The account is the unit. Add accounts (niche × proof source), each with its own automated funnel. Separate create / schedule / convert. Optimize qualified comments × price, not views. Source: "dozens of accounts" and the Tweet Hunter create/schedule/auto-DM split (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:03:42, ~00:19:59). See `/suzuki-scale`, `/suzuki-automate`.

## High-Ticket Offer Layer
Free community + courses → application → founder call → close. Structure the offer with an aligned profit-split, selectivity, and a conditional 90-day guarantee. Sell it with a 6-section mirrorboard. Source: the offer-design segment (`YaonYaeQlA4` ~00:34:42-00:36:25). See `references/scaling-mechanics.md`.

## Compliance Layer (non-negotiable)
Every revenue figure labeled a creator claim; borrowed proof real and disclosed; affiliate disclosed; auto-DM opt-in only; multi-account distinct; real value delivered. Grounded directly in Suzuki's own stated line: "you don't want to post fake stuff... you don't want to create these fake stuff" (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` 00:47:49-00:48:17). See `references/compliance-gate.md`.

## Voice & Texture Layer (watch-grounded — the embodiment gate)
Captured by watching the actual $30K–$100K/post tweets (`references/real-post-teardowns.md`, `references/voice-and-texture.md`). Mechanics without this layer produce generic, unusable output. Every strong post uses 5+ of the 9 moves:
1. **Transformation headline** — line 1 is a before→after with two hard numbers, not a topic.
2. **Enemy line** — name the lazy default everyone else does.
3. **Invented proprietary mechanism** — coin a named system/jargon you own ("Drag Map"). *Most-missed move.*
4. **Before → strip → After**, bulleted specifics.
5. **Payload freebie** — a numbered stack of 3–5 named deliverables, each with a number/scale/duration/sample.
6. **Extreme specificity** — every claim carries a number; "a lot/quickly/results" is banned.
7. **Mechanical CTA** — `Comment "KEYWORD" (must follow + RT so I can DM)`.
8. **Quality-over-reach** — written for a small high-fit buyer; the copy itself filters.
9. **10-min reply sprint + retweet boost** — the ops that make it land.

**Specificity gate:** fail any draft with a topic/question headline, no invented mechanism, a vague "free guide" instead of a spec'd stack, or "a lot/quickly/results" where a number belongs. **Compliant-grip rule:** for affiliate/make-money niches, keep every move but move specificity off *money* and onto *skills/outputs/time* — never strip the grip with the claims.

## Anti-Patterns (source-grounded)
- **Never invent or round up a revenue/results number to fill a proof gap** — Suzuki states this as a direct rule on-camera: "I don't encourage any of you guys to lie or make up any numbers" (`PGeAozxJyho` transcript, ~00:21:35-00:21:40). Mark it UNCONFIRMED instead.
- **Never post borrowed or partner proof without disclosure** — "you don't want to post fake stuff... you don't want to lie... you want to create social proof that's real" (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` transcript, 00:47:49-00:48:15). A partnership post that hides the split is a compliance failure, not a growth hack.
- **Never fabricate social proof to look more credible while partnering** — "You don't want to create these fake stuff. That's why you want to partner with these people [for real proof]" (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` transcript, 00:48:15-00:48:21).
- **Never optimize for broad/low-quality reach over buyer fit** — a wide, wrong-fit audience "don't know how to monetize their audience at all... don't know how to sell" despite volume (`PGeAozxJyho` transcript, 00:23:15-00:23:23; see Pattern 3).
- **Never fire auto-DM on a non-opt-in trigger (cold mass-DM)** — the automation stack (Tweet Hunter, `YaonYaeQlA4` transcript ~00:19:59) is create → schedule → **opt-in** auto-DM; a comment/like is the permission event, not a scraped list. See `references/compliance-gate.md` and Pattern 19.
- **Never chase volume before the format is proven** — Suzuki names his own early over-posting as the model to skip, not repeat: "I made probably like hundreds of posts, more than like 900 posts before I had my first viral post on Twitter... I don't want you guys to create that many posts" (`SpiK7Ux1e8w` transcript, 00:38:06-00:38:17). The system now runs on a proven repeatable format (Pattern 8), not raw volume.
