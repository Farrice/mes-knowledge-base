## Topics over hooks / trust recession [V1]

- "By far the most important thing that you can do in your content is understand topics."
- "In an age where hooks are so strong, or so clickbaity, people don't want to be hooked
  anymore... The bar of trust is so low. People do not trust you and they do not want to be
  hooked... ironically, the best hooks are actually non-hooks. They are just good ideas."
- "Every person has three to five things that they are thinking about more often on a daily
  basis than the other 2,500 things... It's our job as content creators to find those three to
  five things... It's the words that whenever we say it to our people in our niche, they kind of
  just perk up a little bit."

## Proper TAM [V1]

- "I'm not saying that you should go for the highest TAM. I'm saying you should go for the
  proper TAM based off of your specific goals."
- "I've made more money off the 'how to build a media team' video [10K views] than I did the
  'how to unlock creativity' video [~250K views]. And that was on purpose."

## Five inputs / ideation hour [V1]

- "I encourage you to carve out a sacred hour a week."
- "You have to go click on their profile, identify their baseline average views, and see if this
  video was an outlier to them. Just because a video gets 100,000 views does not mean it's an
  outlier. If the person's average views is 200,000, this is actually a flop... if their average
  views was 20,000, then boom, this is a 5x outlier, you can go ahead and rip it."
- "People are anonymous on Reddit, which means they say the thing that is actually on their mind."
- "You take one of the biggest subreddit conversations, you just copy and paste the whole thing.
  You toss it into Claude... and say, 'Hey, what are people talking about here?' And then boom,
  you have both sides. Go make a video about it."
- "[YouTube thumbnails] are a concentrated point of data... If a video is an outlier, you don't
  have to wonder, was it the storytelling? ... It was probably the packaging."
- "You could literally go into your last 100 emails, find the headline that has the highest
  click-through rate, turn that into a hook, and make a video about it."
- "No one's actually going to carve out an hour a week... You're going to be the person who
  actually separates yourself from the rest of the pack."

## Signal doctrine / arsenal loop [V2]

- "Marketing is not a game of guessing, it's a game of knowing." (attributes to David Ogilvy;
  echoes Caleb Ralston: "Content is not a game of guessing, it is a game of knowing.")
- "You got to have at least some signal... We're not starting from zero and just hoping. We're
  starting at one."
- "They lean onto metrics, not data... retention curves, watch time ratios, hook rates... that
  stuff's whenever you are truly crushing content, you're averaging 100,000 views."
- "Content is a game of putting out tests, keeping the winners, discarding the bad. I usually
  give it three to six attempts."
- "We do not have a singular key event like Madison Square Garden... we play Madison Square
  Garden every single night."
- "The life cycle of content online is about 4 to 6 weeks."
- "Going into week four, we want to let off the gas on the proven winners... we might only do
  four of our six proven winners."
- "We end an 8-week cycle not just with seven proven winners, but we have 12 proven winners
  inside of our arsenal."
- "I see too many creators over index on winners and they just spiral their fatigue into the
  ground."
- "You should never, ever, ever be doing a week of only winners... You should always have maybe
  at least a 70/30 split."

## Win audit [V2]

- "I have a four question check I ask myself whenever I'm auditing content... the first one is
  topic... the second one is the format... the third one is the actual hook... and the fourth
  one is the actual body of the video."
- "You're just isolating variables, isolating variables from the equation."
- "You're looking for an arsenal of topics, an arsenal of formats, an arsenal of hook
  principles, and an arsenal of body principles."
- "Anytime I talk about Liquid Death, it does really viral. Anytime I talk about the book
  Cashvertising, I get a lot of comments... parts of a body of a video that I can weave in, you
  know, 30 seconds in the video and I know I'm going to get a couple comments."

## Follower quality economics [V3 — enrichment]

- "It's the thing that makes the creator with 500,000 followers but it's getting like a couple
  hundred views."
- CCN: "Inside of your follower audience, you have your core, your casual, and your new
  audience." (credits Paddy Galloway)
- "Would you rather have 1 million middle schoolers from Zambia following you, or 1,000 computer
  engineers from San Francisco?"
- Follower-to-dollar receipts: business influencer 400K followers/$25K mo = $0.06/follower;
  marketing creator 13K/$45K = $3.46; fitness influencer 1.7M/$125K = $0.07; sales creator 700
  followers, $2.2M launch day, ~$1M/mo average.
- Paid ads: "It's like paying someone for their love... your video is only good enough for 3,000
  views and you're forcing the next 97,000."
- "In the first 3 hours, almost all of my views are [followers], 67%... If my followers hate my
  content, it's not going to send it to the rest of the non-followers."
- Fatigue: "They don't unfollow you, instead they just scroll every time they see you... you
  basically get kind of shadow banned by them."
- Through-line: "There has to be psychological cohesion from the top of the funnel... to the
  bottom of the funnel. You got to have cohesion."
