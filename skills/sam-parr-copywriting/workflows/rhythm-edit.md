# /parr-rhythm-edit — The Rhythm & Cut-a-Third Pass

Take existing copy and make it sing, then make it lean. Sam Parr: "I love rhythm when it comes to writing — that's my favorite thing." And: write the crap, then cut a third (Ogilvy: "I'm a shitty writer. I'm a great editor."). A diagnostic/edit tool, not a from-scratch generator.

## Pre-Flight Gate
Load `genius.md` (Patterns 9, 10). Confirm:
- Paste the copy to edit.
- Preserve voice? (For Parallax/personal voice, keep interiority — don't flatten to ad cadence.)

## Skill Acquisition
- `genius.md` — Rhythm = music (9), Cut a Third / Kill Your Darlings (10).
- `references/swipe-file-and-frameworks.md` — reading-level + sentence-length references (Buffett 17 words; hemingwayapp.com 7th grade).

## Execution
1. **Read it aloud (simulate cadence).** Mark where it drones — runs of same-length sentences, comma-stacked clauses, hedges.
2. **Apply the music rule.** Re-pace to short → medium → long → short. Break compound sentences: one point per sentence. Convert commas to periods where the sentence carries two ideas.
3. **Transition rhythm.** Start some sentences with "and"/"but" for flow. Don't overdo it — vary openings.
4. **Reading level.** Target 7th grade. Replace jargon and Latinate words with plain ones. (Simulate a hemingwayapp pass; flag any sentence that reads above 8th grade.)
5. **Cut a third.** Delete 33% of the words: redundant clauses, throat-clearing, alternate phrasings of the same point, adverbs. If a darling is beautiful but doesn't move the reader, kill it.
6. **Second cut (optional).** For ad/short-form, cut a third again. Constraint forces clarity.
7. **Show the diff.** Original word count → final; what was cut and why (1-2 lines).

## Content Type Adaptations
| Input | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Ad/sales copy | Aggressive cut (a third, maybe twice); staccato cadence |
| Short-form script | Edit for the EAR; cut to fit the beat clock |
| Newsletter (Parallax) | Preserve interiority + memoir rhythm; cut bloat, not breath. Lighter touch. |
| Email | Tighten to one idea; the cut should sharpen the single CTA |
| B2B/LinkedIn | 7th-grade still applies; defer formatting to Lara if platform-specific |

## Output Requirements
- The edited copy.
- Word count before → after (target ≥30% reduction unless voice-preservation mode).
- A 1-2 line "what I cut and why."
- Reading-level note (target 7th grade).

## Quality Gate (from genius.md)
- **Rhythm** ≥8: short-medium-long-short cadence; reads like music aloud.
- **Editing discipline** ≥8: cut a third+; every word earns its place.
- Auto-fail: uniform sentence length, reading level above 8th grade, no meaningful cut.
