## Phrase Frameworks (rhetorical devices that carry memorability)

| Framework | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Anaphora** (repeat the opening) | "We'll fight on the X, we'll fight on the Y, we'll fight on the Z." | Churchill, Dunkirk speech |
| **The single-fear isolate** | "The only thing you have to fear is X itself." | FDR |
| **Curiosity + payoff** | "[Surprising claim]… and here's why." | "They laughed when I sat down…" |
| **Twist the cliché** | Take a known phrase, change one word to own it. | "You may get lost, but not in the crowd." |
| **The reversal** | "[Common belief]. Except it's the opposite." | "Long copy doesn't sell. Boring copy doesn't sell." |
| **Self-deprecating identity** | "[Flattering thing] about me isn't true — [the real reason]." | "My friends think I'm smart. I'm not." |
| **The visceral comparison** | "[Abstract claim] = [disgusting/vivid physical object]." | "Storing food in the toilet bowl." |
| **Time-constraint snap** | "I only have a minute, but [the thing]." | Pickup-artist borrow — earns the next beat |

## Reading-Level & Rhythm References
- **hemingwayapp.com** — paste copy, target **7th grade** (NYT=7th, USA Today=4th).
- **Sentence length**: Warren Buffett's annual letters average **17 words/sentence** (down from 25 in his youth). One point per sentence; period over comma.
- **Cadence**: short → medium → long → short, like music. Start sentences with "and"/"but" for flow.

## Short-Form Retention Benchmarks (from the onion-video teardown)
- **3-second stop rate**: ≥50% at the 3s mark = the hook worked; 80% = exceptional.
- **Skip rate** (pre-roll/feed): above 50% at 3s → re-cut the hook.
- **Drop-off mapping**: find where the retention graph dips, insert a new curiosity gap there (~every 8s).
- **Avg watch time**: 35s on a 45-50s video = elite.
- **Frame-breaking premise**: the surprising truth that defies category expectations (a $10M business that looks like one guy + two phones + a calculator).
