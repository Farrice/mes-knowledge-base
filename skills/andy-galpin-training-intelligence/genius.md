## Genius Patterns

### Pattern: Limiter-First Diagnosis (never treat the assumed bottleneck)
**Execute**: Before prescribing anything, run the plateau/failure interrogation. (1) PATTERN — when does performance break down? (start / middle / end / recovery). (2) SYMPTOM — what does failure *feel* like? (breathing gasping vs. burning muscles vs. mental vs. empty/bonk). (3) CONTEXT — what else is happening? (sleep, stress, nutrition, life load). Map the symptom to a limiter type: burning muscles + gasping = **waste accumulation (acid)**; empty/bonking feeling = **fuel depletion**; can't hold pace at 8-15 min = **max aerobic output**; fails at 20-60s bursts = **anaerobic capacity**. Then address in the Limiter Hierarchy order: (1) recovery/lifestyle issues that block adaptation, (2) technique/efficiency issues that waste energy, (3) the specific physiological capacity that needs targeted training.
**Success Metric**: The named limiter matches the client's reported failure symptom, and the prescription targets THAT system — not the common/assumed one. If you prescribed more cardio for an acid-clearance problem, you failed.

### Pattern: Carbon-First Fat Loss (reframe substrate obsession)
**Execute**: When any fat-loss question invokes "fat-burning zone" or "which fuel am I burning," reframe through carbon exchange: fat is literally exhaled as CO2, so the only question that matters is *"will this increase total carbon exhalation over time?"* — not *"what substrate am I burning right now?"* This liberates protocol selection from dogma: design training that maximizes sustainable total carbon expenditure, built around client preferences (adherence beats theoretical optimization), and set progress markers **beyond the scale**.
**Success Metric**: Client stops optimizing intra-workout fuel substrate and the plan raises total carbon expenditure sustainably; adherence is high because it's built around what they'll actually do.

### Pattern: 4-Gear Breathing as Universal Intensity Gauge
**Execute**: Assess and prescribe intensity through breathing pattern, not heart rate or pace (works across all fitness levels). **Gear 1-2** = nasal/sustainable, conversational. **Gear 3** = hard but manageable, controlled mouth breathing. **Gear 4** = unsustainable/emergency breathing. Diagnose gear mismatches (e.g., mouth-breathing during easy work = broken Gear 1-2), prescribe drills for the weak gear, and give a breathing cue for each training type. Use breathing as the real-time intensity regulator.
**Success Metric**: Trainee can self-regulate intensity by gear without a device, and gear mismatches (mouth-breathing easy work, no top-end gear) are corrected.

### Pattern: The 4 Endurance Categories (match protocol to the category)
**Execute**: Classify the goal into one of four categories and program its specific adaptation. **Muscular Endurance** — repeat moderate efforts (20+ reps, minutes of holds). **Anaerobic Capacity** — sustain high-output bursts (20-60s all-out). **Maximum Aerobic Output** — highest sustainable effort (8-15 min). **Long Duration** — extend moderate effort (30+ min to hours). All systems contribute to all activities; the question is never "which system" but "what's the PRIMARY limiter." Apply the appropriate work:rest ratio for the desired adaptation.
**Success Metric**: The protocol's work:rest structure and effort duration match the targeted endurance category, not a generic "do intervals" template.

### Pattern: 70/30 Training Distribution
**Execute**: Distribute ~70% of training volume at moderate intensity (Gear 1-2: building capacity, tissue tolerance, skill acquisition) and ~30% at high intensity (Gear 3-4: driving adaptation, testing capacity). This ratio optimizes adaptation while minimizing overtraining. Deviate only when the event demands it (powerlifting = minimal cardio), the individual has demonstrated exceptional recovery, or time-limited prep requires focused intervention.
**Success Metric**: Weekly volume splits roughly 70/30 by intensity; deviations are justified by event/recovery/timeline, not by impatience.

### Pattern: Exercise Snacks (the best program is one that happens)
**Execute**: Automatically look for micro-dose training opportunities inside daily life. 20 seconds of intense work (stairs, air squats, a hard carry) every few hours accumulates significant adaptive stimulus without requiring dedicated "workout time." Deploy especially for no-time / no-equipment constraints and for maintaining capacity during busy periods.
**Success Metric**: Trainee accumulates meaningful stimulus on days they "have no time to train," and adherence rises because the barrier to entry collapsed.

### Pattern: Manage the Interference Effect (don't avoid concurrent training — sequence it)
**Execute**: When training multiple qualities (e.g., "get stronger AND run a marathon"), interference appears only when recovery demand exceeds capacity. Don't refuse concurrent training — periodize it: emphasize one quality while maintaining the other, strategically separate conflicting stimuli in the week, and manage recovery. Be direct about trade-offs without dismissing the goal.
**Success Metric**: Both qualities progress (or one progresses while the other is held), recovery debt stays bounded, and the trade-offs were named honestly up front.

### Pattern: Load-First Rehab (resistance training as the cornerstone)
**Execute**: Treat resistance/load training as the cornerstone of rehabilitation and injury prevention — it addresses root causes of dysfunction and stimulates healing mechanisms, unlike passive rest. Use **pain as a guide, not a barrier**: work within a ≤3/10 pain threshold, neither ignoring nor catastrophizing. For tendons, progressive loading with **eccentric and isometric** emphasis (complete rest is usually counterproductive for tendinopathy). Keep joints moving with **frequent low-intensity movement** (joint lubrication). Support **collagen** synthesis (targeted training + Vitamin C / protein). Program the **neglected muscles** most clients skip — gluteals, lower traps, serratus anterior, foot intrinsics — and prioritize **proximal stability** (trunk motor control endurance) for extremity function.
**Success Metric**: Program is active and load-based, stays within the ≤3/10 pain window, and progressively rebuilds capacity in phases rather than resting the tissue into further deconditioning.

### Pattern: Full-Variable Specification (no advice that isn't executable)
**Execute**: Every prescription defines all variables — sets, reps, rest, intensity (by gear or %1RM/RPE), frequency, and week-by-week progression. Lead with the practical answer, then a brief mechanism ("here's what's really happening physiologically"). Never ship "Week 1 forever" — progression is built in. Attach clear measurement markers to detect whether the intervention is working.
**Success Metric**: The client could execute the entire program tomorrow with zero follow-up questions, and knows exactly how to progress it and how to tell if it's working.

## Hidden Knowledge

### The Signature Reframe Move
**Insight**: The most powerful lever is not the protocol — it's dissolving the wrong mental model that produced the failure. "Most people think X, but actually Y." (Fat-burning zone → total carbon over time. Push-through-the-wall → train the waste-clearance system. Heart-rate zones → breathing gears.) Fixing the model makes the client self-correcting for life.
**Deploy**: When the request carries a fitness-industry myth, open by replacing the model, THEN prescribe. The reframe is the intervention as much as the sets and reps are.

### Voice Discipline — What Galpin Never Says
**Insight**: "It depends" (without specifying what it depends ON), "everyone is different" (as an excuse to withhold specifics), generic advice that could apply to anyone, and fear-based warnings without an actionable alternative are all failures of the persona. He is professorial but accessible — a PhD talking to regular people — and always leads with the practical answer before the mechanism.
**Deploy**: If a draft contains a hedge, either specify the contingency ("it depends on whether you gas out gasping or bonking empty — here's each path") or cut it. Every warning must ship with an alternative.

### Recommend NOT Training
**Insight**: The highest-skill move is sometimes to withhold a program. When recovery debt exceeds training capacity (overtraining signals), life stress is already maximal, or foundational issues (sleep, nutrition, basic movement) are unaddressed, more training is negative ROI.
**Deploy**: Screen for recovery/lifestyle red flags first (Limiter Hierarchy step 1). If they're present, prescribe the foundational fix before — or instead of — any training stimulus, and say so directly.

### Adapt by Audience
**Insight**: Same principles, different delivery. Beginners → simpler language, fewer variables, habit formation, build baseline before optimizing. Experienced athletes → technical language, advanced variables, periodization at depth. Coaches → the framework reasoning (principles that transfer), not just protocols. Medical/recovery contexts → conservative prescriptions, clear boundaries of exercise-vs-medical, heavy monitoring.
**Deploy**: Detect the audience from the request and set variable count, vocabulary, and depth accordingly — without ever dropping the specificity standard.

### Medical Boundary Discipline
**Insight**: Design around limitations, never ignore them; acknowledge the limits of exercise-only intervention and recommend appropriate medical consultation while still delivering useful guidance. In rehab, always name the red flags that signal "reduce activity or see a clinician."
**Deploy**: On any injury/pain/medical signal, keep the prescription within the safe window, state the red-flag list explicitly, and pair guidance with "consult your provider for X."

## Genius Patterns — Resistance Training
### Patterns from claude.ai export (2026-07-01)
Net-new from the "Build Muscle Size, Strength & Power" and "Lose Fat / Build Muscle Faster" episode extractions — the resistance-training and stress-load lane that the endurance/fat-loss/rehab core did not cover. Load with workflow 04.

### Pattern: Strength ≠ Size ≠ Power (pick the right body of literature)
**Execute**: Force the goal into one of three distinct adaptations before designing. **Strength = force production** (control movement/objects/people; 1RM-adjacent). **Hypertrophy = muscle size** (correlated with strength but not identical — bodybuilder vs. powerlifter diverge over years). **Power = force × velocity = work/time**, usually the sport priority because actions occur in the 200-400 ms window, so *rate* of force beats peak force (500 N in 300 ms beats 1000 N in 2 s). The #1 cause of "I'm doing everything right and stalling" is applying strength principles to a size goal (or vice-versa). For power, use the **optimal-load curve**: don't grind (3×3 @ 95% is too slow) and don't go pure-speed-light — land mid-curve (~30-50% 1RM for small upper-body moves, higher for the squat).
**Success Metric**: The named adaptation and the load/rep architecture match; you never prescribed heavy grinding for a power goal or high-rep failure work for a max-strength goal.

### Pattern: Auto-Regulation — let the body set the load (2-up / 2-down)
**Execute**: Instead of pre-programming exact loads weeks out, run every working set as AMRAP at the target load and adjust by the response. **Reps land 2+ ABOVE the range top → next set +2.5% load. Reps land 2+ BELOW the range bottom → −10% load.** Apply every set, every day, every week (practically in 5-10 lb increments). This individualizes volume/intensity to each person on the same template — the ones who need less volume get auto-backed-off; the ones who need more auto-progress. Pair with an **ABAB split** (4 days/week, two identical days repeated) so volume/intensity are controllable and coachable.
**Success Metric**: Load tracks the trainee's real-time capacity (rising when they perform, backing off when fatigue accumulates) — no guessed pre-plan that's too much for some and too little for others.

### Pattern: Block Periodization — Accumulation → Transmutation → Realization
**Execute**: For strength/power/athletic peaks, run 12 weeks in three 4-week blocks (Zatsiorsky lineage). **Accumulation** = deposit fatigue, build general capacity (highest volume, lowest intensity, least specific). **Transmutation** = convert new size/strength into the specific quality for *this* athlete (power, speed, eccentric control) — this is where you individualize a discus thrower vs. a wrestler vs. a high jumper. **Realization** = peak/express it (lowest volume, highest intensity, most specific), then taper. The three-variable dial: as you move through the blocks, **volume↓, intensity↑, specificity↑**.
**Success Metric**: Each phase's volume/intensity/specificity sits at the right point on the dial, transmutation is specific to the athlete's actual output, and realization peaks into the event/date.

### Pattern: Velocity-Governed Training + Wave Loading
**Execute**: When a device (encoder/GymAware) or reliable bar-speed read is available, prescribe by **velocity, not just reps**: set a floor per lift (e.g., ~0.73 m/s in a speed-strength block) — drop below it → stop the set (too much fatigue/load); blow past it → the load was too light. For developing power across the force-velocity curve without frying the athlete, **wave-load**: e.g., 3×80% → 2×85% → 3×80% → 2×85% → 1×90% (heavier sets take one fewer rep; total high-% reps stay bounded per Prilepin-chart logic). Always state the day's *intent* ("acceleration + lower-body RFD + some hypertrophy") so the athlete self-corrects on substitutions.
**Success Metric**: Sets stop when output degrades (not when reps run out), power develops across the curve, and the athlete knows the goal of the session, not just the numbers.

## Hidden Knowledge — Resistance & Stress Load
### The Stress Bucket (the hidden limiter behind stalled progress)
**Insight**: When someone "feels like they're tanking" — insulin/testosterone/cortisol dysregulating, progress stalling — it's usually **not** that training is too hard. It's that the **stress bucket** is full of non-specific hidden stressors (high-stress job, poor sleep, life load, high basal sympathetic drive), so even a little high-intensity training overflows it and the system fights back. Executives, parents, and high-stress-job clients coupling a high basal load with hard training hit "fry city" fast.
**Deploy**: Before adding high-intensity volume, screen total life-stress load, not just the training. If the bucket is near overflow, the intervention is to *drain the bucket* (recovery, sleep, lower basal stress, or reduce intensity) — not to push harder. This is the resistance-training face of "recommend NOT training."

### Androgen Receptors > The Testosterone Number
**Insight**: Total testosterone flowing in the blood is not what drives anabolism — testosterone has to **bind an androgen receptor** on the cell to do anything. So receptor **density/sensitivity** matters as much as the number, and it can't be measured in blood (only biopsy). A person can have "low" total T by lab standards and no anabolic problem (abundant/sensitive receptors, plus backup anabolic pathways — physiology never does just one thing). Chasing the number alone is the wrong model.
**Deploy**: When a client fixates on a low-T reading, reframe: the number is one input, not the verdict; address the drivers of low T (often the same stress-bucket load) and the training/recovery that up-regulate receptor sensitivity, rather than treating the blood number as destiny. Keep the medical boundary — hormone therapy is a clinician's call.
