# The Spoken Story (Ben Watkins)
Watkins is an oral storyteller before he is anything else — the pitch room, the dinner party, the writers' room. His anatomy goes back further than the page: *"we can take this all the way back to being around the campfire as cavemen and I'm telling you a story about the bear that attacked me and what you're learning is how to avoid an attack, how to survive an attack, how heavy bears are, how many claws."* A spoken story is not a written story read out loud. It breathes, it plants seeds and waits for the room to lean in — *"there's details you need to keep the story moving forward. And then there are details you withhold because you want people to lean in and ask, make them ask"* — and it cuts itself short the moment it has you: *"As soon as you know you have somebody emotionally, that's when you move on... We're not going to overexplain."* This workflow builds the script for the ear: rhythm marked, pauses planted, ask-moments seeded, truncation points tagged, because *"People buy based on emotion, not logic"* and the ear decides faster than the eye.

## Input Required
- **The message** — what you're actually saying, one line
- **The spoken surface** — talk / VSL / reel voiceover / video script / live pitch or telling
- **Runtime** — 30 seconds, 5 minutes, 20 minutes, or "as long as the room gives me"
- **The listener** — who they are and where they are in their story (cold scroll? seated audience? skeptical buyer?)
- **Your best material** — the scar, number, or scene that only you can tell

## Workflow

### Step 1: Lock the North Star and the one emotion
Answer the four questions before a word of script: *"what are you trying to say? Why are you saying this? Who is this for? And what are you promising the audience?"* Then name the single emotion the listener must feel — spoken pieces have no scroll-back, so one emotion is the ceiling, not a limitation. Everything you're tempted to include gets filtered here first.

```
NORTH STAR: say ______ / why ______ / for ______ / promising ______
THE ONE EMOTION: __________
```

### Step 2: Write for breath — the read-aloud test is THE gate
Draft in spoken units: sentences a human can say on one breath, contour varied so the ear never settles into a drone. Provost's law holds double for the ear — same-length sentences *"become monotonous... The sound of it drones"* — and the CS-02 forensics proved that a script can pass every silent gate and still land jolty. So the drafting loop here is speak-first: say each line out loud *as you write it*, at the listener's pace. *"Your tongue knows before your eye does"* (Harding). Every stumble, stall, or backtrack is a rewrite signal, not a performance error.

Mark the script as you go with the performance notation:

| Mark | Means |
|---|---|
| `(beat)` | half-second pause — plant it right after the word carrying the insight |
| `//` | full stop-and-breathe — before a turn or a reveal |
| **bold** | punch the word — one per line maximum; emphasis is a scarcity economy |
| `⟦CUT-OK → signal⟧` | truncation point (Step 5) — cuttable live when the named buy-in signal fires |

Earn the long sentence: deploy it only after shorts have rested the ear, so it reads as crescendo, not slog. Three same-length lines in a row is a suspect run — break it.

### Step 3: Plant the ask-moments
This is the move that separates a spoken story from a monologue. Sort every detail into momentum (keep) or seed (withhold) — and for the seeds, engineer the moment the room asks: *"what detail do I want to plant seeds that make them ask about it and then you get to show off... But you didn't ramble. You didn't drone on it."* A seed is one concrete, specific, slightly-too-interesting detail dropped without explanation — "I maxed two credit cards to get to that meeting" — followed by a `(beat)` and a subject change. The withhold plus the pause is what makes the lean-in physical. On camera (VSL, reel), where nobody can interrupt, the ask-moment becomes a planted question you voice *for* them — "and you're probably wondering what the meeting even was" — then defer the payoff.

| Seed (the detail dropped, unexplained) | The ask it provokes | Where you pay it off |
|---|---|---|
| | | |
| | | |

Prime before you withhold: deliver real, specific information first — a gap only aches once the listener is invested. Vague teasing is zero-priming.

### Step 4: Build the question spine
*"I actually don't approach it as cliffhangers. I actually approach it as questions."* Open a live question **inside the first ten seconds** — the cold open's whole job — because the gap that holds them to the end is created in the first beat, not the last. Then honor the contract: *"everything I've done up to that point before I ask the question has to also be promising them that the way that gets answered, they're going to really appreciate."* Run the prediction test on the spine (Iha): simulate three listeners at the ten-second mark asking "what comes next?" — 3/3 predict the close means the question leaked; 0/3 means it's tight. Close the loop honestly in the final beat, and if the surface allows, end on a *new* question that hands them the pen.

```
COLD OPEN (first 10 seconds, verbatim): __________
THE QUESTION it opens: __________
THE PROMISE that the answer is worth staying for: __________
WHERE THE LOOP CLOSES: __________
```

### Step 5: Tag the truncation marks
For live delivery, build the body as modular sections and tag each `⟦CUT-OK → signal⟧` with the exact buy-in signal that triggers the cut — a lean-in, a nod, a finished sentence, a question. *"I'm going to truncate that section. If you decide you want to come back and ask more about it, then we're having a conversation. Now I'm winning even more."* The script you carry in is longer than the script you deliver; the room decides which sections die. For recorded surfaces, truncation happens in the edit: cut each section at its emotional peak, before the explanation — the viewer's finger is the buy-in signal you never get to see. Stack `/bw-pitch-room` when the telling is a sell with a decision-maker in the room.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| 30-second reel VO | One seed, one question, one payoff — nothing else fits. Cold open carries the entire question spine in one breath; contour is short-short-long; the single `//` sits before the final line. No truncation marks — the edit already cut everything cuttable. Stack `kcs-script-profile` for the retention mechanics under the voice. |
| 5-minute VSL | The question spine is the lead; open the question in the first ten seconds and re-promise it every 60–90 seconds with a fresh seed. Ask-moments become voiced-for-them questions that defer payoffs down the script. Stack `luke-iha-vsl-leads` for the lead structure inside Watkins's arc. |
| 20-minute talk | Three to four modular sections, each with its own mini-spine, all serving one master question opened in the cold open and closed in the final two minutes. Truncation marks go live here: watch the room, cut on buy-in, let Q&A be the sections they pulled back. Plant one `(beat)` of genuine silence after the biggest line — hold it longer than comfort. |
| Live pitch / dinner-table telling | The purest form: no script visible, but the spine, seeds, and cut-points are memorized as a map, not lines. The ask-moments do the work — every question from the listener is a section they just bought. Assume the cold room (*"you've heard five pitches. I'm the sixth"*) and let the flip be the story's first job. Stack `stanton-dialogue-aloud` to pressure-test that every line survives a human mouth. |

**Worked micro-example** (30s reel VO, coach to invisible experts — model the notation, don't copy the lines):
```
COLD OPEN:  "I spent **fourteen thousand dollars** on courses. (beat) None of them
            fixed the actual problem."
            → question opened: what WAS the actual problem?

SCRIPT:     "Everyone told me to niche down. I tried. Twice. (beat) Both businesses
            died in under a year. // Then one coach said something that rearranged
            me — and it took her **six words**."  ⟦CUT-OK → they're still watching at 0:15⟧
            "She said: stop performing someone smaller. (beat) The niche was never
            the problem. **You** were hiding under it. // So here's the question I'll
            leave with you — what are you pretending to be less than?"
            → loop closed: the six words delivered. → new question handed over.

SEED:       "$14,000" dropped unexplained → "on what?" → paid off by the courses/coach turn
```

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric. No machinery on the surface: the listener hears a story, never a "hook," a "seed," or a "section" — the notation is for the speaker's eyes only. No over-explaining past the sale; no cliffhanger without a promised payoff; no total clarity — the withheld seed IS the mechanism.

**FLOW GATE** (all five must pass; one fail = revise before delivery):
- [ ] **Contour varies.** No run of 3+ same-length sentences; the piece uses short, medium, AND long; long sentences arrive only after the listener is rested.
- [ ] **Every sentence hands off.** Each opens with something the listener already holds (echo word, bound pronoun, named relationship) and ends on the new thing; no subject swap without a bridge; every *it/this/that/all three* binds instantly inside the piece.
- [ ] **Loops are engineered, not asserted.** Each loop = promise + withhold + stake, primed with real information first; 3-persona prediction test run; every opened loop resolves honestly in the same piece.
- [ ] **Survives read-aloud.** Read cold, out loud, at the listener's pace — zero stumbles, stalls, or backtracks. Read-aloud is not optional here — it IS the medium.
- [ ] **Compression kept causality.** Every cut preserved the but/so/which-is-why chain; no garbled fusions, wrong-antecedent pronouns, or amputated lists; each trimmed line still parses first-pass.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Writing prose and reading it aloud after.** A written piece performed is not a spoken piece — the tells are subordinate clauses no breath survives and emphasis nowhere. Recovery: re-draft speak-first (Step 2); say every line before you keep it.
- **Explaining the seed the moment you plant it.** The reflex to clarify kills the ask — total clarity removes the reason to lean in. Recovery: drop the detail, `(beat)`, change the subject; if nobody would ask about it, the seed isn't specific enough — sharpen the detail, not the explanation.
- **A cold open that's a topic, not a question.** "Today I want to talk about niching" opens nothing. Recovery: re-run Step 4 — the first ten seconds must open a gap with a promised payoff; run the 3-listener prediction test until it's 0/3.
- **Punching every line.** Bold on everything is bold on nothing — the broetry drone at spoken scale, dropping the mic after every phrase. Recovery: one punch word per line max, one held silence per piece; emphasis is a scarcity economy (Law 15).
- **Delivering the whole script to a room that already bought.** You rehearsed it, so you say it — past the sale. Recovery: honor the `⟦CUT-OK⟧` marks (Step 5); the question they ask back is a bigger win than your monologue.
