# The North Star Lock (Ben Watkins)
Before Watkins tells a single story he stops and answers four questions — *"what are you trying to say? Why are you saying this? Who is this for? And what are you promising the audience?"* He calls it principles of messaging, and the reason he never skips it is simple: *"When I go to tell a story, I have to have a bigger... purpose for telling that story. that helps become what I would call it a north star... And then I make decisions based on that."* This workflow doesn't write your story. It builds the compass the story gets built against — the fixed point every later cut, hook, and pitch is measured by. Skip it and you'll ramble across five ideas and call it comprehensive.

## Input Required
- The material or goal, raw — the post, the pitch, the offer, the campaign, or just the pile of notes you're trying to make into something
- Who it's actually for (best guess is fine — we'll sharpen the "who" into one person)
- The action or shift you want on the other side of it (what changes in the audience)
- Anything you're already attached to including — flag it now; the promise decides whether it stays

## Workflow

### Step 1: Put the raw material on the table — no polish
State the goal in one plain sentence, the way you'd say it to a friend, before any framing. *"This is going to sound simple, but nobody does it."* Most people never name the raw thing; they jump straight to producing. Write it ugly: "I want to post about X" or "I need to pitch this offer." That sentence is the lump of clay. Everything below turns it into a north star or proves you don't have one yet.

### Step 2: Answer the four questions rigorously — say / why / who / promise
Run all four. Watkins's exact frame: *"the first place you start is what I call principles of messaging, which is what are you trying to say? Why are you saying this? Who is this for? And what are you promising the audience?"* Answer each in a full, specific line — no placeholders, no "everyone," no "to add value."

| Question | What it locks | Answer specifically — failure tell |
|---|---|---|
| **What are you trying to say?** | The ONE idea | If you need "and" to finish it, that's two north stars → split into two pieces. |
| **Why are you saying this?** | The purpose to infect them with | If the why is "because I should post" there's nothing to catch — find the real stake. |
| **Who is this for?** | The one person you're calling by name | A demographic ("founders") isn't a person. Name one human at one moment in their arc. |
| **What are you promising the audience?** | The payoff every later beat must earn | The one people skip. If you can't name it, stop — see Step 4. |

The fourth question is the load-bearing one and the one everyone glosses. Sit in it. *"What are you promising the audience"* is not the topic and not the takeaway — it's the transformation they get for spending their attention on you.

### Step 3: Name the single emotion this has to cause
Watkins's whole engine: *"People buy based on emotion, not logic."* A north star that names a topic but not a feeling is still a feature list. So force the one emotion — not a mood board, one word: *seen, relieved, indignant, hungry, brave, caught-out, less-alone.* The test is his: *"I don't want you to just see my resume. I want you to see me."* If your material only lets them *see* the idea, you've named a topic, not an emotion. Write the single emotion and the reason this audience, at this moment in their arc, feels it.

```
THE ONE EMOTION: __________ (one word)
Because this reader, right now, is: __________ (their interior state — the thing they'd never say out loud)
```

If two emotions compete, pick the one that drives the action in your Step 1 goal. You can only aim one arrow.

### Step 4: Compress to the one-line promise
Fuse say + who + emotion + promise into a single sentence the whole piece is built to keep. This is the north star made portable — the line you'd tape to the monitor. (This is Watkins's promise-as-compass, not a StoryBrand "one-liner" or a screenwriter's "log line" — if you want the branded one-liner/log-line format, that's `donald-miller-storybrand` / Blake Snyder's lane, not this file's. Don't credit those to Watkins.) Form: **for [the one person], this makes them feel [emotion] by promising [the transformation].** Keep it plain and a little rough; polish is the tell. Examples of the shape (not templates to fill blindly):
- "For the expert nobody's found yet, this makes them feel *seen* by promising the reason it isn't their fault."
- "For the founder about to quit, this makes them feel *braver* by promising the small next move that beats the big plan."

> **The gate**: if you cannot write this line — specifically, if the *promise* half is blank — you do not have a story. *You have material.* Go back to Step 2's fourth question. Do not proceed to any downstream workflow with an unnamed promise; you'll produce something comprehensive and cold, and the whole roster is built to prevent exactly that.

### Step 5: Derive the "what I will cut" filter
The north star only earns its keep if it starts *removing* things. Watkins: the master's secret *"is the ability to know what to leave out"* — and every detail sorts into *"details you need to keep the story moving forward"* and *"details you withhold because you want people to lean in and ask."* Now that the promise is fixed, run your raw material against it and write the explicit cut list. This is the filter, stated out loud, that every downstream cut obeys.

| Element in the raw material | Does it serve the promise? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| (a detail, angle, proof point, tangent) | drives the ONE idea toward the emotion? | **KEEP** (momentum) |
| | true but pulls toward a different promise? | **CUT** (off-north-star, no matter how good) |
| | serves the promise but gives it all away? | **WITHHOLD** (seed — make them ask) |

Then write the filter as a standing rule the roster inherits: *"Anything that isn't [the ONE idea] pointed at [the emotion] for [the one person] gets cut — including ______."* Name at least one thing you're attached to that the promise just killed. If the filter cuts nothing, it isn't a north star; it's a wish list.

### Step 6: Lock the brief
Assemble the four answers, the emotion, the one-line promise, and the cut filter into the locked North Star brief below. This is the pre-flight artifact `/bw-story-anatomy`, `/bw-pitch-room`, and the copy roster filter through — hand it off, don't re-litigate it mid-build.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| **Short-form social (Reels / LinkedIn / X)** | One north star per post — if the promise needs two sentences, it's two posts. The "who" narrows to a single scroller at a single moment; the emotion is the thing that stops the thumb. The cut filter is brutal here: at this length one off-promise line is a third of the piece. Lock it before you write the hook, not after. |
| **Long-form (Substack / YouTube / essay)** | The promise is what the reader is *actually* getting for 8 minutes of attention, under the topic. The north star governs which sections live — run every planned section against the cut filter; the "interesting but proves a different promise" section is the one to kill. Reader should *feel* the promise kept by the end without ever being told what it was. |
| **Live pitch / sales / VSL** | The promise becomes the thing you want them repeating in the car — Watkins's KPI: *"the first thing they should do leaving that room is call their boss or call their wife or their husband or grab a co-worker and be talking about the pitch they just heard."* The one emotion is the state you're engineering in the room; the cut filter is your truncate list — the sections you drop the instant they've bought, so you never talk past the sale. |
| **Brand / campaign** | The north star is the promise every asset across the campaign keeps — the single feeling the brand-as-a-show delivers, not the tagline. Each asset is audited as serving-or-fighting the one promise; the "clever" asset that wins applause while proving a different promise is exactly what the filter cuts. This is the brief every downstream conductor (`/bw-story-anatomy`, brand/positioning partners) inherits as the fixed center. |

## Output Format
Worked micro-example (Watkins's own general-meeting bet — he maxed two credit cards to fly out and pitch himself into the room):
```
RAW GOAL (plain): I want to post about betting on myself before anyone else would.
PRINCIPLES OF MESSAGING
  • Say (ONE idea): your first investor has to be you.
  • Who it's for: the talented nobody sitting on a "someday" plan, waiting for permission.
THE ONE EMOTION: braver
THE ONE-LINE PROMISE: "For the person waiting to be picked, this makes them feel braver by promising that the proof they can move first is a receipt they can create today."
CUT: the humble-brag résumé of credits — off-north-star; it makes them see the resume, not the choice.
```

Now fill your own:
```
NORTH STAR BRIEF — [material / goal]

RAW GOAL (plain): __________

PRINCIPLES OF MESSAGING
  • What I'm trying to say (ONE idea): __________
  • Why I'm saying it (the purpose to infect them with): __________
  • Who it's for (one person, one moment in their arc): __________
  • What I'm promising the audience: __________

THE ONE EMOTION: __________
  (because this reader, right now, is: __________)

THE ONE-LINE PROMISE (the compass):
  "For ______, this makes them feel ______ by promising ______."

THE CUT FILTER (what this north star removes):
  • KEEP (momentum): __________
  • WITHHOLD (seed — make them ask): __________
  • CUT (off-north-star, incl. the thing I was attached to): __________
  Standing rule: "Anything that isn't [ONE idea] → [emotion] → for [the one person] gets cut."

STATUS: promise named [yes] — this is a story, not material [confirmed].
HAND OFF TO: /bw-story-anatomy · /bw-pitch-room · copy roster (filter through, don't re-litigate)
```

## Quality Gate
> 🛡️ **Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (esp. *Purpose / North Star* and *Subtraction Discipline*). Flag and fix before delivering.
- **The promise is named and specific** — a transformation, not a topic or a takeaway. If the promise line is blank or generic, you have material, not a story; you failed the one thing this workflow exists to catch.
- **The "who" is one person at one moment**, not a demographic. If it reads "founders" or "creators," narrow it until you could describe their exact thought this morning.
- **Exactly ONE emotion** is named as a single word, tied to the audience's interior state — not a mood, not two feelings hedged with a slash.
- **The one-line promise actually filters** — the cut list removes at least one real thing, including something you were attached to. A north star that cuts nothing is a wish list.
- **The machinery stays off the page** in whatever gets built downstream — this brief is the operator's private compass, never labeled "here's our promise" in the output itself.
- **The idea is singular** — no "and." If two ideas survived, you locked two north stars; split them.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Skipping the fourth question.** Say/why/who get answered, "what am I promising" gets a hand-wave, and the whole thing floats. Recovery: refuse to leave Step 2 until the promise is a full sentence naming the transformation — *if you can't name it, you don't have a story, you have material.*
- **Naming a topic and calling it an emotion.** "I want them to understand X" is a resume-read, not a feeling. Recovery: apply the litmus — *"I don't want you to just see my resume. I want you to see me."* — and rewrite the emotion as one word that moves the body, not the mind.
- **A filter that cuts nothing.** The brief looks locked but every scrap of raw material "still kind of fits." Recovery: force at least one CUT of something you liked; if the promise doesn't kill a darling, it isn't sharp enough — tighten the promise until it does.
- **Re-litigating the north star mid-build.** Downstream, the piece drifts and you quietly redefine the promise to fit what you wrote. Recovery: treat the locked brief as fixed; if the draft fights it, the draft is wrong, not the north star — cut back toward the compass, don't move the compass.
