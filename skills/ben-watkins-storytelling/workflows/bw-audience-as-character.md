# Audience-as-Character (Ben Watkins)
Watkins reads people for a living because he once bet everything on being read right — early on he took two credit cards that were basically at their limit, combined them to buy a plane ticket to New York for a *general meeting*: no role attached, just a casting director putting a face to a name. That instinct hardened into a method — "*becoming a master story is almost like becoming a master in people*." He won't touch a hook until he knows who it's for, and the way he gets there is to study a customer exactly the way a screenwriter studies a character: before a single scene, before plot or structure, you figure out their *want, need, nemesis, and what's standing in their way*. The show distills the whole move into one question — *where are they when they find you?* Are you a GoDaddy, catching them right after they lock in and buy their first domain, all in on the dream? Or are you catching the founder on the cusp of quitting, in their dark night of the soul, who just needs a reminder it's not over? Because once you know where they are in their story, you know exactly what to say to them.

## Input Required
- The audience / ICP (whatever you have — a demographic, a segment, a rough sketch)
- The offer, product, or piece of content you're speaking *from*
- Any existing research (deep-canvass, `content-audience-profile`, reviews, DMs) — optional but sharpens the mirror
- The north star if you have it (say / why / who / **promise**) — the *who* is what this workflow makes specific

## Workflow

### Step 1: Run the character study — want / need / nemesis / obstacle
Stop treating them as a demographic. A demographic hands you a customer; a character hands you the line that lands. Write them out as a protagonist — the four fields Watkins runs before any story. Keep it lighter than a full deep-canvass; you're after the *dramatic* shape of the person, not their whole psychology. (For the full multi-field protagonist build — arc, wound, contradiction — `/bw-character-canvas` owns that move; this is the compressed cut aimed at the marketing line.) If you need identity-level depth (resistances, buried beliefs, the interior no survey reaches), that's the deep-canvass experts' job — stack `mcraney-deep-canvass` / `icp-deep-dive` and feed their map into this table.

| Field | What it is | The Watkins cut |
|---|---|---|
| **Want** | What they say they're chasing (surface, conscious) | The thing they'd name if asked |
| **Need** | What they actually require to get there (often unspoken) | What the offer really delivers underneath the want |
| **Nemesis** | The person/force with the chip-on-shoulder charge | "*if you have a nemesis, that's a chip on your shoulder. People sometimes are motivated by that*" — the unfinished business you can speak to |
| **Obstacle** | What stands in the way right now | The wall between them and the want — your content is the ladder over it |

The nemesis field is the one people skip and the one with the most heat. Watkins probes for it deliberately — "*I want this person to hear that I just sold a show because they told me I'd never get there.*" Find that energy in your audience and you've found a live wire.

### Step 2: Locate them on their emotional journey
Your customer is the hero of their own story and they are somewhere *specific* on it right now — the same emotional arc a screenwriter maps with the **Save the Cat beat sheet** (Blake Snyder's structure, the film-school "holy grail" — this beat-sheet overlay is Snyder's, not Watkins's own; Watkins's own move is *asking where they are*). Plot the moment they collide with your offer. Same buyer, different beat = a completely different thing to say.

| Beat on their arc | Emotional state | What they need to hear |
|---|---|---|
| **The start / "all in"** (the GoDaddy moment — just locked in, bought the domain) | Fired up, naive, hungry | Momentum, permission, "you're not crazy for going for this" |
| **The mess / stuck in the middle** | Frustrated, spinning, doubting the method | A hidden room in the familiar (Pillar 3) — the thing they didn't know was there |
| **The dark night of the soul** (on the cusp of quitting) | Defeated, ready to fold | a reminder that it's not over — proof, a hand, a reason to stay in |
| **The comeback / almost-there** | Cautiously believing, wants the last push | The nemesis payoff — proof the climb was worth it |

Pin the ONE beat where your marketing meets them. The show boils the whole thing down to one question — *where are they when they find you?*

### Step 3: Pick the one person at the one moment
Even if the product serves everyone, the **marketing speaks to one**. This is the discipline most people break — they widen the message to catch the whole market and land on no one. Watkins narrows: one reader, one beat, mirrored so precisely "*it feels like you're talking to them.*" That's *calling them by their name* — recognition, not a name token; describe their exact thought this morning so accurately they turn and look. (The mechanics of that recognition move — the "Just Do It" anchor, name-token vs. mirror — live in `/bw-call-by-name`; here you just aim it at the located beat.) Write the one-line character-moment address:

> *One person: the [role] at [beat], whose [want] is blocked by [obstacle], carrying [nemesis] — the morning they [the exact interior moment they find you].*

Precision test: it should feel slightly *wrong* to everyone it's not for. If it fits the whole segment comfortably, it's a label, not a mirror — cut it finer.

### Step 4: Derive the message that lands at exactly that moment
Now write the message *to* that one person at that one beat — not a summary of the offer. The emotion comes from the fit between what they feel *right now* and what you say. Produce the moment-specific message and the one emotion it causes. Keep the machinery invisible: you are not labeling "here's the recognition beat," you are *saying the thing that makes them feel seen*. If the beat is the dark night, the message is a hand, not a features list. If it's the GoDaddy start, it's fuel, not a warning. Same offer, the line bends to the beat.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| **Short-form social (Reels / LinkedIn / X)** | One person, one beat, one post — the hook *is* the mirror of their exact interior moment ("the morning you almost closed the tab"). At this length a message aimed at two beats lands on neither. Open the recognition in the first line; the demographic never gets named, the *feeling* does. |
| **Long-form (Substack / YouTube / essay)** | Open on the one person at the one beat so the reader thinks *that's me*, then let the piece travel their arc with them — meet them at the dark night, walk them toward the comeback. The character study becomes the through-line the whole piece is spoken to, never stated. |
| **Live pitch / sales / VSL** | Study the *buyer* as the character too — where are *they* in *their* story (sixth pitch of the day, tired, skeptical)? Address the customer-character out loud so the buyer feels the audience is real and specific. The moment-message becomes the emotional core the numbers merely confirm. |
| **Brand / campaign** | The brand meets a whole spectrum of beats, so build a **character-moment map**: one message per beat, each speaking to one person, all under one north star. Don't blur into a single "everyone" message — segment by *where they are in their story*, not just by demographic. The GoDaddy start and the dark night are two campaigns, not one. |

Worked example (a note-taking app running a "get unstuck" video):
```
AUDIENCE-AS-CHARACTER BRIEF
Offer / content: a short "you're not done" video for a note-taking app
North star (who): the solo founder two years in, quietly deciding whether to quit

THE CHARACTER STUDY
  • Want:     to finally ship the thing and prove the leap wasn't stupid
  • Need:     permission to stay in one more week — not another feature
  • Nemesis:  the old boss who said they'd be back at their desk in six months
  • Obstacle: the Sunday-night dread that it's already over
  [depth handed off to deep-canvass? no]

THEIR MOMENT ON THE ARC
  Beat where they find us: the dark night — on the cusp of quitting
  Emotional state at that beat: defeated, ready to fold

THE ONE PERSON, THE ONE MOMENT
  "The solo founder at the dark night, whose want to ship is blocked by the
   Sunday dread, carrying the told-you-so boss — the night they opened the
   'I'm shutting it down' email to their last three users."
  Precision check: would feel WRONG to everyone it's not for? yes

THE MOMENT-SPECIFIC MESSAGE
  The one emotion it causes: relief — someone knows exactly where I am
  The message: Not a feature tour. It's not over. Nobody ever got the easy
  way — it's always been the hard way, and the hard way is still a way.
  Aimed at the person who almost sent that email tonight.
```

## Quality Gate
> 🛡️ **Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (esp. *Recognition / Call By Name* and *Purpose / North Star*). Flag and fix before delivering.
- **One person, one moment** — the message speaks to a single reader at a single beat, not to the whole segment. If it comfortably fits everyone, it fits no one; cut finer.
- **Recognition, not a label** — the address mirrors an *interior* state (their exact thought/fear/dream) so precisely they feel written about, no name token. It should feel wrong to the person it's not for.
- **Located on the arc** — you named the specific beat (GoDaddy start vs. dark night, etc.), and the message *bends to that beat*. Same offer at a different beat would read differently.
- **Nemesis is live** — the nemesis field carries actual charge (the chip on the shoulder), not a generic "competitor." If it's inert, dig again.
- **Depth is honest** — if the piece needs identity-level resistance, you routed to `mcraney-deep-canvass` / `icp-deep-dive` rather than faking canvass-grade depth in the four-field study.
- **Machinery invisible** — the output *says the thing that lands*; it never labels "here's the recognition beat." Execute, never announce.
- **Flow Gate** (references/flow-and-cadence.md § 4, applies to any prose the output carries): contour varies, sentences hand off (known-new), loops engineered not asserted, survives read-aloud — one fail = revise.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Studying the demographic instead of the character.** Age/income/title gives you a customer, not a line. **Recovery**: fill all four fields (want/need/nemesis/obstacle) as a protagonist; if you can't, you're profiling a segment, not a person — go narrower until one human appears.
- **Speaking to the whole arc at once.** Trying to catch the GoDaddy beginner *and* the dark-night quitter in one message dilutes both. **Recovery**: pick the ONE beat where the marketing meets them, ship that; make the other beats separate pieces.
- **Recognition collapsing into a name token / label.** "Hey founders" or "[first name]" is not calling them by their name. **Recovery**: describe the exact interior moment — the tab they almost closed, the message they reread — until it would feel *wrong* to anyone it's not for.
- **Faking identity depth in the craft pass.** The four-field study is the *dramatic* shape, not deep resistance mapping; treating it as canvass-grade produces a confident-but-shallow mirror. **Recovery**: stack `mcraney-deep-canvass` / `icp-deep-dive` for the belief/resistance layer, then feed it back into Step 1.
