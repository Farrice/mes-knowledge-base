# Call Them By Their Name (Ben Watkins)
Watkins builds this move off an old African proverb — *"the most powerful word in the universe is someone's name. In a crowd of people, you can say one person's name, they turn and they look at you."* But he means it figuratively, and it's the one everyone gets wrong. His proof is the shoe: *"Think about how many people woke up every day with some sort of plan to start some sort of New Year's resolution or change some sort of habit or behavior and then a slogan comes out and says just do it and they think it's saying that right to them. That's how easy it is to call people by their name."* Not one person's name is used. Millions feel personally addressed. This workflow builds that mirror — copy so exact one reader stops and thinks *that's me* — without ever typing their name.

## Input Required
- The audience — one person, not a demographic (the more specific the better)
- The message / offer you'll get to *after* they feel named
- Any raw voice-of-customer you have: DMs, reviews, call transcripts, the exact words they use
- The single emotion you want firing at the moment of recognition (frustration, shame, hope, relief)

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Profile the interior state (get inside their head, not their bio)
Demographics give you a customer; the interior gives you the line that lands. Forget age and job title. Write down what's actually running through this person's head — in *their* words, not marketing words. Fill every row; a blank row is a recognition line you'll never write.

| Interior register | The question to answer | Get the exact version, not the category |
|---|---|---|
| **The 3am thought** | What do they think about, alone, when they can't sleep? | Not "worried about money" → *"I make more than my dad ever did and I still check my balance before I buy coffee."* |
| **The specific fear** | What are they scared is *true about them*? | Not "fear of failure" → *"maybe I'm not behind, maybe I'm just not built for this."* |
| **The exact frustration** | What did they mutter under their breath this week? | Not "frustrated with tools" → *"I've bought four courses and I still can't tell you what I actually do."* |
| **The private dream** | What do they want but won't say out loud because it sounds naive? | Not "wants freedom" → *"I want to be the one people text when they don't know what to do."* |

Mine the voice-of-customer for their literal phrasing. Watkins studies a customer the way a screenwriter studies a character — *"becoming a master story is almost like becoming a master in people."* The line you can't fake is the one they already said.

### Step 2 — Write the mirror lines (describe the interior so accurately it feels stolen)
Now turn each interior state into a line that describes it back to them. The standard, stated cleanest by the show: *"It's not actually about saying their name. It's about describing someone's thoughts or fears or frustrations, their dreams or experiences so accurately that they immediately recognize themselves in what you're saying."* You are holding up a mirror, not pasting on a label. Write 3–5 candidates per interior state and keep the ones that make *you* wince a little — that wince is the precision landing.

Two proven shapes to draft against:
- **The bare declaration** (the *Just Do It* register): name the exact resolve or condition in the fewest words, so it reads like a private thought spoken aloud.
- **The knowing address** — *"if you've been struggling with X, you know what I'm talking about."* The second clause does the work: it confirms you've *been there*, so recognition tips into trust.

Rules for the mirror: use *their* nouns and verbs, not yours. Second person or overheard-first-person — never "our customers often feel." Specific enough that it would feel *wrong* to the person it's not for. Vague recognition names no one.

### Step 3 — Run the recognition test (would they stop and think *that's me*?)
Score every candidate against the mirror below. If a line doesn't earn a hard yes on the first three, cut it — a near-miss recognition line is worse than none, because it reads as "generic marketing" and the reader's guard goes up.

| Test | Pass looks like | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **The stop** | They'd physically pause the scroll — *wait, how do you know that* | It's true but skimmable; no jolt |
| **The specificity** | Feels written about them *this morning*, not about a segment | A horoscope — true of anyone, owned by no one |
| **The exclusion** | Would feel *wrong* to the person it's not for | Broad enough that everyone half-nods |
| **The interior** | Mirrors a thought/fear/dream — something inside | Pastes a name/label/job-title on the outside |
| **The voice** | Sounds like *them* talking, not you selling | Marketing diction; words they'd never use |

When cutting: cuts remove details, never the connective tissue between surviving sentences — re-read aloud after each cut pass.

### Step 4 — Land the value on the heels of recognition, and leave a loop open
Recognition is Pillar 1 — attention. It's not the payoff; it's the pin that holds their focus so you can move. The instant they feel named, get to the value — the *here's-what-I'd-do-about-that* turn — but don't close it all the way. Watkins never lets the gap seal: *"I need to establish a question between myself and the audience that they have to get that answered,"* and *"everything I've done up to that point before I ask the question has to also be promising them that the way that gets answered, they're going to really appreciate."* So the recognition line earns the trust; the value line proves you can help; the open loop makes them stay. Sequence, never labeled: mirror → the turn to value → the withheld beat that pulls them forward. And thread the mirror in — the sentence after a recognition line must open on what the mirror just planted (known-new contract); an isolated mirror line is a jolt, not a pin.

Assemble the survivors into a **recognition-line bank** — sorted by interior register, each tagged with the emotion it fires and the value-turn it hands off to — ready to drop into hooks, VSL opens, email subject lines, and ad first-frames. Hand the bank downstream: feed the strongest mirrors to `diandra-headline-engineer` to weaponize as headlines, to `copy-engine` to build the body around, or to `resonate` to pressure-test whether the recognition truly lands before it ships.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| **Short-form social (Reels / LinkedIn / X)** | Recognition line *is* the first frame / first line — it has to land in under 2 seconds or the scroll wins. One mirror, one emotion, immediate value-turn. On-screen text = the bare-declaration shape; the caption carries the "if you've been struggling with X" knowing-address. No windup — the mirror is the hook. |
| **Long-form (Substack / YouTube / essay)** | Open cold on the 3am thought in their words, hold the mirror for 2–3 sentences until they've fully recognized themselves, *then* turn to value. You have room to stack two or three interior registers — but pay each off; a mirror with no value-turn reads as manipulation over a long form. |
| **Live pitch / sales / VSL** | Say the fear *for* them before they say it — *"I don't want you to just see my resume. I want you to see me."* Watch the face: the flicker of being-seen is buy-in. The moment it lands, truncate the empathy and move to the mechanism — over-mirroring past the recognition is the same leak as over-explaining. |
| **Brand / campaign** | The recognition becomes the through-line, not a one-off line — a *Just Do It*-class phrase that names the audience's whole interior stance and repeats across every asset. Test it the same way: does one specific person feel it's about them, or does it try to name everyone and so names no one? |

## Quality Gate
> 🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` (esp. *Literal-name personalization*, *Talking at the audience*, *Naming the machinery*) + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (*Recognition / Call By Name*). Flag and fix before delivering.
- **Zero literal-name tokens.** No `[first name]`, no "hey [job title]." If the effect leans on a name, it isn't recognition — it's a mail merge. Rebuild as a mirror of the interior.
- **Passes the exclusion test.** Each line would feel *wrong* to the person it's not for. If it's broad enough that everyone half-nods, it names no one — cut it.
- **Mirrors interior, not exterior.** Every line reflects a thought/fear/dream in their own words, not a demographic label pasted on the outside.
- **Value arrives on the heels of recognition.** The mirror hands off to a value-turn fast; recognition alone with no payoff is a party trick that reads as manipulation.
- **The loop stays open.** Something is withheld and promised worth-it, so the reader stays past the *that's-me* — the question, not the cliffhanger.
- **Machinery invisible.** Nothing in the delivered copy announces "here's your recognition line." They feel named; they never see the mirror being held.
- **Every line hands off.** Each sentence opens on something the reader already holds and ends on the new thing; recognition lines thread into what follows, never stand isolated.
- **Loops engineered, not asserted.** Promise + withhold + stake, primed with real information first; a mere mention creates confusion, not curiosity.
- **Survives read-aloud.** Read cold, out loud — zero stumbles or backtracks; no run of 3+ same-length sentences.

## Common Pitfalls
- **The horoscope.** The line is true of anyone, so it's owned by no one — reads as generic marketing and the guard goes up. *Recovery*: go back to Step 1 and get the *exact* version — the coffee-balance line, not "worried about money." Specific to one beats resonant to all.
- **Name-token cosplay.** Reaching for `[first name]` or "fellow founders" and calling it personal. *Recovery*: delete the token; mirror the interior state instead — Watkins's whole point is the name is never said.
- **Recognition with no exit.** A gorgeous mirror that just sits there — they feel seen, then bounce, because nothing turned to value or opened a loop. *Recovery*: run Step 4; every mirror needs a value-turn and a withheld beat, or it's empathy theater.
- **Over-mirroring past the buy-in.** Piling on three more "I see you" lines after the recognition already landed. *Recovery*: truncate the instant you sense buy-in — *"if you decide you want to come back and ask more about it, then we're having a conversation"* — and move to the mechanism.
