# Elevate (Ben Watkins)
No piece is ever too good to touch — CS-01 scored ~8.5 with a felt verdict of "one of the most accurate and powerful pieces we've generated together," and its ledger entry still ends with elevation doors left open, because a case study exists to make the next rewrite informed, not to fence the piece off. This is the elevation engine: study the thousand right micro-decisions first, then attack from two directions at once — a scalpel pass that keeps the spine and a blank-page reimagining that tries to beat it — and lay all three versions side by side for the operator's gut to judge. "It's the ability to know what to leave out" cuts both ways here: sometimes what you leave out is the flab in a great draft, and sometimes it's the great draft itself.

## Pre-Flight
Read before executing:
1. `skills/ben-watkins-storytelling/genius.md` (voice + Decision Framework + Anti-Patterns)
2. `skills/ben-watkins-storytelling/references/flow-and-cadence.md` (the Flow Laws + § The Flow Gate — both takes must pass it)
3. `skills/ben-watkins-storytelling/references/case-studies.md` (the ledger — check whether this piece already has an entry)
4. `skills/ben-watkins-storytelling/references/source-quotes.md` (calibration anchors)

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` against the ORIGINAL before touching it — you must be able to state its north star (say / why / who / promise) as built. If the piece has no audit or case-study entry, run `/bw-anatomy-audit` first and come back; an informed elevation beats a blind one, and a blind one is how you trample the thousand right micro-decisions you never saw.

## Input Required
- The piece, in full — post, edition, pitch, page, script; any format, any score, including work already crowned 10/10
- Its audit or case-study entry (CS-XX from `references/case-studies.md`, or the fresh `/bw-anatomy-audit` output)
- The original north star: what it says, why, who for, what it promises
- Who the one author voice is (Watkins alone, or a named single lead — never a committee)
- Any constraint that binds both takes (char cap, platform, compliance, locked frame)

## Workflow

### Step 1: Read the case study — name what must survive
Before you touch a line, know what's firing. From the audit/case study, name the **3 strongest moves** in the piece with a verbatim proof line each — the recognition mirror, the coincidence point where the 3 C's fire at once, the planted seed, whatever the entry proves. These are the survivors: every version you produce either keeps them or *consciously surpasses* them with something named and better. In CS-01 that list would be the fork simile, the "we should be friends" close, the mechanism taught under the cliché — a rewrite that loses those without replacing them didn't elevate, it vandalized.

| # | Strongest move | Proof line (verbatim) | In Take A | In Take B: kept or surpassed by what? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | |
| 2 | | | | |
| 3 | | | | |

### Step 2: TAKE A — the scalpel
Surgical passes only. The spine stays; the north star stays; ONE coherent author voice holds the pen throughout (CS-00, `references/case-studies.md § CS-00`: fusing 4–9 expert voices flattened an elevated piece the judges scored 7.25–8.6 down to Farrice's 3/10 — the multi-engine rebuild is banned here). Run at most three passes, in this order:

1. **Flow pass** — handoffs, contour, bound pronouns per the Flow Laws. Fix the joint, not the sentence: a jarring line is usually an opening that shares nothing with the previous close.
2. **Subtraction pass** — "there's details you need to keep the story moving forward. And then there are details you withhold because you want people to lean in and ask." Sort every detail: momentum, seed, or dead weight. Cut the dead weight; never the connective tissue between what stays.
3. **One pillar strengthened** — pick the weakest pillar from the audit (in CS-01: leave-wanting at 7.5) and raise it. One pillar. Strengthening two is a rebuild wearing a scalpel's badge.

Each edit gets a one-line receipt: what changed, which move it serves.

### Step 3: TAKE B — the blank page
Close the original. Keep only the north star and the Step 1 survivor list. Write the piece again from zero, one author, aiming to **surpass** the original — not to differ from it. Different is cheap; a Take B that swaps the metaphor and shuffles the beats has produced novelty, not elevation. The bar: "I don't want you to just see my resume. I want you to see me." If the original made them see the resume beautifully, Take B has to make them *see the person* — a deeper recognition mirror, a harder coincidence point, a question that opens earlier and pulls longer. If mid-draft you can't name what Take B does better, stop and find it before finishing; a reimagining with no theory of surpassing is expert-soup waiting to happen.

### Step 4: Flow Gate both takes
Run the full 5-item Flow Gate from `references/flow-and-cadence.md § The Flow Gate` on Take A AND Take B independently — including the read-aloud, cold, at the reader's pace. A take that fails gets revised before delivery, not delivered with a caveat. Optionally run `prose_classifier.py check` on both; remember CS-02's lesson — gates measure the floor, the felt read measures the ceiling.

### Step 5: Deliver side-by-side — the felt verdict decides
Present **original + Take A + Take B**, whole, in that order — never a diff, never a summary of changes in place of the text. Then a 3-line honest comparison: what each take trades, no thumb on the scale. "As soon as you know you have somebody emotionally, that's when you move on in the pitch... We're not going to overexplain" — make the case in three lines and stop; the operator's gut does the rest. The original is never silently replaced: no verdict, no swap. Log the verdict (`/jam` taste-ledger pattern; append to the piece's CS entry or create one). If the verdict is "Take A, but the close from B," that's a fourth artifact — assemble it, re-gate it, and log that too.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| Substack / long-form (CS-01 class) | Take A works the listed elevation doors from the CS entry first — they're pre-scouted. Take B must re-earn the named promise; a reimagining that drops the closing contract loses to the original on arrival. |
| Short-form social / Threads (CS-02 class) | Compression doctrine binds both takes: cut adjectives and credentials, never connectors. Take B is often the stronger play here — a jolty compressed post usually needs a new single subject-thread, not patches. Hand ≥500-char refinement verdicts to `writers-room`. |
| Live pitch / spoken | Take A tightens truncation points (where buy-in lets you cut). Take B may restructure which sections exist. Both takes get the read-aloud gate at performance pace — spoken work has no silent-scan loophole. |
| Sales / landing copy | The promise and offer are load-bearing: Take B reimagines the story around them, never the claim itself. Compliance constraints bind both takes identically. |
| Client work | Both takes go to the client's operator as options with the 3-line comparison — never ship a rewrite of approved work as if it were the approved work. |

**Worked micro-example** (a Threads post from the CS-02 rewrite): Survivor #1 = the recognition hook "You bought the courses. You know what to do. You still haven't done it." Take A fronted the echo word, restored the severed "the how," raised Pillar 4 by giving the loop one concrete withheld content. Take B rebuilt the post on a single subject-thread (the gap, start to finish) with the progressive-overload metaphor carrying the teach. Comparison: Original keeps the raw credential punch but jolts; A keeps the exact hook and now flows; B pulls harder but softens the disqualification edge. Verdict: Farrice's gut — pending, logged either way.

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric. Specifically flag: **expert-soup** (more than one author voice in either take — the CS-00 flattening, `references/case-studies.md § CS-00`: judges 7.25–8.6, Farrice 3/10), **silent replacement** (any delivery where the original isn't presented whole beside the takes), **differ-not-surpass** (a Take B with no nameable theory of what it does better), and **naming the machinery** (pillar labels or move names visible inside either take's prose).
- Both takes trace every survivor from Step 1 — kept, or surpassed by something named.
- Take A changed nothing structural; Take B started from nothing but the north star. If the takes converge, one of them cheated.
- The 3-line comparison names a real trade for each version — a comparison where one take has no cost is a sales pitch, not a comparison.
- The verdict is the operator's felt read, and it's logged. No log, no elevation — the ledger is how the next session inherits taste.

**FLOW GATE** (from `references/flow-and-cadence.md § The Flow Gate` — run on BOTH takes; one fail = revise before delivery):
- [ ] **Contour varies.** No run of 3+ same-length sentences; short, medium, AND long; long sentences arrive only after the reader is rested.
- [ ] **Every sentence hands off.** Opens on something the reader holds, ends on the new thing; no subject swap without a bridge; every *it/this/that* binds instantly.
- [ ] **Loops are engineered, not asserted.** Promise + withhold + stake, primed first; 3-persona prediction test run; every opened loop resolves honestly.
- [ ] **Survives read-aloud.** Cold, out loud, at the reader's pace — zero stumbles, stalls, or backtracks.
- [ ] **Compression kept causality.** Every cut preserved the but/so/which-is-why chain; no wrong-antecedent pronouns or amputated lists.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Skipping Step 1 because the piece "obviously" needs work.** A blind rewrite tramples micro-decisions it never inventoried — the exact failure the case-study ledger exists to prevent. Recovery: run `/bw-anatomy-audit`, name the survivors, then cut.
- **Letting Take A creep into a rebuild.** Four passes in, the spine is gone and you've written a bad Take B with the original's fingerprints. Recovery: revert to the original, re-run with the three-pass ceiling honored.
- **Take B written by committee.** Pulling one expert for the hook, another for the body, a third for the close reads as stitched — the CS-00 flattening (`references/case-studies.md § CS-00`: judges 7.25–8.6, Farrice 3/10). Recovery: one named author rewrites alone; other lenses may critique after, never draft.
- **Declaring a winner yourself.** The workflow produces takes and an honest comparison; it does not produce the verdict. Recovery: strip the recommendation, present all three whole, wait for the gut. Offer a `/jam` if the operator wants to trade takes live.
- **Elevating without logging.** An unlogged verdict teaches the system nothing and invites re-litigating the same piece next month. Recovery: append the verdict and one taste-line to the CS entry before closing the session.
