# The Three C's — The Titanic Test (Ben Watkins)
Ask Watkins how a storyteller *creates* emotion instead of hoping for it and he goes straight to the same scene: *"Can you get Titanic where you have two characters you care about? That's the characters professing their love. That's the content. While the ship is sinking, there's your circumstance. If you get all those three working, then you're gonna have Titanic."* That's the whole test. Lovers talking is a nice moment. A ship going down is a special-effects reel. Both alone are facts. All three firing *at the same instant* is the most-remembered scene in cinema — and the difference between the audience *watching* your piece and *being inside* it. This workflow takes something flat and rebuilds one beat until the three coincide.

## Input Required
- The emotionally flat piece, or the single scene/beat you're building (paste it — the actual words, not a summary)
- What you *want* the audience to feel at that beat (one emotion: grief, dread, awe, vindication — name it, don't hedge)
- Who the audience cares about here (the character carrying the feeling — a person, a founder, the customer, the brand-as-protagonist)
- The stakes in play right now (what could be lost or won in this exact moment)

## Workflow

### Step 1: Inventory the three C's actually present in the beat
Take the beat as written and sort what's on the page into the three buckets. Watkins is blunt that a fact isn't a feeling: *"people who state a fact, that's not enough cuz I'm not making this decision based on logic. I need you to tap into my emotion."* So audit honestly — most flat beats have exactly one or two C's and are running on the belief that clarity equals feeling. It doesn't.

| C | Definition | What's in THIS beat | Present? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content** | What's happening / being said right now — the action, the line, the exchange | | YES / NO |
| **Character** | Who we care about *as this happens* — someone with skin in it, not a stranger | | YES / NO |
| **Circumstance** | The stakes / situation pressing in — what's at risk, the *but* under the scene | | YES / NO |

Name each C in one phrase. If you can't fill a cell, that C is missing — write "MISSING," don't fudge it with something vaguely related.

### Step 2: Test simultaneity — are they firing at once, or sequenced?
This is the move everyone skips, and it's the whole thing. *"If you get all those three working"* means **coincident**, not *in a row*. The most common Pillar-2 failure is a character beat, then later a stakes beat — three good moments in sequence — and the operator wonders why it stays cold. Pull the C's apart and each one deflates. Run this table on the beat you have:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| At the single peak moment, which C's are live *simultaneously*? | |
| Are any C's sequenced (one, then later another) instead of overlapping? | |
| If you deleted the circumstance, does the content still carry weight — or is it just a pleasant moment? | |
| Diagnosis | ☐ All three coincide (Titanic) ☐ Two coincide ☐ Sequenced ☐ One C carrying alone |

The tell: **two C's alone is a fact; three simultaneous is a feeling.** Lovers professing love *while* the deck tilts. Not the couple in scene 4 and the flood in scene 9. If your diagnosis is anything but "all three coincide," Step 3 is mandatory.

### Step 3: Rebuild ONE beat so all three fire at the same instant
Don't add more scenes. Take the single highest-stakes moment and collapse the C's onto it — force the content to happen *inside* the circumstance, *to* the character. Watkins's whole quarrel with flat work: *"I don't want you to just see my resume. I want you to see me."* A described situation is a resume. A moment where the thing said, the person saying it, and the ground giving way land together — that's being seen.

The rebuild move, concretely:
- **Find the sinking ship.** What is the circumstance's ticking clock or falling axe? Put it in the same breath as the content — not as backstory before, not as consequence after.
- **Make the content cost something.** The line/action should be harder, riskier, or more tender *because* of the circumstance. The stakes should press on the exact words.
- **Anchor it to the character we're rooting for.** The audience feels it only if someone they care about is standing where it lands.

Keep subtraction live while you do this (`genius.md § The Master's Secret Is Subtraction`) — the rebuild tightens the beat, it doesn't bloat it. One coincident moment beats three explained ones. Remember the altitude, too: this is *sustained* feeling, not the first-beat attention spike — the show's host draws the line in the back-half breakdown, *"this is about keeping people emotionally invested from the beginning to the end."* You're not jolting them once; you're holding them inside it.

Produce the **before/after**: the flat beat as written, then the rebuilt beat where content × character × circumstance coincide — machinery invisible, no labels in the output prose.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| **Short-form social (Reels/LinkedIn/X)** | You get one beat, so all three C's ride the same 2–3 lines. The founder (character) admitting the number scared him (content) *the week payroll was due* (circumstance) — one sentence, three C's. Don't spend line 1 on character and line 6 on stakes; at this length a sequenced C is half the post wasted. |
| **Long-form (Substack/YouTube/essay)** | You have room to build each C, but the *payoff paragraph* must collapse them. Set up the character and stakes across the piece, then engineer the climax beat where the decision (content) is made by the person we've followed (character) with everything on the line (circumstance) in the same moment. Readers should feel it land, not be told it mattered. |
| **Live pitch / sales / VSL** | The Titanic beat is the emotional turn of the pitch — the moment the buyer feels the stakes *on a specific person* while the transformation is happening. Don't state the ROI (fact) then, separately, tell a warm story. Fuse them: the customer (character) hitting the wall (circumstance) the moment your mechanism turns it (content). That's the beat they call their spouse about. |
| **Brand / campaign** | The hero asset carries one coincident beat, not a mood reel. The brand-as-protagonist (or the customer as hero) faces the real circumstance while the brand's promise is enacted — all in one frame/scene. A campaign that shows lifestyle (content) and, elsewhere, a value prop (circumstance) with no character to root for is spectacle. Stack the C's into the single image that does the feeling. |

## Quality Gate
> 🛡️ **Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (row: *The 3 C's (Pillar 2)*). Flag and fix before delivering.
- **All three C's are live at the SAME instant** in the rebuilt beat — not a character moment then a stakes moment. If you can delete the circumstance and the content still reads fine, they're sequenced, not stacked. Redo Step 3.
- **A real character carries it.** The feeling lands on someone the audience is rooting for, not a stranger or an abstraction. Two C's without character is spectacle.
- **The machinery is invisible.** The AFTER prose never labels "here's the content, here's the stakes." The audience feels the beat; they never see the three buckets. Any on-page labeling breaks the spell — rewrite.
- **It's a feeling, not a fact.** Hold the line against `source-quotes.md`: if the beat "states a fact" and hopes, it fails. Name the specific emotion it now causes.
- **Subtraction held.** The rebuild is tighter than the original, not padded with a third scene. Coincidence, not accumulation.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Sequencing the C's and calling it done.** Building a lovely character beat and a big stakes beat back to back — three moments in a row, none coincident. Recovery: pick the single peak and collapse all three onto that one instant; the feeling lives in the overlap, never in the sequence.
- **Circumstance as spectacle with no one to root for.** A ship sinks, an empire falls, the market crashes — and the audience shrugs because there's no character standing in it. Recovery: run `/bw-character-canvas`, put a person we care about at the exact spot the axe falls, then re-fire the beat.
- **Adding instead of stacking.** "It's flat, so I'll add more backstory / another proof point / a second scene." Recovery: subtraction — cut to the one beat and force the C's to coincide there; total clarity kills the feeling, and a third scene dilutes the one that could have landed.
- **Fixing attention when the problem is sustain.** Bolting a louder hook onto a piece that's flat in the *middle*. The Pillar-1 spike stops the scroll; the 3 C's keep them inside it. Recovery: diagnose the altitude — if they watch the open then drift, it's a Pillar-2 sustain failure, so rebuild the coincident beat, don't re-tune the hook. Stack `emotional-value` and `stanton-opposites-engine` on the stakes.
