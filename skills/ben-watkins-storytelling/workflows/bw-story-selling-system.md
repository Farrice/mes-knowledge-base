# The Story-Selling System (Ben Watkins)
Before Watkins was a showrunner he maxed two nearly-dead credit cards to buy a plane ticket to New York for a *general meeting* — no role, no audition, just a casting director agreeing to look at his face. He went anyway, because the only asset a nobody owns is the story of how much he believes. That bet got him the audition. Later, when a deal died on a Sunday and the house went into foreclosure, the thing that carried him back was the same instinct: get the room to feel the why before they weigh the facts. At the end of *The Anatomy of a Dream* the host stops admiring the pieces and does the thing this workflow exists to do: *"I actually want to break down Ben's storytelling principles into the three different areas that we would use them. And that is for your brand, for your pitch, and for your content."* That's the whole conductor in one line. Watkins never separated the writers' room from the pitch room — *"People buy based on emotion, not logic"* is a storytelling claim and a selling claim at once — so this system takes one message and runs it out across brand, pitch, and content without ever telling three different stories. His blunt version of why it matters: *"the only way to get money is to tell the right story."* This workflow doesn't write any of the three. It composes the specialists that do — North Star once, then brand, then pitch, then content — and keeps the same purpose, emotion, and character intact across the whole trip.

## Input Required
- The one core message or objective — the single thing this whole deployment is *for* (a launch, an offer, a positioning shift, a project you're selling)
- Which surfaces are actually in scope this run (default: all three — brand, pitch, content)
- The audience, best guess — we'll sharpen it to one person at one moment in `/bw-north-star`
- Any surface already partly built (an existing brand, a live deck, a content shelf) — flag it so the conductor composes *onto* it instead of overwriting
- The decision-maker or scroller you most need to move first, if there's a priority order

## Workflow

### Step 1: Lock the North Star ONCE — the fixed center all three surfaces swing from
Compose `/bw-north-star` a single time for the whole message. Do not let each surface invent its own promise — that's how a brand, a pitch, and a content set end up telling three unrelated stories. Answer Watkins's four questions once — *"what are you trying to say? Why are you saying this? Who is this for? And what are you promising the audience?"* — name the ONE emotion, and compress to the one-line promise. That brief is now the constitution. Everything below inherits it; nothing below re-litigates it.

Produce the locked brief and pin the three carry-through values every downstream surface must keep identical:

| Carry-through | Locked value (from /bw-north-star) | Non-negotiable across all 3 surfaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** (the why to infect them with) | | Same why on brand, pitch, and content |
| **The ONE emotion** (one word) | | Same feeling engineered on all three |
| **The character** (who + where in their arc) | | Same person, same moment, everywhere |

If you can't fill this table, you don't have a message yet — you have material. Return to `/bw-north-star` before touching a surface.

### Step 2: BRAND layer — compose the log line + the brand-world
Build the surface people *join* before they ever buy. Compose `/bw-log-line` to force the whole message into one clean sentence with the customer as hero and the brand as the bridge — the subtraction test Watkins lives by, *"the ability to know what to leave out,"* applied until the message survives as a single line someone else could repeat after hearing it once. Then compose `/bw-brand-world` to turn that line into a world with recurring characters, sets, and stories (the world-building overlay — Caleb Ralston's frame, not Watkins's; keep that attribution clean). Produce: the one-sentence log line + the three brand-world recurring assets, both carrying the Step-1 emotion and character unchanged. Do not restate the North Star as brand copy; the brand *is* the promise made into a place, never the promise announced.

### Step 3: PITCH layer — compose the live pitch
Build the surface where the message gets *sold* to one decision-maker in real time. Compose `/bw-pitch-room`: run the four pillars as a sell, assume a cold, tired room, and engineer the state-flip Watkins banks on — *"if I flip the script, I just became the best part of your day."* Produce a modular pitch built to truncate on buy-in, with the same purpose and emotion from Step 1 as the thing you want repeated. The KPI is his, not yours: *"the first thing they should do leaving that room is call their boss or call their wife or their husband or grab a co-worker and be talking about the pitch they just heard."* If the pitch would make them call someone about a *different* feeling than the brand delivers, the layers have split — fix it here, not later.

### Step 4: CONTENT layer — compose the hook bank + a finished piece
Build the surface that has to stop a scroll at volume. Compose `/bw-hook-forge` for a bank of hooks that break the pattern — *"as soon as you break the pattern, people perk up"* — each opening the curiosity gap at the first beat and mirroring the Step-1 character so precisely they feel called by name. Then compose `/bw-story-anatomy` to build one finished piece all the way through the four pillars, carrying the same emotion. Produce: the hook bank + one built piece. Same person, same feeling, same why as the brand and the pitch — the content is the message told to the many, not a new message.

### Step 5: Coherence check — one purpose, one emotion, one character across all three
This is the conductor's real job — the only reason to run the whole system instead of three separate workflows. Skip it and you didn't conduct anything. Lay the three surfaces side by side and audit them against the Step-1 carry-through table. The test isn't "are they all good" — each specialist already gated that. The test is whether it's the same story told three ways. Anything that drifts gets bent back to the center or cut, per Watkins's discipline of subtraction — off-center is off-center no matter how good the asset is.

| Carry-through | Brand | Pitch | Content | Coherent? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Purpose (why)** | | | | YES / DRIFT → fix |
| **The ONE emotion** | | | | YES / DRIFT → fix |
| **The character / moment** | | | | YES / DRIFT → fix |

Where a row reads DRIFT, name which surface broke and bend it back — don't move the North Star to accommodate a clever asset. Deliver only when all three rows read YES.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| **Short-form social (Reels / LinkedIn / X)** | The content layer carries the weight; brand and pitch are thin. Compose `/bw-hook-forge` heavily, `/bw-brand-world` as a recurring-format signature only, `/bw-pitch-room` collapsed to a single CTA beat. Coherence check is the recurring character across a *series* of posts — same person addressed at the same moment every time, so the feed reads as one world. |
| **Long-form (Substack / YouTube / essay)** | Content and brand fuse — the piece *is* the brand-world episode. Compose `/bw-story-anatomy` full, `/bw-brand-world` for the recurring set/format the reader recognizes, `/bw-pitch-room` only if it ends in an offer. The promise runs the whole runtime; coherence = the reader finishes feeling the Step-1 emotion without ever being told the message. |
| **Live pitch / sales / VSL** | Pitch layer leads; brand and content are proof props inside it. Compose `/bw-pitch-room` full, `/bw-log-line` as the one sentence they repeat in the car, `/bw-hook-forge` for the cold open. Coherence check is the truncate list — every section you'd drop on buy-in must leave the core emotion intact, so cutting never breaks the story. |
| **Brand / campaign** | All three run at full weight and the coherence check is the deliverable. Compose the full stack; then audit every asset across the campaign against the one promise. The "clever" asset that wins applause while proving a *different* emotion is exactly what Step 5 cuts. Stack `oren-brand` / `grace-andrews` for positioning and `satori-graphics` for the visual system so the world *looks* like one place. |

## Quality Gate
> 🛡️ **Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (esp. *Purpose / North Star* and the whole-message coherence). Flag and fix before delivering.
- **The North Star was locked ONCE** and all three surfaces inherit the same purpose, emotion, and character. If any surface invented its own promise, you told three stories — redo Step 1 and re-derive.
- **The conductor composed, it didn't duplicate.** Each layer is the output of its own /bw workflow, not this file re-writing a hook or a pillar from scratch. If you wrote the brand copy here instead of composing `/bw-brand-world`, you bypassed the specialist.
- **The coherence check has all three rows reading the same emotion** — not three good-but-different feelings. One message means one feeling on every surface; a DRIFT row shipped un-fixed is a failed conductor.
- **The machinery stays invisible in every surface** — no asset labels "here's our brand-world" or "here's the surprise hook." The audience feels one coherent world; they never see the seams between brand, pitch, and content.
- **Attribution is clean** — the two overlays this conductor actually composes stay credited: world-building to Caleb Ralston (inside `/bw-brand-world`), the log line to Blake Snyder (inside `/bw-log-line`); the four pillars, 3 C's, and subtraction stay Watkins's. If a downstream layer pulls in but-and-therefore, that's Trey Parker & Matt Stone — but this conductor never authors it. Don't let the conductor blur the line the references keep.
- **One thing got cut in Step 5** — a real asset that was good but pulled toward a different emotion. A coherence check that spares everything didn't check anything.
- **Flow Gate** (references/flow-and-cadence.md § 4): contour varies, every sentence hands off (known-new), loops engineered not asserted, survives read-aloud, compression kept causality — one fail = revise.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Re-writing instead of composing.** The conductor starts producing hooks and pitch lines itself rather than calling `/bw-hook-forge` and `/bw-pitch-room`, and the specialists' gates never fire. Recovery: for each layer, invoke the named /bw workflow and pull its output; this file only locks the center and audits coherence.
- **Three North Stars wearing one hat.** Each surface quietly sharpens the promise its own way until brand, pitch, and content chase different emotions. Recovery: freeze the Step-1 carry-through table before building any surface; if a layer wants a different emotion, the layer is wrong, not the North Star — bend it back.
- **Skipping the coherence check because each surface passed its own gate.** Three individually excellent assets that don't add up to one story still fail the whole reason to run a conductor. Recovery: never deliver on the specialists' gates alone — run Step 5's side-by-side audit and force every row to YES.
- **Stub composition.** Each layer's cell is a one-line summary instead of the real `/bw` sub-workflow output. Recovery: paste or link the actual sub-workflow deliverable into the layer; the coherence check audits real assets, not placeholders.
- **Running the conductor for a single-surface job.** Firing the full system when you only need a hook wastes the composition and dilutes focus. Recovery: if only one surface is in scope, call that single /bw workflow directly — the conductor is for when one message must land coherently on all three. As the host closes the interview: Ben *"wasn't just a writer, he was the showrunner of his own damn story... remember who is holding the pen."* The conductor is you holding it across all three surfaces at once.
