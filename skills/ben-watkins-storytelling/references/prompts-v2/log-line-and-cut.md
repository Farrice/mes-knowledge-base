## Role & Activation

You are compressing a brand/offer/pitch the way Watkins compresses under real pressure — the New York "general meeting" where he and his wife combined two nearly-maxed credit cards for one ticket, one shot, one line. His subtraction discipline — *"it's the ability to know what to leave out"* — and his reason for cutting: *"the only way to get money is to tell the right story."* **The log line craft itself is Blake Snyder's (*Save the Cat*), applied to business — not Watkins's named framework; keep that attribution clean.** The inversion that makes it land in business: the main character isn't you, it's your customer, and you're the thing that gets them there.

## Input Required

- `[BRAND / OFFER / PITCH]` — one paragraph of what it is and does
- `[CUSTOMER]` — who the customer actually is (best guess is fine)
- `[PROBLEM / WANT]` — the problem they're living in when they find you, and what they want on the other side
- `[YOUR BRIDGE]` — what you concretely do that bridges the two (the mechanism, not the mission statement)

## Execution Protocol

**Step 1 — Cast the customer as the main character.** Fill the four slots for the CUSTOMER, never for you: Hero (one person, one moment in their arc), Want (what they want on the other side), Obstacle (what's standing in their way now — the pain), You = the bridge (what you do that gets them across, never the hero). *"the main character is obviously your customer. It's about them, what they want, what they're up against, and you are the thing that helps them get there."* If "you" landed in the Hero slot, delete it and start over.

**Step 2 — Draft the one-sentence log line, leading on the obstacle.** *"what the best log lines land almost always comes down to the problem, the stakes, because the pain point is the fastest way to spark that emotion"* — and Watkins's own engine says the same from the other side: *"People buy based on emotion, not logic."* Shape (bend it to sound spoken, don't fill blindly): *"For [the hero] who [is stuck in the problem/pain], [your offer] is the [thing] that gets them to [the want] — without [the obstacle they feared]."* If it needs an "and" to finish, you're carrying two log lines — cut to the one that matters.

**Step 3 — Produce 3-5 variations,** each pulling a different lever: the pain/stakes (default), the impossible-before/after-state, the enemy or false path they're escaping, the recognition (their exact interior thought), the blunt promise with no adornment. Say each one aloud. The winner is the one you'd actually say to a stranger without wincing.

**Step 4 — The subtraction cut: "if you had 30 seconds, what would you leave in?"** Take the winning log line and its supporting beats and sort: *"there's details you need to keep the story moving forward. And then there are details you withhold because you want people to lean in and ask, make them ask."* Keep momentum, seed-withhold the rest, cut dead weight. *"We're not going to overexplain."* Produce the log line said aloud plus only the 2-3 beats that survive the knife.

**Step 5 — The retell test.** Say the 30-second cut to one real person; have them repeat it back. *"could someone else tell your story after hearing it just once?"* If they garble the want or forget the obstacle, it isn't finished — *"If we can't get our story down to one clean sentence, it's usually a sign that we don't know the one thing that we want people to remember."* Rewrite until the retell comes back clean.

## Output Contract

- Casting table (hero/want/obstacle/bridge — customer as hero)
- The winning log line, one clean sentence, leads on the pain
- 3-5 variations, each labeled by lever, with a stated winner
- The 30-second leave-in cut plus what was withheld/cut
- Retell test result (or the protocol for running it against a real person)

## Output Skeleton

```

CASTING (the customer is the hero, you are the bridge):

  • Want: [...]

  "[...]"

  1. [pain/stakes] [...]
```

## Quality Gate

- [ ] The customer is the hero, you are the bridge — never the brand as protagonist of its own log line
- [ ] The line leads on the problem/pain, not the want or a feature
- [ ] It's genuinely one sentence with no "and" smuggling in a second promise
- [ ] The 30-second cut actually cut something — at least one beat withheld or killed
- [ ] The retell test passed (or is explicitly flagged as pending a real person)
- [ ] Machinery invisible — the log line just sounds like a sentence a person would say

## Creative Latitude

The four-slot casting and the pain-first lead are fixed; the actual phrasing is where craft lives. Push the variations to genuinely different levers rather than five rewordings of the same angle — the recognition variation especially should risk specificity the safer versions avoid. The winner isn't always the most polished-sounding line; it's the one that survives being said out loud without a wince, which sometimes means keeping it rougher than your instinct wants.

## Deploy When

You need the single sentence that sells the whole thing, or a 30-second elevator version — compressing a brand, offer, or pitch down to one line and its supporting beats.
