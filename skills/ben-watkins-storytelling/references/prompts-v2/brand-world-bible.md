## Role & Activation

You are building a brand the way Watkins learned belief is the product long before anything else is — the maxed-cards bet before there was a show to sell. *"People buy based on emotion, not logic,"* and the emotion doesn't live in the product, it lives in the world the product lets you belong to. **The film/TV framing for brand — "treat your brand like it's a film or a TV show" — is Caleb Ralston's overlay, cited in-source; it is NOT Watkins's own frame. Never restyle it as his.** The underlying instinct it applies — that audiences "aren't showing up for a product, they're showing up for a world they want to be a part of" — is the show's synthesis of Watkins's emotion-over-logic principle.

## Input Required

- `[BRAND]` — name, what it sells, who runs it (the founder/faces on camera)
- `[NORTH STAR]` — say/why/who/promise, if it exists (run first if missing — a brand with no governing purpose can't have a world built around it)
- `[EXISTING RECURRING ELEMENTS]` — a face, a set, a catchphrase, a format that already repeats, if any
- `[CONTENT SURFACES]` — short-form, long-form, live/sales, campaign

## Execution Protocol

**Step 1 — Define the world.** Before any recurring asset, name the ethos, the room, the identity the audience joins by showing up — not the product, the belonging. Apple didn't sell rebels a computer, it sold them a side. Write the world as a place someone wants to be a part of, then pressure-test it against *"People buy based on emotion, not logic"* — if the world reads as a feature list, it's a spec sheet, not a world. Apply subtraction here first: a world the brand is *for* is one clean thing, not five.

**Step 2 — Cast the recurring characters.** A world needs people you come back for: *"Mr Beast has friends in the rest of his crew that you see in his videos... They're characters in a world that you've come to know and love."* Give each recurring face a role, a running trait, and a reason the audience roots for them. The founder is a character too, not a narrator. Names optional; a recognizable role is mandatory.

**Step 3 — Build the recurring sets.** Lock the places the world happens: *"Think about Diary of a CEO. The lighting and the signature interview setup. You know exactly where you are within seconds."* Define 1-3 sets and what makes each unmistakable inside two seconds. The test: the *Friends* purple-backdrop picture frame — strip away the words and the set alone still names the brand.

**Step 4 — Fix the signature visuals and formats.** The motifs recognized before a word is spoken: a color, a frame, an object, a title card, a sound (the visual motif) AND a repeating format — the signature structure the audience subconsciously recognizes before it starts. This is Pillar-1 attention working at the brand level.

**Step 5 — Run the association audit.** The three brand questions: what stories does the audience associate with you now, who are the recurring characters, what are the recurring locations/visuals/formats people subconsciously recognize. Name the gap between what's associated now and what the north star wants associated, the one recurring story to compound, and any recurring element to cut because it dilutes the world.

## Output Contract

- The world in one line + the one emotion of belonging + who's inside it + the one thing it's for
- At least one recurring character, one recurring set, one signature visual/format, each with a stated recognition trigger
- Association audit (now vs. wanted vs. gap vs. one to compound vs. cuts)
- Attribution line (world-building framing = Caleb Ralston; the recurring-asset examples = the show; emotion-over-logic/north-star/subtraction/know-them-like-a-character = Watkins)

## Quality Gate

- [ ] The world is a place people want to belong to, not a product or feature list
- [ ] At least one recurring character, one set, and one signature format/visual are each defined with a stated recognition trigger
- [ ] The 2-second test passes: strip the words and the set/motif alone still names the brand
- [ ] Subtraction held — the world is one clean thing; anything blurring it got cut
- [ ] The association audit names a real gap and a real cut, not just a summary
- [ ] Attribution is clean — the film/TV world-building frame is credited to Caleb Ralston, never presented as Watkins's own

## Creative Latitude

Which recurring elements to invent versus surface from what already exists is where taste lives — a bible that merely catalogs existing assets without pushing toward a sharper, more unmistakable version of each one is incomplete. Push the 2-second test hard: if a set or motif needs a caption to be recognized, it isn't done, no matter how attached the brand is to it. The association audit's "one story to compound" should be a genuine, sometimes uncomfortable narrowing — not the safest, most flattering story the brand already tells about itself.

## Deploy When

Building or leveling up a brand that reads as scattered posts, not a show people follow — turning a brand into a world with recurring characters, sets, and signature formats.
