# Ben Watkins — Roster Stacking & Routing Guide

> Load this when you're deciding *whether Ben is even the right expert*, or *who he stacks with*. The roster is crowded with storytelling masters; Watkins owns exactly one seam — **engineering emotion to sell an idea live and to stop the scroll, plus the subtraction discipline that decides what to leave out.** Route him there. Route him nowhere else. This guide expands `genius.md § Key Differences from Other Storytelling Masters` into a working router — the triggers that call Ben, the triggers that call somebody else, and the concrete chains where Ben's layer sits on top of a partner's. His own words are the anchor throughout, because the router is only as clean as the lane it keeps.

Watkins's whole positioning is that he never separated the writers' room from the pitch room. "People buy based on emotion, not logic" is a storytelling claim *and* a selling claim at the same time. So the routing rule is simple: **if the job is to make a decision-maker feel something and act — pitch, deck, hook, brand-as-a-show, the cut — Ben leads. If the job is to build the template, uncover the premise, or write the converting sentence, someone else leads and Ben stacks on.**

## 1. Route Ben When…

Fire `/ben-watkins` (or the specific workflow) when the task is *commercial storytelling to a live or scrolling audience*. The tells:

| Trigger | Why it's Ben | The move he brings |
|---|---|---|
| **Selling an idea live** — a room, a call, an investor, a greenlight, a client sit-down | His method was forged in the network pitch room, the highest-stakes sell there is. "When I actually go in to sell something now it's all about the emotion." | Infect-with-purpose, read-the-room, truncate-on-buy-in, riff-for-ownership |
| **A pitch, deck, or elevator pitch** that has to land emotionally, not just inform | His KPI: "the first thing they should do leaving that room is call their boss or call their wife or their husband or grab a co-worker and be talking about the pitch they just heard." | The four-pillar anatomy applied to a sell; the log-line compression (Snyder overlay) |
| **Stopping the scroll** — a hook, cold open, first ten seconds | "As soon as you break the pattern, people perk up." The three-way hook (surprise / emotion / call-them-by-name) is his native ground. | Pattern-break, recognition, the open loop at the hook |
| **Engineering a specific emotion into a message** that's currently flat or fact-stacked | "I don't want you to just see my resume. I want you to see me." The 3 C's rescue. | Content × Character × Circumstance *simultaneous* — the *Titanic* stack |
| **Brand-as-a-show** — turning a brand into a world with recurring characters, sets, and stories | The brand layer of his synthesis: audiences "aren't showing up for a product, they're showing up for a world they want to be a part of." | The four pillars aimed at brand association (world-building overlay → Caleb Ralston) |
| **The subtraction cut** — a story/pitch/post that's bloated, over-clear, rambling | "The difference between a good storyteller and a master storyteller… it's the ability to know what to leave out." | Seed-and-withhold: sort every detail into momentum (keep) or seed (withhold so they lean in and ask) |

If the request is any of these, don't shop the roster — Ben is the lane. The one qualifier: he's for the *sell and the scroll*, one person or one piece at a time. The moment the altitude changes (a template, a whole org, a written funnel, the deep identity map), read Section 2.

## 2. Route Elsewhere When…

Ben is a room operator, not a template, not a craft-uncovering tool, not a copywriter, not an audience-research engine. Where the job is really one of those, hand off — and, usually, stack Ben back on afterward.

| Route to… | When the job is… | Why not Ben |
|---|---|---|
| **Donald Miller / StoryBrand** (`storybrand`) | Building the fixed brand-message **template** — customer-as-hero, brand-as-guide, the one-liner, the BrandScript | Ben is not a template. He's the four pillars, the 3 C's, subtraction, read-the-room. He *sells* the message Miller frames; he doesn't hand you the 7-part fill-in. |
| **Andrew Stanton** (`andrew-stanton`) | **Narrative craft** — uncovering the true premise, the moment-to-moment engagement clamp, "make a promise this'll be worth it" architecture | Stanton owns story-*uncovering* and the engagement machinery for the piece itself. Ben owns the *commercial application* — pitching it, hooking it, selling it. Both prize "make them feel"; they operate at different altitudes. |
| **Luke Iha / copy roster** (`luke-iha-copy-blocks`, `georgi-copy`, `stefan-georgi`) | **Written direct-response** — awareness ladders, proof mechanisms, VSL blocks, the converting sentence on the page | They own *written* DR. Ben owns *spoken/live* pitching and the story anatomy that sits above the copy. His pillars set the arc; Luke writes the words inside it. |
| **David McRaney / ICP deep-canvass** (`mcraney-deep-canvass`, `icp-deep-dive`) | **Identity-level audience depth** — mapping resistance, belief, and the interior of a customer before you speak | Ben's "know them like a character" is the craft-side want/need/nemesis version. For deep identity resistance mapping, canvass first — then Ben turns their profile into the line that lands. |
| **Michael Margolis** (`michael-margolis`) | **Organizational** belief-change and change management — moving a *company* through transformation | Different altitude entirely. Ben operates at the individual pitch and the single piece of content, not the enterprise narrative. |
| **Shaan Puri** (`shaan-puri`) | The 5-second **viral moment of change** and extreme compression, with no live sell attached | Overlap on hooks; Ben adds the full four-pillar anatomy *and* the pitch room Puri doesn't touch. On pure short-form virality, Puri can lead and Ben stacks the pillar structure. |
| **Kallaway** (`kallaway-illusion`, `kallaway-content`) | **Content psychology** — addictive perception, the illusion of novelty, dopamine loops | Kallaway sharpens the *novelty and the loop*; Ben sets the *anatomy and the sell*. Stack, don't substitute. |

The discipline: don't ask Ben to do a template's job or a copywriter's job just because storytelling is in the request. "A lot of people want to outsource storytelling… but the truth is, you got to master it first" — the roster is that mastery, split by altitude. Keep the split clean.

## 3. Cross-Expert Stack Chains

This is where the value is. Ben rarely works alone on a real deliverable — he's the emotional-arc-and-sell layer that sits on top of a partner's structural or written layer. Concrete pairings, each with **Ben's layer** and the **partner's layer** named:

### Miller frames → Ben sells it in the room
- **Partner (StoryBrand):** produces the fixed message — customer-as-hero, the guide, the one-liner, the plan.
- **Ben's layer:** takes that clean message into the room and *makes them feel it and act*. He runs it through the pillars, reads the mood ("if I flip the script, I just became the best part of your day"), truncates on buy-in ("as soon as you know you have somebody emotionally, that's when you move on"), and aims for "call their boss the second they leave."
- **Use when:** you have a solid brand message that keeps *informing* but not *closing*.

### Stanton architects and clamps → Ben pitches and hooks it
- **Partner (Stanton):** digs the true premise, builds the engagement clamp, makes the piece's craft airtight.
- **Ben's layer:** takes the architected piece and engineers the *commercial front end* — the three-way hook that stops the scroll, the open question at the first beat, the pitch that sells the whole thing to a buyer.
- **Use when:** the story is well-built but nobody's leaning in yet, or it has to be *sold* to a decision-maker, not just read.

### Ben's pillars set the arc → Luke Iha writes the converting copy inside it
- **Ben's layer:** locks the emotional arc — the north star (say / why / who / promise), the one emotion, the 3 C's firing simultaneously, the open loop. "The way we elicit emotion is through telling stories."
- **Partner (Luke Iha / copy roster):** writes the actual DR sentences — hooks, proof blocks, awareness-ladder copy, VSL beats — *inside* Ben's arc so the words convert without losing the feeling.
- **Use when:** a written asset (ad, VSL, email, landing page) needs both a story spine and DR firepower.

### Ben's brand-world → Oren / Grace / brand-system-builder / Satori supply positioning + visual system
- **Ben's layer:** the brand-as-a-show — recurring characters, recurring sets, the story audiences associate you with, the world "they want to be a part of."
- **Partners:** **Oren/Grace** (`oren-brand`, `grace-andrews`) supply high-end positioning and the strategic frame; **brand-system-builder / `build-bos`** supplies the operating structure; **Satori** (`satori-graphics`) executes the visual system that makes the world *look* like one place.
- **Use when:** the brand needs a narrative world *and* a positioning line *and* a coherent visual identity — Ben supplies the story, the partners supply the strategy and the look.

### Ben's audience-as-character → McRaney deep-canvass / ICP deep-dive supply the depth
- **Partner (McRaney / ICP):** produces the identity-level map — the customer's real fears, beliefs, resistances, where they are in their own arc.
- **Ben's layer:** converts that depth into the *line that lands* — "call them by their name" (recognition, not a name token), the exact interior state mirrored so precisely "they think it's saying that right to them."
- **Use when:** you have (or need) deep audience research and now have to turn it into copy or a pitch that makes one person feel personally addressed.

### Ben's proof-story → Luke Iha proof-ladder makes it stack
- **Ben's layer:** the *proof-as-story* — the "general meeting" reversal, the evidence-of-belief that reads as narrative, not résumé. The most persuasive fact is often how much *you* believe.
- **Partner (Luke Iha proof-ladder):** structures the proof into an escalating written ladder — mechanism, demonstration, testimonial, guarantee — so the belief-story is backed by a converting proof sequence.
- **Use when:** a pitch or page needs both an emotional proof-narrative and a rigorous proof architecture.

**Stacking rule (from `genius.md` and CLAUDE.md's no-forced-wiring feedback):** these are *options*, not a pipeline. Compose the chain the deliverable actually needs. Ben's layer is almost always the *last* pass before delivery — the emotional-front-end and the subtraction cut — because it's the layer that decides what the audience *feels* and what gets left out.

## 4. Lane-Keeping Note

Ben brushes the same ground as two roster members most often — Miller and Stanton. When routing feels ambiguous, hold to these crisp distinctions so the lane stays clean:

**Ben vs. StoryBrand (Miller).** Miller gives you a *thing to fill in* — a fixed 7-part template you can hand to a junior marketer. Ben gives you a *way to operate in a room* — moves that only fire live: reading the mood, truncating on buy-in, riffing for ownership, infecting with purpose. **Litmus:** if the ask is "what's the structure of our message," that's Miller. If the ask is "how do I make them *feel* this and *act*," that's Ben. Miller frames; Ben sells.

**Ben vs. Stanton.** Both live on "make them feel" and "leave them wanting more," which is why they blur. The split is altitude and direction. Stanton points *inward at the craft* — uncovering the premise that was always there, clamping engagement moment to moment, making the piece true. Ben points *outward at the buyer and the feed* — pitching the piece, hooking the scroll, selling the idea to someone who's "on their sixth pitch of the day." **Litmus:** if the work is *building or diagnosing the story itself*, Stanton. If the work is *selling or scrolling the finished thing*, Ben. Stanton architects; Ben pitches.

**The through-line for both:** where Ben overlaps, he's the *commercial* end of the seam. The instant a request is about the template, the premise, the written funnel, or the org, route to the specialist — and stack Ben back on for the sell, the hook, and the cut. That's the whole discipline: "becoming a master storyteller is almost like becoming a master in people," and mastering people is what Ben does at the point of the transaction. Keep him there, and the roster never trips over itself.
