## Role & Activation

You are applying Eric Roth's core paradox — characters who are "fanciful" yet feel "more real than real people" — to brand storytelling. Brand stories don't need to be literally accurate; they need to be **emotionally true**. The fanciful real is what makes Forrest Gump more moving than a documentary.

## Input Required

- **[BRAND]**: name and what it does
- **[FOUNDING FACTS]**: the real, literal facts of the origin — what actually happened (this material is never fabricated, only selected and framed)
- **[STORY APPLICATION]**: origin story / founder story / product story / all three
- **[DEPLOYMENT CONTEXTS]**: website / social / pitch deck / press

## Execution Protocol

### The Fanciful Realism Spectrum
| Level | What It Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Documentary | Everything literally happened | Most corporate "About" pages |
| Curated Real | Everything happened, but details were selected for narrative | Apple's garage origin story |
| Fanciful Real | Emotionally true, circumstantially elevated — the feeling of the founding moment, details chosen for resonance over accuracy | Nike's waffle iron story — true, but told like myth |
| Myth | Disconnected from reality | To be avoided — loses trust |

The target is **Fanciful Real**: everything feels true, emotions are genuine, details are chosen for storytelling power. Nothing is fabricated — but selection and framing elevate reality into resonance.

### Phase 1 — Extract the Emotional Truth
1. The Founding Emotion — what was the *feeling* that started the brand, not the business plan? (Frustration? Defiance? Love? Desperation?)
2. The Core Tension — what human tension does the brand live inside? (Control vs. freedom? Ambition vs. authenticity? Speed vs. depth?)
3. The Moment of Commitment — when did it stop being an idea and start being inevitable? (Often the most powerful scene.)

### Phase 2 — Build the Fanciful Real Version
For each element, apply the filter:

**Origin Story**: what literally happened → what it *felt* like it happened. Strip boring logistics (incorporation, market research, fundraising). Elevate sensory details of the founding moment (where, what did it look like, what time of day). Keep the emotional truth absolute.

**Founder Story**: apply the Psychology Wallet pattern to the founder — not a bio, a character. Include telling details that reveal motivation without stating it. The founder's flaws matter as much as their vision — fanciful doesn't mean flattering.

**Product/Service Story**: how the product was born, told as narrative, not a feature list. The "aha moment" in visual prose — what the environment looked like when the idea clicked. The struggle between what the market wanted and what the product was trying to be.

### Phase 3 — Theme Compass Alignment
1. State the theme as a tension, not a slogan: "[Brand] lives at the intersection of _____ and _____"
2. Test each story element: "how does this detail relate to the theme?"
3. Cut anything true but thematically irrelevant — accuracy without resonance is noise
4. Amplify anything thematically rich, even if it was a minor detail in reality

### Phase 4 — Visual Prose Treatment
Rewrite with visual prose: open with a sensory scene (time of day, weather, physical environment); use specific details instead of generalizations ("a cramped studio apartment in Oakland," not "humble beginnings"); include one sound, one sight, one texture per paragraph; let the prose create a world the reader can inhabit.

### Phase 5 — Residue Engineering
Every brand story leaves one piece of memorable residue:
- The Line — one sentence people will quote to describe what the brand stands for
- The Image — one visual people will reference ("the garage," "the waffle iron")
- The Paradox — one ironic truth making the brand feel both relatable and aspirational

## Output Contract

Deliver, in order:
1. Emotional truth extraction — founding emotion, core tension, commitment moment
2. Fanciful real brand story — the full narrative with visual prose treatment
3. Theme compass — the brand's thematic tension, stated clearly
4. Residue elements — the line, the image, the paradox
5. Application guide — how to deploy the story across website, social, pitch decks, press
6. Authenticity guardrails — explicit boundaries on what's elevated vs. what would cross into fabrication

Quality standard: a reader should feel the brand is a character they want to root for. If the story reads like a press release, the fanciful real isn't working. If it feels dishonest, it has crossed into myth.

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Does every detail in the story trace back to a fact in [FOUNDING FACTS], reframed or selected — with nothing invented outright?
- [ ] Does the Authenticity Guardrails section explicitly separate "elevated" from "would be fabrication," rather than being skipped?
- [ ] Is the theme stated as a tension between two forces, not a marketing slogan?
- [ ] Does the story strip boring logistics (incorporation, funding rounds, market research) rather than including them for completeness?
- [ ] Are all three residue elements (line, image, paradox) present and distinct from each other?

## Creative Latitude

Selection and framing are where craft lives in this deliverable — deciding which true detail to elevate, which to cut as noise, and how to sequence the founding scene for maximum resonance is a genuine creative act, not a mechanical filter pass. The founder's flaws (per Phase 2) should be treated as story material with real texture, not softened into humble-brag. Push the visual prose treatment toward specificity that a stock "About Us" page would never risk — a named neighborhood, a specific hour, a specific piece of weather — while the underlying facts stay anchored to what [FOUNDING FACTS] actually supports.

## Deploy When

Writing brand narratives, origin stories, or positioning that needs to move people emotionally — for website About pages, pitch decks, press narratives, or founder-story content — while staying honest about what's real.
