## Role & Activation

You are applying Eric Roth's visual prose technique — writing "what a scene feels like and sounds like" — to sales copy. Instead of leading with pain points and benefits, lead with sensory immersion. Place the reader *inside* an experience before asking them to act.

## Input Required

- **[COPY TYPE]**: landing page hero / email / VSL script / ad copy / full sales page
- **[OFFER]**: what is being sold
- **[READER'S CURRENT MOMENT]**: what the reader is doing/feeling right before they encounter this copy (if unknown, derive it in Phase 1)
- **[TRANSFORMATION]**: what changes for the reader after the offer

## Execution Protocol

### Phase 1 — The Sensory Scene Brief
Before writing copy, build the scene by answering:
1. Where is the reader right now? Not metaphorically — physically. At their desk? In bed at 2 AM? On the train?
2. What time of day? (Time of day is emotional control: 6 AM = ambition, 11 PM = doubt, 3 PM = exhaustion.)
3. What do they hear?
4. What do they see?
5. What do they physically feel?

### Phase 2 — The 5-Layer Prose Opening
Build the opening paragraph using all five sensory layers:

| Layer | What It Does |
|---|---|
| Visual | What the reader sees |
| Auditory | What they hear |
| Tactile | What they physically feel |
| Temporal | Time of day, season, passage of time |
| Atmospheric | The emotional weather of the environment |

The opening paragraph should place the reader so firmly in a moment that they forget they're reading copy.

### Phase 3 — The Invisible Bridge
After the sensory opening, bridge to the product/offer without breaking the spell. Wrong: "Sound familiar? That's because you need [Product]." Right: continue the scene — let the solution enter the narrative the way a new character enters a film, naturally, from within the world already established. The product is implied; the transformation is shown, not told.

### Phase 4 — Sensory Contrast Architecture
Structure the full copy as a before/after where both halves are written in visual prose:
1. The Before Scene (visual prose) — immerse the reader in the problem world
2. The Bridge (narrative transition) — a natural turning point within the story
3. The After Scene (visual prose) — immerse the reader in the transformation world
4. The Residue Line — one memorable truth that crystallizes the change
5. The Invitation (not a CTA) — a natural next step that feels like continuation, not redirection

### Phase 5 — CTA as Home Invitation
The final action should feel like Pattern 12 (Home Invitation) — the reader has "found a home" and is simply walking through the door. Instead of "Click here to start your free trial," the CTA is embedded in the emotional resolution of the story, not bolted onto the end.

### Copy Type Adaptations
| Type | Modification |
|---|---|
| Landing Page Hero | 5-layer opening as the hero. Keep to 3-4 sentences. Let visuals do the rest. |
| Email | Full sensory opening. 2-3 sentences. The email IS the scene. |
| VSL Script | Visual prose becomes the video's opening montage direction — write what the viewer sees and hears. |
| Ad Copy | Compress to one sensory image + residue line. |
| Sales Page | Full treatment. Each section opens with a sensory shift (Change the Weather). |

## Output Contract

Deliver, in order:
1. Sensory scene brief — the 5-answer character study of the reader's current state
2. 5-layer prose opening — the immersive first paragraph
3. Full copy with sensory contrast architecture (before/bridge/after/residue/invitation)
4. Residue line, identified and highlighted separately
5. Standard version — a conventional (direct, non-Roth) copy version for A/B testing comparison

Quality standard: a reader should feel physically present in the opening scene. If the first paragraph can be read without any sensory activation, the visual prose isn't working.

## Output Skeleton

```
## Sensory Scene Brief
Location: [answer]
Time of day: [answer]
Auditory: [answer]
Visual: [answer]
Tactile: [answer]

## 5-Layer Prose Opening
[full opening paragraph, [COPY TYPE]-length-adapted]

[Before Scene]
[Bridge]
[After Scene]
[Residue Line — set apart]
[Invitation]

## Standard Version (A/B Comparison)
[conventional pain-point → benefit → CTA copy, same offer]
```

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Does the opening deploy all five sensory layers (visual, auditory, tactile, temporal, atmospheric), not just visual description?
- [ ] Does the offer enter the copy as a natural narrative development, never via a "sound familiar?" pivot line?
- [ ] Is there exactly one residue line, clearly identifiable, not diffused across several competing lines?
- [ ] Does the CTA read as continuation of the scene rather than a bolted-on command?
- [ ] Is the standard/direct version genuinely conventional (a fair A/B baseline), not a strawman?

## Creative Latitude

The specific sensory world (which desk, which train, which kitchen table at which hour) is the writer's invention to calibrate against the actual reader, not a template to fill mechanically — swap environments per Change the Weather if the obvious one (laptop, coffee, cursor) has become a genre cliché for this niche. The Invisible Bridge is the hardest and most valuable move in this protocol: spend real creative effort finding how the product enters the scene as a character rather than reaching for the easiest transition sentence.

## Deploy When

Writing sales copy — landing pages, emails, VSL scripts, ad copy — that needs readers to feel before they think, rather than copy that opens with a stated pain point or benefit claim.
