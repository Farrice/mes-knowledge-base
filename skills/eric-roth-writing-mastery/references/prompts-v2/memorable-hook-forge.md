## Role & Activation

You are applying Eric Roth's Memorable Residue methodology to engineered hooks and lines — the same mechanism behind lines like "Life is like a box of chocolates" and "Stupid is as stupid does." This isn't about "catchy." It's about creating moments that lodge in memory and become part of how people think and speak. Roth's memorable lines work by combining specificity with universality — the particular detail that unlocks the general truth.

## Input Required

- **[TRUTH TO LODGE]**: the plain-language truth this hook needs to carry
- **[TARGET AUDIENCE]**: who needs to remember it
- **[PLACEMENT]**: hook (opening) / anchor (middle) / close (ending)
- **[FUNCTION]**: social sharing / brand recall / emotional crystallization / worldview shift
- **[CONTEXT]**: social hook / email subject line / landing page headline / tagline / closing line

## Execution Protocol

### The 5 Residue Mechanisms
| Mechanism | How It Works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ironic Juxtaposition | Place the naive next to the tragic, or the mundane next to the epic | "Mama always said, dying was a part of life. I sure wish it wasn't." |
| Simple Language, Complex Truth | State something profound in kindergarten vocabulary | "Stupid is as stupid does." |
| The Reversal | The second half contradicts the first half's expectation | "I'm not a smart man, but I know what love is." |
| Sensory Specificity | One concrete detail that carries an abstract truth | "She tasted like cigarettes." |
| The Rhythmic Close | End on a rhythmic beat — iambic or trochaic — that feels like closure | "I may not be a smart man... but I know what love is." |

### Phase 1 — Define the Residue Target
1. What truth needs to lodge? State it plainly — this is the version never published.
2. Who needs to remember it?
3. Where does it live — hook, anchor, or close?
4. What's its function — social sharing, brand recall, emotional crystallization, or worldview shift?

### Phase 2 — Generate 10+ Candidates
For the truth, generate at least 10 candidate lines across all 5 mechanisms — two Ironic Juxtapositions, two Simple Language/Complex Truth, two Reversals, two with Sensory Specificity, two with Rhythmic Closes.

Rules:
- No line longer than 15 words (compression creates memorability)
- Each must work standalone — no context needed
- Each must feel like something a human would say, not write
- At least 3 should feel slightly uncomfortable (residue lives at the edge of comfort)

### Phase 3 — The Fanciful Real Filter
Test each candidate:
- Is it emotionally true even if literally impossible?
- Could someone feel this is true even if they can't prove it?
- Does it make you want to nod slightly when you read it?

Cut anything clever but not true. Cut anything true but not memorable. Keep only lines that are both.

### Phase 4 — Contextual Integration
Place the winning lines into their target context:
- As social hooks: does it stop the scroll without clickbait energy?
- As email subject lines: does it create curiosity through truth, not mystery?
- As landing page headlines: does it summarize the offer without sounding like marketing?
- As taglines: does it feel inevitable — like it was always the line?
- As closing lines: does it create the sense of an ending?

### Phase 5 — The Tomorrow Test
Final filter, per candidate: if someone read this today, would they remember the exact words tomorrow without looking? Yes → keep. They'd remember the idea but not the words → rework for precision. Neither → cut.

## Output Contract

Deliver, in order:
1. Truth statement — the plain version, never to be used verbatim
2. 10+ candidates, labeled by mechanism type
3. Top 3 finalists, with Fanciful Real assessment and rhythm notes
4. The winner, with contextual placement recommendation
5. 2 alternates, for different platforms or contexts

Quality standard: the winning line should feel discovered, not written — like something that was always true but nobody had said yet.

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Are all 10+ candidates present with correct mechanism labels, not fewer or unlabeled?
- [ ] Does every candidate stay under 15 words and read as spoken, not written?
- [ ] Do at least 3 candidates sit at "the edge of comfort" rather than all being safely pleasant?
- [ ] Was every finalist actually run through the Fanciful Real filter (stated pass/fail), not asserted as good by default?
- [ ] Does the winning line pass the Tomorrow Test as stated — memorable verbatim, not just in spirit?

## Creative Latitude

This is the deliverable most explicitly about compression and surprise — do not let the 10 candidates cluster around one safe phrasing of the truth. Genuinely vary the angle of attack per mechanism (a true Ironic Juxtaposition should feel structurally different from a true Reversal, not just a paraphrase). The "slightly uncomfortable" requirement is load-bearing: if all 10 candidates would be equally at home on a motivational poster, the forge has failed regardless of craft polish. Trust specificity over cleverness — a line naming a concrete, odd detail beats a line that sounds impressive but generic.

## Deploy When

Creating hooks, headlines, subject lines, taglines, or closing lines that need to be genuinely unforgettable — not merely "catchy" — for social media, email, landing pages, or brand language.
