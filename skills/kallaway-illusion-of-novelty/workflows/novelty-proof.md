# /novelty-proof — Bullseye Proof via the Trust Ladder

Produces the proof beat for a manufactured-novelty piece: the evidence that makes the viewer believe the new reveal is true, framed as close to their own life as the honest facts allow. Fire this once Steps 1–3 are drafted (a new reveal exists, it maps to a wanted outcome, contrast is set) and the open question is "yes, but is it true?" Output is a top-rung proof line plus a one-rung-down fallback, both factually clean.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Answer before generating. If `../genius.md` is not already hot, load it now — bullseye proof is impossible without the avatar specificity and the Trust Ladder it carries.

1. **Who is the avatar, precisely?** Age, look, occupation, the specific problem, the specific situation they're in right now. Bullseye proof presupposes you know all four — "same age, same look, same problem, same situation" is the top-rung definition. A vague avatar caps you at the bottom rung (hidden-knowledge note in `../genius.md`). If you can't name these four, stop and run `./novelty-forge.md` Step 1 or pull the avatar payload from `kallaway-audience-obsession` first.
2. **What exactly is the claim being proven?** The proof must justify *the specific new reveal*, not a vague benefit. Lift the reveal line verbatim from Step 1.
3. **Are you (the creator) the demo?** If the creator IS a viewer-mimic — same age, look, problem — a result the creator personally got is top-rung. Note this; it changes which proof you reach for.
4. **What proof actually exists?** Inventory the real assets: a specific person you've worked with / know of, a generalized cohort result, a study/stat/award. You climb only as high as the *real* facts allow.

## Execution

The job is one decision repeated under a hard constraint: **reach the highest rung the truth supports, then frame it at the viewer.** Five moves.

### 1. Pin the avatar to four axes
Write them down explicitly — not "busy professionals," but the four coordinates a mimic-example has to match:

| Axis | Question | Example (B2B procurement manager) |
|---|---|---|
| Age / stage | How old / how senior? | mid-40s, 12 yrs in the role |
| Look / identity | How do they see themselves? | the dependable one who "never lets a PO slip" |
| Problem | The exact pain? | maverick spend nobody catches until the audit |
| Situation | The live context? | renewing a $400k contract this quarter, lean team |

Without these four, "that's me" is impossible — you cannot aim a bullseye at a blur.

### 2. Climb the Trust Ladder to the highest HONEST rung
Three rungs, top to bottom. Always reach for the top; drop only when the top isn't honestly available.

- **Top — Bullseye (1-to-1 viewer-mimic).** A single named-or-vivid example matching the avatar on all four axes. Or, if the creator is the demo, the creator's own result. Mechanism: *self-identification* — the viewer trusts a version of themselves getting the result. This is why in-demo creators win.
- **Middle — Warm Crowd.** The generalized "we / a bunch of people" — third-party but a proxy for the crowd the viewer sits in. Some identification, less specificity.
- **Bottom — Third-Party.** A study, stat, article, award. Works, but forces the viewer's imagination: *were those people like me? would it work for me?* More questions, weaker trust.

Pick the highest rung your real facts support. Then frame even that rung *at the viewer* — a warm-crowd line said as "people in your exact spot" beats a warm-crowd line said abstractly.

### 3. Generate at the highest rung + one fallback down
Write the top-honest-rung line. Then write the rung directly below it as a fallback (shorter platforms, skeptical audiences, or when the top line risks reading as too-good). Two lines, labeled, so the assembler can choose.

> **Templates — vary, never verbatim.** Genericizing these word-for-word is itself a tell.
> - Bullseye (creator-not-demo): "I had a [avatar age/role] who [exact problem/situation] — [specific, modest, true result]."
> - Bullseye (creator-is-demo): "I'm [avatar match]. I [exact problem]. I [did the thing] and [specific result I personally got]."
> - Warm Crowd: "We've watched a run of [avatar plural] do this — same [problem] going in, same [result] coming out."
> - Third-Party: "[Specific source] found [specific finding]." (Only honest, only sourced.)

### 4. Self-identification check
Read the line cold as the avatar. Would they say "that's me / that's my situation," not "that's nice for them"? If the example doesn't carry the avatar's age/look/problem/situation, it's not bullseye yet — re-aim it. Proximity beats authority: a relatable singular example outperforms an authoritative aggregate every time (`../genius.md` pattern 6).

### 5. HONESTY GATE (non-negotiable)
You climb with truth, never with a lie. If the bullseye example **does not exist**, you do not invent a needle-phobic patient or a "buddy your age." You drop a rung and use the warm crowd or the third-party source *honestly*. The illusion is of NOVELTY only — the proof must be REAL. Fabricated proof is the fastest loop-collapse and the one permanent trust-burn (`../genius.md` pattern 7). A real warm-crowd line beats a fabricated bullseye, always.

### Worked mini-example — same claim, bottom rung vs. top rung

**Topic:** gutter cleaning (about as inert as it gets). **Avatar:** late-50s homeowner, two-story colonial, proud of keeping the house "buttoned up," dreading a soffit-rot repair bill he's heard runs five figures.

**The claim being proven** (from Step 1+2): *"The damage gutters cause isn't the overflow you see — it's the slow fascia rot you don't, and it starts the season AFTER the leaves clog, not during."*

- **Bottom rung (third-party).** "A National Association of Home Builders study found fascia and soffit repairs average several thousand dollars." → True, fine, but the avatar's brain runs the imagination tax: *were those houses like mine? was their gutter situation like mine?* Belief leaks out through the questions.
- **Top rung (bullseye).** "I cleaned gutters for a guy your age, same two-story colonial, who swore his gutters were fine because they never overflowed — we pulled the downspout and found the fascia already soft behind it. He caught it the season before it would've turned into the repair he'd been dreading for years." → Same claim, but now it carries his age, his house, his exact false-confidence, his exact dread. He says *that's me*.

**The honesty fork:** if that homeowner-on-a-colonial job never happened, you do not invent him. You climb as high as the truth allows — maybe "most of the two-story jobs we pull, the owner's surprised what's behind a downspout that never overflowed" (warm crowd, real) — and frame *that* at the avatar instead. Higher honest rung beats a higher dishonest one.

## Content-Type Adaptations

The Trust Ladder is universal; how the proof beat is *staged* changes by asset. Each cell is the move, not a platitude.

| Asset | How the proof beat changes |
|---|---|
| **Short-form video script** | One proof line, spoken late as the trust-closer; reach top rung and shoot the *visual* mimic (B-roll of someone who looks like the avatar) so identification lands by eye, not just ear. No room for stacking — pick the single highest honest rung. |
| **LinkedIn post** | Proof = a 2–4 line micro-story of the bullseye example with one concrete, modest detail. Skip the study unless it's the *only* honest rung. First-person ("I worked with…") reads as warm and lands harder than third-party links, which feel like citations. |
| **X/Twitter thread** | Proof becomes its own tweet, usually the turn after the contrast tweet. Top rung as a vivid one-liner; the fallback third-party rung can be a follow-up tweet for the skeptics who reply "source?". Screenshots/visuals carry the mimic. |
| **Email** | Room to *narrate* the bullseye — name (or vivid stand-in), situation, the moment it turned. Proof can run a full paragraph; this is where warm-crowd ("dozens of readers in your spot") plus one bullseye stacks naturally. Whisper the magnitude. |
| **Ad / VSL** | Proof is load-bearing and often stacked: open with bullseye (viewer-mimic on camera), layer warm crowd, anchor with third-party at the bottom as the "and the data backs it" floor. Climb the whole ladder in sequence — but every rung must be real and disclosable. |
| **Sales / Landing page** | Proof becomes a *block*, not a line: a mimic testimonial up top (face + four-axis match), a "X others like you" warm-crowd stat mid-page, third-party credibility (study/award) as the rational backstop. Use `/proof-ladder-builder` to compose. Every claim substantiable. |
| **Long-form article** | Lead each section's claim with its own proof at the appropriate rung; bullseye anecdotes open sections, third-party sources support them in-line with real citations. The mix reads as rigor, not as a single hero story. |
| **Ghostwritten thought-leadership** | Proof must come from the *principal's* real life — a client they actually served, a result they personally drove. The bullseye is the principal-as-demo or their named case. Never invent a client to manufacture proximity; ghostwriting is exactly where fabricated proof becomes a real reputational liability. Verify with the principal before publishing. |

## Output Requirements

Return a compact proof block:

1. **Avatar (4 axes)** — age/stage, look/identity, problem, situation. One line each.
2. **Claim being proven** — the Step-1 reveal line, verbatim.
3. **Highest honest rung reached** — labeled (Bullseye / Warm Crowd / Third-Party) with a one-line reason it's the *highest honest* rung available.
4. **Top-rung proof line** — the ready-to-place sentence(s), framed at the avatar.
5. **Fallback line (one rung down)** — for shorter assets or skeptical audiences.
6. **Self-identification verdict** — "that's me" / "that's them," with a fix if it's "them."
7. **Honesty note** — one line confirming the example is real (or stating the rung was dropped because the higher one wasn't true).

## Quality Gate

Score against `../genius.md` rubric; this workflow lives or dies on criteria 5 and 8.

- **#5 Trust-Ladder Height** — Bullseye = 9–10, warm crowd = 6–7, third-party only = 5, no proof = fail. Did you reach the highest *honest* rung, and frame it at the viewer?
- **#6 Illusion Intact** — no false modesty smuggled into the proof ("just one example, probably a fluke" is a mascot reveal that guts the proof you built).
- **#7 Whisper Test** — proof shared as a confided observation, not announced as a billboard. "Same fact, two ways" — the gossip whisperer wins.
- **#8 Honesty Spine — non-negotiable.** The illusion is of NOVELTY only. The proof must be REAL: a real person, a real cohort, a real source. Any fabricated example, invented client, or doctored stat is an automatic fail and a permanent trust-burn. If the bullseye doesn't exist, you climbed as high as the truth allowed and no higher.
- **Anti-patterns that auto-downgrade:** fabricated proof; a bullseye aimed at a blurry avatar (no four-axis match); reaching for the prestigious study when a relatable example was available and honest; proof framed abstractly when it could have been framed at the viewer.

**One-line self-check:** *Is this proof both the highest HONEST rung I can reach AND framed so the avatar says "that's me" — with zero invented facts?* If any of the three fails, re-aim or drop a rung; never fabricate to climb.
