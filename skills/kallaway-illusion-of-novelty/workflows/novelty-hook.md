# /novelty-hook — The Hook Compiler

Produces a density-ranked bank of 10+ opener hooks for a single topic-angle, each annotated with the components it carries, plus a recommended pick. Fire this when you have a chosen angle and need the *opening 1-2 lines* engineered — the part of the piece that does Steps 1-3 of the Illusion at max compression (`../genius.md` pattern 10: "Hooks carry Steps 1-3; body carries 4-5"; signature move 2: "open with the densest possible hook").

## Pre-Flight Gate

Load `../genius.md` if it is not already hot. Then answer the four inputs this workflow cannot run without (from the Decision Framework, items 1-4):

1. **Topic** — name it plainly, even if dull.
2. **Chosen angle** — the new aspect/frame/name/finding. If you don't have one, run `./novelty-angles.md` (or `./novelty-reveal.md`) first; do not invent the angle inside the hook compiler.
3. **Held belief** — what the avatar currently believes about this topic, stated as a *true opposite* to the angle (required for Contrast; if you can't name it, you're not ready — go define the avatar).
4. **Wanted outcome** — the result the avatar demonstrably wants (required for Outcome Mapping).
5. **Honest urgency? (y/n)** — is there a *real* window: something that just happened or is about to stop? If n, you skip Urgency and that is correct, not a gap. Never bolt on a fake one.

If items 2-4 are missing or thin, stop and resolve them before generating. A hook compiled on a vague avatar can never reach Bullseye later, and a hook without a held belief can only be a single-job reveal (capped at rubric 5).

- **Always:** `../genius.md` — the five components, hook templates (Steps 1-2), the urgency law, pattern 10, signature move 2, the rubric.

## Execution

### 1. Lock the four ingredients
Write a one-line ingredient card so every hook draws from the same well:
`ANGLE: ___ | BELIEF (old): ___ | OUTCOME (want): ___ | URGENCY: [real window / none]`

### 2. Generate 10+ variants across the density spectrum
Spin hooks deliberately, not randomly. Cover these construction moves (templates — vary, never verbatim):

- **Reveal + Outcome (2-job floor):** "There's a new angle on *[topic]* — *[angle]* — that gets you *[outcome]*."
- **Belief-first contrast (3-job):** "You've been told *[old belief]* your whole life. Turns out *[angle]* is what actually gets you *[outcome]*."
- **Obsolescence contrast (3-job):** "*[Old belief]* used to be the move. *[Angle]* just made it pointless if you want *[outcome]*."
- **Misattribution contrast (3-job):** "Everyone blames *[wrong cause]* for *[problem]*. It's actually *[angle]* — fix that and you get *[outcome]*."
- **Urgency-stacked (4-job, only if real):** "For years *[old belief]* was the only option. *[Real window]* changed that — *[angle]* now gets you *[outcome]*."
- **Named-novelty (cheap Step-1 lever, per genius.md hidden knowledge):** coin a proprietary name for the angle and reveal it as discovered IP — naming is itself manufactured novelty. "There's a thing I call *[name]* — and *[old belief]* is exactly why nobody talks about it."

Push past the comfortable five. Variant 8-12 is where the dense ones usually surface. Keep each to **1-2 lines**.

### 3. Annotate every variant with its component load
Tag each hook with which of the four hook-zone components it carries. Score = count of components actually *present and true* (not gestured at).

| Hook | New Reveal | Outcome | Contrast | Urgency | Jobs |
|------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|

A component only counts if it lands: an Outcome the avatar truly wants, a Contrast that is a *true opposite* of the stated belief (not a strawman, not adjacent — mis-paired contrast scores 0 and is a downgrade, per pattern 4 / hidden knowledge), Urgency only if the window is real.

### 4. Density sort, then force sort
Rank in two passes:
1. **Component coverage** — a 4-job hook outranks a 3-job outranks a 2-job. This is the primary key.
2. **Scroll-stopping force** within a tier — which prediction-break is sharpest, which gap is widest, which phrasing whispers rather than shouts. Tie-breaks go to the line with the most *specific* belief and the most *resemblant* outcome.

**Reject every single-job hook.** A naked reveal with no outcome, or an outcome with no novelty, caps at rubric 5 (genius.md rubric criterion 2) — it does not earn a place in the bank. Cut it or upgrade it.

### 5. Recommend the pick
Name the single best hook plus a one-line rationale tied to component load and the avatar. Flag one runner-up for A/B if the asset supports testing.

### Worked mini-example — Commercial gutter cleaning (a dull vertical)
Ingredient card:
`ANGLE: the damage isn't the clog, it's the freeze-thaw water that wicks UNDER the roofline | BELIEF (old): "gutters are a once-a-year fall chore, you clean them so leaves don't pile up" | OUTCOME (want): avoid a five-figure roof/fascia repair | URGENCY: real — first hard freeze of the season is the trigger window`

Generated bank (annotated):

| Hook | NR | OC | CN | UR | Jobs |
|------|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| "Most people clean gutters so leaves don't pile up. The leaves were never the problem." | ✓ | – | ✓ | – | 2 |
| "There's a reason fascia rot starts at the gutter line — and it has nothing to do with the clog." | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | – | 3 |
| "You think gutter cleaning is a fall chore. It's actually the cheapest roof insurance you'll ever buy." | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | – | 3 |
| "Clogged gutters don't ruin roofs. Water that freezes, thaws, and wicks under the shingles does — and that bill runs five figures." | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | – | 3 |
| "Every fall you clear the leaves. The thing that actually rots your roofline survives the cleaning." | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | – | 3 |
| "Before the first hard freeze hits, there's one thing on your house that quietly decides whether you pay $300 or $30,000 this winter." | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 4 |
| "Leaves in the gutter are annoying. The water sitting behind them is the part that's been eating your roof." | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | – | 3 |
| "I call it the freeze line — and once the first cold snap hits, it's the difference between a clean roof and a torn-off fascia." | ✓ | ✓ | – | ✓ | 3 |
| "Gutter cleaning isn't about the leaves. After the first freeze, it's about whether water has a place to go." | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 4 |
| "Skip the gutters this fall and you don't get a clog. You get a slow leak under the roof you won't see until spring." | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | – | 3 |
| "Clean gutters." | – | – | – | – | 0 — rejected, single/zero-job |

Density sort → the two 4-job hooks rise. Force tie-break: line 6 ("$300 or $30,000") and line 9 ("about whether water has a place to go") both carry all four. Line 6 has the sharpest outcome (a concrete dollar gap = self-interest answered) and a real urgency window; line 9 whispers harder but the dollar specificity wins the scroll.

**Recommended pick:** line 6. Carries all four components, contrasts directly against the "fall chore" belief, ties to a five-figure outcome the homeowner viscerally wants to avoid, and the freeze window is genuinely real — no fabrication. **Runner-up for A/B:** line 9 (same component load, softer/whisper register — test if the audience is sales-resistant).

Note the honesty spine in action: every hook treats the *clog-isn't-the-problem* framing as the manufactured-new angle, but the freeze-thaw mechanism and the repair-cost range must be physically true. If the contractor can't stand behind the $30,000 figure, that hook is cut — the illusion is of novelty, never of facts.

## Content-Type Adaptations

The hook compiler runs identically; the *constraints on the winning line* shift by asset. Each cell is the rule for that format.

| Asset | How the hook output changes |
|---|---|
| **Short-form video script** | Single line, ≤8 words ideal for the on-screen + spoken open; the densest 4-job hook wins because retention is decided in 1.5s. Spoken in gossip-whisperer register, never the town-crier read. Visual reinforces the contrast (show the old belief on screen, cut to the new). |
| **LinkedIn post** | The hook is the first line *before the "...more" fold* — it must survive truncation alone. Contrast-first phrasing pulls the click; outcome lands in line 2. No emoji-shout, lowercase-energy whisper outperforms the bold claim. |
| **X/Twitter thread** | Tweet 1 = the hook, and it must imply the thread's payoff without spending it. Favor the named-novelty or belief-first variant (curiosity gap + novelty stack). Hard character ceiling forces the tightest 3-4 job line; drop Urgency before Contrast if you must cut. |
| **Email** | Two surfaces: subject line (one job minimum, usually Reveal + Contrast, no salesy caps) and the opening sentence (full 3-4 job hook). Subject whispers a secret; first line delivers the gap. Preview text can carry the Outcome. |
| **Ad / VSL** | The hook is the first 3-5 seconds and is doing double duty as a pattern interrupt + qualifier. Lead with the sharpest Contrast against the held belief; Urgency only if a real promo/window exists (fake deadlines = instant trust burn and ad-policy risk). Outcome must be the avatar's, framed close. |
| **Sales / Landing page** | Hook = the H1 above the fold; it states the manufactured-new mechanism contrasted against the status-quo belief, with the outcome as the promise. Subhead carries proof-tease. No exclamation tone — page-level whisper, the secret-revealed register. |
| **Long-form article** | The hook is the headline + the first paragraph (the dek). Headline can run a 2-3 job line; the lede paragraph completes the contrast and plants the gap that the body resolves. Earns the read; never front-loads the answer. |
| **Ghostwritten thought-leadership** | The hook must sound like the *principal's* discovered insight, not a marketer's claim. Named-novelty variant fits best (coin a frame the principal "noticed"). Whisper register is mandatory — credibility dies on a salesy open. Contrast anchors to a belief the principal's peers actually hold. |

## Output Requirements

Return:
1. **The ingredient card** (one line: angle | belief | outcome | urgency status).
2. **The annotated hook bank** — ≥10 variants in the component table (NR / OC / CN / UR / Jobs), single/zero-job hooks struck through and labeled rejected.
3. **The density+force sorted ranking** (numbered, top to bottom).
4. **The recommended pick + one-line rationale**, and a flagged runner-up for A/B.
5. A one-line **honesty-spine confirmation**: which facts in the winning hook must be real, and a yes/no that they are.

## Quality Gate

Score against `../genius.md` rubric, gating on these criteria:
- **Hook Density (criterion 2):** the recommended pick carries ≥2 components (Reveal + Outcome floor) in ≤2 lines. Any single-job hook in the *recommended* slot caps the whole output at 5. Reject single-job hooks from the bank entirely.
- **Contrast Integrity (criterion 3):** the contrast in every 3+ job hook is a *true opposite* of the stated held belief — not a strawman, not an adjacent belief. Mis-paired contrast scores 0 jobs and is a downgrade (confusion, not gap).
- **Urgency Honesty (criterion 4):** any hook tagged UR ✓ rides a *real* window. One bolted-on deadline = automatic ≤4 and the hook is pulled.
- **Whisper Test (criterion 7):** every line reads as a shared secret, not a billboard. No exclamation-point sales tone, no town-crier caps. Salesy register caps at 6.
- **Domain Fit (criterion 9):** the hooks are adapted to the actual vertical and target asset, not generic template fill.

**Honesty spine (non-negotiable, every workflow):** the illusion is of NOVELTY only. The angle may be a fresh frame on an old thing — but every fact, dollar figure, urgency window, and implied outcome in the winning hook must be REAL. A hook that needs a fabricated fact, stat, or deadline to reach 4 jobs is disqualified, not a 4-job hook. Never fabricate to climb the density ladder.

**Self-check (one line):** Does my top pick make a stranger's brain treat an old topic as new — by reveal + a true-opposite contrast tied to a wanted outcome — without a single hedge, a single fake fact, or a single salesy beat?
