# /novelty-forge — The Illusion of Novelty Master Build

**This is Kallaway's CO-WRITER mode** (`../references/illusion-of-novelty-doc.md`, Overview): you give it a topic, it walks the framework questions in order, and *the answers ARE the script*. The build below is the canonical 8-step blank-page workflow (doc Section 9) operationalized into a full asset plus a component map.

Takes a topic (especially a boring, old, or saturated one), an asset type, and an avatar, runs the full five-component pipeline, and returns a finished, fully-engineered asset with a line-by-line component map. Fire this when someone needs the whole thing built — not a single lever. The granular workflows (`./novelty-reveal.md`, `./novelty-contrast.md`, `./novelty-urgency.md`, `./novelty-proof.md`, `./novelty-protect.md`) are the deep-dives for each step; reach for those when one component needs surgery.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Load `../genius.md` if it is not already hot in this conversation. Do not produce a single line of the asset before all six questions below are answered on paper. These are the Decision Framework from `../genius.md`; this flagship needs every one, because it owns the whole pipeline.

1. **Path A or Path B?** Is the topic genuinely new (a new tool, study, method) or old? Genuinely new → Path A (reveal it exists). Old → Path B (mine a new angle). Default is Path B; ~90% of boring verticals land here.
2. **Held belief.** What does the avatar already believe about this topic that you can hold constant as the "old"? You cannot frame contrast (Step 2) without naming this. If you cannot name it, stop and run the mini-canvass below.
3. **Wanted outcome.** What result does the avatar demonstrably want? Required for Outcome Mapping (Step 1).
4. **Honest urgency check.** Is there a real window — something that just happened or is about to stop being available? Yes → Turbo is allowed. No → Step 3 is skipped, and skipping it is correct, not a gap.
5. **Highest honest Trust-Ladder rung.** Do you have a viewer-mimic example or are you the demo (Bullseye, top rung)? A "we / a bunch of people" proxy (Warm Crowd, middle)? Only a study/stat (Third-Party, bottom)? Name the highest rung the *true* facts allow.
6. **Mascot-reveal temptation.** Where will you be tempted to hedge — "this is really just X that's existed for decades," "you've probably heard this," "I'm no expert but"? Pre-spot it now and pre-commit to the storyline.

**Avatar-unknown mini-canvass (run only if the avatar is missing).** Ask exactly two questions, then proceed:
- *"When this topic comes up, what's the single thing your buyer is already sure is true about it?"* → gives you the held belief (Q2).
- *"What's the one result they'd trade money or time to get in this area?"* → gives you the wanted outcome (Q3).
One round, then move. For deep avatar work, load `kallaway-audience-obsession` rather than stalling here.

## Execution

Walk the pipeline in order. Each numbered step has a move, a diagnostic, and a template marked *vary, never verbatim*. A fresh fully-worked example threads through all of them — vertical: **commercial gutter cleaning** (a maximally dull, saturated, low-status category). Avatar: a homeowner who owns a two-story house, hates ladders, and got a $9,000 water-damage repair bill last year.

**Maps to the canonical 8-step blank-page workflow** (`../references/illusion-of-novelty-doc.md` Section 9 — *the answers become the script*). The pipeline below folds those 8 steps in; it does not run a second pass. The mapping: Step 0 Pre-Flight covers canonical steps 1 (viewer + outcome) + 3 (held belief) + 4 (urgency check) + 5 (trust-ladder rung), so they are decided before a line is written; pipeline Step 1 runs canonical step 2 (brainstorm 3–5 candidate angles, pick the strongest outcome connection); Step 6 below runs canonical step 6 (**draft the hook zone first**) followed by 7 (sweep for mascots) and 8 (read it out loud). Do not re-answer Pre-Flight questions mid-build.

**Canonical ordering principle** (`../references/illusion-of-novelty-doc.md` Section 7): gossip-whisper opener → reveal + outcomes → contrast → urgency (if real) → bullseye proof. You may rearrange components for the asset — but **reveal + contrast must land EARLY, in the hook zone (the first ~3–5 lines)**. Never bury reveal or contrast in the body; a build that opens on the outcome alone, with the contrast pushed late, is the most common losing pattern (doc Section 10).

### Step 0 — Lock the Pre-Flight answers
Write the six answers as a header block. For the gutter example:
- Path: **B** (gutters are old; the angle is the product).
- Held belief: *"You clean gutters in the fall, after the leaves drop. That's the schedule."*
- Wanted outcome: *Never pay for water damage again; never touch a ladder.*
- Honest urgency: a real one exists if seasonal — *the pre-freeze window where clogged gutters turn into ice dams* (only usable in the weeks before the first freeze; otherwise skip).
- Highest rung: **Bullseye** — a same-situation neighbor with the same two-story house.
- Mascot temptation: the urge to say *"obviously gutter guards have existed forever"* — pre-commit to not saying it.

### Step 1 — New Reveal + Outcome Mapping
**Move.** Reveal that *something has changed* about the old topic, then tie that change to the wanted outcome. Path A: reveal the new thing exists. Path B (default): reveal a new **aspect, frame, name, update, or finding** — a new door into the old room, not a new room. Naming the mechanism is the cheapest novelty lever there is; coining a proprietary name for the angle is itself a New Reveal.

**Brainstorm 3–5 candidate angles first, then pick one** (`../references/illusion-of-novelty-doc.md` Section 9, step 2). Do not lock the first angle that surfaces. List 3–5 candidate reveals for the topic, then select the single one with the **strongest connection to the wanted outcome** from Pre-Flight Q3 — strength of outcome tie, not novelty in the abstract, is the selection rule. For the gutter avatar, candidates might run: (a) *timing window beats frequency*; (b) *gutter material changes the clog rate*; (c) *the downspout, not the trough, is the real failure point*; (d) *a $40 service vs. a $9k bill is a calendar problem, not a labor problem*. (a) ties most directly to "never pay for water damage again" → it wins.

**Formula:** `reveal new aspect of the thing + tie it to an outcome the viewer wants`.

**Diagnostics:**
1. What's the new angle — genuinely new, or a reframe/name/finding I'm mining to *feel* new?
2. What outcome does the avatar want, and how does this new angle connect to it?

**Template (vary):** *"You've probably handled [old topic] this way — turns out [new aspect] is what actually gets you [wanted outcome]."*

**Gutter draft hook (Path B):** *"It turns out the month you clean your gutters matters more than how often you clean them — and getting that one window right is the difference between a $40 service and a $9,000 ceiling repair."* New aspect = timing window (not frequency); outcome = no water-damage bill. The New Reveal makes them look; the Outcome Mapping makes them stay.

### Step 2 — Contrast Framing
**Move.** Position the new reveal directly against the held belief from Pre-Flight Q2, as a **true opposite**. New means nothing in isolation; the brain only registers "new" through a comparative relationship to "old." The gap between *what I thought was true* and *this new reality* makes the brain suspect it might be wrong — and a brain that suspects it's wrong freezes to recalibrate. That freeze is the intrigue.

**The one rule that breaks everything if violated:** the contrast must be the avatar's *actual* held belief about *this* topic, not an adjacent or strawman belief. Mis-paired contrast produces confusion, which is worse than no contrast.

**Diagnostic:** What does the avatar hold constant as "old"? How does the new angle sit as its direct opposite?

**Template (vary):** *"You've been told [held belief] forever — turns out [held belief] is the wrong lever; [new aspect] is what gets you [outcome]."*

**Gutter contrast:** *"Everyone cleans their gutters in the fall after the leaves drop. That's exactly when it's already too late — the clog that floods your ceiling forms in the three weeks *before* the first hard freeze, not in October."* Same fact as Step 1, now anchored against the held "fall schedule" belief, so it lands as an event instead of a fact.

### Step 3 — Urgency (Turbo — use only if real)
**Move.** A timeliness alert: *pay attention now, because something just changed or is about to stop being available.* It adds time-compression and pulls extra intrigue via recency bias. This is the one component you are allowed to skip.

**The writing law.** Use urgency ONLY when you can honestly coast on something that just happened or is about to close. Never bolt on a fake deadline. If the audience smells fake urgency, you lose all trust and the whole piece reads as a sale. If there's no real window, **write "URGENCY: skipped — no honest window"** in the component map and move on. Restraint here is a skill, not a hole.

**Template (only if real, vary):** *"This window won't stay open long —"* / *"For years this didn't work, until [recent change]."* / *"Barely anyone is set up for this yet."*

**Gutter urgency (real, seasonal):** *"And the catch is the safe window to do this closes the day of your first hard freeze — once the clog ices over, you're not cleaning it, you're replacing it."* Honest if and only if the piece runs in the pre-freeze weeks. Off-season → skip, and say so in the map.

### Step 4 — Bullseye Proof (Trust Ladder)
**Move.** The viewer still doesn't know the reveal is true. Climb the Trust Ladder to the highest rung the honest facts allow. The best proof hits closest to the viewer's own life, because trust travels through resemblance, not credentials.

| Rung | Proof type | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Top — Bullseye | A 1-to-1 example that mimics the viewer (same age, look, problem, situation); or your own result if you ARE the demo | Maximum — "that's me" |
| Middle — Warm Crowd | The generalized "we" — "a bunch of people got this" | Decent proxy |
| Bottom — Third-Party | A study, stat, article, award | Works, but forces "would it work for *me*?" |

**Mechanism = self-identification.** We distrust third-party claims; we trust versions of ourselves getting the result. Never manufacture a lie to climb a rung.

**Gutter proof (Bullseye):** *"Two houses down — same two-story build as yours, same north-facing roofline — the owner kept doing the October clean and ate an $11,000 drywall job two winters running. He moved his service to that pre-freeze window. Bone dry both winters since."* A same-situation neighbor outranks any "studies show gutter maintenance reduces water intrusion." If only a stat is available, you sit at the bottom rung and say so — you do not invent a neighbor.

### Step 5 — Protect the Illusion
**5a — Don't show the mascot.** Commit to the storyline. Do not hedge, downplay, or admit the new frame is an old thing dressed up. Saying *"obviously gutter guards have been around for decades"* takes the head off the mascot and the loop collapses. This is not lying: anyone who didn't hold the old frame experiences the new frame as genuinely new. Scrub every hedge and every piece of false modesty ("I'm no expert but," "you've probably heard this," "this is nothing new").

**5b — The Gossip Whisperer.** Delivery tone. Town Crier ("HUGE NEWS, this changes everything, pay attention NOW") triggers sales-resistance and dies in 2026. Gossip Whisperer ("okay, so not many people have caught onto this yet…") feels like a secret and pulls them in. In text this shows up as lowercase energy, conspiratorial framing ("here's the part nobody mentions"), under-claiming the magnitude while over-delivering the substance, and zero exclamation-point sales tone. Whisper to be believed.

### Step 6 — Assemble + Component Map
**Draft the hook zone FIRST** (`../references/illusion-of-novelty-doc.md` Section 9, step 6). Do not write top-to-bottom. Build the opening ~3–5 lines before anything else: gossip-whisper opener, then the New Reveal + Outcome, then the Contrast — so the recalibration gap is open before the body argues anything. The hook zone is where the piece lives or dies; write it under a real constraint, not as a byproduct of the body. Then stitch the surviving steps into one continuous asset in the requested format (see the Adaptations table — the *order and density* of components shift by asset, but reveal + contrast stay in the hook zone). Then produce a component map: each line of the finished piece labeled with the job it does.

**Final polish — read it out loud** (`../references/illusion-of-novelty-doc.md` Section 9, step 8). Before shipping, read the assembled asset aloud once. Short sentences, spoken rhythm, the cadence of sharing a secret rather than reading a press release. Anything that sounds announced, listy, or stiff gets rewritten to the spoken register; this is also the last pass to catch any Town Crier line the mascot sweep missed.

**Gutter finished asset (short-form script, pre-freeze season):**
> *"Most people clean their gutters in October, after the leaves drop — and that's exactly the timing that floods their ceiling. Turns out the month matters more than how often you do it, and the clog that costs you a $9,000 repair forms in the three weeks before your first hard freeze, not in the fall. Here's the part barely anyone's caught onto: once that clog ices over, you're not cleaning gutters anymore, you're replacing them — so the window's tighter than people think. Two houses down, same two-story build as yours, the owner kept doing the October clean and ate an eleven-grand drywall job two winters running. He moved his service to that pre-freeze window. Bone dry ever since."*

**Component map:**
- Line 1 — Contrast Framing (anchors "October clean," the held belief).
- Line 2 — New Reveal + Outcome Mapping (timing > frequency; outcome = no $9k repair).
- Line 3 — Urgency (real pre-freeze window) + Protect 5b whisper ("here's the part barely anyone's caught onto").
- Line 4–5 — Bullseye Proof (same-build neighbor, viewer-mimic).
- Whole piece — Protect 5a: never admits gutter-guard/seasonal-timing is old; gossip-whisperer register throughout, no exclamation points.

## Content-Type Adaptations

The five components are universal; their *order, weight, and where they live* changes by asset. This table is the domain-agnostic key.

| Asset | How the build changes |
|---|---|
| **Short-form video script** | Hook line (≤2 lines) carries Reveal + Outcome + Contrast (+ Urgency if real); Proof lands by 0:08–0:12; spoken delivery IS the Gossip Whisperer — lower the voice, never announce. Mascot stays hidden for the full clip unless you're deliberately running the meta fourth-wall break. |
| **LinkedIn post** | Line 1 = densest Reveal+Contrast as the scroll-stop; whitespace after it. Proof = a first-person Bullseye (your own result or a named-pattern client). Whisper register reads as "here's the part nobody in [field] says out loud." No urgency unless a real industry shift is live. |
| **X/Twitter thread** | Tweet 1 = Reveal + Contrast + Outcome compressed under 280 chars; the gap *is* the open loop that earns the next tap. Each mid-thread tweet re-stakes one component; proof Bullseye gets its own tweet with the most viewer-resembling detail. Skip urgency unless genuinely time-boxed. |
| **Email** | Subject line = New Reveal + a curiosity gap (the Contrast implied, not spent). Body opens by naming the held belief, then overturns it; Bullseye proof mid-body as a mini-story. Urgency only if a real deadline exists (offer close, seasonal window). Whisper hard — email is where town-crier tone gets marked spam. |
| **Ad / VSL** | Hook (first 3 sec) front-loads Reveal+Contrast+Outcome at max density; Urgency used only when the offer or window is genuinely real; Proof escalates Bottom→Top of the ladder as the VSL runs; mascot must stay hidden because a VSL that hedges loses the sale. Keep the whisper even at scale — salesy register caps trust. |
| **Sales / Landing page** | Headline = Reveal+Contrast; subhead = Outcome Mapping; proof section stacks the ladder visibly (Bullseye testimonials that look like the visitor up top, third-party logos/stats below). Urgency block only if the scarcity is real. Microcopy stays whisper, never billboard. |
| **Long-form article** | Reveal+Contrast open the piece and seed the thesis; the article *is* the recalibration the gap promised. Proof deepens from Bullseye anecdote → Warm Crowd → cited Third-Party as the argument matures. Urgency usually skipped (evergreen decays the recency lever). Protect by never undercutting your own framing mid-essay. |
| **Ghostwritten thought-leadership** | The named angle/frame becomes the author's proprietary IP (naming = the novelty lever); Contrast positions against the field's consensus belief; Bullseye proof is the author's own lived result delivered as a quiet aside. Strongest move: run the framework *on* the reader, then optionally reveal the mechanism as the closing proof (the recursive self-demonstration — see Apex Move 1 in `../genius.md`). Whisper is the entire register of credible thought leadership. |

## Output Requirements

Return three artifacts:
1. **Pre-Flight header** — the six answered Decision-Framework questions (Path, held belief, wanted outcome, urgency yes/no, highest honest rung, mascot-temptation flag).
2. **The finished asset** — fully assembled, in the requested format, ready to publish; whisper register; zero hedges.
3. **The component map** — every line (or section) of the asset labeled with the component job it performs, including an explicit `URGENCY: skipped — no honest window` line when Step 3 was passed and a note on the proof rung reached.

## Quality Gate

**Score with the canonical Gut-Check Scorecard first** (`../references/gut-check-scorecard.md` — 0–2 per component for a /10, then the two overrides). It is the go/no-go instrument: an honest urgency skip earns 1 point (so a piece with no real window honestly caps at 9/10 — correct, not a defect); only *fake* urgency scores 0; any Illusion-Integrity FAIL (a mascot line, or Town Crier delivery) overrides the number regardless of total. Then run the supplementary checks below from the `../genius.md` rubric for depth on *why* a component scored a 1. Cite anchors for any dimension scored ≥8.
- **Three-Question Pass** — name which component delivers Relevant, Novel, Interesting; missing any = fail.
- **Hook Density** — opener carries ≥2 components (Reveal + Outcome minimum) in ≤2 lines; single-job hook caps at 5.
- **Contrast Integrity** — anchored to the avatar's *actual* held belief as a true opposite; strawman/unrelated = confusion = fail.
- **Trust-Ladder Height** — Bullseye 9–10, Warm Crowd 6–7, third-party-only 5; name the rung reached.
- **Illusion Intact** — zero mascot reveals (no hedging, no false modesty); one reveal caps at 5.
- **Whisper Test** — gossip-whisperer, not town-crier; salesy/exclamation register caps at 6.
- **Domain Fit** — execution adapted to the actual asset and vertical, not generic.

**Honesty spine (non-negotiable).** The illusion is of NOVELTY only. The reveal, the urgency window, and the proof must be REAL. Any fabricated fact, fake deadline, or invented testimonial is an automatic fail — this is the one unbreakable line. Manufacture the feeling of new; never manufacture the facts.

**Self-check (one line):** *Could a skeptic verify every fact, find the urgency window real, and meet the proof person — while still walking away feeling they'd just learned something new?* If yes, ship. If no, the failing component goes back to its deep-dive workflow.
