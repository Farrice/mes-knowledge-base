# /novelty-contrast — Contrast Framing (Step 2)

This workflow takes a new angle (from Step 1, New Reveal) and welds it against the *specific* belief the audience walked in holding, so the brain registers a gap, freezes to recalibrate, and feels intrigue. Fire it whenever a reveal lands flat, reads as "mildly interesting," or gets scrolled despite being true — the diagnosis is almost always a missing or mis-paired contrast anchor.

A new claim presented nakedly is a *fact*. The same claim anchored to the belief it overturns is an *event*. This workflow manufactures the event.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Answer before writing. If you can't, you're not ready for Step 2 — go back to the avatar.

1. **What is the new angle?** (Inherited from `./novelty-reveal.md` or supplied. A contrast with nothing to contrast *to* is dead on arrival.)
2. **What does the avatar already believe about THIS topic — stated as a sentence they would nod at?** This is the load-bearing input. Vague belief → weak contrast. "People think water is healthy" is too soft; "People believe more water = better, and the number is 8 glasses" is anchorable.
3. **Is the new angle a TRUE opposite of that belief, or merely adjacent?** If it doesn't directly negate the held belief, it will read as confusion, not contrast (the one rule that breaks everything).
4. **Where will the gap live?** Hook (almost always) vs. body. Contrast is a hook-zone component per `../genius.md` pattern 10.

If `../genius.md` is not already hot in this session, load it now. The Decision Framework questions 2 and 4 are this workflow's spine.

## Skill Acquisition

- **Always:** `../genius.md` (the IP anchor — Component 2 Contrast Framing, pattern 4 "new only exists relative to old," pattern 5 "the gap is the product," the Quality Rubric criterion 3).
- **Upstream input:** `./novelty-reveal.md` (the angle you are contrasting; if not run, surface the new angle first — this workflow does not invent it).
- **To source the held belief when the avatar is fuzzy:** `mcraney-deep-canvass` or any ICP / audience-obsession skill (`kallaway-audience-obsession`). The held belief is *their actual conviction*, not your guess. Mis-sourcing it is the #1 failure.
- **Downstream:** hands the contrasted hook to `./novelty-forge.md` (full assembly) or `kallaway-addictive-storytelling` for the body.

## Execution

### 1. Surface the held belief (be ruthlessly specific)

Write the single sentence the avatar believes about this topic *before* they meet you. Pressure-test it three ways:

- **Specific enough?** Name the actual number, rule, ritual, or received wisdom. "Flossing is good" is unanchorable; "Floss every day, especially before bed, or you get cavities" is anchorable.
- **Held, not assigned?** It must be something they genuinely believe, not a belief you wish they had so your contrast works. Strawmen collapse the second the reader thinks "I never thought that."
- **About THIS topic?** Not an adjacent topic. Contrast against the belief that sits *directly under* your reveal.

Output: `HELD BELIEF: "<one sentence, in their voice>"`

### 2. Construct the TRUE-OPPOSITE

Your new angle must directly negate the held belief — same axis, flipped pole. Run this test: write the held belief and the new angle as a pair and ask *"is the second one the literal opposite of the first, on the same dimension?"*

- Belief: "more is better." Opposite: "amount is irrelevant; *timing* decides." ✓ (same axis: what drives the result)
- Belief: "more is better." Adjacent miss: "you should use a filter." ✗ (different axis → confusion, not gap)

If your angle isn't the true opposite, you have two moves: (a) reframe the angle so it *does* oppose the belief on the same axis, or (b) discard and pick a different held belief that your angle genuinely overturns. Do not force a near-miss through.

Output: `TRUE-OPPOSITE: "<the new angle, framed as the negation>"`

### 3. Write the contrast inline (5+ variants)

Put the old belief and the new angle *adjacent in the same breath*. Templates below — **vary, never verbatim** (Kallaway's standing instruction: don't have a thousand people ship the same line):

- `Everyone thinks <X>. Turns out <Y>.`
- `You've been told <X> your whole life — and the number was basically made up. What actually moves the needle is <Y>.`
- `<X> used to be the gold standard. <Y> makes it obsolete.`
- `For as long as you can remember, the answer was <X>. It was never <X>. It was <Y>.`
- `The thing everyone optimizes — <X> — turns out to be the one thing that doesn't matter. <Y> does.`
- `<X> isn't wrong, exactly. It's just answering a question nobody should be asking. The real question is <Y>.`

Generate at least five distinct phrasings. Vary the rhythm and the entry point (some lead with the belief, some lead with the reveal then snap back to the belief). Keep the gossip-whisperer register from `../genius.md` Component 5b — under-claim the magnitude, let the gap do the work. No "HUGE NEWS," no exclamation tone.

### 4. Confusion-vs-Contrast check (kill switch)

For each variant, ask: *if the old thing and the new thing were unrelated, would a reader feel a gap or feel lost?* The diagnostic: read the line cold and check whether the reader's reaction is **"wait, I might be wrong about that"** (contrast → keep) or **"...what does that have to do with this?"** (confusion → cut). A line that produces a shrug is also a fail — that's contrast that's too weak (the angle isn't a true opposite, loop back to step 2). Discard or repair every variant that confuses.

### 5. State the GAP explicitly (the gap is the product)

The deliverable is not the fact and not the contrast line — it is the **gap** the contrast opens between current belief and new reality (`../genius.md` pattern 5). Name it in one sentence so downstream assembly and proof know exactly what tension they're servicing:

`THE GAP: "<reader currently believes X drives the result; if Y actually drives it, everything they've been doing is aimed at the wrong lever>"`

This gap statement is what Step 4 (Bullseye Proof) must resolve and what Step 5 (Protect the Illusion) must not hedge away.

### Worked mini-example — gutter cleaning (fresh topic)

Roofing / home-services operator, "make content this week," topic surfaces: **cleaning your gutters.** Objectively inert.

- **Step 1 input (new angle):** clogged gutters don't mainly cause overflow — they rot the *fascia and foundation* because trapped water wicks back into the wood and down the wall. (New aspect: the real damage is structural and hidden, not the visible overflow.)
- **Step 1 — Held belief surfaced:** *"You clean your gutters so they don't overflow and dump water on you when you walk to the door — it's an annoyance thing, low priority, do it in spring."*
- **Step 2 — True-opposite:** the overflow is the symptom you *can* see; the damage that matters is the one you *can't* — water wicking into the fascia and tracking down to the foundation. Same axis (what gutter neglect actually costs you), flipped pole (cosmetic annoyance → silent structural rot).
- **Step 3 — Contrast variants:**
  1. "Everyone thinks clogged gutters are about overflow. The overflow is the part that's *harmless*. It's the water you don't see — wicking back into the wood — that quietly eats the foundation."
  2. "You've been told to clean your gutters so they don't spill on you. That spill was never the problem. The problem is the gallon that *doesn't* spill and instead soaks into the fascia."
  3. "Gutter cleaning got filed under spring chores, right next to mowing. Turns out it belongs next to *foundation repair* — the bill it prevents is five figures, not five dollars."
  4. "The visible overflow is the gutter doing its job badly but safely. The danger is the overflow that doesn't happen, because that water has nowhere to go but into your walls."
  5. "Most people treat a clogged gutter as cosmetic. By the time it's cosmetic, the wood behind it has usually been wet for two seasons."
- **Step 4 — Confusion check:** every variant keeps "gutter neglect" as the constant and flips overflow→hidden rot on that same axis. Reader reaction is "wait, I've been thinking about this wrong" — contrast, not confusion. All five pass. (A *failed* version: "Everyone thinks gutters are about overflow — but actually you should get a metal roof." Different axis. Confusion. Cut.)
- **Step 5 — Gap stated:** *"The reader believes gutter cleaning is a low-stakes cosmetic chore aimed at overflow; if the real cost is hidden water damage to fascia and foundation, then the priority they've assigned it — and the season they delay it to — is dangerously wrong."* Honesty spine intact: the structural-damage mechanism is real building science, the only thing manufactured is the *feeling* that this is a fresh reframe.

## Content-Type Adaptations

The contrast is the same; *where it sits, how loud it is, and how much room it gets* changes per asset.

| Asset | How the contrast lands |
|---|---|
| **Short-form video script** | Belief and opposite collapse into the first 1–2 spoken lines; the gap *is* the hook. Lead with the held belief as a relatable shrug, snap to the opposite on the cut. The gap carries the first 3 seconds before any proof. |
| **LinkedIn post** | Held belief as the standalone opening line (the "above the fold" before the See-more fold); the true-opposite as line 2, line break between them so the gap renders as visual whitespace. Body unpacks the gap; never resolve the belief in the first line. |
| **X / Twitter thread** | Tweet 1 = pure belief + opposite in one beat (the contrast must survive a quote-tweet on its own). Subsequent tweets widen the gap before proof; the held belief is restated once mid-thread so late readers catch the axis. |
| **Email** | Subject line carries the held belief *or* the opposite (not both — tease one, deliver the gap in the open). First line of body completes the contrast; the gap becomes the email's spine and the reason-to-keep-reading. Whisper register matters most here — inboxes are where salesy contrast dies. |
| **Ad / VSL** | Contrast is the pattern-interrupt that buys the first 5 seconds. Held belief voiced as "what you've always been told," opposite as the turn. The gap must immediately point at the wanted outcome (this is where contrast hands to outcome-mapping fast, or the ad scrolls). Keep it whisper, not billboard — the framework's own rule. |
| **Sales / Landing page** | Held belief becomes the "old way" column; true-opposite becomes the "new way" — but written as prose tension above the fold, not a feature table. The gap is the page's central claim; every proof block below services it. Subhead names the gap explicitly. |
| **Long-form article** | Belief gets a full paragraph of steelman (state it fairly, even sympathetically — a respected belief makes the overturn land harder); the opposite arrives as the pivot. The gap becomes the article's thesis and recurs as a through-line at each section boundary. |
| **Ghostwritten thought-leadership** | Contrast is sourced from the *principal's* lived contrarian conviction, not a manufactured one — the held belief is "what my industry believes," the opposite is "what I learned the hard way." The gap reads as earned point of view, not a hook trick; whisper register is mandatory (thought-leaders who shout read as junior). |

## Output Requirements

Return, in this order:

1. `HELD BELIEF:` — one sentence in the avatar's voice.
2. `TRUE-OPPOSITE:` — the new angle framed as the direct negation, with a one-line note confirming the shared axis.
3. **5+ contrast variants** — numbered, each old-belief-adjacent-to-new-angle, varied in rhythm, all whisper-register.
4. **Confusion-vs-Contrast verdict** per variant (keep / repair / cut) with one-word reason.
5. `THE GAP:` — the single explicit gap statement (the product).
6. One-line honesty-spine confirmation: which part is the manufactured *feeling* of novelty and which underlying *facts* are real and verifiable.

If the held belief could not be specifically named, return that as the blocker and route to ICP/audience work rather than shipping a strawman.

## Quality Gate

Score against `../genius.md` Quality Rubric — this workflow owns **criterion 3 (Contrast Integrity)** and must also clear:

- **Contrast Integrity (rubric 3):** new angle is anchored to the avatar's *actual held belief* as a *true opposite*. Strawman, adjacent, or unrelated contrast = confusion = fail. (Name the shared axis or lower the score.)
- **Whisper Test (rubric 7):** every variant reads as a shared secret, not a billboard. Any exclamation-tone or "HUGE NEWS" register caps the output at 6.
- **Illusion Intact (rubric 6):** no hedging baked into the contrast ("you've probably heard this," "this is obvious but…") — those are mascot reveals that pre-collapse the gap. One = automatic ≤5.
- **Three-Question Pass (rubric 1):** the gap must deliver YES on *Interesting* (the intrigue this step exists to manufacture). If the reader's honest reaction is a shrug, the contrast is too weak — repair.

**Honesty Spine (non-negotiable, rubric 8):** the illusion is of NOVELTY only. The held belief must be a real belief, the opposing angle must be factually true and verifiable, the gap must be genuine. Never invent a belief the audience doesn't hold to make a contrast land, and never assert a false "actually it's Y" to manufacture the gap. Fabricated belief or fabricated fact = automatic fail, no exceptions.

**Self-check (one line):** Read each variant cold — if the reaction is "wait, I might be wrong about that" it ships; if it's "what does that have to do with this?" (confusion) or a shrug (too weak), it doesn't.
