# Trust Ladder — Step 4 / Bullseye Proof Reference

Lookup reference for the proof layer of the Illusion of Novelty. The viewer's last objection after Steps 1–3 is *"is this actually true?"* — proof answers it. The governing law: **the best proof is whatever hits closest to the viewer's own life.** Climb as high as the honest facts allow; never fabricate a rung you don't have. Load `../genius.md` (Step 4 table + Pattern 6) for the full IP anchor; this file is the operational deep-dive.

---

## The Core Mechanism — Self-Identification

> We don't trust third-party claims. We trust **versions of ourselves** experiencing the result we want.

Proof is not about *credibility*; it's about *resemblance*. The brain runs a silent simulation: "would this work for **me**?" Every gap between the proof-subject and the viewer is a question the viewer has to answer for you ("were those study people like me? same age? same problem?"). Each unanswered question leaks trust. The closer the proof maps onto the viewer's own life — same age, same look, same problem, same situation — the fewer questions remain, and the more the new reveal is simply *believed*.

This is **Pattern 6 from `../genius.md`: proximity beats authority.** A relatable singular example ("a buddy your age who crashes at 3pm") outperforms an authoritative aggregate ("a peer-reviewed cohort study"). Trust travels through resemblance, not credentials. So you climb the ladder *toward the viewer*, not toward prestige.

## The Three Rungs (exact definitions)

| Rung | Name | Definition | Identification strength | Why it costs what it costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Top** | **Bullseye** | A 1-to-1 **singular** example that is a *mimic of the viewer* — same age, same look, same problem, same situation. If the creator **is** the same demo as the viewer, a result the creator personally got also counts as bullseye. | Maximum. "That's me." | Zero imaginative leap required. The subject *is* a proxy for the viewer's exact self. This is why **in-demo creators win** — their own result is automatically bullseye. |
| **Middle** | **Warm Crowd** | The generalized "we" — "a bunch of people got this," "our customers," "everyone who's tried it." Third-party, but a *proxy* for the crowd the viewer belongs to. | Partial. "People like me, maybe." | Some identification, but the viewer must self-sort into the crowd. Less specific than one matched human. |
| **Bottom** | **Third-Party** | An external authority artifact: a study, a stat, an article, an award, a citation. | Weak. "Were they like me?" | Still works, but forces the viewer's imagination to do the matching. Every unanswered "was that person like me?" is a leak. |

**The ranking is not about how impressive the proof is. It's about how little the viewer has to imagine.** A Harvard study (impressive) sits *below* a single matched human (unimpressive but identical to the viewer) precisely because the study forces more imaginative matching.

## Why In-Demo Creators Win

When the creator and the viewer share the demo — same problem, same starting point, same look — the creator's **own first-person result** is the highest-trust proof obtainable, for free, on every piece they make.

- The fitness creator who lost the 40 lbs *is* the bullseye for the viewer trying to lose 40 lbs.
- The bookkeeper who left a 9-to-5 to run a solo practice *is* the bullseye for the viewer eyeing the same jump.
- The needle-phobe dentist who got the painless procedure herself *is* the bullseye for the needle-phobe viewer.

If you are not in-demo, you can't fabricate that you are. What you *can* do is **find or feature someone who is** (a client, a patient, a customer, a peer) and frame the proof through them. The operational move when you lack a personal result: locate a real human in your records who mirrors the avatar, and let that person carry the proof.

## Proof-Type Catalog (by rung)

Pick the highest rung you can honestly fill. Templates below are **vary, never verbatim** — they're scaffolds, not scripts.

### Top rung — Bullseye (singular, viewer-mimic)
- **The age/situation twin:** "I've got a [client / patient / buddy] your age who [exact problem] — they [did the new thing] and [got the outcome]."
- **The own-result (in-demo creator):** "I [had the exact problem]. I [did the new thing]. Here's what happened to me." (visual receipt > claim)
- **The mirror case study:** a single named case whose starting state matches the avatar's starting state, walked through to the result.
- **The visual self-recognition:** before/after, screen recording, photo, or footage where the subject *looks like* the viewer (same demographic, same setting).
- **The texted-from-their-desk proof:** the patient/customer reporting the result in their own voice, in real time, in the viewer's own context.

### Middle rung — Warm Crowd (the generalized "we")
- **The customer aggregate:** "Our customers who switched to [new thing] consistently [outcome]."
- **The cohort:** "A bunch of people in [avatar's situation] tried this and [outcome]."
- **The pattern claim:** "Everyone we've put through this [outcome] — it's the same story every time."
- **The community signal:** "The [niche] community has quietly been [doing the new thing] for a few months now."

### Bottom rung — Third-Party (authority artifact)
- **The study/stat:** "A study found [stat] about [topic]."
- **The article/report:** "There's a write-up / industry report on this."
- **The award/credential:** "This approach won [award] / is endorsed by [body]."
- **The data point:** "[Metric] improved [X%] in the published numbers."

> **Stacking note:** rungs combine. Lead with the bullseye for trust, then *back it* with a third-party artifact for ballast: "My buddy your age fixed it overnight — and there's a study now showing why it works." The human carries the belief; the study makes it durable. Bottom rung alone is the weakest play; bottom rung *under* a bullseye is strong.

## How to Find a Viewer-Mimic Example (honestly)

You cannot reach the top rung without knowing the avatar precisely. **Bullseye proof presupposes avatar specificity** — "same age, same look, same problem, same situation" is meaningless against a vague avatar. Vague avatar → no bullseye possible → you're stuck on the bottom rung by default.

**The honest sourcing sequence:**

1. **Pin the avatar's four coordinates first.** Age band, visible identity/look, the exact problem, the exact situation. If you can't write these in one line, stop and run avatar work (`kallaway-audience-obsession`, or `mcraney-deep-canvass` / ICP skills) before proofing.
2. **Search your real records against those coordinates.** Past clients, patients, customers, students, DMs, testimonials, your own history. You are looking for the one human whose starting state matches the avatar's starting state most tightly.
3. **Select for proximity, not for impressiveness.** The most dramatic result is not the goal. The most *matched* subject is. A modest result in a perfect mirror beats a spectacular result in a stranger.
4. **Frame to surface the resemblance, not hide it.** Lead with the coordinates that match the viewer ("same age as you," "also terrified of needles," "also running a one-person shop"). Make the mirror explicit.
5. **If no real bullseye exists, drop a rung — don't invent one.** Move to warm crowd, then third-party. A real warm-crowd claim outranks a fabricated bullseye every time, and fabrication is the one move that permanently burns trust.

## The Climb-As-High-As-Honest Rule + The Never-Fabricate Gate

Two rules, in tension only if you cheat:

- **Climb as high as you can.** Always reach for the highest rung the real facts support. Defaulting to a study when you actually have a matched client is leaving trust on the table.
- **Never manufacture a rung you don't have.** No invented patient, no fabricated testimonial, no "a study found" when no study exists, no implied own-result you didn't get. The illusion is of **novelty only.** Proof must be **real.**

The resolution: the ceiling of your climb is set by the truth, and you climb *to* that ceiling but never past it. This is **Pattern 7 (honesty constraint) from `../genius.md`** applied to Step 4 — fabricated proof is an automatic rubric fail (criterion 5 and the honesty-spine criterion 8), and practically it's the fastest possible loop-collapse: audiences forgive a modest proof, never a fake one.

## Per-Vertical Examples (top rung shown; drop a rung where the real facts run out)

### Fitness — avatar: 38-year-old desk worker, soft midsection, no time to train
- **Bullseye:** "I had a guy your exact build — sits at a desk all day, hadn't trained in years. He did [the new protocol] three times a week and dropped two pant sizes in eight weeks." (Or, in-demo: "This was me 18 months ago. Same desk, same gut.")
- **Warm crowd:** "Almost every desk-job client we've run through this hits the same two-pant-size mark."
- **Third-party:** "There's research on [training variable] and visceral fat for sedentary adults."

### B2B SaaS — avatar: ops lead at a 50-person company, drowning in manual reconciliation
- **Bullseye:** "We had an ops lead at a company exactly your size — same manual reconciliation nightmare every month-end. After switching, her close went from five days to one." (Visual: a screen-recorded before/after of the workflow she'd recognize.)
- **Warm crowd:** "Our mid-market customers cut month-end close by 60–80% on average."
- **Third-party:** "An industry report pegs manual reconciliation at [X] hours per ops headcount per month."

### Real Estate — avatar: first-time buyer, dual-income, priced out of their target neighborhood
- **Bullseye:** "I just closed with a couple your age — first-timers, dual income, thought they were locked out of [neighborhood]. Using [the new financing angle], they're moving in next month." (In-demo agent who bought their own first place the same way carries it personally.)
- **Warm crowd:** "Most of our first-time buyers this year used this same path into neighborhoods they'd written off."
- **Third-party:** "[Program/stat] data shows first-time buyers qualifying at [X] under this structure."

### Dentistry — avatar: needle-phobic adult who has avoided treatment for years
- **Bullseye:** "We just had a patient who's terrified of needles and put this off for two years. They got it done, texted us from their desk inside the hour, couldn't believe it felt like nothing happened." (Straight from Kallaway's root-canal assembly — a perfect needle-phobe mirror.)
- **Warm crowd:** "Pretty much every anxious patient we've treated this way walks out surprised it was nothing."
- **Third-party:** "Clinical studies on [the technique] report markedly lower pain scores."

### Finance — avatar: late-30s W-2 earner, decent income, no real wealth-building system
- **Bullseye:** "I had a client your exact situation — good salary, W-2, zero system, money just evaporating. We set up [the new structure] and within a year he had [concrete milestone] for the first time in his life." (In-demo planner who ran the same playbook on their own income carries it first-person.)
- **Warm crowd:** "Most of the W-2 earners we work with hit their first real milestone inside twelve months on this."
- **Third-party:** "[Reputable index/data] shows the [structure] historically returns [X]."

**Notice the pattern across all five:** the bottom rung is always *available* (there's always a study), but it always asks the viewer to imagine themselves into a stranger's data. The top rung *removes the imagining* by handing the viewer a near-clone. Reach for the clone first.

## Fast Diagnostic (use when auditing a draft's proof)

1. **What rung is this proof on?** Name it (bullseye / warm crowd / third-party).
2. **Is there a higher honest rung available that I skipped?** If a real matched client exists and I used a study, climb.
3. **How many coordinates does the proof-subject share with the avatar?** (age / look / problem / situation — count them.) Fewer than 2 shared = it's not really bullseye, downgrade your self-score.
4. **Is the resemblance *stated* or buried?** If the viewer has to infer the match, surface it explicitly.
5. **Is every element of this proof TRUE?** Any invented subject, fabricated stat, or implied result you didn't get = stop, rip it out, drop a rung.

**Self-check (one line):** *Am I reaching for the closest honest mirror of the viewer — and is every fact in it real?*
