# Five Components — Structural Quick-Reference

Mid-build lookup for the engine that manufactures the *feeling* of novelty on old/boring/saturated topics. For the full theory, exemplars, and rubric, load `../genius.md`. This file is the scannable companion — find the component, grab the formula, ship.

Load order of the engine (always this order; Step 3 is the only legal skip): **New Reveal + Outcome → Contrast Framing → Urgency (optional) → Bullseye Proof → Protect the Illusion.**

The whole thing exists to win YES on three viewer questions: **Relevant** (do I care?), **Novel** (haven't heard this?), **Interesting** (intrigued to learn more?). Relevance is free — call the audience out. The components do the hard work: manufacture Novel and Interesting.

---

## Component 1 — New Reveal + Outcome Mapping

**One-line definition.** Make an existing thing feel new by revealing *something has changed* about it, then tie that change to a result the viewer demonstrably wants.

**Mechanism / formula.** `reveal new aspect of a thing + tie it to an outcome you know the viewer wants`. Two paths: **Path A (genuinely new)** — a real new tool/study/method, just reveal it exists. **Path B (old topic, ~90% of cases)** — reveal a new *aspect/angle*: a new frame, name, update, or finding. You don't invent a new topic; you find a new door into the old one.

**Brain-default it defeats.** Autopilot consumption filters out the familiar — the brain only stops on what's *new* (Novel). But novel-and-irrelevant still gets scrolled, so Outcome Mapping makes them *stay* (Relevant). Load-bearing line: *the new reveal makes them LOOK; the outcome mapping makes them STAY.*

**Diagnostic questions.**
1. Is this genuinely new, or do I need a reframe / angle / framework / name to make it *feel* new?
2. What outcome does my viewer genuinely want — and how do I connect the new thing to it?

**Templates (vary, never verbatim).**
- "*X* new thing just changed the way you get *Y* result."
- "You've probably heard of *X* — well, *Y* just happened that changed how you do *X* to get *Z*."
- "Naming move: coin a proprietary name for the old mechanic (naming = the cheapest novelty lever)."

**What breaks it.** A single-job hook (reveal with no outcome, or outcome with no reveal). A naked claim with no wanted result attached — Novel without Relevant gets scrolled anyway. Forgetting Path B and waiting for a genuinely-new topic that never comes.

## Component 2 — Contrast Framing

**One-line definition.** Position the new reveal directly against the *specific old belief* the audience already holds, so "new" becomes legible.

**Mechanism / formula.** `state the audience's held belief (old) → set the new angle as its TRUE OPPOSITE`. New means nothing in isolation; the brain reads "new" only through a comparative relationship to "old." The contrast opens a *gap* between "what I thought was true" and "this new reality." When the brain suspects it might be wrong, it *freezes and pauses to recalibrate* — that pause is intrigue (Interesting).

**Brain-default it defeats.** The brain can't perceive "new" standing alone; it needs the old reference point to measure against. An un-contrasted reveal is a *fact*; a contrasted reveal is an *event*.

**Diagnostic question.** What does my audience already believe about this topic that I can hold constant as the "old" belief — and how do I set my new angle as its direct opposite?

**Templates (vary, never verbatim).**
- "Everyone thinks *X*, but actually it's *Y*."
- "You've been told *X* your whole life — turns out *X* is wrong; *Y* gets you *Z*."
- "*X* used to be the gold standard — now *Y* makes *X* obsolete."

**What breaks it.** Grabbing an *unrelated* old thing and slapping it next to the new one — that yields confusion, not contrast. A strawman belief the audience doesn't actually hold (no real gap). An *adjacent* belief instead of the *actual* one. The contrast must be a true opposite of the audience's real held belief about *this* topic, or the gap never opens.

## Component 3 — Urgency (The Turbo Button — OPTIONAL)

**One-line definition.** A timeliness red-alert that says *pay attention now, because something just changed or is about to change* — layered on top of Steps 1–2 for extra intrigue.

**Mechanism / formula.** `compress time: "this just changed / is about to stop changing"`. Engages **recency bias** — the brain over-weights recent events because it wants to know whether it must update its model. The one component you are *allowed to skip*.

**Brain-default it defeats.** Baseline, evergreen things feel low-priority; the brain deprioritizes anything that isn't time-sensitive.

**Diagnostic question.** Is there an HONEST window — something that just happened, or is about to stop being available/true? If yes, use it. If no, skip (and skipping is correct, not a gap).

**Templates (vary, never verbatim) — only if the window is REAL.**
- "This only became possible a couple days ago."
- "For years this didn't work, until this one change."
- "This window won't stay open forever."
- "Barely anyone is trained on this yet."

**What breaks it.** Bolted-on fake urgency — invented deadlines, manufactured scarcity. Audiences smell it instantly, and the moment they do you lose *all* trust and it reads as a sale. Recency also has a half-life: "just dropped yesterday" decays, so urgency on aging content must be refreshed or rotated to a different real window. Evergreen pieces should usually skip it. *"In 2002 that worked. 2026, not a chance."*

## Component 4 — Bullseye Proof (The Trust Ladder)

**One-line definition.** Show evidence the reveal is true, climbing as close to the viewer's own life as the honest facts allow.

**Mechanism / formula.** `pick the highest HONEST rung on the Trust Ladder`. The closer the proof is framed to the viewer's life, the more they believe — driven by **self-identification**: we distrust third-party claims and trust *versions of ourselves* getting the result.

| Rung | Proof type | Trust |
|---|---|---|
| **Top — Bullseye** | A 1-to-1 example that mimics the viewer (same age, look, problem, situation). If you ARE the demo, a result *you* got counts. | 9–10 |
| **Middle — Warm Crowd** | The generalized "we" — "a bunch of people got this." Proxy for the crowd. | 6–7 |
| **Bottom — Third-Party** | A study, article, stat, award. Forces the viewer to imagine "were they like me?" | 5 |

**Brain-default it defeats.** Innate distrust of third-party claims and authority-aggregate proof. Proximity beats prestige; climb toward the *viewer*, not toward credentials.

**Diagnostic question.** What's the highest rung I can reach *honestly*? Do I have a viewer-mimic example (or am I the demo)? If not, warm crowd; if not, third-party — but never fabricate a rung.

**Templates (vary, never verbatim).**
- Bullseye: "I've got someone exactly your age / in your exact situation who [specific result]."
- Warm crowd: "A bunch of people in [audience] just did this and [result]."
- Third-party: "There's research showing [finding]." (use only when no closer rung is honestly available)

**What breaks it.** Fabricating proof to climb the ladder (automatic fail — burns trust permanently). A vague avatar — "same age, same problem, same situation" requires avatar specificity; no avatar = no bullseye, stuck at the bottom rung. Reaching for the prestige-study when a relatable singular example was available.

## Component 5 — Protect the Illusion

**One-line definition.** Don't tear down what the first four built — commit to the storyline and deliver it as a secret, not a sale.

**Mechanism / formula (two halves).**

**5a — Don't Show the Mascot.** A kid at Disney sees Mickey and the magic is total — until he wanders behind a gate, sees the mascot head on the ground and a sweaty guy half out of costume. *Poof.* In content, the mascot reveal is any hedge admitting your "new" angle is just an old thing dressed up. Commit to the storyline; don't hedge, don't downplay, don't reveal the frame is a mascot in disguise. This is *not lying* — anyone who doesn't know/remember the old frame experiences the new frame as genuinely new.

**5b — The Gossip Whisperer.** Same fact, two tones. **Town Crier:** "HUGE NEWS. This changes everything. Pay attention NOW." → reads like a billboard → sales-resistance → distrust. **Gossip Whisperer:** "okay… so not that many people know about this yet…" → feels like a secret behind a curtain → intrigue. The whisper wins every time, and it's not close. You're not selling them something; you're *sharing* something with them.

**Brain-default it defeats.** Loops, once opened, want closure — a self-sabotaging hedge collapses the loop and kills intrigue. And in 2026 the *salesy register itself* is the disqualifier independent of content quality; people are trained to distrust any tone that feels salesy.

**Diagnostic questions.** Where am I tempted to hedge — and did I pre-commit to the storyline? Does this read as a secret being shared, or a billboard being shouted?

**Templates (vary, never verbatim).**
- Commit: lead with the new frame plainly; cut every "of course this is really just…", "people have known this for decades", "I'm no expert but…", "you've probably heard this…".
- Whisper (in text): lowercase energy, conspiratorial framing ("here's the part nobody mentions"), under-claim the magnitude verbally while over-delivering the substance.

**What breaks it.** *The mascot reveal* — any hedge, false modesty, or "this is nothing new really" / "everyone knows this." False modesty is a mascot reveal too: it tells the brain the thing is neither new nor authoritative. *Town-crier tone* — exclamation-point billboard energy that trips sales-resistance. The illusion dies by self-sabotage, not by attack; audiences rarely catch you — you hand them the mascot head.

## Master Cheat-Sheet

| Component | Does what | Hook or Body | Template (vary, never verbatim) | Killer mistake |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. New Reveal + Outcome** | Makes them LOOK (novel) + STAY (relevant) | **Hook** | "*X* just changed how you get *Y*" / "you've heard of *X* — well *Y* just happened" | Single-job hook; reveal with no wanted outcome |
| **2. Contrast Framing** | Opens the gap → intrigue (interesting) | **Hook** | "Everyone thinks *X*, but actually *Y*" / "*X* used to be the gold standard — *Y* makes it obsolete" | Unrelated/strawman contrast = confusion, not gap |
| **3. Urgency (optional)** | Time-compression via recency bias | **Hook** (if used) | "this only became possible a couple days ago" / "barely anyone is trained on this yet" | Bolted-on fake deadline → instant total trust loss |
| **4. Bullseye Proof** | Makes the reveal believable via self-identification | **Body** | "someone exactly your age / in your situation just [result]" | Fabricated proof; reaching for the study over the mimic |
| **5. Protect the Illusion** | Keeps it from collapsing | **Body + delivery** | commit to the storyline; whisper, don't shout | Mascot reveal (hedge / false modesty); town-crier tone |

**Memorize:** Hook carries 1–3. Body and delivery carry 4–5. Diagnose a weak piece by which *zone* fails.

## Worked Mini-Example — Gutter Cleaning (fresh topic, fully assembled)

Avatar: a homeowner who half-remembers being told to clean gutters twice a year and finds the whole chore tedious. Held belief = *"gutter cleaning is about removing leaves so water flows."* Wanted outcome = *avoid the expensive damage without the miserable ladder-and-scoop ritual.*

- **Step 1 (New Reveal + Outcome).** "Turns out the leaves were never the real problem — the *slope* is. Re-pitching your gutters by a quarter-inch does more to protect your foundation than ten cleanings a year." (New aspect = slope/pitch; outcome = foundation protection without the ritual.)
- **Step 2 (Contrast).** "You've been told for years to clean your gutters twice a season. The clogs were never what cracked foundations — water sitting in a *flat* gutter is. Scooping leaves treats the symptom; the pitch is the cause." (New angle set as the true opposite of the held belief.)
- **Step 3 (Urgency).** Honest window check: is there a real "just changed / about to stop" lever? Seasonal pre-freeze framing is honest in autumn ("before the first hard freeze locks standing water into ice that pries the seams apart") — use it only in that window. No seasonal hook? **Skip it** rather than fake a deadline.
- **Step 4 (Bullseye Proof).** Bottom rung: "studies link standing water to foundation settling." Top rung: "I had a homeowner on the same kind of split-level as yours — cleaned religiously, still got a wet basement. We re-pitched two runs in an afternoon, and the next storm her basement stayed bone dry." (Viewer-mimic, not a study.)
- **Step 5 (Protect).** Deliver it as a quiet field secret. Do **not** say "of course, every roofer has known about gutter pitch forever" — that takes the head off the mascot and the loop dies. Whisper the magnitude ("most homeowners have never had anyone check this"), over-deliver the substance.

Honesty spine check: pitch *does* matter, the homeowner result is real, the freeze window is genuine in season. The *novelty* is manufactured; every *fact* is true.

## Full Assembly Order — Build Checklist

Run top to bottom. Stop and fix before advancing if any gate fails.

1. **Pre-flight (from `../genius.md` Decision Framework).** Topic genuinely new or old (default: old → Path B)? · Name the avatar's *actual held belief* (required for Step 2 — if you can't, define the avatar first) · Name the *wanted outcome* (required for Step 1) · Is there an HONEST urgency window? · Highest HONEST trust rung? · Where am I tempted to hedge?
2. **Step 1 — New Reveal + Outcome.** Find the new angle (frame / name / update / finding). Tie it to the wanted outcome. Confirm the hook carries *both*, not one.
3. **Step 2 — Contrast Framing.** State the held belief; set the new angle as its *true opposite*. Confirm a real gap opens (not confusion).
4. **Step 3 — Urgency.** Real window? Layer it onto the hook. No window? Skip — and that's correct.
5. **Step 4 — Bullseye Proof.** Climb to the highest honest rung (mimic > warm crowd > third-party). Never fabricate to climb.
6. **Step 5 — Protect the Illusion.** Scrub every hedge / false-modesty / "everyone knows this." Set the whole delivery to gossip-whisperer, not town-crier.
7. **Final gate — Honesty Spine (non-negotiable).** The illusion is of NOVELTY only. Facts, urgency windows, and proof must be REAL. Any fabricated fact, faked deadline, or invented proof = automatic fail, no matter how good the build. Verify before delivery; if you can't verify a claim, cut it or label it, never assert it.

---

## Honesty Spine (read every build)

The one unbreakable line across all five components: you manufacture the *feeling* of novelty, never the *facts*. Step 1 reveal must be real; Step 3 window must be real; Step 4 proof must be real. Commit to the storyline (Step 5) without hedging — that's allowed, because anyone who doesn't know the old frame experiences the new one as genuinely new. Fabricating a fact, a deadline, or a case study is the fastest loop-collapse and the one move that permanently burns trust. When tempted, say "I don't know" or cut the claim. The framework wins on real substance delivered as a secret — not on invented substance shouted as a sale.
