# /satori-predictive-empathy — Predictive Empathy Pass

Most designs are loud because the designer assumed the viewer was already convinced. They aren't. This workflow rewrites a design (or briefs a draft) for the *next* emotion the viewer will carry — into the rest of their day.

## Pre-Flight Gate

**Use this when**:
- A draft design feels loud / aggressive / shouty
- The brief assumes audience is pre-aligned (it usually isn't)
- A live ad / campaign isn't converting and the diagnosis is "it's not landing emotionally"
- You're working in a category that defaults to alarmist tone (insurance, security, health, safety)

**Do NOT use this when**:
- The design is editorial / artistic and emotional landing isn't a brief priority
- The audience IS pre-aligned (existing customers, internal communications) — assumption check first
- The brief deliberately calls for confrontation / disruption / alarm (ACT-UP-style activist work, urgency campaigns) — predictive empathy can dilute correct intent

## Execution

### Step 1: Identify the Wrong Emotional Assumption

Examine the current design. Document the implicit assumption:

| Loud-default tone | Implicit assumption |
|---|---|
| Shouted headline | Viewer is already paying close attention |
| Dramatic imagery | Viewer is already emotionally engaged |
| Urgent CTA ("ACT NOW") | Viewer is already considering action |
| High-contrast palette | Viewer is already filtering for relevance |
| Stat-led ("87% OF X") | Viewer already trusts the stat-source |

Each assumption should be questioned. Most viewers are **none of those things** when they encounter the design.

### Step 2: Map the Audience State

Who is the actual viewer at the moment of contact?

Document:
- **Pre-state emotion**: What were they feeling 30 seconds before encountering this? (Distracted? Stressed? Bored? Curious? Skeptical?)
- **Awareness level** (Schwartz scale): Unaware / problem-aware / solution-aware / product-aware / most-aware
- **Trust level**: Cold / warm / hot
- **Time-window**: Skim (<3 sec) / read (10-30 sec) / study (60+ sec)

**Most-common reality**: Cold + distracted + problem-aware (at best). Predictive empathy assumes this state by default.

### Step 3: Define the Desired *Next* Emotion

Not the impact emotion. The post-design emotion they carry into the next 60 seconds.

**Desired next-emotions cheat sheet**:
- **Calm reassurance** (insurance, financial, health) — they walk away breathing easier
- **Curious recognition** (thought leadership, education) — they walk away thinking "huh, that's true"
- **Quiet confidence** (premium, lifestyle) — they walk away feeling "I belong with this"
- **Permission relief** (wellness, personal growth) — they walk away feeling "it's okay to want this"
- **Energized clarity** (productivity, tools) — they walk away knowing the next step
- **Generous warmth** (community, family, hospitality) — they walk away feeling welcomed
- **Solemn resolve** (advocacy, social impact) — they walk away with weight
- **Mischievous delight** (creative, lifestyle, food) — they walk away grinning

Pick ONE. Predictive empathy fails when 2+ next-emotions compete.

### Step 4: Apply the Empathy Shifts

Now rewrite the design's emotional architecture. Apply these shifts as needed:

#### Shift A — Tone (Headline / Copy)
- **Loud → Quiet**: "STOP HACKERS DEAD" → "Quiet protection, working in the background"
- **Demand → Invitation**: "BUY NOW" → "Find your fit"
- **Stat-shout → Recognition-quiet**: "87% IGNORE THIS" → "Most people don't notice. You did."

#### Shift B — Visual Energy
- **High contrast → Soft contrast**: Pure black/white/red → muted neutrals + one accent
- **Dramatic crop → Breathing crop**: Tight zoom on alarmed face → mid-shot with breathing room
- **Dense composition → White space**: Stacked layers → fewer elements with intentional emptiness

#### Shift C — One Trust Cue
Predictive empathy almost always benefits from ONE added trust cue:
- A small testimonial line (not a wall)
- A reassurance phrase in body copy ("at your pace")
- A subtle iconic symbol of trust (verified mark, decade-marker, partnership)
- A single soft data point (instead of stat-blast)

#### Shift D — Palette Migration
Migrate the palette toward the *desired-end-emotion*, not the alarm-emotion:

| Desired next-emotion | Palette direction |
|---|---|
| Calm reassurance | Cool blues, warm neutrals, soft greens |
| Curious recognition | Warm beiges, dusty pastels, ink black |
| Quiet confidence | Deep neutrals (charcoal, cream, oxblood) |
| Permission relief | Warm pinks, sage greens, off-whites |
| Energized clarity | Crisp whites, primary accent, high contrast (controlled) |
| Generous warmth | Terracotta, mustard, cream, deep teal |
| Solemn resolve | Dim earth tones, low-saturation, single bright punctuation |
| Mischievous delight | Unexpected pairings (lime + lavender, peach + ink) |

#### Shift E — Pacing Inserts (for sequenced media)
For carousels, scrolls, sequences: insert breathing pages — pure white, single image, no copy. They function as "pull" beats in the temporal flow ladder (Level 6).

### Step 5: Validate the Rewrite

Read the rewritten design with three audience states in mind:
1. **Cold + distracted**: Does it land without demanding pre-attention?
2. **Skeptical, mildly interested**: Does it earn the next 5 seconds?
3. **Already-considering**: Does it confirm the next-emotion or fight it?

If any of the three fails, iterate.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Content type | Common loud-default | Empathy shift |
|---|---|---|
| **Insurance / financial** | Alarmist headline, dark colors | Calm tone + breathing crop + soft palette |
| **Cybersecurity** | "Threats! Hackers!" copy | "Quiet protection" + soft contrast |
| **Health / medical** | Scary symptom headlines | Reassurance + permission tone |
| **Productivity tool** | "Crush your to-do list" | "Move at your pace" |
| **Real estate listing** | "Don't miss out!" urgency | "Imagine yourself here" + warm palette |
| **Streetwear ad** | "Be different!" exhortation | Implied invitation + recognition-quiet tone |
| **Newsletter promo** | Long sales argument | One promise + one trust cue + breathing space |
| **Social activist campaign** | All caps, alarm palette | Solemn resolve + earth tones + single punctuation |

## Output Requirements

Pass must include:
1. Audience state map (pre-state + awareness + trust + time)
2. Wrong-assumption diagnosis
3. Desired next-emotion (ONE)
4. Empathy shifts (tone + visual + trust cue + palette + pacing)
5. Three-state validation
6. Executable directives at element level
7. Anti-pattern checklist passed
