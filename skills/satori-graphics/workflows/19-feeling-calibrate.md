# /satori-feeling-calibrate — Feeling Calibration

> Same content, different feeling — retune premium / cheap / playful / reassuring through typography, color, layout, and surface alone, then prove the target fits who actually sees it.

The content does not change. The message, the offer, the price, the copy stay exactly as they are. Only the *feeling* moves — and it moves through four levers of visual language, not through rewriting a single word.

> *"we can take the same burger for our design here and we can make it appear cheap using layout, color, and typography choices. Or we can conversely make it feel more expensive and more premium... achieved with the visual language decisions alone."* — Satori

## Pre-Flight Gate

**Use this when**:
- The content is locked and correct, but the *feel* is wrong for the target (looks cheap when it should read premium, or stiff when it should read playful)
- You need to move an asset up- or down-market without changing the offer, copy, or price
- A client says "make it feel more expensive / more approachable / more fun" and you need to convert that into concrete lever moves
- You're producing two versions of the same asset for A/B or for different tiers (value line vs. flagship) and need them to *feel* distinct while saying the same thing

**Do NOT use this when**:
- The *content itself* is the problem — wrong offer, weak message, unclear brief → fix that first with `/satori-why-before-what` or a one-sentence reduction (GP-08). Calibrating the feeling of a broken message just makes a broken message feel nicer.
- You have no fixed audience yet. Feeling is meaningless without a viewer to feel it. Establish the audience first, or the audience-fit veto (Step 2) has nothing to check against.
- The task is a raw color-system build from scratch → run `/satori-color` first, then return here to apply it as one of the four levers.
- The design is still at concept/sketch stage → the primitive and leverage point aren't locked yet; feeling is a finish-layer decision, not a foundation one. Route to `/satori-why-before-what`.

## Execution

The whole method rests on one Satori claim: perception is engineered by a small set of visual-language levers, and you can dial it deliberately.

> *"how typography psychology affects a person, how color schemes and layouts, and even things like solid color or gradients all influence perception."* — Satori

### Step 1 — Lock the content, name the CURRENT feeling

Before touching anything, write down what is *not allowed to move*:

- **The fixed content**: the headline text, body copy, offer, price, logo, product. Copy this into the spec as `LOCKED`. If you find yourself rewriting it, stop — that is a different workflow.
- **The current feeling, named in one word + one clause**: e.g. "cheap — the price shouts louder than the product," "sterile — technically clean but cold," "loud — assumes I'm already sold." Be honest; a vague "meh" is not a diagnosis.

Decision forced: *What single word describes the feeling this asset gives off right now?* If you can't name it, you can't calibrate away from it.

### Step 2 — Set the TARGET feeling, then prove it fits the audience (VETO)

Pick ONE target feeling. The canonical four (extendable, but pick one primary):

| Target | One-line intent |
|---|---|
| **Premium** | "This is worth more than it costs. I belong with this." |
| **Cheap / value** | "This is a deal. Grab it before it's gone." |
| **Playful** | "This is fun. I'm allowed to enjoy this." |
| **Reassuring** | "This is safe. I can relax and trust it." |

The target is not a taste preference — it is a match to a specific restaurant, a specific shelf, a specific buyer.

> *"a poster for McDonald's is going to be vastly different in style to that of Gordon Ramsay's burger restaurant."* — Satori

Now run the **audience-fit veto**. The target feeling must be both *reachable* (the levers can get there on this surface) and *appropriate* (the people who actually see it will read it as intended and can act on it).

> *"It is no good trying to make your design premium if the end users are teenagers or students with little to no money at disposal."* — Satori

Answer all three. Any "no" halts the calibration:

- [ ] **Who actually sees this?** (name the real viewer, not the aspirational one)
- [ ] **Can they act on the target feeling?** (premium to a broke audience creates aspiration you can't monetize; cheap to a luxury buyer reads as untrustworthy)
- [ ] **Does the target feeling match the price / offer / brand promise?** (a $9 product dressed as $900 reads as a lie, not a bargain)

If the veto fails, the fix is to **re-choose the target**, not to override the veto. A calibration the audience can't afford or won't believe is a failed calibration regardless of how beautiful it is.

### Step 3 — Match the ARRIVAL emotion (predictive-empathy front)

Feeling is a hand-off, not a broadcast. Before specifying what they walk away with, specify the state they walk *in* with — because the calibration has to meet them there.

- **Arrival emotion**: what is the viewer feeling in the 30 seconds *before* contact? (Distracted mid-scroll? Skeptical, comparing prices? Bored, hungry, hurried?)
- **The gap**: the distance between arrival emotion and target feeling is the work. Big gap (bored-scroller → premium reverence) needs a stronger, more disruptive lever set than a small gap (already-browsing-luxury → premium confirmation).

Decision forced: *Does the target feeling meet the viewer where they are, or does it assume a state they're not in?* If the gap is too large for one asset to cross, stage it (see Pacing, below) or soften the target.

### Step 4 — Prescribe the per-lever moves (the four dials)

This is the heart. For the chosen target, prescribe concrete moves on all four levers. Vague adjectives ("make the type nicer") are banned — every move names a property and a direction.

**The four levers:**

1. **TYPOGRAPHY psychology** — weight, serif vs. sans, letter-spacing (tracking) & leading, case.
2. **COLOR scheme** — palette temperature, saturation/chroma, value contrast, number of hues. *(Run `/satori-color` for the deep pass; use the summary below to set direction.)*
3. **LAYOUT** — density, whitespace, alignment, hierarchy / what dominates.
4. **SURFACE treatment** — solid flat color vs. gradient, texture, finish cues (matte/gloss/foil/emboss), effects (shadow/bevel/sheen).

Use the calibration matrix as the starting prescription, then tailor to the specific asset:

| Lever | → PREMIUM | → CHEAP / VALUE | → PLAYFUL | → REASSURING |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Typography** | High-contrast serif or refined thin sans; wide tracking on caps; light–regular weight; small type on a big canvas; restraint | Heavy/black condensed sans; ALL-CAPS; tight tracking; multiple weights shouting; oversized price numerals | Rounded / hand-drawn sans; mixed case; bouncy baseline; script accents; varied weights with energy | Humanist sans, medium weight; sentence case; comfortable leading; soft, open terminals |
| **Color** | 1–2 colors + neutral; low saturation; deep neutrals (charcoal, cream, ink, oxblood); high *value* contrast, low *chroma*; monochrome + one metallic | Many saturated hues; hot reds & yellows (appetite/urgency); max chroma; loud contrast; "sale" red | Bright multi-hue; mid–high saturation; unexpected pairings (lime + lavender, peach + ink) | Cool blues / soft greens / warm neutrals; low–mid saturation; soft, gentle contrast |
| **Layout** | Extreme whitespace; low density; one leverage point; wide margins; disciplined alignment; asymmetric hero with air | High density; packed; everything big; starbursts / banners; price dominates; minimal whitespace | Asymmetric; tilted / overlapping elements; deliberate grid-breaks; energetic but breathing | Generous breathing space; calm alignment; horizontal emphasis; clear single hierarchy; one trust cue |
| **Surface** | Solid flat fill or subtle matte texture; avoid loud gradients; matte / uncoated / foil / emboss cues; no bevels | Glossy gradients; shiny highlights; drop shadows / bevels; "value" badges; plastic sheen | Soft gradients; stickers; organic blobs; tactile texture; rounded corners | Solid soft fills or one gentle low-contrast gradient; matte; no hard edges or harsh shadows |
| **Walk-away** | Quiet confidence | Urgency / "get the deal" | Mischievous delight | Calm reassurance |

**Rent check (GP-01):** every prescribed move must pay rent toward the target feeling. If a move is there because it "looks designy" rather than because it pushes the dial toward the target, evict it. Feeling is not permission to decorate.

Write each move as an executable directive, not a description:
```
LEVER: [Typography / Color / Layout / Surface]
FROM:  [current property + value]
TO:    [target property + value]
WHY:   [which target-feeling driver this serves]
```
e.g. `LEVER: Typography · FROM: 800-weight condensed caps, 0 tracking · TO: 300-weight serif, +120 tracking, title case · WHY: restraint + air = premium, not shout.`

### Step 5 — Render the CONTRAST (show the two calibrations)

The difference must be *legible*, not asserted. Where feasible, render (or spec) two calibrations of the **same locked content** side by side so the lever moves are visible as a delta — the classic cheap-burger vs. premium-burger, or McDonald's-energy vs. Gordon-Ramsay-energy.

Present it as a delta table so the client compares *feelings*, not two unrelated designs:

| Lever | Calibration A (e.g. cheap) | Calibration B (e.g. premium) |
|---|---|---|
| Content | *[identical — LOCKED]* | *[identical — LOCKED]* |
| Typography | Black condensed caps, huge price | Thin serif, wide tracking, small price |
| Color | Red + yellow, max chroma | Charcoal + cream, one gold accent |
| Layout | Packed, price dominates | Whitespace, product dominates |
| Surface | Glossy gradient + sunburst | Flat matte, faint emboss |
| Reads as | "$5 deal — eat now" | "$28 experience — worth it" |

If you can only ship one calibration, still spec the *rejected* opposite in one line — it proves the chosen feeling was a decision, not a default.

### Step 6 — Close the loop: lock the WALK-AWAY emotion

Every lever move needs a reason, and one of those reasons must be the emotion the viewer carries out.

> *"make sure one of those reasons is what emotions you want the viewer to walk away with."* — Satori

State the single walk-away emotion (from the matrix row, or a tailored one). This is the acceptance criterion for the whole calibration: if the levers are pulled to "premium" but the viewer walks away feeling *pressured* or *priced-out*, the calibration failed even if it's beautiful. Confirm the arrival → walk-away arc actually closes:

- Arrival emotion (Step 3): `[...]`
- Walk-away emotion (locked): `[...]`
- The levers move the viewer from the first to the second. If they don't, iterate the weakest lever.

Pick ONE walk-away emotion. Two competing ones (premium *and* urgent) cancel and the asset reads as confused. (This is the same discipline as `/satori-predictive-empathy` Step 3 — one next-emotion, never two.)

### Step 7 — Audience-fit re-check + output

Re-run the Step 2 veto against the *finished* calibration (levers can drift past the target during execution — "premium" quietly becoming "unreadably subtle" for a fast-scroll feed). Then emit the spec.

## Content-Type Adaptations

| Surface | How the calibration method shifts |
|---|---|
| **Poster / print** | Surface lever becomes *physical* — paper stock, coated vs. uncoated, foil, emboss carry the feeling as much as the pixels. Premium lives in margin + stock; cheap lives in banner + gloss. The big canvas makes the typography scale/tracking move the loudest dial. |
| **Logo / identity** | Nearly all feeling rides on the type primitive + a single color; surface gradients/effects date a mark and are usually off the table (GP-10). Calibrate weight, serif/sans, and one color only. Premium = thin refined mark with air; cheap = fat bold + sunburst. Re-check it holds at thumbnail (transferability). |
| **UI / product** | Feeling = component density, corner radius, shadow depth, motion, accent restraint. Premium = flat, tight type scale, one restrained accent, subtle motion; cheap = loud CTAs, heavy shadows, many colors. Audience-fit is sharp here: don't "premium" a budget tool whose users *want* obvious, unmissable buttons. |
| **Social / feed** | The calibration must survive thumbnail + fast scroll (LIFT-T). A too-subtle "premium" pull goes invisible in-feed — you may deliberately push ONE lever (usually value contrast or type scale) harder than the pure target to stay legible, and note the trade-off. Reconcile with `/satori-lift-audit`. |
| **Packaging** | Surface lever dominates: substrate, foil, matte varnish, emboss, finish. Feeling is read *against shelf neighbors* (the McDonald's-vs-Ramsay shelf), so calibrate relative to the category, not in a vacuum. The price point on the shelf must match the feeling or it reads as deception, not value. |
| **Ad creative** | Calibrate to funnel stage + platform + targeting. Cheap/urgent for performance & discount; premium/quiet for brand & consideration. Audience-fit maps directly to the ad's targeting spec, and the walk-away emotion must point at the specific action the ad is buying. |

## Output Requirements

The deliverable is a **feeling-calibration spec** containing exactly:

```markdown
# Feeling Calibration — [asset name]

## Locked Content (does NOT change)
- Headline / copy / offer / price / logo: [verbatim — LOCKED]

## Feeling Shift
- Current feeling: [one word + one clause]
- Target feeling: [premium / cheap / playful / reassuring / other]

## Audience-Fit Check (VETO)
- Who actually sees this: [...]
- Can they act on the target feeling? [Y/N — reason]
- Does target match price/offer/brand? [Y/N — reason]
- Verdict: [PASS / RE-CHOOSE TARGET]

## Emotion Arc
- Arrival emotion (in): [...]
- Walk-away emotion (out, locked, ONE): [...]

## Per-Lever Moves (executable)
- Typography: FROM [...] → TO [...] · WHY [...]
- Color:      FROM [...] → TO [...] · WHY [...]   (deep pass: /satori-color)
- Layout:     FROM [...] → TO [...] · WHY [...]
- Surface:    FROM [...] → TO [...] · WHY [...]

## Contrast (same content, two feelings)
- [delta table: cheap calibration vs. premium calibration]
- Rejected opposite (if shipping one): [one line]

## Anti-Pattern Check
- [ ] Content unchanged (feeling moved, message didn't)
- [ ] ONE target feeling, ONE walk-away emotion
- [ ] Audience-fit veto passed on the FINISHED calibration
- [ ] Every lever move pays rent toward the target (no decoration)
```

Naked adjectives ("cleaner," "more premium vibe") are not a deliverable. Every line names a property, a direction, and a reason.

## Quality Gate

Guards against these genius.md auto-reject anti-patterns:

- [ ] **#8 Aesthetic-first decisions** — the feeling is a *stated target checked against an audience*, not a mood chosen for its own sake. Levers serve the target; the target serves the viewer.
- [ ] **#7 Loud-by-default** — "cheap/urgent" is a deliberately chosen calibration for a matched audience, never the lazy fallback. If the target is premium or reassuring, no loud-default move survives.
- [ ] **#6 More-equals-better layering** — calibration moves *dials*, it doesn't *stack effects*. Premium especially is subtraction; if the fix is "add more," it's wrong.
- [ ] **GP-01 rent** — every lever move has a documented why tied to the target feeling. Un-justified moves are evicted.
- [ ] **GP-11 anti-AI-slop** — a "premium" calibration must not collapse into sterile AI-perfect symmetry; keep ≥1 deliberate human-imperfection so restraint reads as *intent*, not emptiness.
- [ ] **Workflow-specific veto — content drift**: if any locked content changed, the calibration is void. Feeling moved, message did not.
- [ ] **Workflow-specific veto — audience mismatch**: a target the viewer can't afford, can't act on, or won't believe = FAIL, regardless of aesthetic quality. Re-choose the target; never override the veto.

**Pass criteria**: single target feeling, single walk-away emotion, all four levers prescribed as executable FROM→TO moves, audience-fit veto passed on the finished asset, content demonstrably unchanged. Any veto failure = halt and re-choose, do not deliver.
