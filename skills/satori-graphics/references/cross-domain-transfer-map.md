# Cross-Domain Transfer Map

Satori's frameworks are graphic-design-native but transfer across domains. Use this map to apply Satori thinking to copy, sequences, naming, positioning, voice, and editing.

---

## Why These Transfer

The core principle — **design is decision-making before it is expression** — is domain-agnostic. Every Satori framework structures decisions before output. That structure works wherever output is judged on:
- **Clarity** (is the message clear?)
- **Memorability** (does it lodge?)
- **Emotional landing** (does the receiver carry the desired emotion away?)
- **Hierarchy** (does the receiver know what to focus on?)

Copy, sequences, naming, voice, positioning, editing — all of these depend on the same four properties.

## Transfer Map

### Rent Test (GP-01) → Copywriting

**Original**: Every layout element must serve concept, hierarchy, or psychology — else evict.
**Transfer**: Every sentence in copy must serve **story, proof, or emotion** — else evict.

**Application sequence**:
1. After draft, label each sentence: S (story), P (proof), E (emotion), or D (drift)
2. Evict every D
3. If a sentence is doing two jobs (S + E), confirm both are intended; otherwise split
4. If a paragraph has 3+ sentences with the same label, audit for repetition

**Example**:
- ❌ "We've been in business for 15 years and we're known for our craftsmanship and quality." (3 D-sentences masquerading as proof)
- ✅ "Fifteen years. Two thousand projects. One return rate under 1%." (Three P-sentences earning rent)

### Predictive Empathy (GP-02) → Email Sequences

**Original**: Design for the *next* emotion the viewer carries, not the impact emotion.
**Transfer**: Design email sequences for the *end-of-email* emotion, not the *open-rate* emotion.

**Application**:
- Subject line / preview text engineers the open emotion (curiosity, urgency, recognition)
- BUT the body sequence must engineer the *end* emotion — what they feel as they hit reply / click / close
- Design backwards from desired end-emotion

**Common failure**: Loud subject lines that match loud body copy → reader feels manipulated, doesn't open #2.

**Fix**: Loud open emotion → soft, generous body → end-emotion = "this person gets me." Trust compounds; reply rate grows.

### Memory Encoding (GP-03) → Headline Craft

**Original**: Designs become memorable when they require resolution.
**Transfer**: Headlines that lodge ask a question or stage a paradox vs deliver an answer.

**Headline forms that encode**:
- **Paradox**: "The faster you write, the slower readers go."
- **Specific incongruity**: "I deleted 80% of my portfolio. My booking rate doubled."
- **Question implied**: "What gets ignored when everything fits?"
- **Concrete absurdity**: "She designed her resignation letter as a wedding invitation."

**Headline forms that don't encode** (hand-deliver meaning):
- "5 Tips to Improve Your Writing"
- "How to Get More Clients"
- "The Secret to Better Copy"

### LIFT System (GP-06) → Information Architecture / Page Design

**Original**: Leverage / Eye / Friction / Transferability for layouts.
**Transfer**: Same four for any information-bearing page (landing pages, sales letters, dashboards, app screens).

| LIFT | Page application |
|---|---|
| **Leverage point** | Primary CTA or primary message — what you want them to do/feel |
| **Eye choreography** | The scan path through hero → proof → secondary → CTA |
| **Friction** | Forced pause: testimonial overlay, animation, contrast block; never passive interruption |
| **Transferability** | The page works on mobile, desktop, share-card preview, screenshot |

**Application sequence**:
1. Identify the page's leverage (the one action / one feeling)
2. Choreograph scan from leverage outward
3. Place ONE friction element (testimonial / contrast / unexpected stat) to re-engage at scroll-fall-off point
4. Test responsive integrity at every breakpoint

### Verb-Not-Noun (GP-07) → Brand Naming

**Original**: Logos start from verbs (what the brand does), not nouns (what the brand is).
**Transfer**: Brand names from verbs outperform brand names from nouns.

**Examples**:
- ❌ "PaperLeaf" (noun stack — what is it?)
- ✅ "Compose" (verb — what it does for you)
- ❌ "QuantumStone" (noun stack — random qualifier)
- ✅ "Anchor" (verb — what it does to your finances)

**Verb-derived names also unlock**:
- Tagline naturally ("Anchor — your finances, held steady")
- Brand voice (verbs imply action, not status)
- Visual primitive (verbs map to shape psychology — see genius.md GP-09)

### One-Sentence Brief (GP-08) → Product Positioning

**Original**: Reduce the brand brief to one sentence before designing.
**Transfer**: Reduce product positioning to one sentence before any feature design / copy / launch.

**Format**: *"[Product] is [verb] [audience] [outcome/feeling]."*

**Examples**:
- "Anchor is the savings app for freelancers who want to feel calm about taxes."
- "Parallax is the newsletter for people who see everything from more than one angle."
- "Listing-Content is the Reels system for SoCal first-time buyers who feel priced out."

**Now every feature, every CTA, every copy block gets tested**:
- Does this feel like the verb? (calm? multi-angle? buyer-empowering?)
- Is this for the audience? (freelancers, multi-angle thinkers, FTB)
- Does this drive the outcome/feeling?

If the design / feature / copy contradicts the sentence, the sentence wins.

### Flip Test (GP-12) → Editing

**Original**: Flip the design upside down to see structure not content.
**Transfer**: Read your draft **backwards** (last paragraph first) or **out of order** to surface structural failures.

**What backwards reading reveals**:
- Each paragraph's standalone strength (or weakness)
- Repetition you became blind to
- Sequence breakdowns (paragraph 4 should be before paragraph 2)
- Closing strength independent of build-up

**What out-of-order reading reveals**:
- Whether transitions are doing work or just connective tissue
- Whether key proofs are landing or buried
- Whether the hook still grips when read mid-piece (an A+ hook should)

---

### Concept Primitive Lock (GP-09) → Voice Systems

**Original**: Lock the visual primitive (line type, geometry) before designing.
**Transfer**: Lock the voice primitive (rhythm, register, signature move) before drafting.

**Voice primitives to choose between** (pick ONE per piece):
- **Short-snap rhythm** (sentences <12 words, hard punctuation)
- **Long-river rhythm** (sentences 30+ words, comma-cascades, builds)
- **Vernacular register** (informal, contractions, "ain't" allowed)
- **Formal register** (no contractions, complete sentences, semicolons)
- **Image-led** (every paragraph opens with a picture)
- **Argument-led** (every paragraph opens with a claim)
- **Question-led** (provocations / Socratic)

**Once locked**: every sentence either honors the primitive or breaks it deliberately. No mixed primitives in a single piece without intent.

---

### Anti-AI-Slop (GP-11) → Copy & Voice

**Original**: Human imperfection as competitive moat in design.
**Transfer**: Human imperfection as competitive moat in copy.

**AI-copy tells**:
- Symmetric paragraph lengths
- Triple-beat anaphora ("not X, not Y, but Z")
- "Here's the thing…" or "Here's what nobody tells you…" openers
- Smooth-transition fillers (moreover, furthermore, indeed)
- Em-dash overuse (4+ per piece)

**Human-copy moves**:
- Asymmetric paragraph lengths (one-line paragraphs after long-river paragraphs)
- A non-sequitur sentence that *almost* doesn't belong but earns its rent
- Slightly off-rhythm phrase that breaks the meter
- Specific concrete detail that no AI would invent (smell, time, weather, body sensation)
- A correction in the prose ("I almost wrote X. The truer version is Y.")

## When NOT to Transfer

Some principles are graphic-design-native and should not transfer literally:

- **Grid taxonomy** — transfers as layout structure (typography baselines), not as conceptual framework
- **Logo as memory hook** — transfers as brand-name role, but the visual specifics don't apply
- **Friction & flow** — transfers conceptually but the implementation is medium-specific (tight leading vs short paragraph stacks)

When transferring, extract the **mechanic** (what the framework forces), not the **expression** (how it manifests visually).
