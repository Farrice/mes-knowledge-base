# Paid to Feel: Judgment Training

## Purpose
Train the creative strategist's primary superpower — the ability to detect signal vs slop in AI-produced creative by developing body-sensation judgment. This is the methodology that makes the role AI-proof.

## When to Use
- When onboarding as a creative strategist and developing your judgment muscle
- When setting up an AI production pipeline that requires human curation
- When calibrating your taste sensitivity for ad selection
- When training a team or client on creative judgment methodology

---

### Completion Target
User understands why judgment (not production) is the monetizable skill in 2026.

### Process
1. **Frame the paradigm shift**: AI made production easy → overabundance of creative → new bottleneck = who decides what's signal vs slop
2. **Establish the core principle**: "AI doesn't have a nervous system." The creative strategist's value is their bodily, intuitive, trained response to creative quality.
3. **Define the role**: You are the orchestrator. You direct AI, judge outputs, and make the final selection decisions. You are not the writer — you are the editor of an infinite writing machine.

### Key Concept
> "There's such an overabundance of content and creative that we need people to sit at the hub, organize it, and make decisions about what to test and what not to test." — Luke Iha

## Step 2: The Headline Feeling Test (Sensitivity Calibration)

### Completion Target
User can reliably detect subtle quality differences between headlines using bodily sensation.

### Process
1. **Present two headlines** for the same product — one with 1 copy block, one with 5-6 copy blocks
2. **Instruction**: "Look at each headline. Don't think about which is better. Ask: what do you FEEL in your body? Something in your chest? Gut? Neck?"
3. **Guide the sensation**: The better headline typically produces "a little bit of amplitude, a little bit of elevation — something here is more interesting, it activates something"
4. **Validate**: Confirm the user felt something different. "If you felt like this headline was better, that's good. It means your feelers are working."
5. **Explain WHY**: The better headline isn't better because it's longer — it's better because it has 6 copy blocks vs 1. The feeling led to the right analytical answer.

### Drill Format
- **Round 1**: 2 obviously different headlines (warm-up)
- **Round 2**: 2 similar-quality headlines (sensitivity training)
- **Round 3**: 5 headlines ranked by the user from best to worst using feeling alone
- **Round 4**: User explains their feeling-based rankings, then cross-references with copy block analysis

### Training Cadence
- Daily: 5-10 minutes, 3 headline pairs
- Weekly: Full Facebook Ad Library audit (20+ ads, ranked by feeling)
- Monthly: Back-test feeling predictions against actual ad performance data

## Step 3: The AI Judgment Pipeline

### Completion Target
User has a complete production → judgment → selection workflow.

### Process

**Phase A — Direct AI Production**
1. Choose the AI tool (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) and the ad format (static, video script, advertorial)
2. Provide strategic inputs: product brief, target audience, awareness level, mechanism, key proof points
3. Generate 10-20 variations in a single batch
4. **Do NOT edit yet** — generation and judgment are separate cognitive modes

**Phase B — First-Pass Judgment (The Feeling Sweep)**
1. Read/scan each variation WITHOUT editing
2. For each: note your bodily sensation (1-10 scale)
3. Sort into three buckets: SIGNAL (7+), MAYBE (4-6), SLOP (1-3)
4. Delete SLOP immediately — don't let it pollute your judgment
5. **Target**: 20-30% should be SIGNAL, 30-40% MAYBE, rest SLOP

**Phase C — Second-Pass Analysis (The Copy Block Audit)**
1. For SIGNAL pieces: verify copy block density (Pain, Promise, Proof, Constraints, Curiosity, Conditions)
2. For MAYBE pieces: identify what's missing — usually proof or constraints
3. Promote 1-2 MAYBEs to SIGNAL with targeted edits
4. **Final selection**: 3-5 pieces to test

**Phase D — Testing Decision**
1. Rank your SIGNAL selections by conviction (feeling + analysis)
2. Assign budget allocation based on conviction (highest conviction = most spend)
3. Set kill criteria before launch (CPA threshold, CTR minimum, etc.)
4. **Meta Andromeda note**: Test across awareness levels, not just creative formats

## Step 4: Sensitivity Development Program

### Completion Target
User has a structured program for continuously improving their judgment sensitivity.

### Process

**Daily Practice (5-10 min)**
- Scan Facebook Ad Library for your client's competitors
- Pick 3 ads you love, 3 you hate
- For each: identify the FEELING first, then the REASON
- Log in a journal: "Felt [sensation] because [copy block/structure reason]"

**Weekly Practice (30 min)**
- Review your week's ad performance data
- Compare your feeling-based predictions to actual results
- Calibrate: Where was your feeling right? Where was it wrong?
- Update your "feeling vocabulary" — develop nuanced descriptions of different sensations

**Monthly Assessment**
- Blind test: Have someone show you 10 ads without performance data
- Rank them by feeling alone
- Cross-reference with actual metrics
- Track your prediction accuracy over time (target: 60%+ correlation)
