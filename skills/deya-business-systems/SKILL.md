# Deya — Business Systems & Validation OS

Deya (creator, digital business manager, founder of DBM Bootcamp) built two six-figure businesses and helped clients scale to seven figures without ever writing a 50-page business plan. Her system replaces planning theater with a 1-page, 2-step engine: validate the Person-Problem-Product triad, then run a simplified sales funnel — optimizing for learning velocity, not planning completeness. The core question shifts from "Is this a good idea?" to "Will a specific named person pay for this today?"

## Quick Reference

- **The 1-page plan has 2 steps**: (1) Is the product idea worthwhile? — Person, Problem, Product. (2) Simplified sales funnel — Traffic, Nurture, Sales.
- **Name a specific person** — a real individual you know or follow, never "women 20-35." Do not pass go until you truly understand this person.
- **Problem severity 1-10** — 1 = pen-cap annoyance, 10 = keeps them up at 2 a.m. Severity sets pricing power. Low-severity problems are fine for low-priced products.
- **Dual gate** — the problem must be worth paying to solve AND the person must be able to afford the solution. Both or kill.
- **Customer voice** — capture how the person describes the problem in their own words; those words become the marketing and sales copy.
- **Passion floor** — founder must care 6-7/10 minimum about solving the problem or they won't survive the growing pains.
- **Beta price ladder** — start low ($5 first 10, $10 next 20...), collect testimonials, raise price with each validation cycle; freelancers +10-20% per new client.
- **Specificity beats saturation** — resonate 100% with a small market rather than 0.001% with a huge one. Generic = replaceable (AI demolished the copying barrier).
- **One platform, all in** — pick where your person actually is; master it before expanding.
- **Touch points scale with price** — $15 product needs 2-3 content touches; high-ticket retainers need case studies, email nurture, and a named diagnostic call.
- **Validation = transactions, not compliments** — talk to 5-10 versions of the person, fake landing pages, pop-up taste tests, beta sales. "I paid for it — boom, validated."

## Anti-Patterns (What Deya Explicitly Warns Against)

Six mistakes Deya names in her own words, verbatim and source-anchored. All six are drawn from the same primary source — the 1-page-plan video transcript captured 2025-12-05 in conversation `d81c6e71-fc59-45d2-a618-45a889a71e89` (`_archive/claude-export-2026-07-01.tar.gz` → `claude-export/normalized/conversations/d81c6e71-fc59-45d2-a618-45a889a71e89.md`; full excerpt copies in `references/source-excerpts.md`).

- **Writing a 50-page business plan.** "I have never once written a 50-page business plan I think they are a huge waste of time and get you stuck in that planning and thinking phase instead of taking action." A plan that outlasts one working session has already failed her method.
- **Thinking in demographics instead of naming a real person.** "if you don't know a specific person that the product is for you just end up thinking in like Target groups and like demographics like oh women age 20 to 35... it's way easier to think 'do I truly understand this person.'" Demographics are a stop condition, not a starting point.
- **Building the thing before checking the market will pay.** "this is another huge mistake I see business owners do is they just they're like I have a good idea I'm just going to do it and then they start making the thing without even checking that the market wants to pay money for this thing."
- **Leaving the problem definition vague.** "your problem has to be ultra specific... lack of clarity is not specific enough, overwhelm is not specific enough, being stuck in business is not specific enough — if it's not specific it's just not going to succeed."
- **Chasing a big saturated market with no competitive advantage (the generic-offer trap).** "isn't easier to Target a bigger Market — yes, but if that market is super saturated and you do not have a competitive Advantage it is going to be way harder to compete in that market than to carve out a little niche market just for you." Elsewhere: "AI demolished the copying barrier" makes any generic offer immediately replaceable.
- **Over-engineering the funnel for a beginner offer.** "I don't want the funnel Bros to be in my comments like 'dear that is such a baby funnel'... this is for beginners, we're just trying to get started, we're not trying to overwhelm anybody." Systems should match capacity, not aspiration.
