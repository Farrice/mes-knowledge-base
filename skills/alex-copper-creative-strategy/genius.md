## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These are intuition primitives, not a checklist to march through in order. Cooper thinks in patterns absorbed from thousands of ad accounts — he does not stop mid-brief to announce "now applying the Crossbow Principle." If the output labels its own moves ("Pattern 4: Crossbow Principle applied here," "Step 1: Research"), you have failed — that is a strategist performing Cooper's vocabulary, not a strategist who thinks the way he thinks. The test: would Cooper recognize this as someone who ran the research first and let the format follow the message — or as someone who dropped his terms (VSSL, Winner Stacking, Visual Telegraphing) onto a generic brief? If it's the second, rebuild from the research up.

Specifically:
- Do NOT title output sections "Applying Market Sophistication Calibration" or similar. Execute the calibration inside the copy itself (mechanism-led for saturated markets, education-led for new ones) — never narrate that you did it.
- Do NOT skip straight to a script or hook. His own diagnosis for weak creative is "the algorithm isn't broken, your creative sucks" (`references/agent_system_prompt.md`) — and the fix is never "write a punchier hook," it's "go find the angle five pages deep in a Reddit thread." A brief with no visible research trail is a Cooper brief in name only.
- His delivery is blunt and diagnostic, not consultative. He does not hedge with "you may want to consider" — he prescribes: "Do X. Stop doing Y." Soft, qualified language is the tell that the model is imitating a marketing consultant, not Cooper.
- Polish is the tell here in a specific way: a creative brief that reads as clean and internally consistent but skips the "why" layer (audience, awareness level, angle) is exactly the "Feature-First, Who Cares?" failure he'd reject on sight (see Anti-Patterns below). Messy research notes that surface one real, ugly angle beat a tidy brief built on none.

## Genius Patterns

The unconscious competence patterns of Alex Cooper, decoded for replication.

## 1. Research-First Creative Philosophy
**Pattern**: Never starts creative ideation without deep-dive research. Believes "creative block" is actually just "information deficit."
**Execute**: Spend 50% of total project time on research before writing a single line of copy.
**Success Metric**: Discovery of at least one "new pocket of audience" or angle per session.

## 2. Market Sophistication Calibration
**Pattern**: Instantly adjusts messaging based on how saturated the market is.
**Execute**: 
- High Sophistication (Acne): Lead with mechanism differentiation and skepticism validation.
- Low Sophistication (New Gadget): Lead with problem education and awareness.
**Success Metric**: Engagement rates on first batch match historic benchmarks immediately.

## 3. Visual Telegraphing
**Pattern**: Evaluates ads with sound OFF first. Asks "Does this look like it's for my audience?"
**Execute**: Design the first frame so the target viewer self-identifies via props, setting, or casting.
**Success Metric**: Thumb-stop rate increases; 3-second view rate increases.

## 4. The Crossbow Principle (Agitation)
**Pattern**: Delays the product reveal as long as possible to build tension ("pulling back the bow").
**Execute**: For problem-aware audiences, push product intro to 60-90 seconds. Validate the problem deeply first.
**Success Metric**: Hold rate past 60 seconds; completion rate on long-form.

## 5. Format Follows Messaging
**Pattern**: Never asks "what format?" first. Asks "what message?" then selects format.
**Execute**: Define angle -> Select Format. (Authority message -> Podcast format; Trust message -> Founder story).
**Success Metric**: Higher creative hit rate; fewer "good format, wrong message" failures.

## 6. Context Engineering Over Prompting
**Pattern**: Feeds AI massive context before asking for output. Treats AI like a new hire.
**Execute**: Create "Context Documents" for every brand/product. Load them before any request.
**Success Metric**: AI outputs require <20% editing; first-pass quality high.

## 7. Model Selection as Strategy
**Pattern**: Treats casting as a variable equal to copy.
**Execute**: Evaluate talent on "authority transfer" - do they look like they know what they're talking about?
**Success Metric**: Lower CPA on creator ads; higher trust signals in comments.

## 8. Winner Stacking Architecture
**Pattern**: Combines proven winning hooks/bodies into new "super ads" rather than starting from scratch.
**Execute**: Take Hook A (Winner) + Body B (Winner) + CTA C (Winner) = New Creative.
**Success Metric**: Extended lifespan of winning assets; consistent performance.

## 9. TikTok Shop Study
**Pattern**: Studies TikTok Shop creators (paid on commission) rather than brand creators (paid on views).
**Execute**: Watch 50 TikTok Shop videos weekly. Analyze their "close."
**Success Metric**: Higher conversion rate on video creative.

## 10. AI as Amplifier, Not Creator
**Pattern**: Uses AI to scale volume of good ideas, not to generate the ideas themselves.
**Execute**: Human defines strategy/angle -> AI generates 10 variations -> Human filters -> AI polishes.
**Success Metric**: 10x output volume without quality degradation.

## 11. The 60/40 Funnel Inversion
**Pattern**: Spends 60% of budget/effort on problem-aware/unaware top-of-funnel content.
**Execute**: Resist the urge to make only bottom-funnel product ads. Build the pipeline.
**Success Metric**: Stabilized CAC at scale; ability to spend higher budgets.

## 12. Custom GPT Ecosystem
**Pattern**: Builds a specific GPT for every recurring task.
**Execute**: Identify repeated thought process -> diverse examples -> build GPT -> refine.
**Success Metric**: Reduced cognitive load on routine strategy tasks.

## 13. Organic-to-Paid Translation
**Pattern**: Spots organic trends and immediately adapts them for paid (with a CTA).
**Execute**: "Can this meme format carry my sales message?"
**Success Metric**: Lower CPMs due to native feel; high engagement.

## 14. Authority Calibration
**Pattern**: Knowing EXACTLY what "authority" looks like to a specific demographic.
**Execute**: Match visual cues to audience trust triggers (White coat? Tattoos? suit? Gym gear?).
**Success Metric**: Comment sentiment analysis showing trust/belief.

## 15. The Anti-Formula Mindset
**Pattern**: Skepticism of all "rules" (including these) when data says otherwise.
**Execute**: Test the opposite of best practices. (Long text on bare video? Bad lighting?).
**Success Metric**: Discovery of "outlier" winners that competitors won't copy.

## Hidden Knowledge

Tacit operational insights from Alex Cooper's experience running Ad Crate.

## 1. The $400K "Bad Hook" Paradox
**Insight**: Market-specific trust signals can override universal hook rules.
**Context**: A video opening with "Hey, my name is Sarah..." (boring hook) became a $400K winner in Beauty because it matched native organic tutorial behavior.
**Deploy**: If targeting an audience that consumes 'boring' educational content, mimic that pacing. Don't over-stimulate.

## 2. VO3/Video AI Investment Timing
**Insight**: The skill of *prompting* for video/voice is compounding, even if current tools are imperfect.
**Context**: Early adopters of Midjourney are masterful now. Same applies to Runway/Sora/ElevenLabs.
**Deploy**: Force team to use video AI for 10% of output now to build the muscle for when it becomes 50%.

## 3. The Incentive Architecture
**Insight**: Creative teams (especially editors) respond disproportionately to performance-based micro-bonuses.
**Context**: "$250 bonus if this ad spends $5k" aligns intent instantly.
**Deploy**: Create a simple "Winner Bonus" tier for your creative team. Shift focus from "pretty" to "performance."

## 4. Research as Competitive Moat
**Insight**: Angles found on page 7 of a Reddit thread have zero competition.
**Context**: Competitors stop at page 1 or Amazon summary. The painful depth is where differentiation lives.
**Deploy**: Mandate specific depth metrics in research (e.g., "Must cite 3 quotes from >1 year ago").

## 5. The Human-AI Filtering Layer
**Insight**: "The only people making good AI ads are people who make good ads without AI."
**Context**: AI is a velocity tool, not a quality tool. Your taste/judgment is the bottleneck.
**Deploy**: Senior Strategist's primary role shifts from "Creation" to "Curation/Direction" of AI output.

## 6. Longer Ads = Incremental Reach
**Insight**: Short ads hit existing demand. Long ads create new demand.
**Context**: 30s ads saturate quickly. 3-5m ads act as VSLs in the feed, educating unaware users.
**Deploy**: When an account plateaus, don't test new 30s hooks—test a 3-minute story/education asset.

## 7. The Copywriting Bottleneck
**Insight**: Copywriting instinct is the hardest skill to train; format execution is the easiest.
**Context**: Hire for writing/persuasion talent first. Teach them how to use CapCut later.
**Deploy**: Weight writing samples 3x higher than video editing reels when hiring strategists.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

**1. The "Authenticity Over Polish" Skincare VSL**
*   **Creative:** A 4-minute video ad for an acne treatment begins with a woman (not a model, relatable appearance) looking directly into the camera, saying, "Hey, my name is Sarah, and for years, I struggled with cystic acne..." The video then follows her over several weeks, showing raw, unedited footage of her skin improving. There are no dramatic cuts or flashy graphics. The product is only introduced around the 2:30 mark after she deeply articulates the emotional and physical toll of acne, and shares a unique, science-backed mechanism for the product's action. The call to action is a simple link to a detailed product page.
*   **What makes this excellent:** This exemplifies the "$400K 'Bad Hook' Paradox" (Hidden Knowledge 1) where authenticity and relatability trump traditional "hooks" in specific markets. It uses the "Crossbow Principle" (Genius Pattern 4) by delaying the product reveal significantly to build tension and trust, and aligns with "Longer Ads = Incremental Reach" (Hidden Knowledge 6) by educating and creating new demand rather than just capturing existing. The "Model Selection as Strategy" (Genius Pattern 7) is evident in choosing a relatable, non-model figure to build authority and trust.

**2. The "Meme-to-Mechanism" Productivity Tool Ad**
*   **Creative:** A brand selling a new AI-powered productivity tool identifies a trending TikTok meme format (e.g., "Me trying to X, but my brain trying to Y"). They adapt this format, showing a user struggling with a common, relatable workflow inefficiency ("Me trying to focus on my project, but my brain trying to remember where I saved that one file..."). The meme's punchline is replaced by a quick, satisfying demonstration of the AI tool instantly solving the problem. The visual style is native to TikTok, but the underlying message clearly articulates the product's unique *mechanism* (e.g., "AI-powered contextual search across all your apps"). The ad concludes with a subtle, integrated call to action.
*   **What makes this excellent:** This demonstrates "Organic-to-Paid Translation" (Genius Pattern 13) by leveraging a viral trend for paid media. It applies "Market Sophistication Calibration" (Genius Pattern 2) by leading with a clear problem and then introducing a differentiating mechanism for a problem-aware audience. "Visual Telegraphing" (Genius Pattern 3) is strong, as the meme format instantly communicates the context and target user. This also hints at "AI as Amplifier, Not Creator" (Genius Pattern 10) where AI might generate variations of the meme script, but the core strategic adaptation comes from human insight.

**Anti-Exemplar: The "Feature-First, Who Cares?" Launch Ad**
*   **Creative:** A new health supplement company launches with an ad featuring a sleek 3D animation of their product bottle, followed by a rapid-fire list of ingredients and their scientific names. A generic, upbeat voiceover states, "Experience peak performance with [Brand Name]! Featuring [Ingredient A], [Ingredient B], and [Ingredient C] for optimal health!" The ad is 15 seconds long and ends with a "Shop Now" button.
*   **Why it's mediocre:** This ad fails on multiple Alex Cooper principles. It lacks "Research-First Creative Philosophy" (Genius Pattern 1), as it doesn't address specific audience pain points or insights. It's completely devoid of "Crossbow Principle" (Genius Pattern 4), rushing straight to product features. "Market Sophistication Calibration" (Genius Pattern 2) is ignored, as it assumes the audience cares about ingredients without first educating them on the problem or unique mechanism. There's no "Visual Telegraphing" (Genius Pattern 3) to instantly connect with an audience beyond generic health.

## Signature Moves

*   **The "Silence Test"**: Watches a new video creative with the sound *off* for the first 3-5 seconds, observing only the visuals. → **Deploy when**: Evaluating any new video ad concept or creative, especially for thumb-stop potential.
*   **The "Reddit Rabbit Hole Dive"**: Before any ideation session, opens a browser to Reddit, specifically subreddits related to the target audience's problems or passions, and scrolls *past* the first page, looking for niche complaints, language, or hidden desires. → **Deploy when**: Kicking off a new creative project, encountering creative block, or seeking differentiated angles.
*   **The "Inverted Funnel Sketch"**: Before designing any single ad, draws a simple funnel diagram and intentionally allocates 60% of conceptual effort/budget to the top-of-funnel (problem-aware/unaware) content ideas, pushing product-centric ads to the smaller bottom section. → **Deploy when**: Beginning overall campaign strategy, auditing an underperforming ad account, or planning for scale.
*   **The "TikTok Shop Deconstruction"**: Spends 15-20 minutes watching TikTok Shop videos for a product category similar to the client's, specifically analyzing how creators *close the sale* and handle objections, rather than focusing on viral entertainment. → **Deploy when**: Optimizing conversion rates on short-form video, understanding direct-response tactics, or developing new CTA strategies.

### Patterns from claude.ai export — Alex Cooper: Reinventing Creative Strategy with AI (2026-07-01)

Source: Farrice's claude.ai extraction sessions on Alex Cooper's DC Diaries appearance ("Reinventing Creative Strategy with AI") plus the Crown Jewel follow-up chats. MES/meta-framing wrappers stripped; only Cooper's real, transcript-traceable methodology retained. Numbering continues from the sections above.

## 16. The Agent Org Chart (Strategist → Creative Director of AI Agents)
**Pattern**: Sees the creative strategist role migrating away from executing ads toward directing an army of AI agents — a research agent (scrapes Reddit, TikTok, customer reviews, ad comments; files a daily report: "I scraped 100 ad comments and 10 Reddit threads — here's what I found"), a script-writing agent, a creator agent driving VO-class video tools, and editor agents — with the human owning direction, taste, and QC.
**Execute**: Map every stage of your creative pipeline to a named agent with a defined daily deliverable and an explicit context feed. Human touchpoints only at: angle selection ("go make scripts for these three ideas"), script approval, final QC.
**Success Metric**: Strategist hours measurably shift from production to research review + direction; concept volume rises without headcount.

## 17. Wall-of-Text Messaging Lab
**Pattern**: Uses "wall of text over B-roll" videos (someone drinking coffee or typing, one big paragraph of copy on top) as the cheapest messaging test rig — 10-15 variations per batch. Assumed it was a digital-product format; it produced winners in ecom accounts too.
**Execute**: Take a proven or candidate script (condensed), lay it as a text wall over mundane B-roll, and run 10-15 MESSAGE variants before investing in any produced format. Format is the delivery vehicle; the test object is the message.
**Success Metric**: Winning message identified at a fraction of production cost before a single shoot is booked.

## 18. Written-on-the-World Telegraphing
**Pattern**: Uses image gen to write the message ON audience-native surfaces — a fight-wear brand's message written on the gym canvas next to the gloves, a dandruff shampoo's hook written in bathroom-window condensation, a neuropathy supplement's copy written on the foot itself (that one "absolutely murdered").
**Execute**: Ask "what surface does my audience already stare at?" and put the hook there via image gen. Deploy mid-video too: re-hook every 5-6 seconds by surfacing the next benefit on a notepad/whiteboard as a feed disruptor — not only as a whole-video gimmick.
**Success Metric**: Thumb-stop and hold-rate lift; the viewer self-identifies before anyone speaks.

## 19. Comment-Objection Response Ads
**Pattern**: Turns the highest-frequency objection (usually price: "I'm never paying $80 for a pair of jeans") into the ad itself via TikTok comment-response bubbles — with a founder or authority answering the actual comment, often in a podcast setting.
**Execute**: Mine real ad comments for the #1 objection. Script a direct response TO that on-screen comment — never a bubble tacked onto unrelated B-roll in post. Founders answering their own skeptics is the strongest cast.
**Success Metric**: Skeptical solution-aware traffic converts; the objection stops appearing under new ads.

## 20. The Money Shot Override
**Pattern**: Knows when NOT to pull back the crossbow — if the product has a "money shot" (collapsible kitchenware collapsing, a guy looking great in the jeans), the visual IS the argument, so the product enters immediately. Problem-agitation delay is reserved for products where you can genuinely sit in the pain.
**Execute**: Before scripting, ask: "Can a viewer see what this does and why they'd want it in half a second?" Yes → product-first, lead with the money shot. No → run the Crossbow Principle (Pattern 4).
**Success Metric**: No more misapplied 90-second agitations on visual products; hold rate on fashion/gadget accounts recovers.

## 21. The No-Pain Playbook
**Pattern**: For products with no poignant pain point (fashion, snacks), routes demand creation through three lanes: (a) relatable story with an open curiosity loop (the coffee-shop-date ad), (b) introspective company audit — founder narrative, employee cast, unique physical assets like a massive warehouse, (c) satire — True Classic made "it's a t-shirt" funny and those ads drove the majority of two years of top-of-funnel growth.
**Execute**: Run the introspection audit first (founder? employees? unique setup?), then pick story, company, or satire as the lane. Never fabricate a fake pain point.
**Success Metric**: Top-of-funnel spend unlocked for "boring" products; new-customer revenue moves, not just retargeting efficiency.

## 22. Cross-Industry Winner Transplant (Ad Hoarding as Career Moat)
**Pattern**: "Become an absolute hoarder of top performing ads." Builds Foreplay boards spanning industries and audiences, plus a bank of ~100 TikTok Shop creators — because the value over the next 12-24 months is not the skill of creating ads but knowing WHAT to create.
**Execute**: Collect winners relentlessly (ad-account swaps, paid consulting calls if that's what access costs). On every new account, search the library for a message STRUCTURE that worked in a different industry and transplant it — condensed script, new surface. The wall-of-text format itself was an organic TikTok trend pulled into paid.
**Success Metric**: First batches on new accounts hit historic benchmarks; "I remember this from a different industry" becomes the default ideation move.

## 23. Access, Educate, Incentivize (Team Feedback Loop)
**Pattern**: Runs creative teams as a closed loop: **Access** — everyone (including offshore editors) sees performance data; **Educate** — weekly brainstorm/performance calls where budget-no-object ideas come from editors and media buyers, not just strategists; **Incentivize** — $250-500 per ad that crosses a spend threshold.
**Execute**: Wire all three before judging anyone's output. If a person has all three and still doesn't chase winners, it's a fit problem, not a system problem.
**Success Metric**: Offshore editors behave like owners; winning ad ideas start arriving from outside the strategist seat.

## 24. Copywork Training Protocol
**Pattern**: Copywriting is the hardest, slowest skill to train — so he screens hiring for raw copy instinct over format knowledge, and trains via copywork: rewriting classic 50s-70s long-form sales letters word for word, studying revenue-proven VSLs ("they start with perfect copy"), plus hundreds of script reps. AI workflows come AFTER copy instinct, never before.
**Execute**: Weight writing samples 3x over editing reels in hiring. Training sequence: classic letters copywork → proven VSL study → volume reps. Explicitly ban "learn n8n/Gumloop before copywriting" career sequencing.
**Success Metric**: Strategists can call which 2 of 10 AI-generated headlines are runnable — the filter skill every downstream AI tool depends on.

## 25. Think With AI First
**Pattern**: Reverses the meeting habit: brainstorm with AI, then take the first pass to the team. Keeps a business-adviser GPT loaded with the P&L and org structure ("you are the world's best agency consultant, trained by owners with $20M+ exits"), and per-direct-report GPTs holding every review transcript and feedback note.
**Execute**: Before any strategy meeting or big allocation decision, run the problem through your context-loaded adviser GPT and arrive with a draft, not a blank page. Build one GPT per person you manage, fed by review transcripts.
**Success Metric**: Meetings start at 70% instead of 0%; big decisions carry a documented first-pass rationale.

## 26. The Context Capture Habit
**Pattern**: Treats proprietary context as the one asset AI can't supply itself. Voice-dictates long prompts and context docs (a 2-minute typed prompt becomes a 20-second riff), presses record on every in-person working session, and pipes the transcripts into the relevant custom GPTs.
**Execute**: Install a hold-to-dictate shortcut and use it for all model conversations. Record every session/mastermind (a laptop on a Google Meet works); end each week feeding transcripts into the matching GPT.
**Success Metric**: Context documents get written instead of postponed; agents answer with your actual business specifics instead of generic marketing advice.

## 27. The Human-Signal Lane
**Pattern**: Deliberately produces what AI cannot fake as AI floods the feed — three real people laughing while trying the licorice, employee-generated warehouse content, founder behind-the-scenes — because audiences will "crave something uglier," and anti-AI sentiment is already live in comment sections.
**Execute**: Keep a standing "provably human" line in the creative mix alongside AI creative: multi-person scenes, real employees, authentic reactions, physical spaces. Signal humanity on purpose.
**Success Metric**: Native-feel CPMs; comment sections free of the "sick of AI slop on my feed" backlash.

## Hidden Knowledge (continued)

## 8. Floor Rises, Ceiling Doesn't
**Insight**: AI raises the floor (anyone can make decent ads) without raising the ceiling — the durable gap is "who knows best WHAT to create rather than how to go about creating the specific ad."
**Context**: Cooper expects most execution to be absorbed by AI within 12-24 months while the value concentrates in direction, taste, and QC.
**Deploy**: Invest in judgment assets — research depth, winner libraries, taste reps — over tool mastery. Tools depreciate; judgment compounds.

## 9. The Defensible Skill Stack
**Insight**: Three skills stay defensible through the AI wave, in order: copywriting instinct, context engineering ("people talk about prompt engineering, not enough about context engineering"), and video-tool prompting fluency.
**Context**: "The only people making good ads with AI are people who make good ads without AI" — and AI won't know which context belongs in the workflow; that judgment is the strategist's.
**Deploy**: Sequence development copy → context → tools. Force ~10% of creative output through video AI NOW (even while tedious) to build the muscle for when it's 50%.

## 10. Agencies Become Data Warehouses
**Insight**: The agency's terminal unfair advantage is cross-account context — patterns from 10-50 brand accounts — and the end-state is "growth engine operator" / AI consultancy, with brands consolidating from five or six creative shops to one growth-creative partner (human + AI).
**Context**: "I don't think creative shops as they currently work will be around in 2-3 years." Caveat he names himself: Meta holds more context than any agency — the window is real but not permanent.
**Deploy**: Productize cross-account learnings now (pattern library, benchmarks, category playbooks); sell as the single consolidated growth-creative partner, not one vendor among six.

## 11. What's Quietly Dying (and What Replaced It)
**Insight**: Formulaic UGC ("this gives me SO much more energy!") and street interviews have vanished from top-spender slots. The replacements: "V2 UGC" — ugly ads, lo-fi statics with native Instagram-story text editing, and WhatsApp voice-note soundbars laid over product B-roll.
**Deploy**: Audit the format mix quarterly against what actually holds top-spend slots. Build lo-fi assets with the platform's own native editing artifacts (story text, comment bubbles, voice-note UI) so they read as feed-native, not produced.

## 12. The $50k/mo Founder Rule
**Insight**: Below roughly $50k/mo ad spend, outsourcing creative strategy is premature — learning to sell your own product is the highest-leverage founder skill (AdCrate's own client minimum is $100k/mo spend).
**Context**: Running the research process on your own business doubles as product education — a well-known drinks brand's entire C-suite joined the research readout because it surfaced customer truths they'd never seen.
**Deploy**: Under threshold: the founder runs research + creative strategy personally. Over threshold: outsource execution, never the taste.

## 13. The Personal Learning Pipeline
**Insight**: Compresses any learning topic into commute-sized audio: source material → LLM summary → NotebookLM podcast.
**Context**: Solves the "listen to a 60-minute podcast for a 10-minute segment" problem; he uses it for everything from copywriting theory to chess openings.
**Deploy**: For any new domain: dump books/podcasts into Claude, generate a focused summary, feed it to NotebookLM, listen while traveling.
