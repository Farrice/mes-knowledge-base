# Steven Kotler — Flow & Peak Performance

The transformation layer for premium coaching. Where most coaches sell motivation, Kotler sells mechanism: flow is a neurobiological state ("an optimized state of consciousness where we feel our best and perform our best") produced by identifiable triggers, blocked by identifiable inhibitors, and trainable like any focusing skill. This skill turns a client's vague goal ("perform better," "feel less scattered") into a specific, repeatable protocol grounded in how the brain actually works — because at the neurobiological level, what works for one human works for all humans.

Use this when a coaching client needs a durable performance transformation, not a pep talk: designing a daily flow protocol, building intrinsic drive that survives hard goals, or diagnosing why they keep dropping out of the zone.

## Quick Reference

- **Flow follows focus.** Every one of the 22 triggers does one job under the hood: drive all attention into the present moment. They do it three ways — spike dopamine, spike norepinephrine, or lower cognitive load.
- **Golden rule — challenge-skills balance.** Flow lives ~4% above current skill: stretch, don't snap. Near the midpoint between boredom and anxiety (the "flow channel"). Chunk high-hard goals down until the task in front of you is only 4-5% harder than your skills.
- **Motivation stack (in sequence):** extrinsic (enough money to pay bills + a little fun) → curiosity → passion → purpose → autonomy → mastery. Each is designed to be built into the next.
- **Neurochemistry:** flow cocktails 5-6 pleasure chemicals at once (dopamine, norepinephrine, endorphins, anandamide, serotonin; + oxytocin in group flow) — more powerful than any external drug blend, which is why it's the most addictive experience on earth.
- **Payoffs:** learning +240-500%, creativity +400-700%, productivity ~+500% (McKinsey self-report).
- **Primary blockers:** distraction (#1 — a single interruption costs ~15 min re-entry), fear/anxiety, self-consciousness (breaks task-specific external focus), boredom.
- **Readiness basics (6):** energy side — sleep (7-8h), nutrition/hydration, social support; mental side — gratitude, mindfulness (11 min), exercise (20-40 min). One/day baseline, two/day under stress.
- **Bannister effect:** belief that something is possible precedes the ability to do it. Reframe the impossible into the probable before training the feat.
