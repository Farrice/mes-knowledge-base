## Four source-derived formats

### 1. Competence-moment Reel

- One resolved moment, one visible decision, one concise context line.
- Natural spoken delivery with slightly elevated clarity and energy.
- Strongest when the agent can point to a clause, diagnosis, comparison, or action.
- Weak when it becomes generic instruction or performed charisma.

### 2. Single image + narrative caption

- Strong for an agent who writes better than they perform.
- The image establishes person and property; the caption carries chronology and tension.
- The first line must contain a concrete situation or consequence.
- Weak when the caption lists activity but never reveals what changed.

### 3. Proof carousel

- Specific before/after or time-bound hook.
- Repeated visual anchor keeps production simple.
- One topic per slide; short text blocks.
- Situation → obstacle → diagnosis/proposal → proof → response → result.
- Best receipts: original vs revised positioning, staging, showing response, offer timing, and final outcome.

### 4. Bounded live-stakes series + recap

- Real deadline or constraint, documented with dated field evidence.
- Use live updates only when privacy, negotiation, access, and outcome uncertainty are controlled.
- The final recap is the durable asset.
- Do not promise an outcome while the case is unresolved.

## Hidden mechanics

1. **Invisible-work conversion:** Prospects rarely see diagnosis and judgment. The story turns that hidden layer into inspectable evidence.
2. **Specificity as credibility:** Dollar amounts, dates, durations, contract locations, showing counts, and before/after artifacts make the work legible. Every number still needs support.
3. **Competence before performance:** The content only works because substantive work occurred. A camera cannot repair weak service delivery.
4. **Safe-delay principle:** The most persuasive detail may need to wait until closing, permission, or a future-safe date.
5. **Proof beside claim:** Visual receipts appear at the beat they substantiate.
6. **Lifestyle translation:** Listing language helps a plausible buyer imagine use and tradeoffs without steering by protected class.
7. **Portfolio, not format dogma:** Reel, caption, carousel, and live series solve different communication jobs.
8. **Template the chassis:** Reuse capture fields, slide functions, and production handoffs—not another agent's exact phrasing or personality.
9. **Delayed demand:** Evergreen proof may influence a prospect weeks later; attribution must survive that lag.
10. **Two conversion lanes:** Current inventory/resource posts can capture demand; case stories can compound trust without a forced keyword.

## Transcendence opportunities

The source demonstrates excellent storytelling but leaves five system gaps:

1. Capture facts and proof at transaction milestones rather than reconstructing them from memory after closing.
2. Add an explicit permission, privacy, brokerage, and fair-housing gate.
3. Route narrative dosage before production so thin evidence does not become dramatic copy.
4. Separate attention from CRM-linked pipeline attribution.
5. Give a team one case-receipt standard while preserving agent-specific voice and format fit.

Those five additions are the basis of `/enrico-proof-story`.
