# Omar Eltakrori — Genius Layer

## The Interior Game

Omar's genius isn't the frameworks — it's the spiritual and psychological operating system underneath them. This layer captures the mindset principles that make the tactical layer actually produce results.

---

## Becoming Before Getting

"Becoming the person comes before getting the outcome."

Omar lost 35 lbs before his biggest challenge ($722K vs ~$400K average). He didn't change the offer, the ads, or the script. He changed himself. The subconscious confidence shift translated directly into sales energy.

**Deployment:** When a client is stuck on revenue, don't start with tactics. Start with: "Who are you becoming? Are you showing up as the person who deserves the outcome you're chasing?"

## The $100K Devaluation Principle

Omar lost $100K in a Ponzi scheme. Key unlock:

1. "I lost $100,000 and nothing changed" — mortgage paid, food on table, life continued
2. "If $100K isn't a lot of money, maybe $1M isn't a lot of money"
3. "The decision to be bigger than $100,000 happened when I lost it"
4. "All money is is a tool"

**Deployment:** When someone fears investing in their business or charging premium prices, this is the reframe. Money anxiety is a scale problem — if you can't think in $100K units, you'll never operate at $1M.

## Price = Attention (Psychology of Investment)

"When you pay, you pay attention. When you pay more, you pay more attention."

**Evidence chain:**
- Founders paying $2K/year aren't implementing as aggressively as new members at $25K
- A founder left, came back at $25K, had an $80K week running the prescribed play
- Omar paid $3K when broke — never worked harder than to earn it back
- "Payment creates a void for your creativity to fill"

**Corollary:** "If you want your clients to make fast buying decisions with you, you should be a fast buyer." Omar shook hands on a $10K two-day intensive without hesitation.

**Anti-pattern:** "If you're cheap, if you're always trying to look for the discount, don't be surprised when people want discounts from you."

**Deployment:** This isn't about charging more for ego. It's about understanding the psychology: premium pricing creates commitment, which creates results, which creates testimonials, which creates more premium clients. It's a flywheel.

## Sales as Energy Transfer

"Sales is a transference of energy."

Omar's self-concept discovery (Brian Tracy): "Before you get better at sales, how you think about yourself starts with sales."

The physical health → confidence → sales energy pipeline is real and measurable in his revenue data.

**Deployment:** Before any sales event (challenge, web class, discovery call), the question isn't "Is my script ready?" It's "Is my energy right?"

## Do It for the Data

"If you're afraid to do something or you've been putting off something, it's because you are overthinking the perfectionism and underthinking the benefits."

**Reframe:** "I'm gonna do this for the data" → removes emotional weight → action happens.

**Apple principle:** "We gave Apple 17 iterations. They still haven't gotten it right from their point of view, but they're still going to sell it to us."

**Deployment:** Apply this to first launches, first webinars, first live streams, first offers. The first version is data collection, not a masterpiece.

## Faith & Stewardship Economics

Omar's wealth philosophy is rooted in Christian theology:

1. **Deuteronomy:** God gives the ability/power to get wealth
2. **Love of money ≠ money itself.** Pursuit of wealth isn't evil — idolizing it is
3. **Parable of talents:** Multiply what you're given = "well done, good and faithful servant." Do nothing = "wicked and lazy servant."
4. **Self-care precedes service:** "Love your neighbor as yourself" — you can't pour from an empty cup
5. **Tithing:** First 10% to God → He blesses the 90 more than you could do with 100

**Anti-patterns around money:**
- If money stresses you out, it might be an idol
- If you're afraid to lose it OR afraid to gain it → you serve money
- Rich people ≠ evil (cultural programming from superhero movies)

**Deployment:** For faith-adjacent clients, this resolves the "Is it okay to pursue wealth?" internal conflict that blocks business growth. For secular contexts, the principle still holds: stewardship beats hoarding.

## I Am Boring (Discipline Over Novelty)

"I am boring. I just run the play."

Omar runs the same challenge format, the same offer structure, the same content cadence. The play works. He doesn't chase new tactics — he improves execution of proven ones.

**Key evidence:** His offer went from $2K → $25K without changing the core structure. Same challenge, different price. Different version of himself delivering it.

"If you're willing to be underpaid long enough, you will be overpaid soon enough." (Tom Brady)

**Deployment:** When clients chase shiny objects (new platform, new offer, new funnel), redirect to: "Have you maxed out the play you're already running?"

## The Obsession Signal

"The person who appears most obsessed with a topic gets the business."

Creating frameworks, coining terms, drawing visual models — these signal obsession. Not credentials, not years of experience. Raw obsession.

"What it's doing is communicating to a person that you've thought of this before they have or you've thought of this more than they have."

**Deployment:** For personal branding, obsession is the differentiator. The question isn't "Am I qualified?" but "Am I more obsessed than anyone else in this space?"

## Manufactured Scarcity & Urgency

"Two things that will increase your sales: urgency and scarcity."

# The Exterior Game — The Craft of Teaching-to-Sell

> Source: two 2026 videos — V1 "How To Become A Millionaire in 2026 (Start Teaching)" + V2 "How To Teach and Grow Rich In 2026 (Greatest Business Model)." ~13,000 words. This is the layer ABOVE the Interior Game: the interior game is who you become; the exterior game is *how you teach so people feel like moving.* The two are inseparable — but they are different lenses. Below is the craft.

## Teaching Is the Highest-Paying Skill

The contrarian thesis. Everyone online says "sales is the highest-paying skill." Omar disagrees: *"Sales is not the highest paying skill. Teaching is the highest paying skill."* The proof is structural — he runs a multi-seven-figure business **with no sales team**: *"I've made millions of dollars in my business and continue to make millions of dollars in my business... without a sales team."*

The business model is "teach and get paid." You can build a very *small* business with very *high* profit. This is not the build-to-sell game — it's the clear-owner's-intent game: *"I'm going to make a lot of money in my young years."*

**Deployment:** When someone is grinding on sales scripts, funnels, or "closing," redirect to the higher-leverage skill: *can you teach the thing in a way that makes people feel like taking the next step?* If yes, you don't need a sales team — the teaching IS the selling.

## The Four Phases — Teach at Every One

Every business is four phases: **Lead Generation → Lead Conversion → Fulfillment → Retention/Ascension.** Omar's unlock: *"teaching could be used at every phase of the business."*

- **Lead Gen** — teach to get in front of people (content as the magnet).
- **Conversion** — teach them what your business can do for them, and they buy.
- **Fulfillment** — whatever they bought, you teach them (the delivery IS teaching).
- **Retention/Ascension** — they go to the next level of teaching, or they stay.

*"At this point, we run ads, we teach. The group that came to the teach jumps into the next teach, the next of that teach goes into the next level of teach."* The whole machine is teaching, sequenced.

**Deployment:** Map any expertise-business onto the four phases and ask, at each: *what is the teach here?* No phase is a "sales" phase or a "delivery" phase — every phase is a teaching phase with a different objective.

## The New Objective — Create the Feeling, Not Transfer Information

The single most important reframe. *"The goal to teach is not to get people information. The goal of teaching is to create the feeling in a person to take the next step."*

Behind it, Eileen Wilder's law: *"People do things for one reason and one reason only. Because they feel like it."* So if people aren't moving with you, *they don't feel like it* — and your job is to create the feeling. Everything a person owns, every decision they've made, *something created the feeling.*

This makes you a **servant communicator**: *"someone who's thinking about the person receiving the communication. It's not just what you say, it's how you say what you say."*

**Deployment:** Before writing any teaching asset, set the target feeling, not the target information. Ask: *what should they FEEL at the end that makes the next step obvious?* Then reverse-engineer the teaching to produce that feeling.

## Over-Teaching — The #1 Mistake

*"The biggest mistake that people who teach online make is that they tend to over teach."* The image: *"if someone was thirsty and they needed a drink of water, and you came with a fire hose, you didn't serve them. You hurt them."*

The mechanism: **an overwhelmed person never takes action.** *"An overwhelmed person feels like they got more than enough... will not buy from you and they will actually... be more confused."* Over-teaching comes from being too close to the solution — you dump industry lingo and how-to weeds because you're obsessed with the answer.

Critical distinction: **over-teaching ≠ over-delivering.** You CAN over-deliver (someone watches a free video seven times and feels they got a fortune) without over-teaching (drowning them in information). And the tell of WHY people over-teach: insecurity. *"Insecure people tend to over-teach because they are trying to prove... there's nothing to prove."* Frank Kern: *"Prove to people you can help them by actually helping them."*

**Deployment:** Audit every asset for the fire-hose. If it teaches more than ONE thing, or uses lingo, or front-loads how-to weeds, cut. The discipline phrase: *"Boil it down to one. One thing at a time. It's one drink at a time."*

## Shift Beliefs — The Real Job (Possible · Easy · Trust) + Make-It-Easy

*"Your job is for someone to think about or believe about the thing differently than they did before they met you."* You shift beliefs by making people feel three things: **it's possible, it's easy, and they can trust you.**

The make-it-easy move has a hidden second-order effect: *"if they feel like it's easy because you said it... psychologically they're thinking to themself, it's going to be easy if I work with them."* Making it seem easy IS the close. (Case: Paige — new IG account in January, ~300K followers, $300 product, ~3,000 buyers, ~$1.2M, no sales team — *"she's helped people make like creating content could be so easy."* UNCONFIRMED, Omar's account.)

**Deployment:** For any offer, write the three belief-shifts explicitly: what makes the outcome feel POSSIBLE, feel EASY, and make YOU trustable. The "easy" belief, installed in free content, pre-sells the paid relationship.

## Context Determines the Value of Content

*"The context in which the content is consumed determines the value of the content."* The same information is worth nothing on a transient feed and a fortune in a paid room — because of how the receiver pays attention. *"Where your treasure is, there your heart is also. If you pay them, you'll pay attention more."*

This dissolves the gatekeeping anxiety. Omar gives away the entire play on YouTube *because* YouTube is transient: *"you're going to click away in a couple minutes."* The same content behind a paid threshold creates a **post-transaction consumer** who pays attention differently. *"The secret sauce to not gatekeeping is understanding the context people are receiving information in."* People came from Florida, Denver, Vancouver, Utah to a live recording *they knew would be posted free* — because the context (the room) determines the value, not the information.

**Deployment:** Stop asking "how much do I give away?" Ask "what's the context?" Free/transient → give the WHAT generously (it builds trust and proves obsession). Paid/committed → that's where depth and HOW live, because the attention is different.

## WHAT-Not-HOW (and When HOW Is Allowed)

*"You don't want to teach how-to from your point of view. Don't teach people how to do something tactfully. Teach when or when not. Teach why or why not. Teach where or where not. Teach what or what not. But don't teach how."* Because *"when you start teaching how, you will start to lose people."*

The packaging trick: you CAN **title** it "how to" — *"Here's how to become a millionaire"* — but in the body you deliver WHAT, WHEN, WHY, WHERE (and their negations). Omar's "how to edit in Premiere Pro" video has 2M+ views and never teaches editing — it shows what to do *before* you edit (make a folder, open the app, save, create a sequence). People felt empowered to start; that's the win.

**The reconciled rule** (V1 says "never teach HOW"; V2 softens it): *title in HOW, deliver WHAT in free/transient context; reserve HOW for the right context* — *"when somebody pays you, they'll be more receptive of the how-to"* — and when *"it's delivered in the right context and in a way that anyone can do it."* Hormozi's version: *"teach how-to then sell the implementation"* — disguise the how-to, but still deliver the what.

**Deployment:** Reframe any "how-to" content into WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE + negations. Keep the HOW for the paid container. Never confuse gatekeeping with context-calibration.

## The Parable of the Four Soils (Teach + Learn)

Omar's deepest frame, applied to both sides of the exchange. The same seed (information) lands on four soils:

1. **Path / closed heart** — hears but doesn't understand; birds snatch it.
2. **Rocky / shallow heart** — springs up fast, withers, no roots ("I'm four minutes in and there's nothing" — they missed it).
3. **Weedy / distracted heart** — choked by worries and the deceitfulness of wealth ("a million's not enough, be a billionaire" — focused on the wrong thing).
4. **Good / receptive soil** — *"those who do with what they hear."* Produces a harvest.

Two uses. As a **teacher**: hold the healthy assumption that *not everyone will get it* — *"the only group I am really concerned with is the receptive group."* As a **student**: *"the reason why you suck at teaching is because you're a sucky student... if you suck at learning, you will suck at teaching."* Good soil = implementation. *"There is so much revelation in implementation."* Faith comes by hearing (Romans); be doers not hearers (James).

**Deployment:** Stop trying to convert all four soils — design for the receptive 0.01%. And gate yourself first: are you good soil (do you implement what you consume)? Only good-soil students become great teachers.

## Mastery vs. Amateur (and Objective-by-Context)

Myron Golden's definition Omar adopts: *"mastery is the use of unconscious resources to execute a given task."* If a task feels hard, you have a **grow gap** — you haven't worked your way to unconscious competence yet. Omar didn't start needing only "a light, a camera, a mic" — he watched himself back, slowed his cadence, learned cadence.

And the objective changes by **context**: *"there's different objectives for Instagram and YouTube and having a webinar... a master class... a workshop... a boot camp."* Omar's YouTube objective: long-form so *"you undeniably know who I am"* — *"you can't fake who you are for a long time."* (Trump won, in Omar's read, by doing 2-3 hour long-form formats — Rogan, golf with Bryson, Ramsey — *"how are you going to be a politician for three hours on the golf course?"*) Then the self-selection: *"99.99% of you guys won't even click the link... and that's totally fine. But for the 0.1%, let's go."*

**Deployment:** Set the objective per platform BEFORE creating. Use long-form to become undeniable. Don't optimize for the 99.99% — build for the receptive fraction who self-select into the next step.

## Mindset > Tactics + True Influence

*"It's always mindset."* Broke people want tactics; people who've made it say it was always mindset. Why? *"You've got scales over your eyes"* — experience removes the scales. *"We don't have an information problem. We have an application problem"* — and a **consolidation problem** (you're drowning in unconsolidated inputs).

This defines **true influence**: *"someone thinks about the thing differently than they did after they met you... and then they act on it."* Two laws ride alongside:
- *"The way you sell is the way you buy."* Cheap buyers become cheap sellers; fast buyers close fast buyers.
- **Teaching is a revealer.** *"All you're doing is you're revealing and projecting who you are. So... focus on who you're becoming. We don't become what we don't believe... we live in the lies we take on."* (This is the bridge back to the Interior Game.)

**Deployment:** When a client begs for tactics, give the tactic INSIDE a belief-shift — package the how-to, deliver the mindset. And remember the package can't out-run the person: who you're becoming sets the ceiling on what you can teach.

## Never Run Out — Make Content Only You Can Make

*"If you're breathing, you should never run out of content."* The fear of running out is a diagnosis: *"congratulations, you've identified that you need to grow."* The fix is to become a learner — *"everything is a reference point"* — read often (Omar reads one Proverb a day, by date). *"If eight YouTube videos in you're like 'now what,' you maxed out"* — that's a ceiling you don't want.

And the moat: *"How do you make content no one can copy? You make content only you can make."* Your stories, your failures, your wins, your clients — uncopyable. The engine for it is the **Client Rolodex**: *"list out every single client you had... what situation they were in, what they did, and then what happened... find the thread in it to see — you have a framework in there."* Plus Myron's $110K gem: *"for every letter of the alphabet, create frameworks and formulas for your expertise"* (three A's, three B's, three C's). Name it (alliteration), draw it (*"our brains are always looking for a picture"*).

**Deployment:** Never run dry — build the rolodex, mine the alphabet, convert lived experience into named, drawn frameworks. The content that compounds is the content only you could have made.
