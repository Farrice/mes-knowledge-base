# Over-Teaching Audit

A thirsty person needs a drink of water. Omar's whole diagnostic lives in one image: *"if someone was thirsty and they needed a drink of water, and you came with a fire hose, you didn't serve them. You hurt them."* The expert who knows the most usually serves the least, because they are too close to the solution to feel the receiver drowning. This workflow reads any draft the way Omar reads it cold: not "did I cover enough?" but "does the receptive person leave feeling like taking exactly one next step, or do they leave knowing more and moving nothing?"

The fail-state is specific and it is fatal to revenue. *"An overwhelmed person never takes action... will not buy from you and they will actually... be more confused."* Every asset that goes into the OS passes this gate first, and it leaves with three things: a red-line table flagging each tell by location, the ONE idea worth keeping, and the boiled-down rewrite. A flag without a fix is just a prettier way to ship the firehose.

## Pre-Flight
Read these files before executing:
1. `skills/omar-eltakrori/genius.md` → "The Exterior Game — The Craft of Teaching-to-Sell" (Over-Teaching, Feeling-First, Possible/Easy/Trust, Context Determines Value, WHAT-not-HOW) + the Quality Rubric
2. `skills/omar-eltakrori/references/teaching-craft.md` → **Tool 2 (Over-Teaching Red-Flag Checklist, 10 tells)**, plus Tool 3 (WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE Substitution) for how-to weeds and Tool 1 (Objective-by-Context Matrix) for the asset's context
3. `skills/omar-eltakrori/references/genius-patterns.md` → Patterns 3 (Feeling-First), **4 (Fire-Hose Audit)**, 5 (Possible-Easy-Trust), 6 (Context-Value Law), 7 (Title-How-Deliver-What), 8 (Four-Soils Filter)

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: You must know the asset's CONTEXT and OBJECTIVE before red-lining (Tool 1). A firehose is only diagnosable against a target — an Instagram post is over-teaching at depth a paid workshop would under-teach at. If the context or the intended ONE next step is unknown, get it (or run the asset's objective through the Objective-by-Context Matrix first). Auditing depth without a context is auditing nothing.

## Input Required
- The asset: the full draft (script, email, post, carousel, webinar/masterclass outline, sales-call talk track) — verbatim, not a summary
- Its context and channel: where it gets consumed (free/transient feed vs. paid/committed room) — sets the over-teaching threshold
- The intended ONE next step the receiver should take (follow / save / download / book / enroll / stay) — if the author can't name it, that itself is Flag 5
- The receptive person: who this is FOR (the good-soil receiver, named as a moment, not a demographic) — Pattern 8

## Workflow

### Step 1: Lock the context, objective, and target feeling (before any red-lining)
Restate, in three lines, the bar this asset is being judged against:
- **Context / channel:** free-transient or paid-committed (Tool 1). This sets how much WHAT is allowed and whether ANY how-to weeds are permitted.
- **The ONE next step:** the single action the receptive person should take. There is exactly one.
- **The target feeling:** what they must FEEL to make that step obvious. *"The goal of teaching is to create the feeling in a person to take the next step."* If the author wrote toward information instead of a feeling, note it now — it is the root tell (Flag 6).

### Step 2: Run the 10-tell Red-Flag Checklist line-by-line (Tool 2)
Walk the draft against each tell in order. For every one that fires, log the **location** (quote the offending line / name the section) and the **fix**. Do not summarize — point at the exact words.

| # | The tell to hunt | What it looks like in the draft |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | **More than one core idea** | Teaches two, three, five things. Count the distinct belief-shifts; >1 = fire. |
| 2 | **Industry lingo** | Insider vocabulary the receiver wouldn't say out loud. Flag every term that isn't in their words. |
| 3 | **How-to weeds** | Step-by-step mechanics from the creator's POV in a free/transient context. *"When you start teaching how, you will start to lose people."* |
| 4 | **Verbal processing / options-listing** | *"Oh, you can go this route, or you can do this, or maybe you go here and try it this way."* Thinking out loud; lists of options instead of one decision. |
| 5 | **No single next step** | Ends with zero or with three CTAs. The receiver doesn't know the one thing to do next. |
| 6 | **Teaching to transfer information, not create a feeling** | Success is framed as "they learned a lot." No target feeling was engineered. |
| 7 | **Insecurity tone** | Proving-worth energy: fast cadence, over-explaining, defensive padding. *"Insecure people tend to over-teach... there's nothing to prove."* |
| 8 | **Comprehensiveness as the goal** | Covering the whole topic so no one can say you left something out. Comprehensive = failure. |
| 9 | **No belief moved** | Reader knows more but believes nothing differently about the thing or about you. None of possible · easy · trust shifted (Pattern 5). |
| 10 | **Leaves overwhelmed, not empowered** | They feel they "got more than enough" — and freeze, instead of empowered-to-start. |

Rule of severity: **two or more tells firing = it's a firehose, not a teaching.** A single Flag-1 (more than one core idea) is on its own enough to send the asset back.

### Step 3: For each flag, write the location + the executable fix
Every logged flag gets a fix that is rewrite-ready, not advice. Use Tool 2's fixes and Tool 3 for how-to weeds:
- Flag 1 → name which idea stays and which get parked into future assets. *"Boil it down to one. One thing at a time. It's one drink at a time."*
- Flag 2 → the receiver's-word replacement for each term (give the literal substitute).
- Flag 3 → run the line through the WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE Substitution (Tool 3); relocate any real HOW to the paid container.
- Flag 4 → the one decision the author should make FOR the reader, stated as a single move.
- Flag 7 → a cadence/tonality note (slow it, cut the padding) — calm signals nothing-to-prove authority.
No "make it more focused" slop. If the fix isn't paste-able into the draft, it isn't a fix.

### Step 4: Identify the ONE idea the piece should keep
From the candidates surfaced in Flag 1, choose the single belief-shift this asset exists to install — the one that, for THIS context, best produces the target feeling and earns the one next step. State it as one sentence the receiver would feel, not a topic label. Everything else is cut or parked. This is the spine of the rewrite.

### Step 5: Deliver the boiled-down rewrite
Rewrite the asset around that one idea, at the depth the context allows (Tool 1). The rewrite must: teach exactly ONE thing, use zero unexplained lingo, deliver WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE (HOW only if the context is paid), move at least one of possible · easy · trust, end on the single next step, and over-DELIVER on value/feeling while under-delivering on information count. Over-delivery is value density; over-teaching is information volume — keep the first, kill the second.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | How the audit shifts |
|--------|----------------------|
| Instagram post / carousel | Threshold is tightest: ONE belief-shift, WHAT/WHY only, zero how-to weeds. Flag 1 and 3 fire fastest here. Next step = follow/save/read next. |
| YouTube long-form | More WHAT allowed and over-delivery encouraged (rewatch-worthy), but still essentially ONE thing taught well. Title may be "how to"; body stays WHAT (Pattern 7). HOW stays out. |
| Email | Single idea, single CTA. Flag 5 (no single next step) and Flag 4 (options-listing in the P.S. / multiple links) are the usual offenders. |
| Webinar / masterclass outline | Audit each segment as its own mini-asset; the deck-wide tell is comprehensiveness (Flag 8). "Easy" must be installed (it IS the close) — withhold implementation HOW for the paid container. |
| Sales call / talk track | Over-teaching here tanks the close — verbal processing (Flag 4) and insecurity cadence (Flag 7) dominate. Boil to the one belief that makes the next step obvious; stop selling, start letting them feel it. |
| 5-day challenge / lesson | One core move per day, sequenced. Lightest HOW that produces a same-day win is allowed; anything beyond that is the firehose creeping back in. |

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review the rewrite against `genius.md § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric` — score Feeling-Over-Information, WHAT-not-HOW Discipline, Belief-Shift Primacy, and Receptive-Soil Realism before delivering.
- The rewrite teaches exactly ONE thing — Feeling-Over-Information anchor 10 ("over-delivers in value while teaching only ONE thing — never the fire hose").
- Zero unexplained lingo survives; every term is in the receiver's words (Flag 2 closed).
- HOW-to weeds were converted to WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE for free/transient context, or relocated to paid — WHAT-not-HOW anchor 10. Title may be "how to"; body is not (Pattern 7).
- At least one of possible · easy · trust visibly moved — Belief-Shift Primacy anchor 10.
- The asset is built for the receptive person named in Input, not for the closed/shallow/distracted soils (Pattern 8) — no over-teaching to win receivers who were never going to move.
- Exactly one next step closes the asset; the receiver leaves empowered-to-start, not overwhelmed.
- Every flag carries a paste-able fix, not advice; self-reported numbers, if referenced, carry UNCONFIRMED.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Auditing depth without a context.** Calling a paid-workshop outline "over-teaching" because it has HOW in it. Recovery: lock Tool 1 context first — HOW is correct in the paid room, wrong on the feed.
- **Confusing over-teaching with over-delivering.** Cutting the value density that makes a free video rewatch-worthy. Recovery: keep the value and feeling; cut only the information COUNT and the lingo. Over-deliver, don't over-teach.
- **Killing every idea instead of keeping one.** Stripping the asset to nothing. Recovery: the job is to keep the ONE best idea at full strength, not to minimize — boil down, don't boil away.
- **Flag inflation.** Logging twelve micro-issues so nothing gets fixed. Recovery: rank by severity; Flag 1 (more than one core idea) and Flag 5 (no next step) are structural and override the cosmetic tells.
- **Delivering a critique instead of a rewrite.** A red-line table with no boiled-down asset is the firehose pointed at the author. Recovery: always ship Step 5 — the rewrite is the deliverable, the table is the receipt.
