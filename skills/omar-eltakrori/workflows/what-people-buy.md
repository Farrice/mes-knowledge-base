# What People Buy — The 8 Buyables Audit

Omar's offers-department thesis: *"When you really understand and look at what people are actually buying, they're not actually buying what you think they are buying."* Nobody woke up today wanting a workbook, a Zoom call bundle, a Discord, or a course. Jeep sells adventure, not cars; cleaners don't sell cleanliness, they sell a transformed space. What people pay for online reduces to eight buyables:

1. **Acceleration** — speed to a guaranteeable outcome (*"launch your podcast in under 30 days"*; the $35–40K "Dialed in a Day"). *"Your business could be the easy button."* Only usable when you can truly deliver the outcome in the time.
2. **Affiliation** — connection, cosign, status by association (paying to guest on the podcast; *"there was status in the price point"* — $100K to say "I invested $100K in myself").
3. **Transformation** — who they become plus the result (*"the most lucrative"* buyable; skill-sets are transformations: "six-figure day trader"). Non-negotiable: *"if you sell a transformation and you yourself are unwilling to go first, no one's going to buy from you."*
4. **Organization** — *"people don't buy information, they buy organization of information"* (via Rory Vaden). Everything's on YouTube; sequence it and it's worth $300. *"Scattered information is actually harming the growth process."*
5. **Approximation** (proximity) — getting physically or virtually CLOSE. *"We're not paying for music [at concerts]. We're paying to get close."* Inherently scarce, which is why it prices high (VIP tiers, in-person, small rooms).
6. **Identification** — identity language on the offer (*"content mastery inner circle," "the six-figure aesthetician"*; Iron Man sells an identity with a race attached). *"Most of our purchases is a reflection of our identity."*
7. **Congregation** — belonging among like-minded people headed the same direction (the mastermind, the community offer — sold in identity/acceleration words, never as "join my Discord"). Retention physics: *"when you leave, you're leaving the community."* The facilitator gets the credit.
8. **Consolidation** — the one-stop shop: *"you can create an offer where there is no 'what else do I need to buy' — I thought about everything you're thinking about and it's here."*

The offer behind the paywall may not change at all. What changes is what it is SOLD as — and *"when you use different words, you make different kinds of money."*

## Workflow

### Step 1: Strip the offer naked
Write the offer as the buyer's feed sees it: the THING (course, community, calls, workbook). Then apply the test — *"nobody woke up today wanting to buy [thing] from you."* If the current name/pitch leads with the thing (modules, Zooms, cohort length), flag every instance. *"Hey, what do you think about this seven-week cohort?" — "Sounds like work to me."*

### Step 2: Score the offer against all 8 buyables
For each buyable, score 0–2 (0 = absent, 1 = present but unspoken, 2 = present AND sold in the copy):

| Buyable | Is it truly in the offer? | Is it SOLD (named in the language)? | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acceleration | | | |
| Affiliation | | | |
| Transformation | | | |
| Organization | | | |
| Approximation | | | |
| Identification | | | |
| Congregation | | | |
| Consolidation | | | |

The usual finding: the offer CONTAINS four or five buyables and SELLS zero of them. The more you can honestly hit, the more bulletproof the offer — but there is always one main buyable.

### Step 3: Pick the primary buyable
Default to **Transformation** if you can honestly claim it (*"the most lucrative"*), with two gates:
- **The acceleration gate**: only claim speed if the outcome is genuinely guaranteeable in the window. If not (stocks, long skills), sell the transformation or the identity instead — Omar's own correction on this.
- **The go-first gate**: the operator must have BOUGHT and LIVED what they're selling. *"You can't sell something unless you've boughten something."* If they haven't, the primary buyable must be something they truly embody, or the offer waits.

### Step 4: Rewrite the offer language per buyable
Rewrite name, promise, and bullet copy so the primary buyable leads and the supporting buyables are named, using the buyer's vocabulary:
- Acceleration → outcome + time window ("in a day," "in 30 days")
- Identification → identity phrase in the NAME ("the [six-figure/premium/boss] [who-they-become]")
- Organization → "everything, in order, in one path" framing against the scattered-YouTube alternative
- Congregation → "stop building in isolation" + who's in the room
- Consolidation → the "there is no else" promise: every adjacent need pre-answered
- Approximation → the access tier, explicitly scarce ("only X seats can be this close")

Keep the deliverables untouched. This is articulation, not inflation.

### Step 5: Run the sideways-energy check
Omar retired "Dialed in a Day" at its peak because *"every time you provide a solution to a problem, it creates new problems"* — he'd become tech support. For the rewritten offer, list the second-order obligations each buyable creates (proximity = your calendar; consolidation = vendor management; congregation = community moderation). If a buyable's downstream cost is unsustainable, sell a different one. A great offer you can't fulfill calmly is a bad offer.

### Step 6: Set the two money-raisers
To make MORE from the same result, Omar gives exactly two levers: *"you either sell it to a different person, or you articulate it in a more sophisticated way."* Write both options: (a) the more sophisticated buyer for whom this same outcome is worth multiples (the entrepreneur, not the high-schooler), and (b) the more sophisticated articulation ("I help people with content" → "I help you attract high-quality leads on autopilot 24/7 while building the personal brand you'll wish you'd started"). Pick one as the next iteration.

## Quality Gate
> 🛡️ **Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric` (Feeling-Over-Information, Belief-Shift Primacy, Obsession Signaling) before delivering.
- The rewrite never leads with the thing (modules/calls/cohort) — buyable first, mechanism second.
- Every claimed buyable is HONESTLY in the offer; acceleration only where the outcome is guaranteeable; go-first gate answered.
- Identity language appears in the offer NAME if Identification scored ≥1.
- The sideways-energy check produced at least one real obligation, not "none."
- Self-reported Omar figures (Dialed-in-a-Day pricing, $720K day, client stories) labeled UNCONFIRMED if cited in client-facing copy.
- No banned AI tells; deliverable ≤2 pages.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Claiming all eight.** Stuffing every buyable into the copy recreates the fire-hose. One primary, two or three supporting, the rest silent.
- **Acceleration without a guarantee.** Selling speed on an outcome you can't control (markets, algorithms) borrows trust you'll repay with refunds. Recovery: shift to transformation or identification.
- **Selling congregation as the thing.** "Join my community" names the mechanism, not the buyable. Recovery: sell the belonging and the direction ("stop being alone in your cubicle"), let community be the vehicle.
- **Ignoring the go-first gate.** An operator selling a transformation they never bought is *"a phony"* — Omar's word. Recovery: buy the category first ("let your purchases show where you are in life"; every purchase is R&D), or pick a buyable they've lived.
- **Articulation drifting into fabrication.** The words change what it's sold as, never what it does. If a rewrite promises something the deliverables can't produce, cut it — the belief-shift triad still needs the trust leg.
