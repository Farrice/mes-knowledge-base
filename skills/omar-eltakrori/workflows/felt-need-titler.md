# Felt-Need Titler

Omar put one word in a title — *"teacher"* — and the comments wrote themselves: *"I know I'm called to teach,"* *"I know I'm supposed to teach,"* *"I know I need to teach."* He didn't argue anyone into wanting it. He named a need they already carried, and the right people raised their hands. His model for it is older than any marketing playbook: *"Jesus always took care of the need they knew they had before he helped them with the need he knew they had."* Surface the felt need first; deliver the belief-shift second.

Most titles fail because they name the need the creator thinks the audience *should* have. This one only names needs the audience already says out loud about themselves. The deliverable is a ranked bank of titles, hooks, and openers, each tagged with the exact felt need it calls — and each titled in HOW while reserving the actual HOW for the paid container.

3. `skills/omar-eltakrori/references/genius-patterns.md` — **Patterns 3, 5, 7** (and 9 for platform objective)

> 🔒 **Pre-Flight Gate**: You must have the audience's OWN words — at least 5 phrases they use about themselves, pulled from comments, DMs, reviews, call transcripts, or their bio language. No source material = no titler. Inventing the audience's self-talk produces posters that name needs nobody feels. If you don't have their language, stop and harvest it first (comments under similar creators, the "I know I'm called to..." pattern).

## Input Required
- The audience's actual self-descriptions: 5+ phrases in THEIR vocabulary ("I know I'm called to teach," "I'm good at this but I'm broke," "I feel like I'm meant for more")
- The platform/context this ships to (IG feed / YouTube / email / webinar title / challenge name) — sets the objective per Tool 1
- The next step you want them to take (follow, watch long-form, download, enroll) — the title pulls toward this
- The ONE belief you'll shift in the body (from the possible · easy · trust triad) — the title names the need, the body shifts the belief
- Optional: the offer/topic, so the WHAT you deliver maps to something you actually sell

## Workflow

### Step 1: Harvest the needs they already name (their words, not yours)
List every need the audience states about THEMSELVES, verbatim. The format is first-person, present-tense, in their vocabulary — never your jargon. Mine the "I know I..." construction Omar caught: *"I know I'm called to teach."* That phrase IS the gold, because the person has already done the wanting for you.

| # | Their exact phrase (verbatim) | The need underneath it | Felt-need temperature (HOT = they say it unprompted / WARM = implied) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I know I'm called to teach" | identity permission to start | HOT |
| 2 | "I'm great at what I do but I'm underpaid" | recognition + income gap (Omar's recurring nerve) | HOT |

Discard any need YOU added that they never voice. A need you have to convince them of is not a felt need — it's a poster.

### Step 2: Match each need to a felt-need formula
Map every HOT/WARM need from Step 1 onto a Tool 5 formula. The match is mechanical, not creative — the formula is chosen by the SHAPE of the need:

| If the need is... | Use formula (Tool 5) |
|---|---|
| an identity they already claim | **1. The Identity Name** — "If you're [identity], here's what nobody tells you about [outcome]" |
| frustration aimed at the wrong cause | **2. The Belief-Reframe** — "The real reason you're not [outcome] (it's not [what they blame])" |
| wanting a result via the obvious path | **3. The Contrarian Highest-Leverage** — "[Common advice] won't make you [outcome]. [Reframed move] will" |
| fear of starting / blank-page paralysis | **4. The What-Before-How** — "What to do before you [start the thing]" |
| a secret want they're afraid to admit | **5. The Permission Grant** — "You're allowed to [thing] without [feared cost]" |
| competence-but-broke gap | **6. The Cost-of-Inaction Name** — "Why being great at [skill] is keeping you broke" |
| temptation toward a tempting wrong move | **7. The When-Not Negation** — "When NOT to [common move]" |
| overwhelm / too-many-tactics fatigue | **8. The One-Thing Promise** — "The one thing that changes [outcome] (stop doing everything else)" |

### Step 3: Write 8–12 titles/hooks
Generate 8–12 candidates across the matched formulas — at least 2 formulas represented, never all from one. Fill every bracket from the audience's Step 1 vocabulary, not yours. Omar's own line is the template: *"Sales is not the highest paying skill. Teaching is the highest paying skill"* — it names the need (get paid well) and reframes the path in eight words. Keep each candidate to one breath. If it teaches two needs, split it into two titles.

### Step 4: Apply title-in-HOW / deliver-WHAT (Pattern 7)
For each candidate, run the packaging split. The title can pose a "how to" to capture reach — *"Here's how to become a millionaire"* — but you commit, on the same line, to delivering WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE in the body and reserving the HOW for the paid container. Write the one-line WHAT-promise beside each title so the bank is body-ready, not just a headline list. Omar's Premiere Pro proof: the title said "how to edit," the body delivered *"create a folder on your desktop"* — a WHAT, and people *"felt more empowered to edit, 100%."*

For each title, record: `Title (in HOW) → Body delivers (the WHAT) → HOW reserved for (the paid context)`.

### Step 5: Pick the strongest by the one question
Rank every candidate against a single test: **"Does this call them with a need they already feel?"** Not "is it clever," not "is it complete." Score each 1–5 on felt-need precision (does the right person feel *seen*?) and tag the verdict. The winner is the one where a stranger in the target audience would silently think *"that's me."* Tie-break by which one names a HOT need (said unprompted) over a WARM one. Flag any candidate that produces "huh, interesting" instead of "that's me" — interesting is a poster; *that's me* is the felt need.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| IG feed | One need, one formula per post. Hook = the title; first line repeats the felt need in their words. Objective: identity recognition ("that's me — they see my problem") |
| IG story / Reel cover | The Permission Grant (5) and Identity Name (1) land fastest; the felt need must read in <2 seconds on the cover frame |
| YouTube title | Title-in-HOW is mandatory here for reach (Pattern 9); body delivers WHAT generously so they over-receive and trust. Pair with a thumbnail that shows the identity, not the topic |
| Email subject line | Cost-of-Inaction (6) and One-Thing (8) outperform; the felt need must survive the inbox preview-text truncation — front-load it |
| Webinar / masterclass title | Lead with possible · easy · trust (Pattern 5). The title names the need; the promise installs "easy" so the paid relationship pre-sells. Reserve HOW for inside |
| Challenge / event name | Name the identity + the small win ("the 5-day [identity] foundations"). Felt need + doable outcome = the enrollment pull |
| Sales call opener | Open by naming their stated need back to them in their words before any pitch — "you told me you know you're meant for more but you're underpaid." That's the felt-need title, spoken |

## Quality Gate
> 🛡️ **Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric` (Belief-Shift Primacy, WHAT-not-HOW Discipline, Feeling-Over-Information) before delivering.
- Every title traces to a need the audience NAMES in their own words — zero needs invented by the creator (the "that's me" test, not "interesting").
- Every bracket is filled from the audience's vocabulary, never industry lingo.
- Each title carries its title-in-HOW / deliver-WHAT split; the HOW is reserved for the paid context, never dumped in the title's promise (Pattern 7).
- The bank names ONE belief it shifts in the body (possible/easy/trust) — it isn't just clever headlines (Pattern 5).
- At least 2 formulas represented; no monoculture of one pattern.
- The lead pick names a HOT need (said unprompted), and you can quote the audience phrase it calls.
- No banned AI tells: no "Here's what/why/how" openers in the workflow prose, no twin-sentence endings, no triple-anaphora. (Titles MAY use "Here's how to..." — that's Omar's deliberate title-in-HOW move, not a prose tell.)
- Self-reported numbers (the "teacher" comments, Premiere Pro 2M+ views, the Paige case) carry UNCONFIRMED labels if cited.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Naming the need YOU think they should have.** A title that has to convince them they want it isn't a felt-need title — it's an argument. Recovery: discard every need not voiced verbatim in Step 1; re-pull from comments until you have 5 HOT phrases.
- **Clever over clear.** Wordplay that makes the creator feel smart but makes the audience feel nothing. Recovery: re-run the single question — *"does the right person silently think 'that's me'?"* If it scores on cleverness but not recognition, cut it.
- **Title promises the HOW.** The headline pulls them in with "I'll show you exactly how," then the free body owes a step-by-step it shouldn't deliver. Recovery: re-split — title in HOW, body delivers the WHAT, HOW moves to the paid container (Tool 3). This is calibration, not gatekeeping.
- **One formula for everything.** All 10 titles are Contrarian-Reframes because it's the creator's comfort move. Recovery: force-match needs to formula by SHAPE (Step 2 table); a fear-of-starting need cannot be a reframe, it's a What-Before-How.
- **Stacking two needs in one title.** Trying to call the underpaid AND the called-to-teach AND the overwhelmed in one line names none of them. Recovery: one need per title — boil it down to one (the anti-firehose discipline applies to titles too).
