# Teach-to-Sell Engine

Omar shoots straight into the camera, pulls the SD card, uploads it to YouTube unedited, and gives away the entire play — the same play he charges five figures to deliver. People fly in from Florida, Denver, Vancouver, drive up from California and Utah for a live recording they *know* will be posted free. Nobody feels robbed. They feel like moving. That is the whole craft: *"The goal to teach is not to get people information. The goal of teaching is to create the feeling in a person to take the next step."* This workflow builds that asset — the one that teaches generously and sells without a sales team, because the teaching IS the selling.

The trap this exists to break: experts who are *too close to the solution* answer "how do I make this convert?" by adding more — more steps, more lingo, more proof. Omar's answer runs the other direction. One thing, delivered so it lands as a feeling, with the next step already pre-won in the receiver's mind before any pitch arrives.

## Input Required
- The expertise (what the creator is genuinely good at — the thing people already ask them for)
- The asset type and channel (IG post / YouTube long-form / email / web-class segment / sales call / webinar / DM)
- The next step the asset should make someone feel like taking (book / buy / reply / opt-in / show up live / upgrade)
- Which of the four business phases this asset lives in (Lead Gen / Conversion / Fulfillment / Retention) — Pattern 2 sets the teach-objective
- The receiver's current belief about the topic (what they think is true *before* meeting this creator) — best available read

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Set the ONE idea + the target FEELING (Pattern 3)
Write two single sentences and nothing more:
- **The one idea**: the single thing this asset teaches (not the curriculum — the one drink of water).
- **The target feeling**: what the receiver should FEEL at the end that makes the next step obvious. Phrase it as a felt state, not an information goal: *"they should feel that this is doable for someone like them, and relief that there's a clear first move."*

Servant-communicator test: write from the receiver's seat, not the creator's. *"It's not just what you say, it's how you say what you say."* If the feeling sentence describes what the creator wants to *prove*, rewrite it — proving is insecurity (Pattern 4), and there's nothing to prove.

**Produces:** the ONE-idea line + the target-feeling line. These are the asset's spine; every later beat serves them or gets cut.

### Step 2 — Identify the belief to shift + the next-step the feeling must produce (Patterns 5, 10)
True influence = the receiver thinks about the thing differently after meeting you *and acts on it.* Make that mechanical:
- **Belief BEFORE** (one sentence — what they currently believe makes this hard / impossible / not-for-them).
- **Belief AFTER** (one sentence — what they'll believe once the feeling lands).
- **The next step the feeling produces** (the specific micro-action the AFTER-belief makes obvious — reply, click, book, show up).

The shift is the product, not the info (Pattern 10 — package the tactic, lead with the belief). Name the gap between BEFORE and AFTER in plain words; that gap is what the teach has to close.

**Produces:** a belief-shift line (BEFORE → AFTER) and the named next step.

### Step 3 — Build the teach as servant communication, receiver-first (Patterns 3, 4)
Draft the body to close the BELIEF gap, teaching only the one idea. Discipline rules, enforced as you write:
- **One thing.** *"Boil it down to one. One thing at a time. It's one drink at a time."* If a second idea sneaks in, it goes to a different asset.
- **Zero unexplained lingo.** Industry terms are fire-hose water — cut or translate every one.
- **No how-from-your-POV weeds.** You're not in the paid container yet (see Step 4).
- **Speak to the receptive fraction** (Pattern 8) — write for the person ready to move, not the skeptic you're defending against.

**Produces:** a one-idea teach draft that a cold receiver could follow with zero prior context.

### Step 4 — Deliver WHAT, not HOW (Pattern 7 — title-how / deliver-what)
Reframe the teach so it gives WHAT / WHEN / WHY / WHERE (and their negations: what NOT to do, when NOT to), and reserves step-by-step HOW for the paid container. *"When you start teaching how, you will start to lose people."*
- The **title** may say "how to" (reach + the model of the thing people came for).
- The **body** shows what to do *before* / *around* the how — the way Omar's "how to edit in Premiere Pro" never teaches editing, only the moves *before* you edit, and viewers leave *"empowered to start."*
- Park the granular HOW for the paid/anyone-can-do-it context (Pattern 6) — *"when somebody pays you, they'll be more receptive of the how-to."*

This is **context-calibration, never gatekeeping.** In free/transient channels the WHAT is given generously; the value lives in the *context*, not in what's withheld (Pattern 6: *"the context in which the content is consumed determines the value"*).

**Produces:** the teach rewritten as WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE + negations, with the HOW explicitly parked and labeled "→ paid container."

### Step 5 — Over-DELIVER in value while teaching only ONE thing (Pattern 4)
Over-delivering ≠ over-teaching. The asset should feel like a fortune (someone re-watches a free video for the seventh time and feels they got more than they paid for) *while still teaching one idea.* You raise perceived value WITHOUT adding information by: a real story or client result (lived, uncopyable — Pattern 11), naming the model so it sticks, drawing/diagramming the one idea (*"our brains are always looking for a picture"*), or a specific number that makes the WHAT concrete. Generosity is in the *delivery and feeling*, never the information count.

**Produces:** 1–2 over-deliver moves layered onto the one-idea teach (story / named model / picture / specific) — no new ideas added.

### Step 6 — Install the make-it-easy belief so the paid step feels pre-won (Pattern 5)
Land the close inside the teach itself. The Possible · Easy · Trust triad, written explicitly:
- **POSSIBLE** — the receiver sees the outcome is doable *for someone like them* (proof or analog they recognize).
- **EASY** — the path feels easy *because you said it.* This is the hidden close: *"it's going to be easy if I work with them"* — making it seem easy IS the sale.
- **TRUST** — you're calm, specific, and you proved you can help *by actually helping* (Frank Kern). No straining to prove.

Then write the clean self-selection next step (Pattern 9 — *"99.99% won't click the link, and that's totally fine. But for the 0.1%, let's go."*) — an invitation, not a hard sell, because the easy-belief already did the closing.

**Produces:** the three belief-shift lines + the self-selecting next-step CTA, with the "easy" belief installed in the free body so the paid step is pre-won.

### Step 7 — Margin-note the asset (the feeling map)
Assemble the finished asset and annotate every beat with the ONE feeling it creates. If a beat creates no feeling toward the next step, it's filler — cut it (fire-hose check, Pattern 4).

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | What changes |
|--------|--------------|
| **IG post / Reel** | One idea, one feeling, one frame. WHAT-not-HOW is near-total; HOW is the DM/link. Over-deliver via a single named picture (the diagram IS the post). |
| **YouTube long-form** | Use duration to become undeniable (Pattern 9 — *"you can't fake who you are for a long time"*). Title in "how to," deliver WHAT; self-select at the end. Unedited is fine — it's identity, not polish. |
| **Email** | One idea per send. The feeling is the subject line's job; the body installs *easy*; the P.S. is the self-selecting next step. Never a curriculum — one drink. |
| **Web-class / webinar segment** | This is a *teach phase that converts* (Pattern 2). Shift the belief that makes the next step obvious, install Possible·Easy·Trust live, then open the cart as the natural next teach — selling is a byproduct of the teach. |
| **Sales call** | Reframe the call as a teach, not a pitch. Teach the ONE belief-shift; let "it's easy if I work with them" do the close (Pattern 5). Fast-buyer energy (Interior Game) sets the frame. |
| **Fulfillment / course module** | Here the HOW is finally allowed — the paid, anyone-can-do-it context (Patterns 6, 7). Still one idea per module; over-deliver on clarity, not volume. |

## Quality Gate
> 🛡️ **Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md` → Exterior Game Quality Rubric (Feeling-Over-Information, Context-Calibrated Disclosure, WHAT-not-HOW Discipline, Belief-Shift Primacy) before delivering. Score each ≥7 or revise the weakest beat once.
- Teaches **exactly ONE thing** — the fire-hose check passes (Pattern 4). No second idea, no unexplained lingo.
- The asset is judged by the **impulse it creates** ("I felt ready to start/book/buy"), not by coverage ("I learned a lot") — Pattern 3 / Feeling-Over-Information rubric.
- Body delivers **WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE + negations**; granular HOW is explicitly parked for the paid container — context-calibration, NOT gatekeeping (Patterns 6, 7).
- All three belief-shifts are written and present: **Possible · Easy · Trust**; the *easy* belief is installed in the free body so the paid step is pre-won (Pattern 5).
- **Over-deliver ≠ over-teach**: the value-raise comes from story / picture / specificity, never from added information.
- Every beat has a feeling-margin-note; any beat that creates no next-step feeling was cut.
- Self-reported numbers (Omar's revenue, the Paige case, the $110K to Myron Golden, "2M+ views") carry the **UNCONFIRMED** label.
- **Faith handling**: if the Four-Soils or any Scripture frame appears, the operating PRINCIPLE leads (design for the receptive fraction; receptivity decides the harvest); Scripture is optional grounding, and the asset runs fully in a secular deployment.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Teaching two things "to be generous."** Generosity is over-delivery on feeling, not idea-count. Two ideas overwhelm; an overwhelmed person never acts. Recovery: split into two assets, one drink each.
- **Drifting into HOW from the creator's POV.** The expert is too close to the solution and dumps the weeds. Recovery: rewrite the beat as WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE; move the steps to the "→ paid container" line.
- **Optimizing for information, calling it a "value bomb."** A value bomb that creates no feeling is a fire hose. Recovery: re-run Step 1 — name the feeling first, then keep only beats that produce it.
- **Hard-selling at the CTA.** If you have to push, the *easy* belief never got installed (Step 6 failed). Recovery: go back and make the path feel easy in the body; then the CTA is a self-selection, not a shove.
- **Gatekeeping out of fear.** Withholding the WHAT to "protect the secret sauce" backwards-prices the asset. Recovery: Pattern 6 — the context (committed attention), not the withheld info, is what the paid container sells. Give the WHAT.
- **Proving instead of helping.** Strained authority signals insecurity. Recovery: *"Prove to people you can help them by actually helping them"* — cut the credentials, add the one useful thing.
