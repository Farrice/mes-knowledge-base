# What-Not-How

Omar's video titled "How to edit in Premiere Pro" has 2M+ views and never teaches a single edit. It tells beginners to make a folder, open the app, save the project, create a sequence — "Now you're ready to edit." He didn't gatekeep the craft; he removed the paralysis of the blank start, and people felt empowered to begin. That accident became a rule he paid to learn: "You don't want to teach how-to from your point of view... Teach when or when not. Teach why or why not. Teach where or where not. Teach what or what not. But don't teach how." Because "when you start teaching how, you will start to lose people."

This workflow takes any "how to X" impulse and runs it through the substitution that drives his most-watched content — keeping the felt-need pull in the title, swapping the mechanics in the body for WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE, and parking the actual HOW where the attention can absorb it. The receiver leaves empowered to START, not buried in weeds.

- The receiver's vocabulary — the words THEY use for the problem (never your jargon)
- The one belief you want shifted by the end (possible / easy / trust — Pattern 5). If unknown, the body has no job.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Capture the How-To Impulse (and the weeds it would dump)
Write the asset's title as it currently lives in your head: "How to ___." Then list, brutally, every step-by-step mechanic you're tempted to teach inside it — the tool settings, the lingo, the "first you do this, then you do that." This is the firehose you're about to NOT pour (Pattern 4). Naming it makes it cuttable. If the list is long, good — that's the over-teach you would have shipped.

### Step 2: Substitute — Generate the WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE (+ negations)
Run the impulse through Tool 3's substitution table. Fill all four lanes, and force at least one negation — the negations are often the highest-trust, fastest-clarity teaching you can give:

- **WHAT (and WHAT NOT):** What they actually do, or what to do BEFORE X, or what kills X. (The Premiere move: "create a folder," not "color-grade." Orientation, not mechanics.)
- **WHEN (and WHEN NOT):** The season X works; when it's too early/too late. (Removes the fear of acting at the wrong moment.)
- **WHY (and WHY NOT):** Why X matters; why most people fail; why the obvious approach backfires. (This is the belief-shift lane — Pattern 5 lives here.)
- **WHERE (and WHERE NOT):** Where to start; where people waste effort. (Points them at the right surface.)

Cut everything that survives only as a mechanic. If a line answers "how do I execute the technique," it belongs in Step 4's paid container, not the body.

### Step 3: Write the HOW-Titled Hook That Opens It
Keep the felt-need pull — title in "how to." "You could teach how to, but you're just packaging it in how to." Use the receiver's words, name the need they KNOW they have (Omar: put "teacher" in a title, comments filled with "I know I'm called to teach"). The title promises the outcome; the body delivers the WHAT that shifts the belief. (For sharper hook construction, hand the title to `luke-iha-vsl-leads` — this workflow sets the felt need; that skill weaponizes the open.)

Disqualifier: if the only way the title makes sense is by delivering mechanics, the title is over-promising HOW. Re-title to a WHAT-before-X or a contrarian-leverage frame (teaching-craft Tool 5).

### Step 4: Apply HOW-Reconciliation — Relocate the Real HOW
The HOW does not vanish; it moves to the context where attention can absorb it (Pattern 6). State explicitly WHERE the HOW lives and WHY there:

- Omar's rule: "when somebody pays you, they'll be more receptive of the how-to."
- The single license to teach a tactic in the body: "when it's delivered in the right context and in a way that anyone can do it." If your HOW is creator-POV ("here's my exact settings"), it fails that test — relocate it.
- Hormozi's compatible version: "teach how-to, then sell the implementation."

Write one line naming the paid/committed container that holds the HOW (course, workshop, challenge, coaching, the discovery call). This is the next step the free asset earns — context-calibration, never gatekeeping.

### Step 5: Worked Example (model the output before shipping)
Run one concrete pass end-to-end so the reframe is visible, not abstract:

> **Impulse:** "How to write a high-converting email." **Weeds cut:** subject-line formulas, P.S. tactics, send-time A/B mechanics.
> **WHAT:** Before you write, name the one feeling the email must create. **WHAT NOT:** don't teach two ideas in one email. **WHEN NOT:** don't send when you have no single next step. **WHY most fail:** they write to transfer info, not to move someone (Pattern 3). **WHERE to start:** the receiver's exact words for the problem.
> **Title (in HOW):** "How to write an email people actually reply to." **HOW lives:** in the paid container, where "anyone can do it" — the sequence templates, not the principle.

### Step 6: Firehose Re-Check
Before delivery, run the reframed body against Tool 2. If two or more red flags fire (more than one idea, lingo, options-listing, no single next step, leaves them overwhelmed), you re-substituted incompletely — a mechanic slipped back in. "Boil it down to one. One thing at a time. It's one drink at a time."

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| Instagram post / reel | One belief-shift only. Title poses the "how to," caption delivers WHAT/WHY. No carousel of steps. |
| YouTube long-form | Title in HOW; deliver generous WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE so they rewatch ("watching this for the seventh time"), HOW stays out. Become undeniable (Pattern 9). |
| Email | Subject = felt-need HOW; body = one WHAT + one negation + one next step. Mechanics live in the linked paid asset. |
| Webinar / masterclass | WHAT framed so "easy" installs the belief "it'll be easy if I work with them" (Pattern 5). The implementation HOW is the enrollment promise, not the free teach. |
| Sales call | WHAT/WHY to shift possible·easy·trust; the prospect should feel the HOW is reserved for the engagement they're buying. Never demo the full HOW for free. |
| Challenge (5-day) | Lightest HOW that produces a same-day win (build for implementation); the full system's HOW ascends to the paid program. |
| Workshop / bootcamp / paid program | HOW is fully allowed — this IS the right context, "in a way that anyone can do it." Substitution rule relaxes; deliver everything. |

## Common Pitfalls
- **Substituting the title but not the body.** A "how to" title over a step-by-step body is the exact trap Omar warns about — you lose people in the weeds. Recovery: re-run Step 2; every body line must answer WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE, never "how do I execute."
- **Deleting the HOW instead of relocating it.** That IS gatekeeping, and it leaves no next step. Recovery: Step 4 — name the paid container; the HOW is the thing they're buying attention for.
- **Negation-as-filler.** A throwaway "don't be lazy" isn't a WHAT-NOT teach. Recovery: the negation must remove a real fear or a common wrong move (when NOT, where NOT, what kills it).
- **Confusing context-calibration with stinginess.** If the free asset feels withholding rather than generous, you starved the WHAT. Recovery: over-DELIVER on the WHAT (Tool 2's value density) while still teaching only one thing — over-delivering is not over-teaching.
- **Reframing without a target feeling.** A reframe that moves no belief is just rearranged information. Recovery: set the one belief in Input; if you can't, the asset has no job yet.
