# Teaching-Craft Playbook — Omar Eltakrori

> The operational heart the workflows pull from. The Interior Game answers *who you become*; this answers *how you teach so people feel like moving.* Five ready-to-use tools: the Objective-by-Context Matrix, the Over-Teaching Red-Flag Checklist, the WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE Substitution Table, the Four-Soils Audience Model, and the Felt-Need Title Formulas. Ground everything in one law: *"The goal of teaching is to create the feeling in a person to take the next step."* Information transfer is the amateur objective; the felt impulse to act is the master objective.

## Tool 1 — The Objective-by-Context Matrix

The foundational discipline: *"if you don't know why you're teaching, you won't know what to teach."* Objective is set by context, because *"the context in which the content is consumed determines the value of the content."* The same teaching is worth nothing on a transient feed and a fortune in a paid room — not because the information changed, but because the receiver's attention changed. Set the objective and the target feeling BEFORE you create. Then everything else (how much to give, WHAT vs HOW, the next step) falls out of the objective rather than out of your excitement to dump everything you know.

Read the rows top to bottom as a single funnel: each context's "next step" is the entry point to the row below it. Free/transient contexts carry the WHAT generously to build trust and prove obsession; paid/committed contexts are the only place HOW belongs, because *"when somebody pays you, they'll be more receptive of the how-to."*

| Context | Primary Objective | How much to give | WHAT vs HOW | Target feeling | Next step |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Instagram feed** | Identity recognition — name the need they KNOW they have so they self-identify ("I'm called to teach") | One idea. A single belief-shift per post. Over-deliver on value, never on volume. | WHAT / WHY only. No how-to weeds. Title can pose a "how to," body delivers the WHAT. | "That's me — this person sees my problem." | Follow / save / read the next post |
| **Instagram / IG story** | Proximity and pattern — show up daily so they feel they know you; tease the room | A glimpse. A single thought, a behind-the-scenes, a pointer to deeper content. | Neither — mostly WHY-this-matters and presence. Context-setting, not instruction. | "I want to keep watching this person." | Tap the link / DM / watch the long-form |
| **YouTube long-form** | Become undeniable — *"so that you undeniably know who I am"*; *"you can't fake who you are for a long time"* | Generous WHAT. Over-deliver so they rewatch ("watching this for the seventh time"), but still teach essentially ONE thing well. | WHAT / WHEN / WHY / WHERE (+ negations). Title in "how to," deliver the WHAT. HOW stays out. | "I got more than I paid for, and I trust this person." | Download the free asset (gear guide) / subscribe / DM "watched till the end" |
| **Podcast** | Depth of relationship over time; reveal who you are across hours | Generous, conversational. Stories and reference points, not curriculum. *"Everything is a reference point."* | WHAT and WHY through story. The teaching is revealed, not lectured. | "This person is real, and I'm becoming a regular." | Same-platform free offer / move to the live room |
| **Webinar / Masterclass** | Shift the possible · easy · trust triad and create the felt impulse to enroll | Enough WHAT to prove it's real and easy; withhold the implementation HOW. Make-it-easy IS the close. | WHAT, framed so "easy" installs the belief *"it'll be easy if I work with them."* HOW is the paid promise, not the free deliverable. | "It's possible, it's easy, and I trust this person to walk me through it." | Enroll / book the call / join the paid container |
| **5-day Challenge** | Foundations + momentum — *"a great place to get your foundations"*; let them implement and feel a small win | More WHAT, sequenced into doable daily action. Still one core move per day, not the firehose. | Mostly WHAT, with the lightest HOW that produces a same-day win (build for implementation). | "I already did something and it worked — I want the full system." | Ascend to the core paid program |
| **Workshop / Bootcamp** | Transformation through done-with-you implementation in the right context | Full depth. This is the paid, in-the-room context where you *can* show everything. | HOW is fully allowed — *"in the right context and in a way that anyone can do it."* | "I'm being walked through this; I can actually do it." | Retention / ascend to the next level of teaching |
| **Paid container (program / coaching)** | Fulfillment as teaching + ascension; deliver the HOW they paid attention to receive | Everything relevant to their season. Consolidate and consolidate — they have post-transaction attention. | HOW, WHAT, WHEN, WHY — all of it, calibrated to their grow gap. Post-transaction consumers *"pay attention differently."* | "This was worth it — I'd thank you for selling it to me." | Stay / ascend / become a testimonial that feeds lead gen |

**Operating rule.** Stop asking *"how much do I give away?"* — that question produces gatekeeping anxiety and no clear answer. Ask *"what's the context, and what's the objective for THIS context?"* The disclosure follows automatically. *"The secret sauce to not gatekeeping is understanding the context people are receiving information in."* You are not withholding; you are calibrating. People flew in from Florida, Denver, Vancouver, and Utah for a live recording they knew would be posted free — because the room is the value, not the information.

## Tool 2 — The Over-Teaching Red-Flag Checklist

*"The biggest mistake that people who teach online make is that they tend to over teach."* The image to keep: *"if someone was thirsty and they needed a drink of water, and you came with a fire hose, you didn't serve them. You hurt them."* The mechanism is fatal to revenue: *"an overwhelmed person never takes action."* Run this checklist against any teaching asset before it ships. Each tell has a fix. If two or more fire, you have a firehose, not a teaching.

Keep the distinction sharp: over-teaching is NOT over-delivering. You *want* over-delivery — someone rewatching a free video seven times and feeling they got a fortune. Over-delivery is value density; over-teaching is information volume. You can do the first without the second.

| # | Red flag (the tell) | The fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **More than one core idea.** The asset teaches two, three, five things. | *"Boil it down to one. One thing at a time. It's one drink at a time."* Pick the single belief-shift; cut the rest into future assets. |
| 2 | **Industry lingo.** You're using insider vocabulary because you're close to the solution. | Translate every term into the receiver's words. If they wouldn't say it, you don't say it. Speak their problem, not your jargon. |
| 3 | **How-to weeds.** You drifted into step-by-step mechanics in a free/transient context. | Convert HOW into WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE (see Tool 3). Reserve the HOW for the paid container. |
| 4 | **Verbal processing / options-listing.** *"Oh, you can go this route, or you can do this, or maybe you go here and try it this way."* | Don't think out loud. Decide first, then teach the one decision. *"Boil it down to one."* Lists of options overwhelm; a single clear move empowers. |
| 5 | **No single next step.** The asset ends without one obvious thing to do next. | Engineer exactly one next step that matches the context's objective (Tool 1). One CTA, not three. |
| 6 | **Teaching to transfer information, not to create a feeling.** Success was measured as "they learned a lot." | Reset the objective: *"the goal of teaching is to create the feeling in a person to take the next step."* Name the target feeling first, then teach toward it. |
| 7 | **Insecurity tone.** Proving-worth energy — fast cadence, over-explaining, defensive padding. | *"Insecure people tend to over-teach because they are trying to prove... there's nothing to prove."* Frank Kern: *"Prove to people you can help them by actually helping them."* Slow the cadence; calm tonality signals nothing-to-prove authority. |
| 8 | **Comprehensiveness as the goal.** You're trying to cover the whole topic so no one can say you left something out. | Comprehensive = failure. The format is a feature: *"the format creates its own limitation for the teaching."* Teach what fits the context's objective and let the rest live in the next teach. |
| 9 | **No belief moved.** The reader knows more but believes nothing differently about the thing or about you. | Make sure the asset shifts at least one of possible · easy · trust. *"True influence is thinking differently about something because they met you."* |
| 10 | **The reader leaves overwhelmed instead of empowered.** They feel they "got more than enough" — and freeze. | Aim for empowered-to-start, not fully-equipped. Omar's 2M-view Premiere Pro video never taught editing; it helped people *start*, and *"people felt more empowered to edit, 100%."* |

**The one-line gate before publishing:** *Does this make the receptive person feel like taking exactly one next step?* If it instead makes them feel they now know everything (or that the topic is impossibly complex), it's the firehose. Cut.

## Tool 3 — The WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE Substitution Table

The craft move that drives Omar's most-viewed content. *"Don't teach people how to do something tactfully. Teach when or when not. Teach why or why not. Teach where or where not. Teach what or what not. But don't teach how."* The reason: *"when you start teaching how, you will start to lose people."* Each substitution keeps the receiver oriented and empowered instead of drowning in mechanics. The negations matter as much as the positives — telling someone what NOT to do, when NOT to act, where NOT to go is often the highest-trust, fastest-clarity teaching you can give.

Take any *"how to X"* impulse and run it through this table. The HOW does not disappear — it gets repackaged and relocated to the right context.

| Substitution | Reframe of "how to X" | What it gives the receiver |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **WHAT (and WHAT NOT)** | "What you actually do" / "What to do before you do X" / "What kills X" | Orientation and a starting point without the cognitive load of mechanics |
| **WHEN (and WHEN NOT)** | "When to do X" / "When it's too early or too late" / "The season X works" | Timing and permission — removes the fear of acting at the wrong moment |
| **WHY (and WHY NOT)** | "Why X matters" / "Why most people fail at X" / "Why the obvious approach backfires" | The belief-shift — this is where you change how they think about the thing |
| **WHERE (and WHERE NOT)** | "Where X happens" / "Where to start" / "Where people waste effort on X" | Context and direction — points them at the right environment or surface |

**The packaging rule (HOW-reconciliation).** You CAN title it *"how to"* — the felt-need title pulls them in (*"Here's how to become a millionaire,"* *"Here's how to buy a home"*). But in the body of free/transient content you deliver the WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE (+ negations). The actual HOW is reserved for the paid/anyone-can-do-it container. This is context-calibration, not gatekeeping. Hormozi's compatible version: *"teach how-to, then sell the implementation"* — disguise the how-to, but still deliver the WHAT.

### Worked example — "How to edit videos in Premiere Pro"

Omar's video by this title has 2M+ views and never teaches editing. The HOW impulse ("teach them to edit") gets fully substituted:

- **Title (in HOW):** "How to get started editing in Adobe Premiere Pro — Beginner's Guide." The felt-need pull is real; beginners click.
- **Body delivers WHAT-before-you-start, not HOW-to-edit:**
  - WHAT: *"You're going to create a folder on your desktop."* (A what, not a how.)
  - WHAT next: open Premiere Pro → Save As → create a sequence. *"Now you're ready to edit."*
  - WHY it works: it removes the paralysis of the blank start; *"I helped them start."*
- **The HOW (actual editing technique)** lives in a separate context — a tutorial, a paid course, or a workshop where *"anyone can do it."*
- **Result:** *"Did people feel more empowered to edit after watching that video? 100%."* He shifted the belief that editing is hard by showing how to *begin*, and reserved the mechanics for the right container.

**How to run it on your own content:** Write your "how to X" title. Then, for the body, fill four lines — the WHAT-to-do (or what-to-do-first), the WHEN/WHEN-not, the WHY-most-fail, the WHERE-to-start. Ship that as the free asset. Park the step-by-step HOW in your paid container, where the post-transaction attention can actually absorb it.

## Tool 4 — The Four-Soils Audience Model (Teaching-Design Tool)

**Principle first (secular):** the same teaching lands on four kinds of receiver, and only a small fraction is ready to act on it. You cannot convert all four with one asset, and trying to is the root of over-teaching — you firehose because you're trying to reach the closed and shallow receivers who were never going to move. Design instead for the receptive fraction who will implement. Hold the healthy assumption that *"not everyone's going to get what I'm going to put down — and that's totally fine."*

**Scripture grounding (optional):** Omar draws this from Jesus's parable of the sower (the seed/soil), which Jesus prefaces by saying that if you don't understand this one parable, you won't understand any other. The principle stands without the source; the source deepens it for those who want it.

| Soil | The receiver | What they need (and whether to chase it) | Design implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Path / closed heart** | Hears but doesn't understand; *"birds immediately snatch it away."* Often already decided against you. | Nothing you can give in one asset will open a closed heart. Do not redesign around them. | Accept the loss. Don't soften, over-explain, or firehose to win them — that only buries your signal for the receptive. |
| **2. Rocky / shallow heart** | Springs up fast, withers under the sun, no roots. The *"I'm four minutes in and there's nothing"* commenter — *"bro, you missed it."* Shallow attention span. | Wants the soundbite; can't steward a longer arc. Real transformation *"is not going to happen in a soundbite."* | Don't optimize the whole asset for the shallow skimmer. Some will leave; let them. Long-form filters for depth on purpose. |
| **3. Weedy / distracted heart** | Takes root but is choked by worries and *"the deceitfulness of wealth"* — the *"a million's not enough, be a billionaire"* commenter, focused on the wrong thing. | Wants more/bigger/faster; chasing the wrong variable. *"If you're focused on money to begin with, you lose."* | Don't feed the distraction. Name the wrong focus and redirect to the one core move; refuse to bury them in more. |
| **4. Good / receptive soil** | *"Those who do with what they hear."* Produces a harvest. The only group worth designing for: *"the only group I am really concerned with is the receptive group."* | Needs ONE clear, doable next step and the felt permission to act — then they implement. *"There is so much revelation in implementation."* | **Build the entire asset for this 0.01%.** One idea, one belief-shift, one next step, calibrated to make them feel like moving. |

**The design implication, stated as a rule:** Design for the receptive fraction. *"99.99% of you guys won't even click the link... and that's totally fine. But for the 0.1%, let's go."* Every red flag in Tool 2 traces back to a violation of this — you over-teach because you're trying to convert soils 1–3, who will not move no matter how much you give.

**The self-gate (teaching ↔ learning):** the model runs on you first. *"The reason why you suck at teaching is because you're a sucky student... if you suck at learning, you will suck at teaching."* Good soil = implementation. Before you design any teaching, gate yourself: are YOU good soil — do you do something with what you consume, or just *"consume and consume and overthink"*? Only good-soil students become great teachers. (Romans: faith comes by hearing. James: be doers, not merely hearers. Optional grounding; the implementation principle stands alone.)

## Tool 5 — Felt-Need Title Formulas

A title earns the click when it *names the need they already know they have* — not the need you think they should have. *"Jesus always took care of the need they knew they had before he helped them with the need he knew they had."* The proof case: Omar put the word **"teacher"** in a title, and the comments filled with *"I know I'm called to teach,"* *"I know I'm supposed to teach,"* *"I know I need to teach."* He *"called them with the need they knew they needed, and then you can give them the solution."* That is the whole game of a title — surface a felt need so precisely the right person feels seen, then deliver the WHAT that shifts a belief.

Each formula below is a fill-in pattern. Use them in feeds, video titles, hooks, email subject lines, and challenge/event names. The bracketed slots are yours to fill from the receiver's vocabulary — never your jargon.

1. **The Identity Name** — *"If you're [identity they already claim], here's what nobody tells you about [outcome]."*
   - Names the need by naming *them*. ("If you're a teacher trapped in a job you've outgrown...")

2. **The Belief-Reframe** — *"The real reason you're not [desired outcome] (it's not [the thing they blame])."*
   - Shifts the belief in the title; the body delivers the WHY-most-fail.

3. **The Contrarian Highest-Leverage** — *"[Common advice] won't make you [outcome]. [Reframed skill/move] will."*
   - Omar's own: *"Sales is not the highest paying skill. Teaching is the highest paying skill."* Names the need (to get paid well) and reframes the path.

4. **The What-Before-How** — *"What to do before you [start the thing they want to do]."*
   - The Premiere Pro move generalized. Titles a beginning, not a mastery. ("What to do before you launch your offer.")

5. **The Permission Grant** — *"You're allowed to [do the thing they secretly want] without [the cost they fear]."*
   - Names a need they're afraid to admit. ("You're allowed to build a small business without a sales team.")

6. **The Cost-of-Inaction Name** — *"Why being great at [their skill] is keeping you broke."*
   - Names the felt gap between their competence and their pay — Omar's recurring nerve: *"there's a lot of people who are really good at what you do and you're underpaid."*

7. **The When-Not Negation** — *"When NOT to [common move they're tempted to make]."*
   - Pure WHAT-not-HOW packaging; the negation reads as rare, trustworthy clarity.

8. **The One-Thing Promise** — *"The one thing that changes [outcome] (stop doing everything else)."*
   - Enforces the boil-it-down-to-one discipline in the title itself, pre-selling the anti-firehose feeling.

**The packaging note (read with Tool 3).** Several of these can — and should — be titled in *"how to"* form to capture the felt-need pull: *"Here's how to become a millionaire,"* *"Here's how to buy a home,"* *"Here's how to save on taxes."* But the body still delivers the WHAT, not the HOW. *"You could teach how to, but you're just packaging it in how to."* Title to name the need; deliver to shift the belief; reserve the actual HOW for the paid container. Title-in-HOW, deliver-WHAT — that is the line that lets you give generously in free/transient context without ever confusing gatekeeping with context-calibration.

## How the Tools Chain

These five are one system, not five tactics. Tool 1 sets the objective by context. Tool 5 writes the title that names the felt need for that context. Tool 3 keeps the body in WHAT (not HOW) so the free asset empowers rather than overwhelms. Tool 2 audits the result against the firehose. Tool 4 keeps you designing for the receptive soil and gates your own student-quality first. Run in order, every asset does the one job that matters: *create the feeling in a receptive person to take exactly one next step.* The teaching is the selling. No sales team required.
