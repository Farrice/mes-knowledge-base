# Godin Remarkability Engine

> **Expert**: Seth Godin | **Skill**: seth-godin-brand | **Tier**: Foundation
> **Produces**: Remarkability System Design with structural word-of-mouth
> **Slash Command**: `/godin-remarkability-engine`

---

## Purpose

Engineer remarkability into your product, service, or content so that word-of-mouth is STRUCTURAL — not hopeful. Move from "I hope people talk about us" to "it's impossible NOT to talk about us."

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Product/Service/Content** — What are we making remarkable?
2. **Target Audience** — Who specifically would talk about this?
3. **Current State** — How do people currently describe you? (If the answer is "they don't" — that's the problem.)

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: The "Anyone" Diagnostic

Godin's anti-pattern: "You can pick anyone and we're anyone."

**Test**: If your business disappeared tomorrow, would anyone notice beyond your current customers?
- YES → Remarkability exists somewhere. Find it and amplify.
- NO → You're "anyone." Proceed urgently.

### Step 2: The Carmine's Blueprint

Apply Godin's three-mechanism framework:

#### Mechanism 1: Sensory/Experiential Overload
> What element of your offering is so EXCESSIVE that it demands a story?

Carmine's used garlic — unreasonable quantities. Not "good garlic bread" but "more garlic than it was reasonable to eat."

- What could you make unreasonably generous?
- What could you make unreasonably fast?
- What could you make unreasonably personal?
- What could you make unreasonably beautiful?

**Rule**: The word "unreasonably" is doing the work. Reasonable = forgettable.

#### Mechanism 2: Sharing Architecture
> What structural element makes this a shared experience?

Carmine's used portions 2x normal, meant for sharing. Not "you can share if you want" but "this is DESIGNED for groups."

- How is the product/service/content designed for more than one person?
- What element requires or rewards bringing others in?
- Where does social context amplify the experience?

#### Mechanism 3: Built-in Recruitment
> What dynamic makes one customer bring more?

Carmine's required parties of 6+ for reservations. One person discovering the restaurant = five more people coming.

- What makes a customer need to recruit others to use/enjoy this?
- What referral dynamic is BUILT INTO the experience (not bolted on)?
- What social proof accumulates automatically?

### Step 3: The Next-Day Test

> When your customer goes to work the next day, what MUST they say about you?

Carmine's customers HAD to talk because:
- It was a party (social event worth mentioning)
- They smelled like garlic (physical evidence)
- They had an insane food story (narrative payload)

Design your three "had to talk" elements:
1. **Social proof element** — What makes this an EVENT?
2. **Physical/tangible evidence** — What artifact persists?
3. **Narrative payload** — What's the STORY they tell?

### Step 4: Status/Affiliation Engine

Godin: "They will talk about something that raises their status or gives them affiliation or insider status."

Map each remarkability mechanism to psychological currency:

| Mechanism | Raises Status? | Grants Affiliation? | Creates Insider Status? |
|-----------|---------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Sensory Overload | | | |
| Sharing Architecture | | | |
| Built-in Recruitment | | | |

If none of the three grant at least one psychological currency → the mechanism is gimmicky, not remarkable.

```

- Design: [how sharing is structural]

- Dynamic: [how one customer brings more]

- Social proof: [why it's an event]
- Physical evidence: [what persists]
- Narrative payload: [the story they tell]

- Status: [how it raises the teller's status]
- Affiliation: [what group they join by sharing]
- Insider: [what exclusive knowledge they now have]
```

## Quality Gate

| Dimension | Minimum Standard |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Mechanism Count | All 3 Carmine's mechanisms addressed |
| Unreasonable Element | At least 1 element that's genuinely excessive |
| Built-in Recruitment | Structural, not "refer a friend" bolted on |
| Next-Day Test | Customer MUST talk, not MIGHT talk |
