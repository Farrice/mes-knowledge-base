# Godin False Proxy Purge

> **Expert**: Seth Godin | **Skill**: seth-godin-brand | **Tier**: Practitioner
> **Produces**: Metrics Diagnostic + "Two Numbers on the Wall" Replacement
> **Slash Command**: `/godin-false-proxy-purge`

---

## Purpose

Identify every false proxy your organization is optimizing for — metrics that are easy to measure but actively harmful. Replace them with the two numbers that actually matter, displayed where everyone can see them.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Business/Project** — What are we auditing?
2. **Current Metrics** — What numbers are you tracking today?
3. **Meeting Cadence** — What gets discussed in recurring meetings?
4. **Team Size** — How many people see these numbers?

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: The False Proxy Inventory

Godin's definition: A false proxy is "something that's easy to measure but not helpful." You keep measuring it and go in the wrong direction.

**For each metric you currently track, ask**:

| Metric | Easy to Measure? | Connected to Promise? | Does Optimizing It Serve Customers? | Verdict |
|--------|-----------------|----------------------|-------------------------------------|---------|
| | | | | ✅ Real / ❌ False Proxy |

**Godin's Hall of Shame**:
- Instagram followers (400K → 12 book sales)
- Stock price (3,000 people watching → wrecked Yahoo)
- Quarterly revenue (forces promise-breaking behavior)
- Email blast volume (costs more than stolen laptops)
- Social media engagement (Wendy's: 15-20 people, zero Frostys sold)

### Step 2: The Zuckerberg Test

> "Mark Zuckerberg really wants you to focus on certain numbers. Those numbers aren't important to you. They're important to him. Don't be an unpaid doobie for them."

For each platform metric you track:
- Who benefits when this number goes up — YOU or the PLATFORM?
- Is this number connected to revenue, trust, or transformation?
- Are you an unpaid employee of [platform] by optimizing this?

### Step 3: The Meeting Audit

> "What actually happens in meetings? What are we keeping score of? What question do we ask?"

Record the first question asked in your last 5 meetings or check-ins:

| Meeting | First Question Asked | False Proxy? |
|---------|---------------------|-------------|
| | | |

If the first question is about followers, views, traffic, or revenue — and NOT about customer transformation, trust, or promise-keeping — you're running on false proxies.

### Step 4: The Commotion-Trust-Action Gap

Godin's hidden principle: Most businesses create commotion, then expect action. The missing step is trust.

**Map your current funnel**:

```
COMMOTION: [What you do to get attention]
    ↓
TRUST: [What you do to build trust] ← IS THIS STEP EMPTY?
    ↓
ACTION: [What you want people to do]
```

If the Trust step is empty or weak, your false proxies are probably commotion metrics (views, followers, impressions) masquerading as success metrics.

### Step 5: The Two Numbers Protocol

> Godin's prescription: "Put two numbers on the wall where everyone can see them when they walk in."

Design YOUR two numbers. Criteria:
1. **Number 1**: How many people CHOSE to hear from you (permission earned)
2. **Number 2**: What percentage of them ACTED on the last thing you sent (engagement quality)

**Godin's originals**:
- How many people subscribed to hear from us?
- What percentage opened the last email we sent?

**Adapt to your business**:

| Business Type | Number 1 (Permission) | Number 2 (Action Quality) |
|---------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Service business | Active clients | Client retention rate |
| Content creator | Email subscribers | Reply/save rate |
| Product company | Repeat customers | Net Promoter Score |
| Coaching | Enrolled clients | Completion/transformation rate |
