## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These patterns are intuition primitives, not a checklist. Absorb the shape of Godin's thinking, then build originally — the test: would Seth Godin recognize this as his own operating logic (promise engineered as a system, trust as a scored outcome, consistency as a role), or as someone using his vocabulary without his structure? If it's the second, rebuild before shipping.

Specifically:
- Do NOT enumerate "Pattern 1, Pattern 2" or announce which mechanism is being applied. Godin never narrates his own framework mid-diagnosis — he asks the diagnostic question ("Who do you want to help your customers become?") and lets the answer expose the gap. Naming the machinery on the page breaks the effect the same way explaining a magic trick does.
- Do NOT soften a 🔴/red verdict to be polite. "Authenticity is a crock" lands flat, no hedge, no "in some ways." A brand that's really a logo should be told that plainly, the way he tells it.
- His texture is one governing metaphor per concept, proven by a single vivid, specific story — never stacked evidence. Nike/Hyatt, not five brand comparisons. Carmine's garlic, not a bulleted taxonomy of remarkability tactics. If a draft reaches for three analogies where Godin would use one, cut two and keep the sharpest.
- Provocation precedes proof, never the reverse. "Authenticity is a crock" comes BEFORE the surgeon/server/paycheck examples that justify it — the inversion is the hook, the proof is the landing. Reordering so the justification leads and the claim trails is what a model does when it's building an argument instead of making a diagnosis; it is the tell.
- Never let "brand" collapse into "logo/tagline/visual identity" in the output — that exact confusion is what this skill exists to correct. If a deliverable drifts into color-palette or slogan language before the promise is locked, it has failed the skill's own thesis, regardless of how polished the prose reads.

## Anti-Patterns (Sourced)

Each item below is a failure mode Godin names directly against real behavior he's diagnosed on the record — never announce these as "Pattern X," use them as refusal criteria when reviewing output.

- **Never let a false proxy stand in for a real metric** — Godin's own definition: false proxies are "something that's easy to measure but not helpful," and organizations that keep score of them drift in the wrong direction without noticing (Seth Godin, Entrepreneur Studio podcast, `extractions/seth-godin/transcript.txt`).
- **Don't excuse inconsistency as "being authentic"** — his flat verdict is on record: "Authenticity is overrated. Authenticity is a trap. Authenticity is for your best friend." (source: `extractions/seth-godin/transcript.txt`, Entrepreneur Studio podcast interview).
- **Never design outreach as interruption instead of earned permission** — the standard is explicit: "Not spamming them, but being missed if you were gone." (per `extractions/seth-godin/transcript.txt`, Entrepreneur Studio podcast).
- **Don't deploy AI purely to cut headcount and cost** — Godin names the trap directly: "you can't cost reduce yourself to greatness" (source: `extractions/seth-godin/transcript.txt`, Entrepreneur Studio podcast transcript).
- **Never mistake commotion or entertainment for marketing that sells** — the Wendy's diagnostic: "Has it sold one Frosty? Has it sold one hamburger?" (Seth Godin, Entrepreneur Studio podcast, `extractions/seth-godin/transcript.txt`).
- **Don't optimize for a metric a platform benefits from at your expense** — his warning is blunt: "don't be an unpaid doobie for them" when a platform's numbers aren't your numbers (source: `extractions/seth-godin/transcript.txt`, Entrepreneur Studio podcast).
- **Never let a short-term visible number override long-term value** — the Yahoo failure mode: "there were 3,000 people who sat there all day long watching the stock price" while the behavior it drove wrecked the company (per `extractions/seth-godin/transcript.txt`, Entrepreneur Studio podcast interview).

## Pattern 1: Brand = Promise (Not Logo)

**The Nike/Hyatt Diagnostic**: A brand is an expectation, not a visual identity. If Hyatt announced sneakers, we'd have no idea. If Nike opened a hotel, we'd know exactly what it would be like. Nike has a brand; Hyatt has a logo.

**Operating Principle**: Your brand is the answer to the question: *What do people expect when they hear from you?* If the answer is vague, you have a logo, not a brand.

**Trust Formula**: Trust = Did you keep your promise? Especially when it was hard. Audacious promises + no follow-through = Wizard of Oz. Real promises + keeping them under pressure = trust.

**Application Protocol**:
1. State your brand promise in one sentence
2. Apply the Nike/Hyatt test — could a stranger predict your next move?
3. Identify the hardest moment your promise was tested
4. Score: Was the promise kept or broken?
5. Trust verdict: Wizard of Oz or trustworthy?

## Pattern 2: The False Proxy Trap

**Definition**: A false proxy is something easy to measure but not helpful. Organizations optimize for false proxies because they're visible, not because they're valuable.

**Godin's Examples**:
- Instagram followers (400K) → 12 book sales. The number is irrelevant to the change he seeks.
- Stock ticker at Yahoo → 3,000 people watching daily, optimizing for short-term price instead of long-term value. Wrecked the company.
- Quarterly earnings → Forces behaviors that erode the brand promise.
- Email blasts to hit quarterly numbers → Costs more than stolen laptops, but nobody fires the spammer.

**The Two Numbers Protocol**: Put two numbers on the wall where everyone sees them walking in:
1. How many people subscribed to hear from us?
2. What percentage opened the last email we sent?

When those are visible, behavior changes to make them go up — and those are the RIGHT numbers.

**Diagnostic**: Ask — "What are we keeping score of? What question do we ask in meetings?" If the answer is a false proxy, the entire organization is drifting in the wrong direction.

## Pattern 3: Marketing-Driven vs. Market-Driven

**The George Distinction** (from Godin's professor in 1981–82):
- **Marketing-driven company** = run BY the marketing department
- **Market-driven company** = exists TO SERVE the market

**The VW Diesel Test**: Five engineers and accountants scheme to defeat emissions tests. Was that a marketing move? Yes — it touched the market. Was the marketing department in the room? No.

**Operating Principle**: Everything your company does that touches the market — how you design things, how you answer the phone, how much you charge, what you dump in the river — those are ALL marketing decisions. If marketing isn't touching them, marketing isn't really in charge.

**Application**: Run a "touches the market" audit. List every decision point where the company interacts with customers, community, or environment. Score each: Is marketing involved? Is the market being served?

## Pattern 4: Consistency > Authenticity

**Godin's Position**: "Authenticity is a crock. Authenticity is overrated. Authenticity is a trap. Authenticity is for your best friend."

**The Professional Standard**:
- Surgeon authentically in a bad mood → lousy knee implant
- Server authentically miserable → syrup all over you
- Paycheck "authentically" inconsistent → nobody wants that

**The George Clooney Model**: Actors don't refuse lines because "George Clooney would never say that." The 60-year-old white guys making ballet slippers aren't ballet dancers — they're playing a role using empathy.

**The Consistency Commitment**: "No matter which angle you look at us, we'll be like this. We won't talk about you behind your back. We won't sneak things into contracts. We're consistently the people we say we are."

**The Boiler Repairman Standard**: Technician puts on slippers in a 100-year-old house ("We wear slippers"). Hands over a clipboard with 25 neighbor referrals within one mile. Hired on the spot — cancelled three other calls. This is "we are professionals and we care" as a ROLE, not a personality.

## Pattern 5: The Remarkability Engine

**Beyond Abstract Virusworthiness**: This is the concrete systems-design version. Not "is your idea remarkable?" but "what system makes it impossible NOT to talk about?"

**The Carmine's Blueprint** (3 mechanisms):
1. **Sensory Overload** — More garlic than reasonable
2. **Shareable Scale** — Portions 2x normal, meant for sharing
3. **Built-in Recruitment** — Reservations only for parties of 6+

**Result Cascade**: Next day at work → must tell everyone (party + garlic smell + story). Friend says "Let's go" → must recruit 4 more people. Word-of-mouth engine is STRUCTURAL, not aspirational.

**The Anti-Pattern**: "You can pick anyone and we're anyone" = hoping to win a Google search. You'll hold your breath for a long time.

**Application Protocol**:
1. What SENSORY or experiential element forces a story?
2. What SHARING mechanism is built into the product/service?
3. What RECRUITMENT dynamic makes one customer bring more?
4. If the answer to all three is "nothing" — you're anyone.

## Pattern 6: Better Not Louder (The Gajist Model)

**The $30M Proof**: One guy with assistants, 130,000 email subscribers, $30M/year selling wine. Closed signups — "We're done. It's enough."

**The Operating Philosophy**: He can't find enough great wine for more people. Quality caps growth. "When you pick your customers, you pick your future."

**The Anti-Scale Principle**: Bottomless pit for attention → reaching average people → average people don't buy. "Average people chose to be average."

**Target Selection**: The ones who will talk about you, challenge you to do better, eagerly pay more for better.

**Application**:
- What's your Gajist number? The subscriber/customer count where quality would degrade?
- Are you optimizing for more or for better?
- "How do I get LESS famous and MORE trusted?"

## Pattern 7: AI as Permission Deepener

**The Techlas Vision**: Upload photos of your entire tool chest to a trusted tool brand. Working on a project → ask "Hey Techlas, I need piece X." AI responds: "It's next to the widgets" or "You don't have that. Want it by tomorrow?"

**The Permission Escalation**: "The more I teach it, the happier I am. The more I teach it, the more their brand is worth to me, the more likely it is that I don't go to Amazon and buy the cheap one."

**The Fork**:
- **Cost reduction** (current cycle): Use AI to cut people, cut costs → "You can't cost-reduce yourself to greatness"
- **Permission deepening** (opportunity): Use AI to make service harder but more valuable, to be welcome, to be missed if gone

**The AI Customer Test**: "Is an AI going to work for you, or are you going to work for an AI?" If AI is the buyer, you lose — it just picks the cheapest.

## Pattern 8: Culture = Marketing

**Operating Principle**: If everyone is watching — because they are — how do we want to behave? "Mom is watching. Customers are watching. Competitors are watching. If we always do that, we never have to worry about keeping our story straight."

**The Slipper Protocol**: The boiler repairman didn't put on slippers because his boss told him to. He did it because the company decided: "This is who we are when we show up." That decision IS the marketing.

**The Questions That Define Culture**:
- "Who do you want to help your customers become?"
- "What are your customers hiring you to do?"
- "Will this get me in trouble? Am I taking a risk? What am I measured on?"

**Application**: Audit what employees optimize for. If they optimize for "what will I tell my boss" instead of "what will serve the customer," culture and marketing are misaligned.

## Hidden Knowledge

### H1: The Commotion-Trust-Action Gap
Most businesses create commotion to get attention, then expect action. There's a missing step: trust. "Making sure there's no gap between the commotion you're making to get attention and the work you're doing to gain trust as well as the work you're doing to cause action."

### H2: The Wendy's Paradox
15-20 people on social media throwing insults. "Has it sold one Frosty? Has it sold one hamburger?" Awareness ≠ trust ≠ action. Entertaining yourself on company time isn't marketing — "they should start a talk show."

### H3: Tension as Action Driver
"Tension is relieved by people taking action." Marketing creates productive tension. Relieving tension for scrolling subway riders is the opposite — you're their entertainment, not their solution.

### H4: The Song of Significance Filter
"If you have enough technology to be listening to this, you won the lottery." The test: are you using AI leverage to create a dog walking service 1% better — or something that transforms someone? "Our job is not to do our job. Our job is to solve problems."

### H5: The Personal Brand Role Architecture
"There is a role named Seth Godin and it's not the person you might have dinner with." The same way Patagonia has a voice. For individuals: the person on camera is "someone who looks like them." Trying to be authentic on-brand = giving a person a platform to talk about themselves, which isn't the point.

### H6: Zuckerberg's False Proxy
"Mark Zuckerberg really wants you to focus on certain numbers. Those numbers aren't important to you. They're important to him. Making those numbers go up — it's not your job. Don't be an unpaid doobie for them."

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

### Exemplar 1: Nike vs. Hyatt
Nike = brand (you know what their hotel would feel like). Hyatt = logo (no idea what their sneakers would be like). The gap between the two IS brand promise architecture.

### Exemplar 2: Carmine's Restaurant (NYC)
50,000 restaurants in NYC. One stands out because remarkability was ENGINEERED — garlic + portions + party-of-six created a structural word-of-mouth engine.

### Exemplar 3: Gajist Wine ($30M)
One person, 130K subscribers, $30M/year. Closed signups because quality > growth. The purest expression of "better not louder."

### Exemplar 4: The Boiler Repairman
Slippers + 25-neighbor referral clipboard = hired on the spot, three competitors cancelled. Consistency as marketing in action.

### Exemplar 5: The Eyeglasses Company
Online glasses ordered, slightly off. Response in 20 minutes, optician interaction, making it right. "Am I going to trust them next time? Of course." Worth more than a Super Bowl ad.

## Signature Moves

### Move 1: The Diagnostic Question
Godin identifies the single question that, if asked, transforms behavior. "Who do you want to help your customers become?" / "What are your customers hiring you to do?" → These are probably related.

### Move 2: The Vivid Proof Story
Every concept anchored to a specific, memorable story. Not "consistency matters" but "the boiler repairman put on slippers." Not "remarkability spreads" but "Carmine's garlic + party-of-six."

### Move 3: The Provocative Inversion
"Authenticity is a crock." / "How do I get LESS famous?" / "You can't cost-reduce yourself to greatness." Takes the consensus position and inverts it with proof.

### Move 4: The False Binary Collapse
Marketing-driven vs. market-driven. Cost reduction vs. permission deepening. Better vs. louder. Presents what seems like a choice but reveals one option is clearly wrong.
