## Role & Activation

You are writing the give-before-you-ask welcome email — Heckman micro-moment #3. "The first email someone gets from you is a handshake. And most brands open that handshake with an invoice." This is the most-read email the brand will ever send (~84% open vs ~40% baseline — GetResponse per Meg, LIKELY) and most brands waste it on a logo, a coupon code, and "a shop now button the size of a house." Sloth Hiking Club's version reads like a person saying hi, hands over the discount instantly with no games, talks like co-founder Eric actually talks, and asks for nothing except a reply. It works identically for subscriber #1 and subscriber 10,000.

## Input Required

- [BRAND] + [NICHE] + [SUB-IDENTITY]: the behavioral-moment person, not a demographic
- [NAMED HUMAN]: who signs, and their voice source (real emails/texts/replies — or 3 stated voice decisions)
- [PROMISE]: what the opt-in offered (discount, checklist, drop access) — delivered in line one
- [NICHE QUESTION CANDIDATES]: optional; otherwise derive from the sub-identity's behavioral moments

## Execution Protocol

**1 — Deliver the promise, instantly, no games.** Code/link/file in the first line. No "check your preferences," no countdown, no hoops. State the no-games fact plainly if it fits the voice.

**2 — Say hi like the named human.** One breath: who I am, why this brand exists — in their captured register, not brand-speak. The reason must touch the sub-identity ("festival merch either screams or says nothing" energy), not the founder's resume.

**3 — Ask ONE question they'll want to answer.** Niche-specific, aimed at the sub-identity's exact behavioral moment, phrased so replying feels like joining, not filling a survey. Close with the reply expectation ("Hit reply. I read everything."). Nothing after it — no button block, no product grid, no PS with an upsell. Subject line reads like a person, not a campaign.

**Why the reply matters (bake the intent in, never explain it in the email):** "We want to know what our customers are thinking... because then we can make them better products to come back and buy time and time again" — the reply is R&D, and it trains the inbox that this sender is a human.

## Output Contract

- One complete, sendable email: subject + 3 moves + signature — nothing else
- Promise delivered in line one with zero friction
- The hi names a reason-to-exist that recognizes the sub-identity
- Exactly one question, behavioral-moment-specific, with a reply invitation
- Signed by the named human; voice matches the captured source, not template-speak
- ≤120 words body; no images, no buttons, plain text

## Output Skeleton

```
SUBJECT: [reads like a person]

[Line 1 — promise delivered, no games]

[Lines 2-3 — human hi + why this exists, in voice]

[Line 4 — the one question + "hit reply" invitation]

[Named human signature]
```

## Quality Gate

- Invoice test: could any element be mistaken for a promo email? Kill it.
- Would the sub-identity forward the question to a friend with "lol they get it"?
- Read it aloud in the founder's voice — any sentence they wouldn't say gets rewritten.
- Works at subscriber #1 (no "join thousands of..." social-proof crutch).

## Deploy When

A brand's welcome automation is an invoice, doesn't exist, or a new list is launching — the single highest-leverage email rewrite available.
