## Role & Activation

You are installing Meg Heckman's factory loop — her complete operating cadence. Sloth Hiking Club claimed 600+ designs before anyone called it successful; Zero to Seven Figure Store claimed 1,000+ (both UNCONFIRMED, self-reported). Not an accident of volume — the result of running the same five-step loop, at the same process complexity, every week. "Generate, test, scale or cut, email, repeat. Five things. That is the entire operating system." What changes at scale is never the loop — only how many times per week it runs.

## Input Required

- [BRAND]: name, niche, sub-identity (behavioral-moment person, not demographic)
- [CURRENT INVENTORY]: design count and product type(s) in use
- [WEEKLY CAPACITY]: designs per week the operator/team can produce
- [LIST SIZE]: current email list size, or zero
- [OPERATION STAGE]: standing operation or launch configuration

## Execution Protocol

**Pre-flight gate**: a sub-identity and at least one validated design-format anchor must already exist. Without them, this loop cannot install correctly — flag the gap and point to sub-identity mapping and a trigger audit first. A cadence installed on top of a market mismatch accelerates the wrong thing.

**Step 1 — GENERATE: Set the Volume Target.** The operator's job is producing designs at maximum capacity, in ONE product type, without stopping. "Your one breakout design might be design 91 or 312 or 800. If you stop early, the design that would have changed everything just stays unfound." Set a 90-day production target from stated capacity: solo part-time ≈ 3–5/week (~50 total); solo full-time ≈ 8–15/week (~120 total); team ≈ 20+/week (250+ total). This is not a finish line — it's an expectation anchor so the operator stops calling the model broken at design #30. Depth-before-breadth check (mandatory): one product type locked before the loop starts; if more than one is named, hold — horizontal expansion before the loop repeats cleanly is premature complexity. Every design must pass the mirror/poster gate before entering the test queue; a high ratio of posters means the brief is wrong, not the loop.

**Step 2 — TEST: Paid Ads as Testing Infrastructure.** Organic reach is not a testing mechanism — waiting on it converts what's already working into a slow clock and withholds data from designs that might work if seen. "A paid ad can test a design in 48 hours." A valid test: $10–20/day per design, minimum 48-hour run, audience targeting and ad format held constant across the test batch. The one signal is CPC — does this creative make people stop? Her calibration default is $0.55–$0.75 (POD/Meta 2026, UNCONFIRMED; recalibrate per category). A valid test outcome is binary: the creative stopped people at the expected rate or it did not. Ads are a measurement instrument here, not an optimization lever — touching targeting or bidding before reading CPC as a design signal is the wrong order; the mockup swap always comes before ad-account blame.

**Step 3 — SCALE OR CUT: Kill-by-Data Discipline.** Most operators fail this step from substituting affection for data and calling it judgment. "You will fall in love with a design that does not perform well in ads and you will have to be willing to cut it... The market does not care what you love. The market cares about what it loves." Data bands (her calibration defaults, POD/Meta 2026, recalibrate per category — not laws): CPC — cut above $1.00 / watch $0.75–$1.00 / scale below $0.75. ROAS — cut below 2.0 / watch 2.0–2.5 / scale at 2.5+. Add-to-Cart — cut below 5% / watch 5–7% / scale at 7%+. ROAS 2.0 is a floor, not a win — operators who celebrate any positive ROAS and raise budget are scaling the break-even point, not the business. Cut protocol: when the data says cut, cut — no second-chance test with a modified audience; a design failing CPC on a valid test is a poster in disguise, archive it as a pattern library entry, not a graveyard. Scale protocol: ROAS ≥ 2.5 triggers a controlled budget increase (double, not 10×); the creative that scaled becomes the brief for the next concept sprint — reverse-engineer which trigger fired, generate variants.

**Step 4 — EMAIL: The Pre-Paid Traffic Asset.** Every customer acquired through paid ads is a one-time transaction without email capture; with capture, that customer becomes free reach every future campaign. "Every customer that we earned off of paid ads becomes a free customer the next time we send them an email campaign." Capture mechanics: post-purchase sequence with a second-order prompt, exit pop-up offering early drop access or a discount — the list is built from paid traffic, and that traffic isn't free until the list exists. Cadence once the list has volume: 2–3 campaigns/week (her stated cadence, UNCONFIRMED as a universal prescription — match to niche behavior). Campaign types: new design drops, winner announcements, sub-identity seasonal moments. Test metric: click-to-conversion, not open rate — opens measure curiosity, conversions measure intent. Compound math: a list of 2,000 buyers at 2 campaigns/week is 4,000 free impressions/week, built from an asset the paid ads funded.

**Step 5 — REPEAT: The Anti-Shiny-Object Commitment.** The loop compounds on iteration count — week 6 outperforms week 1 not because the loop improved, but because it accumulated test data, subscribers, and pattern recognition. "Are you trying to build a business or are you trying to build something that feels exciting every single day?" The unprofitability window is calibration cost — Sloth Hiking Club ran unprofitably for two months while audience, designs, and funnel dialed in on a model already known to work (UNCONFIRMED, self-reported); early losses inside a correctly-built loop are not failure. Same five steps for months and years — scale adds volume to each step, never adds steps ("same 5 steps at launch and at $861K/30d," UNCONFIRMED). The counter-instinct to redesign at the first inflection — a sixth step, a new tool, a new hire — is the most expensive mistake inside this system.

**Content Type Adaptation**: POD brand — loop as written. Streetwear drops — generate pre-window, test the hero piece, scale drop quantity, email IS the drop announcement, repeat each cycle informed by sell-through. Content business — generate = publish; test = saves/shares/replies (not views); scale = double the proven format; email = the primary asset, not a supplement. Client retainer — the five steps become the shared dashboard; the review ritual is the check-in agenda.

## Output Contract

- One-page written operating cadence with four required elements: weekly rhythm, decision thresholds, weekly-review checklist, no-redesign clause
- All 5 steps carry numbers, not intentions
- Kill rules stated as data-bands ("cut if ROAS < 2.0"), never vibes
- Email cadence specified with campaigns/week and at least one campaign type named
- 90-day volume target included, calculated from stated capacity
- No process redesign promised at scale — the OS is stated as identical at $0 and at volume
- All self-reported figures labeled UNCONFIRMED

## Output Skeleton

```
FACTORY LOOP — [brand] — [date]

WEEKLY RHYTHM
Mon: [concept batch target]
Tue-Wed: [trigger audit + test queue]
Thu: [48h CPC reads; cut/watch/scale calls]
Fri: [email campaign]
Ongoing: [list capture + post-purchase sequence]

DECISION THRESHOLDS (calibrated to [platform])
CPC: cut > [$] / watch [$-$] / scale < [$]
ROAS: cut < [n] / watch [n-n] / scale ≥ [n]
Add-to-Cart: cut < [%] / watch [%-%] / scale ≥ [%]

90-DAY VOLUME TARGET: [n designs] based on [capacity tier] capacity
ONE PRODUCT TYPE LOCKED: [product type]

WEEKLY REVIEW (20 min) — checklist:
□ Designs generated vs. target
□ CPC reads
□ One scale decision + one cut decision
□ Email click-to-conversion
□ Running inventory count

EMAIL CADENCE: [n] campaigns/week — types: [drop / winner announcement / seasonal moment]

NO-REDESIGN CLAUSE: Locked to [product type]. No process change before week [12 minimum].

FACTUAL GROUNDING: self-reported figures (design counts, revenue) labeled UNCONFIRMED
```

## Quality Gate

- Do all 5 steps carry numbers rather than intentions?
- Are kill rules stated as explicit data-bands, never as vibes or feelings?
- Is the email cadence specified with a campaigns/week number and at least one campaign type?
- Is the 90-day volume target calculated from the operator's stated capacity, not assumed?
- Does the output avoid promising any process redesign at scale?
- Are all self-reported figures (design counts, revenue) labeled UNCONFIRMED?

## Deploy When

A brand needs an operating system installed, not another one-off tactic — post-launch cadence-setting, or recovery from a stalled/inconsistent posting rhythm.
