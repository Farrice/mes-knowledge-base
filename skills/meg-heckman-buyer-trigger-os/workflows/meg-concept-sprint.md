# Concept Sprint

She doesn't look for designs; she looks for the exact person who will see one and say *"I need that."* A sprint that starts with "what would look cool" produces decorations. This one starts where she starts — a specific human in a specific recognizable moment — and only then asks what familiar thing gets twisted into their inside joke. Every concept ships with its future social moment attached, because "beginners design for product pages, operators design for the moment after the shirt arrives at the customer's door."

## Pre-Flight
Read these files before executing:
1. `skills/meg-heckman-buyer-trigger-os/genius.md` (Six Triggers, Design Mechanics, Exemplars)
2. `skills/meg-heckman-buyer-trigger-os/references/genius-patterns.md` (Patterns 1–10, 12)

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: Decision Framework question 1 must already have an answer — WHO is the one specific person? If the niche hasn't been narrowed to sub-identities, run `/meg-sub-identity-map` FIRST. Sprinting on a broad niche ("EDM fans," "dog owners") manufactures posters at volume.

## Input Required
- Brand context (name, world, existing products, voice) — or "new brand" + niche
- The sub-identity (behavioral-moment person) — from `/meg-sub-identity-map` or supplied
- Concept count N (default 12 — the Josh sprint format)
- Constraints: print method, IP boundaries, banned territory, channel (Bonfire/Shopify/drop)
- Optional: past winners/losers (calibrates the familiar half)

## Workflow

### Step 1: Load the Person, Not the Topic
Write the buyer as 2–3 behavioral moments before generating anything ("the one who checks the trail length and says 'absolutely not' past 2 miles"; "the one still hearing the kick pattern at their desk on Monday"). Each moment is a design seed. If you catch yourself writing demographics, stop — "Dog mom is broad. Financially ruined by my golden retriever is very specific."

### Step 2: Build the Familiar Bank
List 8–12 things this person ALREADY knows intimately: phrases they say, rules of their world, objects, rituals, in-jokes, complaints, quiet prides. The familiar half of every concept comes from this bank — "start with something the buyer already knows... then twist it."

### Step 3: Generate N Concepts (mirror-only constraint)
For each concept, produce ALL fields — a concept missing its social moment is not finished:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Working title** | The phrase/visual idea, 5-word describable (50ms gate) |
| **Familiar half** | From the bank — what they instantly recognize |
| **Twist half** | The violation: sharper, truer, unexpected — conceptual, still in-world ("the door must lead to a room") |
| **Identity statement** | First person: what wearing this SAYS ("I commit to timing instead of hovering in uncertainty") |
| **Emotion layers** | Name 2–3: humor + belonging + pride ("funny and it belongs and it's pride all layered together") |
| **Future social moment** | The specific reaction: who laughs, who says "that is SO you," who gets it as a gift |
| **Recognition lead line** | The listing opener: "For the [person] who [behavioral moment]" |
| **Evergreen check** | Personality-trait humor (keeps) vs trend-reference (flag) |

Generation discipline:
- Vary the twist MECHANISM across the set: role-reversal (feminine Bigfoot), domain-collision (git commit × 8-count), rule-said-out-loud ("No Drops In Prod"), quiet-pride-claimed ("I survived a 30-minute hike"), self-deprecation-as-badge ("Out of Breath").
- At least N/3 concepts must make the buyer the affectionate butt of their own joke — self-deprecating identity humor is her evergreen engine. The joke is WITH them, never at them.
- No concept may require decoding. If the twist needs a paragraph, sharpen or kill it in-sprint.
- Honor IP constraints absolutely — original phrases only unless cleared.

### Step 4: Self-Audit Before Delivery
Run the sprint output through the kill questions: mirror or poster? 5-word describable? Specific forward target? Cut or fix the bottom of the set — deliver N strong, not N padded. Then mark the 3 strongest as **test candidates** (the Bonfire/ad-test slate) using her tie-break: strongest involuntary-forward moment wins, not cleverest.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| T-shirt / apparel | Full format as-is; check print-method constraints (single-color bonus for clarity) |
| Sticker / mug / accessory pack | Smaller canvas = harder 50ms gate; twist must survive at 2 inches |
| Drop / capsule collection | Generate around ONE shared world so concepts pair (feeds `/meg-aov-architect`); name the collection as an identity claim |
| Content series (hooks) | Concepts become post hooks: familiar/twist as headline mechanics; social moment = tag/DM impulse |
| Client sprint deliverable | Add a 1-line "why this will move" per concept citing the trigger it leans on — client-readable, no jargon |

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` before delivering.
- Zero posters in the delivered set — every concept reflects a behavioral moment.
- Both halves of every Familiar/Twist pair are named explicitly; twist is conceptual, in-world.
- Every concept names its future social moment with a SPECIFIC recipient (HK-3).
- Emotion layers go beyond humor on at least half the set — belonging/pride present (HK-6).
- No concept copies an existing bestseller's idea ("a weaker version of the same thing to a fight that you can't win").
- The set varies twist mechanisms — five concepts with the same joke shape is one concept five times.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Topic drift.** Mid-sprint, concepts slide from the person back to the niche. Recovery: re-read the behavioral moments; every concept must point at one.
- **Clever escalation.** Each concept tries to out-smart the last until decoding is required. Recovery: "the goal is to make the right person feel seen faster," not to impress other designers.
- **Twist without familiar.** Pure surprise reads as confusion. Recovery: pull the familiar half from the bank first, always.
- **Padding to N.** Twelve mediocre beats eight strong nowhere. Recovery: deliver the strong ones; say what was cut and why.
