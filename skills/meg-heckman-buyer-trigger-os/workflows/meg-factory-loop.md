# The Factory Loop

Sloth Hiking Club had 600+ designs before anyone called it successful. Zero to Seven Figure Store crossed 1,000 (both UNCONFIRMED, self-reported). Not an accident of volume — the result of running the same five-step loop, at the same process complexity, every week. What changes at scale is not the loop. It is how many times per week you run it.

## Workflow

### Step 1: GENERATE — Set the Volume Target

The operator's job: produce designs at maximum capacity, in ONE product type, without stopping.

> "Your one breakout design might be design 91 or 312 or 800. If you stop early, the design that would have changed everything just stays unfound."

Set a 90-day production target from stated capacity. Solo part-time: 3–5/week (~50). Solo full-time: 8–15/week (~120). Team: 20+/week (250+). Not a finish line — an expectation anchor so the operator stops calling the model broken at design #30.

**Depth-before-breadth check (mandatory):** One product type locked before the loop starts. If the operator names more than one, hold. One product until the loop repeats cleanly. Horizontal expansion before that point is premature complexity.

Every design passes the `/meg-trigger-audit` mirror/poster gate before entering the test queue. A high ratio of posters means the brief is wrong, not the loop.

---

### Step 2: TEST — Paid Ads as Testing Infrastructure

Organic reach is not a testing mechanism. Waiting on organic is the reject behavior — it converts what is already working into a slow clock, and it withholds data from designs that might work if they were seen.

**The paid-ads frame:**
> "A paid ad can test a design in 48 hours."

What a valid test looks like: $10–20/day per design, minimum 48-hour run. Audience targeting and ad format held constant across the test batch. The one signal: CPC — does this creative make people stop? Her calibration default is $0.55–$0.75 (POD/Meta 2026, UNCONFIRMED; recalibrate per category). A valid test outcome is binary: the creative stopped people at the expected rate, or it did not.

This step uses ads as a measurement instrument, not an optimization lever. Touching targeting or bidding before reading CPC as a design signal is the wrong order — the mockup swap always comes before ad-account blame.

---

### Step 3: SCALE OR CUT — Kill-by-Data Discipline

Most operators fail this step — not from lack of information but from substituting affection for data and calling it judgment.

**The discipline, verbatim:**
> "You will fall in love with a design that does not perform well in ads and you will have to be willing to cut it... The market does not care what you love. The market cares about what it loves."

**Data bands that trigger the decision (her calibration defaults — POD/Meta 2026; recalibrate per category, not laws):**

CPC: cut above $1.00 / watch $0.75–$1.00 / scale below $0.75. ROAS: cut below 2.0 / watch 2.0–2.5 / scale at 2.5+. Add-to-Cart: cut below 5% / watch 5–7% / scale at 7%+.

**Rule**: ROAS 2.0 is a floor, not a win. Operators who celebrate any positive ROAS and increase budget are scaling the break-even point, not the business.

**Cut protocol:** When the data says cut, cut. No second-chance test with a modified audience. If the design fails CPC on a valid test, it is a poster in disguise — the trigger audit missed it, or the execution diluted the trigger. Archive it. The archive is a pattern library for what the market rejected, not a graveyard.

**Scale protocol:** ROAS ≥ 2.5 triggers budget increase — controlled increments (double, not 10×). The creative that scaled is the brief for the next concept sprint: reverse-engineer which trigger fired, generate variants.

---

### Step 4: EMAIL — The Pre-Paid Traffic Asset

Every customer acquired through paid ads is a one-time transaction if there is no email capture. With capture, that customer becomes a free reach every time a campaign sends.

**The framing:**
> "Every customer that we earned off of paid ads becomes a free customer the next time we send them an email campaign."

**Capture mechanics:** Post-purchase sequence with a second-order prompt. Exit pop-up offering early drop access or a discount. The list is built from paid traffic; that traffic is not free until the list exists.

**Cadence once the list has volume:** 2–3 campaigns per week (her stated cadence, UNCONFIRMED as universal prescription — match to niche behavior). Campaign types: new design drops, winner announcements, sub-identity seasonal moments. Test metric: click-to-conversion, not open rate. Opens measure curiosity; conversions measure intent.

**The compound math:** A list of 2,000 buyers at 2 campaigns/week is 4,000 free impressions per week — from an asset the paid ads built. Without the email layer, every loop iteration starts from zero reach.

---

### Step 5: REPEAT — The Anti-Shiny-Object Commitment

The loop compounds on iteration count. Week 6 is worth more than Week 1 not because the loop improved but because it accumulated test data, email subscribers, and pattern recognition.

**The commitment framing:**
> "Are you trying to build a business or are you trying to build something that feels exciting every single day?"

**The unprofitability window is calibration cost.** Sloth Hiking Club ran unprofitably for two months while the audience, designs, and funnel dialed in — on a model she already knew worked (UNCONFIRMED, self-reported). Early losses inside a correctly-built loop are not failure. They are the cost of calibration.

**Same five steps for months and years.** Scale adds volume to each step; it never adds steps. Her HK-10: "Same 5 steps at launch and at $861K/30d." (UNCONFIRMED.) The counter-instinct to redesign at the first inflection — a sixth step, a new tool, a new hire — is the most expensive mistake inside this system.

## Format Adaptations

**POD brand:** Loop as written. **Streetwear drops:** Generate pre-window, test the hero piece, scale drop quantity, email IS the drop announcement, repeat each cycle informed by sell-through. **Content business:** Generate = publish; Test = saves/shares/replies (not views); Scale = double the proven format; Email = the primary asset, not supplement. **Client retainer:** five steps become the shared dashboard; review ritual is the check-in agenda.

## Quality Gate

- All 5 steps carry numbers, not intentions
- Kill rules are data-bands ("cut if ROAS < 2.0"), not vibes
- Email cadence specified: campaigns/week + at least one campaign type named
- Volume expectations set using her math, 90-day target included
- No process redesign promised at scale (HK-10 — the OS is identical at $0 and at volume)
- All self-reported figures labeled UNCONFIRMED

## Common Pitfalls

- **Cadence collapse after week 2.** The named failure mode: "upload 50 designs then nothing for two months." The loop requires a fixed weekly rhythm. The review ritual is the forcing function — miss it twice and rebuild from a hard restart date.

- **Scaling the loved loser.** ROAS 1.8 with "it just needs more time" is taste overriding data. Cut it. The time and budget belong to what the data is asking to scale.

- **Premature horizontal expansion.** Adding mugs, hoodies, or phone cases before the t-shirt loop repeats cleanly is complexity that fragments attention before the core loop has proven itself.

- **Redesigning at the first growth inflection.** When a winner emerges, the instinct is to build something new. The loop says: generate variants of what just won, run the same test, make the same decision. New complexity earns its place only when the current process has a documented constraint it cannot solve.
