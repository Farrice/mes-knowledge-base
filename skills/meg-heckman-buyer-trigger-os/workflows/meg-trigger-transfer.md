# Trigger Transfer

The offer "we help businesses use AI" is a poster. Nobody buys a poster. They buy a mirror — the surface that reflects their private failure back at them fast enough to create recognition before explanation. "Your AI is producing. Your buyers are not moving." Same psychology. Different medium. The context changes. The psychology does not.

## Pre-Flight

1. `skills/meg-heckman-buyer-trigger-os/genius.md` — Six Triggers, Decision Framework (7 questions), Anti-Patterns
2. `skills/meg-heckman-buyer-trigger-os/references/genius-patterns.md` — Patterns 1–10

> **Gate**: Complete the Decision Framework before touching the asset. If you cannot name the buyer as a behavioral moment — not a demographic — stop. Transferring onto a generic ICP produces generic output.

**Input required**: the asset (offer, headline, landing page hero, email subject line, hook, positioning statement, or service description) · the buyer as behavioral moment · the conversion surface · what taking that action says about the buyer (the identity sentence)

## Workflow

### Step 1: Mirror-or-Poster Gate

A **poster** describes a category ("we help businesses use AI," "lessons from building a business"). A **mirror** reflects a specific person's lived failure or behavioral moment ("what I do when I haven't posted in 3 weeks and feel like a fraud"). Classify before touching anything.

| Asset | "By [action], I'm saying: I ___" | Mirror or Poster? |
|---|---|---|
| (input) | | |

Posters cannot be scored — rewrite first. The gateway question: "What does this let the buyer say about themselves in front of peers?" Nothing = fails before the psychology can work.

**B2B note**: service offers flatten toward posters because buyers fear looking irrational. The mirror in professional contexts is a private frustration stated aloud — specific enough to be wrong (and therefore credible). The twist is how it opens.

### Step 2: Surface Translation Table

The mechanic transfers; the apparel skin does not. "Wearability" on a landing page is not about fabric — it is about whether the buyer would share the claim where peers can see it.

| Trigger | Apparel mechanic | Cross-vertical translation |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity Signal** | What wearing it says about you | What clicking / buying / subscribing / hiring says about who they are — the first-person sentence the buyer wants associated with them in public |
| **Recognition Speed** | Instant "that's me" in 50ms | Above-the-fold single message; first line of a hook; subject line before preview text — the right person recognizes themselves without decoding |
| **Specificity** | Behavioral moment, not demographic | The private failure or micro-situation named ("your AI is producing. Your buyers are not moving") — not a market label, a person you can picture |
| **Social Currency** | Involuntary tag/gift impulse | The involuntary forward — "I need to send this to Sarah." Public sharing of a claim, post, or offer signals what the buyer believes about themselves. Shareability governs non-apparel the way wearability governs clothing. |
| **Familiar/Twist** | Known phrase + violation | Known problem or category (familiar) + unexpected frame, inverted failure, or stated truth nobody says aloud (twist). Both halves must be named. One without the other is either boring or confusing. |
| **Emotion-First** | Feeling before proof; buyer supplies reason | Recognition or relief before features, proof, or logic. The buyer should feel diagnosed before they understand the offer. Logic is what they tell themselves afterward. |

### Step 3: Asset Transformation (All 6 Triggers)

Identify the behavioral moment first — the exact situation the buyer is in when this reaches them. Not their job title. The situation: "They just hit post on a piece of content that got 4 likes and are now in the doubt-loop." That's what the mirror reflects.

**Worked proof from production:**
> Before: "We help businesses use AI."  
> After: "Your AI is producing. Your buyers are not moving."

The before names a service. The after names a private failure — the gap between a capability acquired and a result not delivered. Recognition before explanation. The buyer feels diagnosed before they understand the offer.

Apply the same transformation:

### Step 4: Wearability Substitution

Wearability in apparel = "Would I wear this in public and want to be noticed?" The cross-vertical substitute is **shareability where peers see it**: would they send this to a specific person, reference it to someone they respect, or name this practitioner's method in a room of peers?

"I found this useful" = passes. "I need you to see this" = social currency. Utility brings a reader; identity keeps a subscriber.

### Step 5: Trigger Audit (Lite)

Score the transferred asset. IP Safety and Wearability are replaced for cross-vertical surfaces.

| Criterion | Score (1–5) | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Identity Signal | | First-person sentence the buyer wants associated with them |
| Recognition Speed | | No decode — single message, immediate |
| Specificity | | Behavioral moment named; a person you can picture |
| Social Currency | | Involuntary forward to a SPECIFIC person — name them |
| Familiar/Twist | | Both halves explicit; one missing = max 2 |
| Emotion First | | Feeling precedes proof |

Score from the buyer's seat, cold. "That's nice" = automatic fail. Verdict: avg <3 = rewrite · 3–4 = revise weakest trigger · ≥4, no criterion <3 = ready to test.

## Surface-Specific Adaptations

**Offer / landing page**: One claim loudly above the fold. If the headline names the service AND the benefit AND the ICP in the same sentence, it lands nothing. Mirror-or-poster on the hero headline alone governs whether the rest of the page can convert.

**LinkedIn-X hooks**: The behavioral moment is the entire job of line one. The Familiar/Twist pair stops the scroll. Without the twist, the hook is a topic sentence. Poster relapse at scale: volume pressure pushes specificity toward category language — the result is an account full of posts nobody forwards.

**Email subject lines**: One concept, one interrupt. Subject line + email body operate as the Familiar/Twist pair: the subject is the setup, the email is the room the door leads into. An interrupt that the body cannot pay off buys unsubscribes, not opens.

**Newsletter positioning / sub-identity gap**: The person nobody writes for. The dominant newsletter in any category writes for the advanced practitioner or the curious generalist. The gap is the specific person in between — experienced enough to know what doesn't work, not yet positioned as the expert. The tagline is the identity signal: "subscribing says ___ about me." If it says nothing, the subscriber base will be people who consume everything and act on nothing.

**Service productization**: Decoration frameworks vs. identity — the coach who IS the methodology. "I help you build X system" is a poster. "I work exclusively with operators who plateaued at 7 figures because their hiring reflects their anxiety, not their standards" is a mirror. The buyer hiring the second version is buying an identity claim; the deliverable is the reason they give afterward.

## Quality Gate

- Behavioral moment named, not demographic. "Founders" fails; "the founder who hired a content manager three months ago and still has nothing to show for it" passes.
- Mirror/poster stated on every headline and hook. A poster with a trigger upgrade is still a poster.
- Before/after shown — transformation visible, not described.
- Familiar/Twist: both halves named. Missing half = max score 2.
- Social Currency: specific person named for the involuntary forward.
- One concrete revision directive per weakness — never "make it more emotional."
- Claims labeled: VERIFIED / LIKELY / UNCONFIRMED.

**Anti-pattern lock**:
- **Metaphor-stretching** — translate the MECHANIC, drop the apparel skin.
- **Threshold transfer** — her POD metrics don't apply to digital offers. Psychology transfers; numbers don't.
- **Twist-flattening in B2B** — professional fear of irrationality flattens violations into safe language. A precisely named failure is MORE disarming in B2B, not less. Never sand down the violation before the buyer has had the chance to feel seen.
- **Poster relapse at scale** — volume pressure drifts specificity toward category language. Behavioral moment test on every piece, not just launches.

## Common Pitfalls

- **No buyer re-grounding.** The identity sentence is different per surface. Rerun the Decision Framework before touching copy.
- **Hook ≠ mirror.** Stopping the scroll is not feeling seen. "Did they stop?" fails; "Did they feel seen?" is the test. Engagement without identification is entertainment.
- **Single-trigger upgrades.** One strong trigger surrounded by posters produces a more sophisticated poster. All six must clear the gate.
