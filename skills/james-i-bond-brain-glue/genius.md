## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

STEAM ATTRACTORS is a diagnostic checklist for choosing which lever to reach for — it is not a labeling scheme for the output. If the finished asset reads like "Tribal Alignment: ✓, Trigger Word: ✓," rebuild it. Bond's own worked examples (references/genius-patterns.md's Hall of Fame Exemplars) never name the mechanism inside the copy itself — the demonstration, the reversed line, the one-feature name simply appear, doing their job silently.

Specifically:
- Do NOT enumerate which of the 14 levers you used unless the workflow is explicitly a diagnostic.
- Do NOT title output sections "Tribal Alignment Line" or "Chiasmus Line" for a client-facing asset — ship the line, not the taxonomy.
- The test: would James I. Bond recognize this as a sticky buying idea deployed in the wild — or as someone using framework vocabulary to describe copy that isn't actually sticky yet? If it's the second, the levers were named, not built.
- Bond's own texture is direct, commercial, and a little blunt — closer to a sales trainer diagnosing a broken pitch than an academic naming rhetorical devices. Sand off any line that sounds like it's explaining Brain Glue rather than performing it. Polish that reads as "clever copywriting" instead of "the phrase I'd actually repeat to a friend" is the tell that a workflow drifted into decoration instead of the sticky core Bond insists comes first (references/hidden-knowledge.md: "Brain Glue is a sequencing method. Do not use every tool.").

## Core Genius

James I. Bond's Brain Glue method (the 204-page source book, extraction dated 2026-06-11 — see `references/genius-patterns.md`) treats persuasion as **memory engineering for buying decisions**. The central move is to stop arguing harder and start shaping the prospect's emotional, sensory, and pattern-recognition systems so the message becomes easy to understand, hard to forget, and natural to repeat.

The method is especially useful for entrepreneurs because most offers fail before the proof is evaluated: the buyer does not grasp the idea fast enough, does not feel it, or cannot remember it later. Brain Glue fixes the message at the level of first impression, cognitive simplicity, emotional charge, and verbal stickiness.

## Decision Framework

Before executing any workflow, answer these in order. This sequence mirrors the 14-lever STEAM ATTRACTORS table below, distilled from the 204-page extraction (`references/genius-patterns.md`, dated 2026-06-11):

1. **What must stick?** A product name, core promise, sales argument, hook, metaphor, offer, campaign idea, or behavior change.
2. **Who must repeat it?** Buyer, audience, referral source, salesperson, founder, customer, investor, or team.
3. **What is blocking stickiness?** Confusion, blandness, abstraction, mistrust, wrong tribe, weak sensory image, no surprise, no verbal pattern.
4. **Which Brain Glue lever fits the block?**
   - Confusion: understanding ease, analogy, perspective, demonstration.
   - Blandness: trigger words, odd/unexpected, sensory elevation.
   - Weak recall: rhyme, alliteration, chiasmus, anchor word ownership.
   - Wrong audience feel: tribal alignment, rejection to attract.
   - Low trust: expectation setting, honest admission, demonstration.
   - Low desire: metaphor, sensory benefit, humor, oxytocin, social identity.
5. **Can the phrase carry a business job?** It must name, clarify, differentiate, reassure, provoke, qualify, or convert.

## STEAM ATTRACTORS

Brain Glue's master framework is STEAM ATTRACTORS — all 14 levers extracted from the 204-page book (`references/genius-patterns.md`, extraction dated 2026-06-11):

| Lever | Job |
|---|---|
| Setting Expectation | Control the first frame so the buyer sees what you need them to see |
| Tribal Alignment | Connect to identity, belonging, pride, language, values, and shared enemies |
| Easing Understanding | Make the offer cognitively simple enough to grasp in a second |
| Analogies and Metaphors | Turn abstract claims into pictures, objects, scenes, and comparisons |
| Anchoring | Attach the idea to something already known, valued, feared, loved, or owned |
| Toning Voice | Express calm confidence, authority, and emotional control |
| Triggering Oxytocin | Use humor, fascination, warmth, or emotional safety to lower resistance |
| Rhyming | Create completion pressure and memory through sound patterning |
| Alliteration | Use repeated sounds to make names and phrases easier to process |
| Chiasmus | Reverse structure or meaning to make a statement feel profound and repeatable |
| Trigger-Words | Wake attention with emotionally charged words, used ethically and precisely |
| Odd and Unexpected | Break the expected pattern so the brain has to pay attention |
| Rejecting to Attract | Increase desire by naming who it is not for and who qualifies |
| Sense Elevation | Add sight, sound, smell, taste, touch, gesture, or demonstration |

## Signature Moves

These seven moves are drawn from the same 204-page extraction (`references/genius-patterns.md`, dated 2026-06-11) that produced the 14 STEAM ATTRACTORS levers above:

- **Start with the obvious asset**: Mine the existing name, category, audience phrase, competitor, feature, or customer behavior before inventing something clever.
- **Find the one feature that deserves the spotlight**: Do not cram all benefits into the first frame. Hook with the most memorable buying reason, then earn the rest.
- **Add perspective**: Compare the claim to something the audience already knows so scale, speed, risk, or value becomes visible.
- **Turn logic into a scene**: Replace abstract claims with visuals, demonstrations, gestures, sounds, and sensory language.
- **Create completion pressure**: Use rhyme, repeated sound, and reversible phrasing so the phrase feels finished and wants to be repeated.
- **Tell the truth early**: An honest admission or clear expectation can make the buyer relax and trust the rest of the message.
- **Use the edge carefully**: Surprise, trigger words, rejection, and line-approaching names work only when they serve the buyer's understanding and the brand can tolerate the heat.

## Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4: Acceptable | 7: Strong | 10: Brain Glue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | The idea is understandable | The idea is easy to explain | The buyer can repeat it after one exposure |
| Emotional Charge | The words are competent | The words create feeling | The phrase triggers an involuntary reaction |
| Memory | The line is readable | The line has a pattern | The line sticks like a jingle, proverb, or nickname |
| Business Function | Sounds good | Helps the asset perform | Directly clarifies, differentiates, qualifies, or sells |
| Audience Fit | Generic buyer language | Uses audience cues | Feels native to the buyer's tribe |
| Sensory Vividness | Abstract | Some concrete detail | The buyer sees, hears, feels, tastes, or smells it |
| Ethical Force | Persuasive | Strong but fair | Powerful without deceiving or manufacturing false claims |

## Anti-Patterns

- **Clever but jobless**: A phrase that sounds witty but does not clarify, differentiate, or sell. (Source: `references/hidden-knowledge.md`, Practitioner Constraints, extracted 2026-06-11 — "Never let clever phrasing obscure the offer.")
- **All tools at once**: Stuffing rhyme, alliteration, metaphor, and shock into the same line until it feels fake. (Source: `references/hidden-knowledge.md`, Practitioner Constraints, extracted 2026-06-11 — "Brain Glue is a sequencing method. Do not use every tool.")
- **Wrong tribe mimicry**: Borrowing identity markers without evidence of real audience understanding. (Source: `references/genius-patterns.md`, Pattern 2 — Tribal Belonging Before Argument, extracted 2026-06-11.)
- **Trigger-word abuse**: Using intense words for cheap attention when the offer cannot support the emotion. (Source: `references/genius-patterns.md`, Pattern 11 — Trigger Word Wake-Up, extracted 2026-06-11 — "The word increases attention while staying connected to a real point.")
- **Metaphor fog**: A metaphor that creates a picture but makes the actual offer less clear. (Source: `references/genius-patterns.md`, Pattern 5 — Metaphor as Product Interface, extracted 2026-06-11.)
- **Feature pileup**: Trying to say every benefit before the buyer understands the main one. (Source: `references/genius-patterns.md`, Anti-Exemplar — The Logical Feature Dump, extracted 2026-06-11 — "A page, pitch, or script lists every feature, proof point, audience benefit, and explanation at once.")
- **Edgy mismatch**: Using rejection, shock, or line-approaching language for a brand that needs trust, safety, or discretion. (Source: `references/hidden-knowledge.md`, Tacit Insights, extracted 2026-06-11 — "Shock works only when it points to truth.")

## Voice DNA

Brain Glue execution should sound direct, commercial, vivid, and practical. It should not sound academic. The output should move quickly from diagnosis to usable language. Favor short candidate lines, clear rationales, and immediate deployment notes — the register of the extraction's own dry-run sample, "Stop rebuilding your genius from scratch," not a lecture about why rhyme works (`_active/harness/codex-harvest-2026-06-11/brain/brain-glue-extract-forge/04-verification.md`, dated 2026-06-11).

When producing assets, use punchy labels:
- **Sticky Core**
- **Buyer Memory Hook**
- **Tribal Cue**
- **Perspective Shift**
- **Sensory Upgrade**
- **Rejection Line**
- **Campaign Spine**

Before shipping, ask: would James I. Bond recognize this as Brain Glue — a single buying idea carried by a named lever from the 14-item STEAM ATTRACTORS table (`references/genius-patterns.md`, extracted 2026-06-11), tested against the Quality Rubric above — or does it read as generic "sticky copy" borrowing Made to Stick's vocabulary without any phrase-level mechanic (rhyme, alliteration, chiasmus, a real anchor, a real trigger word) actually present? If no lever is identifiable in the finished line, it did not pass Brain Glue's method — it only used the name.

## Ethical Boundary

Brain Glue can make weak, misleading, or manipulative ideas more persuasive. Do not use it to hide defects, invent proof, exploit protected vulnerabilities, or make false claims. Strong persuasion should make a true value easier to understand and remember.
