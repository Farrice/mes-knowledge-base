Website traffic is vanity. Stripe transactions are reality. Team size is vanity. Revenue per team member is reality. ## Execution

### 1. The Vanity vs. Reality Audit
Classify every metric the founder currently tracks:

| Metric | Current Value | Vanity or Reality? | Why |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|-----|
| [Metric] | [Value] | [V/R] | [Explanation] |

**Vanity Metrics** (stop tracking these): likes, followers, website traffic, team size, open rates (in isolation), social media impressions, "engagement"

**Reality Metrics** (start tracking these): Stripe transactions, revenue, profit margin, revenue per team member, conversion rate, customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, churn rate, cash in bank

### 2. Leading vs. Lagging Indicator Architecture
Most founders only track lagging indicators (what already happened). Leading indicators predict what WILL happen.

**Design the leading indicator chain** for this business:

```
[Activity you control] → [Early signal] → [Conversion event] → [Revenue]
```

Example:
```
Outreach messages sent → Discovery calls booked → Proposals sent → Revenue closed
```

For each step, define:
| Stage | Leading Indicator | Target | Frequency |
|-------|------------------|--------|-----------|
| Top of funnel | [What you do daily to create opportunities] | [Daily target] | Daily |
| Mid-funnel | [Signals that predict conversion] | [Weekly target] | Daily |
| Bottom of funnel | [Conversion event] | [Monthly target] | Daily |
| Revenue | [Money collected] | [Monthly target] | Daily |

### 4. Output-Based Compensation Design
Pay for results, not time. Design compensation structures:

For each team role:
| Role | Key Output | How Measured | Base % | Variable % | Variable Trigger |
|------|-----------|-------------|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| [Role] | [Result] | [Metric] | [%] | [%] | [What earns the variable] |

**Principles:**
- Lower base + higher variable = role self-corrects (the worse they perform, the less they make)
- Give unlimited upside (cap demotivates top performers)
- Use simple goals tied to pay that are easy to measure
- Never track hours. Never install monitoring software. Track OUTPUT.

### 5. The Revenue Per Team Member Test
Martell's favorite metric: **profit per team member.**

Calculate:
- Annual revenue / team size = Revenue per head
- Annual profit / team size = Profit per head
- Target: $1M revenue per team member (aspirational but directional)

If the number is low:
- Are you over-staffed? (subtract before adding)
- Is any role failing to contribute to revenue? (buyback audit)
- Are you measuring hours when you should measure output?

## Creative Latitude
The dashboard structure is the foundation. Within it, find the ONE metric that matters most for THIS business. Martell uses "enterprise value created per dollar spent." What's the equivalent for this business? Find the compression point — the single number that tells the whole truth.
