# Use Cases — Where to Point This Skill

Stanton's method is not a story-only tool; it is an attention engine plus a truth-finder, and almost everything you ship asks a reader to want the next line. The cases below are the surfaces where the premise-sentence, the spine, and the clamp pay off hardest — including several you are probably not yet running through this lens.

---

## 1. Parallax Edition That Refuses to Cohere

**Surface**: A Parallax Substack draft with a strong opening and a body that wanders — research piled in, no through-line, the famous "comprehensive but inert" problem.

**The gap it covers**: `/parallax` and the writers-room sharpen prose and structure, but neither forces the one true sentence the whole edition is *about*. You ship a topic, not a premise. Stanton's litmus catches this before the polish does.

**Run**: `/stanton-premise` to extract the character+conflict+conclusion sentence (the way *Nemo* became "fear denies a good father from being one"), then `/stanton-clamp-audit` walking each section for where the beach ball drops.

**Expected lift**: The edition stops being a survey and starts being *about* one thing the reader can feel. Sections that don't turn a face toward that premise get cut or re-angled. Retention through the middle, where Parallax editions historically sag.

---

## 2. LinkedIn Daily Post With a Flat Middle

**Surface**: A `/linkedin-daily` post that hooks well and then loses the reader in lines 3-8 before the payoff.

**The gap it covers**: The hook-bank and voice gates protect the open and the voice; nothing audits the *handoff between lines* — whether each line makes you dying to read the next, not just the next paragraph. Stanton's "make them want to know what the next line of dialogue is" is line-level clamp, finer than any current gate.

**Run**: `/stanton-clamp-audit` at line granularity — mark the exact line where forward pull dies and rebuild it with a tap-at-the-window (anticipation manufactured from nothing).

**Expected lift**: Dwell time and read-to-end on the posts that currently die mid-scroll. The clamp is conjurable; a sagging post usually has two slack lines, not a bad idea.

---

## 3. Team / Client Review Run as a Brain Trust

**Surface**: A draft (post, edition, listing, deck) about to go to Farrice, a client, or the writers-room for notes.

**The gap it covers**: Reviews collect *prescribed fixes* ("make the intro punchier," "cut this section," "recast the father"). Stanton's hardest-won lesson is that the note is real but the fix is usually a symptom — on *Nemo*, every smart note said "recast the neurotic father" and the real answer was an emotional prologue that left the father untouched.

**Run**: `/stanton-root-diagnose` on the assembled notes — separate the felt symptom from the upstream cause, find the one move that dissolves a dozen downstream complaints. Pair with `/stanton-behavior-read`: weight what reviewers *did* (where they stopped reading, what they quoted back) over what they *said* they disliked.

**Expected lift**: One upstream change instead of ten surface edits. You stop "changing the wrong thing for the wrong reasons," which is where revision cycles usually go to die.

---

## 4. Stalled Draft Rescue (Fit-the-Dinosaur)

**Surface**: A piece you committed to a structure for — a promised Parallax angle, a client deliverable scoped a week ago — where mid-build you realize the real story is different from the one you sold.

**The gap it covers**: `/deepen` and the craft roster improve what's there; they don't give you permission to *change the dinosaur*. The instinct is to blink or shrink — force the piece back onto the promised skeleton. Stanton's entire production discipline exists so the late pivot is survivable.

**Run**: `/stanton-fit-the-dinosaur` — name the bone you actually uncovered (it's a Stegosaurus, not the T-Rex you promised), then rebuild the piece to the truth instead of the commitment.

**Expected lift**: The rescue that incremental edits can't reach. This is the explicit hypothesis in the skill's evolution log: does "fit the dinosaur" rescue stalled drafts better than tinkering. The answer on a genuinely-wrong-spine draft is yes.

---

## 5. Diagnosing Flat Content Nobody Can Explain

**Surface**: A post or page that "tests fine" on the gates but feels dead, and no one can say why.

**The gap it covers**: `prose_classifier.py` and the quality gate measure the floor — slop, voice, structure. They cannot tell you the audience *unclamped*. A piece can pass every deterministic check and still drop the beach ball on line four.

**Run**: `/stanton-clamp-audit` as a pure diagnostic — produce a beat-by-beat map of where anticipation-mingled-with-uncertainty exists and where it flatlines. Output is a heat-strip, not a rewrite, so you know *what* to fix before spending a craft pass.

**Expected lift**: A named failure point on content the gates call clean. Turns "this feels off" into "the clamp releases between the example and the takeaway."

## 6. MyBPM Listing / Product Drop With No Pull

**Surface**: A MyBPM streetwear listing or drop announcement — copy that describes the shirt accurately and moves no one.

**The gap it covers**: The Meg Heckman buyer-trigger OS handles the 50ms identity signal and the trigger stack. What it doesn't do is give the *drop itself* a spine and a moment of change — turn "here is a new tee" into a small change story the buyer is the protagonist of.

**Run**: `/stanton-spine` on the buyer (the fixed inner wiring an EDM-streetwear fan gets up in the morning solving — belonging, recognition, the scene), then `/stanton-telling` on the copy: control the rhythm with Walter-Hill line breaks so the moments worth savoring read slow and the rest gets out of the way.

**Expected lift**: Drop copy that clamps instead of catalogs. Stacks cleanly with Meg's triggers — Stanton supplies the forward pull and the savor, Meg supplies the buyer-psychology hooks.

---

## 7. Jen Santulan Real Estate — Emotional Staging of a Listing

**Surface**: An SFV listing description or first-time-buyer post for Jen that lists square footage, schools, and finishes.

**The gap it covers**: Listing-content skills cover the facts and the format. None of them *cast the buyer's spine* — the thing a first-time buyer is actually solving (safety, arrival, proof they made it, a place their kids grow up). A listing is a change story: renter to owner, waiting to arrived.

**Run**: `/stanton-spine` to wire the buyer's core motivation, then `/stanton-change` to locate the single moment of change the listing is really selling (the key in the door, not the granite), and `/stanton-telling` to underwrite — trust the buyer to fill the gap instead of explaining the feeling to them.

**Expected lift**: Listings that make the SFV first-time buyer lean forward instead of comparison-shop on spec sheets. Emotional staging in copy, the way a stager stages a room.

## 8. Andrea / Resonance — The Daytime Sober Party as a Change Arc

**Surface**: Launch copy, the event page, or the IG announcement for the Resonance POC dance party — a daytime sober DJ event in Chicago that has to make sobriety feel like a gain, not an absence.

**The gap it covers**: Event copy defaults to logistics (where, when, who's spinning). The harder job is wonder and opposites — the involuntary "oh" of a daytime dancefloor with no bar, and the dramatic engine of opposing expectations (party / sober, daytime / nightlife). Stanton built *WALL·E* from wonder and from making the leads opposites (a box and a circle).

**Run**: `/stanton-wonder` to find the human-condition moment the event actually offers (presence, the body, joy you remember the next morning), and `/stanton-opposites` to turn "sober party" from an apology into the source of the drama.

**Expected lift**: Copy that sells the feeling of the room, not the rules of the room. Sobriety reframed as the wonder, not the constraint.

## 9. Investor / Sales Pitch Deck as a Protagonist Arc

**Surface**: A pitch deck — the Authority Flywheel offer, a client proposal, a prediction-market or product pitch — built as a feature list (problem, solution, market, ask).

**The gap it covers**: Decks are assembled as dominoes, each slide knocking the next. Nothing makes the *prospect* the protagonist with a spine, or clamps the deck so the reader wants the next slide. A flat deck is a deck where the beach ball drops on slide three.

**Run**: `/stanton-spine` on the buyer (what they get up solving), `/stanton-clamp-audit` across the slide sequence (every slide must make them want the next), and `/stanton-ending` so the close is surprising and inevitable — they didn't see the ask coming yet of course it had to be this.

**Expected lift**: Decks that pull a reader through to the ask instead of letting them skim to the price. The "anticipation mingled with uncertainty" definition applies slide-to-slide exactly as it does beat-to-beat.

---

## 10. Client Case Study as a Before / After Change Story

**Surface**: A case study or testimonial write-up — "we worked with X, here are the results" — structured as a results dump.

**The gap it covers**: Proof skills assemble the receipts; they don't structure the case study as a story with a moment of change. A case study with no named change is a static beat — nothing transforms, the cable car has disconnected.

**Run**: `/stanton-change` to locate the fundamental before/after the engagement turned on, `/stanton-spine` to make the *client* the protagonist (not your agency), and `/stanton-premise` to write the one true sentence the case study proves.

**Expected lift**: Case studies that read as transformation, not as a brag sheet. The reader sees themselves in the client's spine and wants the change that case study delivered.

---

## 11. Personal-Brand Origin Story (Farrice's Suppression Wound)

**Surface**: The Parallax / LinkedIn origin narrative — the founder story Farrice tells to anchor the brand, rooted in the 90% suppression pattern.

**The gap it covers**: Voice skills and `/deepen` reach the interiority, but the origin story needs a *premise-sentence* (character + conflict + conclusion) and a spine, or it becomes confession without architecture. And the memory note is explicit: the brother/mother specifics aren't ready for public telling — so the *telling* matters more than the content. This is precisely Stanton's "a bad joke told well," and his bittersweet-by-temperament register.

**Run**: `/stanton-premise` to find the one sentence the origin is really about, `/stanton-telling` to control what's withheld (underwrite the wound; trust the reader), and `/stanton-wonder` to land it on the human condition rather than the trauma detail.

**Expected lift**: An origin that resonates without over-disclosing — the architecture carries the weight the unsaid specifics can't yet. Restraint as craft, not avoidance.

---

## 12. Knowledge-Work Explainer — Make a Dry Memo Clamp

**Surface**: A system audit summary, a strategy brief, a Notion library explainer, an internal directive — necessary, accurate, and unread because it's inert.

**The gap it covers**: The whole repo produces dense knowledge work, and "comprehensive" is a stated failure mode. Nothing currently makes a *memo* clamp. Stanton's claim that the telling is the content applies hardest exactly where people assume it can't — dry, factual, internal docs.

**Run**: `/stanton-telling` to control rhythm and withhold exposition (left-justified Walter-Hill breaks on the moments that matter), and `/stanton-clamp-audit` to find where the reader's attention dies and restore one piece of forward pull per section.

**Expected lift**: A memo someone actually reads to the end. The driest doc has a premise; finding it turns a wall of text into a thing with a heartbeat.

---

## 13. Ad Pre-Skip — The First 30 Seconds as a Complete Arc

**Surface**: A video ad, a Reel/Short hook, or a YouTube pre-roll where the first few seconds decide everything and the skip button is the cruelest test screening ever built.

**The gap it covers**: The ad and full-stack-ad skills handle offer and structure; they don't treat the pre-skip window as a self-contained clamp with its own anticipation and uncertainty. The skip is Stanton's test audience in the dark — it tells you what they *do*, not what they say.

**Run**: `/stanton-clamp-audit` on the first 30 seconds as if it were the whole film — manufacture a tap-at-the-window in the first three, never let the beach ball drop before the turn — and `/stanton-behavior-read` to treat skip-rate as the only honest note.

**Expected lift**: Pre-skip retention. An ad open engineered like a cold open: clamped from frame one, no exposition the audience would skip to escape.

---

## 14. Course / Lesson Design — Every Module a Change

**Surface**: A course, a teaching sequence, or a multi-part content series (the `oe-teach-to-sell` work, an educator series, a Parallax prompt-pack arc) where modules are organized by *topic* rather than by transformation.

**The gap it covers**: Curriculum defaults to a syllabus — a list of subjects in logical order. Stanton's rule is that no change means no story; a module that only delivers information is a static beat, and the learner unclamps. Each lesson should turn on a moment of change in the student.

**Run**: `/stanton-change` per module (what does the learner become able to do/see that they couldn't before this one), `/stanton-clamp-audit` across the sequence so each module makes them want the next, and `/stanton-premise` for the one sentence the whole course is about.

**Expected lift**: Completion rate. Courses where every module clamps into the next instead of sitting as a topic the learner can abandon between sessions.

---

## 15. Podcast / Long-Form Video Narrative Spine

**Surface**: A long-form piece — a podcast episode, a `/watch` remix, a 20-minute video essay — where the content is good but the *shape* is a transcript, not an arc.

**The gap it covers**: Long-form has no enemy but the drift. The clamp problem compounds over an hour: where does the listener mentally leave the room. Stanton's cable-car question — "when did we accidentally unclamp?" — is the only audit built for length, and the premise-sentence is the spine that holds 20 minutes together.

**Run**: `/stanton-premise` for the through-line, `/stanton-clamp-audit` across the runtime to mark every drop point, and `/stanton-fit-the-dinosaur` if the recorded material reveals the episode is really about something other than the planned topic (it usually is).

**Expected lift**: Long-form that holds to the end because it has a spine and re-clamps at every sag, instead of relying on the guest being interesting enough to survive the slack.

---

## 16. /convene and Writers-Room — Stanton as the Engagement Seat

**Surface**: A `/convene` council or writers-room run on any high-stakes deliverable, where multiple experts deliberate but no one owns *forward pull* and *root-vs-symptom*.

**The gap it covers**: The room sharpens craft, structure, and voice. It doesn't have a seat whose entire job is the clamp-audit and the root diagnosis — the two things Stanton owns that no other roster member does. Hawley sets the arc; Stanton tunes the engagement inside it (the documented stack).

**Run**: Seat Stanton in the council via `/stanton-clamp-audit` and `/stanton-root-diagnose` as the penultimate pass — after the arc is set and before final polish — so the room's output is audited for where attention drops and whether the fixes target roots or symptoms.

**Expected lift**: Council outputs that are not just well-built but *unputdownable*, and revision rounds that fix the upstream cause once instead of chasing symptoms across three passes.
