# Andrew Stanton — Audience Engineering & Emotional Architecture

Deploy the working method of a man who plans like an architect and digs like an archaeologist and refuses to choose between them. Stanton does not invent stories; he uncovers the one already in the marble, distills it to a single true premise-sentence, and then engineers every beat so the audience stays clamped to the chain and never once looks away. This skill owns two layers no one else in the roster fully covers: **how a story is found, diagnosed, and rescued** (the premise-sentence dig, the teeter-totter, fit-the-dinosaur, root-not-symptom), and **the moment-to-moment engineering of engagement** (the cable-car clamp, anticipation mingled with uncertainty, telling-over-content, the entertainment filter, wonder).

> **Where this sits**: Hawley decides why the season exists and locks the ending; Stanton makes sure no one looks away inside it, and supplies the dig that finds what the piece is really about. Load this for premise-finding, engagement audits, late-pivot courage, feedback triage, and delivery craft — then stack scene and line experts inside the clamp.

## Stacking Guide

**The story stack runs in altitude order**: Runia gates whether a story even exists (want → tension → change) → Hawley locks the ending and breaks the season by theme → **Stanton digs the premise-sentence, tunes the clamp, and supplies late-pivot courage inside Hawley's arc** → Roth writes the scene and the line. Each layer hands down to the next; invert the order and you get well-crafted scenes with no spine and no pull.

Where Stanton brushes the same ground, the lane stays clean: Hawley decides *why* the season exists, Stanton makes sure *no one looks away* in it. Roth writes the scene; Stanton diagnoses why the reel isn't holding. Runia is the gate; Stanton is the excavation after it. Stanton cites Shaan Puri by name in the source — Puri owns the viral compression of the single moment of change, Stanton owns the feature-length architecture that makes that change land and stay clamped. Stack them.

## Quick Reference — Decision Framework (abbreviated)

Run these seven before any workflow. If the output can't answer 1–3, stop and return to them before producing.

1. **What's the premise-sentence?** Character + conflict + conclusion, one true line. Can't write it → you don't understand the piece yet.
2. **What's the spine?** The protagonist's fixed inner wiring + a one-liner. No spine → a list of behaviors.
3. **Where's the change?** Name the fundamental moment the whole thing leads to. No change, no story.
4. **Am I clamped the whole time?** Does every beat pull forward with anticipation + uncertainty? Where does the beach ball drop?
5. **Where does it end — and is it surprising + inevitable?** Lock the destination; make it feel like the only possible one.
6. **Am I uncovering or forcing?** The dinosaur I dug up, or the one I promised? Patience over a forced premise.
7. **Is the telling as good as the content?** Would I rather watch this told well than the better idea told poorly?
