# The Clamp Audit (Andrew Stanton)
A cable car climbs the hill because a clamp grips the chain running under the street; the second it lets go, you stop. This is a diagnostic pass over something already written — a draft, a reel, a page — that walks it beat by beat to find where the clamp slipped, names why, and grips the chain again before the reader's attention goes back to checking the time.

## Input Required
- The existing piece in full — draft, script, reel transcript, ad, page, post (whole thing, not an excerpt)
- The format and the channel it ships on (sets the unclamp tolerance — see Adaptations)
- The premise-sentence and the spine, if already known (if not, derive them in the Pre-Flight gate before walking)
- The one moment of change the piece is built to deliver (you audit toward whether every beat pulls the reader to it)
- Where you suspect it sags, if you have a hunch (optional — the walk should find it cold either way)

## Workflow

### Step 1: Walk it beat by beat, clamped or not
Read the piece at the reader's pace, not the writer's. Break it into its actual beats — a beat is any unit small enough that attention could plausibly drop inside it: a paragraph, a shot, a line of dialogue, a slide, a sentence in a hook. At the end of each beat ask the only question that matters: *does this make me want to know what comes next?* Not the plot question (that's the low-hanging fruit) — the line-level pull, the reason the eye moves to the next beat instead of away.

Mark each beat CLAMPED (forward pull live) or SLIPPED (you'd glance up). Don't fix anything yet. Find where the beach ball dropped before you decide why.

| # | Beat (1 line) | Clamped? | First glance-up moment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | CLAMPED / SLIPPED | |
| 2 | | | |
| 3 | | | |
| … | | | |

The honest version of this catches the slips you wrote *because you knew the material already* — the beats that pull for the author and stall for a cold reader.

### Step 2: Name WHY each unclamp happened (diagnose the root)
For every SLIPPED beat, name the cause — and name the *root*, not the symptom. A flat line is usually downstream of a structural miss. The four engines of an unclamp:

| Cause | What it looks like on the page | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| **No anticipation** | Nothing ahead to lean toward; the beat resolves itself and leaves no debt | Reader has no reason to turn the page |
| **No uncertainty** | Outcome already obvious; you've shown your hand | Reader can finish your sentence for you |
| **Static** | Nothing changed — same emotional/factual position as the beat before | The cable car disconnected (HK-7: even "it was active, then it wasn't" is change) |
| **Over-explained** | Exposition carrying what gesture, restraint, or a gap would carry better | The telling got in the way of the content |

Resist the first fix that comes. When several slipped beats cluster, the real cause is often one beat upstream — like the *Nemo* notes that all said "fix the father" when the fix was an earlier prologue. Find the single upstream beat that, re-clamped, makes the downstream slips evaporate.

| Slipped beat # | Surface cause | Root cause (often upstream) |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

### Step 3: Prescribe the re-clamp
For each slip, grip the chain again. The move follows the cause — and the materials are conjurable, at the ready, the way a tapping at a window that grows louder manufactures uncertainty out of nothing.

| Cause | Re-clamp move |
|---|---|
| No anticipation | Plant a debt the beat doesn't pay — a promise, a question opened, a thing about to happen |
| No uncertainty | Cut the tell; withhold the outcome; let the reader not know which way it breaks |
| Static | Inject a change, even a small one; if the beat truly can't move, cut it |
| Over-explained | Delete the exposition; trust the gap; let rhythm or a withheld line do the work |

Prefer the smallest grip that holds. Re-clamping is surgical — you are not rewriting the piece, you are restoring forward pull at named points and leaving the rest alone.

| Slipped beat # | Cause | Re-clamp prescription | Cut / Rewrite / Add |
|---|---|---|---|

### Step 4: Re-walk and confirm clamped end to end
Read the revised piece once more at the reader's pace, cold. Confirm the marked beats now pull, and confirm no re-clamp introduced a *new* slip one beat over (a planted debt that's never paid reads worse than no debt). The pass is done when you can walk the whole thing and never find the place you'd glance up — clamped the entire way, the reader so engaged they never think about anything else.

If any beat is still SLIPPED after one re-clamp, treat it as a root-cause miss: go back up the chain, not deeper into the same beat.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| Screenplay / film / video | Beat = shot or line; walk the reel at playback speed, not reading speed. The cruelest slip is the post-climax lull — a static stretch where the change has landed and nothing pulls toward the close. Re-clamp with a withheld beat or an inversion, never a service scene. |
| Long-form essay / Substack | Beat = paragraph. Slips cluster at section seams and after the best line (the reader feels permission to stop). Re-clamp the *last sentence of each section* with an opened debt the next section pays. Kill the tidy mid-essay takeaway that resolves the tension early. |
| Short-form social (LinkedIn / X / Reels) | Beat = line; the unclamp tolerance is near zero — one flat line and they scroll. Audit the line-to-line pull above all else. The hook clamps; every following line must hold the grip; the last line stays open so the reader finishes it, never a tidy bow. |
| Sales / marketing copy | Beat = claim/section. Slips hide where proof gets explained instead of shown, and where the next benefit is predictable. Re-clamp by withholding the mechanism one beat longer and opening a curiosity debt before each new section. Static "feature lists" are dropped beach balls — give each one a change. |
| Brand / campaign | Beat = asset in sequence. Audit the *campaign* as one clamped line, not each asset in isolation — the drop is usually between assets, where the through-line goes static. Re-clamp the handoffs; cut any "clever" asset that resolves the campaign's central tension before the payoff. |

  • Beat #__ → [move] — [CUT / REWRITE / ADD]
  • Beat #__ → ...

RE-WALK CONFIRMATION:

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against genius.md § Anti-Patterns + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric. Flag and fix before delivering.
- Every SLIPPED beat names a root cause, not just a symptom — no prescription acts on the surface fix when an upstream beat is the real miss.
- Each re-clamp uses the move that matches the cause (anticipation / uncertainty / change / cut exposition), not a generic "make it punchier."
- The re-walk was actually performed cold and confirms forward pull end to end; no planted debt is left unpaid.
- The fixes are surgical — the piece's premise, spine, and voice are preserved; this is a clamp restoration, not a rewrite.
- Nowhere does the delivered output label its own machinery ("here I added anticipation"); the grip is executed, not announced.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Walking at writer's speed, not reader's.** You know what's coming, so every beat feels clamped to you. Recovery: read cold at playback/reading pace, or have a fresh pair of eyes mark the glance-up points — trust where they actually drift, not where they say they liked it.
- **Fixing the symptom beat.** Patching the flat line when the cause is a missing setup three beats earlier just moves the slip. Recovery: when slips cluster, hunt upstream for the single beat that re-clamps the whole run — the *Nemo*-prologue move.
- **Over-clamping into noise.** Stacking a debt onto every beat turns urgency into hysteria and exhausts the reader. Recovery: smallest grip that holds; let some beats simply move cleanly so the planted ones land.
- **Re-clamping by adding exposition.** Explaining the stakes to manufacture tension usually kills the very uncertainty you need. Recovery: withhold instead of explain — the unsaid pulls harder than the spelled-out.
