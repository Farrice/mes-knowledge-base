# The Brain Trust (Andrew Stanton)
The Pixar Brain Trust ran every film through one instinct — is this more entertaining now? — and nothing got through without becoming so, because the room stayed small enough that no one performed and safe enough that a director could say "I have no idea what I'm doing" out loud. This workflow stands up that room for any draft, deck, or campaign and runs critique the way Stanton runs it: a good note is not an accurate inventory of what's wrong, it is whatever makes the maker want to redo their homework.

## Input Required
- The artifact under review — the draft, reel, deck, post, or campaign concept (raw is fine)
- The maker's own one-sentence premise for it, and where they think it is weakest
- The candidate reviewers available (names, what each is working on, who is on this same project)
- The stage: early dig (still uncovering) vs. late polish (locked structure) — it changes who you want in the room
- The change you want out of the session: a rewrite the maker is energized to do, not a list they have to defend against

## Workflow

### Step 1: Cap the room and check for an audience
Past roughly ten people there is an audience, and the moment there is an audience people perform — they hide their real reaction and tell you what plays instead of what is true. Build the smallest room that still makes the maker smarter, and stop well before the threshold. Score each candidate: do they make the maker sharper, and will the maker feel safe being wrong in front of them?

| Candidate | Makes the maker smarter? | Maker feels safe being wrong? | On this same project? | Seat / Cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (name 1) | | | | |
| (name 2) | | | | |
| (name 3) | | | | |
| (name 4) | | | | |
| (name 5) | | | | |

Keep fewer than six seats. A "no" in either of the middle columns is a cut, regardless of how senior or smart the person is — smart-and-unsafe is exactly the failure mode that produced "recast the father" on *Nemo*.

### Step 2: Mix in fresh eyes to protect objectivity
A room made entirely of people on the project loses the ability to see it; they share the maker's blind spots and have skin in the same beats. Seat at least one person who is *not* on this project — someone running everything through the entertainment filter cold, with no stake in which scene survives. Their job is not domain expertise; it is the involuntary reaction of a first-time audience.

- On-project seats (depth, can fix it): ____, ____
- Off-project seats (objectivity, cannot be invested in the love-list): ____
- If every seat is on-project, the room is an echo, not a Brain Trust — recruit one outsider before proceeding.

### Step 3: Set the safety so the truth comes out
The room only tells the truth at a specific temperature. Open by making it cheap to be wrong — the maker says, out loud, what they are unsure of and where they think it is broken, and the room treats notes as a file nobody has to ship ("it's just a file, we don't have to show anybody"). Establish three standing rules before the first note:

- **No mandate.** The room diagnoses; the maker decides. Nobody in the room can force a fix — that protects the maker's nerve to keep digging.
- **Behavior over verdict.** Report what you *did* watching it — where you leaned in, where you checked out, where the beach ball dropped — before any opinion about what to change.
- **Resistance is unreliable.** When the maker flinches hard at a bold note (HK-3, the *Nemo* prologue), the room lowers the cost of testing it instead of arguing — try it as a throwaway file, show no one.

### Step 4: Critique to inspire the rewrite, not to list the flaws
A note's value is measured downstream — did it produce a better next draft? An accurate tally of failures that defeats the maker is a worse note than an incomplete one that reignites them. For each issue, separate the *felt symptom* (where attention dropped, what rang false) from the *prescribed fix* (which is usually wrong), name the likely *root*, and frame the note as the smallest move that would make the maker want to go re-dig.

| Felt symptom (what the room saw/did) | Prescribed fix (distrust this) | Likely root (one upstream cause) | Rewrite-igniting note (makes them want to redo it) |
|---|---|---|---|
| (e.g. "I disliked the lead the whole time") | (e.g. "recast / soften him") | (e.g. "no scene earns empathy up front") | (e.g. "what's the one moment that would make us forgive him before he opens his mouth?") |
| | | | |
| | | | |

Trust the felt symptom; distrust the prescribed fix; hunt the root. The note ships only if you can honestly say it would energize a rewrite rather than win an argument.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| Screenplay / film / video | Watch it in the dark before talking — report leaning-forward vs. watch-checking, not Q&A opinion. Off-project seat reacts as a cold first audience; notes target the reel that does not hold, framed as the next dig, not a recast order. |
| Long-form essay / Substack | Reviewers mark the exact line where they stopped reading and the takeaway that resolved the question into advice. Root note targets the section that lost the thread, phrased as "what were you actually trying to say here?" — not a sentence-level edit list. |
| Short-form social (LinkedIn / X / Reels) | Two or three trusted readers, one off-niche for the cold reaction. They flag the scroll-past line and whether the hook clamps in the first beat. The note is one move that would make the maker want to rewrite the open, not a redline of every line. |
| Sales / marketing copy | Seat someone who is *not* sold on the offer for objectivity. Notes separate "I didn't believe this" (symptom) from "drop the guarantee" (prescribed fix, distrust) from "the proof arrives after the ask" (root). Frame as the rewrite that earns the belief earlier. |
| Brand / campaign | Small cross-functional room with one outsider who has no stake in the hero concept. Run every asset through the entertainment filter cold; the beloved campaign idea that wins the room's applause but contradicts the brand's real conflict gets the hardest, most rewrite-igniting note. |

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against genius.md § Anti-Patterns + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric. Flag and fix before delivering.
- The room is **fewer than six** and every seat passed both the smarter-and-safe test; no smart-but-unsafe seat survived (HK-8).
- At least one seat is **off-project** — the room is not an echo chamber of people sharing the same blind spots.
- Every note separates the **felt symptom** from the **prescribed fix** and names a single upstream **root**; no note acts on the prescribed fix as if it were the cure (Pattern 7).
- Each note is framed to **inspire the rewrite** — you can honestly say it would leave the maker energized to re-dig, not defeated by a tally (HK-9).
- No note carries a **mandate**; the room diagnosed and the maker decides. The maker's hard flinch was met with a cheap throwaway test, not an argument (HK-3).

## Common Pitfalls
- **Letting the room grow to "everyone with an opinion."** Past ten people there is an audience and the truth stops coming out — you harvest performance, not reaction. Recovery: cut back under six; widen depth per seat, not headcount.
- **An all-on-project room.** Everyone shares the maker's investment and blind spots, so no one can see the beach ball drop. Recovery: seat one outsider running the entertainment filter cold before the session starts.
- **Accurate notes that kill the will to rewrite.** A perfect inventory of failures that defeats the maker is a worse note than an incomplete one that reignites them. Recovery: re-frame each note as the smallest move that makes them *want* to go back to the marble.
- **Acting on the prescribed fix.** The room's proposed solution is usually a symptom-patch ("recast the father"); the real fix is upstream. Recovery: trust the felt note, distrust the prescription, dig for the one root cause that dissolves the cluster.
