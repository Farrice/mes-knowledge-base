# The Entertainment Filter (Andrew Stanton)
The Brain Trust passed every line, scene, and decision through one instinct — is this more entertaining now than it was a minute ago — and nothing got through them without becoming more entertaining. This is a PASS workflow: you take a finished draft and run each part across that gate, raising the toe-tapping without dumbing anything down, and anything that survives the room unchanged either earns its keep or gets cut.

The filter raises entertainment; it does not fix a piece that has no premise, no spine, and no change. If those three are missing, route to /stanton-premise-sentence first.

## Input Required
- The existing draft, in full (post, essay, scene, script, page, email, ad — whatever ships)
- The natural unit to walk it in (line, paragraph, beat, section, slide)
- Who the draft is for, only so you can refuse to write down to them (Pattern 9 — you write to your own standard, not a dumbed-down imagined "them")
- Any sections already locked or sacred (you still grade them; locked means you flag, you don't silently rewrite)

## Workflow

### Step 1: Lay the draft on the table
Take the existing draft as-is. Do not improve it yet. Break it into the units you'll walk — the same way the room would put a reel up and watch it pass by. Number each unit so you can grade it. The point of this step is to stop treating the draft as one undifferentiated blob and start treating it as a sequence of things that each have to earn their seat.

| # | Unit (line / beat / section) | One-phrase content |
|---|------------------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | | |
| 2 | | |
| 3 | | |

### Step 2: Run each unit across the green thumb
Now you are the Brain Trust. For every unit ask the one question: did this get *more* entertaining as it passed through me — more pull, more pleasure, more wanting-the-next-line — or did it pass through unchanged? This is binary on purpose; the filter only works because it's easy to apply and ruthless. Keep the room small and honest (HK-8): you are not voting for what plays to a crowd, you are telling the truth about each unit. Grade with a green thumb (lifted), a flat thumb (survived unchanged), or a cut.

| # | More entertaining now? | Verdict |
|---|------------------------|---------|
| 1 | green / flat / cut | LIFT / FLAG / CUT |
| 2 | | |
| 3 | | |

A green thumb means the unit became more entertaining on the way through — keep it. A flat thumb means it survived the room unchanged — that is the failure state of this workflow, and Step 4 hunts those down. A cut means it was deadweight nothing could lift.

### Step 3: Raise the toe-tapping without dumbing it down
For every unit you can lift, lift it — make it more entertaining without simplifying it. Entertainment here is not jokes; it's anticipation, surprise, rhythm, wonder, the savored moment, the line you can't wait to read next. You write to the standard of what *you* would actually want to consume (Pattern 9), never to an imagined audience that "couldn't follow it." Dumbing down sets a false floor and the piece goes flat; raising the craft sets a true ceiling. For each lift, name the specific move that added entertainment — sharper rhythm, a withheld beat, a turn of surprise, a hit of wonder — and write the lifted version.

| # | Before (flat) | The move that adds entertainment | After (lifted) |
|---|---------------|----------------------------------|----------------|
| | | | |

> Guardrail: if a "lift" makes the unit simpler, shorter on substance, or more spoon-fed, it is not a lift — it's a dumb-down. Reject it and find a craft move instead.

### Step 4: Flag everything that survived unchanged
Walk the flat-thumb list. Each one passed through the room and came out the same — which means the filter never actually touched it. For every flat unit, make the call: lift it (return to Step 3 and find the move), cut it (it was filler protecting nothing), or — only if it is load-bearing and genuinely cannot be lifted without breaking truth — keep it and log *why* it survives unchanged. The default verdict for an unchanged unit is not "fine." It's "not finished." A good note here inspires the rewrite rather than just tallying flaws (HK-9): when you flag, say what *would* make it sing, not only that it doesn't.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| Screenplay / film / video | Walk it scene by scene the way the room runs a reel; the flat-thumb scene is where the beach ball drops. Lift via rhythm and withheld exposition (the telling), not added dialogue. A scene that only advances plot and adds no pleasure is the flattest unit in the cut. |
| Long-form essay / Substack | The unit is the paragraph or section. Flag the paragraph that's true but inert — it survives because it's *correct*, not because it's entertaining. Lift with image, surprise, and pace; never lift by simplifying the idea (that's the dumb-down trap). |
| Short-form social (LinkedIn / X / Reels) | Every line is a unit and the bar is brutal — a line that doesn't make the reader want the next line is a flat thumb and gets cut, not kept. The hook and the last line take two passes through the filter, not one. |
| Sales / marketing copy | Run each claim, proof, and transition across the gate; the flat unit is usually a feature stated without pleasure or pull. Lift by making the benefit felt and the rhythm propulsive — entertainment sells; do not "lift" by stacking more claims (that's louder, not better). |
| Brand / campaign | The unit is the asset (post, ad, page, line of voice). Grade each against the green thumb in a small, safe room (HK-8) — not a stakeholder crowd that rewards what plays safe. The clever asset that wins the meeting but adds no entertainment to the brand is a flat thumb; flag it. |

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against genius.md § Anti-Patterns + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric. Flag and fix before delivering.
- Every kept unit can be named as *more entertaining now* than the draft you started with; no unit was kept on the excuse that it "stayed the same" (Pattern 10).
- Nothing was lifted by dumbing it down — no beat simplified, shortened on substance, or spoon-fed on the theory the audience couldn't follow (Pattern 9 / Anti-Pattern: writing for an imagined "them").
- At least one unit that survived the first pass unchanged got hunted down in Step 4 and either lifted, cut, or kept-with-a-logged-reason — the flat thumb was never silently passed.
- The filter ran in a small, honest room (HK-8): grades reflect what's true, not what plays to a crowd or wins a meeting.
- The machinery stayed off the page — the delivered draft reads as transported, not as a labeled list of "entertainment moves."

## Common Pitfalls
- **Treating the filter as a final polish.** Running it once at the end, lightly, over the whole blob. Why it fails: the gate only compounds when *every unit* has to pass it to stay in. Recovery: break the draft into units and grade each one; the floor rises pass by pass, not in one sweep.
- **Dumbing down and calling it a lift.** Mistaking "simpler / shorter / more obvious" for "more entertaining." Why it fails: it sets a false floor and the piece goes flat. Recovery: reject any change that reduces substance; require a named craft move (rhythm, surprise, withheld beat, wonder) for every lift.
- **Letting the flat thumb pass as "fine."** Accepting unchanged units because they're correct or already locked. Why it fails: unchanged is the failure state of this workflow, not a pass. Recovery: default verdict for any flat unit is "not finished" — lift it, cut it, or log exactly why it must survive unchanged.
- **Widening the room to feel safe.** Crowd-sourcing the grades to stakeholders so no single cut is on you. Why it fails: past ~ten people there's an audience and you harvest what plays, not what's true (HK-8). Recovery: keep the room small and safe; grade for honest entertainment, not consensus comfort.
