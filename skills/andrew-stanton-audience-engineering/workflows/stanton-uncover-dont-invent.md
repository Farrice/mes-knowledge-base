# Uncover, Don't Invent (Andrew Stanton)
The statue is already in the marble; the trash-compactor robot alone on a dead planet was a feeling Stanton sat on for years before the dig handed him the romance, the opposite, the box and the circle. You do not take credit for what comes up — only for where you choose to dig and how aggressively you dig there. This workflow runs the dig as a real procedure: pick the ground, start with the two bones you have, work the teeter-totter, and refuse to nail a forced premise in place when the clock starts whispering.

This workflow is for when the true line will not come and the temptation is to manufacture a fake one.

## Input Required
- The seed, raw — whatever you actually have: a feeling, an image, a fragment, a topic, a stuck draft. Two real bones is enough; you do not need a skeleton.
- What you keep *wanting* it to be (the premise you would force if you ran out of time — name it so you can refuse it later)
- The deadline and what it is allowed to do to you (so the clock does not get to nail a fake premise in place)
- The piece's format and length (essay, post, screenplay, campaign — governs how the dig site is bounded)

## Workflow

### Step 1: Pick where to dig — and what you take credit for
You do not get credit for the story that comes up; you get credit for choosing the ground and for being aggressive once you are there. So choose deliberately. Name the dig site as the smallest area that still holds the feeling, and start with the two bones you already have — the only things you are certain are true about this piece. Everything else is speculation until the dig confirms it.

| The dig | Fill in |
|---|---|
| The seed-feeling (one phrase, pre-plot — "the saddest thing, that it doesn't know its job is futile") | |
| Where I am choosing to dig (the bounded site — a relationship? a single moment? one decision?) | |
| Bone #1 (something I am *certain* is true here) | |
| Bone #2 (a second certain thing — these two are your whole starting kit) | |
| What I take credit for (the choice of site + the aggression — never the dinosaur itself) | |

If you cannot name two certain bones, the site is wrong or the feeling is borrowed. Move the dig before you spend an hour excavating bedrock.

### Step 2: Run the teeter-totter — alternate ANALYZE and JAM, never sit in the middle
The dig has two sides and you are forbidden the middle. On the ANALYZE side you look for the structure, the meaning, the patterns you are *accidentally* making — what is this shaping into? On the JAM side you just play, no thinking, "because thinking restricts me" — you follow the bone, you write the bad sentence, you let it go somewhere dumb. The failure state is the middle: sitting there *sweating a half-built sentence*, neither playing nor analyzing. The instant you feel that sweat, jump to the other side.

| If you are stuck this way | You are stuck on this side | Jump to | Concrete move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Over-planning; outlining the symphony instead of writing it; everything theoretical | ANALYZE | JAM | Close the outline. Write the next bad sentence / draw the next beat with zero plan. |
| Lost in an undirected mess; pages of play with no shape | JAM | ANALYZE | Step back. Name the pattern you keep *accidentally* making — that pattern is the dig pointing somewhere. |
| Sweating one sentence; rewriting the same line; "is this the theme?" on a loop | THE MIDDLE (forbidden) | Either side, immediately | Pick a side and move. The middle is where forced premises get born. |

Log two or three swings. The dig is the swinging, not either pose held still.

| Swing | Side I was on | What I did | What surfaced (a pattern, a bone, a dead end) |

| 3 | | | |

### Step 3: Let it be lost for a while — refuse the forced premise
When the true line will not come, the trap is forcing one because the clock ran out. A premise you manufacture under deadline is a false one by definition, and everything you build on top inherits the lie. So before you commit, hold the premise you *wanted* (from Input) against the bones that actually surfaced, and ask honestly: did the dig give me this, or am I nailing it in place to feel finished?

| Check | Answer |
|---|---|
| The premise I was tempted to force | |
| Do the surfaced bones actually *prove* it, or do I just want them to? | proves / wishing |
| If wishing: what is the smallest true thing I *can* say right now? | |
| Can I let it stay lost — capture what I have and come back? (HK-10: capture, then trust what sticks) | yes / no — and if no, why is the deadline really forcing it |

If it is wishing, you are not done. Write down everything so you cannot lose the good thought, name the next time you will return to the dig, and stop. A thing left honestly unsolved beats a fake spine that collapses the whole reel later.

### Step 4: Name it when you can see the dinosaur
You stop digging when you have enough bones to *see the animal* — when the surfaced pieces imply the rest of the shape, the way a neck bone and a tailbone tell you it is a Stegosaurus, not the T-Rex you assumed. You are not waiting for the complete skeleton. You are waiting for the moment the bones stop being a list and start being a creature — and for the often-found unlock that an **opposite** is the engine (the second robot, the box and the circle, opposing agendas making the drama).

| Naming the dig | Fill in |
|---|---|
| Bones now in hand (the two you started with + what surfaced) | |
| The shape they imply (the animal, not the inventory) | |
| The opposite / engine, if the dig handed you one (opposing force, will-they-won't-they, a circle to the box) | |
| The premise-sentence the dig *earned* (character + conflict + conclusion — uncovered, not forced) | |
| Honest call | NAMED — hand to `/stanton-premise-sentence` to audit · STILL DIGGING — return to Step 2 · LET IT REST — capture and set a return date |

If you can write the earned premise-sentence, the dig succeeded; pass it to `/stanton-premise-sentence` so the line gets pressure-tested as a litmus, not obeyed as an outline.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Format | Adaptation |
|--------|-----------|
| Screenplay / film / video | The two bones are usually one image and one feeling (a robot alone on a trash planet). Dig site = a single relationship or sequence, not the whole film. JAM in scene fragments and voiced lines; ANALYZE for the accidental pattern across them. The opposite often arrives as a second character. |
| Long-form essay / Substack | Bones are the two things you *know* are true about the subject before you have an argument. The dig site is the question under the topic, not the topic. JAM in paragraphs that go somewhere dumb; ANALYZE for the through-line you keep circling. Refuse the tidy thesis the deadline wants you to force. |
| Short-form social (LinkedIn / X / Reels) | Compress brutally: one bone is the moment, one is the feeling. The dig is fast — two or three teeter-totter swings, then name it. Danger is forcing a "lesson" because a post needs to land; let the true small thing be the post, not the manufactured insight. |
| Sales / marketing copy | Bones are a real customer truth and a real product truth. Dig the site where they touch — do not invent a benefit, uncover the one already living in how people use the thing. The opposite (their current pain vs. the after-state) is frequently the drama engine. Forced premise here = a claim the product does not actually prove. |
| Brand / campaign | The dig site is the brand's real conflict, not its category. Bones are two things genuinely true about the brand before any positioning. JAM in territory and voice fragments; ANALYZE for the pattern the founder keeps returning to. The "clever campaign idea" that arrives early is usually a forced premise — make it survive the dig or drop it. |

## Quality Gate
> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Anti-Patterns` + § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric. Flag and fix before delivering.
- The premise you ship was **uncovered, not forced** — you can cite the surfaced bones that prove it, not a wish you nailed in under deadline (Pattern 4, HK-2). If it is wishing, the honest output is LET IT REST, not a fake line.
- Credit is claimed only for the **choice of dig site and the aggression**, never for the result. Any language taking authorship of "the idea I invented" is the wrong frame — rewrite it.
- The log shows real **teeter-totter swings**, not one pose held still, and no beat was produced from the forbidden middle (the half-built sentence you sat and sweated over).
- The piece is named only when the bones **imply the animal** — enough to see the shape, not a full skeleton, and not a single bone over-read into a creature it cannot support.
- Nothing on the page **names the machinery** — no "here is my dig site" labels in the delivered work; the procedure stays in the worksheet, the result stays clean.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Forcing the premise because the deadline whispered.** The clock rewards the *look* of resolution, so a manufactured line feels like finishing. Recovery: run the Step 3 refusal honestly; if it is wishing, capture everything (HK-10) and set a real return date. A thing left lost beats a false spine the reel inherits.
- **Sitting in the middle, sweating one sentence.** Neither playing nor analyzing — just grinding the same line, which is exactly where fake premises are born. Recovery: pick a side and move *now*; if you have been planning, JAM; if you have been messing, ANALYZE the pattern you keep making.
- **Reading one bone as the whole dinosaur.** A single beat or feeling over-interpreted into a story it cannot carry, so you build on speculation dressed as a skeleton. Recovery: demand two certain bones before naming the shape; if the second will not surface, the site is wrong — move the dig.
- **Inventing instead of uncovering.** Manufacturing a benefit, twist, or theme from nothing because the dig is slow. Recovery: go back to the site and the bones; the truthful engine is usually an *opposite* already present in the material, not a clever thing you add on top.
