# Andrew Stanton — User Guide

A practical guide to the audience-engineering skill: what it does, the highest-leverage ways to use it, how to drive it, and where it stops.

## The one-line version

Stanton is your **story-uncovering + audience-engagement** layer. It does not write the beautiful sentence and it does not invent a story from a blank page. It tells you what a piece is *really* about (the premise-sentence), who its protagonist is at the core (the spine), where the audience stops paying attention (the clamp), and what to fix (the root, not the symptom). Front door: `/stanton-premise-sentence`.

## Mental model: the architect who digs

Plan like an architect, work like an archaeologist. Get the premise, spine, and ending ahead of the build — but have the nerve to refit the whole thing to the truth you actually uncover. Reach for Stanton when something is built (or half-built) and isn't landing, and you need to know *why* before you touch it.

## Highest-leverage use cases (start here)

Ranked by value-per-minute on your actual projects.

**1. Diagnose why a draft is flat → `/stanton-clamp-audit`**
The single highest-ROI tool. Point it at any existing piece — a Parallax edition, a LinkedIn post, a video script, a sales page — and it marks the exact beats where the audience "unclamps" (the beach ball drops) and prescribes the re-clamp. Works in minutes on anything already written. Use this constantly.

**2. Sharpen a piece that's "about everything" → `/stanton-premise-sentence`**
Distills a draft to one true sentence (character + conflict + conclusion) and audits every element against it. Cuts the paragraphs that are interesting but prove a *different* point. Best on Parallax editions, long LinkedIn posts, and any piece that feels unfocused.

**3. Make a brand / customer / founder feel alive → `/stanton-spine`**
Gives a protagonist a fixed inner wiring + one-liner (want vs. need). For Jen's first-time buyers (want a house, *need* to feel financially safe), MyBPM's wearer, Resonance's dancer, your own founder story. Turns a demographic into a person copy can actually move.

**4. Rescue a stalled or wrong-turn draft → `/stanton-fit-the-dinosaur`**
When you realize halfway through that the piece is actually about something else, this gives you the nerve and a surgical map to refit it instead of scrapping it. Saves work you'd otherwise throw away. The "it's just a file" reframe alone unblocks most stuck rebuilds.

**5. Convert with an emotional arc → `/stanton-sales-arc` + `/stanton-brand-origin`**
The two direct-revenue workflows. `sales-arc` builds a VSL/landing page as customer-spine → clamp → change → surprising+inevitable close (feeds copy-engine/Georgi/Fladlien). `brand-origin` builds a founder/brand story for MyBPM, Resonance, or Jen (feeds build-bos/StoryBrand).

**6. Upgrade how you take feedback → `/stanton-root-not-symptom` + `/stanton-brain-trust`**
`root-not-symptom` triages a pile of notes to the one upstream fix so you don't "change the wrong thing for the wrong reasons" (the *Nemo* prologue lesson). `brain-trust` restructures client/team review into a small, safe, objective circle whose critique makes people *want* to redo the work. Transferable far beyond writing.

## How to drive it

- **Invoke by name:** type `/stanton-clamp-audit` (or any command), then paste the draft / brief. Each workflow loads Stanton's voice, runs real fill-in steps, and ends with a quality gate.
- **Stack in the story chain (never invert):** Runia/story-compass (is there even a story?) → Hawley (season/arc architecture) → **Stanton** (uncover the premise + audit the clamp) → Roth (write the scene/line).
- **Pair across the roster:** `sales-arc` → copy-engine/Georgi/Fladlien · `brand-origin` → build-bos/StoryBrand · `30sec-arc` → Seena Rez/Dara Denney · `series-escalation` → Hawley content-season/parallax · `invoke-wonder` → Ocean Vuong · `telling-over-content` → prose-doctor.
- **Council:** `@andrew-stanton` is now a Content Council seat for `/convene`.

## What it's NOT for (honest limits)

- **Not a from-scratch generator.** It uncovers and engineers; it assumes you have material or at least a topic. Blank-page-to-finished-essay is not the job (use `/parallax`, `/ghostwrite`, copy-engine for production).
- **Not the line / the prose voice.** Stanton sets the arc and the clamp; he does not write the beautiful sentence. Hand the actual prose to Roth, Nicolas Cole, Ocean Vuong, or your own voice rules.
- **Not the story-vs-topic gate.** Stanton assumes a story exists. If you're not sure there's a story at all, run Runia/`story-compass` first.
- **Not season/arc architecture.** For multi-part theme design across a whole series, Hawley runs first; Stanton tunes engagement inside his frame.
- **Not a fact-checker or researcher.** It will not verify claims or source facts. Run `fact-verifier` / `execution/research.py` separately.
- **Not a one-click magic button.** The workflows are diagnostic frameworks. They need your real material and your judgment in the fill-in tables; garbage in, garbage out.
- **Weakest fit:** pure reference/technical docs where emotion genuinely isn't the point. `clamp-audit` and `telling-over-content` can still help an explainer hold attention, but most of the roster won't apply.

## Three quick-start recipes

1. **Flat Parallax draft:** `/stanton-clamp-audit` (find the drops) → `/stanton-premise-sentence` (cut what doesn't serve) → hand to your voice rules for the line.
2. **New MyBPM drop or Jen listing:** `/stanton-spine` (cast the wearer/buyer) → `/stanton-brand-origin` or `/stanton-sales-arc` → copy-engine to execute the blocks.
3. **Stuck client deliverable:** `/stanton-root-not-symptom` (find the real note) → `/stanton-fit-the-dinosaur` (refit on a copy) → `/stanton-brain-trust` for the review.
