# Andrew Stanton — User Guide (v2)

How to use the audience-engineering skill: what it does, the highest-leverage entry points, and where it stops. Companion: `OPERATORS-GUIDE.md` (how the system runs it underneath you). Updated 2026-07-13 after the conductor, the six-engine wiring, and the enrichment pass.

## The one-line version

Stanton is your **story-uncovering + audience-engagement** layer. It tells you what a piece is *really* about (the premise-sentence), who its protagonist is at the core (the spine), where the audience stops paying attention (the clamp), what to fix (the root, not the symptom) — and now it can run **end-to-end production** by conducting your existing engines. "Pixar-level" has an operational definition here: the eight-point **Stanton-Grade Gate** in `references/exemplars.md`.

## Three ways to use it

**1. Do nothing — it already fires.** Stanton is embedded inside six engines: `parallax` (architecture pass + clamp-audit), `linkedin-daily` (premise litmus + line-to-line clamp), `copy-engine` (Phase 6 clamp), `autopilot` (G3 internal clamp), `writers-room` (10th seat: Premise & Clamp Architect), and the depth layer (`/deepen` + `/depth-stack`, co-owner of the architecture deficit). Every edition, post, page, and deepening pass gets the premise check and the engagement audit without you invoking anything.

**2. `/stanton-produce` — the end-to-end front door.** Objective + raw material in, finished piece out: story gate → Stanton architecture (premise/spine/change/arc) → routed to the engine that owns the format → clamp-audit QA → Stanton-Grade Gate → voice/prose/fact gates. It composes your engines; it never rewrites them. Proven run: the Receipt Arc launch sequence (`_active/linkedin-launch/content-os/launch-sequence-stanton.md`).

## The highest-leverage plays (proven this session)

1. **`/stanton-clamp-audit` on anything flat.** Found the one real slip in a strong post (P1-1) in minutes — the "after the best line" drop. Use constantly; it's the cheapest quality lift in the system.
2. **`/stanton-series-escalation` on any content BATCH before sequencing.** Turned 14 pillar-filed posts into one escalating launch argument with a finale. The series premise is usually already in your work, hidden by topic-bucket ordering.
3. **`/stanton-produce` for launches.** The Receipt Arc run shipped a postable order + 13 paste-ready loop lines with bodies untouched — and the gate caught two lines that would have quietly spoiled their own payoff.
4. **`/stanton-spine` for anyone you sell to or for.** Jen's buyers, MyBPM's wearer, Resonance's dancer — want vs. need turns a demographic into a person copy can move.
5. **`/stanton-fit-the-dinosaur` + `/stanton-root-not-symptom` on stuck work.** The rescue pair: refit to the truth you uncovered; fix the one upstream cause instead of the pile of notes.

Six patterns are confirmed by both sources 14 years apart — treat those as load-bearing.

## What "Pixar-level" means here: the Stanton-Grade Gate

Every output should survive all eight (full text in `references/exemplars.md`): a true premise not a topic · never hand them 4 · no machinery on the page · a spined protagonist · no static beat · no vague-where-specific-belongs · an ending that's surprising + inevitable · every opened promise paid. These are **vetoes, not suggestions** — a clean, verified, on-voice piece can still fail. When a run earns your felt PASS, tell the session to append it to the exemplar bank; that's how the skill's taste compounds.

## What it's NOT for (honest limits)

- **Not the prose voice / the line.** Stanton sets the arc and the pull; Roth, Cole, Vuong, or your voice rules write the sentence.
- **Not the story-vs-topic gate.** Run `story-compass` (Runia) first if you're not sure there's a story at all.
- **Not season/theme architecture.** Hawley runs first on multi-part arcs; Stanton tunes engagement inside his frame. Stack order: Runia → Hawley → **Stanton** → Roth.
- **Not a fact-checker or researcher.** `fact-verifier` / `execution/research.py` own that.
- **Weakest fit:** pure reference/technical docs where emotion isn't the point — though `clamp-audit` and `telling-over-content` still help an explainer hold attention.

## Three quick-start recipes

1. **Flat draft:** `/stanton-clamp-audit` → fix the named slips → voice rules for the line.
2. **New launch:** `/stanton-produce` with the objective + raw material → review the gate receipt → ship.
3. **Stuck client deliverable:** `/stanton-root-not-symptom` → `/stanton-fit-the-dinosaur` on a copy → `/stanton-brain-trust` for the review.
