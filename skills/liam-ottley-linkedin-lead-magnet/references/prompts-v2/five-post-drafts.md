## Role & Activation

You are operating in the frame Liam Ottley teaches in "How to Turn LinkedIn into a Personal Brand Lead Magnet with AI." This is the drafting step, and the source method is strict about sequencing: AI is production support, not strategy replacement, and every draft here inherits its strategy from the positioning, arbitrage map, and sprint table that came before it — this prompt does not invent new angles, it executes the five already-chosen highest-leverage ones in full.

- Positioning statement, proof inventory, and voice constraints: [POSITIONING / PROOF / VOICE]
- Paid offer and lead magnet concept (validated via the bridge score): [OFFER / LEAD MAGNET]

## Execution Protocol

1. **Draft exactly these five posts**, each using the structure and lane the source method assigns to it:
   - 1 SLAY story post (Story Lane)
   - 1 PAS conversion post (Conversion Lane)
   - 1 authority framework post (Authority Lane)
   - 1 trend or authority-jacking post (Arbitrage Lane)
   - 1 lead magnet teaser post (Lead Magnet Lane)

2. **For the SLAY post**, follow the four-part structure exactly: Story (open with a specific lived moment or concrete setup — the reader should see themselves in it, not just observe the creator), Lesson (name the business or buyer insight), Advice (tactical steps usable immediately), You (point the ending back to the reader with a question, CTA, or next action).

3. **For the PAS post**, follow the three-part structure exactly: Problem (name the buyer's current stuck state), Agitate (make the cost of leaving it unresolved concrete), Solution (teach the method or offer the diagnostic/asset that helps them move) — and the CTA must be clear, not needy (download, comment, DM, audit, checklist, walkthrough, or book).

4. **Every draft, regardless of structure, must include all six of these components** — a draft missing any one is incomplete:
   - Hook (using one of the named hook raw-material types: exact result, specific number, named mechanism, "how I" not "how to", buyer pain with commercial consequence, or a timely trend translated into a useful take)
   - Rehook (adds specificity, proof, tension, or outcome — never restates the hook or jumps into a generic list)
   - Body (following the assigned structure)
   - CTA (clear, tied to the lead magnet or offer, not needy)
   - "Why this works" (a short note explaining the strategic reasoning — which arbitrage angle, which proof, which buyer trigger)
   - Risk or revision note (what could misfire — tone, specificity, a claim that needs the expert's confirmation)

5. **Keep stories reader-centered, not creator-centered.** The source method is explicit: stories are not about the creator, they are about what the reader sees in themselves — their goal, fear, or future self. Any SLAY or story-lane draft that centers the creator's ego rather than the reader's stakes should be revised before it's presented as final.

## Output Contract

- Exactly five full drafts, one per required structure/lane combination.
- Each draft contains all six components: hook, rehook, body, CTA, why-this-works, risk/revision note.
- The SLAY draft names its Story/Lesson/Advice/You beats explicitly; the PAS draft names its Problem/Agitate/Solution beats explicitly.

## Quality Gate

- Are there exactly five drafts, one per required lane/structure?
- Does every draft carry all six components (hook, rehook, body, CTA, why-this-works, risk note)?
- Does the SLAY draft name its four beats explicitly, and the PAS draft its three?
- Does every hook use a named raw-material type, and every rehook add something beyond restating the hook?
- Are stories framed around the reader's stakes rather than the creator's ego?

## Creative Latitude

The six-component shape and the structure assignments (SLAY vs PAS) are the floor that keeps a draft from shipping as a hollow template. Inside that floor, voice, specific proof points, sentence rhythm, and the exact angle of the story are entirely the model's creative territory — the source material explicitly values variation in sentence length and specificity over formula-following. Push for the story detail that only this expert could tell, the number or mechanism that makes the hook feel earned rather than generic, and a "why this works" note that shows real strategic reasoning rather than restating the structure name. This is the deliverable where the system's floor should be least visible in the finished copy.

## Deploy When

After the 20-post sprint table has been built and (ideally) the five highest-leverage rows selected, and after positioning/proof/voice inputs are available. This produces ready-to-review copy, not a plan — publishing still requires explicit user approval per the source system's default (internal drafts only unless the expert says otherwise).
