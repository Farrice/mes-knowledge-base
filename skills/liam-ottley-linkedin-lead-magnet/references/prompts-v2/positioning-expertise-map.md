## Role & Activation

You are operating in the frame Liam Ottley teaches in "How to Turn LinkedIn into a Personal Brand Lead Magnet with AI": before any content or AI drafting happens, an expert's raw knowledge has to be converted into **monetizable expertise** — knowledge that changes a buyer's outcome in a way they would pay for. The content does not start with "what I know." It starts with "what changes for the buyer if they understand this, and why would that create demand for me?" This prompt produces the positioning and expertise foundation everything else in the system is bridged from — skip it and every downstream hook will "sound manufactured" because the upstream pieces (ICP, outcome, proof, timing) were never made concrete.

## Input Required

- Expert or brand: [NAME / BRAND]
- Offer (what they sell, if anything, today): [OFFER OR "none yet"]
- ICP (who they serve or want to serve): [ICP DESCRIPTION]
- Buyer outcome (the change the buyer is actually paying for): [OUTCOME]
- Proof available (results, case examples, client moments, personal track record, observed process): [PROOF INVENTORY]
- Current LinkedIn stage (dormant / posting inconsistently / active with no lead magnet / active with a weak bridge): [STAGE]
- Existing posts, calls, documents, or swipe examples to mine for raw expertise: [SOURCE MATERIAL OR "none"]

If any of these is missing, proceed with a clearly labeled assumption rather than stalling — but flag every assumption in the output so the expert can correct it.

## Execution Protocol

1. **Write the one-line positioning statement** using the exact template: "I help [ICP] achieve [buyer outcome] through [method]." Do not let this drift into a vague identity line ("I help businesses grow") — it must name a specific ICP and a specific, buyer-felt outcome.

2. **Build the monetizable expertise map: 8-12 knowledge assets.** For each asset, apply the test from the source material directly — "what changes for the buyer if they understand this?" An item that cannot answer that test is not ready to become lead-generating content and should be cut or reworked, not padded in to hit the count.

3. **Build the offer advantage map.** For each advantage, name specifically why this expert's way is different, faster, safer, more specific, or more profitable than the obvious alternative the buyer would otherwise choose. Vague superiority claims ("better results") fail this step — the advantage must be falsifiable/specific.

4. **Build the proof inventory.** List results, examples, assets, client moments, personal story, or observed process the expert can point to. Do not invent proof that was not supplied in Input Required — an empty or thin proof inventory is a real finding to surface, not a gap to paper over.

5. **Apply the quality gate from the source method**: every expertise item must name a buyer outcome AND a commercial consequence (why the buyer's business, income, time, or risk is affected). Cut or flag any item that only names a topic.

## Output Contract

- One-line positioning statement (exact template format).
- Monetizable expertise map: 8-12 items, each with buyer outcome + commercial consequence named.
- Offer advantage map: one advantage per differentiator, each specific and falsifiable.
- Proof inventory: only what was supplied or explicitly marked as a gap.
- An "Assumptions Made" list naming every input that was inferred rather than given.

## Quality Gate

- Does the positioning line name a specific ICP and a specific buyer outcome (not a category)?
- Does every expertise item pass the "what changes for the buyer" test with a named commercial consequence?
- Is every advantage specific enough to be falsifiable, not a generic superiority claim?
- Is the proof inventory honest — no invented results, gaps marked explicitly?
- Are all inferred inputs listed in Assumptions Made rather than silently assumed?

## Deploy When

The expert has knowledge or a track record but has not yet translated it into buyer-outcome language — before any hook, post, or content lane work begins. This is the required first step in the Liam Ottley operating plan; downstream prompts (arbitrage map, content sprint, drafts) assume this positioning foundation exists.
