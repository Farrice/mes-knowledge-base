# The Strategic Wrongness Navigator — Stake the Position Before the Crowd Arrives (Lulu Cheng Meservey)

"Go direct" wasn't controversial. It wasn't even a new word — Brian Armstrong told Lulu "just use it, it already exists." It was *early*. The whole skill of strategic wrongness is not being edgy; it's being right before it's safe to be right — naming the thing everyone is thinking and nobody is saying yet, then owning the language so that "by the time everybody catches up, we own it." This workflow finds where the consensus is quietly going false, scores the openings, and picks the one calculated contrarian position you can plant a flag on. Then it does the part most "hot take" generators skip: it builds the conviction backing — the lived experience, the evidence, the line-in-the-sand — so the position survives the months between when you say it and when the world (and the language models trained on the old consensus) finally agree. The point is not to be wrong. The point is to be *early*, and to have the spine to hold it. AI can't do that part: it has no conviction. "Screw it, this is what I believe" is the thing that makes writing sharp enough to pierce culture, and it's the one thing a model averaging the consensus will sand right off.

## Pre-Flight

Read before executing — load these `genius.md` sections (do not paraphrase from memory; the patterns are precise, and this workflow is dangerous *because* manufactured contrarianism reads as fake the instant the seam shows):
- **Pattern 14 — The Trajectory Arbitrage (Play Where the Puck Is Going)** — the core timing engine: form a thesis on where a cultural zone is *heading*, arrive "early but not so early as to be irrelevant," and either make a concentrated high-conviction bet or dollar-cost-average. The reframe that removes the fear: "the posts that flopped — by definition, nobody saw."
- **Pattern 15 — The Naming-Gives-Shape Move (Solidify the Fluid)** — the position must name a thing your field is *already feeling but can't articulate*. It "shouldn't be completely unfamiliar; it should have familiarity, but you're giving it shape." You usually don't need a brand-new term — reshape an existing one in your image.
- **Pattern 16 — The Experience Monopoly (Speak From What Only You Lived)** — the conviction backing: anchor the position in lived, first-hand experience so it "gives you a monopoly." Opinions are commodities; experiences are not. This is also why AI can't hold the position for you.
- **Tacit Knowledge #2 — The Template Half-Life** — the timing guard: a contrarian take has a decaying window. "Founder mode" went from catharsis to cringe. Once 3+ competitors echo your position, the early-mover advantage is gone — you've become consensus, which is the *goal*, but it means it's time for the next bet.
- **Decision Framework** — the gate below.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` before staking anything. Specifically confirm:
> - **Q5 (trajectory):** the position attaches to a receptor the audience *already has open* and is *rising* — you are playing where the puck is going, not installing a foreign obsession or reheating a burned take. A contrarian position the audience has no latent receptor for isn't brave, it's irrelevant.
> - **Q3 (first-person monopoly):** there is a specific human who can say this and be believed — ideally backed by lived experience only they have (Pattern 16). A contrarian take from "a spokesperson" is discounted 90%+ and has no spine to hold.
> - **Q6 (gerrymandered line):** the wrongness draws a line that puts your tribe — customers, employees, the people who'd *want* this to be true — on the inside, and only legacy players / the "old way" outside it. If the line splits your own base, redraw it (us-who-see-it vs. them-who-don't), not (faction A vs. faction B).
> - **Q1 (business goal):** the eventual being-proven-right converts into recruiting / fundraising / sales / regulatory — not just "being known as the person who called it." Being early with no conversion built is a dopamine sugar high in slow motion.
>
> **Honesty spine:** the *position* can be contrarian; the *evidence under it* must be real. Strategic wrongness means betting the consensus is wrong, not fabricating data to make it look wrong. The whole bet pays off only if you're *actually* right — fake proof gets exposed the moment the position gets attention, and you get one bullet.

## Input Required

- **Industry / domain** — the space whose consensus you're stress-testing. Be specific (not "marketing" but "B2B SaaS demand-gen," not "fitness" but "online strength coaching for desk workers").
- **Who you want to influence** — the tribe. The people who, when you stake the position, should feel "finally, someone said it." Not the whole market — the pocket whose receptor is open.
- **Your standing to speak** — why you, specifically, can hold this position. Credentials matter less than *lived experience* (Pattern 16): the thing you watched happen, the bet you made, the scar. "If you're saying something a lot of other people could say, there's no reason they should buy that message from you."
- **The goal it converts into** — book deal / category creation / client attraction / fundraising / recruiting. The thing being-proven-right cashes out as.
- **Known frustrations with the status quo** — what already bothers you about how the space does things. This is the richest seam; your felt-irritation is usually a trajectory signal others haven't articulated yet.
- **Timing read (optional)** — anything you already sense is *shifting* in the space (a technology, a regulation, an exhaustion, a generational handoff). Trajectory is the whole game; if you have a read, surface it.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Map the consensus: 10-15 "truths" everyone repeats (Consensus Mapping)

A contrarian position is only contrarian relative to a named consensus. So first, inventory what "everyone knows." Don't editorialize yet — just collect the air everyone breathes. Use these five intake prompts to surface 10-15 beliefs; the goal is breadth, including the ones so obvious they feel beneath stating (those are often where the richest wrongness hides).

| Intake prompt | What it surfaces | Example (online strength coaching) |
|---|---|---|
| What do experts repeat until it's cliché? | The mantras nobody questions | "Progressive overload is everything." |
| What advice do beginners get that no one challenges? | The default onboarding dogma | "You need a personalized program." |
| What metric / outcome does everyone optimize for? | The false proxy the whole field chases | "Total followers / reach is how you grow." |
| What "best practice" has gone universal? | The copied playbook | "Post daily, be everywhere." |
| What would get someone *criticized* for questioning? | The third-rail belief (highest-value seam) | "More volume = more results." |

Write each as a flat declarative sentence — the way a true believer would say it. You're not arguing yet; you're loading the board.

> **The "what is everyone thinking but nobody saying" probe.** Run it across the whole list. Lulu's sharpest move isn't finding a belief to *attack* — it's finding the thing the room is privately uneasy about but nobody will say out loud (the cultural erogenous zone, Pattern 8). Ask: *which of these does the smartest person in my field secretly doubt at 2am but would never post?* That belief — quietly cracking, publicly defended — is your richest target. Remember: it doesn't have to be controversial. "Go direct" wasn't. It just had to be the true thing nobody had given shape to yet.

### Step 2 — Stress-test each belief: where and why is it going false? (Belief Stress-Testing)

Now interrogate the board. For each consensus truth, run the five-question stress test. The single most important column is **trajectory** — you're not looking for beliefs that are *currently* false (everyone already knows those); you're looking for beliefs that are *becoming* false. That's where the puck is going.

| Stress-test question | What it diagnoses |
|---|---|
| Is this actually true, or just commonly repeated? | Genuine truth vs. inherited cliché |
| Under what conditions is it false? | The crack — the context where it breaks |
| Who benefits from it staying unchallenged? | The incumbent interest defending it (agencies sell reach, platforms sell impressions) |
| What evidence already contradicts it? | The proof you'll need for the conviction backing |
| **Is it becoming *less* true over time?** | **Trajectory — the only question that matters most** |

Tag each belief's trajectory:
- **STILL TRUE** — leave it alone; betting against a load-bearing truth is just being wrong-wrong, not strategically wrong. (In the mine's worked example, "email has best ROI" stress-tested to *still true, actually* — Lulu's discipline is to admit it and move on.)
- **WEAKENING** — going false but the crowd hasn't noticed. *This is the target zone.*
- **RISING-BUT-CONTESTED** — a newer belief that's not as universal as its evangelists claim. A contrarian "it doesn't actually work for X" can be strong here.
- **ALREADY-FALSE-AND-KNOWN** — too late; the crowd's already there, no arbitrage left.

The "WEAKENING" beliefs are your candidate positions. You are looking for the one where you can credibly say: *this is true today, but I can see it's going false, and I'm going to plant my flag on the other side now so I own the language when everyone else arrives.*

### Step 3 — Score the wrongness-opportunities (Wrongness Opportunity Scoring)

Take your WEAKENING and RISING-BUT-CONTESTED candidates (usually 3-6 of the 10-15) and score each on six dimensions, 1-5. This is the discipline that separates a calculated bet from a reckless hot take. Total out of 30; **25+ is a stakeable position.**

| Dimension | Scores LOW (1-2) when… | Scores HIGH (4-5) when… | Maps to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Divergence** | barely different from consensus | genuinely the *opposite* of standard advice | (the wrongness itself) |
| **Provability** | you'd be asserting on vibes | you have real data / cases / a clear mechanism | Honesty spine + Pattern 16 |
| **Trajectory** | the trend is moving *against* you | the puck is moving *toward* your position | Pattern 14 |
| **Resonance** | nobody secretly wants this true | people will feel *relief / permission* ("finally") | Pattern 15 |
| **Uniqueness** | many could say this; you can't own it | only you can say it; you can own the language | Pattern 16 |
| **Risk-Reward** | credibility downside ≥ upside | low downside (early, by-definition-unseen if it flops), high upside | Pattern 14 reframe |

> **The trajectory-resonance multiplier.** Two columns carry disproportionate weight. **Trajectory** because being right *eventually* is the entire payoff — a high-divergence position moving the wrong way is just a wrong opinion you'll have to walk back. **Resonance** because a position with no latent receptor isn't contrarian, it's alienating; the win-state is people feeling *catharsis and permission* (the "founder mode" effect — it taught founders nothing new, it gave shape and relief to what they already felt), not being lectured at. A position can survive a mediocre Divergence score; it cannot survive a low Trajectory or low Resonance score. If your top candidate is high-Divergence but low on those two, you have a *spicy* take, not a *strategic* one — go back to Step 2.

Select the highest scorer (25+). If nothing clears 25, the honest answer is *you don't have a strategic-wrongness position yet* — say so, and either deepen the stress-test or pick the dollar-cost-average path (Step 6).

### Step 4 — Name it: give the fluid feeling a shape (Pattern 15)

You have the position. Now make it a *thing people can grab and repeat in their own words*. A position stated as a paragraph dissipates; a position with a name spreads. This is the naming-gives-shape move — and the discipline is restraint:

- **Name what they already feel, don't invent a foreign concept.** The name should land with *familiarity* — "you're giving it shape," not teaching a new vocabulary word. The mine's worked example doesn't coin a neologism; it reshapes two words everyone already knows into a fixed opposition: **"Resonance > Reach."** That's the whole move. (See also: "founder mode," "time to build," "go direct" — none were new ideas, all were giving shape to a felt thing.)
- **You usually don't need a brand-new term.** Often the strongest name is an existing phrase, *reshaped in your image* with your idea attached — Brian Armstrong on "go direct": "just use it, it already exists." Reach for the new coinage only if no existing phrase fits.
- **Fix the slogan; vary the stories (Pattern 13).** Pick the 3-5 words that must cut through, and repeat *those exact words* across every surface while the examples and context change every time. "Do you want people to remember one thing or zero things?"

```
THE POSITION (one flat sentence): __________
THE NAME (the few fixed words people will repeat): __________
THE FELT THING IT GIVES SHAPE TO: "[what your tribe already feels but couldn't say]"
THE RELIEF / PERMISSION IT GRANTS: "finally, _________"
```

### Step 5 — Build the conviction backing (Pattern 16 + Evidence Architecture)

This is the step that lets you *hold* the position when the world says you're wrong — and the step AI structurally cannot do for you, because it has no conviction and no lived experience to draw on. The backing has two layers: the *evidence* (so the position is defensible) and the *experience* (so the position is *yours*).

**5a — Evidence architecture (the defensible spine).** Assemble:
- **3-5 data points** that already contradict the consensus (from your Step 2 "evidence" column). Real numbers, sourced. These must be *true* — strategic wrongness is betting the consensus is wrong, never faking that it is.
- **2-3 concrete examples** illustrating the principle in the wild (a company, a case, a moment).
- **The mechanism** — the *why*. Not "consensus is wrong" but "consensus is wrong *because* [structural reason]." (Mine example: "broad reach attracts browsers; narrow reach attracts buyers. When you optimize for reach, you dilute message potency.") The mechanism is what makes it a *position* rather than a *contrarian feeling*.
- **The top 3 objections + responses** — pre-loaded, because you *will* be challenged. (Below.)

**5b — The experience monopoly (the spine that's yours alone).** Find the lived, first-hand experience that anchors the position to you specifically. "It gives you a monopoly — makes you the number one expert in the world on something really small and specific." The mine's manifesto pivots on exactly this: *"I watched a company I cared about blow $2M on a 'reach expansion' campaign... That experience radicalized me."* That sentence is the conviction. A model can generate the data points; it cannot generate the scar.
- *Guard (Pattern 16):* do NOT let this curdle into "I spent 487 hours so you don't have to" engagement-farm slop. The experience is told because it's *true and load-bearing*, not as bot-bait.

**5c — The hold-the-line doctrine.** Write the one sentence you'll repeat to yourself (and to critics) in the months before the consensus catches up. The position is contrarian *now*; it pays off *later*. The discomfort in between is the price of the arbitrage. ("I could keep this for clients. It would be safer. But—" is the mine's version: naming the safer path, then refusing it.)

### Step 6 — Pick the deployment lane: concentrated bet vs. dollar-cost-average (Pattern 14)

How you stake the position depends on your conviction level and your tolerance for the wait:

| Lane | When to use | How it runs |
|---|---|---|
| **(A) High-conviction concentrated bet** | Score 27+, deep experience monopoly, you can *see* the puck clearly | Prepare heavily, stake the full position (manifesto + named slogan + evidence), double down, "get here and own this so by the time everybody catches up, we own it." One big, well-built flag. |
| **(B) Dollar-cost-average** | Score 25-26, or you're earlier than you can fully defend, or you want to read the market first | Publish the position constantly in low-stakes shots; when one hits, read the feedback and refine the language (the Jobs "many iterations" path). The fear-killer: "the posts that flopped — by definition, nobody saw. It's lower risk than people think." |

> **The half-life exit (Tacit Knowledge #2).** Whichever lane: track adoption. The day 3+ others are echoing your exact position is the day you've *won* the arbitrage — the contrarian take has become consensus, which was always the goal. But it also means the early-mover edge is gone and it's time to map the *next* weakening belief. A position held past its half-life curdles ("founder mode" → cringe). Owning the language is the win condition *and* the exit signal.

### Step 7 — Don't let AI sand the wrongness out (the conviction guard)

If you draft or refine any of the output with a model, run this guard before shipping. Models are trained on the consensus you're betting against, so they will instinctively *regress your position toward the mean* — softening the line, hedging the claim, adding "of course, both have their place," smoothing the broken-grammar conviction into textbook-correct mush. "Sometimes it actually takes away the wrongness that is the point."

- **The flatten check:** did the edit add hedges ("it depends," "to be fair," "both sides")? A strategic-wrongness position that hedges is no longer a position. Restore the line.
- **The conviction check:** does at least one sentence still read as "screw it, this is what I believe" — sharp enough to pierce? If the model rounded off every hard edge, it rounded off the whole point.
- **The "bad and honest beats textbook and dead" check:** if the model's version is more *correct* but less *alive*, keep yours. "I would rather see writing that is suboptimal, that has personality, that has intent and has conviction than writing that is textbook correct."
- **Don't fix the manifesto into compliance.** Manifestos get *worse* when AI "fixes" them. Tune for clarity, never for safety.

### Worked example A — Online strength coaching (client attraction)

**Domain:** online strength coaching for desk workers. · **Tribe:** lifters 30-45 who've "done everything right" and stalled. · **Converts into:** client attraction + a paid program. · **Experience monopoly:** "I ran the high-volume protocol on 200 clients for three years and watched the ones who *cut* their training in half outprogress the ones who added more."

> **Consensus map (excerpt):** "More volume = more results" → **WEAKENING** (defended by every program-seller who profits from longer programs; recovery science increasingly contradicts it for time-constrained adults). · "You need a personalized program" → RISING-CONTESTED. · "Progressive overload is everything" → STILL TRUE (leave it). · "Post daily to grow" → ALREADY-KNOWN-false.

> **Wrongness scores:** "Less volume, more recovery, more results" — Diverg 5, Prov 4 (200-client dataset + recovery research), Traject 5 (busy-adult exhaustion is rising), Reson 5 (every burned-out lifter *wants* permission to do less), Uniq 4 (the lived dataset is mine), Risk-Rew 5 (if it flops, by definition nobody saw). **TOTAL: 28/30 → STAKE IT.**

> **The name:** **"Train Less, Recover More."** Gives shape to what they already feel — *"I'm exhausted and adding more isn't working"* — and grants permission: "finally, someone said the grind is the problem, not the proof."
>
> **Line in the sand:** INSIDE = time-constrained adults who want strength *and* a life. OUTSIDE = the more-is-better grind cult (not other coaches — the *ideology*; that's the gerrymander).
>

Then I watched my most disciplined client — the one who never missed a session — get *weaker* for a year while overtrained and under-recovered. >

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review output against `genius.md § Lulu Cheng Meservey Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric. Flag and fix before delivering.

- **Trajectory, not just divergence (Pattern 14, Q5):** the staked position attaches to a *rising* receptor — you're playing where the puck is going, not being edgy for its own sake or reheating a burned take. A high-Divergence, low-Trajectory position is a wrong opinion, not a strategic one. The position is *contrarian-now, right-later*, not contrarian-forever.
- **Names a felt thing, doesn't install a foreign one (Pattern 15):** the position gives *shape and permission* to something the tribe already feels ("finally") — it doesn't lecture them into a stance they have no receptor for. If readers' reaction would be "huh?" rather than "yes, exactly," it has no resonance.
- **Conviction backing is real (Pattern 16 + honesty spine):** the position is anchored in a lived experience only this person has, and the supporting data is *actually true*. Strategic wrongness bets the consensus is wrong; it never *fakes* that it is. Strip the experience monopoly and ask: could a model have written this from the consensus? If yes, there's no spine to hold the line.
- **The line is gerrymandered around the tribe (Q6, AN-8):** INSIDE unites customers + employees + believers; OUTSIDE is only the legacy "old way" / non-targets — never a split of your own base. The enemy is an *ideology*, not a faction of your people.
- **The wrongness survived the edit (Step 7):** the position still reads as conviction — at least one sentence is "screw it, this is what I believe." No hedges crept in to round off the line. Bad-and-honest beats textbook-and-dead.
- **A name exists and is fixed (Pattern 13, Pattern 15):** there's a 3-5 word slogan the tribe can repeat in their own words, and it's the same words across surfaces. One thing remembered, not ten things blurred.
- **It converts (Q1):** being-proven-right cashes out as a named business goal, with at least a sketch of the path. Being known as "the person who called it" with nothing to convert into is a slow-motion dopamine treadmill.
- **Scored, not asserted:** the winning position cleared 25/30 on the six dimensions. If nothing cleared 25, the output honestly says "no stakeable position yet" and routes to deeper stress-test or the dollar-cost-average lane — it does not manufacture a fake flag to have something to ship.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Spicy ≠ strategic — picking the most *divergent* take instead of the most *right-soon* one.** The loudest contrarian position often scores high on Divergence and low on Trajectory and Provability — it gets attention, then gets you exposed when the puck doesn't arrive. **Recovery:** re-run Step 3 and weight Trajectory and Resonance hardest. If your winner is high-Divergence but low on those, you have a hot take, not a position. The goal is to be *early and right*, not *loud and wrong*.
- **Manufacturing wrongness with no real receptor or no real proof.** Inventing a contrarian stance the audience has no latent feeling for (it reads as alienating, not brave) — or worse, bending data to make a still-true consensus *look* false. **Recovery:** for the receptor problem, return to Step 1's "what is everyone thinking but nobody saying" probe — the position must give shape to a *felt* thing. For the proof problem, honor the honesty spine: if the consensus isn't actually weakening, it's not a strategic-wrongness target. Drop it; betting against a load-bearing truth is just being wrong.
- **No conviction backing — a position you can't hold when challenged.** Staking a flag with no lived experience and no pre-loaded objection responses, then folding the first time someone pushes back. The arbitrage *requires* you to sit in being-called-wrong for months. **Recovery:** do not ship without Step 5 complete — the experience monopoly (the scar only you have) and the hold-the-line sentence. If you can't write the scar, you don't own the position; either find your real lived stake or pick a different one.
- **Letting the model regress the position to the mean.** Drafting with AI and shipping its "improved" version, which has quietly hedged the line, balanced both sides, and sanded off the conviction — because the model is trained on the very consensus you're betting against. **Recovery:** run Step 7's flatten check. Restore every hedge the model added back to a hard line. Manifestos get *worse* when AI fixes them; tune for clarity, never for safety. The "screw it, this is what I believe" is the whole point — guard it.
