# Viral Trajectory Predictor — Time the Market, Not the Take (Lulu Cheng Meservey)

The difference between a post that 10x's and the same post that lands flat is usually not the writing. It's the *week*. "You always want to play to where it's going, not where it is today" — Lulu times cultural entry the way a good investor times a market: not by reacting to the price today, but by forming a thesis on where the price is *going* and getting there before the crowd. The catch is that a slow gauge will betray you — a peaking topic can flip to burnout in under 36 hours of mass adoption, and "founder mode" went from electric to ironically-cringe in roughly that window. So this workflow does one job and does it cold: take the topics you're *already* considering, read each one's trajectory off six concrete signals, and net the timing window against the one variable most timing advice ignores — *how long it takes you to ship*. A two-week window on a topic with a three-day production cycle is a GO. The identical window on a topic that takes you three weeks to produce is a KILL, no matter how hot it is, because you'll arrive at the burnout. This is the timing engine, not the receptor radar (`lulu-erogenous-zones` tells you *which* zones are charged; this tells you *when to fire*) and not the content build (`lulu-zeitgeist-content` writes the post). It answers exactly one question: **of the topics in front of me, which do I post now, which do I wait on, and which do I never touch — given how fast I can actually ship?**

- **Pattern 14 — The Trajectory Arbitrage (Play Where the Puck Is Going)** — the core move. Form a thesis on where a zone is *heading*, then pick a lane: **(A) high-conviction arbitrage** (spot the thing people are thinking-and-not-saying, prepare heavily, double down, own it before the crowd) or **(B) dollar-cost averaging** (publish constantly, low effort per shot, refine on what hits). The fear-killer: "the posts that flopped — by definition, nobody saw. It's lower risk than people think."
- **Pattern 8 — The Cultural Erogenous Zone Radar** — *what* you're timing. - **Tacit Knowledge #2 — The Template Half-Life** — the decay clock. Every format that goes viral has a rapidly-decaying window: Week 1–2 max impact, Week 3–4 competition emerging, Week 5–6 fatigue, Week 7+ burned-exit-immediately. The named-zone clock at peak virality runs *faster than that* — days, not weeks (Devon-launch copied tweet-for-tweet within weeks; manifesto glut; sizzle-reel sameness; the Tolkien-name glut; the "class of Avas"). Three-plus competitors on the same format = 2–3 weeks before effectiveness collapses.

## Input Required

- **Candidate topics** — the 3–6 topics you are *actually* weighing posting about. Specific, not categories. Not "AI" but "AI agents vs. assistants," "the GPT-wrapper death," "prompt engineering is over." The slate is the unit of work; this is a *comparative* timing call across a real shortlist.
- **Your production lead time** — the single most important and most-skipped input. How many days from *decide-to-publish* to *actually-live*, honestly measured. A LinkedIn post you can ship in 2 hours and a researched newsletter that takes you 10 days are *completely different* timing animals on the same topic. If it varies by format, give the range per format.
- **Domain / audience** — specific enough that "high-status carrier" and "the question is being asked" mean something. "Seed-stage technical founders on X / Hacker News," not "tech people."
- **Where you publish** — the channels and their real pockets (LinkedIn, X, a specific newsletter, a niche podcast). Trajectory reads differently per pocket; a topic peaked on X can be rising on LinkedIn (the lag is the arbitrage).
- **Your lane preference** (optional) — high-conviction arbitrage (you can prepare heavily and double down on one bet) vs. dollar-cost-average (you publish constantly and cheaply). If unstated, the workflow recommends a lane per topic based on its phase and your lead time.
- **Live-signal access** (optional) — whether you can actually check search trends / social velocity right now, or are reasoning from known state. Drives whether Step 2's signals are *measured* or *estimated* — and the output must label which.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Establish the trajectory frame: thesis on where it's GOING, not where it IS

Before reading any topic, fix the mental model, because it's the one most people get backwards. You are not scoring how hot a topic is *today*. Today's heat is the *price* — and by the time a topic is unmistakably hot to you, the early money is already in. You're scoring where the topic will be **when your content actually lands**, which is today + your production lead time. That single shift — from present heat to future heat-at-publish — is the whole technique.

State, in one line per topic, your **trajectory thesis**: *"By the time I ship (T+[lead time]), this topic will be in the ___ phase, because ___."* This forces the prediction to the front, the way Lulu times to where the puck is going. Everything downstream either confirms or breaks this thesis.

### Step 2 — Read the six trajectory signals per topic (the instrument panel)

Phase is not a vibe; it's a reading off six instruments. Score each topic on all six. Where you have live access, *measure*; where you don't, *estimate and label it [est]* — a confident phase call built on six guessed signals is theater (the cardinal sin is faking the instrument readings).

| Signal | The question | RISING reads like… | PEAKED/BURNED reads like… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Search trend** | Is the curve climbing, plateauing, or falling? | Slope up and to the right, off a low base | Plateau at a high level, or rolling over downward |
| **Social velocity** | Is the *daily* mention count accelerating? | Day-over-day mentions still increasing | Mentions flat/declining; the spike already happened |
| **Status adoption** | Are high-status accounts posting *for the first time*? | Respected names just starting to weigh in | Status accounts already moved on, or never touched it (orphan) |
| **Question density** | Are "what is X? / how does X work?" queries rising? | Genuine confusion — "what exactly *is* an AI agent?" | Nobody's asking anymore; it's assumed knowledge / explained to death |
| **Competition density** | How much *quality* content already exists? | Thin — the framework isn't claimed yet | Saturated — 50+ good threads, every angle covered |
| **Counter-narratives** | Are "actually, X is wrong / X is dead" takes appearing? | **Absent** (a rising tell — nobody's bothered to push back yet) | **Present and multiplying** ("prompt engineering is dead," ironic usage) |

> **The counter-narrative inversion (the most diagnostic single signal):** the appearance of "actually, X is overhyped / X is dead" takes is the clearest marker that a topic has crossed from rising into peaking-or-past. Counter-narratives only emerge once a topic is established enough to be *worth* contradicting. **Absence of counter-narratives on a charged topic = still rising = the window is open.** First wave of counter-narratives = peak is here or just past, enter only to differentiate. Counter-narrative-as-meme (ironic) = burnout, exit. Read this signal first; it often settles the phase call before the other five.

### Step 3 — Identify the phase (and clock the half-life)

Roll the six signals into one phase call per topic. The phases are a lifecycle, and each one has a *different verb*:

| Phase | What the signals show | The verb | Half-life note (Tacit #2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FORMATION** | Insider circles only; needs explanation; zero mainstream; counter-narratives absent; question density low (nobody's asking because nobody knows to) | **Stake an early flag** (high-conviction lane) — or skip if lead time can't reach it before it moves | Weeks of runway, but illegible to the mainstream; the arbitrage is *patience* |
| **RISING** | Spreading past insiders; first mainstream mentions; question density climbing; status accounts just arriving; competition thin; **counter-narratives still absent** | **GO — own the language now** | The prize window. 2–4 weeks typical; less for a fast-moving named zone |
| **PEAKING** | Everyone discussing; maximum volume; competition heavy; first counter-narratives appearing; language fixed | **Differentiate or wait** — ride only with same-day speed | Days, not weeks. The 36-hour clock is live |
| **DECLINING** | Volume falling; smart money moved on; "we've covered this" fatigue; question density dropping | **WAIT / pivot to adjacent** | The puck has left; salvage only via a fresh adjacent angle |
| **BURNOUT** | Ironic/meme usage; feels dated; cringe associations; the format is a punchline ("class of Avas," manifesto glut) | **KILL — never publish here** | Dead. Arriving here actively *lowers* you |

> **The template-half-life overlay:** distinguish the *topic's* phase from the *format's* phase — they decay on separate clocks and either one can kill you. A rising topic delivered in a burned format (the manifesto when manifestos are glutted, the sizzle reel when every launch has one, the Devon-launch tweet structure copied tweet-for-tweet, a Tolkien-name product in the name-glut) reads as late even though the *topic* is early. **Three-plus competitors on the same format = 2–3 weeks before it collapses; exit the format even if you stay on the topic.** When you call a phase, call it for *topic × format*, not topic alone.

### Step 4 — Net the window against YOUR production lead time (the step everyone skips)

This is where the workflow earns its keep. A timing window in the abstract is useless; what matters is the window *minus how long it takes you to ship*. Compute it explicitly:

```
EFFECTIVE WINDOW = (days until the phase degrades) − (your production lead time)
```

- **Effective window comfortably positive** → you can produce and still land inside the live phase. Proceed.
- **Effective window near zero or negative** → you will *arrive at the burnout*. The topic is hot but it is **not hot for you** — by the time you ship, the 36-hour clock has run. KILL or downgrade to a faster format.
- **The format escape hatch:** if the topic is GO but your default format's lead time blows the window, *change the format, not the verdict*. A 10-day newsletter on a 4-day window becomes a 2-hour X post on the same topic. Match the production speed to the window the topic gives you — this is the single most common rescue.

| Phase at decision time | Lead time vs. window | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| RISING | Lead time ≪ window | **GO** — full production, own the framework |
| RISING | Lead time ≈ window | **GO, fast format only** — ship lean or miss it |
| FORMATION | Any (you have runway) | **GO if high-conviction lane** — prepare heavily, stake the flag, double down |
| PEAKING | Lead time ≪ window AND you have a genuinely differentiated angle | **GO to DIFFERENTIATE only** — same-day speed, contrarian or exclusive data |
| PEAKING | Lead time ≥ a day or two | **WAIT for the adjacent / second wave** — you'll arrive at burnout |
| DECLINING | — | **WAIT** — pivot to a rising adjacent angle (Step 5) or hold |
| BURNOUT | — | **KILL** — never, regardless of window |

### Step 5 — For WAIT/KILL topics, hunt the rising adjacent (don't waste the charge)

A peaked or burned topic still sits on a *charged zone* (Pattern 8) — the energy is real, the entry point is just spent. Don't abandon the charge; find the rising adjacent that lets you ride the same emotional current from an open receptor. For each WAIT/KILL topic, scan four adjacent vectors:

| Adjacent vector | The question | Example (from a burned "Custom GPTs" topic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Emerging subtopic** | What narrower thing is just starting to form underneath? | "What to build *instead of* GPT wrappers" |
| **Fresh counter-narrative** | Which "actually, X" take has rising energy of its own? | "The GPT-wrapper startup is already dead" |
| **Underserved segment** | Which audience slice isn't being spoken to yet? | Solo founders who *just* shipped a wrapper |
| **Second-wave conversation** | What's the *next* question this topic forces, that nobody's answered? | "How do you compete when OpenAI can clone you in a weekend?" |

The selection rule for an adjacent: it must itself pass the rising test (counter-narratives absent, question density climbing) *and* still attach to the original charge. The best adjacents are usually the second-wave conversation — the question the now-peaked topic *creates* but hasn't resolved.

### Step 6 — Assign the lane and render the verdict

Pull it together into a per-topic verdict, and assign the lane (Pattern 14):

- **High-conviction arbitrage lane** — for FORMATION/early-RISING topics where you can prepare heavily, double down, and own the language before the crowd. Higher prep cost, concentrated bet, biggest upside. Use when you have a real thesis and the lead time to stake the flag early.
- **Dollar-cost-average lane** — for everything else, and as the *default posture between bets*. Publish constantly, cheap per shot, read the feedback, refine on what hits. Use when you can't predict which topic pops, or your lead time is short and your format light.

> **The flops-are-invisible reframe (the fear-killer, state it in the output):** the dollar-cost-average lane terrifies people because they imagine every miss is a public embarrassment. It isn't. "The posts that flopped — by definition, nobody saw." A flop has no audience; that's what *makes* it a flop. So the downside of a cheap-shot miss is ~zero and the upside of a hit is large — which means iterating fearlessly is the *low*-risk strategy, not the high-risk one. Bake this into any DCA recommendation so the operator actually fires the shots instead of agonizing over each one.

The verdict per topic is one of: **GO NOW** (rising, window clears your lead time), **GO TO DIFFERENTIATE** (peaking but you have a contrarian/exclusive angle and same-day speed), **WAIT** (declining/peaked-no-angle — hold or pivot to the rising adjacent from Step 5), **KILL** (burned — never).

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Social (LinkedIn / X)** | Lead time is hours, so the effective window is almost always positive — social's whole edge is that you can ride PEAKING topics with same-day speed that long formats can't. Default to the **dollar-cost-average lane**: cheap shots, constant, refine on hits, and *say the flops-are-invisible thing out loud* so the operator fires freely. Read trajectory per-pocket — a topic peaked on X is often still rising on LinkedIn (post the X-burned topic to LinkedIn while the lag holds). The 36-hour clock is a *social-native* risk; re-check the counter-narrative signal the morning you post. |
| **Marketing / brand** | Long lead times (campaigns, not posts), so this is a **high-conviction-arbitrage** instrument: you can't react to PEAKING (you'll arrive at burnout), so you must bet on FORMATION/early-RISING and prepare to *own the language* by the time the campaign lands. Net the window against the *campaign* lead time ruthlessly — a 6-week build can only target a topic with 8+ weeks of runway, i.e. formation. Distinguish topic-phase from *format*-phase hard here: a campaign in a burned format (the manifesto glut, sizzle-reel sameness) is late even on a fresh topic. |
| **Copywriting (VSL / landing / email)** | Trajectory governs the *recognition hook* and the proof's freshness, not the offer. Time the *angle*, not the product — wrap the evergreen offer in a rising cultural angle so the hook reads "finally someone's saying this" instead of "here's another pitch." Email's short lead time lets you ride peaking angles; a long-build VSL must anchor to a topic with real runway or its hook will read dated on launch. KILL any angle built on a burned format (the "I spent 487 hours so you don't have to" hook is itself a burnout signal). |
| **Ghostwriting** | Time the topics *the client* can speak to from experience-monopoly authority — a rising topic the client has lived something real adjacent to. The client's *own* pocket sets the trajectory read (where do THEY get information, who are THEIR high-status peers). High-conviction arbitrage suits a client building a durable position; dollar-cost-average suits a client testing voice. Never time a client onto a topic just because it's rising — bandwagoning without experience is the discounted-paid-messenger failure (AN-7). |
| **Content / media (newsletter, series, podcast)** | The headline use of the whole workflow. Run a *portfolio across phases*: one FORMATION bet to own (high-conviction), one RISING to ride for reach, plus DCA filler between issues. Net every topic against the *issue cadence* — a weekly newsletter has ~7 days of lead, so a 4-day-window peaking topic is a KILL for the main slot but a GO for a same-week social teaser. Re-run the radar *every cycle* — the template half-life means last issue's hot format may be this issue's glut. |

### Worked example B — Production lead time KILLS a hot topic (the netting catch)

**Setup:** a brand-marketing team is weighing a campaign around **"founder mode"** — surface read: *enormous* intensity, everyone's posting it, looks like a slam-dunk to build a brand campaign on. Lead time for the campaign: **~4 weeks**.

**Six-signal read:** Search ↑ (still climbing). Social velocity ↑ (huge). Status adoption: peak — every founder posting. Question density: dropping (everyone already knows what it means). Competition: saturating fast. **Counter-narratives: just emerging** ("founder mode is just an excuse for micromanagement"). → **PHASE: PEAKING, hours from the 36-hour flip.**

**The netting catch:** window until burnout ≈ **1–2 days** (a named zone at peak virality runs on the fast clock; founder-mode flipped electric-to-cringe in under 36 hours). Lead time = **4 weeks**. **Effective window = 1 day − 28 days = catastrophically negative.** By the time the campaign ships, "founder mode" will be a burned meme and the brand will have arrived at the exact cringe. The raw heat is real — but it is *not heat for a 4-week-lead format*.

**Verdict: KILL for the campaign.** The format escape hatch *cannot* save it (you can't compress a brand campaign to same-day). **Rising adjacent rescue:** instead of the burned named-zone, target the *durable underlying charge* — the un-burnable status anxiety beneath "founder mode" ("am I building a company or a job?") — which is a FORMATION/early-RISING zone with weeks of runway, perfectly matched to a 4-week build. The radar's job was exactly this: override the "it's so hot!" surface read by netting the window against the real lead time, and route the campaign to a topic whose window the team can actually reach. *(Had the same topic come to a 2-hour LinkedIn post instead, the verdict flips to GO-DIFFERENTIATE same-day — the lead time, not the topic, is what changed the call.)*

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Lulu Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (row: **Cultural Erogenous Zone Resonance**). Flag and fix before delivering.

- **The thesis is forward, not present (Pattern 14):** every topic carries a "where it's GOING at T+lead-time" thesis, not a "how hot is it today" reading. If the analysis scores present heat and stops, the core technique was skipped — you'll buy at the top.
- **The window was netted against real lead time (Step 4):** every verdict shows `window − lead time = effective window`, and KILLs the topics where you'd arrive at the burnout. A GO that ignores production time is the signature failure this workflow exists to prevent.
- **Six signals, measured-or-labeled (Step 2):** each phase call rolls up six concrete signals, with any estimated reading marked `[est]`. A phase asserted as a vibe, or six guessed signals presented as fact, fails — the instrument readings must be honest.
- **Counter-narrative signal read first and correctly (Step 2 inversion):** absence-of-counter-narratives is treated as a *rising* tell, presence as *peaking/past*, ironic-usage as *burnout*. Getting this backwards (treating pushback as "engagement = good") inverts the entire timing call.
- **Topic-phase distinguished from format-phase (Tacit #2):** the phase is called for *topic × format*; a fresh topic in a burned format (manifesto glut, sizzle-reel sameness, Devon-copy, name-glut) is flagged as late even when the topic is early.
- **WAIT/KILL topics got a rising-adjacent rescue (Step 5):** no peaked/burned topic is just discarded; each gets an adjacent angle that itself passes the rising test and still rides the original charge. The charge is salvaged, not wasted.
- **A lane was assigned and the flops-are-invisible reframe stated (Step 6):** each topic is on the high-conviction or DCA lane, and any DCA recommendation explicitly de-risks iteration with "the flops, by definition, nobody saw." Without the reframe, the operator won't actually fire the cheap shots.
- **It stayed a timing call (Pre-Flight Gate):** the output is theses + signals + verdicts + lanes — *not* finished copy and *not* a defense of whether the topics matter. If a draft post or a "is this topic good" argument snuck in, cut it; the build is `lulu-zeitgeist-content`'s job and the receptor scan is `lulu-erogenous-zones`'.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Scoring present heat and posting on the peak.** The topic is unmistakably everywhere, so it feels like the obvious GO — and you build on it, ship after your lead time, and arrive at the saturation or the burnout. By the time a topic is *clearly* hot to a casual observer, the early-mover window has closed. **Recovery:** run Step 1 and Step 4 in sequence — write the *forward* thesis (where will this be at T+lead-time?), then net the window against your actual production time. The honest effective-window number almost always re-rates a "hot" peaking topic from GO to WAIT/KILL.
- **Ignoring your own production lead time.** The trajectory read is flawless, the verdict is GO — but it's a GO on a 4-day window for a topic that takes you 12 days to produce, so you ship into a dead zone with a great analysis attached. **Recovery:** Step 4 is non-optional. Compute `window − lead time` explicitly for every GO. If it's negative, either change the format to a faster one (the escape hatch) or KILL the topic — never ship a long-lead format into a short window and hope.
- **Reading topic-phase but missing format-burnout (the half-life blind spot).** The topic is genuinely rising, so you GO — but you deliver it in a manifesto (when manifestos are glutted), a sizzle reel (when every launch has one), or a tweet structure copied tweet-for-tweet from last month's launch. The topic was early; the *format* was burned, and the audience reads the whole thing as late. **Recovery:** call the phase for topic × format (Step 3 overlay). If 3+ competitors are using your intended format, that format is 2–3 weeks from collapse — keep the rising topic, change the vehicle.
- **Abandoning the charge on a KILL.** A topic comes back BURNOUT, so it's discarded entirely — and with it, the real emotional energy still sitting in the underlying zone. **Recovery:** Step 5 — every WAIT/KILL gets a rising-adjacent hunt (emerging subtopic, fresh counter-narrative, underserved segment, second-wave conversation). The entry point was spent; the charge wasn't. Ride the same current from an open receptor instead of walking away from live energy.
