# Launch Communications & Attention Harvest — From Spike to Durable Value (Lulu Cheng Meservey)

Anduril's "Don't Work Here" recruiting campaign is the gold standard for a reason. It wasn't a clever video. It was S-tier people, honed over months, with a *website and a recruiting funnel sitting behind it before the video ever shipped* — so when the attention came, it had somewhere to go. That is the whole discipline of a launch done right: the spike is the cheap part. Attention is potential energy; if you don't convert it the same week, you were "sitting on a can of gasoline hoping you'll end up at your office." This workflow refuses the dopamine high. It builds the launch backwards from the *one* business goal it must convert into, finds an angle that isn't a corpse of last quarter's viral template, packages the message→medium→messenger for *this specific moment*, pre-wires the 2-click path before the noise, and ships the harvest sequence that catches the energy on its way down. Two truths anchor the whole thing: launches happen constantly, so breaking through is genuinely hard — and a launch is not the be-all/end-all anyway (Facebook and Airbnb grew over years, not on day one). So you do not bet the company on the spike. You bet on the *infrastructure* that turns a small, real spike into hires, sales, or term sheets.

## Input Required

- **What's launching** — the product, feature, campaign, fund, hire, or program. The actual thing, not a category. We launch *this*, not "a SaaS tool."
- **The ONE business goal** (the gravity well — non-negotiable, pick one): **recruiting** (attract talent) / **fundraising** (attract investors) / **sales** (convert customers) / **regulatory** (protect the ability to operate). If you're tempted to pick two, rank them and the second becomes a *secondary harvest path*, never a co-primary. Two primaries = zero pressure.
- **The target — narrowed.** Not "developers." *These* people: "the ~400 senior infra engineers at FAANG who are quietly miserable optimizing ad clicks." Name the smallest audience whose conversion makes the launch a win. Pressure = force / area; you're about to shrink the area on purpose.
- **The real thing shipped** (ship-to-yap evidence) — what tangible progress backs this? What can a skeptic verify? This is the substantiation the launch is allowed to amplify.
- **The cultural current** — what is the target audience *already* charged about, talking about at dinners and in DMs, that this launch can attach to? (If nothing — the launch will have to swim against the current, which kills virality; flag it.)
- **The messengers available** — founder, employees (which ones?), customers, investors, advisors. Who can speak, and what's each one's moral authority?
- **Format/surface** — where the launch lives: X/LinkedIn thread, a video, a landing page, a manifesto/essay, a podcast appearance, an email to a list, a press moment. (Drives the medium step.)
- **Timeline & burned-template check** — when does this go live, and what launch formats are currently saturating the target's feed? (Drives the half-life decision.)

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Set the gravity well; build the whole launch BACKWARDS from it (Tacit #6)

Before the angle, before the words: name the one business outcome, and the *one converting action* that outcome reduces to. Everything downstream is engineered to produce that action. This is the move that separates a launch from a vanity spike — you decide where the energy lands before you generate any.

Fill this in first. If you can't, you are not ready to launch:

| | Answer for this launch |
|---|---|
| **The ONE goal (gravity well)** | recruiting / fundraising / sales / regulatory — pick one |
| **The single converting action** | the literal thing a converted person *does* (submits an application / books an investor call / starts a trial / signs the waitlist / emails their rep) |
| **What "the launch worked" looks like in 1 number** | e.g. "40 qualified senior-eng applications in 7 days," not "lots of views" |
| **The honest failure mode** | what this launch is NOT for (so attention from the wrong crowd doesn't read as success) |

> **The gravity-well test:** picture the launch going maximally viral and producing *zero* of the converting action. If that scenario is still tempting ("but the brand awareness…"), you've been seduced by the dopamine treadmill. A real launch is indifferent to attention that doesn't convert. Lock the number; you'll measure the harvest against it.

### Step 2 — Find the non-fungible angle; kill the burned template (Tacit #2 + the experience monopoly)

A launch breaks through on its *angle*, not its volume — and the angle must be two things at once: (a) attached to a live cultural current the target already feels, and (b) *not* a corpse of the template everyone is currently copying. Run both filters.

**2a. The template half-life audit.** List the launch formats currently saturating the target's feed. If your instinct is one of these, it's probably already decaying:

| Half-life stage | Signal | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Fresh (week 1-2)** | You've seen it from 0-1 people; it still surprises | Safe to ride — early-mover advantage |
| **Crowded (week 3-4)** | 2-3 competitors running it; audience recognizes the shape | Risky — you'll read as "me too"; differentiate hard or skip |
| **Burned (week 5+)** | The format itself is the joke ("another manifesto," "another sizzle reel," the Devin-launch tweet copied tweet-for-tweet) | Dead. Do NOT launch on it. Find the next thing. |

If the format is crowded or burned, the angle has to come from somewhere the template can't reach.

**2b. The non-fungibility test.** The strongest angle is the one *only you* can run — your lived experience, your specific origin, the thing you saw that the field hasn't named yet. Opinions are commodities; experiences are not. Generate the angle by asking, in order:

1. **What does the target already feel but can't say?** (The thing brewing with no place to go — name it, give it shape; don't install a foreign obsession.)
2. **What did we live/build/see that nobody else can claim in first person?** (The experience monopoly — makes you the #1 expert in the world on something small and specific.)
3. **What's the one true, slightly-too-bold claim we'll stake the launch on?** (The line in the sand — but gerrymandered: it must put your *whole* target inside it and only an external "old way" outside.)

Then run the **removal test**: write the launch's core sentence, then strip your company's name and the founder's specifics. If nothing is lost — if anyone could have published it — the angle is fungible. Inject the origin, the conviction, the specific detail only this person could know, until the sentence *could only be yours*.

```
CULTURAL CURRENT it attaches to: __________ (rising / peak / fading — must be rising or peak)
THE NON-FUNGIBLE ANGLE (one sentence, could only be us): __________
WHY THE TEMPLATE IS NOT BURNED: __________
```

### Step 3 — Concentrate: one audience, one thing, one slogan (Pattern 12 + Pattern 13)

Now reduce. Pressure = force / area; a launch that talks to everyone about everything punctures nothing. This step is subtraction, and it will feel like you're leaving reach on the table. You are. That's the point — Jobs gave the best speech of his life and people remember *one line*.

**3a. Cut the surface area.**

| Reduce | From (the temptation) | To (the launch) |
|---|---|---|
| **Audience** | "everyone who might care" | the narrowed target from Input — and only them |
| **Message** | the 10 true things you could say | the ONE thing they must remember tomorrow |
| **Proof** | every feature/stat | the single most-undeniable piece of ship evidence |

**3b. Forge the slogan (the Rex).** Choose the 3-5 fixed words that must cut through and repeat across *every* launch surface — while the stories, examples, and context around them change every time. Not the whole paragraph (that reads scripted and robotic); the few words ("go direct," "time to build," "don't work here"). The Far Side rule: the dog hears "blah blah blah Rex blah blah Rex" — you are choosing the Rex.

The slogan must be: short, repeatable from memory, ideally Saxon-hard (rings, doesn't float), and *something the target would want to say themselves*. Test: would a target person repeat this phrase back unprompted, in their own caption, as if it were already theirs? If it sounds like ad copy, it's not the Rex yet.

```
THE ONE THING (single takeaway): __________
THE SLOGAN (3-5 fixed words, repeated everywhere): "__________"
```

### Step 4 — Package the moment: message → medium → messenger, in that order

Now assemble the launch artifact. Order is law — message first, because it's the highest-leverage thing to get right; a perfect medium and messenger carrying a weak message is "selling encyclopedias nobody wants."

**4a. Message** — iterate the launch's core lines like a comedian working a joke until "it's all down to certain words." Wrap the commercial ask in candy coating: lead with a statement about the world that's novel/helpful/educational to the target, segue to the launch later or not at all. Run the two comms tests on the opening: (a) Could you say this *without* the CTA in the next sentence? (b) If your company didn't exist, what could you say that makes the target feel understood? If the opener is "I'm excited to announce," it's solipsistic — rewrite to lead with *their* obsession.

**4b. Medium** — pick the *pocket*, not the megaphone. Not the biggest audience — the place this specific target actually gets their information. The right Hacker News thread, the right podcast, the right Substack's comment section, the right group chat that screenshots out. Five right pockets beat 5,000 indifferent feeds.

| Message component | Lives best in | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The angle/manifesto | the surface where the target *reads deeply* (essay, thread, video) | conviction needs runway |
| The slogan | every surface, repeated | ubiquity engine |
| The proof | wherever the skeptic checks (changelog, demo, customer quote) | ship-to-yap, verifiable |

**4c. Messenger** — assign each claim to the person with moral authority to say *that specific thing*, because audiences discount anything said by someone paid to say it (founder personal conviction ≈ 100%; talking-points employee ≈ 20%; PR statement ≈ 10%; customer ≈ 80%+).

| Claim type | Authorized messenger | Authority test |
|---|---|---|
| **Vision / why this matters** | Founder, first person | "Who is the *only* person who can say this in first person?" If "anyone," it's discounted 90% |
| **"It's a great place to work" / culture** | A junior employee, unscripted | Would they say it if they didn't work here? |
| **Market size / why now** | Investor / analyst | Independent authority on the market |
| **"It actually works"** | Customer / engineer | Not on payroll to say it |

> **The first-person anchor:** the launch needs one piece that *must* come from the founder in first person — the vision/origin/line-in-the-sand. Go direct: the crux comes directly from the founder speaking, not a "spokesperson," not a ghost-voice a VP of Marketing could've written.

### Step 5 — Pre-build the 2-click conversion path BEFORE the noise (Pattern 7)

This is the step every excited founder skips and every Anduril-grade launch does first. The conversion infrastructure must exist *before* the launch ships, so the moment attention hits there is a frictionless path from "felt something" to the converting action in **2 clicks**.

Design the path for the gravity-well goal from Step 1:

```
ATTENTION SOURCE (the post/video/article)
        │  click 1
        ▼
LANDING PAGE (headline connects DIRECTLY to the launch angle — "you felt something reading that; here's what to do")
        │  click 2
        ▼
THE CONVERTING ACTION (apply / book / start trial / sign / contact)
```

| Conversion-infrastructure element | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Headline** | echoes the launch angle + slogan; names the feeling the target just had |
| **Recognition hook** | articulates the target's current reality so precisely they feel seen (candy coating, not pitch) |
| **The ask, crystallized** | what you need, stated plainly + anti-pitch honesty (what you're NOT offering — credibility through candor) |
| **Proof placement** | the verifiable ship evidence, where the skeptic looks |
| **The form** | absolute minimum fields. Every field is friction. Mobile-first, fast, tracked (UTMs by source) |
| **Real urgency** | true scarcity only ("reviewing first 50," "next batch closes Friday") — never fake counters |
| **The not-ready fallback** | secondary capture (newsletter/waitlist) for those who feel it but won't act today — feeds the harvest |

> **The 2-click test (hard gate):** can the target go from seeing the launch to completing the converting action in exactly two clicks, on a phone, in under 60 seconds? If it's 3+ clicks, or the form has 8 fields, or the page isn't built yet — the launch is not ready. Attention you can't catch is attention you wasted. Build this before you make a sound.

### Step 6 — Ship the harvest sequence: convert the spike on its way down (Pattern 7, the kinetic conversion)

The launch will peak and decline within days — that's physics, not failure (Facebook and Airbnb grew over *years*; the spike was never the point). The harvest is what turns the descending energy into durable value. Build the sequence *before* launch so it fires automatically while you're slammed.

| Window | Move | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Hour 0-48 (peak)** | Immediate confirmation to every converter ("we actually read these," what happens next, real human voice) | lock the high-intent converts; set expectations so they don't go cold |
| **Hour 0-48 (peak)** | Founder amplifies in the *pockets*, not a favor-ask. Give people something they already wanted to say (FYI, not "please share") | extend reach without burning relationship capital |
| **Day 2-5 (decline)** | Follow-up to the not-ready fallback list: the insider thing you *didn't* put in the launch + the converting CTA | convert the fence-sitters as the spike fades |
| **Day 5-14 (tail)** | Re-message non-converters who engaged; surface the converted ("congrats to [name] who joined from the launch") | social proof + second-chance conversion |
| **Day 14+ (durable)** | Audit: attention → converting actions → the Step-1 number. Feed the survivors into the long game (pipeline, talent bench, investor list) | the spike becomes a durable asset, not a memory |

> **The harvest discipline:** the post-launch high-five is the trap. The week *after* the spike is when most of the value is left on the table because the team is exhausted and celebrating. The sequence runs without heroics because it was built in advance. Measure ruthlessly against the Step-1 number — if attention was high and the converting action low, the failure is the *infrastructure*, not the content; fix the path, not the copy.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Launching for the spike, not the goal (the dopamine treadmill).** Optimizing the launch for views/likes/reach and treating conversion as a "we'll figure it out after." The attention comes, dissipates in 72 hours, and produces nothing — gasoline with no engine. **Recovery:** go back to Step 1. Name the single gravity-well goal and the converting action, then build the launch *backwards* from it. If you can't name the conversion, you don't have a launch, you have a party.

- **No conversion path, or a 5-click one, built after the fact.** The launch ships, goes better than expected, and *then* someone scrambles to build a landing page while the spike is already declining. By the time the path exists, the energy is gone. **Recovery:** Step 5 is a *pre-flight*, not a follow-up. Build and test the 2-click path before you make a sound. The Anduril rule: the recruiting funnel existed *before* the video.

- **Launching on a burned template (the corpse).** Reaching for the manifesto, the sizzle reel, the "I spent 487 hours so you don't have to" thread, or the Devin-launch format tweet-for-tweet — because it worked for someone two months ago. It now "disgusts on a visceral level" and reads as me-too. **Recovery:** run the half-life audit (Step 2a). If 3+ competitors are on the format, it's crowded-to-burned; find the angle the template can't reach — usually your non-fungible lived experience (Step 2b).

- **Betting the company on the spike.** Treating the launch as the be-all/end-all, pouring everything into one day, and reading a modest spike as failure. But launches happen constantly and breaking through is hard *by design* — and the great companies grew over years, not on launch day. **Recovery:** reframe. The spike is a *small, real* input the infrastructure converts into a durable asset (a talent bench, a nurture list, an investor pipeline). Measure the harvest, not the peak. A launch that converts 40 of the right people beats one that gets a million of the wrong ones.
