# Zeitgeist-Entry Content Engine — Surf the Current, Don't Fight It (Lulu Cheng Meservey)

Most content calendars fight the audience. They pick a topic the creator finds interesting, write it cleanly, ship it — and it bounces off a wall of indifference, because there was no charge in the room for it to attach to. Lulu's whole reframe is aquatic: you are a swimmer in a current, and you have three choices. Swim *against* it (fight the zeitgeist — you exhaust yourself and go nowhere; this is "anti-mimetic," the thing nobody wanted to hear). Swim *perpendicular* to it (ignore the current entirely — you get dragged off course). Or **swim with it on a diagonal** — let the current carry you while you still steer toward *your* shore. That diagonal is the entire craft: ride what the culture is already feeling, in the direction of *your* business goal. This workflow is the production engine for that swim. It takes a charged obsession-landscape, hunts the live zeitgeist across six grounds, scores each topic by where the current is *heading* (not where it is today), and runs every single piece through a naming-and-candy-coating pass — so each post gives the audience *words for what they already wanted to say* and feels like it names something, rather than teaching them something foreign. It is the *build*: the calendar, the hooks, the ready-to-ship posts. The radar that finds the receptors is `lulu-erogenous-zones`; this is what you do once they're found and you have to feed the beast every week.

## Pre-Flight

Read before executing — load these `genius.md` sections (do not paraphrase from memory; the distinctions are load-bearing and the failure modes are quiet):

- **Pattern 8 — The Cultural Erogenous Zone Radar** — the upstream truth: you attach to an *already-charged* zone, you never install a foreign obsession. The metric is "names what they were already feeling," not "introduces a new idea." This workflow assumes the radar pass is done (or runs a fast version of it inline).
- **Pattern 14 — The Trajectory Arbitrage (Play Where the Puck Is Going)** — the timing engine: "you always want to play to where it's going, not where it is today." Pick the lane — high-conviction early bet, or dollar-cost-averaging constant low-stakes shots. The reframe that removes the fear: *the posts that flopped, by definition, nobody saw — it's lower risk than people think.*
- **Pattern 15 — The Naming-Gives-Shape Move (Solidify the Fluid)** — the per-piece engine: take the feeling that's "brewing and sitting there with no place to go," give it a name and a form. *"Writing on the wall." "Founder mode."* It shouldn't be foreign — it should have familiarity, you're just giving it shape. People then spread it *in their own words*.
- **Pattern 3 — The Candy Coating Message Wrapper** — the rule for every piece: lead with a statement about the *world* that's novel/helpful/educational; the company comes later or not at all. Pass the two comms tests on each post.
- **Tacit Knowledge #2 — The Template Half-Life** — the calendar discipline: every viral format decays in 6–8 weeks; a *named* zone at peak can decay in 36 hours. Rotate formats and retire burned ones; a calendar that ships the same template for three months is a calendar dying on a delay.
- **Decision Framework** — the gate below.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` before building a single calendar slot. Specifically confirm **Q1** (every recurring piece converts toward *one* named business goal — recruiting / fundraising / sales / thought-leadership-as-pipeline; a "content stream" that converts to nothing is the dopamine treadmill at scale), **Q2** (the two comms tests pass per piece — could you say it *without* the CTA in the next sentence, and if your company didn't exist could you still say it and make the audience feel understood; if a slot is pure self-interest, it gets candy-coated or cut), and **Q5** (you are riding *already-open*, *rising-or-fresh-peak* receptors on a diagonal toward your shore — not fighting the current with a foreign obsession, not riding a burned-out zone, not swimming straight at the goal so hard the reader feels the sell). If you cannot name the current you're swimming *with*, stop — there is no zeitgeist to enter, only a topic you find interesting, and that fights indifference.

## Input Required

- **Audience** — specific, not "founders" but "seed-stage technical founders who post on X and read Hacker News." The hunt is only as sharp as the audience; "everyone" surfaces a zeitgeist charged for no one.
- **Core expertise / what you can credibly speak to** — the experience-monopoly boundary. A zone is only usable if you've lived something real adjacent to it (else riding it reads as bandwagoning — the discounted-messenger failure).
- **Business goal** — recruiting / fundraising / sales / thought-leadership-as-pipeline. The shore you're steering the diagonal toward. Drives which grounds and trajectories earn calendar slots.
- **Channels + cadence** — where you publish (LinkedIn / X / newsletter / a series) and how many pieces per week. Drives the format mix and the calendar density.
- **Obsession-landscape input** (strongly preferred) — the ranked receptors + articulation gaps from `lulu-erogenous-zones`. If you have them, skip the inline radar in Step 1 and start from the hunt. If you don't, Step 1 runs a compressed version.
- **Known frustrations / inside signal** (optional, high-value) — anything heard in DMs, churn calls, at drinks. Private signal is worth ten public ones; the edge is reading the small room before the big one.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Map the obsession landscape (or import it)

Before hunting topics, lay out *what this audience returns to* — the standing obsessions the zeitgeist will charge. If you have the `lulu-erogenous-zones` output, import its receptors and articulation gaps and go to Step 2. If not, run this compressed obsession map — six probes, generating 8–12 standing obsessions:

| Probe | The question | What you're reading for |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily friction** | What frustrates them *every day*, the papercut they've stopped noticing? | The frustration cluster's source |
| **Private vs public** | What do they discuss in DMs and at drinks but *not* with their name on it? | The articulation gap — the gold |
| **Emotional charge** | Which topics make the reaction *bigger than the topic warrants*? | A status anxiety or wound underneath |
| **Status motion** | What used to be cool that isn't — or vice versa? Who's rising, who's falling? | Status shifts (the richest ground) |
| **Fear** | What are they quietly afraid of — being left behind, exposed, irrelevant? | Fear undercurrents (usually displaced into humor) |
| **Aspiration** | What do they want to *become* but aren't yet, and won't say out loud? | Aspiration signals + permission to want it |

> **The depth rule (Lulu's whole arbitrage):** a standing obsession that lives only in *public discussion* is already commodity — everyone's attaching there, the receptor is crowded. The value is in the **private concern, status anxiety, and articulation gap** — "what high-status individuals discuss privately *before* it becomes public discourse." If your obsession map is all surface, you haven't listened in the real rooms yet. Go listen.

### Step 2 — Hunt the zeitgeist across the six grounds (the spine)

Now hunt the *live* current — what the standing obsessions are charging *right now*. Lulu's instinct is to scan six distinct hunting grounds; each surfaces a different kind of zeitgeist signal, and most creators only ever hunt one (usually frustration). Scan *all six* against your audience and harvest 2–4 live topics per ground:

| # | Hunting ground | The signal it surfaces | Hunt question | What a hit looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Frustration clusters** | Many people independently complaining about *the same thing* | Where is the same complaint clustering across the pocket? | A repeated gripe that nobody's named yet — "everyone's mad about X and there's no word for it" |
| 2 | **Emerging language** | New words / phrases the audience is *minting* | What slang, acronym, or coinage is spreading in the pocket? | A term in its first 1–2 weeks of life — you can reshape it before it sets (Pattern 15) |
| 3 | **Permission-seeking** | "Is it okay if…" questions, hedged confessions | What are people quietly asking permission to do/think/feel? | A widely-held private wish nobody's granted permission for — pure naming gold |
| 4 | **Status shifts** | What used to be cool that isn't, or vice versa | What's gaining or losing status in this world right now? | A re-ranking in progress — the moment a thing flips from cringe to cool or the reverse |
| 5 | **Fear undercurrents** | Anxieties surfacing through *humor or displacement* | What are the jokes/memes secretly about? What's the dread behind the laugh? | A fear too raw to state, leaking out as irony — the displaced wound |
| 6 | **Aspiration signals** | What people want to *become* but aren't yet | What identity is the audience reaching for? | A nascent self-image people are trying on — give them words and you give them the upgrade |

> **The trend-vs-erogenous-zone distinction (do NOT skip this):** a *trend* is a topic that's popular — anyone can see it, everyone's already on it, the receptor is crowded, the template is half-burned. A *cultural erogenous zone* is a topic that's producing emotional *charge* — often not yet fully public, sitting and brewing "with no place to go." You are hunting the **second**. The grounds that most reliably surface true erogenous zones over mere trends are **permission-seeking, status shifts, and fear undercurrents** — because those live in the private/displaced register where the charge outruns the visibility. Frustration clusters and emerging language are richer but easier to mistake for trends; weight your hunt toward the deep three. If a "topic" is loud and public and on everyone's feed, it's probably a trend (peak, crowded) — note it but don't anchor the calendar on it.

For each topic harvested, write one line: **the ground it came from**, **the standing obsession it charges** (from Step 1), and the **articulation gap underneath** (the thing they feel-but-can't-say — this becomes the naming target in Step 5).

### Step 3 — Score every topic on trajectory (play where the puck is going)

The same topic lands or dies entirely on *timing*. "You always want to play to where it's going, not where it is today." Score each harvested topic on where the current is *heading* — and the score dictates the move, not just a label:

| Trajectory | What it looks like | The move | Calendar treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-awareness** | The charge is in private rooms / among high-status carriers; no public language yet | **Educate and be first — coin the name.** Highest arbitrage. | Anchor a *flagship* piece; this is where you own the language (Pattern 15) |
| **Rising** | Showing up beyond the private rooms, language still forming, not yet mainstream | **Position and stake your take while there's runway.** The prize. | The backbone of the calendar — most slots; own it before the crowd |
| **Peak** | Fully public, everyone's using it, language fixed, maximum volume | **Ride it now with same-day speed, or go contrarian.** Crowded, decaying. | At most a *fast* reactive slot; never a multi-week build (it'll be burnout by ship) |
| **Declining** | Volume falling, smart people have moved on | **Exit.** The puck has left. | Skip — or harvest the *next* zone the smart people moved to |
| **Burnout** | Used ironically, feels dated, has acquired cringe | **Exit immediately — attaching here lowers you.** | Never; attaching to a burned zone is cringe-by-contagion |

Then **pick the lane** for the calendar overall — Lulu names two valid strategies, and a good calendar usually blends them:

- **(A) High-conviction arbitrage.** Spot "the thing people are thinking and not saying" at *pre-awareness/rising*, prepare heavily, double down, and "get here and own this so by the time everybody catches up, we own it." Fewer, bigger, earlier bets. Use for your flagship slots.
- **(B) Dollar-cost averaging.** Publish constantly, low effort per shot, and when something hits, read the feedback and refine (the Jobs many-iterations path). Use for the high-cadence connective slots. **The fear-killer:** "the posts that flopped — by definition, nobody saw. It's lower risk than people think." So iterate fearlessly; a flop costs nothing, a hit compounds.

> **The trajectory-scoring depth note:** if you need a rigorous viral-trajectory read (S-curve stage, velocity, ceiling, decay-risk) rather than this four-bucket gauge, hand the topic to `lulu-viral-trajectory` and bring the score back here. This step is the calendar-level gauge; that workflow is the per-topic instrument.

### Step 4 — Relevance-gate against your experience monopoly (the credibility cut)

A charged, well-timed topic you can't credibly speak to is a trap — riding it reads as bandwagoning, the discounted-paid-messenger failure ("would you say this if it weren't for the engagement?"). Cut every topic where you have no lived, first-hand standing. Score each survivor 1–5 on the **attach test**:

- **5** — you have an experience monopoly here: something you lived that *only you* can recount; the topic is yours by right.
- **3–4** — you have real, credible standing; you can contribute a take that isn't a commodity opinion.
- **1–2** — you'd just be an opinion among millions, or worse, an outsider commenting. **Cut.** "If you're saying something a lot of other people could say, there's no reason they should buy that message from you."

Keep only topics scoring ≥3, ranked. This is the cut that keeps the stream *uncopyable* — the AI-slop-detection corollary: a piece that could've been "cobbled together, run through ChatGPT, and hit publish" fails here, because nothing in it is yours.

### Step 5 — Run the naming + candy-coating pass on each kept topic (the per-piece engine)

This is where a topic becomes a *piece*. Two passes, in order — naming first (give the fluid a shape), then candy-coating (wrap the shape so the audience feels understood before the company appears).

**Pass A — Naming-gives-shape (Pattern 15).** Take the articulation gap (Step 2) — the thing they feel-but-can't-say — and give it a name and a form. Three rules from the transcript:

| Rule | What it means | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Familiar, not foreign** | The named thing should produce recognition, not instruction. "It should have familiarity; you're giving it shape." *Founder mode* taught founders nothing new — it gave catharsis and permission for something they already felt. | If the reader thinks "huh, never heard of that" → fail. If they think "oh my god, *yes*, that's exactly it" → hit. |
| **You usually don't need a brand-new word** | Often the move is to *reshape an existing one*. Brian Armstrong on "go direct": "just use it, it already exists — reshape it in your image, attach your ideas to it." | Can you take a word the audience already has and bend it to your meaning? Prefer that to coining from scratch. |
| **Give them words, don't force them** | "People say what they already wanted to say." If you give them language for a held feeling = virality (they spread it in their own words). If you force them to say *your* thing = anti-mimetic (it dies). | Will they *repost this in relief* ("finally, a word for it")? Or do you need to *ask* them to share it? The first is the goal. |

The output of Pass A is, for each piece, a **name/frame** — the handle the audience will grab and spread.

**Pass B — Candy-coating (Pattern 3).** Now wrap the named frame so the piece leads with the *world*, not the company. Run both comms tests:

1. **The CTA test:** Could you say this *without* a "buy/apply/click" in the next sentence? If the piece collapses without the ask, it's an ad, not a contribution — rewrite the open to stand alone.
2. **The if-we-didn't-exist test:** If your company/offer didn't exist, what could you say here that makes the audience feel *understood*? Lead with *that* — a statement about the world that's novel, helpful, or educational. The company segues in later, or not at all.

The candy coating is the *opening* of every piece: the named articulation gap, stated as a truth about their world. The conversion (Step 6) is downstream of it, never the lead.

> **The diagonal check (the swim metaphor, made operational):** for each piece, confirm you are riding *with* the current toward *your* shore. Three failure modes to catch: **against the current** = you're arguing the audience out of what they feel (anti-mimetic, dies); **perpendicular** = the piece ignores the live zeitgeist entirely (no charge, bounces off indifference); **straight downstream** = you're so on-trend you've added nothing and steered nowhere (commodity, converts to no goal). The hit is the *diagonal*: their current carries the reach, your angle carries it toward recruiting/sales/funding. If the piece doesn't move toward a goal, it's downstream drift; if it has no current under it, it's a perpendicular flail.

### Step 6 — Wire each piece to its business goal + assemble the calendar (Q1)

Attention is potential energy; a stream of it that converts to nothing is a dopamine treadmill running for months. For each piece, name the one business goal it serves and the 2-click path the attention should convert into (apply / demo / reply / subscribe-then-nurture). Then assemble the calendar — themed by week or sprint, mixing trajectories and formats so no single format burns out:

- **Theme by current, not by topic-of-the-day** — group the week around one rising zone so the slogan/name compounds (Pattern 13: drive the *few fixed words* — the name from Pass A — across the week while the stories around it change).
- **Mix trajectories** — most slots on *rising* zones (lane B, fearless iteration), a flagship on a *pre-awareness* bet you're trying to own (lane A), and reactive slots reserved for *peak* zones you can ride same-day.
- **Rotate formats against the half-life** — track which format each slot uses; when one format has run ~6 weeks or you see it everywhere, retire it (Tacit #2). A calendar is a living thing, not a locked grid.
- **Re-run the hunt each cycle** — last cycle's hot zone may be this cycle's burnout (the 36-hour rule). The calendar is a rolling instrument; Step 2 fires again every cycle.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Social (LinkedIn / X)** | The articulation gap (Step 2) becomes the *first line* — lead with their obsession, never your offer (candy coating is non-negotiable at scroll speed). The Pass-A name is the post's spine; repeat the *exact name* across the week's posts while the stories change (Pattern 13 — slogan ubiquity). Trajectory matters most here: ride *rising* zones, abandon *burnout* on sight. Lane B (dollar-cost-averaging) is native — ship fearlessly, the flops nobody saw. One zone per post; never cram the calendar into a caption. |
| **Newsletter / series** | Trajectory arbitrage is the headline use: claim *pre-awareness/rising* intellectual territory and own the language by the time it peaks ("the writing on the wall" before the crowd). Run a *portfolio* across trajectories per issue — one rising bet to own, one fresh-peak to ride for reach. The naming pass is high-leverage here: a named frame in a newsletter becomes the reader's vocabulary, repeated back unprompted. Re-hunt every issue; the half-life is real. |
| **Marketing / brand content** | Prioritize **status-shift** and **aspiration** grounds — those are what "standing for something" attaches to (the brand becomes the identity the audience is reaching for, not a fungible product). Candy-coating is the whole game: the brand contributes to the cultural conversation first, sells second. Wire the line so it unites your tribe + an external "old way" (never split the base). |
| **Copywriting (the lead/hook for converting copy)** | The top articulation gap becomes the **recognition hook** — the "finally, someone gets it" opener that earns the read before any claim. Score grounds for *sales*-goal fit: frustration-clusters and fear-undercurrents convert; pure-aspiration entertains. **Honesty spine applies hardest here** (see Quality Gate): the named frame amplifies a *true* mechanism/claim; it never manufactures one the offer can't deliver. |
| **Ghostwriting** | Hunt only the zones *the client* can speak to with experience-monopoly authority (Step 4 is the gate — a zone the client hasn't lived reads as the discounted paid messenger). The naming pass must produce a frame that's unmistakably *the client's* — strip anything a VP-of-Marketing could've named. Engineer authentic moments (real recordings → best lines selected), never fabricate the lived detail under the frame. |

### Worked example A — Defense-tech startup, recruiting goal, X + newsletter

**Audience:** elite software engineers at FAANG / top AI labs, late-20s–30s, post on X, lurk in private group chats. · **Expertise boundary:** the founders are ex-FAANG who left for mission work — they've lived the dread. · **Goal:** recruiting. · **Lane:** blend — one flagship arbitrage bet + dollar-cost-averaging on X.

**Zeitgeist hunt (selected hits):**

| Topic | Ground | Articulation gap underneath | Trend or zone |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I make line go up and I'm bored" | Frustration cluster | *"I'm too good for this and scared to leave the comp"* | zone (private, charged) |
| "American dynamism" un-cringing | Status shift | *"I'm allowed to want to build for my country now"* | zone (rising) |
| Memes about ad-optimization meaninglessness | Fear undercurrent (displaced into jokes) | *"I'm scared I'll look back on a wasted decade"* | zone (raw nerve) |
| "FAANG is slow now" dunking | Frustration cluster | (loud, public, everyone's posting it) | **trend** (peak, crowded) |

**Trajectory + selection:** the wasted-decade fear is **pre-awareness/rising** (charged in private rooms, no public name yet) — the **flagship arbitrage bet** (lane A). "FAANG is slow" is **peak-and-public** — a *trend*, crowded; demoted to a fast reactive slot only. Relevance = 5/5 (founders lived it).

**Naming pass (Pass A):** the articulation gap is *"I'm scared I'll look back on a wasted decade making line go up."* Familiar-not-foreign frame, reshaping an existing phrase: **"the prime-decade question"** — *what will you have built when your best engineering years are gone?* (Recognition, not instruction — every senior engineer has felt the 2am version of this; they'll repost in relief, not because you asked.)

**Candy-coated open (Pass B):** *"Nobody at a top lab admits this out loud, but the smartest engineers I know are quietly asking the same thing: when this decade is over, what will I have actually built? You can answer that with a thousand A/B tests that moved a metric 0.3%. Or you can answer it with something that mattered."* — leads with the world (their dread), passes both comms tests, company appears two paragraphs later as the *answer* to the named question, never the lead. Diagonal: rides the wasted-decade current toward recruiting. 2-click path: line in bio → "what we're building / open roles."

### Worked example B — A trend mistaken for a zone, caught at the hunt

**Audience:** indie SaaS founders. · **Candidate topic surfaced by the hunt:** "build in public" (frustration + emerging-language grounds, *huge* surface volume — MRR screenshots everywhere).

**Trend-vs-zone read:** the volume is real but it's **public, crowded, and template-saturated** — the loud carriers are now low-status growth-hackers, and the "I spent 487 hours so you don't have to" format is its death rattle. Trajectory = **burnout** (used semi-ironically now). Trend-vs-zone verdict: **trend, not erogenous zone** — popular, not charged with *fresh* emotion; the receptor is crowded and the template is burned (Tacit #2). Relevance might be 5/5, but timing kills it.

**Verdict: CUT at the hunt.** Anchoring a calendar on a burned trend is shipping cringe-by-contagion on a delay. The recovery the engine forces: *what zone did the respected founders move to when they got quiet about build-in-public?* That re-hunt surfaces the rising successor (e.g., a quiet shift toward "boring profitable" over "viral growth") — a real erogenous zone with runway. The discipline is to override the "but it's so big!" surface read with the trajectory + trend-vs-zone senses, and steer the calendar to where the current is *going*.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Lulu Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (rows: **Cultural Erogenous Zone Resonance**, **Business Goal Gravity Well Alignment**). Flag and fix before delivering.

- **Riding a current, not fighting one (the diagonal):** every piece attaches to an *already-charged* zone and steers toward the goal. No piece argues the audience out of what they feel (against), ignores the live zeitgeist (perpendicular), or rides a trend so hard it adds nothing and converts nowhere (straight-downstream). If you can't name the current under a piece, cut it.
- **Zones, not trends (Step 2 distinction):** the calendar is anchored on *charged* receptors (often private/rising), not merely *popular* topics. Any peak-and-public *trend* appears only as a fast reactive slot, never the backbone. A calendar built on trends is a calendar arriving at the burnout.
- **Trajectory scored and burnout exited (Pattern 14):** no calendar slot rides a *declining* or *burnout* zone; any *peak* slot carries the 36-hour-clock warning and same-day-only treatment. You're playing where the puck is going.
- **Naming gives shape, doesn't instruct (Pattern 15):** each piece's frame produces recognition ("finally, a word for it"), not foreign instruction. The familiar-not-foreign test passed; prefer reshaping an existing word over coining from scratch. They'd repost it in relief — you never had to *ask* (forcing = anti-mimetic, AN-4 favor-ask territory).
- **Candy coating on every piece (Pattern 3):** every opening leads with a statement about the *world* (the named articulation gap), not the company. Both comms tests pass per piece — could you say it without the CTA, and could you say it if your company didn't exist. No solipsistic "excited to announce" leads (AN-3).
- **Experience-monopoly cut applied (Step 4):** every kept piece scores ≥3 on credible lived standing. Nothing in the stream is a commodity opinion millions share or a topic the author/client hasn't lived. The "could ChatGPT have cobbled this together?" slop test fails any piece without a real human's fingerprint (AN-1, AN-5).
- **Every piece traces to one business goal (Q1):** each slot names recruiting / fundraising / sales / thought-leadership-as-pipeline and a 2-click conversion path. No "awareness for its own sake" slots — that's the dopamine treadmill at calendar scale (AN-6).
- **Format half-life respected (Tacit #2):** the calendar rotates formats and flags any format approaching its ~6-week burn; the hunt re-runs each cycle. It's a living instrument, not a locked grid shipping a dying template on a delay.
- **Honesty spine intact:** every named frame and candy-coated open *amplifies a true claim* about the audience's world or the offer's real mechanism. The naming move makes a *real* feeling articulable and a *real* benefit ring; it never manufactures a feeling the audience doesn't have or a claim the offer can't deliver. A frame that makes an empty claim *feel* true is the failure mode to kill on sight — the technique gives shape to what's real, never invents what isn't.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing a trend with an erogenous zone (anchoring on the crowded receptor).** The hunt surfaces something loud and public, it *feels* undeniably charged, so the whole calendar gets built on it — and by ship time it's a burned trend everyone's already tired of. **Recovery:** run the Step-2 trend-vs-zone distinction *before* anchoring. Loud-and-public = probably a trend (peak, crowded). True zones often live in the private/displaced register (permission-seeking, status shifts, fear undercurrents) where charge outruns visibility. If the respected carriers have gone quiet on a "hot" topic, it's burning out — re-hunt for where they moved.
- **Forcing the name instead of giving it (anti-mimetic death).** Coining a clever foreign term and then *pushing* the audience to adopt it — "please use #MyNewFramework." Nobody repeats a word that isn't already theirs. **Recovery:** Pass A's familiar-not-foreign rule. Find the feeling they *already have*, give it words they'll grab in relief, and prefer reshaping an existing word to inventing one. The test isn't "is it clever?" — it's "will they repost this without being asked?" If you'd have to ask, it's against the current.
- **Candy coating the calendar but not the pieces (self-interest leaking back in).** The strategy doc says "lead with their obsession," but the actual posts open with "I'm excited to share our new…" because the deadline hit and discipline slipped. **Recovery:** the two comms tests are *per piece*, not per strategy. Before any post ships, confirm: could you say this without the CTA in the next sentence, and would it still make the audience feel understood if your company didn't exist? If not, rewrite the *open* — the conversion is always downstream of the contribution, never the lead.
- **A perfectly-timed, perfectly-named stream that converts to nothing (the dopamine treadmill at scale).** Every piece rides a rising zone, every frame is sticky, engagement is up — and not one applicant, demo, or dollar resulted, because no piece was wired to a goal. **Recovery:** Step 6 + Q1. Before the calendar ships, every slot names one business goal and a 2-click path. Attention is potential energy; a calendar that never converts it is "sitting on a can of gasoline hoping you'll end up at your office." Build the conversion path *into* the slot, not after the moment.
