# LENS CARD — Lulu Cheng Meservey (go-direct communications strategist; founder narrative architecture)
> Compiled 2026-07-27 from genius.md (canonical). FIRING RULE: this card decides WHETHER this lens has something to say about a draft line; when a lens fires, READ the matching genius.md section before treating. Never treat from the card alone.

## Lenses
### The First Person Vision Monopoly
Before any major communication, ask: "Who is the only human on Earth who can say this in first person?" That person delivers the core message; everyone else provides supporting evidence. If the answer isn't immediately clear, re-evaluate the message or the messenger.
**Test:** Audience can name the founder and repeat the vision after one exposure.

### The Ship-to-Yap Ratio Detector
Audit communication volume against substantive output: every week of public visibility matched by demonstrable progress on product, hiring, or customer success. If the output doesn't justify the "yap," halt or scale back.
**Test:** Every tweet or post can be linked to something tangible shipped in the past 30 days.

### The Candy Coating Message Wrapper
Never deliver a commercial message without first wrapping it in something the audience already wants to hear. Identify 3 "cultural erogenous zones" your audience obsesses over; frame every company message as a contribution to those conversations first, company benefit second. Two comms tests: (1) Could you say this without the CTA to give you money in the next sentence? (2) If our company didn't exist, what could we say that makes the audience feel understood?
**Test:** Your communications get shared by people who don't work for you and aren't being paid.

### The Emotional Altitude Matching Protocol
In any crisis response, express MORE concern than your most upset customer would expect — they'll moderate their anger to meet you in the middle.
**Test:** Angry customers respond with "it's okay, we understand" rather than escalation.

### The Deterrence Score Builder
Never announce something you're not committed to completing. Build a track record of announced → delivered; let it compound over 2–3 years.
**Test:** Industry observers reference your past follow-through when predicting your future behavior.

### The Gerrymandered Line in the Sand
Before taking a stand, map your entire stakeholder universe. Draw the line so customers, employees, and investors are ALL inside it; let the "enemy" be external (us-who-believe vs. them-who-don't).
**Test:** The controversial position increases employee applications and customer loyalty simultaneously.

### The Evanescent Moment Conversion System
Attention is potential energy that must be converted to kinetic business value. Pre-build the conversion infrastructure before any launch: awareness → applicant/customer/investor in 2 clicks.
**Test:** Viral moments produce measurable increases in job applications, demo requests, or investor outreach within 48 hours.

### The Cultural Erogenous Zone Radar
Track what high-status individuals in your target audience are discussing privately (dinners, DMs, small groups) before it becomes public discourse — not what's popular, what's brewing.
**Test:** Your public statements feel like they "name something people were already feeling," not foreign concepts.

### The Messenger Authority Matching Protocol
Messenger matrix: Vision = Founder · Culture = Employees · Market opportunity = Investors/Analysts · Product quality = Customers. Always ask: "Would this person say this if they weren't employed by us?" — audiences apply an automatic 90%+ discount to paid opinions (founder conviction ≈ 100%; employee talking points ≈ 20%; PR agency ≈ 10%; customer testimonial ≈ 80%+).
**Test:** Each message comes from the speaker the audience would most trust on that specific topic.

### The Cringe vs. Resentment Feedback Decoder (Negative Space Reading)
Monitor WHO engages, not just how much. Three negative-space signals: silent supporters = cringe territory; missing engagement from key influencers = becoming irrelevant; absence of criticism on controversial takes = you haven't actually taken a stand. Keep a watchlist of 10–15 key supporters and check them after every major communication.
**Test:** Detractors vocal (good sign) while supporters stay enthusiastic. "Negative comments mean you matter; silence means you don't."

### The Message-First Triad (Message → Medium → Messenger, In That Order)
"The message is the highest leverage thing to get right. If the message isn't good, you've just wasted all of that effort." (1) Message — iterate it like a comedian working a joke until "it's all down to certain words." (2) Medium — "show up where they actually get their information," the right pocket not the biggest audience. (3) Messenger — the moral authority for this specific claim. Never reorder.
**Test:** You can complete the founder's core sentences from memory; it shows up in the 5 right pockets, not 5,000 indifferent feeds.

### The Pressure-Over-Surface-Area Concentrator
"Pressure equals force over area." "If you want to talk to these five people, don't talk to 5,000 and hope these five catch it... And if there's one thing they care about above everything, don't talk about ten things — talk about one." "Do you want people to remember one thing or zero things?"
**Test:** A reader, asked the next day, names exactly one takeaway — the one you chose.

### The Slogan Ubiquity Engine
Choose the 3–5 words that must cut through; repeat *those exact words* everywhere while stories and context change every time. "If you say the entire paragraph over and over, now you're scripted and robotic. But it's fine to say 'focus on the customer' over and over." The Far Side rule: pick the Rex.
**Test:** The audience repeats your exact phrase back unprompted; the slogan feels like it was always there.

### The Trajectory Arbitrage
"You always want to play to where it's going, not where it is today." Lane A: high-conviction concentrated bet — spot "the thing people are thinking and not saying," double down, "own this so by the time everybody catches up, we own it." Lane B: dollar-cost averaging — publish constantly, low effort per shot, refine what hits. Fear-remover: "the posts that flopped — by definition, nobody saw."
**Test:** You arrive "early but not so early as to be irrelevant" and own the language when the mainstream gets there.

### The Naming-Gives-Shape Move
Find what your field is already feeling but can't articulate, and name it — "it should have familiarity, but you're giving it shape." You usually don't need a brand-new term: "just use it, it already exists — reshape it in your image, attach your ideas to it."
**Test:** People adopt your name for a feeling they already had and feel relief/permission ("finally, there's a word for it"), not instruction.

### The Experience Monopoly
Prefer the thing only you have lived through over the opinion millions share — "it gives you a monopoly — makes you the number one expert in the world on something really small and specific." "If you're saying something a lot of other people could say, there's no reason they should buy that message from you." Smaller audience is fine — "100% of the people interested in that thing have to come to you." Guard: never let this curdle into the "I spent 487 hours so you don't have to" engagement-farm template.
**Test:** The piece could only have been written by this one person.

### Template Half-Life
Every viral format decays: weeks 1–2 maximum impact; 3–4 competition emerging; 5–6 audience fatigue; week 7+ the format is burned — exit immediately. When 3+ competitors use the same format, you have 2–3 weeks.
**Test:** Format freshness checked before choosing it.

### The Favor Depletion Equation
Amplification asks deplete relationship capital: (their discomfort) × (how inauthentic they feel) × (how visible the compliance is). Never directly ask people to share; create content worth sharing organically; track who shares voluntarily — those are your real advocates.

### The Authenticity Engineering Paradox
Don't fake authenticity — engineer situations where authentic expression naturally emerges, then capture and amplify: record the founder speaking naturally, extract the genuinely good moments, present those. The authenticity is real; the selection is strategic.

### The Business Goal Gravity Well
Every communication converts into exactly one of: Recruiting / Fundraising / Sales / Regulatory. "If attention doesn't convert to one of these, it's entertainment, not strategy." If conversion is low, fix the infrastructure, not the content.
**Test:** Before any campaign: "What business goal does this serve?" After any viral moment: "How did we capture this?"

## Anti-patterns / calibration
- **Polish is the tell.** "If the writing is bad, it's better for it to be bad and honest" than textbook-correct and dead. "I would rather see writing that is suboptimal, that has personality, that has intent and has conviction than writing that is textbook correct." Don't sand the wrongness out of a manifesto — "sometimes it actually takes away the wrongness that is the point." Never announce the machinery.
- **The recognition gate:** would Lulu recognize this as someone speaking with conviction from a real position — or as someone running a PR play? If it could have been "cobbled together, run through ChatGPT, and hit publish," it has failed regardless of frameworks present. "You get one bullet, you get one shot" — the audience never forgives the fake twice.
- **AN-1 Voiceless, founder-detached content** — "if you didn't exist, that exact same post could have gone out." Detection: remove the founder's name and personal stakes; if nothing is lost, inject origin, conviction, specifics only this person could know.
- **AN-2 Passive-voice responsibility dodge** — crisis comms "as far away from first person as you can get" (the CrowdStrike rewrite). Detection: scan for passive constructions and orphaned blame; rewrite first person, active, founder owning it, matching emotional altitude.
- **AN-3 Self-interest-first announcements** — "congratulations to me" copy that "forget[s] to tell people why any of it should matter to them." Detection: count *us* sentences vs. audience-feel-understood sentences.
- **AN-4 The favor ask for amplification** — produces "the energy of an al-Qaeda hostage video... coming through in their post," the "joyless LFG reply." Replace with content they already wanted to say.
- **AN-5 Engagement-farm template slop** — the "487 hours" formula and tweet-for-tweet launch copies; "disgusts me on a really visceral level... I feel exploited." Detection: trending viral template = past its half-life; find the next thing.
- **AN-6 Attention with no conversion** — "like if you were to just sit on a can of gasoline and hope that you'll end up at your office." Confirm the 2-click path exists *before* making noise.
- **AN-7 The discounted paid messenger** — "you didn't go buy a Lincoln when you saw the commercial." Would they say it unpaid? If no, reassign.
- **AN-8 The base-splitting line in the sand** — "Coca-Cola versus Pepsi... created civil war among your employees." If any core group lands outside the line, redraw it.
- **Decision gate (pre-workflow, stop if 1–3 fail):** (1) What business goal does this convert into — name one; "attention" = dopamine sugar high. (2) Does it pass the two comms tests? (3) Who is the *only* person who can say this in first person? (4) Ship-to-yap ratio grounded? (5) Which cultural zone, and where's its trajectory — attach to the current, don't swim against it. (6) Is the line gerrymandered around your people? (7) Would this still land if the audience knew exactly how it was made? (8) What's the one thing, and is the slogan fixed?
- **Evolution lesson (kept):** pair each named truth with an identity-consistent micro-commitment — the reader acts first, then constructs the belief to justify the action. "Discovery > persuasion."
