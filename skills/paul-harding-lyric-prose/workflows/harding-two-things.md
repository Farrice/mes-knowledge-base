# The Two Things — Literal Thing + Felt Experience (Description Becomes Character) (Paul Harding)

A creek is, objectively, a nice creek. One character sees it and thinks "means death." Harding's whole move is right there: he does not write *she was afraid of the water* — he gives you the creek, accurately, and then he gives you what the character's experience of the creek *is*, and now you know something about her that no stated trait could carry. "You let the reader experience the thing itself, and then you give them what the character's experience of it is." The feeling is never told; it is refracted through a real, precisely-seen thing. The gap between the neutral world and the charged perception is a narrative dimension — and because the reader was shown the true thing first, they *trust* the feeling. This workflow builds that double layer on purpose, then fuses it so the seam vanishes (Marilynne Robinson's *co-extensive* — no gears clanking between outside and inside).

## Pre-Flight

Read before executing — load these `genius.md` sections (do not paraphrase from memory; the patterns are precise, and this workflow is dangerous *because* the easiest failure is to skip the literal thing and just *tell the feeling*):

- **Pattern 6 — The Two Things (Literal Thing + Felt Thing)** — the core device: write the precise literal image first (the world, neutrally true), then layer the character's charged experience of it; keep both, mine the gap.
- **Pattern 5 — Description Becomes Character (Refraction)** — *what* gets noticed and *how* it's named is the characterization. The felt layer must be built from this character's private repertoire — their colors, their light, their kind of verb — not from a generic emotion word.
- **Pattern 4 — Co-extensivity (No Wall Between Inside and Outside)** — once both layers exist, dissolve the seam so a reader can't point to where description ends and character begins. No "she looked out and thought…".
- **Paul Harding Would Never... AN-3 (Explaining the Feeling / Landing a Lesson)** — telling the reader the emotion is "the most violent thing you could do." If a sentence's job is to inform the reader how to feel, it has failed this workflow's entire premise.
- **Paul Harding Would Never... AN-5 (The Clanking Gears)** — mechanical "now inside / now outside" switching; transition phrases that mark a gear-shift. Dissolve them.
- **Decision Framework Q3 (Am I giving the literal thing AND the felt experience — or explaining the feeling?)** and **Q4 (Is the seam between inside and outside showing?)** — the two gates this workflow lives or dies on.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` before writing a word. Specifically confirm **Q3** — you intend to give the precise literal thing *and* refract the feeling through it, NOT to state the emotion (if you've already written "she felt afraid / he was proud / it was devastating," you have skipped the observation and owe the reader the thing instead). And **Q4** — you will fuse the two layers into one fabric, not bolt an interior sentence onto an exterior one. Also confirm **Q1** (you slowed down to what's *literally* there, sense by sense, instead of reaching for meaning) and **Q2** (no received, off-the-rack language in either layer). **Honesty spine (commercial use):** the literal thing must be a *real, true* particular — Harding's craft makes a true thing land harder; it never manufactures a vivid detail to fake a feeling. A false "vivid" detail is the most dangerous failure here, because the better the craft, the more convincing the lie. If you cannot name a real literal thing to anchor the charge, stop — you don't have the scene yet.

## Input Required

- **The moment** — the specific emotionally loaded beat you're rendering. One or two sentences of plain situation is enough ("she comes home to the empty house after the funeral"; "he opens the box of his father's tools").
- **The character** (or brand / client / narrator) — *whose* perception this is. The felt layer is built from *their* repertoire, so we need to know who is doing the seeing.
- **The feeling you're tempted to state** — name it plainly, *to yourself, here, once* (grief, dread, relief, contempt, longing). You will NOT write this word in the passage. Naming it now is the diagnostic that tells you what the literal thing has to carry. (This is the feeling you owe the reader *through* a thing, never *as* a label.)
- **The literal anchor** (if you have one) — the real object/place/action present in the moment. If you don't have one yet, Step 1 finds it. **It must be true** (honesty spine).
- **Format** — fiction/memoir scene, social post, brand/marketing prose, converting copy, ghostwritten client voice. (Drives length, how much literal runway you get, and how the honesty spine applies.)

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Find the literal anchor; slow down to what is actually there (Pattern 6, Q1)

Before any feeling, name the *thing*. Not what it means — what it *is*. Harding's headstone line is "slow down": the most common failure is rushing past the moment toward the meaning. So sit in the scene and inventory what is literally present, sense by sense, as if you'd never seen it. Resist the first label your mind hands you — it is almost always habituated (AN-2).

| Sense | What is literally there? (neutral, true, no feeling-words) |
|---|---|
| **Sight** | (the exact thing — color, light, shape, the way light is *moving* on it) |
| **Sound** | (the actual sound, or the actual silence and what shapes it) |
| **Touch / body** | (temperature, weight, texture, what the body is doing) |
| **Smell / taste** | (if present — often the most un-received, the hardest to fake) |
| **Motion / change** | (Pattern 8: how is the thing *changing over time* — the light shifting, the surface moving?) |

Pick the **one literal anchor** that is most *charged for this character* (not most "beautiful"). The creek is just a creek; it's the anchor because *this* character's death-fear lives in it. The anchor is a real thing that the feeling has, for this person, attached itself to.

```
LITERAL ANCHOR (the real, true thing): __________
Why it's charged for THIS character (not stated in the passage — diagnostic only): __________
```

### Step 2 — Write the LITERAL layer: the world, neutrally true (Pattern 6, Pattern 8, honesty spine)

Render the anchor with **botanist's precision** — exactly, and *in motion* — and with **zero feeling**. No emotion words, no adjectives doing emotional work ("the lonely house," "the cruel light"). A neutral observer with no stake would agree with every word. This is the floor of trust: the reader must receive an accurate picture *before* they'll trust any charge you put on it.

- Describe the thing exactly, then describe how it *changes* (light shifting, surface moving, dust turning). Precision plus motion releases wonder — do not slather adjectives to make it "feel important" (AN-4).
- Drop received language. If a phrase arrived pre-fabricated ("a stream babbled," "sunlight streamed"), cut it and re-see the actual thing.
- **Honesty test:** every detail here is *literally true* of the real moment. No invented vividness.

> **The neutral-witness test:** read the literal layer to someone who doesn't know the character. They should be able to *picture* it and should *not* be able to tell you how anyone feels about it. If they can already feel the mood, you've smuggled the feeling in early — strip it back to the bare true thing.

### Step 3 — Write the FELT layer from THIS character's repertoire (Pattern 5, Pattern 6, AN-3)

Now — and only now — give the character's experience of the same thing. But do NOT state the emotion. State *how the anchor lands inside this specific perceiver*: what they notice in it, the verb they'd put on it, the unlike thing it becomes for them, the depth of field they register. Character is refracted through description; the felt layer *is* characterization.

The transcript's key example: a neutral creek + the experience "means death." The charge is not "she was scared." The charge is the creek perceived *as* something — the still water reading as a held breath, the cold of it reaching up before her feet touch, the far bank as a place she is being asked to cross.

| The literal anchor | Stated feeling (BANNED in passage) | The felt charge — the anchor *as this character experiences it* (the keeper) |
|---|---|---|
| (the real thing from Step 1) | (grief / dread / longing — diagnostic only) | (what it *becomes* for them: the unlike thing it turns into, the verb it takes, what it asks of them) |

Two guardrails:
- **Build from the repertoire (Pattern 5).** The colors, verbs, and depth of field must be *this character's* — the ones no other character in the world would use. A grieving father and a relieved daughter would not perceive the same kitchen with the same verbs. If the felt layer could belong to anyone, it's generic; re-see through *this* mind.
- **Counterpoint lives here (optional, Pattern 7).** The most charged felt layers often describe the thing in the verbs of its opposite category — the silent thing roaring, the still thing about to lunge. Mine the *verbs*; that's where the charge lives.

### Step 4 — Fuse the two layers; erase the seam (Pattern 4, AN-5, Q4)

You now have a true picture and a charged perception. The amateur move is to set them side by side with a hinge — *"The water was still and grey. She felt it meant death."* That hinge ("she felt") is the clanking gear. Dissolve it. Let the *way the thing is described already carry who is perceiving it*, so the reader can't locate where the world ends and the character begins.

| Clanking gears (AN-5) — DELETE | Co-extensive (Pattern 4) — KEEP |
|---|---|
| "She looked at the creek and thought it meant death." | "The creek lay flat and grey and waiting, the far bank further than it had any right to be." |
| "The house was empty. He felt the loss of her." | "Her coat was gone from the hook and the hook held the shape of where it had been." |
| Announcing the switch: *"and then she realized…"*, *"it made him feel…"* | Perception *is* interiority — the verb, the depth of field, the unlike thing do the feeling silently. |

Fuse by folding the felt charge *into* the literal description — make the precise true detail and the character's reading of it the same sentence, the same continuous fabric. The seam is gone when removing the felt charge would still leave a true sentence, and removing the literal anchor would leave nothing to stand on. Both load-bearing, neither announced.

### Step 5 — Read aloud; check the gap is doing the work, not a label (drummer's ear + Q3/Q4 final gate)

Read the fused passage aloud, cold, as a first-time reader. Run four checks:

- **The no-label test (Q3, AN-3):** is there any sentence whose job is to tell the reader the emotion? If you can point to the feeling-word, delete it and let the thing carry it. The reader should *arrive* at the feeling, not be handed it.
- **The no-gears test (Q4, AN-5):** can a reader point to where description ends and character begins? If there's an audible "now inside / now outside" click, re-fuse Step 4.
- **The recognition test (Pattern 10):** does the gap between the true thing and the charged perception make the reader *recognize* a felt truth ("yes — that's exactly how dread feels in an ordinary kitchen") rather than be *informed* of one? Recognition is the win; instruction is the failure.
- **The cadence test (Pattern 11):** does it play aloud — accents falling where the meaning wants them, no stray eighth note, no sing-song? Tune by ear.

If the passage states the feeling, or the gears clank, or the literal thing isn't true — return to the failing step. Do not ship a told feeling.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Fiction / memoir scene** | Native habitat. Full literal layer with botanist's precision and motion, then the felt charge folded in seamlessly. The character's repertoire (Pattern 5) accrues across the work — reserve their colors/verbs for them. This is where the Two Things has the most runway; use it. |
| **Social media (LinkedIn / X / Substack)** | Open on *one* precisely-seen real moment (the literal anchor), let the felt charge sit *under* it, and never close with "and that's when I learned…" (AN-3). The gap between the true detail and how it landed *is* the post's resonance. Depth, not width — one fused image can carry a whole point. Honesty spine: the moment must be real. |
| **Marketing / brand** | The brand's "character" emerges from what its world *notices* and how it names things (Pattern 5), not an adjectives-on-the-about-page list. Render the real product/experience precisely (and in motion) as the literal layer; let the felt charge — what it's *like* to live with it — refract through, never stated as a benefit-claim. The detail must be real. |
| **Converting copy (ad / landing / VSL)** | Give the reader the literal product detail (true, specific, neutral) *and* the felt experience of it — let them *infer* the benefit through recognition, which converts harder than a stated claim. The gap is the desire. **Honesty spine is strictest here:** the literal anchor must be a real feature/outcome; craft that makes a false detail vivid is the most dangerous failure. Pairs with copy-engine / luke-iha for the proof; Harding tunes the *felt* layer only. |
| **Ghostwriting (client voice)** | The felt layer must be built from the *client's* repertoire, not yours and not a master's (AN-6) — surrender to their grain. Find how *they* would perceive the anchor (their verbs, their depth of field), dissolve the seam in *their* register, tune cadence to *their* ear. The voice must read as theirs; the Two Things is a structure, not a style imposed on them. |

### Worked example A — Memoir scene (coming home after the funeral)

**Moment:** she comes back to the apartment the evening of her mother's funeral. · **Whose perception:** the daughter. · **Feeling owed (banned in passage):** grief, the specific kind that is also relief and is ashamed of the relief.

> **Literal anchor:** the kitchen — specifically her mother's mug, still on the drying rack from the last visit, and the late light coming across it.

**Literal layer (neutral, true, in motion):**
"The mug was upside down on the rack where it had dried days ago, a hairline of brown inside the rim the dishwater hadn't reached. The six o'clock light came in low and moved along the counter as she stood there, finding the mug, the chrome of the rack, the chip in the laminate, then sliding off the edge and onto the floor."

**Felt layer (the charge — banned: grief / relief / shame):**
The mug isn't a memory object; it's a thing still *waiting to be used by someone who isn't coming*. The moving light isn't pretty; it's *time continuing to happen in a room that should have stopped*. The relief she won't name is in how *quiet* it is — and the shame is in noticing the quiet as good.

**Fused passage (no seam, no told feeling):**
> "The mug was upside down on the rack where it had dried days ago, waiting, a hairline of brown inside the rim her mother would have rinsed and hadn't. The six o'clock light came in low and crossed the counter without hurrying, the way it had every evening she could remember and would now keep doing, finding the mug, the chrome, the chip in the laminate, and the apartment was very quiet, and the quiet was not unkind, and she stood in it and did not put the mug away."

*Why it lands:* a neutral witness would agree to the mug, the brown ring, the moving light. But "waiting," "her mother would have rinsed and hadn't," "would now keep doing," and the long held clause of the quiet that "was not unkind" refract grief, relief, and shame through the true thing — never naming one of them. The reader recognizes the feeling; they are never told it. The gears never clank: there is no "she thought," no "she felt." The not-putting-the-mug-away is the whole grief, undischarged.

### Worked example B — Converting copy (a hand-built knife, honesty spine live)

**Moment:** the reader holds the knife for the first time. · **Whose perception:** the buyer, a cook who has used cheap knives for years. · **Feeling owed (banned in passage):** the relief of a tool that finally meets the work. · **Honesty spine:** the knife is, truthfully, full-tang, hand-ground, and balanced at the bolster — real specs, not invented.

> **Literal anchor:** the balance point and the weight of the handle in the palm. (True feature.)

**Literal layer (neutral, true):**
"It is full tang — one piece of steel from edge to butt — and it balances at the bolster, so the weight sits in your palm and not out at the tip. Pick it up and it settles back toward your hand."

**Felt layer (the charge — banned: relief, ease):**
The cheap knives *fought* him — tip-heavy, asking to be wrestled. This one doesn't ask anything; it *waits where the cutting is*. The relief is recognition: the tool finally doing its share.

**Fused passage:**
> "Full tang — one piece of steel from edge to butt — balanced at the bolster, so the weight settles back into your palm instead of pulling out at the tip the way the others always did, the way you've spent ten years correcting for without noticing you were correcting. Pick it up and it sits where the work is. You stop bracing."

*Why it lands:* every literal claim is true and substantiated (full tang, balanced at bolster) — the honesty spine holds. The benefit ("relief," "ease") is never stated; it's refracted through "settles back into your palm," "the way the others always did," and the buyer's recognition of the bracing they didn't know they were doing. The reader *infers* the benefit and trusts it because they were shown the real mechanism first. "You stop bracing" is the felt charge landing as recognition, not as a claim.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Paul Harding Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (rows: **The Two Things (literal + felt)**, **Co-extensivity (no clanking gears)**, **Pre-Linguistic Attention**, **Recognition over Lesson**). Flag and fix before delivering.

- **Both things are present (Pattern 6):** the passage contains a precise, true literal layer *and* the character's charged experience of it. Not the feeling alone (told), not the neutral thing alone (inert). If only one is there, it has failed the device.
- **The feeling is never told (AN-3, Q3):** no sentence's job is to inform the reader of the emotion. No "she felt / he was / it was [emotion]." If you can point to the feeling-word, it's a told feeling — delete it and let the thing carry it. The reader arrives at the feeling; they are not handed it.
- **No clanking gears (AN-5, Q4):** a reader cannot locate where description ends and character begins. No "she looked out and thought…", no "and then she realized…", no hinge announcing the switch from outside to inside. The seam is gone; perception *is* interiority.
- **The literal layer is neutrally true (Pattern 8, honesty spine):** a neutral witness would agree to every word of it, and — in commercial use — every detail is a *real, substantiated* particular. No invented vividness; no adjective slathered to fake importance. The craft makes a true thing land; it never gilds a false one.
- **The felt layer is THIS character's (Pattern 5):** the charge is built from this perceiver's repertoire — their verbs, colors, depth of field. If the felt layer could belong to any character, re-see through *this* mind. You should be able to identify the character from the felt layer alone.
- **The gap is doing the work (Pattern 10):** the resonance comes from the distance between the true thing and the charged perception, landing as *recognition* ("that's exactly how it feels") — not from a stated point or a takeaway. Open the door; don't close it.
- **Precision over ornament, no received language (AN-2, AN-4):** the literal layer earns wonder through exactness and motion, not adjective-slather; neither layer leans on off-the-rack phrasing. Cadence plays aloud with no stray eighth note (Pattern 11).

## Common Pitfalls

- **Stating the feeling and calling it the felt layer (AN-3).** Writing "the literal thing: the creek. the felt thing: she was terrified." That second line is a *label*, not a felt layer — it's the exact violence Harding warns against. **Recovery:** re-run Step 3. The felt layer is never the emotion word; it's the anchor *re-perceived* — what the still water *becomes* for her (a held breath, a far bank further than it should be). Refract the fear through the creek; never name it.

- **The clanking hinge ("she felt / she thought / it made her…") (AN-5).** Building both layers correctly, then bolting them together with a connective that announces the switch from world to mind — the gears clank and the spell breaks. **Recovery:** Step 4. Delete the hinge and fold the charge *into* the literal sentence, so the way the thing is described already carries who's perceiving it. Removing the charge should still leave a true sentence; removing the anchor should leave nothing.

- **A vivid literal anchor that isn't true (honesty-spine breach).** In copy or brand work, inventing a "specific" detail because it lands well — the most dangerous failure, since the better the craft, the more convincing the lie. **Recovery:** before writing the literal layer, confirm the anchor is a *real* feature/object/moment. If you don't have a true one, that's a proof/research problem (route to copy-engine / luke-iha or go find the real detail) — never a thing the Two Things can manufacture. Devices make a true thing land harder; they cannot make a missing one exist.

- **A generic felt layer that could belong to anyone (Pattern 5 failure).** The charge is real but it's *anyone's* dread, *anyone's* relief — built from default emotion-perception rather than this character's specific repertoire. **Recovery:** Step 3, second guardrail. Ask what verbs, colors, and depth of field *this* perceiver — and no other character in the world — would put on the anchor. If a grieving father and a relieved daughter would perceive the same kitchen identically, you haven't refracted it through anyone yet.
