# Brand-Sensory World — Give a Brand Texture Through What It Notices (Paul Harding)

Most brand "worlds" are an adjective list wearing a coat: *bold, authentic, innovative, warm.* You could paste that paragraph onto a bank, a kombucha, or a funeral home and nothing would break — which means it characterizes nothing. Harding's deepest craft move solves exactly this: "character is always being refracted through description." A character in *Tinkers* has no stated traits; you know them entirely by what their world *notices* — the verbs around them, the colors and light they register, the depth of field they hold. Over a novel each character accrues a private "repertoire" of nouns, verbs, and palette no other character uses, and that repertoire *is* the character. This workflow takes that move cross-domain: a brand's character emerges the same way — from a *reserved perceptual signature* (its colors, its light, its kind of verb, the world it sees in focus) rendered as precise, in-motion sensory prose. The brand stops *telling* you it's warm and starts *noticing* warmly, in front of you. The honesty spine is absolute: the rendered world must map to the *real* product and experience — Harding's craft makes a true thing luminous; it never invents sensation to fake a soul.

## Pre-Flight

Read before executing — load these `genius.md` sections (do not paraphrase from memory; the patterns are precise, and this workflow is dangerous *because* the easiest failure is to slather "evocative" adjectives onto a brand and call it texture):

- **Pattern 5 — Description Becomes Character (Refraction)** — the core device: render character not by stating traits but by controlling *what gets noticed and how it's named*; reserve a private repertoire (colors / light / verbs / depth of field) for one entity alone. This is the brand's sensory signature.
- **Pattern 4 — Co-extensivity (No Wall Between Inside and Outside)** — the brand's worldview and the world it describes are one fabric. Don't write "we value craft, and here is our world"; let the way the world is *seen* already carry the values. Erase the seam between positioning and perception.
- **Pattern 7 — Counterpoint Description (Describe a Thing in Terms of Its Unlike)** — the move that makes a brand's repertoire *distinctive* rather than pretty: borrow the verb/noun register of an opposite category so the world is freshly seen. Mine the verbs — that's where a brand's character lives.
- **Cross-Domain Applications — Marketing / Brand row + the Honesty Spine** — "a brand's character emerges from what its world *notices* and how it names things, not an adjectives-on-the-about-page list… The brand's perceptual repertoire (its colors, light, verbs) becomes its signature." And: **the precisely-rendered detail must be a real, true particular** — a false vivid detail is the most dangerous failure, because the better the craft, the more convincing the lie.
- **Paul Harding Would Never... AN-4 (Ornamental "Racoo Baloney" / Showing Off)** — beautiful-sounding prose written to be admired, every word in the dictionary and nothing said; clutter that never got distilled. The single most likely way to wreck this workflow is to make the brand-world *pretty* instead of *characterizing*.
- **Decision Framework Q1 (slowed down to what's literally there), Q5 (precision over ornament), Q7 (no landed lesson/values-statement)** — the three gates this workflow lives or dies on.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` before writing a word. Specifically confirm **Q5** — you will render the brand's *real* world with precision and motion, NOT slather "evocative" adjectives to make it feel premium (if you've already written "rich, warm, timeless, artisanal," you're listing values, not building a repertoire — start over from a real noticed thing). And **Q7** — the brand world will *open a door* (the reader recognizes a character), never close on a values-statement or tagline-moral. Also confirm **Q1** (you slowed to what is *literally* present in the real product/space/ritual, sense by sense, before naming anything). **Honesty spine, non-negotiable:** every sensory particular in the rendered world must be *real* — a true material, a true light in the actual space, a true step in the actual ritual. This is a perception engine, not a fiction engine: it makes the brand's *true* texture visible, never manufactures a texture the brand doesn't have. If the brand's reality is thin, the deficit is positioning/product (route to oren-brand / build-bos), not something prose can paper over. **If you cannot name three real noticed things, stop — you don't have the world yet, you have a logo.**

## Input Required

- **The brand** — name, category, and the one-paragraph "about" / positioning as it currently stands (the thing that reads generic). Bring the *actual* current copy, not a summary — we're diagnosing real off-the-rack language.
- **The real material world of the brand** — the true particulars we have to draw from: the product itself (its materials, surfaces, how it's made, how it's used), the space (store / studio / kitchen / site), the ritual (what the customer actually *does* with it), the maker's hands and process. **These must be real.** If positioning exists (oren-brand / build-bos), bring the promise + voice + visual system so the repertoire stays coherent with it.
- **The character the brand IS (or wants to be)** — named *plainly, to yourself, here, once* (e.g. "a patient old craftsman," "a precise modern scientist who is secretly delighted," "a tough tender working-class kitchen"). You will NOT write this label in the brand world — it's the diagnostic that tells you which repertoire the world must hold. (Same discipline as the banned feeling-word in `harding-two-things`.)
- **Neighboring brands** (2–3 competitors) — so the repertoire can be *reserved*: a signature that two competitors could also claim is not a signature. We're finding the colors/verbs no neighbor would use.
- **Format & length** — about-page paragraph, homepage hero, brand bible "world" section, product-page sensory block, founder-letter, ghostwritten brand voice. (Drives runway and how the honesty spine applies.)

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Inventory the brand's REAL world, sense by sense; slow down past the brochure (Pattern 5, Q1, honesty spine)

Before any adjective, before any "feel," go to the *real* product/space/ritual and inventory what is *literally there* — sense by sense — as if you'd never seen a brand deck. Harding's headstone line is "slow down": the most common failure is rushing past the actual material toward the *positioning* (premium, authentic, bold). Resist the first label your mind hands you — "artisanal," "timeless," "elevated" are all received language (AN-2); they describe a *category aspiration*, not this object on this bench.

Fill this from the real thing in front of you. Every cell must be a *true* particular.

| Sense | What is literally there in the REAL product / space / ritual? (neutral, true, no brand-words) |
|---|---|
| **Sight (color + shape)** | (the actual colors — not "rich earth tones" but the specific dun, the green-black, the bruise-blue; the real shapes and surfaces) |
| **Light** | (the actual light in the real space, and *how it moves* — Pattern 8: low afternoon raking across the bench, the cold even fluorescence, the screen-glow) |
| **Sound** | (the real sounds of making/using — or the real silence and what shapes it) |
| **Touch / body** | (the true weight, temperature, grain, drag, give — what the hand actually meets) |
| **Smell / taste** | (if present — often the most un-received and hardest to fake; usually the most honest) |
| **Motion / process** | (the real verbs of how it's made and used — what actually *happens*, step by step) |

Now pull the **three to five most charged real particulars** — not the prettiest, the most *characterizing* (the ones that, for *this* brand's character, carry the most). These become the raw stock of the repertoire.

```
THREE-TO-FIVE REAL NOTICED THINGS (true particulars, the world's raw stock):
  1. __________
  2. __________
  3. __________
HONESTY SPINE: every one is a real material / light / sound / step in the actual product or ritual [confirmed]
THE CHARACTER (named here, BANNED in the brand world): __________
```

### Step 2 — Build the reserved REPERTOIRE: this brand's palette, light, and verbs (Pattern 5, the signature)

This is the heart of the workflow. A repertoire is the controlled vocabulary of *what this brand notices and how it names things* — and it is **reserved**: these colors, this light, these verbs belong to this brand alone, the way no two of Harding's characters share a palette. You are not writing prose yet; you are casting the brand's perceptual instrument.

Build all four columns from the Step-1 real particulars. The right-hand column (the *reserved* choice) is the work — the specific, un-received, characterizing version.

| Repertoire layer | What it controls | Generic default (the trap — DELETE) | This brand's RESERVED signature (built from real Step-1 things) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Palette** | the colors this world sees | "rich, warm, earthy" | (the specific named colors of the real materials — *dun, ox-blood, the green of weathered copper*) |
| **Light** | the quality + behavior of light | "bright and inviting" / "moody" | (the real light, in motion — *the low four o'clock light that finds the grain and leaves the room*) |
| **Verbs** | how things move / are made / are used | "crafted, designed, made" | (the true verbs of the real process — *planed, settled, worried smooth, banked, let rest*) ← **mine these hardest** |
| **Depth of field** | what's in focus vs. blurred — the *scale of attention* | (unstated) | (does this brand notice the single thumb-worn edge, or the whole horizon? the pore or the panorama?) |

Two non-negotiables:

- **Verbs carry the character (Pattern 5 + Pattern 7).** Harding: most of the magic lands in the verbs. A brand that *planes, settles, and lets rest* is a different soul from one that *snaps, fires, and ships*. Spend your effort here. Where a verb has gone habitual ("crafted," "engineered"), test **counterpoint** (Step 3) to break it open.
- **Reserve it against the neighbors.** Take each repertoire choice and ask: *would competitor A or B credibly claim this exact color / verb / depth of field?* If yes, it isn't a signature — push to the version only this brand, with its real world, could own. (This is the brand-equivalent of "you could identify the character from a paragraph of pure description.")

> **The repertoire test:** show the four-column repertoire to someone who knows the category and ask them to guess *what kind of being* this brand is — a craftsman? a scientist? a tough little kitchen? If they can't read a character off the colors/light/verbs alone, the repertoire is still generic; re-cast it from sharper real particulars. If they read the *wrong* character, your repertoire contradicts your Step-1 intent — reconcile.

### Step 3 — Counterpoint the repertoire so the world is freshly seen, not pretty (Pattern 7, AN-4)

A repertoire built only from expected words will be *coherent* but inert — a tasteful mood board, not a living mind. Harding breaks language out of received usage by describing a thing in the verbs/nouns of its *opposite* category: clouds (vaporous, silent) described as stone, as a stampeding herd. Apply the same to the brand's most habitual perceptions to snap them awake — while staying *literally true* of the real thing.

| The real thing's essential category | Borrow the register of its UNLIKE | The freshly-seen line (still literally true) |
|---|---|---|
| (e.g. a soft fabric — yielding, quiet) | (something hard, loud, geological) | (e.g. *"the wool holds its shape like cooled wax holds a thumbprint"* — true, and newly seen) |
| (e.g. a precise instrument — cold, exact) | (something warm, living, breathing) | (the cold steel rendered as something that *waits* and *settles*) |

Rules of the road:
- **Mine the verbs** — counterpoint lands hardest there (the silent thing roaring, the still thing about to lunge).
- **Stay literally true (honesty spine + Q1).** Counterpoint re-*sees* a real property; it never invents one. The wool really does hold its shape — you've just found the truer, stranger verb for it. If the counterpoint asserts something the product doesn't actually do, it's a lie wearing craft. Cut it.
- **Restraint (AN-4).** Counterpoint is a spice, not the meal. One or two startling perceptions per world; if every line is doing a metaphor-trick, the craft is showing and the brand reads as a writer flexing, not a character living.

### Step 4 — Write the SENSORY WORLD prose: let the world notice, co-extensively (Pattern 4, Pattern 8, AN-3)

Now write the brand world — a short, dense passage in which the brand's character *emerges entirely from what its world notices and how it's named*, using only the reserved repertoire. The reader should finish it knowing *who this brand is* without a single stated trait or value.

The discipline:
- **Render, don't declare (AN-3, Q7).** No "we believe in craft," no "warmth is at our core," no closing values-moral. Show the world *being noticed* the brand's way; the values are *refracted through* the perception, never stated. (The brand-equivalent of "never explain the feeling.")
- **Co-extensivity (Pattern 4).** Dissolve the seam between worldview and world. Don't write "we see the world differently — here it is." Let the *way* the world is described already be the worldview. No "and that's the kind of attention we bring to…" hinge — that's the clanking gear (AN-5) in brand drag.
- **Precision in motion releases the wonder (Pattern 8).** Describe the real thing exactly, then let it *change* — light moving across the grain, steam rising and going, the edge worn over years. Precision plus motion turns the ordinary product luminous. Do NOT reach for "beautiful" adjectives to install premium-ness; that's slather (AN-4).
- **Depth, not width (Pattern 9).** One paragraph that a reader could return to beats a page of texture. Compress the world *down*, dense and lucid, never cluttered.

> **The neutral-witness + character test (run both):** (1) *Neutral witness:* a stranger should be able to *picture* the real product/space from the prose — every sensory claim is true. (2) *Character read:* the same stranger, asked "what kind of being made this / lives here?", should land on your Step-1 character *without* you having named a trait. If they can picture it but read no character, you wrote a product description; if they read a character but can't trust the picture, you slathered. Both must pass.

### Step 5 — Coherence check: against positioning, against neighbors, against the honesty spine (the final gate)

Read the brand world aloud, cold, then run four checks. This is the step that separates a *signature* from a nice paragraph.

- **The "colors look different depending on what colors they're around" check (cross-domain coherence).** Harding's color-theory borrowing: a repertoire isn't judged in isolation — it's judged *against its neighbors*. Set your brand world beside competitor A's and competitor B's. Does yours read as a *distinct being*, or could a reader swap the logos? If it's swappable, the repertoire isn't reserved — return to Step 2 and sharpen until only this brand could have noticed this way.
- **The positioning-coherence check (Pattern 4 + oren-brand stack).** Does the world's repertoire *agree* with the brand's stated promise, voice, and visual system? A "patient craftsman" repertoire under a "move fast, disrupt" positioning is a contradiction the customer will feel as inauthenticity. If they fight, reconcile — usually the repertoire is the truer signal (it came from the *real* world), and the positioning copy is the part that drifted into received language. Flag it.
- **The honesty-spine re-check (the most dangerous failure).** Walk every sensory particular back to a real thing: this color is the *actual* material; this light is the *actual* light in the space; this verb is a step that *actually happens*. Any detail you can't anchor to reality is a craft-gilded lie — the better it reads, the more dangerous it is. Cut it or make it true. Devices make a true world luminous; they cannot make a missing one exist.
- **The cadence + no-ornament check (Pattern 11, AN-4).** Read aloud: does it *play* — accents where the meaning wants them, no stray eighth note, no sing-song? And: is there a single line whose only job is to be admired? If a sentence is "look what we can write," cut to depth instead of decoration.

If the world is swappable, contradicts positioning, contains an invented detail, or shows its craft — return to the failing step. Do not ship a pretty paragraph that characterizes nothing.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **About page / brand story** | Native habitat — the place where the "values list" usually lives, and where this workflow earns its keep most. Replace the adjective paragraph with one dense sensory-world paragraph that lets the reader *meet the character*. Keep any factual claims (founding year, what you make) true and plain; the *world* around them carries the soul. End by opening a door (the reader wants to know this being), never on a values-moral (AN-3). |
| **Marketing / campaign** | The brand world becomes the *atmosphere* a campaign lives inside — the reserved palette, light, and verbs cascade into headlines, captions, and art direction so every touchpoint *notices the same way*. Hand the repertoire (Step 2) to design/copy as a constraint, not a mood board. Coherence across touchpoints (Step 5, the "colors around colors" check) is the whole point: a campaign whose pieces notice differently has no character. |
| **Copywriting (product page / landing)** | Use the repertoire to render the *real* product sensorily so the reader *feels* it before they read a benefit — the literal true detail in the brand's reserved verbs, the felt charge inferred (pairs tightly with `harding-two-things` for the felt layer). **Honesty spine is strictest here:** every sensory claim is a real, substantiated feature/material — craft that makes a false detail vivid is the most dangerous failure. Harding tunes the *texture*; the offer/proof is copy-engine / luke-iha's job. |
| **Ghostwriting (brand or founder voice)** | The repertoire must be built from *this* brand's / founder's real world and grain — never yours, never a master's, never a competitor's borrowed palette (AN-6). Surrender to *their* materials, *their* light, *their* verbs; dissolve the seam between their worldview and the world they describe, in *their* register; tune cadence to *their* ear. The world must read as *theirs*. |
| **Social (brand account, LinkedIn / IG / Substack)** | One precisely-seen real moment from the brand's world, in the reserved verbs — the workshop at the hour the light comes low, the one real step of the ritual — and *never* close with "and that's our commitment to quality" (AN-3). The gap between the true noticed thing and the character it reveals *is* the post. Depth over width: one fused image can carry the whole brand. The moment must be real. |
| **Internal (brand bible / team alignment)** | Document the four-column repertoire (Step 2) explicitly *as a tool* — palette, light, verbs, depth of field — so writers and designers can deploy a consistent signature without you in the room. Pair the worked sensory-world paragraph as the exemplar. This is the deterministic backstop: the repertoire table, not a vibe, is what keeps the brand coherent at scale. |

### Worked example A — About page (a one-person furniture workshop, honesty spine live)

**Brand:** a maker of hardwood chairs and benches. · **Character it IS (banned in prose):** a patient old craftsman who is not sentimental, only exact. · **Neighbors:** a "modern Scandinavian" flat-pack brand (palette: pale, airy, *designed*); a "luxury heritage" brand (palette: mahogany, brass, *timeless*). · **Honesty spine:** the wood, the hand-planing, the four-o'clock window light, the oil finish are all real and verifiable in the actual shop.

**Diagnosis (generic before):**
> *"We craft timeless, heirloom-quality furniture using traditional techniques and the finest sustainable hardwoods. Our passion for authentic craftsmanship is woven into every piece."*
> Received language flagged: *timeless, heirloom-quality, finest, passion, authentic craftsmanship, woven into every piece.* Paste it onto either neighbor and nothing breaks. It characterizes no one.

**Reserved repertoire (built from the real shop):**
- **Palette:** the dun of unfinished ash, the honey the oil pulls up out of it overnight, the grey of bench dust, the dark of the chamfered edge where the hand rests most. (Not "rich warm tones" — the *specific* colors of the actual wood and process.)
- **Light:** the four o'clock light that comes in low across the bench, finds the grain raised by the plane, and leaves the room by five. (Real light; in motion.)
- **Verbs:** *planed, eased, worried smooth, let rest, oiled, set down.* (No "crafted," no "designed." The true verbs of the real work — and the brand's whole patience is in *let rest* and *worried smooth*.)
- **Depth of field:** the single thumb-worn edge of a chair arm, not the showroom. (This brand notices the *one worn place a hand will find*, not the panorama.)
- **Counterpoint (real property re-seen):** the ash, sanded and oiled, *holds the light the way still water holds it* — a hard, dry thing rendered in the register of a liquid surface. True (oiled ash really does pool light), freshly seen.

**The brand-sensory world (deliverable):**
> "The ash comes in pale as bone and stays that way until the oil goes on, and then overnight the grain pulls up a honey it had been keeping. The four o'clock light comes in low across the bench, finds the ridges the plane raised, lays a long shine down the seat, and is gone by five. He does not hurry the chamfer where a thumb will rest a thousand times; he eases it, sets it down, worries it smooth, and lets the piece rest a day before the oil — and the oiled wood holds that low light the way still water holds it, only drier, only warmer, only meant to be sat in."

*Why it works:* not one stated trait — no "patient," no "exact," no "authentic." But you *meet* the craftsman in *eases, worries smooth, lets rest, does not hurry, a thumb will rest a thousand times.* The repertoire (dun/honey palette, the four-o'clock raking light in motion, the slow verbs, the worn-edge depth of field) is reserved — neither the flat-pack nor the heritage neighbor could have noticed this way; one would say *clean lines*, the other *brass and mahogany*. Every particular is real (the wood, the oil, the window, the chamfer). The counterpoint ("holds light the way still water holds it") snaps the world awake while staying true. It opens a door — you want to sit in the chair — and closes on no values-moral.

### Worked example B — Product-page sensory block (a small-batch hot sauce, honesty spine live)

**Brand:** fermented hot sauce, one founder, a home kitchen scaled up. · **Character it IS (banned):** a precise, slightly obsessive cook who treats fire as a craft, not a dare. · **Neighbors:** a "macho extreme heat" brand (palette: red-black, skull iconography, verbs: *destroy, burn, dare*); a "gourmet artisanal condiment" brand (palette: cream label, gold foil, verbs: *curate, elevate, pair*). · **Honesty spine:** the 21-day ferment, the specific peppers, the vinegar's pulled-back sharpness, the slow build of heat are all real and true of the product.

**Diagnosis (generic before):**
> *"A bold, artisanal hot sauce, lovingly crafted in small batches with the finest ingredients for an unforgettable flavor experience."*
> Flagged: *bold, artisanal, lovingly crafted, small batches, finest, unforgettable flavor experience.* Swappable onto either neighbor. No character.

**Reserved repertoire (from the real kitchen):**
- **Palette:** the dull brick-red of the fermented mash (not bright "fire" red), the cloudy gold of the strained vinegar, the green-black of the charred pepper skins.
- **Light / texture:** the slow rise of CO₂ bubbles in the ferment jar over three weeks; the way the sauce coats the back of a spoon and lets go slowly.
- **Verbs:** *ferments, settles, builds, opens, lingers, pulls back* — never *destroys / burns / dares.* (The whole "fire-as-craft, not dare" character is in *builds* and *pulls back*.)
- **Depth of field:** the *back* of the palate and the *second* after the swallow — this brand notices the slow second movement of heat, not the first hit.
- **Counterpoint (real, re-seen):** the heat *arrives like weather, not like a match* — a slow front rolling in, not a struck flare. True (a 21-day ferment really does build heat slowly), freshly seen.

**The brand-sensory world (deliverable):**
> "It sits in the jar for twenty-one days, dull brick-red, and you can watch the slow rise of bubbles work the mash while it ferments and settles into itself. On the spoon it's cloudy gold at the edge, and it coats the back and lets go slowly. The heat doesn't strike — it builds, the way weather builds, a front moving in across the back of the palate a full second after you've swallowed and thought you were clear; it opens, lingers, and pulls back, and leaves you wanting the next bite, not water."

*Why it works:* no "bold," no "artisanal," no stated character. But the *precise, obsessive cook who respects fire* is fully present in *ferments and settles into itself, builds the way weather builds, opens, lingers, pulls back, leaves you wanting the next bite, not water.* The repertoire is reserved against both neighbors — the macho brand would say *destroy*, the gourmet brand would say *pair*; neither would notice the *second after the swallow*. Every particular is true (the 21-day ferment, the brick-red mash, the slow heat-build, the vinegar). The counterpoint ("arrives like weather, not like a match") re-sees the real slow build. It opens a door, lands on no moral, and you can picture *and* trust every word.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Paul Harding Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Expert-Specific Quality Rubric (rows: **Pre-Linguistic Attention**, **Precision-as-Wonder (no ornament)**, **Recognition over Lesson**, **Co-extensivity (no clanking gears)**). Flag and fix before delivering.

- **Character emerges from what's noticed, not from traits (Pattern 5):** the brand world contains *no stated trait or value* ("warm," "authentic," "we believe in…"), yet a stranger can read the intended character off the prose. If the character is *told*, it has failed the device; if no character is readable, it's a product description.
- **The repertoire is reserved (Pattern 5, the "colors around colors" check):** set against 2–3 neighbors, the world is not swappable — only this brand, with its real materials, could have noticed this way. If a competitor could credibly claim the same palette/verbs, sharpen until they can't.
- **Verbs carry the load (Pattern 5 + Pattern 7):** the brand's character lives primarily in its *verbs*, which are the true verbs of the real process — not "crafted / designed / made." Counterpoint is used (1–2 times) to re-see a real property freshly, never to assert a false one.
- **Precision in motion, no ornament (Pattern 8, AN-4):** the world earns its luminosity through *exact, moving* description of the real thing — not through "beautiful" premium adjectives slathered on to feel expensive. No sentence exists only to be admired. If the craft is *visible*, cut to depth.
- **Co-extensive — no clanking gears (Pattern 4, AN-5):** the worldview and the world are one fabric; there's no "and that's the kind of attention we bring to everything we do" hinge welding a values-statement onto the description. The seam is gone.
- **Recognition, not a values-moral (Pattern 10, AN-3, Q7):** the world *opens a door* — the reader recognizes a being they want to know — and closes on no lesson, no tagline-moral, no "our commitment to quality." Open, never close.
- **Honesty spine intact (cross-domain, the most dangerous failure):** every sensory particular walks back to a real material / light / sound / step. No invented vividness. The craft makes the brand's *true* texture luminous; it never gilds a texture the brand doesn't have. A false vivid detail is the worst failure here — the better the prose, the more convincing the lie.
- **Coherent with positioning (Pattern 4, oren-brand stack):** the repertoire agrees with the brand's promise, voice, and visual system; any contradiction is reconciled or flagged (usually the repertoire, drawn from the real world, is the truer signal).

## Common Pitfalls

- **Slathering "evocative" adjectives and calling it texture (AN-4).** Replacing "we value craft" with "rich, warm, timeless, sumptuous, artisanal craftsmanship" — a *prettier* adjective list, still an adjective list, still characterizing nothing. The mistake is treating sensory = more adjectives. **Recovery:** re-run Steps 1–2. Texture comes from precise *real noticed things* in reserved *verbs*, in motion — not from stacking modifiers. Strip every "evocative" adjective, return to a true particular on the real bench, and let the verb do the work. If you can't picture the literal thing, you're decorating, not describing.

- **A pretty world that any competitor could own (Pattern 5 / reservation failure).** The prose is lovely and coherent — and reads identically to the category's other "premium handcrafted" brands, because the palette and verbs are the genre's defaults, not this brand's. **Recovery:** Step 5, the "colors around colors" check. Set it beside 2–3 neighbors. Wherever a neighbor could claim your color/verb/depth of field, push to the version only *this* brand's real world could produce. A signature that two competitors share is not a signature.

- **Welding a values-statement onto the world (AN-3 / AN-5).** Building a genuine sensory world and then closing with "…and that's the timeless quality we pour into everything we make" — the clanking gear in brand drag, the door slammed shut on a moral. **Recovery:** Step 4 + Step 5. Delete the hinge and the moral. The values must be *refracted through* the perception, never stated after it. If the world is doing its job, the reader already feels the value; saying it out loud is the violence Harding warns against, and it instantly cheapens what the prose earned.

- **A vivid detail that isn't true (honesty-spine breach — the most dangerous).** Inventing a "specific" sensory particular because it lands beautifully — a light that isn't in the actual space, a material the product isn't actually made of, a step that doesn't happen. The most dangerous failure precisely because the better the craft, the more convincing the lie, and a customer who meets the real product will feel the betrayal. **Recovery:** Step 5 honesty re-check. Walk every particular back to a real thing in the actual product/space/ritual. Any you can't anchor, cut or make true. If the brand's real world is too thin to render, that's a positioning/product gap (route to oren-brand / build-bos) — prose cannot manufacture a soul the brand doesn't have. Harding's whole faith is "the world means itself": render the *true* world precisely and the wonder is released. There is nothing to fake.
