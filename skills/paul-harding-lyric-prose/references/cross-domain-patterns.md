# Paul Harding — Cross-Domain Patterns

> How the lyric-prose genius behind *Tinkers* transfers out of fiction and into commercial writing. Harding's load-bearing moves — slow pre-linguistic attention, the literal chain, the two things, description-as-character, counterpoint, precision-as-wonder, recognition-over-argument, clutter-then-distill — operate on the **reader's perception and felt recognition**, not on the subject matter. That makes them domain-agnostic.
>
> **Honesty spine (non-negotiable, every commercial domain).** In fiction the precisely-rendered detail is invented; in marketing, copy, and ghostwriting it must be a *real, true* particular. Harding's craft makes a true thing land harder — it never manufactures sensation to fake authenticity. A false "vivid" detail is the single most dangerous failure here: the better the craft, the more convincing the lie. Render real things precisely. Never invent the texture.

---

## How to read the before/after examples

Each "before" is the off-the-rack version a competent writer ships — not a strawman, but the default that scores fine and dies in the feed. Each "after" deploys a named Harding move. The point is never "add flourish." It is the opposite: **the after is usually shorter, plainer, and more exact** — because precision releases the wonder the adjectives were trying to fake.

## 1. Social Media (LinkedIn / X / Substack)

**Moves that earn their place:** Slow pre-linguistic attention (Pattern 2) · the literal chain (Pattern 3) · recognition over lesson (Pattern 10) · depth, not width (Pattern 9).

The feed's gravity is toward the abstract claim and the "here's what I learned" close. Both are received language. Harding's correction: open on one precisely-seen real moment, let the post *dilate* the reader into recognition, and end by opening a door instead of handing over a takeaway the reader can file and forget.

**Before (the lesson-engine default):**
> Consistency is the most underrated skill in business. I learned this the hard way after years of starting and quitting. The lesson? Show up even when you don't feel like it. That's how you win.

This explains the feeling, lands a tautological lesson ("show up even when you don't feel like it — no kidding"), and closes the door. AN-3.

**After (open on the literal thing, persuade by recognition):**
> Year three, a Tuesday. The cursor blinking in an empty doc at 6:14 a.m., coffee already cold because I'd reheated it twice and forgotten it twice. I'd shipped 400 posts. This was the one I didn't want to write — and the only difference between this morning and the morning I quit the first time was that I didn't have anywhere else I'd rather be failing.

No stated lesson. The reader supplies "consistency" themselves — and trusts it because they recognized the cold-coffee Tuesday, not because they were told. The post is short and dense (depth, not width). Recognition: *somebody finally put texture to that.*

**Workflow:** `/harding-perception-engine` to build the opening moment; `/harding-recognition-audit` to strip any lesson and verify the door opens.

## 2. Marketing / Brand

**Moves that earn their place:** Description-becomes-character / refraction (Pattern 5) · the two things (Pattern 6) · botanist's precision releases wonder (Pattern 8).

A brand's "character" does not live in an adjectives-on-the-about-page list ("bold, authentic, human"). It lives in **what the brand's world notices and how it names things** — its private repertoire of nouns, verbs, light, and palette that no competitor uses. Render the real product or experience precisely, and in *motion*, and the ordinary turns luminous without a single "premium" adjective doing the lifting.

**Before (adjective-stacking the about page):**
> We're a bold, authentic, human-first coffee company obsessed with quality and committed to crafting unforgettable, premium experiences for our passionate community.

Every word is received. Nothing is noticed; everything is asserted. AN-2, AN-4.

**After (character emerges from what the brand notices, rendered with precision + motion):**
> We roast in eleven-pound batches because that's the largest amount one person can keep their eyes on. You can hear the first crack at minute nine — a sound like rain starting on a tin roof — and the second crack, faster, near minute twelve, the one we usually pull just before. The beans go from the color of raw cedar to the color of a worn saddle, and the smell in the room shifts from grass to caramel to something almost like toast about to burn. We stop it there.

No "premium," no "passionate." The brand's character (obsessive, hand-scaled, attentive, slightly anti-industrial) is *refracted through what it notices* — the eleven-pound limit, the sound of cracks, the color shift over time (precision + calculus). The reader infers the quality. That noticing-signature becomes the brand's reusable repertoire.

**Workflow:** `/harding-perception-engine` for the core brand-world rendering; `/harding-precision-wonder` whenever brand copy starts slathering "premium/bold/unforgettable."

## 3. Copywriting (Ad / Landing / VSL)

**Moves that earn their place:** The two things (Pattern 6) · counterpoint description (Pattern 7) · botanist's precision (Pattern 8) · **honesty spine**.

Feature/benefit lists tell the reader the benefit. A stated claim is a thing the reader has to *decide whether to trust*. The Two Things gives the literal product detail (true) plus the felt experience of it, and lets the reader **infer** the benefit by recognition — which they trust more than any claim, because they reached it themselves. The detail must be real. Always.

**Before (feature/benefit list — tells the benefit):**
> Our mattress uses premium memory foam for the ultimate in comfort and support, so you'll finally get the deep, restful sleep you deserve and wake up refreshed and ready to crush your day.

Stated benefit ("deep restful sleep," "wake up refreshed"), received language ("crush your day," "you deserve"). The reader has to take it on faith.

**After (the literal thing + the felt thing — benefit inferred by recognition):**
> The foam takes about four seconds to forget the shape of your hand after you press it. So at 2 a.m. when you roll from your back to your side — the toss that used to wake you all the way up because the old mattress shoved back — there's nothing to argue with. It just lets you go, and closes the space behind you. You don't wake up. That's the whole product: the night you don't remember having.

The literal, *true* detail (four-second recovery; the press test the reader can verify) carries the felt experience (the 2 a.m. roll that no longer wakes you). The benefit — better sleep — is never stated; it's recognized. Counterpoint quietly at work ("nothing to argue with," "closes the space"). Honesty gate: every detail is a real, checkable product property.

**Workflow:** `/harding-perceptual-copy` is purpose-built for this — it runs the honesty gate (inventory the *real* particulars first), then the Two Things, then verifies benefit-by-recognition. Use `/harding-counterpoint` to snap a familiar product category awake.

## 4. Ghostwriting

**Moves that earn their place:** Brain-as-fingerprint / surrender to the grain (Hidden Knowledge) · co-extensivity (Pattern 4) · drummer's cadence (Pattern 11).

The ghostwriter's whole job is the inverse of self-expression: surrender to the *client's* grain, not yours. Harding: "Anybody who imitates anybody sounds like a fool" — and that includes imitating a master *onto* a client. Find the client's natural register, dissolve the seam between their world and their worldview (co-extensivity), and tune the cadence to *their* ear. The voice must read as unmistakably theirs.

**Before (generic LinkedIn-thought-leader voice stamped onto every client):**
> I've been reflecting on leadership lately. The best leaders don't just manage — they inspire. They create cultures where people feel seen and valued. Here are three things I've learned about building high-performing teams.

This is no one's grain. It's the homogenized default — and it would read identically over any executive's headshot. AN-6.

**After (surrendered to a specific client's grain — a blunt former line-cook turned ops VP):**
> Ran a kitchen for nine years before I ran anything else. You learn fast that "morale" is a word people use in offices. On the line it's narrower: does the guy next to you cover your station when the printer's ten tickets deep and you're in the weeds, or does he let you drown to look fast? Every team I've built since, that's the only question I'm actually asking when I hire. Not "culture fit." Will you cover the station.

The cadence is short, declarative, kitchen-grained ("in the weeds," "ten tickets deep"). World and worldview are co-extensive — the kitchen *is* his theory of teams, no seam announced. No master is being imitated; the register is surrendered to *this* person. Tune it by reading aloud in the client's voice.

**Workflow:** `/harding-cadence` to tune the line-music to the client's ear (the final polish layer). Pair with `/harding-perception-engine` to ground the client's claims in their *real* remembered moments rather than borrowed abstractions.

## 5. Entrepreneurship / Founder Narrative

**Moves that earn their place:** Improvise toward emergence (Pattern 1) · ambition-as-reverence (Hidden Knowledge) · get-it-wrong-particularly (Hidden Knowledge) · the literal chain (Pattern 3).

The founder origin story is almost always retrofitted into a hero template — "I saw a problem, I had the vision, I never gave up." That's an outline filled with words. Harding's correction: the real story is *uncovered* by following real moments, not designed into an arc. Frame ambition as reverence for the thing being built (not "I want to be the founder who…"), and treat the early wrong versions as the instrument that found the right one — not as embarrassments to hide.

**Before (retrofitted hero template):**
> I always knew I wanted to build something that mattered. I saw a massive problem in the market, trusted my vision, and despite the doubters, I never gave up. Today, we're proud to be transforming the industry.

Pure template. No real moment survives; the ambition is ego-shaped ("something that mattered," "the doubters"). AN-6, and the literal chain is absent.

**After (uncovered from real moments; ambition as reverence; the wrong version as the instrument):**
> The first version was a spreadsheet I emailed to four restaurant owners. Three never replied. The fourth wrote back one line: "this is solving a problem I don't have." She was right, and it stung for about a week before I understood she'd handed me the actual problem for free — the one underneath the one I'd assumed. I rebuilt it around her sentence. The thing I wanted to make well was never the spreadsheet. It was a tool that would have made *her* reply on the first try.

The narrative follows literal moments ("then what?" — the spreadsheet, the four emails, the one reply, the rebuild). The wrong version (the spreadsheet) is the instrument, not a confession. Ambition is reverence (make the *thing* well), not ego (be the founder). The reader recognizes the founder's mind at work rather than being sold a legend.

**Workflow:** `/harding-improv-draft` (Light Out Without a Map) to generate the origin narrative from one real seed moment without a template; `/harding-then-what` to build any pivotal scene link-by-true-link.

## 6. Content Creation (Essays / Newsletters / Long-Form)

**Moves that earn their place:** Re-see the ordinary (Pattern 2) · the literal chain (Pattern 3) · recognition over argument (Pattern 10) · clutter-then-distill (Pattern 13) · depth, not width (Pattern 9).

Most content is a hot-take engine: a claim, three supporting points, a CTA. Harding offers a *perception engine* instead — make the overlooked everyday luminous through precise attention, so the piece resonates rather than instructs. Overwrite first (let everything that pulls at you onto the page), then distill to a dense, lucid piece a reader wants to return to. End by opening a door.

**Before (hot-take + listicle scaffold):**
> Why most productivity advice fails. Here's the truth: it's not about doing more, it's about doing less. In this post I'll cover the 3 reasons your to-do list is sabotaging you and the 1 mindset shift that changes everything.

Received structure, "it's not X, it's Y" formula, promised takeaways. The reader files it and forgets it. AN-3.

**After (re-see the ordinary; literal chain; recognition; distilled):**
> There's a particular silence in the ten seconds after you close the laptop on a day where you crossed off everything and finished nothing. The list is clean. The screen goes dark and reflects the room back at you — the unwashed mug, the chair you bought to fix your back, the window you keep meaning to open. Eleven things, done. And the one thing the whole day was supposedly *for* is still sitting exactly where it was at 9 a.m., untouched, because it was never on the list. It couldn't be. You don't know how to start it yet.

No "3 reasons," no mindset-shift promise. The piece re-sees a real, overlooked moment (the dark-screen reflection) and lets the reader recognize the false-productivity trap themselves — and *keep thinking about it*, because no door was closed. Dense and lucid; substantive in a short space.

**Workflow:** `/harding-perception-engine` to build the luminous core; `/harding-distill` (Systole & Diastole) to carve an overwritten draft back to density; `/harding-recognition-audit` to kill any listicle-takeaway residue.

## Cross-domain failure modes (watch for these in *every* domain)

| Failure | What it looks like in commercial writing | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The convincing lie (honesty-spine breach)** | A beautifully-rendered "vivid" detail that isn't a real product/founder/client particular. The craft makes a false thing land. | Run the honesty gate *before* the craft: inventory only real, checkable particulars. Then render. |
| **Slather to fake importance** | Stacking "premium / bold / unforgettable / transformative" to make a thing feel significant. | `/harding-precision-wonder`: describe it more *exactly*, add motion, drop every "beautiful" adjective. |
| **Lesson-landing** | "Here's what I learned…", "The takeaway is…", "It's not X, it's Y." | `/harding-recognition-audit`: cut the lesson, open a door, let the reader recognize it. |
| **Outline-then-fill** | Template-shaped origin stories and listicle content with no real moment in them. | `/harding-improv-draft`: start from one real seed and follow the literal chain. |
| **Imitation onto a client** | Stamping a master's (or your own) voice onto a ghostwriting client. | `/harding-cadence` + surrender to the client's grain; never pastiche. |

> **The single transfer rule across all six domains:** *the world means itself.* Render the real thing precisely and let the reader recognize the meaning. Every commercial failure above is a failure of faith — the writer didn't trust the true thing to carry, so they slathered, explained, or invented. Trust the thing.
