# Belief-First Audience Intelligence

**Cross-Stack: McRaney Belief Change Architecture × Dai Media Consumer Posture**

> **Load before executing**: `skills/david-mcraney-belief-change/genius.md` + `skills/dai-media-consumer-posture/genius.md`

## What This Produces

A **Belief-Aware Consumer Posture Profile** — a document that answers not just "who is this person?" but "what do they believe, why do they believe it, what would it take to change their mind, and how should you talk to them given all of that?"

Traditional audience research produces demographics + psychographics. This produces **belief architecture + processing route diagnosis + threshold calculations + consumer posture** — a fundamentally different and deeper intelligence layer.

## When to Deploy

- Entering a new market where you don't understand why prospects resist
- Sales/content consistently gets agreement but no conversion (assimilation without accommodation)
- Existing ICP research feels shallow — you know WHAT they want but not WHY they won't act
- Cross-stacking with `/icp-deep-dive` to add belief-layer depth to Dai Media profiles
- Launching a contrarian or paradigm-shifting offer that requires genuine belief change

## Phase 1: Belief Excavation

**Uses**: McRaney Patterns 1 (Resistance Hierarchy), 4 (Processing Chain), 17 (Emotional Sediment), 26 (Metacognitive Unlock)

Map the belief landscape for your target audience in your category.

### Execute

For the target audience in the specific category/offer context, answer:

1. **Surface Beliefs** — What does this audience EXPLICITLY say about this topic? What are their stated positions, common objections, and go-to arguments?
2. **Belief Classification** — For each surface belief, classify using the Resistance Hierarchy:
   - **Fact-level** (updateable with evidence)
   - **Attitude-level** (requires emotional/experiential shift)
   - **Value-level** (needs social permission + identity bridging)
   - **Identity-level** (requires paradigm reconstruction + face-saving narrative)
3. **Processing Chain Trace** — For the 2-3 most critical beliefs, trace backwards:
   - What was the **origin experience** that seeded this belief?
   - What **emotional reaction** formed around it?
   - What **social group** reinforced it?
   - What **identity** integrated it?
   - What **evidence** was recruited to support it?
4. **Emotional Sediment Map** — What past failures, social rejections, authority betrayals, or formative experiences cemented these beliefs? Map the sediment layers.
5. **Metacognitive Accessibility** — How self-aware is this audience about their own reasoning? High metacognitive accessibility = central-route persuasion may work. Low = must activate metacognition first via process questions.

### Output Format

```
## Belief Excavation: [Audience] × [Category]

### Surface Belief Map
| Belief | Classification | Strength (1-10) | Social Anchoring |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| ...    | ...           | ...             | ...             |

### Processing Chains (Top 3 Critical Beliefs)
**Belief 1**: [statement]
- Origin: [formative experience]
- Emotion: [what they feel about this]
- Social Reinforcement: [who/what keeps it alive]
- Identity Integration: [how it became "who I am"]
- Evidence Recruited: [what they cite to support it]

### Emotional Sediment Layers
[Narrative description of the emotional history that created these beliefs]

### Metacognitive Profile
- Self-awareness level: [High/Medium/Low]
- Recommended activation strategy: [process questions / experience design / social proof]
```

## Phase 2: ELM Route Diagnosis

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 24 (ELM Route Selection), Pattern 25 (Rebuttal Type Detection)

Determine HOW this audience is processing information in your category.

### Execute

1. **Motivation Assessment** — Score 1-10:
   - Does this topic affect them personally? (+1-3)
   - Are there consequences for being wrong? (+1-3)
   - Do they have personal stake in the outcome? (+1-4)
2. **Ability Assessment** — Score 1-10:
   - Do they have domain knowledge to evaluate arguments? (+1-3)
   - Do they have time/bandwidth for deep processing? (+1-3)
   - Are they in a context conducive to reflection? (+1-4)
3. **Route Determination**:
   - Both ≥ 7 → **Central route active**: Lead with evidence, logic, novel arguments
   - Either < 7 → **Peripheral route active**: Lead with credibility cues, social proof, visual quality, likability
   - Mixed (one high, one low) → **Dual strategy**: Peripheral to capture attention, central for the motivated subset
4. **Dominant Rebuttal Type** — For this audience, what type of resistance is most likely?
   - **Technique rebuttal** ("you're trying to sell me") → Deploy transparency + permission
   - **Topic rebuttal** ("your claims are wrong") → Deploy better evidence + strategic concession

### Output Format

```
## ELM Route Diagnosis: [Audience] × [Category]

Motivation Score: [X]/10 | Ability Score: [X]/10
Active Route: [Central / Peripheral / Dual]
Dominant Rebuttal Type: [Technique / Topic / Mixed]

### Messaging Implications
- Lead with: [what type of content/proof/signal]
- Avoid: [what will trigger resistance]
- Rebuttal counter-strategy: [matched to type]
```

## Phase 3: Threshold Calculation

**Uses**: McRaney Patterns 5 (Social Death Calculation), 13 (Threshold Equation + 30% calibration), 18 (Minimum Viable Change Path), Hidden Knowledge 13 (30% Empirical Calibration)

Calculate the precise cost of belief change for this audience.

### Execute

1. **Score the Threshold Equation** (each variable 1-10):
   - **Change Drivers**: Trigger + Trusted Source + Social Safety + Identity Bridge = [sum]
   - **Change Resistors**: Investment + Social Risk + Identity Threat + Uncertainty = [sum]
   - **Binding Constraint**: The single variable that, if changed, would tip the balance
2. **Social Death Calculation** across four dimensions:
   - **Tribal cost**: Who knows they hold this position? What group membership depends on it?
   - **Face cost**: What face would they lose by changing?
   - **Relationship cost**: What relationships are at risk?
   - **Status cost**: What status or authority depends on this belief?
3. **30% Calibration Check**:
   - Estimate current counter-evidence burden available (0-100%)
   - If Social Risk + Identity Threat > 12: you're in high-cost territory — stop adding evidence, start reducing cost
   - If < 12: calculate evidence gap to 30% threshold
4. **Minimum Viable Intervention**: What is the single sentence, experience, or proof point that would tip this?

### Output Format

```
## Threshold Calculation: [Audience] × [Change Requested]

### Equation
Drivers: [sum] | Resistors: [sum] | Gap: [+/- number]
Binding Constraint: [specific variable]

### Social Death Scores
| Dimension | Score (1-10) | Key Risk |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| Tribal    | ...         | ...      |
| Face      | ...         | ...      |
| Relationship | ...      | ...      |
| Status    | ...         | ...      |

### 30% Calibration
Evidence available: ~[X]% | Social Cost Multiplier: [X]x
Effective threshold: ~[X]% | Evidence gap: [X]%
Strategy: [Add evidence / Reduce cost / Both]

### Minimum Viable Intervention
[One sentence describing the smallest change that tips the balance]
```

## Phase 4: Consumer Posture Translation

**Uses**: Dai Media Patterns 2 (Individual Before Community), 3 (Kristen Stewart Test), 4 (Consumer Posture Architecture), 7 (Emotional Outcome), 14 (Analog Identity Marker); Tacit Knowledge 2 (Co-Conspirators), 3 (Waiting to Be Articulated)

Translate the belief intelligence into a Dai Media consumer posture profile — enriched with belief-layer depth.

### Execute

1. **Individual Finder** (Dai Media Pattern 2): From Phase 1's belief map, find the ONE specific, findable individual who embodies this belief structure. Not a composite — a real person.
2. **Kristen Stewart Test** (Pattern 3): Would this individual recognize themselves in your description? If not, go deeper.
3. **Belief-Aware 3D Posture** (Pattern 4, enriched):
   - **Occupation** (in your brand's world): What role do they play? What does their belief architecture say about how they relate to your category?
   - **Activity**: What rituals reinforce their beliefs? What media do they consume? What conversations do they have? What behaviors are downstream of their belief system?
   - **Thought Process**: What's their internal logic? What feels aligned vs. inauthentic? — enriched by Phase 1's processing chain and emotional sediment
4. **Belief-Enriched Emotional Outcome** (Pattern 7): Not just "what feeling do they want?" but "what belief-level shift would produce that feeling?"
5. **The Unarticulated Feeling** (Tacit Knowledge 3): What does this consumer feel that they can't yet articulate — and that your brand could name? Use Phase 1's emotional sediment to identify the unnamed feeling.
6. **Co-Conspiracy Definition** (Tacit Knowledge 2): Given their belief architecture, what are you and this consumer conspiring AGAINST together?

### Output Format

```
## Belief-Aware Consumer Posture: [Brand/Offer]

### The Individual
[Name/archetype] — [1-sentence description]
Kristen Stewart Test: [Pass/Fail + why]

### 3D Posture (Belief-Enriched)
**Occupation**: [Their role in your brand's world + how beliefs shape this role]
**Activity**: [Belief-reinforcing rituals + media + behaviors]
**Thought Process**: [Internal logic, what feels aligned/inauthentic, traced to belief origins]

### Belief-Level Emotional Outcome
Current belief state produces: [feeling/identity]
Desired belief shift would produce: [feeling/identity]
The emotional gap: [what they're yearning for but can't name]

### The Unarticulated Feeling
"[What they feel but can't say — your brand's job is to say it for them]"

### Co-Conspiracy
Together we are against: [shared enemy/rejection]
Together we are building: [shared vision/identity]
```

## Phase 5: Messaging Architecture

**Uses**: All McRaney patterns + Dai Media integration from Phases 1-4

Design the messaging strategy that targets the right processing route, handles the predicted rebuttal type, and triggers accommodation (genuine change) rather than assimilation (comfortable agreement).

### Execute

1. **Route-Matched Messaging** (from Phase 2):
   - Central route → Evidence-first sequence with novel arguments
   - Peripheral route → Authority/proof/quality-first with credibility cues
   - Dual → Peripheral hook into central depth
2. **Accommodation Design** (from Phase 1 + McRaney Pattern 2):
   - Design for **surprise**: What would this audience genuinely NOT expect? What can't their current model explain?
   - Establish **relevance**: Why does this matter to THEM personally (not abstractly)?
   - Ensure **safety**: Pre-build the face-saving narrative before the challenge
3. **Rebuttal Pre-emption** (from Phase 2):
   - If technique rebuttal expected → Build transparency into the message itself
   - If topic rebuttal expected → Front-load strongest evidence + strategic concession
4. **Belief Sequence** (McRaney Pattern 16 — Staged Information Delivery):
   - Map the belief journey from current state to desired state
   - Design each touchpoint to shift exactly ONE belief
   - Sequence: Metacognitive activation → Surprise/gap creation → Evidence delivery → Social permission → Identity bridge
5. **Threshold-Optimized CTAs** (from Phase 3):
   - If evidence gap is the constraint → CTA delivers proof
   - If social cost is the constraint → CTA reduces social risk (private action, anonymous trial, social proof)
   - If identity threat is the constraint → CTA provides face-saving narrative

### Output Format

```
## Messaging Architecture: [Brand/Offer] → [Audience]

### Processing Route Strategy
Active route: [Central/Peripheral/Dual]
Lead with: [specific element]
Hook type: [matched to route]

### Accommodation Triggers
Surprise element: [what their model can't explain]
Relevance anchor: [why it matters to THEM]
Safety mechanism: [face-saving narrative]

### Rebuttal Defense
Expected type: [Technique/Topic]
Pre-emption: [specific counter built into message]

### Belief Sequence (Staged Delivery)
| Stage | Belief to Shift | Content Type | Success Signal |
|-------|----------------|-------------|----------------|
| 1     | ...            | ...         | ...            |
| 2     | ...            | ...         | ...            |
| 3     | ...            | ...         | ...            |

### Threshold-Optimized CTAs
Binding constraint: [from Phase 3]
CTA design: [matched to constraint type]
```

## Quality Gate

Score the output 1-10 on each criterion. Minimum passing: all ≥ 7.

| Criterion | Question | Score |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| **Belief Depth** | Did you trace beliefs to their emotional/experiential origins, not just surface positions? | |
| **Route Accuracy** | Is the ELM route diagnosis based on specific evidence about this audience's motivation + ability, not assumptions? | |
| **Threshold Specificity** | Can you name the SINGLE binding constraint and the minimum viable intervention? | |
| **Individual Specificity** | Does the consumer posture describe a findable individual, not a composite? Would they pass the Kristen Stewart Test? | |
| **Accommodation Design** | Is the messaging designed for genuine surprise, or could it be comfortably assimilated into existing beliefs? | |
| **Cross-Stack Integration** | Do the Dai Media posture elements genuinely incorporate belief-layer intelligence, or are they just stapled together? | |
| **Actionability** | Could a copywriter, content strategist, or sales team execute directly from this output? | |

## Integration with /icp-deep-dive

This workflow enhances the `/icp-deep-dive` pipeline at three points:

1. **Phase B (Research)** — Replace or supplement step with Phase 1 Belief Excavation for belief-layer research depth
2. **Phase C Step 3 (Consumer Posture Profile)** — Use Phase 4 output as a belief-enriched posture instead of standard Dai Media posture
3. **Phase C Step 5 (Internal World Storytelling)** — Use Phase 1's emotional sediment + the unarticulated feeling as the entry point for Internal World Storytelling
