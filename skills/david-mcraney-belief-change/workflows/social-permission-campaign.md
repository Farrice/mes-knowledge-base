# Social Permission & Norm-Shift Campaign

**Produces**: A campaign designed to reduce the social cost of adopting, buying, or changing — for when adding more evidence DOESN'T work because the real barrier is social risk.

> **Load before executing**: `skills/david-mcraney-belief-change/genius.md`

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This workflow fires Perplexity research at MULTIPLE stages. The entire premise depends on understanding social dynamics from REAL community data. Guessing at social costs produces campaigns that target phantom barriers.

## When to Deploy

- Your audience agrees your offer is good but won't publicly adopt it
- Selling in a space where the community has a dominant anti-narrative
- Launching something innovative that requires the audience to go against their peer group
- When you've piled on evidence and it hasn't moved the needle (classic sign of social-cost binding)
- Any market where "I want to but my [peers/colleagues/friends] would think..." is the barrier
- Category creation where no one wants to be first

## Inputs Required

- **Product/Offer/Position**: What are we asking people to adopt?
- **Audience**: Who needs to change?
- **Community**: What social group are they embedded in?
- **Suspected Social Barrier** (optional): What social cost do you think is blocking adoption?

## Phase 1: Social Death Calculation 🔬

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 5 (Social Death Calculation)

### Research Step 🔬

Fire Perplexity query:

```
Search for: Social dynamics around [audience] and [product/category/position].
Find: What do [audience's] peer groups think about [product/category]?
Is there a dominant community narrative against it?
What happens to community members who publicly adopt [alternative/product]?
Look for: Ridicule, ostracism, label-assignment ("sellout", "gullible", "elitist", etc.)
Also find: Private vs public sentiment — evidence that people privately disagree
with the community consensus but don't say so publicly.
Return: 10-15 social dynamic signals with source attribution.
```

### Social Death Scoring

Score each cost dimension 1-10 based on research:

| Dimension | Score | Evidence | Specific Consequence |
|-----------|-------|----------|---------------------|
| **Tribal Cost** — Who knows they hold this position? What group membership depends on it? | [X]/10 | [cite] | [what they'd lose] |
| **Face Cost** — Would changing make them look stupid, naive, or disloyal? | [X]/10 | [cite] | [how they'd look] |
| **Relationship Cost** — What specific relationships are at risk? | [X]/10 | [cite] | [which relationships] |
| **Status Cost** — What authority or expertise depends on the current position? | [X]/10 | [cite] | [what status] |
| **Total Social Death Score** | [sum]/40 | | |

### Severity Classification:
- 1-10: **Low** — Change is socially safe. This workflow may not be needed.
- 11-20: **Medium** — Some social risk. Permission architecture needed.
- 21-30: **High** — Significant social penalties. Full campaign needed.
- 31-40: **Extreme** — Changing could cost community membership. Multi-touch, long-term strategy.

## Phase 2: Pluralistic Ignorance Detection 🔬

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 6 (Pluralistic Ignorance)

### Research Step 🔬

Fire Perplexity query:

```
Search for: [audience/community] members who privately doubt [dominant position]
OR secretly agree with [alternative/our position].
Find: Anonymous posts (throwaway accounts, "unpopular opinion" threads),
private survey data, whispered dissent, "I know I'll get downvoted but..." posts.
Also search for: Moments when someone in [community] publicly broke ranks —
what happened? Was there support from unexpected places?
Return: 8-12 pluralistic ignorance signals with sources.
```

### Analysis

Determine:

1. **Is pluralistic ignorance present?** — Do community members privately doubt the consensus but publicly maintain it? (YES/NO with evidence)
2. **Estimated private dissent rate** — What percentage likely disagree privately? (<20% / 20-40% / >40%)
3. **Cascade potential** — If ONE person breaks ranks loudly, would others follow? (Low / Medium / High)
4. **First-mover penalty** — What's the cost to the first person who publicly changes? (Low / Medium / High / Extreme)

### Output

```
## Pluralistic Ignorance Detection

Present: [Yes / No / Uncertain]
Estimated Private Dissent: ~[X]%
Evidence:
1. "[quote/signal]" — [source]
2. "[quote/signal]" — [source]

Cascade Potential: [Low / Medium / High]
First-Mover Penalty: [Low / Medium / High / Extreme]

Strategic Implication: [If present — this is a massive leverage point. Surface the dissent.]
```

## Phase 3: Permission Architecture Design

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 21 (Permission Architecture), Pattern 8 (Anxiety Detection)

### Execute

Map all permissions the audience needs before they can change:

### 3A: Permission Types

| Permission Type | Question It Answers | Current State | Required Intervention |
|----------------|-------------------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Self-Permission** | "Is it okay for me to change my mind?" | [granted / missing] | [intervention needed] |
| **Social Permission** | "Will my peers accept this?" | [granted / missing] | [intervention] |
| **Authority Permission** | "Does someone I respect endorse this?" | [granted / missing] | [intervention] |
| **Moral Permission** | "Is this the RIGHT thing to do?" | [granted / missing] | [intervention] |

### 3B: Anxiety Diagnosis

**Uses**: Pattern 8

Check: Is the real barrier ANXIETY (emotional, pre-rational) rather than BELIEF (cognitive, rational)?

Signals of anxiety-based resistance:
- They avoid the topic entirely (not arguing, just not engaging)
- Physical avoidance language: "I don't want to think about that"
- Disproportionate emotional response to neutral information
- Procrastination on action despite stated agreement

If anxiety-based → the campaign needs anxiety-reduction interventions, NOT more evidence or social proof.

### 3C: Environment Design

**Uses**: Pattern 10 (Environment-First Change Design)

Design the environment in which change feels safe:

1. **Physical/Digital Context** — Where does the change happen? Is that environment safety-conducive?
2. **Social Context** — Who else is in the room (real or virtual)? Are they supportive?
3. **Temporal Context** — When is the change expected? Is there time pressure? (Remove it)
4. **Choice Architecture** — How is the change presented? Opt-in? Default? Gradual?

## Phase 4: Face-Saving Narrative Design

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 11 (Face-Saving Narrative Architecture)

### Execute

Pre-build the story the audience can tell themselves AND others about why they changed:

### Face-Saving Templates

1. **The New Information Story**: "I used to think [X], and that made sense given what we knew. But [new data/insight] changes the calculation."

2. **The Evolution Story**: "I haven't changed my mind — my understanding has evolved. I still value [core principle], and that's exactly WHY I now [new position]."

3. **The Deeper Truth Story**: "Most people think [old position] and [new position] are opposites. Actually, understanding [new position] is the MORE sophisticated version of [old position]."

4. **The Integrity Story**: "I'd rather be honest about what I've learned than pretend I haven't grown."

Select the 1-2 templates that best fit this audience's identity and social context.

### Output

```
## Face-Saving Narrative

Primary Narrative: "[the story they can tell]"
Why This Works: [connects to their identity values without requiring an admission of error]
Secondary Narrative: "[backup story for different contexts]"
```

## Phase 5: Campaign Architecture

### Execute

Design the multi-touchpoint campaign that systematically reduces social cost:

### Campaign Sequence

```
Touchpoint 1: NORMALIZATION
Objective: Surface the pluralistic ignorance — "you're not alone in thinking this"
Pattern: 6 (Pluralistic Ignorance)
Format: [Content/ad showing that private dissent exists]
Key Message: "More people agree with you than you think."

Touchpoint 2: MODELING
Objective: Show someone LIKE THEM who changed — and survived socially
Pattern: 21 (Permission Architecture — social permission)
Format: [Case study / interview / testimonial from someone they respect who made the shift]
Key Message: "[Name] changed their position on [topic]. Here's what happened."

Touchpoint 3: IDENTITY BRIDGE
Objective: Show that changing is CONSISTENT with their existing identity
Pattern: 7 (Identity Bridge Engineering)
Format: [Content that connects new position to existing values]
Key Message: "As someone who values [X], this is the natural evolution."

Touchpoint 4: FACE-SAVING DELIVERY
Objective: Provide the narrative they can use when others ask
Pattern: 11 (Face-Saving Narrative)
Format: [Content that supplies the face-saving story — "new information changes things"]
Key Message: "[Face-saving narrative from Phase 4]"

Touchpoint 5: PERMISSION + LOW-COST FIRST STEP
Objective: Make the first action as small and reversible as possible
Pattern: 18 (Minimum Viable Change), 21 (Permission — self-permission)
Format: [CTA for the smallest possible commitment]
Key Message: "Try [smallest step]. You can always go back."
```

### If Anxiety Is the Binding Constraint (from Phase 3B):

Insert between Touchpoints 2 and 3:

```
Touchpoint 2.5: ANXIETY REDUCTION
Objective: Reduce the emotional barrier (pre-rational)
Pattern: 8 (Anxiety Diagnosis), 10 (Environment-First)
Format: [Content that creates safety — "here's what the worst case actually looks like"]
Key Message: "The worst-case scenario is [specific, survivable outcome]."
```

### Output: Full Campaign Map

```
## Campaign Map

| # | Touchpoint | Pattern | Format | Key Message | Success Signal |
|---|-----------|---------|--------|-------------|---------------|
| 1 | Normalization | P6 | [format] | [message] | [signal] |
| 2 | Modeling | P21 | [format] | [message] | [signal] |
| 3 | Identity Bridge | P7 | [format] | [message] | [signal] |
| 4 | Face-Saving | P11 | [format] | [message] | [signal] |
| 5 | Permission + First Step | P18, P21 | [format] | [message] | [signal] |
```

## Phase 6: Content Briefs

### Execute

For each touchpoint, produce a content brief:

```
## Touchpoint [#]: [Name]

**Objective**: [What social-cost variable this touchpoint reduces]
**Format**: [Specific content format]
**Key Message**: [1-2 sentences]
**Accommodation Design**:
  - Surprise: [what's genuinely unexpected]
  - Relevance: [specific personal stake]
  - Safety: [face-saving element]
**Proof Type**: [matched to this social context — NOT evidence-heavy if cost is social]
**Anti-Pattern**: [what NOT to do — e.g., don't pile on evidence when cost is social]
**Transition**: [How this sets up the next touchpoint]
```

---

## Output Schema

**Deliverable**: A full multi-touchpoint Campaign Map (Phase 5 table) plus one Content Brief per touchpoint (Phase 6 blocks) — the map alone is a plan, the briefs make it executable.

- **Structure**: Campaign Map table (# | Touchpoint | Pattern | Format | Key Message | Success Signal) followed by one Touchpoint block per row, each carrying Objective, Format, Key Message, Accommodation Design (Surprise/Relevance/Safety), Proof Type, Anti-Pattern, and Transition.
- **Social-cost discipline**: Every touchpoint's Proof Type must be matched to social, not evidence, cost when the binding constraint is social — evidence-heavy proof on a social-cost touchpoint fails the campaign's own design logic.
- **Sequencing**: Each Touchpoint's Transition field must name specifically what the next touchpoint builds on — a campaign map with unconnected touchpoints is not a sequence.
