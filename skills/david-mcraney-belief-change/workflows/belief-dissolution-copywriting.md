# Belief Dissolution Copywriting

**Produces**: Finished copy that dissolves a specific blocking belief preventing purchase, signup, or action — using metacognitive activation, cognitive dissonance engineering, and face-saving narrative design.

> **Load before executing**: `skills/david-mcraney-belief-change/genius.md`

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Phase 1 fires Perplexity research to identify and validate the blocking belief from real audience data. Copy built on assumed beliefs fails.

## When to Deploy

- A specific belief is blocking your funnel (not generic "they don't trust us")
- Your audience agrees your offer is good but still won't buy (belief barrier vs desire barrier)
- Launching a paradigm-shifting product that requires the audience to abandon a prior framework
- Cold traffic ads where you're fighting an established mental model

## Inputs Required

- **Blocking Belief**: What do they believe that prevents action? (If unknown, Phase 1 will find it)
- **Desired Replacement Belief**: What should they believe instead?
- **Product/Offer**: What are we selling/promoting?
- **Audience**: Who holds this belief?
- **Copy Format**: Ad / Email / Sales page / LinkedIn / Article / Script

## Phase 1: Belief Identification & Validation 🔬

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 1 (Resistance Hierarchy), HK 11 (Morty Lefkoe's Belief Elimination)

> Skip if you already have a validated belief from `/mcraney-deep-canvass` output.

### Research Step 🔬

Fire Perplexity query:

```
Search for: Why [audience] doesn't [desired action] despite wanting [desired outcome].
Find: Reddit threads, forum posts, reviews, social media comments where [audience]
EXPLICITLY STATES the reason they haven't [acted/bought/changed].
Focus on the STATED REASON — not your assumption of the reason.
Also search for: "[audience] tried [product/approach] and quit because..."
Return: 10-15 belief statements with source attribution.
```

### Validation

From the research, identify:

1. **The Blocking Belief** — State it in the audience's own words
2. **Belief Tier** — Fact / Attitude / Value / Identity
3. **Belief Strength** — 1-10 (how central is this to their worldview?)
4. **Prevalence** — How common is this belief across the research sources?

### Output

```
## Blocking Belief Validation

Belief: "[exact statement in audience's words]"
Tier: [Fact / Attitude / Value / Identity]
Strength: [X]/10
Prevalence: Found in [X] of [Y] sources
Source Quotes:
1. "[quote]" — [source]
2. "[quote]" — [source]
3. "[quote]" — [source]
```

## Phase 2: Origin Trace

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 17 (Emotional Sediment), HK 11 (Lefkoe Belief Elimination)

### Execute

Trace the belief to its experiential roots using Lefkoe's method:

1. **Observation Layer** — What real experiences led the audience to form this belief?
   - "I saw [X] happen, so I concluded [Y]."
   - "When I tried [X], the result was [Y]."

2. **Interpretation Layer** — What meaning did they assign to those experiences?
   - "This means that [generalized conclusion]."
   - "Since [X happened], it must be that [broader belief]."

3. **Generalization Layer** — How did one or few experiences become an absolute rule?
   - "It's ALWAYS like that."
   - "[Category] is just [generalization]."

4. **Emotional Sediment** — What unresolved emotion is trapped beneath the belief?
   - Betrayal? ("I trusted [authority/product] and got burned")
   - Shame? ("I failed at this before")
   - Fear? ("If I try again and fail...")
   - Vindication? ("I've been saying this for years and I'm finally being proven right")

### Output

```
## Origin Trace

Observation: [real experience that seeded the belief]
Interpretation: [meaning assigned to that experience]
Generalization: [how it became an absolute rule]
Emotional Sediment: [the unresolved feeling underneath]

Key insight: The belief isn't wrong — it was a REASONABLE conclusion given [their experience].
The dissolution target is the GENERALIZATION, not the observation.
```

## Phase 3: Metacognitive Activation Design

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 26 (Metacognitive Unlock), Pattern 22 (Evaluation Mode Trigger)

### Execute

Design the metacognitive sequence that shifts the reader from **defending the belief** to **examining how they arrived at it**:

1. **Process Question** — Not "why do you believe X?" but "how did you first come to think about X that way?"
   - This shifts from content (defensive) to process (reflective)
   - Design 3-5 candidate process questions

2. **Evaluation Mode Trigger** — Shift from "Is this correct?" to "How would I know if this were wrong?"
   - Design the specific scenario that makes the reader evaluate rather than produce

3. **Model-Breaking Moment** — The specific piece of information or experience the reader's current model genuinely cannot explain
   - This must be TRUE and VERIFIABLE — not a rhetorical trick
   - It should cause the reader to pause and genuinely reconsider

### Output

```
## Metacognitive Activation

Process Questions (pick best for copy format):
1. "[question]"
2. "[question]"
3. "[question]"

Evaluation Mode Trigger:
"[scenario/question that shifts to evaluation]"

Model-Breaking Moment:
"[the specific true fact/experience their model can't explain]"
Source: [verification source]
```

---

## Phase 4: Cognitive Dissonance Architecture

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 20 (Cognitive Dissonance Engineering), Pattern 2 (Accommodation), HK 12

### Execute

Design the dissonance experience — the moment where the reader holds two contradictory cognitions simultaneously:

1. **Cognition A** — Something they already know is true (grounded in their own experience)
2. **Cognition B** — Something you've just shown them that contradicts A (from Phase 3's model-breaking moment)
3. **The Gap** — The space between A and B where the old belief can't survive

> [!CAUTION]
> DO NOT resolve the dissonance immediately. Hold the gap for 2-3 beats. Let the reader feel it. Premature resolution destroys the effect.

### Output

```
## Dissonance Architecture

Cognition A (they already know): "[statement grounded in their experience]"
Cognition B (you introduce): "[model-breaking fact/experience]"
The Gap: "[why these two can't coexist with the old belief]"
Hold Duration: [X sentences/seconds before offering resolution]
```

---

## Phase 5: Permission & Face-Saving Design

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 7 (Identity Bridge), Pattern 11 (Face-Saving), Pattern 21 (Permission Architecture)

### Execute

Pre-build 3 elements that let the reader update without identity damage:

1. **Identity Bridge** — Show that changing this belief is CONSISTENT with who they are, not a betrayal of it
   - "As someone who [identity trait they value], you'd naturally want to..."
   - "The same [quality] that made you [old action] is what drives you to [new action]"

2. **Face-Saving Narrative** — The story they can tell themselves and others about why they changed
   - "You were right to [old belief] given [old information]. New data changes the calculation."
   - NOT: "You were wrong and here's why"

3. **Permission Stack** — The permissions they need:
   - Self-permission: "It's okay to rethink this"
   - Social permission: "Others like you are doing this too"
   - Authority permission: "[respected figure] endorses this"

### Output

```
## Permission & Face-Saving

Identity Bridge: "[specific bridge sentence]"
Face-Saving Narrative: "[the story they can tell]"
Permission Stack:
- Self: "[self-permission message]"
- Social: "[social permission signal]"
- Authority: "[authority endorsement]"
```

## Phase 6: Copy Production

### Execute

Assemble the dissolution copy in this exact sequence:

| Section | Purpose | Source |
|---------|---------|-------|
| **Hook** | Use the model-breaking moment as the opening | Phase 3 |
| **Shared Experience** | Validate their observation layer — you've seen the same thing | Phase 2 |
| **Process Question** | Insert the metacognitive activation — soft, not aggressive | Phase 3 |
| **Dissonance Gap** | Present Cognition A + Cognition B, hold the gap | Phase 4 |
| **Face-Saving Bridge** | "You were right given [old info]. Here's what changed..." | Phase 5 |
| **New Framework** | Deliver the replacement belief WITH the identity bridge | Phase 5 |
| **Permission CTA** | Include permission stack in the call to action | Phase 5 |

## Quality Gate

| Test | Question | Pass? |
|------|----------|-------|
| **Grounding** | Is the blocking belief validated from real audience data, not assumed? | |
| **Origin Respect** | Does the copy acknowledge the REASONABLE origin of their belief? | |
| **Metacognition** | Does the reader examine HOW they think, or are they just told WHAT to think? | |
| **Dissonance Hold** | Is there a genuine moment of cognitive tension, or does the copy rush to resolution? | |
| **Face-Saving** | Can the reader change their mind while maintaining self-respect? | |
| **Identity Preservation** | Does changing feel like growth from who they are, not betrayal of who they are? | |
| **Truth Test** | Is the model-breaking moment TRUE and verifiable? Not a rhetorical trick? | |
