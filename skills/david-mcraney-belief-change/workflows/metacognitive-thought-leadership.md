# Metacognitive Thought Leadership Engine

**Produces**: Thought leadership content (LinkedIn posts, newsletters, YouTube scripts, articles, Substack essays) that triggers genuine self-examination in the reader — not just information delivery, but genuine model restructuring.

> **Load before executing**: `skills/david-mcraney-belief-change/genius.md`

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Phase 1 fires Perplexity research to ground the topic in real audience beliefs. Metacognitive content that challenges beliefs the audience doesn't actually hold is empty performance.

## When to Deploy

- Writing thought leadership that needs to change how people THINK, not just what they know
- LinkedIn content that drives profile visits and follows (not just "likes" and "so true!")
- Newsletter essays that readers forward with "this changed how I think about..."
- YouTube scripts where the core value is perspective shift, not information
- Any content where "informative but forgettable" is the failure mode

## Inputs Required

- **Topic**: What are we writing about?
- **Audience**: Who are we writing for?
- **Platform/Format**: LinkedIn / Newsletter / YouTube / Article / Substack
- **Current Audience Belief** (optional): What does the audience currently believe about this topic?
- **Desired Shift** (optional): What should they think differently after reading?

---

## Phase 1: Belief Audit 🔍

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 1 (Resistance Hierarchy)

### Research Step 🔬

Fire Perplexity query:

```
Search for: What does [audience] currently believe about [topic]?
Find: LinkedIn posts, Twitter threads, Reddit discussions, blog comments where
[audience] STATES their position on [topic], especially:
- The dominant "common wisdom" view
- Points of active debate
- Assumptions that go unquestioned
Return: 10-15 belief statements with source attribution.
```

### Analysis

From the research, identify:

1. **The Dominant Narrative** — What most of this audience believes without questioning
2. **The Hidden Assumption** — The assumption underneath the dominant narrative that nobody examines
3. **The Model Gap** — What the dominant narrative can't explain (this becomes your content's core)

### Output

```
## Belief Audit

Dominant Narrative: "[what they all think]"
Hidden Assumption: "[what they assume but don't examine]"
Model Gap: "[what their current belief can't explain]"
```

## Phase 2: Metacognitive Question Design

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 26 (Metacognitive Unlock), Pattern 22 (Evaluation Mode Trigger)

### Execute

Design the metacognitive architecture that shifts the reader from **defending their position** to **examining their reasoning**:

### 2A: Process Questions

Design 3-5 process questions that redirect from CONTENT to PROCESS:

| Content Question (defensive) | Process Question (reflective) |
|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| "Is [topic] important?" | "How did you first come to think about [topic] the way you do?" |
| "Is [claim] true?" | "What would change your mind about [claim]?" |
| "Should we [action]?" | "When you decided to [current approach], what were you optimizing for?" |

Pick the 1-2 strongest for this specific topic × audience.

### 2B: Evaluation Mode Trigger

Design the specific shift from production mode to evaluation mode:

- **Production mode** (default): "Here's what I think, and here's why I'm right"
- **Evaluation mode** (target): "Hmm, let me examine how I arrived at this position"

Trigger mechanisms:
- Present a scenario where the audience's usual framework produces a clearly WRONG answer
- Ask "How would you know if you were wrong about this?" (specific to their claim)
- Present two experts they respect who disagree — the disagreement itself triggers evaluation

### 2C: Production/Evaluation Asymmetry Detection

**Uses**: Pattern 9

Check: Is this topic one where the audience is in "production mode" (confident, defending) but hasn't done "evaluation mode" (critical examination)?

Signal: "If they're very sure about this but have never seriously tried to falsify it — they're in production-only mode. This is your leverage point."

### Output

```
## Metacognitive Architecture

Process Question: "[strongest process question]"
Evaluation Trigger: "[specific trigger mechanism]"
Production Mode Signal: [what they say that proves they haven't evaluated]
```

## Phase 3: Surprise Engineering

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 2 (Accommodation), HK 12 (Accommodation Requires Surprise)

### Execute

Design the genuine cognitive surprise — the thing their model can't contain:

1. **Identify the surprise** — What TRUE fact, statistic, example, or insight genuinely contradicts the dominant narrative from Phase 1?
   - This must be verifiable, not just contrarian
   - It should create an "I never thought of it that way" moment, not a "that seems wrong" reaction

2. **Research Step** 🔬 — If needed, fire Perplexity to find the surprising data:

```
Search for: Surprising/counterintuitive findings about [topic] that contradict
the common wisdom that [dominant narrative].
Find: Research studies, data, expert positions, case studies that show the
dominant narrative is incomplete or wrong in a specific way.
Return: 5-10 surprising findings with sources.
```

3. **Model-Breaking Test** — For each candidate surprise, ask:
   - Can the audience absorb this into their existing worldview? If yes → not surprising enough
   - Does it require them to restructure how they think about [topic]? If yes → use this one

### Output

```
## Surprise Element

The Surprise: "[specific counterintuitive finding]"
Source: [verifiable citation]
Model-Breaking because: [why this can't be absorbed into the dominant narrative]
```

## Phase 4: Content Architecture

**Uses**: McRaney Patterns 16 (Staged Delivery), 11 (Face-Saving)

### Execute

Structure the content as a metacognitive journey:

### Platform-Specific Architectures:

**LinkedIn (1,000-1,300 words)**:
```
1. Hook — Model-breaking surprise as the opening (1 sentence)
2. Model Validation — "You're right that [dominant narrative]. And here's why it made sense." (2-3 sentences)
3. The Gap — Present the specific thing the dominant model can't explain (2-3 sentences)
4. Process Question — The metacognitive redirect (1 question)
5. New Framework — Your alternative model that explains the gap (3-5 sentences)
6. Face-Saving Bridge — "This isn't about being wrong. It's about [growth/evolution]." (1-2 sentences)
7. Application — How to actually use this new way of thinking (2-3 sentences)
8. Close — Question or statement that sustains metacognition after reading
```

**Newsletter/Article (1,200-2,500 words)**:
```
1. Opening Scene — Specific moment that anchors the topic (3-5 sentences)
2. The Unexamined Assumption — What everyone takes for granted (2-3 paragraphs)
3. The Evaluation Trigger — Scenario or question that activates metacognition
4. The Surprise — Data/insight the current model can't contain
5. [Hold] — Let the dissonance sit. Don't resolve immediately.
6. New Framework — The replacement model that explains both old observations AND new data
7. Identity Bridge — How this is growth, not error correction
8. Practical Application — Specific actions from the new model
9. Metacognitive Close — Leave them thinking about HOW they think, not just what
```

**YouTube Script (8-15 minutes)**:
```
0:00 — Hook: The model-breaking surprise (15 seconds)
0:15 — Context: What everyone believes and why it made sense (1-2 minutes)
2:00 — The Question: Process question + evaluation trigger (30 seconds)
2:30 — The Evidence: Staged delivery of surprising data (3-5 minutes)
7:00 — The New Framework: Alternative model + face-saving bridge (2-3 minutes)
10:00 — Application: What to do with this insight (1-2 minutes)
12:00 — Metacognitive Close: "The question isn't whether [topic]. The question is how you think about thinking about [topic]."
```

---

## Phase 5: Draft Production

### Execute

Write the full content piece using the architecture from Phase 4.

### During Drafting, Verify:

- [ ] **Metacognition > Information**: Is the reader examining their own reasoning, or just consuming new facts?
- [ ] **Surprise Integrity**: Is the model-breaking moment TRUE and verifiable?
- [ ] **Face-Saving Present**: Can the reader update without feeling stupid?
- [ ] **Staged, Not Dumped**: Is there one shift per section, with proper sequencing?
- [ ] **The Hold**: After presenting the dissonance, is there at least 1-2 beats before resolution?

---

## Quality Gate

| Test | Question | Pass? |
|------|----------|-------|
| **Metacognitive Activation** | Does the reader examine HOW they think, or is this just "here's a new opinion"? | |
| **Surprise Authenticity** | Is the model-breaking moment true, verifiable, and genuinely surprising to this audience? | |
| **Research Grounding** | Are audience beliefs validated from real data, not assumed? | |
| **Accommodation Test** | Would the reader say "I never thought of it that way" or "yeah, I already knew that"? | |
| **Face-Saving** | Can the reader update while maintaining self-respect? | |
| **Platform Fit** | Does the structure match the platform's native format and processing route? | |
