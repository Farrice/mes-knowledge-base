# Accommodation vs Assimilation Content Audit

**Produces**: Pass/fail diagnosis on any content piece with specific rewrite prescriptions for failing dimensions.

> **Load before executing**: `skills/david-mcraney-belief-change/genius.md`

> [!TIP]
> This is a **universal quality gate** — it plugs into ANY content workflow as a final check. Compatible with `/slop-check`, `/grace-quality-gate`, `/word-sprint`, and every content engine.

## When to Deploy

- Before publishing any content designed to change minds, not just inform
- When content gets lots of agreement ("so true!") but no behavior change
- As a final gate on output from `/persuasion-copy`, `/metacognitive-content`, `/proof-copy-engine`
- When you suspect content is being "assimilated" (absorbed without impact)

## Inputs Required

- **Content**: The finished piece to audit (any format — copy, post, email, script, article)
- **Intended Belief Shift**: What should the reader think/feel/do DIFFERENTLY after reading?
- **Audience** (optional but recommended): Who is this for?

---

## The 7-Point Audit

### Test 1: Surprise Diagnosis

**Pattern**: HK 12 (Accommodation Requires Surprise)

**Question**: Does this content contain genuine cognitive surprise — something the audience's current mental model CANNOT comfortably explain?

**How to test**:
- Read the piece from the audience's perspective
- At each major claim, ask: "Could someone who disagrees absorb this into their existing worldview without changing anything?"
- If YES → the claim will be assimilated, not accommodated

**Scoring**:
- ✅ PASS: At least one moment where the audience's current model genuinely breaks — "Wait, that doesn't fit what I thought..."
- ❌ FAIL: Every claim can be comfortably filed under "things I already believe"

**Rewrite Prescription if failing**:
Find the most surprising implication of your argument — the one that contradicts the audience's expectations — and lead with it. Don't work up to surprise; open with it.

---

### Test 2: Relevance Anchor

**Pattern**: Pattern 2 (Accommodation Detection), Pattern 24 (ELM)

**Question**: Is the personal stake specific enough to warrant processing effort?

**How to test**:
- Find every "this matters" or "this is important" claim
- Does it specify WHY it matters to THIS reader's specific situation?
- Generic: "Communication skills are important." Specific: "The next negotiation you walk into, this difference costs you $10-50K."

**Scoring**:
- ✅ PASS: The reader can immediately picture a specific moment in THEIR life where this applies
- ❌ FAIL: The relevance is abstract, categorical, or generic

**Rewrite Prescription if failing**:
Replace every generic relevance statement with a specific scenario the reader would recognize from their own experience. Use second person, present tense, sensory detail.

---

### Test 3: Safety Mechanism

**Pattern**: Pattern 11 (Face-Saving Narrative)

**Question**: If this content challenges the reader's existing belief, does it provide a face-saving path for them to update without feeling stupid?

**How to test**:
- Find the moment where the content asks the reader to change their mind
- Is there a preceding narrative that validates their previous position?
- Template: "You were right to think X given Y. Here's what changed..."

**Scoring**:
- ✅ PASS: The reader can update their belief while maintaining self-respect
- ❌ FAIL: Changing their mind requires admitting they were "wrong" or "foolish"

**Rewrite Prescription if failing**:
Insert a face-saving bridge BEFORE the challenging claim. Attribute their old belief to reasonable-given-the-circumstances, not to error.

---

### Test 4: Route Match

**Pattern**: Pattern 24 (ELM Route Selection)

**Question**: Is the messaging style matched to the audience's processing route?

**How to test**:
- **Central route audience** (high motivation + high ability): Is the content leading with evidence, logic, and novel arguments? NOT authority signals, social proof, or design quality?
- **Peripheral route audience** (low motivation OR low ability): Is the content leading with credibility cues, social proof, and visual quality? NOT dense arguments?

**Scoring**:
- ✅ PASS: Messaging style matches the audience's processing route
- ❌ FAIL: Central-route arguments on a peripheral-route platform, or peripheral cues for a deeply engaged audience

**Rewrite Prescription if failing**:
For mismatched central → peripheral: Replace argument chains with authority signals, social proof, and design quality. Shorten. Add credibility markers.
For mismatched peripheral → central: Replace surface cues with substantive evidence, novel arguments, and logical chains. Deepen.

---

### Test 5: Metacognitive Activation

**Pattern**: Pattern 26 (Metacognitive Unlock)

**Question**: Does the content activate the reader's metacognition — making them examine HOW they think, not just WHAT to think?

**How to test**:
- Search for process questions: "How did you arrive at...?" "What would change your mind about...?" "When you think about this, what comes up first?"
- Search for perspective-shift moments: "What if the reason you believe X is actually because of Y?"
- These should appear in the first 25% of the content

**Scoring**:
- ✅ PASS: The reader pauses to examine their own reasoning at least once
- ❌ FAIL: The content delivers conclusions without ever causing the reader to reflect on their own thinking process

**Rewrite Prescription if failing**:
Insert a metacognitive question or reflection prompt within the first quarter of the piece. Frame it as genuine inquiry, not rhetorical.

---

### Test 6: Staged Delivery

**Pattern**: Pattern 16 (Staged Information Delivery)

**Question**: Does the content try to shift everything at once, or does it shift one belief at a time in the right sequence?

**How to test**:
- Count the number of distinct belief shifts the content attempts
- Are they sequenced (each building on the last) or dumped simultaneously?
- Is the cognitive load manageable at each stage?

**Scoring**:
- ✅ PASS: One belief shift per section, sequenced logically, each building on the previous
- ❌ FAIL: Multiple simultaneous belief shifts creating cognitive overload or "wall of claims"

**Rewrite Prescription if failing**:
Identify the most important belief to shift. Structure everything around that ONE shift. Move secondary shifts to follow-up content or later sections.

---

### Test 7: The Accommodation Test (Final)

**Pattern**: Pattern 2 (Accommodation vs Assimilation)

**Question**: After reading this, would the audience say "I never thought of it that way" or "Yeah, I already knew that"?

**How to test**:
- Read the piece one more time, from the audience's perspective
- After reading, honestly assess: did the piece expose a GAP in your thinking, or did it CONFIRM what you already believed?
- "I never thought of it that way" = accommodation (genuine change)
- "That's exactly what I was saying" or "Yeah, so true" = assimilation (comfortable absorption)

**Scoring**:
- ✅ PASS: Accommodation — genuine model restructuring
- ❌ FAIL: Assimilation — comfortable agreement without change

**Rewrite Prescription if failing**:
The piece is probably too aligned with the audience's existing worldview. Find the ONE implication of your argument that CONTRADICTS their expectations, and make it the centerpiece. If you can't find one, the piece may not be worth publishing — it's not adding anything.
