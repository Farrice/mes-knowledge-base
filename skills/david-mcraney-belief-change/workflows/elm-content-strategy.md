# ELM-Informed Content Strategy

**Produces**: A platform × processing route matrix with matched content format, proof type, and messaging style prescriptions for every channel — the anti-mismatch content strategy.

> **Load before executing**: `skills/david-mcraney-belief-change/genius.md`

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Phase 1 fires Perplexity research to ground audience processing profiles in real platform behavior data. Assumptions about how audiences process on each platform produce structurally mismatched content.

## When to Deploy

- Building or auditing a multi-platform content strategy
- Content is getting engagement but not conversion (likely route mismatch)
- Planning content for a new platform entry
- Diagnosing why "the same content" performs differently across platforms
- When a content team keeps producing the same style everywhere

## Inputs Required

- **Brand/Creator**: Who are we building strategy for?
- **Audience**: Who is the target?
- **Active Platforms**: Which platforms are currently in use or planned?
- **Current Content** (optional): Examples of existing content per platform
- **Business Goal**: What should content ultimately produce? (leads, sales, authority, hiring)

## Phase 1: Audience Processing Profile 🔬

**Uses**: McRaney Pattern 24 (ELM Route Selection)

### Research Step 🔬

Fire Perplexity queries (2 parallel):

**Query 1 — Platform Behavior**:
```
Search for: How does [audience] consume content on [platforms]?
Find: Research on [audience's] platform-specific behavior — scroll speed,
content completion rates, engagement patterns, attention spans per platform.
Also find: What content formats perform best with [audience] on each platform.
Return: Platform-specific engagement data with sources.
```

**Query 2 — Audience Motivation**:
```
Search for: [audience] relationship with [topic/category] content.
Find: How motivated is [audience] to engage deeply with [topic]?
Are they casually interested or deeply invested?
What's their expertise level with [topic]?
Return: Motivation and ability signals with sources.
```

### Processing Route Diagnosis

For each platform, score two dimensions:

**Motivation** (1-10): How much does this audience WANT to process this content?
- 1-3: Low — they're scrolling past unless something grabs them
- 4-6: Medium — interested but not seeking deep engagement
- 7-10: High — actively looking for substantive content on this topic

**Ability** (1-10): How ABLE are they to process deeply on this platform?
- 1-3: Low — platform constraints prevent deep processing (short-form video, fast-scroll feeds)
- 4-6: Medium — some depth possible but attention is fragmented
- 7-10: High — platform enables and encourages deep engagement (long-form, subscriber-based)

### Route Classification

| Motivation | Ability | Route | What It Means |
|-----------|---------|-------|---------------|
| High | High | **Central** | They'll evaluate arguments, weigh evidence, process deeply |
| High | Low | **Dual** | Motivated but platform-constrained — peripheral hook into central depth |
| Low | High | **Peripheral-to-Central** | Platform allows depth but audience needs activation first |
| Low | Low | **Peripheral** | They'll be influenced by cues, not arguments |

## Phase 2: Mismatch Diagnosis

### Execute

If existing content was provided, audit each platform for route mismatch:

**Central-Route Messaging on a Peripheral-Route Platform** (most common error):
- Signal: Dense arguments, logical chains, evidence-heavy posts on Instagram/TikTok/Twitter
- Problem: Audience can't or won't process this deeply on this platform
- Fix: Swap from arguments to cues (credibility markers, social proof, design quality)

**Peripheral-Route Messaging on a Central-Route Platform** (under-leveraging):
- Signal: Surface-level, clickbaity, authority-signal-heavy content in newsletters or long-form
- Problem: Audience came for depth and got shallow
- Fix: Swap from cues to arguments (evidence, analysis, novel insights)

**Dual-Route Misalignment** (YouTube/Podcasts):
- Signal: Starting with depth (loses initial audience) or staying shallow (frustrates engaged viewers)
- Fix: Peripheral hook → central depth transition at 2-3 minute mark

## Phase 3: Content Prescriptions

### Execute

For each platform × route, prescribe:

### Central Route Platforms (Newsletter, Long-form LinkedIn, Podcast, Blog)

| Element | Prescription |
|---------|-------------|
| **Content Format** | Arguments, analysis, evidence chains, novel frameworks |
| **Hook Style** | Intellectual curiosity, counterintuitive insight, model-breaking idea |
| **Proof Type** | Empirical data, case studies, logical demonstrations |
| **Length** | Long enough to build the full argument (1,200-3,000 words) |
| **Engagement Driver** | Quality of thinking → people share because the IDEAS are valuable |
| **McRaney Patterns** | 26 (Metacognitive), 22 (Evaluation Mode), 2 (Accommodation) |

### Peripheral Route Platforms (Instagram, TikTok, Twitter/X scrolling)

| Element | Prescription |
|---------|-------------|
| **Content Format** | Authority signals, social proof, strong visuals, simple messages |
| **Hook Style** | Credibility cues, relatability, pattern interrupt, emotional trigger |
| **Proof Type** | Social proof (numbers, testimonials), authority markers (logos, credentials) |
| **Length** | One core message per post (< 280 words or < 60 seconds) |
| **Engagement Driver** | Identity recognition → people share because they feel SEEN |
| **McRaney Patterns** | 6 (Pluralistic Ignorance), 21 (Permission), 11 (Face-Saving) |

### Dual Route Platforms (YouTube, LinkedIn Feed, Podcast shorts)

| Element | Prescription |
|---------|-------------|
| **Content Format** | Peripheral hook (first 15 seconds / 2 lines) → central depth (rest) |
| **Hook Style** | Surprising statement or visual that earns attention (peripheral cues) |
| **Proof Type** | Start with social/authority proof, transition to empirical/demonstration |
| **Length** | Medium (500-1,500 words or 5-15 minutes) |
| **Engagement Driver** | Curiosity → depth → transformation arc |
| **McRaney Patterns** | 3 (Pattern Interrupt) → 16 (Staged Delivery) → 26 (Metacognitive) |

## Phase 4: Content Calendar Architecture

### Execute

Map content types to platforms using the route matrix:

```
## Weekly Content Architecture

| Day | Platform | Route | Content Type | Purpose |
|-----|----------|-------|-------------|---------|
| Mon | Newsletter | Central | Deep analysis / framework | Build intellectual authority |
| Tue | LinkedIn | Dual | Counterintuitive insight | Curiosity → profile visit |
| Wed | Twitter/X | Peripheral | Authority signal + hot take | Visibility + credibility cue |
| Thu | LinkedIn | Dual | Case study / proof point | Central-route conversion content |
| Fri | Instagram | Peripheral | Identity-resonant visual | Community building |
| Sat | YouTube | Dual | Long-form exploration | Deep authority + search SEO |
```

**Cross-Platform Sequencing** (not just repurposing):
- Newsletter (central) → Extract 1 surprising insight → LinkedIn post (dual) → Extract hook → Twitter (peripheral)
- Each platform gets the RIGHT processing-route version, not a reformatted copy of the same content
