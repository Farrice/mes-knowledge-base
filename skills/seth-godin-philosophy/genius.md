## Genius Patterns

### Pattern 1: Resistance as Navigation Compass
**Execute**: When resistance appears, say "Thank you — this tells me I'm about to do something important." Move the resistant task to the TOP of your priority list. Resistance is information, not failure.
**Success Metric**: Resistance-triggering tasks executed first, not last

### Pattern 2: The Fear-Status-Affiliation Triad
**Execute**: Decompose any stuck behavior into three forces — fear (of death/rejection), status (comparison/competition), or affiliation (fitting in). The root cause determines the intervention. Fear → name it. Status → quit the game. Affiliation → change your scene.
**Success Metric**: Root cause identified in under 60 seconds; intervention matched to driver

### Pattern 3: Problems vs Situations Triage
**Execute**: Classify any obstacle: "Does this have a solution I just don't want to execute? → Problem. Is this unchangeable like physics? → Situation." Problems get action plans. Situations get acceptance. Zero energy on fighting situations.
**Success Metric**: Clear classification before any strategy work begins

### Pattern 4: But vs And Diagnostic Reframe
**Execute**: Scan any complaint, excuse, or narrative for "but." Replace with "and." "I want to grow BUT my spouse doesn't like it" → "I want to grow AND my spouse doesn't like it." The second version holds both truths without one canceling the other.
**Success Metric**: "But" eliminated from problem statements; both truths held simultaneously

### Pattern 5: Smallest Viable Audience First
**Execute**: Ask "What's the smallest group of people that if I made an impact on them, it would be enough?" Then: "What's the smallest piece of work that would be worth it?" Start microscopically small. One person. Two hours. A PDF to 20 people.
**Success Metric**: First action is tiny, specific, and serves a named audience

### Pattern 6: Pick Yourself Protocol
**Execute**: Stop waiting for external authorization. Write it and email it to 20 people. Record it and post it tomorrow. Start under a pseudonym if needed. Four words: "Here, I made this." Nobody is forcing you to wait — you're choosing to.
**Success Metric**: Time from "I want to do this" to "it's public" in days, not months

### Pattern 7: Intentional Design (Who/What)
**Execute**: Every project starts with two questions written down: "Who is this for?" (by name if possible) and "What is this for?" (what change does it create?) If you can't answer them, you're floundering. If the answer is "everyone," you're hiding.
**Success Metric**: Both questions answered in writing before production begins

### Pattern 8: Perfectionism as Hiding
**Execute**: Define the spec. Three types of quality exist: meets-spec (attainable standard), luxury (unnecessary for function), zero-defect pursuit (trap). Once spec is met, merely ship it. "The point of perfectionism is not to make it better — it's to keep you from shipping it."
**Success Metric**: Spec defined BEFORE work; shipping happens at spec, not at "perfect"

### Pattern 9: Consistency Over Authenticity
**Execute**: "Authenticity is a crock." Nobody wants you to be authentically cranky. They want the consistently excellent version of you. Define the role of "the best version of me." Play that role every day. Authenticity is for your best friend; everyone else wants consistency.
**Success Metric**: Output quality doesn't fluctuate with mood

### Pattern 10: The Generous Ruckus Frame
**Execute**: "Go make a ruckus" means "work that matters for people who care." Not attention-seeking. Not rule-breaking. Generous work offered without attachment to outcome. "Here I made this" is a gift, not a performance.
**Success Metric**: Work evaluated by "did it serve?" not "did it perform?"

### Pattern 11: Where Do You Put the Tired
**Execute**: Accept upfront: "I am going to get tired AND I need to be able to do both." Like a marathon runner — you don't eliminate tired, you figure out where to put it. Don't optimize for painlessness. Optimize for endurance.
**Success Metric**: Fatigue anticipated and planned for; not treated as failure signal

### Pattern 12: Name It to Defuse It
**Execute**: Say it out loud: "I am buying $400 sneakers because I'm afraid of being left out." "I'm procrastinating because I fear rejection." Named fears lose power. The more absurd it sounds when named, the more power it had unnamed.
**Success Metric**: Hidden motivations articulated in plain language; behavior shifts follow

### Pattern 13: "We Become What We Do"
**Execute**: Action creates identity, not the reverse. Don't wait to "become" a writer — write, and you become a writer. Want to be truthful? Start telling the truth. The sequence is always action → identity.
**Success Metric**: Identity statements follow completed actions, not precede them

### Pattern 14: The Lipstick-to-Death Cascade
**Execute**: When paralyzed, trace the full fear chain: "I'll make a mistake → shame → ostracized → alone → die." Takes under 2 seconds. Then notice: it's absurd that lipstick on your teeth leads to death. Name the absurdity. The chain breaks.
**Success Metric**: Fear chain traced, absurdity recognized, action taken

## Hidden Knowledge

### Tacit Knowledge 1: The Architect vs Victim Story
The first noise in your head each morning tells which story you're in. The architect says "I can change the circumstances." The victim says "I can't." Both feel equally true. The choice determines everything.

**Deploy**: Before strategy, name which story you're operating from. Architect = design. Victim = exit that story first.

### Tacit Knowledge 2: "If No One Comes, No One Will Know"
The downside of trying is almost always imagined. His mother staged a museum event fearing nobody would show (5,000 came). But even if nobody had — the failure would have been invisible. Most failures are invisible.

**Deploy**: Before any launch: "If this fails, who will actually know?" Almost no one. Neutralizes catastrophe.

### Tacit Knowledge 3: The Seed-in-a-Bag Problem
Ideas kept inside don't grow. Seeds don't bloom in bags in cold sheds. They need to be planted in the open. The nagging ache of an unexpressed idea is a seed dying in a bag.

**Deploy**: Any idea nagging 30+ days must be planted microscopically. Email it. Draft 200 words. Exposure to air = growth.

### Tacit Knowledge 4: The System Wants You to Hide
High school taught hiding in uniforms, cliques, mobs. The consumer system needs you hiding — passive people buy armor. You're not failing to act. The system is working perfectly to keep you passive.

**Deploy**: When stuck, ask: "Am I choosing safety or was I trained to?" Name the system. Then defy it.

### Tacit Knowledge 5: The Safe Hiding Place Trap
"Congratulations. You've built a perfect place to hide." Every excuse — "20 publishers turned me down," "waiting for TED to call," "need 300K followers first" — is a carefully constructed hiding spot. Looks like effort. IS avoidance.

**Deploy**: Audit excuses. If removing the barrier would force you to ship, it was a hiding spot.

### Tacit Knowledge 6: Action-First Identity Formation
"We become what we do. We don't do what we become." Want to be brave? Do one brave thing. The feeling follows the doing, never precedes it.

**Deploy**: Replace "I need to feel ready" with one micro-action that proves the identity.

### Tacit Knowledge 7: The Cohort Multiplier
Everything works with a truth-telling cohort. 1-5 people who hold each other accountable. Not optional support — structural infrastructure. "It will work every single time."

**Deploy**: Before starting anything major, recruit 1-5 accountability partners. The cohort IS the infrastructure.

### Tacit Knowledge 8: The Dishwasher Truth
Most friction has a known solution. You just don't want the 10 minutes of discomfort to execute it. The solution to the dishwasher argument: one respectful 10-minute conversation.

**Deploy**: For any friction: "Do I know what would fix this?" If yes: "Am I willing to endure 10 minutes of discomfort?"

### Tacit Knowledge 9: The Role of the Best Version
The mock turtleneck story — walking to work as "seth godin, small s" but putting on the mock to become "the person who wrote 10,000 blog posts." The role is intentional, consistent, and service-oriented. Not fake — the best version made habitual.

**Deploy**: Define your professional role explicitly. Every morning: "If I were playing the best version of me today, what would I do?"
