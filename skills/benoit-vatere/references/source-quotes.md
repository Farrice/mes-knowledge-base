# Benoit Vatere — Source Receipts

Source: Marketing Against the Grain, "The #1 Marketing Lesson From Building a $1B+ Brand" (YouTube 9zBThLCpPh4, 34:25, watched 2026-08-04). Host: Kipp Bodnar. Captions garble the name ("Benwa"/"Benwis"/"Benino Veter") — correct: **Benoit Vatere** (VERIFIED via The Org / LinkedIn / Groceryshop 2025).

All timestamps approximate caption times. Quotes lightly de-duplicated from rolling captions; texture preserved.

## Origin / posture
- [02:10] "I'm not a CPG guy. I'm a founder. I'm a startup guy. I built a lot of ad tech platforms over the years… I never had really what I wanted to buy media properly."
- [02:33] Built "a rebate redemption platform for measurement… to measure the impact of creators on social in retail sales, brick and mortar" → got him in front of Liquid Death "before actually even the water was in the can, with Mike [the] co-founder."
- [03:40] Host: "Distribution is undefeated. You have to figure out distribution at a reasonable cost that works for your business or you don't have a business."

## Audit + measurement
- [04:15] "First order of business was to understand where the money was spent and at what stage of the funnel… Categorize the whole thing. Now you start understanding, hey, we may spend too much on one stage, not enough on the other."
- [05:06] "I don't need perfect, I need signals… if you look for the right metric, the right confidence level, you will never get it, or it's going to take forever, and by the time you get it, it's over."
- [05:52] "I can as a media buyer drive very high return, but those high returns are actually fake… Meta will tell you you spend $1, you made three. The problem is those $3… would have happened without the ad as well."
- [06:38] "The best signal of incrementality is I'm acquiring new people to the brand… I couldn't run incremental tests on previous data. It was going to take me months, an MMM of two years… that will never get me where I need to be within the next couple weeks."
- [07:02] "When I start mapping ROAS with [new-to-brand], if the ROAS is high and the percentage of new-to-brand is high, that means it is probably very incremental. So now I can just triage my spend based on that filter."
- [09:47] "I'm looking at what I call now incremental ROAS… there's a school of thought right now where we can get that data pretty quickly because models move… better."
- [10:14] **Geo holdout**: "Let's say LA and New York… very similar in terms of shoppers. I'm going to spend money in New York and not spend money in LA. And I'm going to see if my sales go up in New York and don't go up in LA… If people are very deep in incrementality, they'll say, 'What is he talking about?' But this is kind of the fundamentals."
- [08:55] On social commerce: "TikTok Shop… for me is mostly a discovery tool more than it is a scalable tool — what is the impact on the scalable sales, retail sales… on Amazon.com, on walmart.com, on target.com." (Liquid Death sells no D2C — retailer sites only.)

## Mid-funnel chain
- [12:20] Consideration = "the efficiency of driving from someone that sees your ad to go to your PDP — your product page on the retailer."
- [12:45] "Clickthrough rate is important, but if you have a good click-through rate on a high CPM, it still drives a pretty expensive cost per click… a pretty expensive cost to get someone to your product page view."
- [13:23] "There's a popup window that says you're leaving the site… people click but don't land on your product page view. So you need to measure that as well… I look at the cost per product page view."
- [13:52] "My lever is the clickthrough rate… my lever is the CPM… and I need to also measure if there's any kind of drop-off from the click to the product page."

## Testing doctrine
- [14:21] "I'm only looking at big gains… I'm only looking for home runs. We are a brand in hyper growth mode. I'm not looking for 5% gain. I'm looking for double-digit gains… even more than 20% every time."
- [14:46] "If you're at a point like, hey, I may get 5% but I need more clicks to understand if it's true — I don't care if it's 5%. Let's move on."
- [14:55] "If you have a 30% gain, you don't need a lot of data… It's either working or it's not."
- [15:08] "Sorry for people that don't follow baseball, but I'm swinging for the fences every single time."
- [16:50] Host+Benoit: "If you run a test and you're four days in and the data is still not conclusive, that means the outcome will never be big. So just move on."

## Channel selection
- [17:40] "What I look for [in] a platform that gives me awareness is ability to have high frequency. If I cannot drive high frequency, awareness won't work… If the platform dictates the frequency without letting you play with it, you are in trouble. Perfect example: Meta."
- [18:21] "If you are showing up only once a week, which is most of the algo [default], 1 to 1.5 times a week, and you're in the middle of a thousand pieces of content, nobody will remember you. So in my opinion, **social is not for awareness, period.** But CTV, radio, podcasts — all those channels are really good for awareness."
- [18:45] "For consideration… I look for someone that is going to let me optimize against a click-out… Meta may be good for that… I can have the algo optimize against a click, no problem. I can do retargeting."
- [19:14] "The platforms that I love are the platforms where people convert, which is amazon.com, Walmart.com."
- [19:35] "It's really looking at what is the platform good at based on what I need… If they don't let me control [the levers], then I won't touch it."
- [20:04] Host: "So many marketers are just like, 'Oh, all my customers are on Instagram, I'm just going to spend some money there' — the complete wrong way to think about it."

## Creative mapping
- [21:19] "It's the same for the creatives… each stage of the funnel has its own creative."
- [21:33] Awareness creative: "has to be about remembering the brand — not remembering that it has zero sugar, not remembering that it's available at that very specific retailer… even if I have to do that, remembering that we are a beverage and we're called Liquid Death."
- [21:56] "You cannot have everything in a single creative — who you are, how much you cost, what are the RTBs… You need to have one focus."
- [22:15] "We picked our lane, which is comedy. Not an easy lane, but this is our lane… as we are funny, people will remember."
- [22:44] Consideration: "now that you remember me… I need to basically link the brand name with the benefits of the product… why the product is healthy, why the product is affordable, where to find it."
- [23:13] "If I try to turn a crazy funny creative into also telling them why the product is good and where they can buy it, it's not going to happen."
- [23:33] Retargeting: "I probably not say the exact same thing I told them the first time around… if they clicked and they didn't convert, why they didn't convert? …you get even more precise on what you touch."
- [23:47] "People bought once but didn't repeat yet — how do you target people that are lapsed? At that point it's again a different messaging."
- [24:52] "You cannot have a campaign that has everything… [the industry] making you believe that you can do the full funnel in a single ad unit."
- [25:05] "There's all those people trying to sound cool by saying, 'Yeah, the funnel has collapsed. The funnel is shorter.' The funnel will always exist. You need to get aware of the brand, consider the brand, buy the brand. Can you make that happen faster? Maybe for some people. But some people are going to take a long time."
- [25:52] Host: "It's the collision point of data and taste… there's some cool shoots, but that's a small part of what it actually takes to sell something."

## Golden Core + control doctrine
- [26:46] "If you chase conversion from day one, you're going to fail… if your only metric is CAC-LTV ratio, you are going to fail."
- [27:03] "You always have what I call the golden core… people that are going to convert pretty fast… this golden core has a certain size — a million dollars of revenue, $3 million, $500,000. As you get out of this, there's no more scale. Your CAC is going to start rising… your CAC-to-LTV ratio is going to go upside down. And now you're starting to say, 'What am I doing? Oh, the Meta algorithm is not working anymore.' No, no, no. The true people outside of this golden core need a full funnel."
- [27:47] "From the very beginning we worked the awareness campaigns to know the brand… things that you will remember — not about the benefits of the product but about the brand. And that's a very uncomfortable thing to do because you cannot measure the dollar impact… But if you don't do that, you don't feed the top of the funnel… you have nobody to convert at the bottom."
- [28:48] "Investing money on LTV-to-CAC and highly converting media [feels like] a good way to not waste money. And the reality is it's counterintuitively false… you are wasting money because you would get a lot of that revenue anyway."
- [29:20] "Especially in the early stage… you have investors on your back telling you, 'Oh, what was your ROAS? What was your CAC?' That's going to lead to trouble very fast."
- [29:49] Host: "Investors are good investors. They're not good marketers." Benoit: "Exactly. And they're the ones that believe [they] are good marketers and are going to give you all their ideas."
- [30:15] Time-machine lesson: "**Retail media is key for CPG** people. Don't focus only on your D2C… your Shopify revenue. Focus on what you can drive at Amazon, at Walmart, at Target… I wish I had paid more attention from day one in retail media."
- [31:42] "You have to focus on where you think you can get scale… You go where you have the most control, which is most likely your Shopify website, but this is not necessarily where you get the most scale. **Where you get the most scale is mostly where you get the least amount of control.**"
- [32:18] "If you only think about the first step, you may not take the right first step. Think where you want to go."
- [32:48] "That one is driving me crazy — this notion of throw as many creatives as you can to Meta and the algo will figure it out. I don't want Meta to decide what creatives get in front of each person. **I will decide.** Consistency of creatives matters because what Meta sees is just one small fragment of the entire funnel. If I let Meta play around with it, it's going to completely distract my full funnel."
- [33:17] "**Let control go on where you need to scale and the channels to distribute. Take control over where you buy and how you buy.**"
- [33:29] Host close: incentives "sometimes aligned but not always aligned — you want control to make sure you're using them in the ways that are aligned."
