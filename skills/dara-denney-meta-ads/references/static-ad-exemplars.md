## Winners she opened with (the "why is this working" set)

### Wandering Bear Coffee — headliner + primal desire
- **On screen (t≈00:09 / 06:03):** Cold-brew latte can held mid-pour (coffee splashing), yellow bear logo, headline **"SO GOOD IT SHOULD BE BAD FOR YOU"** + a small spec badge, "SHOP LATTE" button.
- **Format:** Headliner ad (message is the focal point) fused with product-in-action visual.
- **Why it works (her frame):** The headline sells the *desire* (permission to indulge), not a product spec. Product = mechanism; the desire is the headline. Clean hierarchy — headline first, product second, badge supporting.

### TIME / supplement — headliner + authority quote + youth desire
- **On screen (t≈07:25):** White capsules in a dish, vertical ad, headline in quotes **"…THE CLOSEST WE'VE GOTTEN TO A FOUNTAIN OF YOUTH"**, the **TIME** logo (red box) as attribution, bottom bar **"USE CODE TRY25 FOR 20% OFF"**.
- **Format:** Headliner + golden-nugget/authority quote.
- **Why it works:** Borrows a third-party authority's words (TIME) as the headline — proof and desire in one beat. Primal desire = youth/status. The quote persuades; the brand just frames it.

## The 7 format archetypes — real examples she used

### 1. Educational infographic — Sweetgreen "The Economics of $15 Salads"
- **On screen (t≈10:21):** A Chartr/CHARTR-style bar-chart infographic titled **"The Economics Of $15 Salads,"** "Sweetgreen Q1 2024," a green "Sweetgreen Sales $15" bar vs a red "Total Opex $17.56" bar broken into cost components (Food/Packaging $4.15, Labor $4.35, Occupancy, etc.), footer noting an operating loss per $15 of revenue.
- **Why it works (her frame):** "Masquerades as something that does well organically and really doesn't feel like an ad" — leads with *education/value* any persona benefits from. Top-of-funnel workhorse. Build by asking "how can I create a piece of content any one of my personas actually benefits from" (education / entertainment / narrowing choices).

### 2. Headliner ad — Happy Tuesdays "The cheat code to your big weekend."
- **On screen (t≈11:02):** Silver sachet product held against a soft-neutral background, headline top-left **"The cheat code to your big weekend."**, small product name "Happy Tuesdays / Post-drink wellness pack."
- **Why it works:** "The focus is on that sexy messaging." Ogilvy print-era lineage — one big headline front and center. The message *is* the ad; the product is small. Best format for a taboo-style angle.

### 3. Benefits callout (Ogilvy "features callout" → "benefits callout")
- **Her note:** The callout format still works today (she pointed to the opener ads as examples). Test the top headline two ways: (a) tap the **core desire** vs saying something generic; (b) slide in a **golden-nugget testimonial** as the headline.

### 4. Comparison / us-vs-them — GRO Shampoo vs "Other Hair Growth Products"
- **On screen (t≈12:10):** Two-column layout. Left: **"GRO Shampoo & Conditioner"** with the pink bottles; right: **"Other Hair Growth Products"** with a bowl of green supplement pills. Middle rows compared with **green ✓ (GRO) vs red ✗ (others)**: "No harmful side effects," "Hormone free," "Visible results in 90 days," "Certified vegan & clean."
- **Why it works:** "The infamous us-versus-them style. Still cranking." Makes the brand's advantages legible in one glance via the ✓/✗ grid. Negative marketing baked in (names what the alternative lacks).

### 5. Transformation — "My secret for getting rid of dandruff" (lo-fi creator)
- **On screen (t≈12:36):** UGC-style vertical. Top quote **"My secret for getting rid of dandruff,"** a **before/after split** (flaky scalp labeled BEFORE, clean scalp labeled AFTER 2 USES), creator below holding two white bottles to camera.
- **Why it works:** "Don't just describe the after state — actually show it. This is why before-and-after ads work so well." Biggest gap + biggest opportunity for problem/solution-aware audiences ("they're just looking for proof"). This is a **lo-fi creator** static — her current needle-mover. (Before/afters are NOT illegal; cosmetic/weight-loss categories carry more restriction.)

### 6. Grid static — "Meet the Cook & Bake Set" ($1,090 → $632)
- **On screen (t≈12:54):** Sage-green vertical, header **"MEET THE Cook & Bake Set,"** struck-through **$1,090** next to **$632**, "STORAGE INCLUDED," a **grid of ~9 product tiles** (bowls, pans, baking sheets) each labeled.
- **Why it works:** "OG print-era format, especially for apparel brands or brands with multiple SKUs… always have on deck, especially during sales periods." Shows range + value density; the price anchor drives sale-period use. (She noted the Laura Geller top performer follows a similar grid pattern.)

### 7. Text-only ad — totallee "iPhone Cases Are Weird."
- **On screen (t≈13:10):** Near-all-text, minimal. Headline **"iPhone Cases Are Weird."** then a short founder's-letter body ("They don't make sense. Some of us woke up early to pre-order the new iPhone. Because we love its sleek design. But then we hide it away in a case…"), a slim phone image at right, brand **totallee**, "Called it 'The Best Case'" with Wired/CBS/Forbes.
- **Why it works:** "Borrowing from what's working as an organic format… founders' letters do really, really well." Primarily text-driven; reads like a post, not an ad. Clients get nervous; it performs.

## The AI production demo (SuperScale) — taste calibration

> Method is tool-agnostic (we render on Nano Banana 2 / gpt-image / fantastic-posters). Captured here for what she accepted vs rejected, verbatim.

- **Dr. Squatch "Blame your D.O., not your shirts" / "Your Deo is ruining your shirts"** (t≈20:18) — a **before/after split**, a **"disgusting closeup"** of a stained armpit, and a **"bold statement flatlay."** Reaction: "That's really visceral… I'm having kind of a strong reaction to this — and I think it's for the better." **Accepted** the visceral angle; advised Dr. Squatch to "lean a little more into the visceral because right now they're playing it very clean and very safe."
- **Rejects she named (verbatim):** the **em dash** ("I don't like this M dash" → removed it); **misspellings** ("Deodorant spelled wrong — please spell it correctly"); **too much text / no focal point** ("a little too much going on… less is more"); **review-collage / quote-heavy social-proof statics** ("I don't really love how those always turn out").
- **Edit-to-refine loop (verbatim edits):** "remove the m dash," "change the background to a beige Dr. Squatch color… the white-on-white makes it hard to see it's a t-shirt… please make the t-shirt smaller," "make me five more variations of this ad that could fit three different personas — I want to target mostly problem-aware audience." → **Our analog: `python3 execution/generate_image.py --edit <img.png> "<natural-language edit>"`.**
- **Research-first, always:** she runs research before generating — the platform ran sub-agents scanning competitor ad libraries (Native, Every Man Jack, Harry's), scored competitor ads (avg winning-ad-score ~62–80), then produced a **creative gap analysis** (which awareness levels / personas / formats are missing → lack of creative diversity). She generates in **3-variation batches** ("I often like to stick to about three in the beginning").

## Recognition test (use before shipping)

A static is ready when you can say: **(1)** which of the 7 formats it is, **(2)** which single goal + specific persona + awareness level it serves (Layer 1), **(3)** it passes the 1-second comprehension test (a stranger names what's sold in ~1 second), and **(4)** its headline sells a *desire/outcome*, not a spec. If it reads as "this and this and this and this" with no focal point — it's the ad she scrapped. Kill it.
