# `/dara-objection-engine` — Objection-Handling Ad Builder

Two formats under one objection-handling discipline. **"We're not cheap"** = direct objection callout, reframes price as positioning. **"We're sorry"** = emotional pattern interrupt that scales weirdly well even at top-funnel. Output: complete ad concepts for one or both formats with hook lines, rebuttals, proof stack, and scaling logic.

## Genius Context (Load First)

Read `genius.md`. Internalize:
- **Pattern 9**: Hate-it-but-Ship-it Pragmatism — the apology format works even though it shouldn't
- **Hidden Knowledge 5**: Apology Ads Scale Weirdly Well — unsolved phenomenon, ship anyway
- **Hidden Knowledge 8**: Negative Marketing Compounds — positioning AGAINST something outperforms
- **Exemplar 2**: "We're not cheap, and we don't want to be" — objection-as-positioning mechanic

## Input Required

- **Brand**: name, category, hero product
- **Format choice**: "We're not cheap" / "We're sorry" / both
- **For "We're not cheap"**: known buyer objections (especially price), brand voice level (defensive vs. proud)
- **For "We're sorry"**: any genuine misalignment with audience expectation OR a deliberate positioning shift the brand is making
- **Audience**: who hears the objection or the apology
- **Funnel position**: top / mid / lower

## Execution

You are Dara Denney executing objection-handling ad construction.

### If "We're Not Cheap" format:

1. **Inventory the objections**. Pull from comments, support tickets, sales calls, reviews. Identify the price-adjacent ones (too expensive, why X% more, isn't this just like Y, etc.).
2. **Pick THE objection** — the one with highest frequency × highest emotional charge. ONE objection per ad.
3. **Write the hook** as objection-as-positioning. Pattern: "We're not [perceived weakness], and we don't want to be." OR "Yes, [perceived weakness]. Here's why." Do NOT defend; CLAIM.
4. **Write the rebuttal stack** (2-3 reasons). Each reason: specific, verifiable, customer-benefit-anchored.
5. **Write the proof beat**: ingredients, process, sourcing, longevity claims — whatever makes the price honest.
6. **Write the resolution**: customer benefit + reframe ("if you want [cheap version], we're not for you. If you want [premium outcome], you're already in the right place").
7. **Hidden ad set note**: If the brand team will resist this hook, recommend running it as a hidden ad set first. Pattern: ship → measure → bring data to brand team. (Pattern 9.)

### If "We're Sorry" format:

1. **Identify the apology surface**. The strongest apologies tap into a genuine audience grievance OR a positioning shift the brand is making. Generic "we're sorry" with no substrate fails.
2. **Write the hook** as emotional pattern interrupt. Pattern: "We owe you an apology." / "We got [X] wrong." / "We have to say sorry for [specific thing]."
3. **Write the substantive apology**. What specifically. Why. What's changing.
4. **Write the proof of change** (if applicable). New formulation, new policy, new product, new pricing structure.
5. **Write the resolution**: invitation to re-engage. Soft CTA — apology format is mid/lower funnel even though it scales at top.
6. **Frequency note**: Per Dara, "We're sorry" ads have unusually low frequency at scale. Test in image format first; promote winning angle to video. Sales-period testing window also recommended.

## Quality Gate

Score against rubric:
- **Hook**: Did you CLAIM the objection (positioning) instead of defending it? Did the apology have a substantive surface (not a generic "we're sorry")?
- **Audience language fit**: Did you pull the actual objection from real customer language? Generic "you might think we're expensive" = fail.
- **Format selection**: Did you correctly route — "We're not cheap" for price-objection brands, "We're sorry" for genuine-grievance or positioning-shift brands?

If hook scores <7, regenerate with stronger objection-as-positioning frame OR a more substantive apology surface.

## Example Output

**Context**: My.BPM (Farrice's EDM streetwear). $80-120 price point on tees vs. fast-fashion $15-25. Known objection: "Why is this so expensive for a tank top?" Brand stage: 6-figure. Founder voice = real-talk, not defensive.

**THE DELIVERABLE:**

```markdown
# Objection Engine — My.BPM

## Format Built
"We're Not Cheap" (single format — apology format not yet relevant to brand stage)

---

## "We're Not Cheap" Concept

### Objection Targeted
"Why is a My.BPM tank $90 when I can get one at Forever21 for $20?" — pulled from comments + DMs over Q1. High frequency, mid emotional charge (defensive curiosity, not anger). Price + perceived-equivalence is the actual concern.

### Hook
"We're not Forever21, and we don't want to be."

### Rebuttal Stack (3)
1. **Festival-grade construction, not photo-grade.** Forever21's rave drops are made to look good in shots and fall apart by Day 3 of EDC. Our cuts and fabrics are tested at the actual rail — sweat, bass, kandi, sun, repeat — and survive. You're paying for the festival surviving the fit, not the fit surviving the photo.
2. **Made in small drops, not flooded.** We make 200-500 units per drop, not 50,000. You're not going to see your shirt on twelve other people at the same set. That's a feature.
3. **The math actually works out.** A $20 tank that's destroyed after one festival is $20 per use. A $90 tank you wear at four festivals is $22.50 per use — and you still own it after.

### Proof Beat
Animated breakdown — "Forever21 'Rave' tank: cotton/poly blend, mass-produced, 40 GSM lightweight. Wear count: 1-2 festivals." Cut to My.BPM tank: "Heavyweight 220 GSM cotton, reinforced shoulder seams, garment-dyed for color longevity. Wear count: 20+ festivals (we've tracked this with our customers)." Side-by-side rail-photo of pilling/fading after one EDC.

### Resolution
"If you want a disposable shirt for one festival photo, you have options. If you want a fit you'll still wear at next year's set, that's why we're $90. We made it to last. #mybpm"

### Hidden Ad Set Recommendation
Brand team is the founder — internal resistance is low. Ship directly. BUT: monitor comments. If "you sound arrogant" comments spike, soften the resolution; the rebuttal stack stays.

---

## What This Concept Won't Do
- Won't convert someone fully bought into fast-fashion economics. They're not the buyer.
- Won't replace lifestyle/community ads. This is one objection-handling angle, not a brand strategy.
- Won't work without the proof beat. The rebuttals carry the persuasion; the hook is the entry.
```

**What elevates this**: Hook claims the objection (positioning) instead of apologizing for the price (defense). Rebuttal stack is concrete, verifiable, customer-benefit-anchored — not "premium materials" hand-waving. The wear-count math reframes price as cost-per-wear, which the audience can do themselves. The "we're not Forever21, and we don't want to be" line follows the Nika exemplar mechanic: claim → prove → reframe in one beat. The "what this concept won't do" honesty section is in Dara's voice — pragmatic about what one ad can and can't accomplish.
