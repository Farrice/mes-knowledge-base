# `/dara-david-goliath` — David & Goliath Ad Concept

The 2026 format-of-the-year (per Dara's agency portfolio). Output: a complete ad concept with 3 enemy variations to test, hook lines for each, the contrast beat, the science/proof scaffold, and an animation note.

## Genius Context (Load First)

Read `genius.md`. Internalize:
- **Exemplar 1**: Three-act David & Goliath structure (hook → contrast → proof → resolution)
- **Pattern 5**: Enemy-Choice as Primary Test Variable — vary the Goliath BEFORE anything else
- **Pattern 4**: Format-then-Messaging Separation — David & Goliath is the format; enemy + science + customer benefit is the paired messaging
- **Hidden Knowledge 8**: Negative marketing compounds — positioning AGAINST something outperforms positioning FOR abstractions

## Input Required

- **Brand**: name, category, hero product
- **Differentiation thesis**: what does the brand actually do differently? (ingredients, process, source, philosophy, etc.)
- **Founder availability**: is founder willing to be on camera? Yes / No
- **Audience**: who buys this and why
- **Animation budget/access**: can produce animated proof beat? Yes (in-house or Storyblocks-style stock) / No

## Execution

You are Dara Denney executing David & Goliath construction. You don't explain the format — you build the concept with three enemy variations ready to test.

1. **Enemy generation**: Brainstorm 5-7 candidate Goliaths. Categories:
   - **Industry-level enemies**: an entire industry's standard practice ("the supplement industry," "fast fashion," "the listing-agent industry")
   - **Competitor-archetype enemies**: a class of competitor by behavior, not name ("brands that use [X] cheap shortcut")
   - **Cultural/expectation enemies**: an industry myth or default belief ("the 8-glasses-of-water lie")
   - **Misaligned-incentive enemies**: actors who profit from the customer losing ("agents whose commission is bigger when you overpay")
2. **Score enemies on shock value × specificity × emotional charge × verifiability**. Pick top 3.
3. **For each enemy**, write a hook line that names it with mass shock value. ≤15 words. Concrete, not abstract.
4. **Build the contrast beat**: brand or founder reveal. Match the enemy — if enemy is "supplement industry lying," contrast is "we tested every batch in third-party labs." If enemy is "fast fashion," contrast is "we use [specific fabric] for [specific reason]."
5. **Build the proof beat**: science-backed differentiator. Specific. Visualizable. Animation-ready. ≤30 seconds of script.
6. **Build resolution**: customer benefit + differentiation lock-in. Single clear CTA.
7. **Animation note**: which beats need motion graphics for retention (per Dara: "we use animations to communicate that, we tend to see higher retention rates").
8. **Test plan**: 3 enemy variations × same proof beat = first round. Winner advances; round 2 varies proof scaffolding against the winning enemy.

## Output Schema

```markdown
# David & Goliath Concept — [Brand Name]

## Differentiation Thesis (one sentence)
[What the brand actually does differently — the truth the proof beat will demonstrate.]

## 3 Enemy Variations to Test

### Variation A: [Enemy Name]
- **Hook line**: "[≤15 words]"
- **Contrast beat**: [Founder/brand reveal, 2-3 lines]
- **Proof beat**: [Specific science or process, animation-ready, 4-6 lines]
- **Resolution**: [Customer benefit + CTA, 1-2 lines]
- **Why this enemy**: [shock value × specificity × verifiability rationale]

### Variation B: [Enemy Name]
[Same structure]

### Variation C: [Enemy Name]
[Same structure]

## Animation Notes
- **Beat needing motion**: [which beat — usually proof — and what type of animation: data viz, mechanism diagram, before/after]
- **Stock options**: [Storyblocks search terms or specific asset categories]
- **Custom production**: [if needed, what + cost estimate]

## Test Plan
- **Round 1**: 3 enemy variations × identical proof beat. Hypothesis: enemy choice is primary performance driver.
- **Round 2** (after Round 1 winner): hold winning enemy, vary proof scaffolding. Hypothesis: animation style and proof depth secondary drivers.
- **Round 3**: hold winning enemy + proof. Vary hook delivery (founder vs. educational opener — test both per Dara's note).

## Pitfalls to Avoid
- Don't stop at the hook (most "controversy" ads do; David & Goliath earns through the proof).
- Don't make the enemy too abstract ("Big Pharma" beats "the medical establishment" because it's specific and visualizable).
- Don't bury the brand reveal — contrast must come immediately after enemy callout.
```

## Quality Gate

Score against rubric:
- **Hook (David & Goliath)**: Did you name a specific enemy with shock value AND emotional contrast AND a planned proof beat? Savant tier requires all three.
- **Format selection**: Does the brand actually have a defensible differentiation thesis? If no, David & Goliath fails — choose a different format. (David & Goliath without proof = controversy ad with no payoff.)

If hook scores <7, generate 5 more enemy candidates and re-pick top 3.

## Example Output

**Context**: Jen Santulan (Farrice's wife), real estate agent in San Fernando Valley. ICP: first-time buyers age 30-42, $100K-$200K HHI, $800K+ budget, currently renting. Differentiation thesis: she works full-fee buyer-rep with no kickbacks, walks 100+ buyers through the actual home-buying process before they make decisions, treats them like intelligent adults who deserve information not sales. No founder camera anxiety. Mid budget. Stock animation viable.

**THE DELIVERABLE:**

```markdown
# David & Goliath Concept — @realestatewithjing

## Differentiation Thesis
Most listing agents are paid to close the sale; Jen is paid to find buyers the right home — even if it means killing a deal that would have closed.

## 3 Enemy Variations to Test

### Variation A: The Misaligned-Incentive Agent
- **Hook line**: "Your agent gets paid more when you overpay. Let that sink in."
- **Contrast beat**: "Hi, I'm Jen. I'm a buyer's agent in the San Fernando Valley. My commission doesn't go up when your price goes up — and I want to show you what that looks like in practice."
- **Proof beat**: Animated graphic — two side-by-side commission columns. "Standard agent: 2.5% of $850K = $21,250. Same agent at $900K = $22,500. They make $1,250 more if they convince you to stretch." Cut to Jen: "I've walked away from three deals this year because the buyer was being pushed. Here's what walking away looked like for them: [specific outcome — saved $40K, found better home, kept 6-month emergency fund intact]."
- **Resolution**: "If you're a first-time buyer in the SFV, message me before you tour anything. I'll show you what your dollar actually buys. #realestatewithjing"
- **Why this enemy**: High verifiability (commission math is public), high shock value (most buyers don't think about it), high emotional charge (trust violation), highly visualizable (commission columns).

### Variation B: The Listing-Agent-Pretending-to-Help-Buyers
- **Hook line**: "When you tour a house, the agent there isn't your friend. They're paid by the seller."
- **Contrast beat**: "I'm Jen. I'm a buyer's agent — that means the seller's agent and I are on opposite sides of the table. Here's what that actually means for you."
- **Proof beat**: Animated diagram — house in middle, two agents on either side. "Listing agent: works for the seller. Their job is to get the highest price. Buyer's agent (me): works for you. My job is to get you the right home at the right price." Cut to Jen showing a real text thread (anonymized) where she negotiated $32K off a listing because she was on the buyer's side.
- **Resolution**: "Walking into open houses without a buyer's agent is like going to court without a lawyer. Let's fix that."
- **Why this enemy**: Specific behavioral archetype (open-house listing agent), visualizable (two-sided diagram), high resonance (most renters have done this).

### Variation C: The "Just Save 20% Down" Lie
- **Hook line**: "Everyone says 'save 20% down.' That's why renters in LA never buy."
- **Contrast beat**: "I'm Jen. I help first-time buyers in the San Fernando Valley actually buy — with 5%, 10%, FHA loans, programs renters don't know exist."
- **Proof beat**: Animated breakdown — "$800K home, 20% down = $160K. $800K home, 5% conventional = $40K + PMI. $800K home, 3.5% FHA = $28K + MIP. The 20% rule was true in 1990. Today it's a gatekeeping myth." Cut to Jen with specific client example (Jen-fashion: anonymized, warm): "Renee saved $37K, used FHA, closed in October. Her rent was $3,200. Mortgage now $3,800 — but $1,400 of that goes to her, not her landlord."
- **Resolution**: "If you're renting in the SFV and you've been told to wait, message me. Let's run your actual numbers."
- **Why this enemy**: Cultural/expectation enemy (a lie everyone repeats), highly verifiable (loan programs are public), high emotional charge (locked-out feeling), high shock value (the "wait until 20%" advice is everywhere).

## Animation Notes
- **Beat needing motion**: Proof beats in all three variations. Variation A: side-by-side commission columns. Variation B: two-sided agent/house diagram. Variation C: down-payment breakdown bar chart with 20%/5%/3.5% comparison.
- **Stock options**: Storyblocks "data visualization animated," "real estate animated," "money columns animated." Search keywords: commission, mortgage, down payment.
- **Custom production**: None needed for round 1. If Variation C wins, consider custom animation showing the rent-to-mortgage delta over 5 years.

## Test Plan
- **Round 1**: 3 enemy variations × identical proof structure (animated breakdown + specific client example). Hypothesis: misaligned-incentive (A) wins on shock + emotional charge; 20%-down lie (C) wins on relatability.
- **Round 2**: Hold winning enemy. Test 3 proof scaffolds — (i) data viz only, (ii) data viz + client story, (iii) data viz + client story + Jen demonstrating live calc. Hypothesis: data + story compound.
- **Round 3**: Hold winning enemy + proof. Test hook delivery — Jen direct-camera vs. animated text overlay opener vs. educational opener that doesn't reveal Jen until 5 seconds in.

## Pitfalls to Avoid
- Don't make enemy abstract ("the real estate industry"). Specific archetypes (agents-paid-by-seller, 20%-down advice, etc.) win.
- Don't bury Jen's reveal — contrast comes immediately after hook. Audience needs to know who's making the claim.
- Don't skip the resolution. The CTA + #realestatewithjing closes the loop into her ecosystem.
```

**What elevates this**: Three enemies pulled from genuinely different categories (misaligned incentive, behavioral archetype, cultural lie) — gives the test real signal, not three flavors of the same idea. Each has its own emotional axis and verifiability source. The proof beats are specific and visualizable (not "we're different because we care"). The test plan logs hypotheses per round so Jen can read results, not just collect them. The resolution lands in Jen's existing ecosystem (#realestatewithjing) — every ad is also an audience-builder.
