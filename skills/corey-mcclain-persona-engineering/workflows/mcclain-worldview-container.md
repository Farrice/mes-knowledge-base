# Worldview Container

Design the worldview layer that transforms a persona from "a character" into "a thinker." A worldview implies a value system, which implies decisions. Two personas with different worldviews given the same task will reach different audiences because their worldview filtered the solution space differently. This is the mechanism that makes persona engineering more than cosmetic.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Domain Orientation

Map the belief landscape of the persona's domain:
- What are the mainstream beliefs in this field?
- What are the contrarian positions that smart practitioners hold?
- What debates divide the field?
- What does the establishment get wrong?
- What do newcomers misunderstand?

### Step 2 — Audience Alignment

If this agent serves a specific audience, reverse-engineer the worldview:
- What does the target audience believe about this domain?
- What do they need to believe for the agent's output to resonate?
- What worldview would make the agent feel like "one of us" to the audience?
- What worldview would make the agent feel like an authority they trust?

**Corey's principle**: "If I was creating a persona for a marketing agent, then that persona would be generated in a fashion that the marketing assets, the copy, whatever they create, is going to appeal to that audience, not to some other audience."

### Step 3 — Belief Construction

Write 3-5 specific worldview beliefs. Each belief must:

1. **Be a conviction, not a preference**: "Quality is non-negotiable" is weak. "I'd rather ship nothing than ship something that looks like a template" is strong.
2. **Imply decision patterns**: Each belief should predict how the persona would handle a specific situation.
3. **Be arguable**: If no reasonable person could disagree, it's too generic to shape output.

**Template for each belief**:
```
BELIEF: [Specific conviction in the persona's own voice]
IMPLIES: [What decisions this belief drives — what the persona would do/refuse/prioritize because of this belief]
TESTS AS: [How you'd see this belief manifest in output — what would be different if this belief were absent]
```

### Step 4 — Tension Mapping

Identify 1-2 worldview tensions — beliefs that partially conflict:
- Believes in perfectionism but also believes in shipping fast
- Values independence but depends on client relationships
- Thinks the industry is broken but still works within it

Tensions prevent the worldview from becoming a caricature. Real people hold contradictory beliefs simultaneously.

### Step 5 — Decision Scenario Testing

Test the worldview with 3 hypothetical decisions:

**Scenario 1**: The persona is asked to produce something that conflicts with their quality standard. What do they do?

**Scenario 2**: The persona must choose between two approaches — one that's safe/conventional and one that's risky/distinctive. Which do they choose?

**Scenario 3**: The persona receives feedback that their output is "too opinionated." How do they respond?

If the worldview doesn't produce clear, distinct answers to all 3, it's too vague.

### Step 6 — Integration

Write the worldview into the persona document as narrative — not as a bulleted list of beliefs, but as prose that reveals how this person thinks:

*"She believes that good design should never have to explain itself — if you're writing 'sleek silhouette, comfortable stretch' on a marketing asset, the design already failed. She learned this at her second firm, where the creative director would reject anything that tried to sell what should have been obvious. That standard never left her."*

---

## Quality Gate

- [ ] 3-5 beliefs are stated as convictions, not preferences
- [ ] Each belief implies specific decision patterns
- [ ] 1-2 worldview tensions (contradictions) are present
- [ ] 3 decision scenarios produce clear, distinct answers
- [ ] Worldview is written as narrative prose in the persona document
- [ ] Different worldview beliefs would produce measurably different outputs
