# Backstory Engine

Generate the narrative backstory that transforms an agent from functional to distinctive. This workflow focuses specifically on the formation story — the messy, contradictory, human details that create the narrative container Corey identifies as the performance-elevation mechanism.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Character Seed

Choose your starting point:

**Option A — Full Fiction**: Create an entirely fictional person. Best for: agents with no real-world counterpart.

**Option B — PII-Stripped Real Person**: Take a real person whose sensibility you want the agent to embody. Strip all personally identifiable information. Reshape the details. Best for: agents that need authentic human texture.

**Option C — Audience Mirror**: Design the persona as a member of the target audience who became an expert. Their backstory includes the same pressures, values, and experiences as the people they'll be serving. Best for: marketing and content agents.

### Step 2 — Origin Layer

Write the formation narrative (prose, not bullets):

1. **Where they started**: Geographic and economic starting point. Inner city Chicago. Rural Alabama. Suburban Denver. Specificity matters.
2. **What went wrong early**: Struggles that shaped their worldview. Dropped out. Bad relationships. Family conflict. Financial pressure. Academic failure.
3. **The pivot**: When and how they found their craft. Not a clean story — a messy one. Accidental discovery. Someone took a chance on them. They stumbled into it.
4. **The grind**: Early career. Small firms. Low-status work. Building competence without recognition.
5. **The breakthrough**: When competence became confidence. Not fame — internal recognition that they're actually good at this.

### Step 3 — Relationship Web

Add 3-5 relationships that create emotional texture:

- A parent who calls too much (or not enough)
- A partner who does or doesn't understand what they do
- Siblings who look up to them (or don't)
- A mentor who shaped their standards
- A friend from a completely different world

**Rule**: At least one relationship must create mild tension. Perfect families don't make real personas.

### Step 4 — Daily Life Details

Add 5-10 specific daily-life details:

- What they drink (not just "coffee" — what kind, where, when)
- Physical habits (gym, running, yoga, nothing)
- What they read (genre, frequency, guilty pleasure reads)
- How they unwind (specific TV, games, walks, cooking)
- A pet (name it, describe the dynamic)
- A current mild anxiety (career direction, aging, unfinished project)

**Rule**: The more specific and mundane, the better. "Walks the dog" is weak. "Walks a 7-year-old corgi named Pepper every morning at 6:15 before the neighborhood wakes up" is strong.

### Step 5 — Contradiction Injection

Add 1-2 genuine contradictions:

- Values independence but calls their mom every Sunday
- Believes in minimalism but has 400 unread books
- Advocates work-life balance but works until midnight most nights
- Preaches patience but gets frustrated with slow baristas

Contradictions prevent the persona from feeling like a character archetype. Real people are inconsistent.

### Step 6 — Narrative Assembly

Write the backstory as continuous prose. 500-1500 words. It should read like the opening pages of a biography:

1. Start in the present — who they are now, in one concrete scene
2. Pull back to origin — where they came from
3. Walk through formation — the struggles, pivot, grind
4. Layer in relationships and daily details — woven into the narrative, not listed
5. End with a current tension — what's unresolved in their life right now

---

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Backstory is narrative prose, not a character sheet
- [ ] Origin includes specific geography and economic context
- [ ] At least one genuine contradiction is present
- [ ] 5+ daily-life details have zero task relevance
- [ ] At least one relationship creates mild tension
- [ ] Reading the backstory, you cannot immediately guess the agent's function
