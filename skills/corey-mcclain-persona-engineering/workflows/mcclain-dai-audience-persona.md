# Consumer Posture → Persona Worldview

Reverse-engineer an agent's persona from the audience it needs to reach. Dai Media's Consumer Posture framework identifies the *radical individual* — not a demographic, but a real findable person with an occupation, activities, and thought processes. McClain's persona architecture then builds an agent whose worldview, voice, and messy details are shaped to naturally produce content that resonates with that specific individual.

**Why this combination matters**: McClain's persona without audience reverse-engineering produces an agent with a rich identity that may or may not appeal to the right people. Dai Media's consumer posture without persona installation produces sharp audience intelligence that's then handed to a generic agent for execution. The stack closes the gap — the audience research *becomes* the persona architecture.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — The Individual Before the Community

Apply Dai Media's Genius Pattern 2 — find ONE specific findable individual before any generalization:

1. **Who is the single person** this agent's output needs to resonate with? Not a segment — a person. Named or nameable.
2. Apply **The Kristen Stewart Test** (Dai Media Pattern 3): Write a description of this individual. If you showed it to them, would they say "Yes, that's me"?
3. If you can't pass the Kristen Stewart Test, your audience understanding is too abstract. Research more before proceeding.

### Step 2 — Consumer Posture Architecture

Build the three-dimensional posture profile (Dai Media Pattern 4):

**Dimension 1 — Occupation** (What they do):
- Not just job title — their relationship to their work
- How does their work shape their identity?
- What professional pressures do they carry into their personal life?

**Dimension 2 — Activity** (How they move through the world):
- What rituals structure their day?
- What do they do when no one is watching?
- What are their "No Phone Policy" moments? (Dai Media Pattern 6)
- What activities do they use to decompress, signal identity, or process emotion?

**Dimension 3 — Thought Process** (How they decide):
- How do they evaluate options? (Logic-first? Feeling-first? Social-proof-first?)
- What makes them trust vs. distrust?
- What do they actively dislike? (Dai Media Pattern 11 — The Stunned Brand Owner Test)
- What can they not yet articulate but deeply feel? (Dai Media Pattern 13 — The Content Impossibility Diagnosis)

**Output**: Consumer Posture Card — a living reference for the persona construction

### Step 3 — Emotional Outcome Mapping

Apply Dai Media Pattern 7 — Emotional Outcome Over Problem-Solution:

1. What feeling does this individual want from the content this agent produces?
2. Not "information" or "value" — the actual emotional state. (e.g., "unreachably polished," "quietly confident," "seen without having to explain")
3. What emotional states would repel this individual? (e.g., "desperate for validation," "try-hard," "basic")
4. What is the gap between how they feel now and how they want to feel?

**This emotional outcome becomes the persona's core motivation**. The agent doesn't just know its audience — it *feels* what the audience wants to feel, and that feeling drives every output decision.

### Step 4 — World Design

Apply Dai Media's hidden knowledge — "World Design Precedes Marketing":

1. What world does this individual want to inhabit?
2. What values govern that world?
3. What is excluded from that world? (People, aesthetics, behaviors, language)
4. What role does the brand/product play in that world? (Not "solve a problem" — what space does it occupy?)

**This world becomes the persona's native environment**. When you write the persona's backstory, they live in a version of this world. Their messy details are drawn from this world's texture.

### Step 5 — Persona Forge with Audience Reverse-Engineering

Run `/mcclain-persona-forge` with the following modifications:

**Identity Foundation**: Build the persona as someone the target individual would trust, admire, or want to be friends with:
- Same cultural universe, but slightly ahead — a half-step of aspiration
- Shared references, shared annoyances, shared values
- NOT a mirror image — a co-conspirator (Dai Media hidden knowledge: "Co-Conspirators, Not Customers")

**Backstory Construction**: The persona's formation narrative should include:
- Experiences the target individual would recognize from their own life
- Struggles the target individual is currently navigating
- Achievements the target individual aspires to
- Contradictions the target individual would find relatable, not off-putting

**Worldview Design**: Derive directly from the Consumer Posture and World Design:
- 3-5 beliefs that the target individual holds (or wants to hold) but rarely hears articulated
- The persona's worldview should *name the unnamed* (Dai Media hidden knowledge: "Articulation Wins")
- Include at least one belief that is slightly provocative — something the individual secretly agrees with but wouldn't say publicly

**Voice Design**: Calibrate to the individual's thought process dimension:
- If they're logic-first → the voice is precise, evidence-based, understated
- If they're feeling-first → the voice is warm, image-rich, sensory
- If they're social-proof-first → the voice references shared communities and trusted figures
- Forbidden phrases should include whatever the individual associates with "try-hard" or "inauthentic" content

**Messy Details**: Draw from the Activity dimension of the Consumer Posture:
- The persona's daily habits should overlap with the target individual's
- Same kind of morning routine, same kind of media consumption, same kind of decompression activities
- These overlapping details create unconscious familiarity — the output *feels right* without the audience knowing why

### Step 6 — The Anti-Sell Calibration

Apply both frameworks' anti-sell principles:
- McClain's **Prada Principle**: The output states, doesn't sell. Premium positioning through restraint.
- Dai Media's **"Socials Can Be Anti-Social"**: Withdrawal and high-taste curation attract the audience this persona serves. No thirsty energy.

Test by generating a sample output and asking:
1. Would the target individual save this? (Not just like — save.)
2. Would they share it with one specific friend? (Not broadcast — targeted sharing.)
3. Would they feel like this content "gets them" without explicitly trying to?

If yes to all three → the persona is calibrated. If not → the worldview and voice need refinement.

### Step 7 — The Prediction Test

Apply Dai Media's Pattern 12 — "By The Tail" Methodology:

Present the persona-installed agent with a novel scenario the target individual might encounter. Before the agent responds, predict what the target individual would want to hear. Then compare.

If the agent's response matches your prediction → the persona is accurately audience-shaped.
If not → trace the misalignment back to the Consumer Posture Card and correct.

## Quality Gate

Before marking complete:
- [ ] Single findable individual identified and Kristen Stewart Test passed
- [ ] Consumer Posture Card covers all three dimensions (Occupation, Activity, Thought Process)
- [ ] Emotional outcome is specific (not "value" or "information" — a feeling)
- [ ] World Design documented — values, exclusions, and brand role
- [ ] Persona is narrative prose with audience-reverse-engineered worldview
- [ ] Voice calibrated to individual's thought process dimension
- [ ] Prada Principle and Anti-Social calibration both applied
- [ ] Prediction test passed — agent output matches audience expectation
- [ ] Sample output passes the save/share/gets-me test

## When to Use

- Building marketing agents for specific audience segments
- Creating content agents that need to "feel like" they understand the audience
- Designing brand voice agents that need to produce audience-resonant copy
- Any agent where "who consumes this" is as important as "what it produces"
- Client engagements where audience-market fit is the core deliverable
