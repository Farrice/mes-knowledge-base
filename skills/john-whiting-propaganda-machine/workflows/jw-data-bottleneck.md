# Data Bottleneck — The Master Question

> "What does the data say to do today? That is the number one question." — John Whiting

One job: refuse to act on "I feel like," pull the actual numbers, and isolate the single bottleneck that's holding the whole machine. Not 76 things. One. Releasing it is psychology; finding it is math.

---

## Pre-Flight Gate

Per the genius.md Decision Framework, this is the deploy for "I don't know what to work on next" / "I feel stuck." Gate before running:

1. **Is there a stated Point B?** A bottleneck only exists relative to a destination. No clear target ("I want to grow") → route to `jw-the-map` / `jw-freedom-number` to set the number first. You can't diagnose a road trip when the driver won't name the city.
2. **Does real data exist, or only a story about the data?** If the operator has no funnel numbers — leads, booked, showed, closed, cash collected, spend — send them to instrument the funnel for 14 days before any bottleneck talk. Diagnosing on vibes is the thing we're here to kill.
3. **Has a feeling already smuggled in a conclusion?** Catch any pre-loaded "I think I need a new offer / a book funnel / a VSL." Note it. That's almost always the wrong question, and we test it against the math, not the other way around.

## The Diagnosis (6 steps)

### 1. Confiscate the feeling
Every question arrives dressed as a feeling: "I feel like my offer's the problem," "I think I need more content." Refuse to answer it on its terms. Whiting: *"There's not a single Fortune 500 board meeting that starts with 'Hey Frank, how you feeling about what we should do?'"* Reframe in one line: *"I don't answer that until the numbers tell us what to do. What does the data say?"* If you skip this step, you'll solve the wrong problem beautifully.

### 2. Pull the funnel stage-by-stage
Lay the whole road trip out as numbers, not impressions. Two layers:
- **Conversion at each stage:** spend → leads → booked → showed → offers made → closed → **cash collected upfront** (this last one hides the most leverage). Get the rate *between* each stage, not just totals.
- **Gross-bulk metrics:** total volume at the top vs. the volume the stated Point B actually requires. Run the simple arithmetic: at current conversion, how many leads/day does the target demand, and how many are you feeding? (Justin: dashboard said $927/day; he was running $300.)

### 3. Find the ONE bottleneck — not 76 things
Walk the funnel and find the single stage where throughput or efficiency breaks. The bottleneck is the stage whose rate, if fixed, unlocks the most downstream — not the stage that *feels* worst. Whiting on Rodri: *"vehemently railed him, nicely."* One thing. The discipline is subtraction: list every candidate, then strike everything that isn't the binding constraint. *"If you ask the right questions, you create enormous amounts of leverage. If you ask the wrong questions, you create an enormous amount of work."* Name one constraint and the action that releases it. If you've named three, you haven't found it yet.

### 4. Diagnose the TYPE: volume, or a 747-sized opportunity
The bottleneck is almost always **volume** — conversion is fine, the top of the funnel is "nowhere near what's required." But before you tell anyone to spend more, verify efficiency, because an outside read often finds a **747-sized opportunity** that cuts required volume 5–10×:
- **Price raise** — Rodri: 75% close on $36K, collecting $7,500 upfront. Data said raise prices and collect more, not build the book funnel he wanted. He asked for $50K PIF instead of $7,500; paid in full in two days.
- **Cash-collected, not new traffic** — Harry: 64% close, 80%+ show, perfect fits, but **28% cash collected upfront.** *"You don't have an offer problem. People are buying. Why aren't perfect-fit buyers giving you more money upfront? Start pitching PIFs. That's the only thing you change."* An entire offer rebuild, avoided.
- **Niche-down** — tighter ICP raises every downstream rate, shrinking the volume the target needs.
Decision rule: if conversion is healthy and a price/cash/niche lever exists, pull it FIRST — it makes the volume problem 5–10× smaller before you spend a dollar.

### 5. Separate the math from the fear
Finding the bottleneck is math — you've done it. Releasing it is psychology, and it's almost always a **fear charge sitting on a number.** Justin's bottleneck wasn't strategy; it was that he'd never typed 927 into the ad-budget field. Whiting: *"Cool — go edit that ad budget."* Tripled in 30 days. Name the specific fear out loud (raising the price, spending the spend, telling a buyer to PIF, firing the bad-fit client). Pre-frame it the way he does: *"This will feel like the worst thing you've ever done. On the other side of it, you get what you said you wanted."* The action is small. The charge is the whole game.

### 6. Verify against history before you ship the call
Don't trust the elegant new idea over what the numbers already did. *"We don't make business decisions based on feels. We make business decisions based on what made stats go up."* Cross-check: does the historical data corroborate this bottleneck, or are you reaching for a shiny mechanism? If the diagnosis survives the spine of the math, ship it as ONE instruction. Get the gut to match the data — *"that's where ultimate confidence comes from."*

## Content Type Adaptations

When the read-out becomes market-facing content, apply **Mode B** — keep the confrontational spine, drop the profanity. The wrong-fit reader should still squirm; the right-fit one should feel seen.

| Deliverable | How the diagnosis adapts |
|---|---|
| **Coaching / high-ticket service, no sales calls (Authority Flywheel, $2K–$5K)** | Hero use case. Pull YOUR funnel first; the bottleneck on a no-call offer is almost always cash-collected or price, not traffic — the Harry/Rodri pattern. Before "make more content," check: are perfect-fit buyers self-closing but underpaying? Then the released bottleneck becomes the content (`jw-objection-arsenal`): the belief that "this number isn't a problem, your fear of asking for it is" gets installed at frequency. |
| **LinkedIn-native (× Lara Acosta)** | Turn the data slap into a post: lead with the wrong question the market obsesses over ("everyone's asking for a new hook") and replace it with the one the math actually points to. The 747-opportunity story (price/cash beats volume) is the post; one number is the proof. Polarize — name the feeling-based move you refuse to make. |
| **Newsletter / Substack (× Nicolas Cole)** | The bottleneck becomes the edition's one idea: a worked diagnosis (Harry's 28%, Justin's $927) as the teaching spine, ending on the reader's own confiscate-the-feeling move. The big domino: "you don't have a strategy problem, you have a fear-charge-on-a-number problem." |
| **Paid ads (× Luke Iha)** | Hook on the misdiagnosis ("you don't need a new offer"); body is the real lever with a hard number; CTA assumes the price/cash fix. Awareness-ladder it to operators sophisticated enough to have data and dumb enough to be acting on feelings about it. |
| **Low / no-budget organic** | No paid lever, so volume diagnosis points back to efficiency: price, cash-collected, niche — the levers that cost zero. Diagnosis content carries its own distribution by polarizing harder; the call-out of the comfortable-wrong belief IS the reach mechanism. |

## Output Requirements

Deliver a **One-Bottleneck Read-Out** (one page, no padding):
1. **The wrong question** the operator walked in with (the feeling, named).
2. **The funnel, stage-by-stage** — conversion rates between every stage + gross volume vs. required volume, with the actual numbers.
3. **THE ONE bottleneck** — single binding constraint, stated in one sentence, with the data that proves it's the constraint (not the runner-up).
4. **Type verdict:** volume, OR the specific 747-sized lever (price / cash-collected / niche-down) and the math showing how much it shrinks the volume need.
5. **The ONE action** — the single thing to do this week, phrased as an instruction, not an option.
6. **The fear charge** — the specific psychology blocking that action + the pre-frame that clears it.
7. **Ethics Gate sign-off** if the released bottleneck ships as a market-facing belief.

## Quality Gate

Score against the genius.md **Expert-Specific Quality Rubric**:
- **#1 Reality-grounded?** Built on pulled numbers, zero "I feel like." If a single recommendation rests on a feeling, the deliverable fails outright.
- **#2 Reverse-engineered?** Bottleneck named relative to a stated Point B, worked backward from required volume.
- **#5 Edge intact?** The read-out confronts the comfortably-wrong belief by name. Spine test: the diagnosis still makes the wrong-fit operator uncomfortable. Sanded into "consider exploring your pricing" = dead.
- **#7 Ethics Gate passed?** Any installed belief (e.g. "ask for the PIF") is true, defensible, and in the buyer's interest.
- **#8 One big domino?** The read-out lands ONE belief that makes the other 75 worries irrelevant.

**Failure modes specific to this workflow:** (a) answering the feeling-question on its terms instead of confiscating it; (b) shipping a list of 76 fixes instead of the one binding constraint; (c) defaulting to "spend more / make more content" without verifying efficiency and checking for the price/cash/niche lever first; (d) finding the math but never naming the fear charge, so nothing actually moves.

If any scored dimension <6, re-run the weakest step once and re-score.
