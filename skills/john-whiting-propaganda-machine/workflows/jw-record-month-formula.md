# Record Month Formula — Reverse-Documentation

> "Most coaches have inconsistent profit… because when stats go up, they don't document what caused that stat to go up." — John Whiting

Your best month already taught you the answer. You just didn't write it down, so you can't repeat it. This workflow forces the documentation that turns a lucky month into a printed playbook you run on purpose. Genius pattern #10.

---

## Pre-Flight Gate

Before building, confirm against the genius.md decision framework. This workflow only fires on a *real* peak — not a hope.

1. **Did stats actually go up?** A record month is a measured fact, not a feeling. If there's no dashboard / no before-and-after number, route to `jw-data-bottleneck` first. *"We don't make business decisions based on feels. We make business decisions based on what made stats go up."* No record = nothing to reverse-document.
2. **Is the data captured BEFORE memory rots?** Reverse-documentation is worthless 60 days late. Run this within the same month while the operator still remembers the deviation from baseline. If the month closed weeks ago, do it now anyway and flag the gaps as estimates, not facts.
3. **Run the `jw-ethics-gate` intent check.** The playbook will be handed off and rerun at scale. Every action documented must be one the operator would defend under oath — no documenting a fluke caused by a misleading claim and then systematizing the deceit.

If all three pass, build.

## The Formula (6 steps)

### Step 1 — Document EVERYTHING (no editing, no flattering)
The moment you have a record month, dump every category that touched the result. Do not skip the boring ones; the driver hides in the boring one. Capture, per category:
- **KPIs** — screenshot the dashboard. Actual numbers: leads, calls/replies, close rate, cash collected, AOV, ad spend, ROAS, churn. The numbers anchor everything else.
- **Marketing** — exact ad creative running (hooks, angles, offers), targeting/audiences, organic posting cadence + which platforms, what changed from baseline month.
- **Sales** — both **low-ticket** and **high-ticket**: the process, the scripts, the objection-handlers that worked, who you talked to, what you said when they hesitated.
- **Fulfillment** — what you delivered, the format, what clients actually did, what got results.
- **Operations** — your weekly rhythm, what got systematized, what you stopped doing.
- **Data / Tracking** — what tools fired (dashboard, attribution), what you measured daily.
- **Accounting** — cash in, allocation, what you paid for.
- **PERSONAL** (non-negotiable — Whiting includes this): routine (wake/work/sleep), relationship state, recreation, fitness/diet, education (what you read/learned), mindset. The operator IS the driver (#8: the driver = your mental performance). A record month often traces to "I slept and trained," not a funnel tweak.

### Step 2 — Don't type. Voice-ramble, as if interviewed.
Typing makes people compress and posture before the data is on the table. Speak the answers out loud as if someone is interviewing you, category by category, and transcribe (Descript, phone voice memo, Whisper). You want the unfiltered "and then I also…" — that's where the real cause lives. Quantity over polish here; the LLM cleans it next.

### Step 3 — LLM-compress into a scannable playbook
Paste the full voice transcript into Claude/ChatGPT with this prompt (Whiting's own, lightly tightened):
> "Here are the successful actions from my record month across every category. Create a simple, concise, step-by-step playbook of the actions that made stats go up. Use ~100-character bullets I can scan in 30 seconds and hand to someone else to run. No fluff, no theory, just the do-able actions in order."
Iterate once if it's vague: "Cut anything that isn't a specific action with a number or a name."

### Step 4 — Print it. Keep it on the desk.
The playbook is not a Notion doc you'll never reopen. Print it. It lives on the desk where you see it daily. *"That which is measured improves; that which is measured and reported back improves exponentially."* The physical reminder is the reporting-back loop.

### Step 5 — Isolate the TOP-3 DRIVERS (the 20% → 80%)
Of everything documented, which 3 actions caused most of the upswing? Ask the operator directly and pressure-test against the KPIs — the felt answer and the data answer must match. *"What were the top three key drivers that specifically caused the upswing?"* For each driver, then ask the leverage question: **"How can I strengthen it?"** (spend more on the one ad that worked, double the cadence that landed, deepen the offer the best client bought). This is the strengthen-plan: 3 drivers, 1 strengthening move each.

### Step 6 — Rerun, and audit on the dip
Run the playbook next month on purpose. When stats dip, the diagnostic is one question, not a panic: **"Are we running the playbook?"** Find the deviation — the day you stopped, the ad you turned off, the routine that slipped — and correct it. *"When you just document what works and then run that playbook, you kind of can't screw up."* Nine times out of ten the dip traces to a stopped action you can simply restart.

## Content Type Adaptations

The core formula is operator-internal. These rows govern what gets *published* from the documented win — turning a record month into proof assets. Client-facing rows use Mode B (spine intact); a translated line must still make the wrong-fit reader uncomfortable and the right-fit reader feel seen.

| Deliverable | How the formula adapts |
|---|---|
| **Coaching / high-ticket service (Authority Flywheel — selling $2K–$5K without sales calls)** | Hero use case. The compressed playbook *is* the proof your method is real and repeatable — feed the top-3 drivers into the Propaganda Machine as belief-shift assets ("Here's the exact 3 things that 3x'd my month — and why you won't do them"). The doc closes the no-call sale by showing a system, not a vibe. Mode B spine: "If you want me to motivate you to follow a printed playbook every day, we're not a fit." |
| **LinkedIn (× Lara Acosta)** | Render the top-3 drivers as a 4-3-2-1 carryover post: the record number → the 3 boring actions that caused it → the one most people skip. Documentation-as-content. The "personal" category often produces the most-shared post (the routine, not the funnel). |
| **Newsletter / Substack (× Nicolas Cole)** | The playbook becomes an edition: "I reverse-engineered my best month. Here's the whole thing." The top-3 drivers are the through-line; the strengthen-plan is the cliffhanger for next edition. Show the screenshot. |
| **Paid ads (× Luke Iha)** | The single best-performing ad from the documented month gets *strengthened* (Step 5) and scaled, not replaced. Document the exact hook + targeting that won; rerun it at higher spend before testing anything new. Volume on the proven winner beats novelty. |
| **Low/no-budget organic** | No spend to scale, so the lever is *cadence + the personal/routine driver*. Document the posting rhythm and the one piece that hit; the strengthen-move is "do that, more often, consistently" — the cheapest top-3 driver to amplify. |

## Output Requirements

Deliver a **Record Month Dossier**:
1. **The filled template** — every category from Step 1, populated from the voice transcript (with the dashboard screenshot referenced/attached). Gaps flagged as estimates.
2. **The compressed playbook** — the LLM-generated ~100-char-bullet, scan-in-30-seconds, hand-off-ready action list, in order. Print-formatted (one page if possible).
3. **The top-3 drivers** — named, each tied to its supporting KPI (the data answer, not just the felt answer).
4. **The strengthen-plan** — one specific strengthening move per driver, with the number it should move.
5. **The dip protocol** — the single-question audit ("Are we running the playbook?") + where to look for the deviation.

## Quality Gate

Score against the genius.md rubric. The dimensions that govern this workflow:
- **#1 Reality-grounded?** — Is the dossier built on the dashboard screenshot and actual numbers, or on a flattering memory of the month? If a driver can't be tied to a KPI, it's a feeling. Fails.
- **#2 Reverse-engineered?** — Does it work backward from the recorded result to the causes, or forward from "what I think I did well"?
- **#5 Edge intact?** — On the client-facing rendering, run the spine test. "Here are some helpful tips from a good month!" is sanded mush. Whiting's version names the 3 things the reader won't actually do. Keep it.
- **#6 Leveraged?** — Could someone else *run the business* from the compressed playbook? If it needs the operator to interpret it, it's not compressed enough — back to Step 3.
- **#7 Ethics Gate passed?** — Every documented action defensible under oath; the published numbers real; the screenshot unaltered. Phantom proof violates Gate #1–2 and the Factual Grounding Standard.

**Failure modes specific to this workflow:** (a) documenting *feelings* about the month instead of actions tied to data (#1 violation — the cardinal sin Whiting names); (b) skipping the PERSONAL category because it feels unrelated to revenue (the driver hides there); (c) a playbook too long/abstract to scan or hand off (Step 3 not done); (d) confusing correlation with cause — listing everything that happened instead of isolating the 3 that *drove* it (Step 5 not done). If any rubric dimension scores <6, redo that step once and re-score.
