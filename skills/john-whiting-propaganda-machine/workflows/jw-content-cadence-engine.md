# Content Cadence Engine — The Production System That Feeds the Machine

> "I control every single word that you hear. That's why I cut my own clips and why I don't outsource it." — John Whiting

The asset army (`jw-objection-arsenal`) is what to say. This is how you produce it at volume with a team of you + one VA + AI. Record once, clip into many, schedule, re-clip your best live moments, and render each piece in the format the platform actually rewards. Treat the cadence itself as a Record-Month driver: document what made stats go up, then run it again.

---

## Pre-Flight Gate

Confirm against the genius.md decision framework before building the engine:

1. **Is there a steady source of long-form raw material?** A weekly YouTube, a workshop, a recorded mastermind, recorded sales calls. The engine multiplies a source — it does not create one. No long-form input → no clips. Route to "commit to one weekly long-form recording" first.
2. **Does the operator cut, or at least final-approve, every clip?** Whiting's whole control thesis: *"I cut my own clips and I don't outsource it."* A VA can run Descript and schedule. The VA does NOT decide what gets said or where the cut lands. If the plan is "hand the whole thing to an editor," that's abdication (genius #14) — fix it before building.
3. **Does the asset inventory already exist?** This engine produces and distributes assets; it does not invent the objection map. If there's no objection/belief inventory yet, run `jw-objection-arsenal` first, then come back.
4. **Run `jw-ethics-gate` intent check.** Every clip force-fed at frequency installs a belief. Defensible under oath, real proof, buyer's interest, reversible respect — or it does not ship.

If all four pass, build the engine.

## The Engine (6 stages)

### Stage 1 — Record One Long-Form Piece (the source)
Pick ONE weekly anchor and protect it: a 30–60 min YouTube episode, a live workshop, or a recorded mastermind session. Talk through the objection map from `jw-objection-arsenal` — handle one real belief per recording so the clips have a spine, not filler. Record screen + face. One session a week is the entire input cost. *"If I just keep reminding you how I roll… I use group format — workshop, YouTube, retargeting with reels or carousels — to just reinstill belief systems."*

### Stage 2 — Clip It (1 → ~6) with Descript + an AI cut-finder
Drop the long-form into Descript. Run a clip-finder prompt against the transcript:
> *"Here is a long-form transcript. Find the 6 strongest standalone moments. For each: the exact start/end timestamps, the objection or belief it kills, and a hook headline that names that objection. Prioritize moments that make the wrong-fit viewer uncomfortable and the right-fit viewer feel seen."*
Target ~6 short-form pieces per source. The operator (not the VA) approves each cut — the headline names the objection, the cut lands clean, the spine survives. *"You take your objection handling skill, you move it all the way out front."*

### Stage 3 — Schedule the Cadence (Metricool + VA + Canva)
Lock a default weekly rhythm and let the VA run it on rails:
- **~1 long-form / week** — the source piece (YouTube + repurposed to newsletter, Stage 6).
- **~1 short-form / day** — the clips from Stage 2, drip-scheduled.
- **Occasional carousel** — Canva, for save-driven platforms; the VA builds from a clip's transcript, operator approves the hook slide.
Metricool schedules and reports back. *"That which is measured and reported back improves exponentially."* The VA owns the calendar; the operator owns the words.

### Stage 4 — Re-Clip Your BEST Live Moments (the highest-leverage assets)
Your live recordings are a goldmine the algorithm has never seen. Mine sales calls, masterminds, coaching sessions for the moment you handled an objection or shifted a belief *artfully*, in real time. Clip it. Headline it with the objection. These convert harder than scripted content because they are real. *"If you're smart, you record yourself on video while taking sales calls. As you're handling an objection, you clip that out and you have a recording of you artfully handling it."* Feed these into the MOF belief-shift layer.

### Stage 5 — Render Platform-Native (stack the content experts)
A clip is not finished until it is shaped for the surface it lands on. Do not post the same cut everywhere. Stack:
- **× Lara Acosta** — LinkedIn: rebuild the clip's payoff in the 4-3-2-1 structure, write the scroll-stopping hook line, add a one-line "you're my people / not your guy" qualifier.
- **× Nicolas Cole** — Newsletter/Substack: the long-form source becomes a written edition; the big-domino belief is the through-line; the CTA is the assumptive next step.
- **× Diandra Escobar / Kallaway** — Short-form: format the vertical cut for retention — pattern-interrupt cold open, on-screen objection headline, caption pacing, the psychological loop that earns the rewatch.
Each render keeps the confrontational spine. A polite repurpose has defeated the mechanism (Mode B spine test).

### Stage 6 — Control Every Cut, Forever (the non-negotiable)
The operator cuts (or final-approves) every clip, every render, every headline. The VA executes Descript exports, Metricool scheduling, Canva builds. The AI finds candidate clips and drafts hooks. Neither chooses the words. When the engine produces a record month, run the Record Month Formula on it: document which cadence, which clip types, which platforms drove the upswing → isolate the top-3 drivers → ask *"how can I strengthen it?"* → rerun the playbook. When stats dip, the first question is *"are we running the playbook?"*

The weekly long-form handles one objection to the offer; ~6 clips force-fed to the warm audience install the big domino; re-clipped live coaching moments are the proof layer; the assumptive-close onboarding clip runs to the warmest tier. Cadence IS the sales team. Voice: Mode B — keep the polarizing edge so the $5K-ready buyer self-selects in and the tire-kicker self-selects out. The cadence carries the sale so no live call is needed. |

| **Newsletter / Substack (× Nicolas Cole)** | The long-form source is the edition. One belief per send; big domino as through-line; the back catalog of editions is the searchable asset army. CTA = assumptive next step, never a hard ask. |
| **Paid ads (× Luke Iha)** | Best-performing organic clips graduate to paid retargeting. Luke's vicious hooks + proof ladders on the front 3 seconds; awareness-ladder targeting decides which clip hits which tier. Liquidate ~30% of TOF spend per genius #5. |

Lean harder on polarization for organic reach; lengthen the time-to-close expectation (~67 days is the paid baseline; organic runs longer). ## Output Requirements

Deliver a **Content Cadence Build**:
1. **Record plan** — the chosen weekly long-form source, what gets covered each session (mapped to the objection inventory), recording setup (screen + face).
2. **Clip workflow** — the Descript clip-finder prompt, the ~6-clips-per-source target, the operator-approval checkpoint, the headline-names-the-objection rule.
3. **Schedule** — the Metricool calendar: ~1 long-form/week + ~1 short/day + carousel cadence; who owns what (VA executes, operator approves words); the report-back loop.
4. **Live re-clip plan** — which live recordings to mine (sales calls, masterminds, coaching), how the moments get tagged, where they feed in the funnel.
5. **Platform-native render map** — per platform, the stacked expert + the format spec (LinkedIn 4-3-2-1, newsletter edition, short-form retention structure).
6. **Control + Record-Month loop** — the cut-approval rule stated explicitly, plus the documentation cadence that turns this engine into a strengthenable playbook.
7. **Ethics Gate sign-off** on the asset set the engine will distribute.

## Quality Gate

Score against the genius.md rubric (1–10). Failure modes specific to this engine:
- **Leveraged? (Rubric #6)** — one weekly recording must yield ~6+ assets that keep working without the operator. If the plan is hand-craft-each-piece-from-scratch, it is one-time labor, not an engine. Fail.
- **Edge intact across renders? (Rubric #5)** — run the Mode B spine test on the LinkedIn and newsletter versions. If the repurpose sanded off the confrontation to fit a brand-safe feed, the mechanism is dead. Fail.
- **Objections pre-handled? (Rubric #3)** — every clip kills a real objection from the inventory before any conversation. A cadence of generic value posts that saves nothing for the buyer's actual hesitation is just noise.
- **Reality-grounded? (Rubric #1)** — the schedule and clip-type mix are driven by what the data showed worked (Record Month), not by what feels productive to post.
- **Abdication check (genius #14)** — confirm the operator cuts or final-approves every clip. A VA-decides-the-words plan auto-fails regardless of composite score.
- **Ethics Gate passed? (Rubric #7)** — every clip force-fed at frequency clears all four gates. Any clip that fails: kill it.

If any dimension <6, rebuild that stage once and re-score.
