# Self-Selection Filter — Polarize to Qualify

> "I've clearly defined what are the traits of the people that I want to punch in the face and I make sure that I tell them to go fuck themselves in my content. And what are the criteria of the people that I want to hang out with? I say 'you're my people' in my content and they self-select. It's wonderful." — John Whiting

The cheapest support ticket is the wrong-fit client you never enrolled. This workflow builds the polarization layer that does the qualifying upstream, in the feed, before a dollar changes hands. Genius pattern #3, signature move #2. The mechanism isn't the attitude; it's the qualification the attitude performs.

---

## Pre-Flight Gate

Per genius.md Decision Framework ("I'm attracting the wrong clients / too much fulfillment drag" → this workflow). Confirm before building:

1. **Can you name your wrong-fit by behavior, not vibe?** "Tire-kickers" is not a definition. "Wants weekly hand-holding on tasks they could Google" is. If you can't name the trait, you can't repel it. → Define it first.
2. **Do you have a real perfect-fit to point at?** Per pattern #12 (One Best Client): paid you the most, executes exactly, owns their results, you'd hang out for free. If no real exemplar exists, the "you're my people" line is hollow. → Route to `jw-one-best-client`.
3. **Is fulfillment cost actually the problem, or is it volume?** Per Hidden Knowledge: "volume is almost always the real answer." If margin is fine and the operator just wants fewer clients for ego, this is the wrong tool. → Confirm the support drag is real (refunds, churn, tear-wiping, support headcount).
4. **Ethics Gate intent check.** Disqualification is a claim. "You're not a fit" must be true, not a fake-scarcity stunt. If you'd enroll the person you publicly refused, you're lying for reach. → Kill it.

## The Filter (5 steps)

### 1. Define who you'd punch in the face
List the wrong-fit by observable behavior, not feeling. For each, write the trait AND the downstream fulfillment cost it creates, so you (and the reader) see the math: this client = this drag. Example rows: "needs me to validate every decision → 4 support DMs/day"; "blames the market when they skip the inputs → refund risk + bad testimonial"; "shopping 6 coaches at once → never executes, churns at month 2." This is the upstream cause. Disorganized client = disorganized fulfillment.

### 2. Define your people and tell them so
Mirror step 1 with the perfect-fit, drawn from your real best client (Pre-Flight #2). Observable behaviors again: "disappears for a month, executes, comes back with a record month"; "owns the result, good or bad." Then say it to their face in content: **"you're my people."** The right-fit reader should feel recognized, almost called out by name. If the line could describe anyone, it magnetizes no one.

### 3. Build disqualification flexes
Turn a real public refusal into a content asset. Whiting's move: "you couldn't pay me $15K to spend an hour with you." The flex does three jobs at once — raises status (you turn money away), repels the wrong-fit (they see themselves in the rejected profile), magnetizes the right-fit (they want what you protect). Sourcing: real fired clients, real declined applications, real "I told them no." Per the Ethics Gate, the refusal must have actually happened or be one you'd actually make. Format candidates: "Here's why I fired a client who asked for X" / "The application I rejected this week and why."

### 4. Draw the lines
State the rules of the game in the open. The structure: **here is how I roll → if you want that, here is exactly what happens → if not, here is the door.** This is pre-framing (Core Philosophy #5: what happens before a thing dictates how it goes). Make the bad-fit's exit clean and the right-fit's entry obvious. Whiting: "I don't work that way. You should — is that working for you? I'm not your guy. Don't apply. Next." No apology in the line. The non-apology is what makes the boundary credible.

### 5. Let the polarization BE the qualifier
The output isn't decoration with attitude — it's a filter that pre-solves support cost by making consumption do the qualifying. Wire it so consumption does the qualifying: by the time a right-fit reaches your DMs, they've already agreed to your rules and self-identified as your people; the wrong-fit removed themselves. **Do not sand the edge.** Per the Voice Reference: a polite, hedged version ("we find clients do best when they're a little independent!") has defeated the mechanism. Sanded-down polarization does not filter — it just produces forgettable content that converts everyone equally, which is the failure.

## Output Requirements

Deliver a **Self-Selection Content Set**:
1. **Wrong-fit profile** — 3–5 named behavioral traits, each paired with its fulfillment-cost consequence.
2. **Right-fit profile** — 3–5 named behaviors, drawn from the real best client.
3. **Repel lines** — 5–8 ship-ready lines that tell the wrong-fit to leave (Mode A raw or Mode B translated to the client brand; label which).
4. **Magnetize lines** — 5–8 ship-ready "you're my people" lines.
5. **Disqualification flexes** — 2–3 real-refusal content concepts (headline + the true story behind each).
6. **Drawn-lines asset** — one "here's how I roll / here's what happens / here's the door" piece.
7. For any line shipping under a client brand: the **spine-test verdict** (does it still make the wrong-fit uncomfortable and the right-fit feel seen?). Failed lines do not ship.
8. **Ethics Gate sign-off.**

## Quality Gate

Score against the genius.md rubric. Load-bearing dimensions for this workflow:
- **#4 Self-selecting?** This is the whole job. If a line tries to please everyone, it has converted no one. Name the line that repels and the line that magnetizes, or the score drops.
- **#5 Edge intact?** Run the spine test on every repel line and disqualification flex. A line that makes nobody uncomfortable is decoration, not a filter. Sanded-down = automatic fail on this dimension.
- **#1 Reality-grounded?** Wrong-fit and right-fit profiles are observable behaviors tied to real cost, not vibes.
- **#7 Ethics Gate passed?** Every disqualification flex is a true refusal; every "not a fit" claim is one you'd defend under oath. A fake-rejection stunt for reach fails this gate and gets killed.

Failure modes specific to this workflow:
- **Polite polarization** — the line reads "edgy" but offends no one. Defeats the filter. (Fails #5.)
- **Vibe-based targeting** — "I want serious people" with no observable behavior. Repels nothing. (Fails #1/#4.)
- **Fake disqualification** — manufactured "I turned down $50K" with no real story. (Fails #7, kill.)
- **Status flex with no filter** — refusal that flatters the operator but doesn't help the right-fit self-identify.

If any load-bearing dimension scores <6, rewrite that element once and re-score. The spine test is non-negotiable: a translated line that lost its edge has lost its function.
