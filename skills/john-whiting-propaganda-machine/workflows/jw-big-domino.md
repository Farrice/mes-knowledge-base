# The Big Domino — Install the One Belief That Kills Every Objection

> "If I just keep reminding you how I roll… I use group format — workshop, YouTube, retargeting with reels or carousels — to just reinstill belief systems." — John Whiting

One belief, installed deep enough, makes objections evaporate before the prospect can voice them. Whiting's domino is "you need all 7 components to get from A to B." Once a prospect buys *that*, "I don't have time / it's too expensive / I'll do it myself" all become irrelevant — they're missing components, and the only place to get them is you. This workflow finds your domino, states it in one sentence, and engineers the repetition that turns it into a foregone conclusion.

---

## Pre-Flight Gate

Per the genius.md Decision Framework — this fires when "my offer / pitch isn't converting." Confirm before building:

1. **Is there ONE belief that, if true for the prospect, collapses every objection at once?** If you can only name a list of selling points, you don't have a domino yet — you have features. Keep digging until you find the single load-bearing belief the whole sale rests on.
2. **Can you defend that belief under oath?** (Ethics Gate #1.) The domino is the most-repeated claim in your entire machine. If it's even slightly inflated, you're force-feeding a lie at frequency. Kill it.
3. **Does installing it move the BUYER toward something genuinely good for them, not just toward your invoice?** (Ethics Gate #3.) Whiting's defense of his own propaganda: "when I do it, their business doubles." If your domino only profits you, stop.

If all three pass, build.

## The Methodology (5 steps)

### 1. Find the domino — isolate the ONE load-bearing belief
List every objection the prospect throws ("too expensive," "no time," "I'll figure it out myself," "I tried something like this," "now's not the right time"). Then ask the inversion: *what would have to be true in their head for every single one of those to stop mattering?* That answer is the domino. Whiting's domino is: you need all 7 components or the trip crashes. If that's true, "too expensive" is irrelevant (you can't afford to keep crashing), "I'll do it myself" is irrelevant (you've been missing components for years), "no time" is irrelevant (you're driving the wrong direction fast). The domino is upstream of the objections, not a rebuttal to them.

### 2. Write the domino statement — one sentence, no escape hatch
State it as a single declarative the prospect either accepts or reveals themselves as a wrong-fit by rejecting. Format: *"[If you want B], you need [the thing only I credibly provide] — and without it [the specific failure continues]."* Example domino built from Whiting's 7-component map: *"Every business is a road trip with 7 components; the average operator runs at 54% — and you'd never put your family in a car driven at 54%."* (Constructed illustration, not a Whiting quote.) A benefit makes your offer attractive; a domino makes the prospect's current situation untenable and your offer the only exit. Spine test (Mode B): the statement should make the wrong-fit reader defensive and the right-fit reader feel caught.

### 3. Build the install sequence — dissolve, don't argue (× McRaney)
You do not install the domino by asserting it louder. You surface the belief they currently hold, present a contrasting frame, and let them arrive at the domino themselves (McRaney backward-dissolution). Sequence per asset:
- **Surface the current belief** out loud, generously, so they feel seen ("You think the problem is traffic. Most people do.").
- **Contrast it with the frame** that exposes the gap ("Your 64% close rate says you don't have a traffic problem. You have a 28%-cash-collected problem.").
- **Let them arrive** at the domino as their own conclusion (× Fladlien indirect close — never "so buy my thing"; instead "huh, I'd been measuring the wrong number this whole time"). The strongest install is the one where the prospect thinks they figured it out.

### 4. Re-anchor in EVERY piece of content — repetition is the certainty engine
The domino is not stated once. It is the open or the through-line of every asset in the machine (Signature Move #6). Practically:
- **Open or close each piece by re-installing the frame** ("Remember: it's a road trip, and you're at 54%.") so the belief compounds with every view.
- **Vary the surface, never the spine.** Same domino, fifty angles — a testimonial proves it, a teardown demonstrates it, a rant defends it, an objection-clip kills a competing belief that threatens it. "Number of times over = certainty."
- **Map your existing assets to the domino.** Any piece that does NOT re-anchor or reinforce it is dead weight — either retrofit it or cut it.

### 5. Reconfirm post-purchase — retention is reselling the domino
The sale doesn't end the install; it raises the stakes. Every piece a *client* sees either reconfirms "I made the right call" or seeds doubt (Hidden Knowledge: "Retention = continuously reselling the big idea"). Run the domino in onboarding, in the community, in client-only content. Whiting: "we're shifting beliefs and then reconfirming beliefs constantly with people that you've already sold, increasing retention." A client who still believes the domino renews, refers, and executes. A client who quietly stopped believing it churns and blames you.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Deliverable | How the domino deploys |
|---|---|
| **Coaching / high-ticket service, no sales calls ($2K–$5K Authority Flywheel)** | Hero use case. The domino IS the offer's reason-to-exist. Pick the single belief that makes "I'll just figure it out / coach is too expensive / no time" all collapse (e.g. "You don't have a tactics problem, you have a [missing-component] problem, and you've been lying to yourself about which one"). Install it across every post + email + lead asset so the warm reader self-closes into a low-friction enrollment, no call required. The spine is what does the qualifying the call used to do. |
| **LinkedIn (× Lara Acosta)** | Domino becomes the recurring "core belief" thesis under varied hooks. Re-anchor in the opening line; 4-3-2-1 body carries one contrasting frame per post; comments/DMs let them arrive (× McRaney). The feed IS the frequency. |
| **Newsletter / Substack (× Nicolas Cole)** | Domino is the publication's through-line — every edition is the same belief from a new entry point. Cole's "the book never ends": each issue re-installs and deepens the one idea so a binge-reader arrives pre-sold. |
| **Paid ads (× Luke Iha)** | Domino headlines the campaign; MOF retargeting hammers it at frequency cap (~2×/7 days). Vicious hook surfaces the competing belief, body dissolves it, the domino is the inevitable conclusion. Frequency = certainty, literally bought. |
| **Low / no ad budget (organic)** | No paid frequency, so the domino must be tighter and the cadence relentless. One belief, posted into the ground from every angle until the audience can recite it. Lengthen time-to-close expectation; lean harder on polarization for reach so the right-fits keep arriving. |

## Output Requirements

Deliver a **Big Domino Install Kit**:
1. **The domino statement** — one sentence, no escape hatch, spine-tested (Mode A raw + Mode B translated version if shipping to a client brand).
2. **The objection-collapse map** — the top 5–7 objections listed, each with one line on *why the domino makes it irrelevant* (proof the belief is actually upstream, not a feature).
3. **The install sequence** — the surface → contrast → arrive structure (× McRaney), with the specific current-belief you're dissolving named.
4. **The re-anchor plan** — where the domino opens/closes each asset type, plus a list of existing assets to retrofit or cut.
5. **The post-purchase reconfirmation plan** — where the domino runs to clients (onboarding, community, client content) to drive retention.
6. **Ethics Gate sign-off** on the domino specifically (it's the most-repeated claim in the machine — it gets the strictest check).

## Quality Gate

Score against the genius.md rubric. This workflow lives or dies on:
- **Rubric #8 — One big domino?** The deliverable installs/reinforces the ONE belief that makes objections irrelevant. If you produced a list of selling points instead of a single load-bearing belief, you failed the only dimension that matters here. Name the exact sentence that earns the score.
- **Rubric #5 — Edge intact?** Run the spine test on the domino statement. A domino that's been sanded into "we help people get better results!" installs nothing and qualifies no one. The wrong-fit reader must feel the discomfort. (Mode B failure = dead asset, don't ship.)
- **Rubric #3 — Objection pre-handled?** The objections die because the domino is true upstream of them, in the content — not because a human saves the sale live.
- **Rubric #7 — Ethics Gate passed?** The domino is force-fed at frequency; if it's even slightly false, you're brainwashing toward harm. All four gates or kill it.

**Failure modes specific to this workflow:** (a) a domino that's actually a benefit statement, not a belief ("you'll grow faster"); (b) too many dominoes — if you can't pick ONE, you haven't found it; (c) re-anchoring that varies the spine instead of the surface, so the belief never compounds; (d) installing by asserting louder instead of dissolving the competing belief (× McRaney), which triggers resistance instead of arrival.

If Rubric #8 or #5 scores <6, the domino isn't real yet — re-run steps 1–2 and re-score.
