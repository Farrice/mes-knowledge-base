# The Map — Point A to Point B, Reverse-Engineered

> "The real gap is between where somebody actually is and where they genuinely think they are… If you think you're in Texas yet you're in Oregon" — you'll never arrive. — John Whiting

The diagnostic underneath every other Whiting system. You cannot navigate from a location you're lying about. This workflow forces the operator to face their real coordinates, plot a Point B with real numbers, rate the seven components that keep the vehicle moving, and reverse-engineer the track. Output is a Map Diagnostic the operator can run a business from.

---

## Pre-Flight Gate

Confirm against the genius.md decision framework before running:

1. **Will the operator face real numbers, or are we doing a vision-board session?** The map runs on data, not feelings. If they can't produce actual monthly income, margin, close rate, and ad spend, the first deliverable is *go get the numbers* — there is no map without coordinates. Whiting: *"What does the data say to do today? That is the number one question."*
2. **Is there a real Point B candidate, or just "more"?** "100k/month" is not a destination, it's a vibe. We need a freedom number with real coordinates (Pattern #11). If the operator only has a revenue figure, route through the Destination step before anything else.
3. **Run the `jw-ethics-gate` intent check.** The reality slap we're about to deliver is in service of the operator's actual outcome, not to look smart or sell a bigger package. If we're inflating the gap to upsell, kill it.

If all three pass, run the map.

## The Map (5 steps)

### Step 1 — Locate where the operator ACTUALLY is (face reality)
Do not accept the self-reported location. The #1 human fear is being wrong, so the operator will round up, average over good months, and quote topline instead of profit. Pull the real coordinates cold: trailing-3-month profit, real close rate, real show rate, real cash-collected-upfront %, real ad spend, real margin, real hours worked.
- Pre-frame the pain (Signature Move #3): *"This is the worst part. We're about to find out you've been lying to yourself about where you are. Survive it and the rest is downhill."* The pre-frame inoculates the sting and makes survival a win.
- Name the Texas-vs-Oregon gap out loud when it appears: the distance that matters is not A→B, it's the distance between where they are and where they *thought* they were.
- Watch for smart-people justifications — the more articulate the excuse, the higher-quality the delusion. Redirect every "yeah but my situation is different" to "what does the number say?"

### Step 2 — Define a crystal-clear Point B with real coordinates
Refuse "100k/month." Drive to the **Freedom Number** (Pattern #11): how the operator wants to live → what that life costs monthly → the portfolio that throws off that monthly spend at a 4% safe withdrawal. That's the real destination; revenue is just the vehicle's speed, not the place you're going.
- Translate the Freedom Number back into business coordinates: required monthly profit → required monthly revenue at current margin → required units/clients → required leads at current close rate. Now Point B has GPS coordinates, not a feeling.
- If the operator can only state a revenue goal, that's a tell they're navigating to someone else's destination. Whiting's framing: the bar is hilariously low — clarity plus writing it down, which 99.9% never do. That gap is the entire opportunity.

### Step 3 — Rate each of the 7 components 1-5 (the 54%-chauffeur diagnostic)
Every component must be dialed or the vehicle crashes. Rate each 1 (broken) to 5 (dialed), then sum and convert to a percentage.

| # | Component | What it governs | A 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Driver** | Mental performance — the operator's clarity, fear-charge, decision quality | Acts on data, not feels; gut already matches the GPS |
| 2 | **Destination** | The freedom number — a real Point B, not "more" | Written, costed, back-calculated to KPIs |
| 3 | **Vehicle** | The business model — does it fit how they want to live? | One offer, one client, engineered not copied (#13) |
| 4 | **GPS** | Tracking — do they know their real numbers daily? | KPIs measured AND reported back; no flying blind |
| 5 | **Speed** | Monthly income — the actual rate of travel | Predictable, documented, repeatable (see `jw-record-month-formula`) |
| 6 | **Fuel Efficiency** | Margin + effort — profit per unit of life spent | High profit on lean topline; not "a million with a team of 50" |
| 7 | **AAA / Lifeline** | The advisor who knows their WHOLE map | One person who sees all 7 components, not 6 disconnected coaches |

Run the diagnostic verbatim: *"If a chauffeur rated himself 54% on this checklist, would you let your family go on that trip with him?"* 54% is the industry average. Any single component at 1-2 is a crash risk regardless of the others — a Ferrari with no GPS still ends up in Oregon.

### Step 4 — Reverse-engineer the track from B back to A
Start at Point B and work *backward*, not forward from A (forward = vague, hope-driven; backward = a route). From the Step-2 coordinates, lay down the track in reverse: the monthly profit → the weekly KPIs → the daily inputs that produce them. Then identify the **single weakest component from Step 3** that is throttling the whole trip — releasing a bottleneck is psychology, but *finding* it is math. Volume is usually the real answer, but verify efficiency first: an outside perspective often finds a "747-sized opportunity" (a price raise, a niche-down) that cuts required volume 5-10x. The deliverable is a route with daily inputs, not a goal.

### Step 5 — Identify the two ways off-track
Once the track is set, the only two failure modes are internal states, and both are detectable in advance:
- **Mentally drunk (upset):** the operator is emotionally charged — angry, anxious, defensive — and starts making decisions from feeling instead of the GPS. Tell: any sentence that starts "I feel like we should…" without "the data says…". Correction: *"Don't argue with the GPS. Get the gut to match the data."*
- **Mentally coked up (shiny object):** the operator abandons the track to chase a new mechanism — the book funnel, the 2026 funnel, the new platform — because it's exciting, not because the numbers asked for it. Tell: a new tactic that doesn't trace to a Step-4 bottleneck. Correction: see Rodri — *"Your numbers say raise prices. Don't build a fucking book funnel."*
Bake the two tells into the deliverable as a standing self-check the operator runs whenever they want to deviate from the track.

## Content Type Adaptations

When the map ships into a brand's content (not just the operator's private diagnostic), apply **Mode B**: keep the confrontational spine, swap the vocabulary, run the spine test on every line. The wrong-fit reader should feel uncomfortable; the right-fit reader should feel seen.

| Deliverable | How the map adapts |
|---|---|
| **Coaching / high-ticket service sale (Authority Flywheel, $2K-$5K, no calls)** | The map IS the indirect sell. Publish a "rate your business 1-5 on these 7" self-diagnostic; the right-fit prospect scores ~54%, feels the Texas-vs-Oregon gap, and self-selects in. The cold-shower reality (Signature #1) does the qualifying so no sales call is needed — they arrive already-sold, the way `jw-propaganda-machine` intends. Mode B example: *"If you can't tell me your real margin right now, that's the problem — and it's the only problem we'd work on first."* (Spine intact, profanity gone.) |
| **LinkedIn-native (× Lara Acosta)** | One component per post in 4-3-2-1 format; the "rate yourself 54%" line is the hook; carousel renders the 7-component table. The reverse-engineering becomes the CTA: "DM me your number." |
| **Newsletter / Substack (× Nicolas Cole)** | A 7-part series, one component per edition; the Texas-vs-Oregon gap is the through-line big-domino belief; each edition reverse-engineers one piece of the reader's track. |
| **Paid ads (× Luke Iha)** | The 54%-chauffeur diagnostic is the vicious hook; the freedom-number reframe is the mechanism; ad targets the awareness-ladder rung where the operator already suspects they're lost. |
| **Low / no-budget organic** | Run the map live as content — record yourself diagnosing a real operator (with consent), publish the reality slap; polarization carries reach with zero spend. Lengthen the expected time-to-trust. |

## Output Requirements

Deliver a **Map Diagnostic**:
1. **Point A — real coordinates.** Trailing-3-month profit, close rate, show rate, cash-collected-upfront %, ad spend, margin, hours. The Texas-vs-Oregon gap named explicitly (where they thought they were vs. where the data puts them).
2. **Point B — the Freedom Number.** Monthly lifestyle cost → portfolio target → back-calculated business coordinates (profit → revenue → clients → leads).
3. **The 7-component scorecard.** Each rated 1-5, summed to a %, with the 54%-chauffeur framing and every component at 1-2 flagged as a crash risk.
4. **The reverse-engineered track.** B → daily inputs, with the single throttling bottleneck named and the efficiency-vs-volume call made.
5. **The two-tells self-check.** The "mentally drunk" and "mentally coked up" detectors written as a standing rule the operator runs before any deviation.
6. **Ethics Gate sign-off** on the whole diagnostic.

## Quality Gate

Score against the genius.md rubric. Specifically:
- **Reality-grounded? (Rubric #1)** — built on actual pulled numbers, not the operator's self-report. If Point A is a feeling, the map is fiction.
- **Reverse-engineered? (Rubric #2)** — the track starts at B and works backward to daily inputs. Forward-from-A vagueness = fail.
- **Self-selecting? (Rubric #4)** — when shipped as content, the 54% diagnostic repels the comfortable and magnetizes the lost.
- **Edge intact? (Rubric #5)** — run the spine test on every Mode B line. A map that lets the operator keep believing they're in Texas has been sanded into mush.
- **Ethics Gate passed? (Rubric #7)** — the gap is real and named in the operator's interest, not inflated to upsell.

**Failure modes specific to this workflow:** accepting the self-reported location (the whole point is they're lying about it); a Point B that's a revenue number instead of a Freedom Number; rating all 7 components a polite 4 to avoid the sting; producing a goal instead of a track of daily inputs. If any scored dimension <6, redo that step once and re-score.
