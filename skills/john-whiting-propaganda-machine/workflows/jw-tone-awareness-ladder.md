# Tone & Awareness Ladder — Walk Them to the Buying Line

> "The buying line — we want them aggressively antagonistic towards the problem… demanding improvement. 'I don't care what it takes, I need to fix this.'" — John Whiting

Genius pattern #9. Prospects do not arrive ready to buy. They arrive low on the tone scale — fear, anxiety, covert hostility — and you cannot close someone from the bottom of the ladder. The sale is the climb. This workflow maps the rung they're on right now and writes the content/copy that raises them one rung at a time until they hit the buying line, where the only move left is to demand the fix.

---

## Pre-Flight Gate

Confirm against the genius.md Decision Framework ("My offer / pitch isn't converting" → jw-tone-awareness-ladder + jw-big-domino):

1. **Is the offer real and the Point B verifiable?** You cannot raise someone toward a buying line for a result that doesn't exist. If the outcome is vague or unproven → route to `jw-one-best-client` + `jw-the-map` first. You climb a real ladder, not a painted one.
2. **Do you know who you're aiming the anger AT?** The problem must be nameable and external to the prospect's identity. If the only "enemy" is the prospect's own competence, you're not climbing — you're shaming, and shame drops them DOWN the scale. Name the problem before you write a line.
3. **Run the `jw-ethics-gate` intent check.** Pattern #9 pushes "awareness buttons" in someone's mind. That's the mechanism for healing OR harm (the Ethics Gate tell). The belief you're raising them toward must be true, proven, and genuinely good for them — or kill it.
4. **Have you had real conversations with this avatar?** You diagnose tone from how people actually talk, not from a persona doc. If zero reps with this avatar → gather 10+ raw quotes (DMs, call recordings, comments) before diagnosing.

If all four pass, climb.

## The Climb (5 steps)

### Step 1 — Diagnose the current rung (tone + awareness)
Read the prospect's actual language and place them on BOTH scales, which run in parallel:

- **Emotional tone scale** (top → bottom): create / cause / light → interest → boredom → antagonism → anger → covert hostility → fear → grief → apathy.
- **Awareness ladder** (Schwartz-adjacent, Whiting-flavored): unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → mechanism-aware → you-aware → ready.

Most prospects who reach you are clustered **mid-to-low**: fear, anxiety, covert hostility ("yeah, I've tried things like this"). That covert hostility is the tell — they're not attacking you, they're protecting a wound from being wrong (the #1 human fear). Output: a one-line diagnosis — *"This prospect sits at covert-hostility / solution-aware: they believe the category works but secretly suspect THEY are the reason it won't work for them."*

### Step 2 — Write the rung-by-rung ascent (one rung per piece)
You do not jump them from apathy to buying. You move them ONE rung at a time. Map a content/copy sequence where each piece is built to raise the SPECIFIC rung you diagnosed:

- **From apathy/grief →** name the problem they've given up on as solvable. Not hype. Evidence. "You stopped trying because the thing you tried was wrong, not because you're broken."
- **From fear →** show someone exactly like them who crossed. Borrowed proof lowers the threat charge on the number/decision (see Justin's $927 — the fear was on the number, not the strategy).
- **From covert hostility →** call it out and rejoin (Step 3). Validate the suspicion, redirect it.
- **From antagonism → the buying line:** aim the heat at the problem. "You should be furious. Not at yourself — at the [thing] that's been quietly costing you [Point-B gap] every single month."

The buying line = **demand for improvement.** The piece succeeds when the reader's internal voice goes "I don't care what it takes, I need to fix this." Write toward that sentence, not toward "buy now."

### Step 3 — Aim the anger at the problem, not at you
This is the rung most operators botch. Pattern #9's hinge: *if you do this right they get worked up at the problem; if you do it wrong they get worked up at you.* The energy you're raising is volatile. When it turns on you (defensive comment, "who is this guy," DM hostility), do NOT defend and do NOT pander. **Rejoin:** "We both want the same thing here. Let's aim this at the situation, not at each other." Then point the recovered energy back at the external problem. The rejoin is itself a pattern interrupt (#4) — they expected you to fight back or fold; you did neither, which raises trust and pulls them up a rung.

### Step 4 — Remember you're talking to their MIND, not them
You are not writing to a person. You are writing to the *mind* of a person at a known rung. Scripts work because they push specific awareness buttons in a known sequence — not because they're clever. Before each piece, name the exact button: *"This piece pushes the 'I'm-the-exception' button that smart prospects use to stay stuck"* (smart people generate higher-quality delusion — see Hidden Knowledge). Match the language to how that mind actually defends itself, then dismantle that defense from the inside. One button per piece.

### Step 5 — Pre-frame inoculation before the climb's hardest rung
The jump toward the buying line is the painful one — it requires admitting where they actually are (Oregon, not Texas). Inoculate it up front. Deploy the pre-frame: **"This is going to be the worst thing you've ever done… and on the other side of it, you get rich."** (Mode B brands: keep the inoculation spine, swap vocabulary — *"The next part is uncomfortable on purpose. Most people quit here. The ones who don't are the ones I work with."*) Pre-framing the pain as the price means that when the pain arrives, surviving it feels like proof and a win — not a reason to bail. What happens before the hard rung dictates whether they climb it.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Deliverable | How the ladder adapts |
|---|---|
| **Coaching / high-ticket service, no sales call (Authority Flywheel, $2K–$5K)** | Hero use case. The ladder IS the no-call close: a retargeted sequence where each asset raises one rung, the pre-frame inoculation runs before the price reveal, and the buying line ("I need to fix this") lands them in the DM/checkout already-sold. First real conversation happens AFTER payment. Spine non-negotiable — soft copy at $2K–$5K converts no one. |
| **LinkedIn-native (× Lara Acosta)** | One rung per post. Hook names the rung's emotional state in the prospect's own words; body raises one rung; the climb plays out across a posting cadence, not paid retargeting. The "rejoin" move lives in the comments — handle defensive replies by redirecting at the problem in public. |
| **Newsletter / Substack (× Nicolas Cole)** | The ladder is the editorial arc. Each edition raises one rung; the big-domino belief is the through-line that compounds across issues. End each edition at the next rung's threshold so the open loop pulls them to the next send. CTA = the assumptive next step, not a question. |
| **Paid ads (× Luke Iha)** | Build the sequence as an awareness-ladder retargeting stack: cold creative for problem-aware, warm retargeting for solution/mechanism-aware, hottest pool gets the buying-line + inoculation pieces. Each ad gets a vicious hook calibrated to its rung's emotional tone. Aim heat at the problem in the copy; the comment-section rejoin protects ad sentiment. |
| **Low / no ad budget (organic)** | Run the full climb organically; accept a longer time-to-close. Lean harder on polarization (#3) so each rung-raising piece earns reach. The pre-frame inoculation does double duty as a filter — wrong-fits self-select out at the hard rung, so the warm audience that remains is pre-qualified. |

## Output Requirements

Deliver a **Tone & Awareness Ladder Brief**:
1. **Tone + awareness diagnosis** — the one-line placement (which tone rung × which awareness rung), grounded in 3+ verbatim prospect quotes that prove the placement.
2. **The named problem** — the external enemy the anger will aim at (and the explicit confirmation it is NOT the prospect's identity).
3. **The rung-by-rung message ladder** — each piece mapped to (a) the rung it raises, (b) the one mind-button it pushes, (c) the headline/hook, (d) the line that does the lifting. One rung per piece, in sequence, ending at the buying line.
4. **The buying-line target sentence** — the exact internal sentence the final piece is engineered to trigger ("I don't care what it takes, I need to fix this").
5. **The rejoin script** — the verbatim move for when anger turns on you (Mode A and Mode B versions).
6. **The pre-frame inoculation** — placed before the hardest rung, in the right voice mode for the destination.
7. **Ethics Gate sign-off** on the full ladder.

## Quality Gate

Score against the genius.md **Expert-Specific Quality Rubric** (1–10). This workflow lives or dies on:

- **#5 Edge intact?** Run the spine test on every rung-raising line. If a Mode B line no longer makes the wrong-fit reader uncomfortable and the right-fit reader feel seen, it's sanded mush — rewrite it. Comfortable copy cannot raise tone.
- **#1 Reality-grounded?** The diagnosis must rest on actual prospect language and a real Point B, not a vibe. Name the quotes.
- **#3 Objection pre-handled?** Each rung kills the objection that keeps them stuck on it — the climb pre-handles, it doesn't save live.
- **#8 One big domino?** The whole ladder should compound toward the single belief that makes every remaining objection irrelevant.
- **#7 Ethics Gate passed?** All four gates. Pushing awareness buttons toward a false or self-serving belief is the exact "destroy" half of the tell. Kill it.

**Failure modes specific to this workflow:**
- **Anger aimed at the prospect** (shame, not antagonism-at-problem) → drops them down the scale. Fails #5 and the Ethics Gate.
- **Rung-skipping** — trying to jump apathy straight to buying line → the reader disengages; you lost the climb.
- **No rejoin plan** — you raised volatile energy and have nothing scripted for when it turns on you. Build the rejoin or don't raise the heat.
- **Writing to the person, not the mind** — generic copy that names no specific button. If you can't name the button a piece pushes, you haven't built the rung.

If any rubric dimension scores <6, rebuild that rung once and re-score. If you score ≥8 on any dimension, name the exact line that earns it — can't name it, lower the score.
