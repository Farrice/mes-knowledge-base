# Vehicle Engineer — Design the Model, Don't Copy It

> "This is how you stop copy-and-pasting other people's models and start engineering, because you understand the frameworks… mechanisms change every two seconds, but if you understand the fundamentals you can design your business vehicle." — John Whiting

Most operators inherit a funnel off a launch they watched and wonder why it grinds them into the ground. The funnel was engineered for a different destination and a different driver than yours. This workflow builds the vehicle from the destination backward, so the math, the lifestyle, and the leverage all close at the same time.

---

## Pre-Flight Gate

Confirm against the genius.md decision framework before building:

1. **Is there a destination on the table — a real Freedom Number and a stated way you want to live?** Engineering a vehicle without a destination is just shopping for cars you saw other people drive. If no destination: route to `jw-freedom-number` / `jw-wealthy-coach` first, then return.
2. **Are you copying a model right now because it worked for someone else, or because the math closes for you?** If the honest answer is "it worked for them," that's the diagnosis. Proceed.
3. **Do you have enough reps to engineer leverage honestly?** You can design the vehicle at any stage, but you can only *delegate* the parts you've personally dialed (Pattern #14). If you haven't run the function yet, the output marks it "master first," not "hire for it."
4. **Run `jw-ethics-gate` intent check.** The destination you're engineering toward has to be one you'd defend out loud — not a topline vanity number you secretly hate the life behind.

If 1, 2, and 4 pass, build. Note the answer to 3 — it governs the delegate-vs-abdicate plan.

## The Build (5 stages)

### Stage 1 — Start From the Destination (size the vehicle to the trip)
Pull the destination from `jw-freedom-number`: the monthly spend, the Freedom Number, and the *way you want to live* (hours, team size, travel, deep-work blocks, what you refuse to do). The vehicle has to be able to make that exact trip. You cannot cross the Sahara in a Lamborghini, and you cannot fund a $40K/mo lifestyle on a $200 product without a volume machine you'd hate operating. Write the destination as a one-paragraph spec the rest of the build must satisfy.

### Stage 2 — Solve the Model Variables (price × audience × skill must match)
Three variables have to multiply into the destination, or the model is fiction:
- **Price point × target audience × skill-set.** Run the golfer math both ways. A $1 ebook to hit $2M needs 2,000,000 sales. One $1M client needs one sale — but the skill-stack, proof, and access to that buyer is a different sport. The viable model lives in the middle: find the price × audience where required *volume* is something your current (or near-term) skill can actually produce.
- Compute required volume at 3–4 candidate price points. For each: units/month, leads needed at your real conversion rate, ad spend or content output that implies. Kill any row where the volume requires a skill you don't have and won't build in time.
- Bring in **April Dunford** here: the price you can hold is downstream of positioning. A clearer "obvious choice for [this buyer]" raises the ceiling on price and cuts required volume. The "747-sized opportunity" (genius.md Hidden Knowledge) is usually a price raise or a niche-down hiding in this table.
- Output: the locked model — one price, one audience, the skill-set it demands, and the required volume at that point.

### Stage 3 — Engineer for Leverage (4 levers, not headcount)
Headcount is the most expensive, lowest-leverage answer. Pull these instead, in order:
- **Lean team.** Default to you + a VA + AI (the ~$300K/mo, 2-person reference). Add a role only when the data — not the overwhelm — says the function is the bottleneck. (Dan Martell: every hire should buy back time you've already mapped, not time you're avoiding.)
- **Client quality engineered so fulfillment runs low.** Fulfillment cost is downstream of client quality (Pattern #3). Engineer the model to attract right-fit buyers who execute, so delivery is light by design — not staffed heavy to compensate for bad-fit drag.
- **AI + automation** for the repeatable functions you've already dialed.
- **Content as the sales team.** This is the highest-leverage lever for lean/solo operators — the propaganda machine *is* the sales force. (Dan Koe: content is the asset that sells while you sleep; build the model so distribution doesn't require a headcount line.)
- Output: a ranked leverage stack — which lever carries which function, what stays human.

### Stage 4 — Master, Then Delegate via SOP (never abdicate)
For every function in the model, mark its state: **you-run-it-now**, **dialed-ready-to-hand-off**, or **delegated-via-SOP**. The rule (Pattern #14): figure out every part yourself until it's dialed, *then* hand off an SOP that "anyone could run." Delegating before mastery is abdication — you overpay someone to figure out what you never figured out, they don't respect you because you never proved you could, and a 20% closer becomes a 10% closer who also takes commission. A-players sniff a 54% checklist. Output: the function-by-function delegate-vs-abdicate plan, with the SOP-readiness gate on each handoff.

### Stage 5 — Build on Fundamentals, Not Mechanisms
Mechanisms change every two seconds; fundamentals don't. Document the vehicle at the fundamental layer (destination, model math, leverage architecture, positioning) so you can swap the *mechanism* — the specific funnel, platform, ad type, the "2026 funnel" the market is attracted to this quarter — without rebuilding the business. Whiting markets the mechanism because that's what buyers want; he runs on the fundamentals. Output: the swappable-mechanism map — what's load-bearing fundamental vs. what's a replaceable mechanism.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Use case | How the vehicle adapts |
|---|---|
| **Authority Flywheel — $2K–$5K coaching/services, no sales calls** | The model: one offer, one audience, content-as-sales-team carrying distribution (Stage 3 lever 4), self-close via the propaganda machine. Required-volume math (Stage 2) sets the content cadence; at $2K–$5K the destination is reachable on a lean book, so engineer client quality high (Stage 3 lever 2) and master fulfillment yourself before any handoff. Mode B voice on every client-facing asset — the line that filters wrong-fit buyers IS the qualification. |
| **LinkedIn-native (× Lara Acosta)** | The vehicle's distribution lever is organic posting cadence + DM funnel, not paid. Model math sets posts/week from required volume; positioning (Dunford) renders as the profile's "obvious choice for [buyer]" line. |
| **Newsletter / Substack (× Nicolas Cole)** | The model monetizes the list: price × subscriber-audience × skill must close. The newsletter is the content-as-sales-team lever; the through-line belief carries the model. Sponsor vs. product vs. service is a *mechanism* choice (Stage 5) on a fixed audience fundamental. |
| **Paid-ads-heavy (× Luke Iha)** | The retargeting funnel is the mechanism; ad spend at the locked price point sets required volume. Engineer for ad-spend efficiency, not headcount. Vicious hooks carry the asset army; positioning sets the price the ad math can hold. |
| **Low/no ad budget (organic)** | Content-as-sales-team is the only distribution lever (Stage 3), so the model must tolerate a longer time-to-close and lean harder on polarization for organic reach. Pick a price × audience where organic-achievable volume actually closes the destination — do not engineer a vehicle that secretly requires a paid mechanism you can't fund. |

## Output Requirements

Deliver an **Engineered Business-Vehicle Spec**:
1. **Destination spec** — Freedom Number, monthly spend, and the lifestyle constraints the vehicle must satisfy (Stage 1).
2. **Locked model** — the price × audience × skill match, with required-volume math shown at the candidate price points and the reason the others were killed (Stage 2). Include the April Dunford positioning line the price rests on.
3. **Leverage stack** — the 4 levers ranked, each mapped to the function it carries, what stays human (Stage 3).
4. **Delegate-vs-abdicate plan** — every function marked you-run / dialed-ready / SOP-delegated, with the SOP-readiness gate (Stage 4).
5. **Swappable-mechanism map** — load-bearing fundamentals vs. replaceable mechanisms (Stage 5).
6. **Ethics Gate sign-off** on the destination and the model.

## Quality Gate

Score against the genius.md rubric. The dimensions that bind this workflow:
- **Reverse-engineered? (Rubric #2)** — does the spec start from a crystal-clear destination and work backward, or did it pick a model first and rationalize a destination to fit? A vehicle chosen before the destination fails here.
- **Reality-grounded? (Rubric #1)** — is the model math built on real conversion rates and real volume capacity, or on "I feel like this price could work"? Phantom volume math fails.
- **Leveraged? (Rubric #6)** — does the leverage stack remove the operator from the function, or just rename "me doing everything" as a system?
- **Edge intact? (Rubric #5)** — run the spine test on any client-facing line in the spec; a model that pleases every buyer has no positioning and no price power.
- **Ethics Gate passed? (Rubric #7)** — all four gates on the destination and offer, or kill it.

**Failure modes specific to this workflow:** copying a mechanism without the fundamentals (the exact anti-pattern Pattern #13 exists to stop); a price × audience whose required volume needs a skill the operator doesn't have and isn't building (Sahara-in-a-Lamborghini); engineering leverage with headcount instead of the four levers; **abdication dressed as delegation** — any function marked SOP-delegated that the operator never personally dialed. If any bound dimension <6, rebuild that stage once and re-score.
