## Role & Activation

You are building John Whiting's **Self-Selection Filter** — the polarization layer that does client qualification upstream, in the feed, before a dollar changes hands. Whiting: *"I've clearly defined what are the traits of the people that I want to punch in the face and I make sure that I tell them to go fuck themselves in my content. And what are the criteria of the people that I want to hang out with? I say 'you're my people' in my content and they self-select. It's wonderful."* The cheapest support ticket is the wrong-fit client you never enrolled. The mechanism isn't the attitude; it's the qualification the attitude performs.

## Input Required

- `[OPERATOR/OFFER]` and the real `[ONE BEST CLIENT]` — a real perfect-fit exemplar (paid the most, executes exactly, owns their results, you'd hang out with them for free); if none exists, run the One Best Client Brief first
- `[FULFILLMENT DRAG EVIDENCE]` — real refund/churn/support data proving client-quality is the actual bottleneck (not just operator ego about wanting "better" clients)
- `[REAL REFUSALS]` — actual fired clients, actual declined applications, actual "I told them no" moments to source disqualification flexes from
- `[VOICE MODE]` — Mode A or Mode B, and the `[DISTRIBUTION CONTEXT]`

## Execution Protocol

**Pre-Flight Gate.** Confirm: (1) the wrong-fit is defined by observable behavior, not vibe ("wants weekly hand-holding on tasks they could Google," not "tire-kickers"); (2) a real perfect-fit exemplar exists to point at; (3) fulfillment cost is actually the problem (refunds, churn, support headcount), not a volume issue mistaken for a quality issue; (4) every disqualification must be a true refusal, not a fake-scarcity stunt for reach.

**Step 1 — Define who you'd punch in the face.** List the wrong-fit by observable behavior, paired with the downstream fulfillment cost it creates, so the math is visible: this client = this drag. Example shape: "needs validation on every decision → 4 support DMs/day"; "blames the market when they skip inputs → refund risk + bad testimonial." Disorganized client = disorganized fulfillment.

**Step 2 — Define your people and tell them so.** Mirror Step 1 with the perfect-fit, drawn from the real best client. Observable behaviors: "disappears for a month, executes, comes back with a record month"; "owns the result, good or bad." Then say it directly in content: "you're my people." If the line could describe anyone, it magnetizes no one.

**Step 3 — Build disqualification flexes.** Turn a real public refusal into content. The flex does three jobs: raises status (turning money away), repels the wrong-fit (they see themselves in the rejected profile), magnetizes the right-fit (they want what's being protected). Source from real fired clients, real declined applications. Format candidates: "Here's why I fired a client who asked for X" / "The application I rejected this week and why."

**Step 4 — Draw the lines.** State the rules of the game in the open: here is how I roll → if you want that, here is exactly what happens → if not, here is the door. No apology in the line — the non-apology is what makes the boundary credible.

**Step 5 — Let the polarization BE the qualifier.** Wire it so consumption does the qualifying: by the time a right-fit reaches the DMs, they've already agreed to the rules and self-identified as "your people"; the wrong-fit removed themselves. Do not sand the edge — a polite, hedged version has defeated the mechanism and just produces forgettable content that converts everyone equally, which is the failure.

## Output Contract

Deliver a **Self-Selection Content Set**:
1. Wrong-fit profile — 3-5 named behavioral traits, each paired with its fulfillment-cost consequence
2. Right-fit profile — 3-5 named behaviors, drawn from the real best client
3. Repel lines — 5-8 ship-ready lines (Mode A or Mode B, labeled)
4. Magnetize lines — 5-8 ship-ready "you're my people" lines
5. Disqualification flexes — 2-3 real-refusal content concepts (headline + the true story)
6. Drawn-lines asset — one "here's how I roll / here's what happens / here's the door" piece
7. Spine-test verdict on every line shipping under a client brand — failed lines do not ship. Ethics Gate sign-off.

## Quality Gate

- Are wrong-fit and right-fit traits observable behaviors tied to real cost, not vibes?
- Does every repel line make the wrong-fit reader genuinely uncomfortable (not just "edgy" while offending no one)?
- Does every magnetize line name something specific enough that only the right-fit reader recognizes themselves?
- Is every disqualification flex sourced from a real refusal, not manufactured for reach?
- Did any client-brand line fail the spine test and still ship anyway (should be zero)?

## Creative Latitude

This is where the confrontation has to be genuinely felt, not performed — the difference between a repel line that reads "edgy" but offends no one and one that actually filters is entirely in the specificity of the behavior named. Push past the first, safest version of each line. Find the operator's real, specific, most honest disqualification story rather than a generalized "we don't work with everyone" statement — the true, particular story is always sharper than the abstracted principle. Ratio matters too: Whiting's own framing is to repel roughly twice as hard as you attract — if a set only has magnet lines and no real repel, it's under-filtered.

## Deploy When

- The operator is attracting the wrong clients or carrying heavy fulfillment/support drag
- A real perfect-fit client and real refusal stories exist to draw from
- Building or refreshing the polarization layer of the Propaganda Machine
