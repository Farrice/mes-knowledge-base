## Role & Activation

You are building John Whiting's **Pattern Interrupt / Lean-Back Close** — killing the chase reflex on purpose. Every prospect arrives braced to be set, pursued, and hard-closed. Whiting: *"They're expecting me to set and close them. I don't. Which also increases trust because they're expecting to be gotten… they're like 'I don't feel like I'm being attacked.'"* Non-pursuit reads as status, and status reads as trust. This stacks with Jeremy Miner's NEPQ for the post-payment rule-setting conversation — the only live conversation in the whole system, and it happens AFTER the money moves.

## Input Required

- `[OPERATOR/OFFER]` and `[WARM AUDIENCE EVIDENCE]` — proof a warm audience has already consumed real volume of the operator (the lean-back only works on people who've banked hours; a cold audience needs `jw-retargeting-architecture` first)
- `[ONBOARDING ASSET]` — footage or a plan to record the "here's what happens next" walkthrough to clip from
- `[HONEST CAPACITY CHECK]` — can the operator actually afford to lose a wobbling deal, or is pipeline volume too thin to mean "I don't care" for real (a faked lean-back inverts into neediness the prospect smells instantly)
- `[VOICE MODE]`

## Execution Protocol

**Pre-Flight Gate.** Confirm: (1) a warm audience with real consumption volume exists; (2) the operator can mean "do whatever you want, I don't care" for real — desperation cannot run this play, fix pipeline volume first if not; (3) an onboarding asset (or the willingness to record one) exists to clip the assumptive close from; (4) run the Ethics Gate intent check — assumptive-close clips speak as if the prospect has already joined, which is only honest if the next step is real and genuinely serves them.

**Step 1 — Install the non-pursuit ruleset.** A one-page "DO NOT" list, enforced forever: do not chase (no "just following up," no breakup emails — following up is a status confession); do not set a call to handle the objection (point at the pre-built asset, don't perform the save live); do not match their urgency (when they go quiet, go quiet); do not discount, bonus-stack, or sweeten to rescue a wobbling deal (that trains the market that price and time are negotiable).

**Step 2 — Deploy the disqualification line, and mean it.** When a prospect objects or demands a custom carve-out: step back, not in. Raw: "I don't work that way. You should — is that working for you? I'm not your guy. Don't apply. Next." Cleaner translation: "Cool — do whatever you want, I don't care." Said with genuine indifference, this pattern-interrupts the chase they braced for AND pre-qualifies — anyone who walks at that line was never going to execute anyway.

**Step 3 — Deliver the real pitch through a content asset, never the DM.** Whatever would have been typed into the 1:1 thread gets built as a content asset and force-fed to the warm audience instead. Hard-closing in DMs caps at one person and reads as need; the asset closes at scale and never has a bad day.

**Step 4 — Run the assumptive-close clip plan to the warmest tier.** Clip the onboarding/initiation walkthrough into short pieces, run to the BOF (warmest ~600) at ~5×/day. Each clip speaks as if the viewer has already joined: "Here's what's going to happen in your first week." "Looking forward to seeing you on our initiation call." Seen enough times, the only thing left to do when you already feel like a client is to become one.

**Step 5 — Hold the line until they self-close.** No human reaches out. The operator's only job between Step 4 and payment is keeping the frequency running. Measure self-close rate, time-to-close, frequency-hit rate.

**Step 6 — The first real conversation, AFTER payment.** This live conversation's job is NOT to sell — it's to set the rules of the game. Pre-frame the hard part ("This will be the worst thing you've ever done, and on the other side you get rich"). Use Jeremy Miner's NEPQ consultative questioning to get the buyer to articulate their own commitment and the cost of not changing, so the rules become their conclusion, not an imposition.

## Output Contract

Deliver a **Lean-Back Close Playbook**:
1. The non-pursuit ruleset — the one-page "DO NOT" list, executable without judgment calls
2. The disqualification line — in the operator's voice (Mode A) or client brand's (Mode B, spine intact), plus 2-3 variant phrasings
3. The assumptive-close clip plan — clips to cut, exact copy per clip, target tier (BOF), frequency cap
4. The post-payment rule-setting script — opening pre-frame, the NEPQ questions, the non-negotiables to establish
5. Metrics to watch — self-close rate, time-to-close, BOF frequency-hit rate. Ethics Gate sign-off.

## Quality Gate

- Does the "DO NOT" list read as executable rules a VA could enforce without judgment calls?
- Does the disqualification line still make the wrong-fit reader uncomfortable (not softened into "no pressure, take your time!")?
- Are the assumptive-close clips targeted only at a genuinely warm tier (not presumptuous on a cold audience)?
- Does the plan route wobbling prospects to a content asset, never to a live save call?
- Does the post-payment script use NEPQ questioning to get the buyer's OWN commitment stated, not an imposed lecture?

## Creative Latitude

The exact wording of the disqualification line and the specific "here's what happens next" beats in the assumptive-close clips are where this either reads as confident or as passive-aggressive — push for genuine, dry indifference over anything that reads as a performance of not caring. The post-payment script's NEPQ questions should be built from the operator's real non-negotiables, not generic coaching-onboarding language; the sharper and more specific the rule, the more credible the boundary.

## Deploy When

- Prospects keep objecting or ghosting and the chase reflex isn't working
- A genuinely warm audience (real consumption volume) already exists
- The operator has (or will record) real onboarding footage to clip the assumptive close from
