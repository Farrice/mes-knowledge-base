# LENS CARD — Joanna Wiebe (conversion copywriter; System 1/System 2 persuasion architect)
> Compiled 2026-07-27 from genius.md (canonical). FIRING RULE: this card decides WHETHER this lens has something to say about a draft line; when a lens fires, READ the matching genius.md section before treating. Never treat from the card alone.

## Lenses — Persuasion Hierarchy
### The 5-Level Persuasion Hierarchy
Persuasion is a progressive skill tree; each level subsumes the previous. Level 5 invisibly incorporates 1–4. Most copywriters plateau at Level 2 (tricks and biases).
**Test:** Diagnostic for any copy — which level is it operating at?

### The "What's In It For Me?" Inversion + Pronoun Audit
Shift from "we/our/I" to "you/your" language. Count first- vs. second-person pronouns: if first-person > second-person, the copy is Level 0 — not even Level 1.
**Test:** 30-second pronoun count; second-person must dominate.

### Cognitive Bias Deployment System
Anchoring: show a high number first. Loss aversion: frame the cost of inaction (losses felt ~2x as intensely — "you're losing $500/month" beats "you'll save $500/month"). Social proof: specific numbers with context ("12,847 founders" > "thousands of users"). Goldilocks: 3 options, middle is target. Bizarreness: unexpected details stick.
**Test:** Each bias operationalized, not named.

### Money Words — Identity Language Mine
Money words are nouns, verbs, and identity markers mined from customer reviews, forums, interviews — you don't invent them, you extract them. "Sovereignty" is a money word. "Amazing" is not; hype adjectives are never money words.
**Test:** Every key term is VOC-derived and audience-specific.

### System 1/System 2 Traffic Control + Toll Booth Detection
Keep System 2 (analytical) asleep while System 1 drives toward action. Toll booths that wake System 2: unfamiliar terminology, unexpected tone shifts, cognitive load, broken expectations, anything requiring "thinking." Audit and remove every one.
**Test:** Effortless read; no pause or re-read anywhere.

### The Napoleon Identity Hook
Copy tells the reader who they BECOME, not what they GET. ("Every soldier carries a marshal's baton in his knapsack" — identity, not features or benefits.)
**Test:** Reader can name the identity transformation, not just the outcome.

### The Story Seller Framework (Level 5) + Invisible Integration Test
Intrinsic motivation through story where the audience is the hero; all lower levels woven in invisibly. "Level 5 writing passes when a reader cannot identify where the persuasion techniques are."
**Test:** "That was really persuasive" = Level 3–4. "I just knew I wanted it" = Level 5.

### The "Good Decision" Reframe
The copywriter's job is "make the good decision easy" — removing friction and adding motivation for a product that genuinely helps is service, not manipulation.

## Lenses — Authority Craft (sentence level, written)
### The Catchy Phrasing Engine (Meme/T-Shirt Test)
Four engineerable traits: familiar syntax, one distinctive word choice, portability, compression (no conjunctions, no fluff). Familiar syntax + one surprising word = the brain tags it "important."
**Test:** "Could this work as a meme? Could you put it on a t-shirt?" If not, strip to one distinctive word, remove every conjunction, add white space after the line.

### Order of Revelation (Schema Programming)
The first fact becomes the framework for interpreting everything after. Conservative buyer → lead with the result; innovation buyer → lead with the rule-breaking.
**Test:** Ask before any important statement: "What do I want the audience to assume is true before they learn this next thing?" — "This question will change everything."

### Stealing Thunder (Preemptive Disqualification)
Volunteer when you're NOT the right choice before the prospect brings it up — shifts you from sales mode to expert mode. Templates: "If you want [fast/cheap thing], this isn't for you." / "Our approach works best for [qualifier]. Are you there? If not, solve that first." / "I'm not the right fit if your team [anti-pattern]."
**Test:** Prospect stops defending their budget and starts moving money to bring you in.

### Anti-Hype (Understatement Engine)
Hype signals desperation; anti-hype signals confidence. "It wasn't dramatic. We just stopped doing what everyone else was doing." Find the moment you're tempted to hype and do the opposite ("Game-changing breakthrough" → "A small shift that happened to matter").
**Test:** Language deflated, meaning heavy; zero hype adjectives.

### Bucket Brigades (Cognitive Pacing)
1–5 word transitions creating micro-curiosity payable in the next line. Deploy after dense ideas, before belief shifts, at drop-off points.
**Test:** If you can edit the bucket brigade out and the writing improves, it wasn't earning its place.

## Lenses — Funnel-Stage Triggers (ToFu → MoFu → BoFu)
The funnel is a System 2 management game: ToFu keep it asleep; MoFu put it back to sleep when it stirs; BoFu give it just enough to sign off. Brain states: ToFu "Am I in the right place?"; MoFu "Will this actually work?"; BoFu "Can I justify this decision?"
- **Framing (ToFu):** Name the narrow category in the opening; force a binary sort. **Test:** reader determines fit in <2 seconds without thinking.
- **Identity Matching (ToFu):** Mirror the reader's self-image, never threaten it ("Most marketers get this completely wrong" wakes System 2 — "that defensive response actually costs millions"). **Test:** "That's me," not "Are they saying I'm wrong?"
- **Fluency (ToFu):** Short sentences, familiar words, predictable rhythm. Read aloud; where you stumble = friction. **Test:** no cognitive pause or re-reading. Checklist: does this ask them to decide, imply they're wrong, make life harder? If yes → rewrite.
- **Unique Mechanisms (MoFu):** Name the WHY. Template: "Our solution works because of [mechanism name] which means [specific outcome]." Can't fill it in 10 words → no mechanism yet. **Test:** reader shifts from "How?" to "Oh, that makes sense."
- **Typically Atypical (MoFu):** Specific time frames, realistic ratios (9/10, not "all"), understated quantifiers ("more than"). Skepticism scales with specificity of promise — distribute the claim across qualifiers. **Test:** claims feel credible; System 2 stays relaxed.
- **Boring by Design (MoFu):** List your 3 biggest objections; kill each with one boring monotone sentence at the moment of tension. A dramatic rebuttal confirms the fear is a big deal — the boringness IS the persuasion. **Test:** reader doesn't pause or tense at the objection point.
- **Choice Framing (BoFu):** Exactly 3 options; Option 1 = what they're doing now (failing), Option 2 = your solution (safe middle), Option 3 = harder alternative. **Test:** prospect can articulate to a boss/partner why this is the logical choice (the Defensibility Test — they're rehearsing the explanation to someone not in the room).
- **Transparent Trade-offs (BoFu):** "[Result], but [limitation or effort required]." Deploy once, right before the close. **Test:** prospect stops looking for hidden problems; trust spikes.
- **Big Proof, Small Bite (BoFu):** Cross out all proof except the single most impressive, relevant example. Less proof = more belief; overwhelming proof creates "why are they trying so hard?" suspicion. **Test:** one proof point — specific, credible, impossible to dismiss.

## Lenses — Storytelling Psychology
- **Predictive Processing Hooks:** create uncertainty the brain can't resolve, satisfy with a twist; favorite structure = the sensory anchor ("It all started with the smell of gasoline"). **Test:** read the first line to someone: (1) Do you know what it's about? (2) Are you intrigued? Both must be yes.
- **Establishing Shot:** answer the four unconscious orientation questions in the opening lines — Where? Who? Vibe? Focus. **Test:** a reader given only the opening can answer all four without effort.
- **Word Pictures (Dual Coding):** concrete words store twice (verbal + visual); put the picture on the thing you want remembered. **Test:** close-your-eyes test — the image the listener reports IS your takeaway.
- **Structure-to-Emotion Matching:** pick the FEELING first, then the structure that produces it (Save the Cat = satisfaction/rhythm; PAS = urgency; hero's journey = transformation; And-But-Therefore = clarity). **Test:** structure invisible; reader reports the target emotion.
- **Chekhov's Gun Editing (the "Jobs" Audit):** every line must name its job — builds desire, handles objection, provides proof, creates urgency. Can't name it → it's fired. **Test:** 20–40% lifts from firing, not adding.
- **The Mega Mean Mouse (Villain Construction):** sell a bigger, scarier mouse, not a better mousetrap. 4 build steps: stack consequences; blame external forces (protect the ego); list dead solutions tried; explain why they failed. **Test:** prospect recognizes themselves and feels seen, not blamed.
- **Sugar Coating:** pair the medicine (what they need to know) with sugar (conflict, drama, humor, movement). **Test:** retention of the boring-but-critical content — what can they repeat back?
- **Bookending:** plant something in the opening worth returning to; payoff must arrive with a twist. **Test:** deletion test — if the bookend removed and the story still feels complete, the ending is decorative, not structural.

## Lenses — Live Authority (spoken twins of the written stack)
- **Challenger's Paradox:** command respect with questions — Implication ("What happens when your top developer leaves?"), Reframe, Assumption ("Why do you believe...?"). Formula: listen for their core assumption → ask what happens when it breaks → let them discover the flaw. **Test:** prospect feels SEEN, not schooled.
- **Frame Control:** set frames BEFORE they're set against you ("Let's discuss whether we're a fit for each other"). **Test:** never scrambling to reframe mid-conversation.
- **Strategic Silence:** pre-response pause 2–3s; emphasis pause 1–2s before the key point; power pause 3–7s after an offer. **Test:** the other party speaks first after your offer.
- **Precision as Power:** replace every vague claim with a counted one; name your frameworks and walk them by name. **Test:** prospect repeats your named framework back to you.
- **Certainty Gradient:** delete hedge words entirely ("just," "maybe," "sort of," "I think," "actually," "really"); "I feel like" → "I've observed." Sentence strength ladder: Level 1 = bare declaration; Level 2 = declaration + support. **Test:** zero hedges; every sentence survives the "does this fight for its life in an 8-second attention span?" test.
- **Authority Face + Total Package:** align posture, vocal (chest voice, slow 20%), gesture (no fidgeting), presence; neutral thoughtful baseline, strategic animation only — never default-smile. **Test:** same content that got polite nods now gets "lead the implementation."
- **Written↔Live Bridge:** prep any high-stakes talk track through the written mechanics FIRST, then layer live mechanics on delivery.

## Anti-patterns / calibration
- Execute the move; never announce it — a reader who spots the technique caps you at Level 3.
- Never open with "we/our/I" when "you/your" fits (Level 0 failure).
- Never lead a hook with a vague universal claim — the brain "keeps scrolling while trying to figure out if this matters."
- Never threaten the reader's self-image to create urgency.
- Never confuse hype adjectives ("revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "unparalleled") with money words — that's the Level 0/1 failure mode.
- Never pile proof past one credible boulder.
- Never answer a soft objection with a dramatic rebuttal — one boring sentence instead.
- Never hedge: one client email signaled doubt 17 times before reaching the point.
- Sequencing matters (Evolution Log): deploy triggers at the reader's exact micro-state — proof when they're hungry for justification, not before the objection forms. Persuasion isn't just WHAT triggers — it's WHEN.
- When unsure, run the test she'd run (t-shirt test, pronoun audit, boring-sentence kill) instead of inventing a new flourish.
