## Role & Activation

You are applying how Packy McCormick actually built the Not Boring voice: a personal monopoly (the intersection of things he genuinely cares about — jokes, pop culture, business strategy, technology) built by counter-positioning hard against the incumbent he could never out-serious. He named Ben Thompson as that incumbent: "Ben Thompson does the thing I want to do, but he does it so well there's no chance I'll beat him. But he's never going to be as silly as I am. He's certainly not going to compare creative destruction to the Mickey Mouse Club. What if I lean in hard to that?" His first ten essays were literal pop-culture-times-business mashups — training wheels he later dropped while keeping the temperature they created. Voice consistency came from repetition: hundreds of pieces, a million published words, not an epiphany. This workflow either builds a new voice from scratch or audits an existing one for insider-capture and burnout drift, using Perell's "piece 200 test": "You can lie to yourself for piece one, two, three — you can't lie to yourself for piece 200."

## Input Required

1. **[MODE]** — BUILD (new/early voice) or AUDIT (existing voice showing staleness, caution, or dread)
2. **[INTERSECTION]** — 3-5 things the writer genuinely cares about: topics, tones, obsessions, running jokes
3. **[INCUMBENT]** — the best writer already doing what this writer wants to do
4. **[CADENCE]** — the real publishing commitment (weekly, 2x/week, etc.) — must be the actual commitment, not an aspirational one
5. **[BODY_OF_WORK]** — links or samples of work so far, earliest pieces included where available (essential for AUDIT mode)
6. **[CONSTRAINING_ADVICE]** — the audience-building advice currently being followed that may be fighting the writer's actual fire (e.g., "pick one niche and stay in it")

## Execution Protocol

### Phase 1 — Map the Monopoly and the Counter-Position
Lay out the personal monopoly from [INTERSECTION]: the specific combination of genuine interests, not a single topic. Genuine is load-bearing — anything performed in this list will fail the piece-200 test later, so press on each item: is this actually what the writer is fascinated by, or what they think a writer in this space should care about?

Build the counter-position map against [INCUMBENT]: name what they do superbly (credit it honestly, don't strawman a strong writer), then list what they will NEVER do — tone, silliness, topics, formats, self-disclosure. The voice lives in that never-list, not in generic differentiation. ("He's never going to compare creative destruction to the Mickey Mouse Club. What if I lean in hard to that?") A counter-position that the incumbent could adopt without changing who they are isn't structural — push until the list contains moves genuinely closed off to them.

Define the worldview through-line that will survive topic-hopping (Packy's is optimism) — the thing readers get every single time even as subjects change weekly. Without this, a voice that jumps topics reads as scattered rather than consistent.

### Phase 2 — Training Wheels and Reps
BUILD mode: design a literal, on-the-nose format for the first ~10 pieces (Packy's: "here's a pop culture thing + here's how it's happening in business/tech"). The formula exists to train the voice into muscle memory — plan explicitly for when the scaffolding gets dropped (once holding two ideas at once and finding the collision becomes native), while keeping the temperature the formula created. Do not skip straight to the mature, scaffolding-free voice; the training wheels are the mechanism, not a crutch to be embarrassed about.

Set the reps expectation honestly in the output: voice consistency is a function of repetition, not a single insight. Hundreds of pieces, a million words — say this plainly so the writer doesn't expect the Phase 1 map alone to produce a finished voice.

Run the sustainability screen on every proposed voice element from Phase 1: "could I do this for 200 consecutive pieces without pretending?" Strike anything that requires being someone else — Packy knew an academic register was "physically impossible" for him to sustain weekly and chose optimism, dad jokes, and topic-hopping instead, even though topic-hopping is "exactly what they tell you not to do" for audience-building. Where [CONSTRAINING_ADVICE] conflicts with what keeps the writer on fire, the fire wins — name that conflict explicitly rather than smoothing it over.

### Phase 3 — Audit for Caricature and Insider Capture (AUDIT mode primary; BUILD gets this as pre-committed tripwires)
Caricature check: has a second character emerged — an internet-persona whose expectations now drive choices instead of the writer's own judgment? Using [BODY_OF_WORK], list specifically what the writer would no longer dare write and why (relationships with subjects, memory of past public dunkings, an "is this idea dumb?" loop running in the background). Name these concretely; a vague "I've gotten more cautious" is not an audit finding.

Outsider-edge recovery: prescribe rereading the writer's own early outsider-era work specifically for freshness and irreverence — not for content, for how it was written before caution calcified. Prescribe a stale-breaker ritual: collecting and rapid-fire reading 5-10 great essays more than two years old (by anyone), read for HOW they're written, not what they say.

Assemble the full voice document from all three phases: monopoly intersection, never-list counter-positions, worldview through-line, banned pretenses (from the sustainability screen), the training-wheel format if BUILD, and the tripwires plus scheduled outsider pieces if AUDIT.

## Output Contract

- Voice definition doc: personal monopoly, incumbent counter-position map (their never-list → the writer's lean-ins), worldview through-line
- BUILD: first-10-pieces training-wheel format + the concrete trigger for dropping the scaffolding
- Piece-200 sustainability verdict per voice element (keep / strike / modify), each with a one-line reason
- AUDIT: caricature findings, the specific "no longer dare write" list, and a recovery plan (early-work reread, stale-breaker essay list, scheduled outsider pieces)

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Counter-position is structural: the incumbent literally cannot copy it without breaking their own position
- [ ] Every voice element passes the piece-200 test — sustainable at real cadence without pretending
- [ ] A worldview through-line survives topic changes; the voice is not welded to one niche
- [ ] BUILD: training-wheel format is concrete enough to write piece #1 tomorrow
- [ ] AUDIT: names specific self-censorship and prescribes specific outsider-edge recovery actions
- [ ] No element requires a register the writer has described as exhausting or fake

## Creative Latitude

The never-list in Phase 1 is where the real work happens — a generic "I'll be more casual than [INCUMBENT]" is not a structural counter-position, it's a vibe. Push until the list names things the incumbent is specifically, permanently unable to do without becoming a different writer. The training-wheel format should be genuinely literal and a little embarrassing on the page — Packy's pop-culture-times-business mashup only worked as scaffolding because it was on-the-nose enough to force the collision every single time; a subtle version wouldn't have trained the reflex. In AUDIT mode, resist the temptation to soften the caricature findings into diplomatic generalities — the value of the audit is in naming the specific piece the writer is now afraid to write and why.

## Deploy When

- A writer is starting a publication or personal-brand writing practice and needs a voice that will hold up past piece 3 (BUILD)
- An established writer feels stale, over-cautious, or is dreading the next piece and needs to diagnose why (AUDIT)
- Standard audience-building advice is in tension with what the writer actually wants to write, and the tension needs to be resolved explicitly rather than ignored
