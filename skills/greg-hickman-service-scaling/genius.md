# Greg Hickman — Genius Context

Productization strategist and founder of AltAgency. 900+ service providers moved from custom done-for-you work to productized, leveraged offers. Core conviction: the value was never in the grind, the team, or the hours — it's in the process and the ability to install it in someone else's business. His own pivot: one leveraged group program replaced all done-for-you revenue in 11 months and hit his first six-figure month.

## How to Use This Skill (Model Calibration)

These patterns are intuition primitives, not a checklist. Absorb the shape, then build for the specific business in front of you — nothing in this skill is generic; every claim ties to a dollar figure, a timeframe, or a named client outcome.

- Do NOT enumerate which pattern you applied ("Pattern 1: Signature Outcome, Pattern 2: Pre-Sell..."). Hickman's own material never narrates its own framework mid-sentence — it states the number and the move, nothing else.
- Do NOT reach for "leverage," "systemize," or "scale" as unadorned buzzwords. Every claim in this skill pairs the abstraction with a concrete figure ("7 clients in 6 days at $500/month," "5-15 hours/week recovered," "31K in 31 days") — match that discipline or cut the sentence.
- His texture is founder-to-founder, numbers-forward, allergic to hype: name the false belief before the fix (customization wins clients / more hours-staff / more clients), and make the fix a smaller, provable claim — never a moonshot.
- Recognition test: would Hickman recognize this as a founder walking another founder through the exact trade he made — dollar figure for dollar figure, fixed scope named explicitly — or as someone reciting "productization" vocabulary without ever naming the number, the timeframe, or what got cut? If it's the second, rebuild around one real figure.
- Polish is the tell here specifically: his own definition of bad delivery is "90-minute trainings of someone reading off a Google doc" (Hidden Knowledge, below). If the output is long, hedged, and comprehensive where his own material is short and blunt, it has drifted from the instrument — cut it back to the one number and the one move that prove the claim.

## Genius Patterns

### Pattern: Signature Outcome Extraction
Custom projects only look different. After dozens of them, every client really wanted the same outcome (for Hickman's agency: a webinar funnel that reliably sold coaching/courses). The offer is built around the ONE outcome you're already world-class at — not whatever clients dream up on kickoff calls.
**Execute**: List the last 10-20 client engagements. Strip the deliverable labels and name the underlying outcome each client was actually buying. Find the repeated outcome with the strongest results and the most energy. Kill everything else from the offer.
**Success Metric**: One sentence — "I help [ICP] get [one outcome] in [fixed timeframe]" — that covers 70%+ of your best past work. Sales get easier, pricing goes up, delivery improves each rep.

### Pattern: Productized Prototype Pre-Sell
Never build the program first. Package the signature process into a "productized prototype" and pre-sell it informally: one clear result, fixed timeframe (90 days if it's a big problem, ideally shorter), a few bullets on what's included, and a founder price that requires feedback plus a testimonial. Hickman's first version was an email, not a proposal — 7 clients in 6 days at $500/month; the next iteration got 11 clients at $2K.
**Execute**: Write the offer as a plain email (result, timeframe, 3-5 bullets, founder price, feedback/testimonial condition). Send to warm prospects. Deliver the reps, learn what they value, THEN systematize.
**Success Metric**: First 3-5 paying founder clients enrolled before any assets, funnel, or sales page exists. Goal is testimonials + delivery reps, not polish.

### Pattern: Warm Pipeline Three-Bucket Activation
The first five clients are under your nose, not in an ad campaign. Three buckets: (1) past pipeline — deals lost on timing or price who can now afford the leaner offer; (2) past/current clients — the new offer as refresh or complement; (3) peers who match or have access to the ICP (Hickman got clients from Amy Porterfield posting in her community).
**Execute**: Build a 30+ name list across all three buckets. Send the ICP interview request email: "I'm working on a new program to help [who] get [outcome] and I'm in the research phase. Open to a quick 15-20 minute Zoom? This is not a sales call." Include the optional forward/intro tweak.
**Success Metric**: A handful of 15-minute calls booked within a week of outreach, from people who already know you.

### Pattern: ICP Interview That Sells Without Selling
The interview is framed as research and genuinely is — but it reliably produces sales. Structure: welcome/frame (nothing to sell) → business snapshot → desired outcome ("If I waved a magic wand and solved this, what would change?") → share your hypothesis and test your language → have them rank their top 3 blockers → probe what they've tried and whether it's urgent now.
**Execute**: Run the seven-phase conversation. Capture their exact words — prospects describe the problem differently than providers do, and their phrasing becomes your marketing copy. Note must-have vs nice-to-have problems and current urgency.
**Success Metric**: A language bank of verbatim pain/desire phrases per interview, plus a clear fit/no-fit read on each prospect.

### Pattern: The Golden Question + Referral Cascade
End every interview: "I'm designing something to solve exactly what we discussed. If it turns out it would be a fit, would you want to know when it's actually ready? Also, is there anyone else you know working through a similar challenge?" High-value problems make prospects self-select ("Yeah, tell me about it") and the referral ask compounds the pipeline.
**Execute**: Deliver the golden question verbatim after thanking them. Follow up interested prospects with the informal founder offer email. Book interviews with every referral.
**Success Metric**: Each interview yields a follow-up offer opportunity, a referral, or a documented no-fit — never a dead end.

### Pattern: Capacity Reset Before Growth
Founders stuck in delivery can't think. Step one is always reclaiming 5-15 hours/week with simple playbooks, then phasing out wrong-fit custom work WITHOUT blowing up cash flow — a structured transition (still servicing legacy clients while delivering the new prototype), not a cliff.
**Execute**: Audit the calendar for custom work that doesn't feed the signature outcome. Systematize or stop the biggest time sinks first. Sequence client exits against revenue stability; stop taking one-off projects that pull you off course.
**Success Metric**: 5-15 hours/week recovered while monthly revenue holds; bandwidth exists to build the new model.

### Pattern: Minimum Marketing System
No full marketing engine before clients. The minimum system: consistent short-form content + clearer messaging + warm outreach on ONE preferred platform (usually Instagram or LinkedIn). Mature architecture stays at three: long-form channel for authority/nurture (YouTube), short-form for testing ideas, and email for depth + distribution — both platforms drive to the list.
**Execute**: Pick one platform. Ship the cadence that's sustainable (Hickman: one video/week, three emails/week — "not sexy, but it works"). Use short-form to vet topics before investing in long-form. Everything drives to email.
**Success Metric**: Audience and email list growing monthly; leads and calls arriving weekly from a system that takes hours, not the whole week.

### Pattern: Customer Generation Over Lead Generation
More unqualified leads is nobody's bottleneck. Acquire BUYERS: a $7 tactical book funnel and $100 live workshops monetize prospects not ready for the core offer, cover marketing costs at break-even, and convert faster (book buyers upgraded to $3K-$25K programs within a week; workshop attendees experience the expertise live, then ascend).
**Execute**: Package the overarching methodology into one low-ticket asset (short tactical book or 90-120 min paid workshop). Sell it as the front end; make the core offer the natural upgrade. Only add this AFTER the core offer is validated and delivery is smooth.
**Success Metric**: Front-end revenue covers acquisition cost; a measurable percentage of low-ticket buyers ascend to the core program.

### Pattern: Demonstration + Transparency Marketing
In a trust recession, content that SAYS you're good is invisible; content that SHOWS it wins. Only talk about what you've personally done or helped a client through — that's where conviction comes from, and people feel it. Show the offer openly: sales pages with what's included, how it works, and the price. No fake strategy calls. Don't convince skeptics — align with the already-convinced (Hatch.fm stopped arguing for content marketing and just asked bought-in prospects "are we the best fit?"). Consistency compounds under the 7-11-4 rule: ~7 hours, 11 touchpoints, 4 platforms before a buying decision.
**Execute**: Every content piece shares a real lesson from your own or a client's work. Publish the price. Cut convincing arguments from marketing and sales calls; qualify for existing belief instead.
**Success Metric**: Prospects arrive pre-sold ("I've watched everything, how do I start?"); sales calls shrink or go async.

### Pattern: Identity Beats Outcomes
Results are table stakes — many agencies promise the same outcome. Differentiation is the new identity the client becomes: Hormozi's clients became "gym lords"; Hickman's become "alt agency owners" (multiple income streams, lean team, high profit, 3-4 day week). Pair identity with a smaller, clearer, believable promise ("double your take-home in 12 months") rather than moonshots that trigger skepticism.
**Execute**: Name the identity your best clients transform into. Anchor the offer promise to one specific, provable number/timeframe. Let content and cases back the promise so belief precedes the call.
**Success Metric**: Prospects use your identity language unprompted ("I want to be an X"); promise passes the skeptic test without caveats.

## Hidden Knowledge

### Insight: Informality Is a Feature of the Founder Offer
**Insight**: Making the founder offer in a plain email sets the correct expectation — a raw, unpolished experience — which is exactly what v1 delivery will be. Polished proposals promise polish you can't yet deliver and slow everything down. The goal of the first cohort is reps + testimonials, not revenue maximization.
**Deploy**: Refuse to build proposals, decks, or sales pages for a founder offer. Email only. Price it so the feedback/testimonial condition feels like a fair trade.

### Insight: Custom Requests Are the Same Outcome in Disguise
**Insight**: Prospects asking for custom scope are usually describing the signature outcome in their own words. The skill is translating the request back to the productized solution during the sale instead of reshaping the business around it — every yes to custom scope is how the agency trap gets rebuilt (Hickman's ballooned to 13 people before he saw it).
**Deploy**: When a custom ask arrives, restate the underlying outcome, map it to the standard process, and hold the line. If it truly doesn't map, it's a no, not a new service line.

### Insight: The 6-Month Container Exists Because Results Compound at Months 5-6
**Insight**: Across 900+ clients the biggest results cluster around months five and six — when bottlenecks are fixed, systems align, and skills sharpen enough for speed. Shorter commitments truncate the compounding; ROI often lands much earlier (31K in 31 days, investment covered in 45 days) but the transformation needs the container.
**Deploy**: Structure core programs as 6-month minimum commitments (ideally frame a 12-month horizon). Design the container so early wins fund patience for the compounding phase.

### Insight: Scarcity Is Real When Support Is the Product
**Insight**: Capped monthly enrollment isn't a marketing trick when a lean, founder-involved team delivers support — quality of support IS the results engine, and results grow both businesses. Enrollment caps protect the mechanism.
**Deploy**: Set intake limits from actual support capacity. Close enrollment when full and say why honestly; it reads as integrity, not scarcity theater.

### Insight: Training Assets Are Short or They're Garbage
**Insight**: "90-minute trainings of someone reading off a Google doc" is Hickman's definition of garbage delivery. Leveraged delivery = tight, short, actionable videos, each with a workbook, many prompting work submission for expert review, plus templates and AI tools so clients never build from scratch. Constantly prune what stopped working.
**Deploy**: When productizing delivery, cap lesson length, pair every lesson with an action artifact, and build a review loop. Audit the curriculum quarterly: update what works, delete what doesn't.
