## Professionalize Your Operation (the meta-thesis)

> "'You need to professionalize your operation.' ... 'Until you professionalize your operation, its potential is limited. The amount of money you make and your ability to have a positive impact on the world will be limited.'"

> "In the seven years since that conversation, my small business has gone from about $3 million to nearly $20 million in revenue... if I leave for a few weeks on vacation, the business performs as though I were still there."

## The Airplane Standard + Rule of Proportion (Patterns 1–2 / Wf 01)

> "Some people come off as business savants because of their ability to know what's wrong with a business after asking just a few questions. The truth is, though, they aren't savants. What they have is a standard to which they are comparing the business in question."

> The six parts: "1. The cockpit... represents the leadership. 2. The right engine represents the marketing effort... 3. The left engine represents sales... 4. The wings... represent the products or services you sell. 5. The body... represents overhead and operations. 6. The fuel tanks represent cash flow."

> "The simple metaphor of the airplane created a powerful decision-making filter... whenever we hired a new team member, I'd ask myself how we would offset their salary. Would this investment make the wings larger... or increase thrust... or enlarge the body of the airplane (increasing overhead and making it heavier, contributing to a crash)?"

> "In order to grow your business safely, you'll want to grow its parts in proportion to each other."

> "Beware of looking successful without being successful... an airplane with a massive body, tiny wings, weak engines, and a cockpit that more closely resembles a first-class cabin flowing with alcohol than a small room where serious pilots can study the numbers."

## The S-Curve (Pattern 3 / Wf 01)

> "Every successful business has come face-to-face with the 's-curve.'... the business owner is pulled out of their sweet spot... They spend too much time trying to put out fires, and the business starts to decline because the owner is managing problems rather than continuing to create the magic that grew the company... All this despite the fact that they had a product people wanted."

## Business on a Mission (Pattern 4 / Wf 02)

> "A Mission Statement like this is missing one critical element: A mission."

> "What we need to create a Mission Statement that drives narrative traction are three economic objectives, a deadline, and a clear reason the mission is important... We will accomplish X by Y because of Z."

> "if you prioritize everything, you prioritize nothing."

> "You can edit your Mission Statement every few years (and you should) because missions are meant to end. No mission that is open ended is going to inspire action."

> "the 'because' part of our Mission Statement is the actual mission. Without the 'because,' all you have is a goal."

> Key Characteristics: "Core values are great, but often words like 'integrity' are too elusive to live out... Key Characteristics are more specific." (Restaurant example: "We love people and enjoy serving them. We are obsessed with great-tasting food. We are calm under pressure.")

> Critical Actions: "three actions every member of your team can take nearly every day that will move the business toward its three financial objectives." (Real estate: "sign a thank-you card" for every closing.)

## Product Optimization (Pattern 5 / Wf 04)

> "How can I work just as hard as I'm working now and generate two, five, or even ten times the revenue?" (Dance studio: kids @ "$250 for six classes" → corporate team-building "$10,000 is a steal for that kind of value.")

> The six value categories: "1. Making Money... 2. Saving Money... 3. Reducing Frustrations... 4. Gaining Status... 5. Creating Connection... 6. Offering Simplicity."

> "let's make the fire bigger by pouring gas on what's already burning!"

> "Kill your darlings... If you can cut products and streamline your product offering, do it today."

> Product brief: "the purpose of a product brief is to create doubt. Doubt, it turns out, is your friend... Put your products through the wind tunnel of the product brief before launching those products."

## Management & Productivity (Pattern 6 / Wf 05)

> "For most small businesses, out-of-control overhead comes from a single place: labor."

> "the best way to decrease the size of the body of your airplane is not to decrease it at all, but to make the rest of the airplane larger while your payroll stays exactly the same."

> The five meetings: "the All-Staff, Leadership, Department Stand-up, Personal Priority Speed Check, and Quarterly Performance Review meetings."

> The three leader types: "the artist, the entrepreneur, and the operator... The person you are looking for who can install the Management and Productivity Made Simple Playbook will be an operator. If you hand this assignment to an artist, they will forget to show up at 75 percent of the meetings."

> COVID proof: "When our small business should have crash-landed into a cattle pond in Kansas, we grew by more than 20 percent in revenue and nearly 30 percent in profit. We didn't lay off a single team member."

## Five-Account Cash Flow (Pattern 7 / Wf 03)

> "Manage your small business cash flow using five checking accounts." (Operating, Personal, Business Profit, Tax, Investment Holding.)

> "You must live off of a fixed salary so the business can establish a predictable rhythm, allowing you to manage and grow the business."

> High-water marks: "if your payroll is $25K every two weeks, you never want your account to go below $35K or so... take the excess... and split it between your Business Profit Account and your Tax Account." And: "your Business Profit Account should be about six times your Operating Account's high-water mark... a reserve fuel tank that will allow you six months' worth of circling the airport in the event of a crisis."

> "Your business is a machine that makes you money so you can buy another machine that makes you money."

> Profit First credit + tweak: "If you're familiar with Mike Michalowicz and his book Profit First, you'll see a resemblance... I take the profit that 'boils over' a high-water mark in my Operating Expense Account."
