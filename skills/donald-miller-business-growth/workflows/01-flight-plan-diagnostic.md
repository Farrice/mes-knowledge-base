# 01 — Flight-Plan Diagnostic

The orchestrating tool for this skill. You can only fix a business if you have a **standard** to compare it to. Miller's standard is a commercial airplane with six critical parts. This diagnostic does the "fast comparison that reveals weaknesses" — scoring each part, checking proportion, detecting the S-curve, and routing to the fix.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Run this when:
- A business is growing but "everything feels heavy" / the owner is firefighting (classic S-curve).
- Revenue is fine but profit, cash, or morale is not — and nobody can say which part is the problem.
- Before any big hire, expansion, or investment — check whether the plane stays in proportion.
- As the entry point to the whole skill — it routes to workflows 02–06 and to the messaging skills.

**Do NOT** use this to diagnose a single *message* — that's the messaging skills. This diagnoses the *business as a machine*.

## Skill Acquisition

Load `genius.md` Patterns 1 (Airplane Standard), 2 (Rule of Proportion), 3 (S-Curve) + the Quality Rubric. Have the other Don Miller skills' names handy for routing the engines.

## Execution

**Step 1 — Score the six parts (1–10 each):**
| Part | Business function | Healthy looks like | Owned by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cockpit** | Leadership / mission | 3 measurable economic priorities, team knows the mission | Wf 02 |
| **Right engine** | Marketing | clear message, collateral converts | `storybrand` / `cognitive-load` |
| **Left engine** | Sales | a repeatable pitch that closes | `messaging-evolution` Wf 02 / `storybrand` |
| **Wings** | Products | profitable + in-demand, ranked | Wf 04 |
| **Body** | Overhead / operations | lean, labor aimed at the 3 priorities | Wf 05 |
| **Fuel tanks** | Cash flow | 5 accounts, reserve fund, fixed salary | Wf 03 |

**Step 2 — Apply the rule of proportion.** A part isn't "good" or "bad" in isolation — it's *in or out of proportion* to the rest. A huge body (overhead) with weak engines crashes. Big wings (products) with no engine to sell them don't fly. Flag every part that's grown out of proportion — especially **body > engines** (the most common crash: hiring before sales can carry it). Watch for *"looking successful without being successful"* — a beautiful brand + expensive office on weak engines.

**Step 3 — Detect the S-curve.** Ask: is the owner still in their **sweet spot**, or pulled out into firefighting? Is growth being *managed* or is the owner *managing problems*? If the founder is in meetings all day instead of creating the magic that grew the company, the brand is on the S-curve and the cure is to *professionalize the operation* (install the systems in Wf 02/05).

**Step 4 — Name the one part crashing the plane.** Like any postmortem, a business usually fails for one of six reasons. Identify the single weakest, most-out-of-proportion part — that's the priority. *"If a flight crew is extremely good to their customers but the plane runs out of gas, the story ends in tragedy all the same."*

**Step 5 — Route.** Output the diagnosis + the one workflow/skill to run next:
- Cockpit weak → Wf 02 (Business on a Mission)
- Engines weak → **route OUT** to `storybrand`/`cognitive-load` (marketing) or `messaging-evolution` Wf 02 (sales). This skill does not fix engines.
- Wings weak → Wf 04 (Product Optimization)
- Body heavy → Wf 05 (Management & Productivity)
- Fuel low → Wf 03 (Five-Account Cash Flow) — **start here if cash is the fire.**

## Content Type Adaptations

| Business shape | Diagnostic emphasis |
|---|---|
| Solopreneur / side hustle | Tiny plane — cockpit + one engine + fuel; skip body/meetings until you hire |
| Scaling past ~$1M, firefighting | S-curve detection + body-vs-engine proportion |
| Funded startup | "Looking successful without being successful" — body bloat before engines |
| Multi-product / retail | Wings first (rank products) + proportion of marketing spend to profit |
| Service / agency | Engines + fuel tanks; wings = productized offers (route to Wf 04) |

## Output Requirements

- **Six-part scorecard** (1–10 each, with the business function named).
- **Proportion flags** — which parts are out of balance, especially body > engines.
- **S-curve verdict** — in the sweet spot, or firefighting?
- **The one part crashing the plane**, named.
- **Routing line** — the single next workflow/skill, with the engine parts routed OUT to the messaging skills.

## Quality Gate

Reject the diagnosis if (per `genius.md`):
- It grades the business generically without scoring against the six-part standard.
- It scores a part in isolation, ignoring proportion to the rest.
- It tries to fix the engines (marketing/sales) here instead of routing to the messaging skills.
- It names no single priority part (a diagnosis that can't be acted on).
