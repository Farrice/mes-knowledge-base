# 07 — Entry-Offer & Product-Ladder Playbook

The wings, extended downward. Wf 04 optimizes the products you HAVE; this workflow builds the **bottom rung and the ladder** — the low-friction entry offer that converts leads into buyers and funds its own lead generation, plus the ascension path that turns $49 buyers into high-ticket clients. Source: Miller's "$400K independent marketing business" series (video 4, "Entry Offers That Close Themselves") — the mechanics generalize to any small business with a high-value core offer.

> **Miller's goal statement**: "Give real value and create appetite for more." An entry offer is a *paid lead generator* — valuable on its own, priced to remove friction (impulse-buy range), a taste of the core work, a stepping stone to the bigger offer. It is NOT throwaway bait, not so cheap it feels worthless, not the full service, and not create-once-and-forget.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Run this when:
- There is a high-ticket core offer but no low-friction way for a stranger to become a *buyer* (the list is all free-lead, zero-buyer).
- Paid ads are unaffordable as pure spend — the owner needs acquisition to pay for itself.
- Leads stall between "downloaded the free thing" and "bought the expensive thing" (missing rung).
- Revenue goal requires upsells the current single-product structure can't produce.

**Not this workflow**: ranking/cutting the existing catalog (→ Wf 04); the free lead generator and nurture *copy* (engines — route out, see hand-offs below).

## Skill Acquisition

Load `genius.md` Pattern 5 (Product Optimization — six value categories, product brief) + Pattern 2 (Rule of Proportion). The entry offer must still pass the **product brief** gate (Wf 04 Step 5) before build.

## Execution

**Step 1 — Design the entry offer.** Criteria: valuable standalone, impulse-buy priced, a taste of the core work, a stepping stone. Miller's pricing anchor: **charge ~10% of the value delivered** ("if I'm charging 49 bucks, I try to estimate that it's $500 worth of value"). Menu by delivery model:
- **Digital/passive** (create once, sell forever): mini-course (5–10 short videos, one skill, $49–150), paid newsletter archive ($49), template pack ($49–99), industry-specific system ($49–199), personalized assessment/report (make the result feel worth the price — no gimmick quizzes).
- **Done-with-you** ($100–200): 30-minute audit call, screen-share website/copy review, template walkthrough.
- **Done-for-you** (no less than $300; typically $400+): one-page sales letter, welcome email sequence, lead-generator build.

**Step 2 — Wire the self-funding lead machine.** The math: ~$30 ad spend acquires a buyer of a $49 product → ad cost covered + ~$19 margin + a *paying* lead on the list. Miller reinvests **the entire entry-offer revenue back into ads** — the list grows at zero net cost and every name on it is pre-qualified for high-ticket. Platform fit: Meta (B2C/local, visual, proven for low-ticket), Google (intent searches), YouTube (trust-first, educational), LinkedIn (B2B, pricier clicks but qualified). **Start with one platform, master it, then expand.** Expected funnel shape (Miller's example): ~1,000 video views → ~75 lead-gen downloads → ~7 entry-offer buyers.

**Step 3 — Package industry-specific variants.** "People love to read their own name." Formula: **"The [Industry] [Outcome] System That Works"** — dental practice marketing system, chiropractic growth blueprint. Content stays ~90% identical; the packaging says *for you only*. Side effect Miller observed repeatedly: variant testing reveals a niche, and the business profitably collapses into it (a guide went $100K→$400K serving ONLY executive coaches). Treat variants as niche-discovery probes, not just SKUs.

**Step 4 — Exploit the commitment psychology deliberately.** Why the small sale matters (Miller's three mechanisms): **commitment & consistency** (Cialdini — a $49 purchase installs the self-image "I'm the kind of person who invests in this," making $500/$5K/$50K consistent, not a leap); **sunk cost** (people who pay, pay attention — buyers consume and implement); **the trust ladder** (a loved $49 product makes the next rung feel natural). "People who invest in you, even a little, usually chase that investment." Every entry offer therefore needs a designed NEXT rung and an explicit invitation to it.

**Step 5 — Build the product ladder with the 10x rule.** Miller: **"10% of your customers will pay you 10 times what they've already paid you"** — but only if the rung exists. Reverse-engineer the revenue goal into rungs (e.g., $49 entry → $500 workshop/package → $5K project → $50K retainer/engagement), each rung a product brief-gated offer in one of the six value categories. Automation: when a buyer purchases, **switch their email track** — off the lead-gen nurture, onto the sequence selling the next rung. Missing rungs = the 10% have nowhere to climb; that's forfeited revenue, not customer frugality.

**Step 6 — Position the owner as strategist, not implementer.** Miller's AI stance: use AI to build products in hours ("a mathematician using a calculator") but sell the **controlling idea and the campaign**, not the artifact — "AI is not going to get the messaging right... that's where the money is." The entry offer demonstrates judgment; the ladder sells increasing access to it.

## Output Requirements

- **Entry-offer spec**: deliverable, delivery model, price at ~10% of delivered value, standalone-value statement, and the appetite it creates for the next rung.
- **Self-funding math**: target CPA vs entry price, reinvestment rule, one starting ad platform with rationale, expected funnel ratios.
- **2–3 industry variants** named by the formula, with the niche-discovery hypothesis each tests.
- **Product ladder map**: every rung priced, 10x spacing sanity-checked, next-rung invitation defined per rung, email track-switching noted.
- **Completed product brief** (Wf 04 Step 5) for the entry offer before build.

## Quality Gate

Reject if (per `genius.md`):
- The entry offer is bait — it wouldn't be worth the price with no upsell attached.
- Pricing ignores the 10%-of-value anchor (too cheap to respect, too dear to impulse-buy).
- Ad spend is treated as a cost center instead of wired to reinvest entry revenue (no self-funding loop).
- The ladder has a >10x gap or a missing rung between entry and core offer.
- Buyers stay on the same email sequence after purchasing (no track switch).
- The entry offer skipped the product brief (no manufactured doubt).
- Marketing/sales copy is being written here instead of routed to the messaging skills (engine hoarding).
