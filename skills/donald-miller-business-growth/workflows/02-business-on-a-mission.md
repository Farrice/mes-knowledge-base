# 02 — Business on a Mission

The cockpit. The leader's primary job is to *"clearly define a destination and then reverse-engineer a plan to get there"* — and to keep repeating it. This workflow produces the **Guiding Principles** one-pager: a Mission Statement, Key Characteristics, and Critical Actions that create *narrative traction* and align the team.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Run this when:
- The team can't recite the mission, or "everybody is busy but the business isn't growing."
- Priorities are improvised; the owner is "diving for dollars" with no long-term direction.
- Hiring is a guess (no defined characteristics to hire for).
- The cockpit scored weak in the flight-plan diagnostic (Wf 01).

**Do NOT** confuse this with `donald-miller-culture-turnaround` — that fixes a *losing* culture via messaging. This *installs the foundational guiding-principles system* for any business. (Run culture-turnaround if the culture is actively broken; run this to set the destination.)

## Skill Acquisition

Load `genius.md` Pattern 4 (Business on a Mission) + the "forgettable mission" anti-pattern. Cross-load `donald-miller-storybrand/genius.md` for the story-loop mechanism (narrative traction).

## Execution

**Step 1 — Mission Statement, part one: three economic priorities (X).** Identify the three *measurable, numeric* ways the business will win. *"If you prioritize everything, you prioritize nothing"* — cap at three (the brain's limit); batch categories if you sell many SKUs (e.g., "sell 35 bags of dog food/day"). Lead measures (email opt-ins, applications) count if they reliably lead to sales. Each must be specific and drive revenue *and* profit. *"We will sell 100 units of X, 300 of Y, 50 of Z."*

**Step 2 — Part two: a deadline (Y).** Every important assignment gets a deadline; a mission without one creates no urgency. Keep it **under ~18–24 months** — humans don't feel urgency about a future that "belongs to somebody else." All three priorities share the same deadline; re-write the mission when you hit it (missions are *meant* to end).

**Step 3 — Part three: a "because" (Z).** The "because" is what turns a goal into a mission — *"a vision of a better world"* or *"a counterattack against an injustice."* It's the part that invites the team's whole heart. *"...because everybody deserves to come home to a lawn they love."* Assemble: **"We will accomplish X by Y because of Z."**

**Step 4 — Key Characteristics (3).** The specific traits you and the team must *embody* to accomplish the mission — better than "core values" because they're concrete and aspirational. "Calm under pressure," not "integrity." Ask: what traits do we need to make the product, to persist through challenge, to build a safe culture? Make them so specific they guide hiring (ask candidates for *evidence* of the trait, not whether they "have it").

**Step 5 — Critical Actions (3).** Three habits **nearly every** team member can do **nearly every day** that move the mission AND define the culture. Restaurant: "ask every table if they've tried our desserts." Real estate: "sign a thank-you card for every closing." These create the daily narrative-traction toward the economic priorities.

**Step 6 — Output the Guiding Principles one-pager + the repetition plan.** The point isn't the page; it's *repetition* — open All-Staff meetings with it, honor team members who live the Characteristics, paint it on the wall, ask new hires why the mission matters to them. *"If you and your team can't remember the Mission Statement, you can't remember the mission."*

## Content Type Adaptations

| Business shape | Mission emphasis |
|---|---|
| Solopreneur | Personal Critical Actions ("I call every new customer," "1 IG post/day of real value") |
| Multi-department | Same 3 company priorities; each dept reverse-engineers its Critical Actions |
| Nonprofit | "Because" carries the weight; economic priorities = funds raised / families served |
| Retail (many SKUs) | Batch economic priorities into categories |
| Agency / B2B | Economic priorities = retainers/clients; Characteristics = consultative traits |

## Output Requirements

- **Mission Statement**: "We will accomplish [3 economic priorities] by [deadline] because of [reason]."
- **3 Key Characteristics**, specific and aspirational (hireable evidence for each).
- **3 Critical Actions**, doable by nearly everyone nearly every day.
- **The Guiding Principles one-pager** + a repetition/communication plan.

## Quality Gate

Reject if (per `genius.md`):
- The economic priorities aren't measurable, or there are more than three.
- There's no deadline, or it's open-ended / >2 years.
- There's no "because" — it's a goal, not a mission.
- Key Characteristics are generic ("integrity"); Critical Actions aren't daily/universal.
- The mission opens no story loop (vague = forgettable = forgotten).
