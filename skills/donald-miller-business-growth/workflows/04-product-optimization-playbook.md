# 04 — Product-Optimization Playbook

The wings. Engines (marketing/sales) create thrust, but the **wings** (profitable, in-demand products) create lift. Most owners grow by *adding* products; the faster lever is *optimizing the offering for profit.* This is the highest-ROI move most owners forget they have.

## Pre-Flight Gate

Run this when:
- The owner doesn't know which products actually make money.
- Growth is assumed to require a new location / more SKUs (bloating the body) when the offering could be optimized instead.
- A new product idea is on the table (run the product brief before building).
- The wings scored weak in the flight-plan diagnostic (Wf 01).

**For the *positioning* question** — whether a new product blurs the lane / floods the river — route to `donald-miller-messaging-evolution/04-lane-discipline-diagnostic`. This workflow is the *profit/operations* side; that one is the *messaging/positioning* side. Run both for a major expansion.

## Skill Acquisition

## Execution

**Step 1 — Rank every product by true profitability.** Not "cost of goods sold" — the difference between price and the *full* cost to produce, market, support, and (if perishable) carry unsold. List products (top ~50 for retailers; this covers 50–80% of revenue), rank most→least profitable. This reveals where the money *actually* comes from — often a surprise.

**Step 2 — Sell more of what's already working.** Most businesses haven't saturated their market. Before building anything new, pour marketing/sales energy on the top-ranked products — *"make the fire bigger by pouring gas on what's already burning."* End-cap the profitable dog food; feature it in the next email; have the team mention it to every walk-in.

**Step 3 — Kill your darlings.** Cut low-profit / low-demand products that drag the wing — *unless* they're genuine loss-leaders that pull profitable purchases (the gum that sells the soda). *"If you can cut products and streamline your product offering, do it today."*

**Step 4 — Add high-value products (the six categories).** Ask the optimization question: *"How can I do the same work and provide 2x, 5x, or 10x the value?"* (dance studio: kids @ $250/6wks → corporate team-building filmed @ $10K). People always pay a premium in six categories: **making money, saving money, reducing frustration, gaining status, creating connection, offering simplicity.** Package as **subscriptions** (recurring), **certifications** (duplicate your expertise), or **bundles/package deals** (solve a whole problem in one purchase — "New Puppy Package").

**Step 5 — Gate every new product through the product brief.** *"The purpose of a product brief is to create doubt. Doubt is your friend."* Before building, interrogate: name + does it confuse the market? problem solved + benefit + features? who's it for + do we have access to them? proven demand + survey + competition + price? profitable + cost to build/maintain? does it interfere with existing revenue or bloat overhead? sales projections + key dates. Put the product through this "wind tunnel" before bolting the wing on.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Business shape | Product emphasis |
|---|---|
| Retail (many SKUs) | Rank top 50; end-cap winners; batch-cut the bottom |
| Service / consulting | Productize into packages; certification to scale beyond your hours |
| Single-product | Subscription + bundle to raise value without new SKUs |
| Time-bound expert (planner, coach) | "Do-it-yourself" cohort product (the wedding-planner move): 1→15 clients, same hours |
| Reseller / commodity | Subscription delivery (simplicity) is the value-add you control |

## Output Requirements

- **Profitability ranking** of the current offering (true cost, not COGS).
- **"Pour gas" list** — top products to push harder before adding anything.
- **Kill-your-darlings list** — products to cut (minus genuine loss-leaders).
- **New-product proposals** mapped to the six value categories + a packaging model (subscription/certification/bundle).
- **A completed product brief** for any new product before build.

## Quality Gate

Reject if (per `genius.md`):
- Products aren't ranked by *true* profit (using COGS or gut alone).
- It jumps to "add a new product" without first selling more of the winners and cutting the losers.
- A new product is green-lit without the product brief (no manufactured doubt).
- The new offer doesn't sit in one of the six value categories at a premium.
- It ignores the positioning side (route a major expansion to `messaging-evolution/04`).
