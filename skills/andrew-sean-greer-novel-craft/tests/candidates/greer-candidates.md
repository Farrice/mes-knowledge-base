## FIC-01
At the funeral, Mara could not remember a single story her mother had told about herself. She remembered instead the small white birds her mother left in hotel bathrooms: towels pinched into necks, washcloth wings spread over the sink.

The key was in the sugar bowl. `214`, stamped so hard the metal had risen on the other side.

Room 214 held no lover, no money, no second family. It held a closet. Inside, sheets patched with squares from other sheets; pillowcases whose hems had been unpicked and patiently done again; a ledger with dates and room numbers but no explanation. Mara ran one repair between finger and thumb. It was nearly invisible. That was not the same as being gone.

At home, she opened the trash and removed the blouse with the torn cuff. She did not know why her mother had mended hotel linen in secret. She only knew the first stitch was ugly, and kept it.

## FIC-02
Three weeks before Eli moved out, the apartment began keeping weather.

The bathroom mirror stayed fogged long after either of them had showered. Noor found a wet ring beneath Eli's empty coffee cup and wiped it without asking whether the mark was his. At night the refrigerator clicked on and the kitchen window clouded at one corner, a thumbprint of rain that never reached the glass outside.

They divided the books, the pans, the good lamp. They did not divide the blue bowl because neither could remember buying it.

After Eli carried the last box downstairs, Noor stood at the sink with the blue bowl in both hands. A small rain cloud entered the kitchen. It paused above the place where his chair had been, darkened, and went after him.

## DOM-03
The product did not begin with a market revelation. It began with a spreadsheet row called `Friday rescue`.

The founder had missed three vendor invoices in one month. The first prototype did one unglamorous thing: it highlighted invoices due within seven days. That is the whole verified origin so far. There is no customer or revenue event yet—only a recurring Friday problem made visible enough to build against.

## DOM-04
The meeting ends, but one sentence keeps following you: “I’ll send that today.” Then the tabs multiply. The transcript sits in one place, your promise in another. By tomorrow, “today” has become a small archaeological problem.

This tool turns the transcript into a draft follow-up, so the loose ends appear together: what was asked, what you said, what still needs a date. It does not send the message for you. A human reviews every draft before send, because a transcript can preserve words without understanding the room. It cannot know that “fine” meant approval, hesitation, or the moment everyone wanted lunch.

Read it. Correct it. Decide what should leave your name. Let the software gather the pieces; keep the judgment. Try the demo.

# The themes hiding in the fourth interview

Interview notes rarely announce the pattern. One person calls it delay. Another describes a workaround. By the fourth conversation, the same problem is wearing four different coats.

This product organizes interview notes into themes so you can inspect recurrence without pretending the software has understood your market. You must review every theme. No buyer, revenue, or outcome event exists yet.
