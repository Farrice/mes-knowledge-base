# Dan Wang Literary Analysis — Cross-Domain Patterns

> How this expert's genius transfers outside the annual-China-letter into the
> domains Farrice actually ships in. Load on-demand when adapting Wang's method
> to social media, marketing, copy, ghostwriting, entrepreneurship, or content
> strategy. Read `genius.md` first — this file *applies* those patterns; it does
> not restate them.

## The Portable Core (what actually transfers)

Wang's genius is **not** about China. It is a method for *how insight is made and
delivered*. Strip the subject and five engines remain, and all five are
domain-agnostic:

1. **Friction mining** (Pattern 4) — the gap between the official/stated story and
   the observed/ground truth IS the content. No gap, no reason to publish.
2. **Texture-grounded abstraction** (Patterns 3, 8) — every claim opens on a
   specific observed detail; every detail ladders up to an idea. Neither half
   stands alone.
3. **The one coherent voice** (Decision Framework Q6) — the personal register and
   the analytical register are the *same* mind oscillating, never two authors
   bolted together.
4. **Big-questions-first** (Pattern 17) — name the 2-3 tensions the audience is
   actually arguing about *this period*, then stake a real answer, not a survey.
5. **Radical infrequency × radical quality** (Pattern 20) — one considered,
   re-readable, cornerstone piece beats ten reactive ones. Quality is the moat.

Two non-negotiable guardrails travel with the method into every domain:
- **The honesty spine.** The ground-truth column / the gateway texture must be
  *real* (AN-2, AN-3, AN-6). A fabricated "I watched a customer..." is the AI-slop
  move wearing Wang's coat. A dry true line beats a vivid false one — *every*
  domain below inherits this.
- **Dissolve the scaffold.** Never label the moves ("the official story says X
  but..."). The reader *feels* the friction and the zoom; they never see the
  columns. This is the difference between Wang-grade and template-wearing-vocab.

The anti-pattern that recurs in *every* domain: **insight-porn** (AN-4) — one
anecdote → one takeaway, run as a repeatable formula, sourced from a single voice.
Wang's cure is always the same: triangulate (literature + several voices + data +
observation) and make insights converge across 2-3 independent observations before
they earn a thesis.

## Domain 1: Social Media (X / LinkedIn)

**What the Wang genius does here.** Turns a generic "take" into a textured,
friction-driven observation that rewards a second read — the post people save and
quote rather than skim and forget. The platform's gravity pulls toward ever-more-
frequent, ever-shorter, ever-more-reactive output (Pattern 18); Wang's move is to
*bring down the average* (Pattern 20) — one considered post beats ten hot takes.

**Constraint of the medium.** One oscillation, not five. One gateway texture, one
lift, one anchor line. More machinery than that and the scaffold shows at this
length.

**Before → After**

> **BEFORE (generic LinkedIn take — AN-1, dry):**
> "Most companies say they're data-driven, but the data often isn't actually
> used in decisions. Leaders need to build a culture where data informs strategy
> rather than just decorating slide decks."

> **AFTER (Wang-grade — friction + texture + anchor):**
> "The dashboard had 47 metrics. In the Q3 review, the VP scrolled past all of
> them in four seconds and made the call on a gut read of one Slack thread.
> Nobody flinched, because everyone in the room knew the dashboard was the
> *liturgy*, not the decision. We don't have a data problem. We have a data
> theater problem — and the tell is that the people who built the dashboard
> aren't the people who get listened to."

*Why it transfers:* opens on the well-traveled detail (47 metrics, four seconds,
the Slack thread — Pattern 3), mines the official-story/ground-truth gap (data-
driven vs. data theater — Pattern 4), and lands on the insider-uncomfortable
anchor sentence (Pattern 6). Irony over earnestness (Pattern 13). Recognition
anchoring is available for the second-person variant ("you've sat in that
review" — Pattern 16).

**Workflow path:** `/wang-friction-map` (one gap, not five) → `/wang-anchor-sentence`
→ `/wang-texture-zoom` (single oscillation). For zeitgeist-timed posts route the
friction through `/wang-big-questions` first.

## Domain 2: Marketing / Brand

**What the Wang genius does here.** Replaces category-cliché positioning with
*friction*: what the category SAYS vs. what customers actually EXPERIENCE — the gap
is the campaign. Plus the cover-photo strategy (Tacit 7): reject the category's
visual/verbal defaults; choose the evocative true image/line that captures the
real thesis, not the expected trope.

**The critical honesty-spine inversion for marketing.** When you run the friction
map on your *own* brand and the ground-truth column indicts your *own* product, the
matrix just told you to fix the product before you write the copy. The ground-truth
column must be *true of your product*, not aspirational.

**Before → After**

> **BEFORE (category-cliché positioning — AN-1):**
> "Premium, clinically-backed supplements for your wellness journey. Trusted by
> thousands. Feel the difference."

> **AFTER (Wang-grade — friction becomes positioning):**
> "Your last three supplements sold you a clinical study you were never going to
> read. So did we, once. Then we read ours — n=24, in-house funded, placebo-tied
> by week eight — and stopped selling the study. Here's what's actually true:
> the morning scoop is a *ritual*, and the ritual is worth keeping. We'll sell
> you that, and we'll show you the paper."

*Why it transfers:* steelmans the category story, grounds the ground truth in
observed texture (the n=24 paper's own tables — Pattern 4 + Pattern 3), and turns
the friction into the position. The disappointment is productive, not contemptuous
(Pattern 21) — the ritual is *genuinely good*, sell *that* honestly. The cover-
photo strategy applies to the visual: not the stock-photo serene woman, but the
actual scoop on the actual counter at 6am.

**Workflow path:** `/wang-friction-map` (category-says vs. customer-experiences)
→ `/wang-observation-engine` run on *customer fieldwork* (anchors = moments in the
customer's real day; the said-vs-done friction IS the insight) → `/wang-anchor-
sentence` for the line on the page. Pairs with `godin-false-proxy-purge` (the
official story is often a false proxy the whole category measures).

## Domain 3: Copywriting

**What the Wang genius does here.** Grounds every benefit in a concrete, sensory,
observed moment instead of an abstract claim (texture over feature-list), and mines
the friction the prospect *lives but the category won't admit*. Recognition
anchoring (Pattern 16) is the copy-native move: second-person, name the private
moment the reader is currently living, then lift.

**Before → After**

> **BEFORE (feature-list copy — AN-1):**
> "Our platform reduces operational overhead and streamlines your workflow,
> saving you valuable time so you can focus on what matters most."

> **AFTER (Wang-grade — texture-grounded recognition):**
> "It's 2:14am and you're answering the same email you answered last Tuesday,
> because the answer lives in your head and nowhere else, and the head is awake.
> You've built a company that can't run an hour without you and called it
> indispensability. It isn't. It's a bottleneck you happen to like the feeling
> of. The platform doesn't save you time. It makes the 2am email someone else's
> 9am — and that's a different life, not a faster one."

*Why it transfers:* the abstraction ("operational overhead") becomes the lived 2am
scene (Pattern 3, recognition anchoring Pattern 16). The friction the prospect
won't say out loud — *you secretly like being indispensable* — is the angle (AN-3
gate: would an insider find this uncomfortable-but-true?). The anchor line ("a
different life, not a faster one") is built to be quoted. Note: avoid the banned
"It's not X, it's Y" literal construction — let the idea emerge from the detail.

**Workflow path:** `/wang-texture-zoom` (copy variant — ground each benefit in a
lived moment, recognition-anchoring pairing) → `/wang-friction-map` (the thing the
last three vendors won't say) → `/wang-anchor-sentence` (build the page around one
quotable line). Pairs with `luke-iha-copy-blocks` / `copy-engine` for the
conversion architecture; Wang supplies the *texture and the friction*, those supply
the *structure*.

## Domain 4: Ghostwriting

**What the Wang genius does here.** Finds and sustains the *client's* one coherent
voice across the analytical and personal registers — oscillating the zoom without
ever sounding like two authors. This is the hardest constraint: the texture beats
and the idea beats must be the *same* author rising and falling, never your
showpieces inserted into their mouth. Influence, never imitation.

**The ghostwriting-specific discipline.** Run friction-mapping and the observation
engine *in the client's outsider position* — what can *they* observe in their world
that the insiders have normalized? The disappointment (Pattern 21) must be the
*client's* genuine standard, not yours. The grounding details must be ones the
client would actually have noticed, in language the client would use.

**Before → After**

> **BEFORE (generic thought-leadership ghostwriting — voice-less, AN-1):**
> "As leaders, we must embrace change and foster innovation. The companies that
> win tomorrow are the ones building adaptive cultures today. Here's what I've
> learned about staying ahead."

> **AFTER (Wang-grade — the client's real position, grounded):**
> "I spent eleven years on the floor before I ever sat in a strategy meeting, and
> the first thing I noticed in that room was that nobody had spent eleven years on
> the floor. They had a slide about 'frontline empathy.' I had the actual
> callus. The gap between the slide and the callus is the entire reason our
> retraining program failed twice before it worked — and why I now hire for the
> callus and teach the slide."

*Why it transfers:* the analytical point (frontline knowledge can't be
abstracted) arrives through the client's *own* observed texture (the floor, the
callus, the slide — Pattern 3) in the client's plain register, not a writer's
literary flourish. The friction (slide vs. callus — Pattern 4) is the client's
genuine, hard-won disappointment (Pattern 21). One coherent voice: the personal
("eleven years on the floor") and the analytical ("the gap is the reason it
failed") are obviously the same person.

**Workflow path:** `/wang-observation-engine` (client's immersion + outsider angle)
→ `/wang-friction-map` (client's industry insiders have normalized) →
`/wang-texture-zoom` (ghostwriting variant — the two-author seam fix IS the whole
job) → `/wang-musical-pass` *only* in the client's register, never a literary
overlay. Pairs with `ghostwriting-voice` / `nicolas-cole-sentence-craft`.

## Domain 5: Entrepreneurship / Operator Writing

**What the Wang genius does here.** The annual-letter form, ported to a
company/founder/year: texture-grounded, big-questions-first, contingency-honest
reflection that builds founder authority through radical infrequency. One
considered letter a year — accumulated across twelve months of notes (Pattern 5),
synthesized at a forcing-function deadline (Tacit 2) — answering the questions
actually animating your market, written from productive disappointment with the
status quo.

**The operator-specific power move: contingency over just-so stories** (Pattern 19).
When explaining why something happened (a pivot, a near-death, a breakout), resist
the founder-mythology trap of the tidy narrative where the outcome was fated.
Reconstruct how it actually felt *before* — when nobody knew it would work. This is
what separates a credible founder letter from a survivorship-bias humblebrag.

**Before → After**

> **BEFORE (founder LinkedIn humblebrag — just-so story, AN-7):**
> "When we started, everyone said it couldn't be done. But we believed in our
> vision, stayed relentless, and three years later we hit $10M ARR. The lesson?
> Never give up on your dream."

> **AFTER (Wang-grade — contingency + friction + texture):**
> "In month fourteen we had six weeks of runway and a product nobody was paying
> for, and I want to be honest that it did not feel like destiny — it felt like a
> spreadsheet that ended in March. The thing that saved us wasn't grit. It was a
> single churned customer who told me, over a coffee I almost cancelled, that she
> didn't want our product, she wanted the *report* it generated as a byproduct.
> We sold the byproduct. The vision I'm now credited for was a thing a leaving
> customer described to me when I'd run out of my own ideas. I tell it that way
> because the version where I knew all along is a lie that would make you worse
> at this than I am."

*Why it transfers:* refuses the fated-success narrative (Pattern 19), grounds the
turn in observed texture (six weeks, the coffee, the churned customer's exact words
— Pattern 3), and the friction (the credited vision vs. the actual accident) is the
honest analytical engine (Pattern 4) charged with productive humility (Pattern 21).
This compounds reputation precisely *because* it's infrequent and true (Pattern 20).

**Workflow path:** `/wang-big-questions` (what is my market actually arguing about
this period — stake an answer) → `/wang-observation-engine` (a year of operator
fieldwork → note bank) → `literary-cornerstone-sprint` (the full annual-letter
synthesis) → `/wang-musical-pass`. Pairs with `daniel-priestley` /
`andrew-wilkinson` for the operator-authority frame.

## Domain 6: Content Strategy / Newsletters / Essays

**What the Wang genius does here.** Escapes the platform's frequency pull
(Pattern 20) and the genre's incentive pull (Pattern 18) to produce cornerstone
pieces that compound reputation. This is Wang's *native* habitat ported to a
content system: reject the piece-a-week treadmill for the considered piece; map the
format's lazy default and write against it; layer for re-readers; ship at 85%
(Tacit 1) rather than polishing to paralysis.

**The strategist-specific lens: genre-incentive mapping as a content-calendar
decision** (Pattern 18). Before committing to a format, name what it *rewards into
mediocrity* — the newsletter pulled toward "5 tips," the YouTube essay toward the
retention-hook formula, the Substack toward the hot-take cadence — and deliberately
build the flagship to transcend that pull. The flywheel can be frequent and newsy;
the *cornerstone* product is rare and poured-into.

**Before → After**

> **BEFORE (newsletter genre-default — AN-4 insight-porn formula):**
> "5 Productivity Hacks That Changed My Life
>  1. Wake up at 5am. 2. Time-block your calendar. 3. ..."
> (One anecdote → one takeaway, run five times, sourced from the author's own
> habits, indistinguishable from a thousand other newsletters.)

> **AFTER (Wang-grade — one cornerstone piece, friction-driven):**
> A single 3,000-word annual essay, "The Year I Stopped Optimizing": opens on the
> texture of a specific over-engineered morning (the 14-app stack, the cold
> plunge timed to the second), mines the friction between the productivity-culture
> official story (optimization = output) and the observed ground truth (the
> author's actual best work happened on the disorganized days), stakes a real
> contestable answer to the live question the audience is arguing about (is the
> optimization industry net-negative?), and earns a single anchor sentence the
> readers quote for a year.

*Why it transfers:* trades the treadmill of disposable tip-lists for one re-readable
cornerstone (Pattern 20), escapes the genre's "5 tips" pull (Pattern 18), grounds
the abstraction in observed texture (Pattern 3), and runs on friction (Pattern 4)
not formula (AN-4). The piece *waits to be read* and compounds authority
disproportionate to its volume.

**Workflow path:** `/wang-big-questions` (the live tension worth a cornerstone) →
`/wang-xray-read` (mine the year's source material 3x faster — fire vs. skip) →
`/wang-observation-engine` (the year's note bank) → `literary-cornerstone-sprint`
or `strategic-insight-synthesis` (the synthesis) → `/wang-texture-zoom` →
`/wang-musical-pass`. Pairs with `nicolas-cole-newsletter-flywheel` (frequent
flywheel) where Wang owns the *cornerstone* slot, not the cadence.

## The One Test That Travels Everywhere

In every domain, before shipping, ask the question from `genius.md`:

> **Would Dan Wang recognize this as his thinking — or as someone filling
> [social-post / ad / page / founder-letter] slots with his vocabulary?**

If the texture is real, the friction is genuine (an insider would find it
uncomfortable-but-accurate), the abstraction is grounded, the voice is one mind,
and the scaffold is dissolved — it transferred. If it reads as a template wearing
Wang's words, it failed, regardless of how many patterns you can name in it.
