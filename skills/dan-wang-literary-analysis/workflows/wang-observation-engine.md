# Wang Observation Engine — Meal-Structured Travel Into Insight (Dan Wang)

There is no boring place in China, Wang says — and his proof is the method, not the destination. He gets to a city by bus, plane, train, hook or crook, and then he refuses the tour bus and the highlight reel. He picks three or four places to eat, spaces them across the map so that getting from one to the next means *walking hungry through the actual city*, and the walk is the point. Chongqing stacked on its cliffs, a subway line punched straight through an apartment building, a bowl of minced-pork noodles that arrives and somehow bombards him with an idea about how the whole place works — these come from the in-between, not the itinerary. The eateries are anchors and rewards; the road between them is the instrument. And the failure he names, the one this engine exists to prevent, is the tourist who comes home radiant with personal ecstasy — the sunset, the charming market, "what is in front of them" — and not one observation that marries to an analytical point. That is travel writing's disease (AN-2). This workflow runs Wang's method as a repeatable protocol for any primary-observation gathering — travel, site visit, customer field research, immersion — and it does not let you call the day done until the notes can answer the test that separates a note bank from a photo roll: **does each observation ladder to a question, or is it just something I saw?**

## Pre-Flight

Read before executing — load these `genius.md` sections (do not paraphrase from memory; the patterns are precise, and this engine fails the instant the notes become a diary of personal ecstasy instead of observations tagged to ideas):
- **Pattern 9 — Meal-Structured Travel** — the core device. Organize the day around 3-4 eateries; walk between them; *the walking is where insight happens*. Return with observations, not photos. The whole protocol is this pattern made operational.
- **Pattern 3 — Texture Over Tectonic Plates** — the unit of capture. Every note is a specific observed detail (a meal, a street scene, an object, an overheard line), because texture is the gateway to the big idea, never a garnish on it.
- **Pattern 4 — Formal/Informal Friction Mining** — the sharpest notes are *gaps*: what the place advertises vs. what you walked past. While observing, hunt the divergence between the official story and the ground truth; those notes tag straight to the highest-value questions.
- **AN-2 — Self-Indulgent Texture With No Idea** — the failure this workflow exists to prevent. Lush detail that never ladders up to an analytical point is travel-writing self-indulgence — "people are really only thinking about what is in front of them." The tagging step is the deterministic check against AN-2.
- **Tacit 3 — Writing Retreats as Environmental Variation** — why location is not neutral. The barbecue eaten while observing becomes part of the creative substrate; environmental variation creates associative diversity. The eateries aren't pit stops — they're the day's varied environments, deliberately chosen for contrast.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` before designing the day — specifically **Q1** (can I name the 2-3 questions actually animating this — what am I out here trying to *answer*, not just see?), **Q3** (am I prepared to make every detail *earn* its place by carrying an idea, and every idea ground in something observed?), and **Q4** (where is my outsider distance — what can I see *because* I don't live here / don't run this / am not the customer?). Two hard stops: (1) if you cannot name at least one live analytical question before you set out, you are sightseeing, not gathering — you will come home with photos. Name the questions first. (2) If the plan is a list of landmarks and "things to see" rather than eateries-as-anchors with walks-as-generators, you have built a tour, not an observation engine — the insight lives in the in-between, and a landmark-hopping day skips the in-between entirely. Rebuild the route around meals and the walks they force.

## Input Required

- **The field** — the place, site, market, neighborhood, store, event, or community you're going to immerse in. One line is enough. (Generalizes: a city for travel; a competitor's stores for market research; a customer's home/workplace for ICP fieldwork; a conference floor for industry immersion.)
- **The live questions** — the 2-3 analytical questions actually animating this (Pattern 17 / Q1). What are you trying to *answer* by being here? "How does this city actually make money?" "Why do customers say they want X but buy Y?" "What does this category get wrong that everyone's normalized?" Without these, every note is orphaned.
- **The outsider angle** — what makes you *not* a native here, and therefore able to see the normalized dysfunction (Pattern 7 / Q4). Load-bearing: the sharpest notes come from this distance.
- **The eateries (or their analog)** — 3-4 anchor stops, chosen for *contrast* and *spread*, so the walks between them cross real ground. In non-travel fields these are the day's anchor environments (three competitor stores across the city; the customer's morning routine / commute / workplace; three booths at opposite ends of the floor). They must be far enough apart to force a walk.
- **Format** — essay / Substack / LinkedIn / X / marketing / copy / ghostwriting / ICP research (drives how the note bank gets harvested into prose vs. stays as the engine under the analysis).

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Name the live questions FIRST, before you design the route (Q1, the anti-photo gate)

The difference between a note bank and a photo roll is decided before you leave the house. A photo is "something I saw." A note is "something I saw *that bears on a question I'm holding*." So you must hold the questions first — they are the magnets that will make raw observation cohere into insight on the walk.

Write 2-3 questions actually animating this field right now — the ones a sharp person would argue about, not the ones with obvious answers. Then commit to *answering* them, however imperfectly (Pattern 17: "let's also try to have an answer"), not merely cataloguing them.

| A live question is… | Why | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Contested** — smart people disagree | A settled question needs no fieldwork; you'd just look it up | "Is this city big?" — answerable from Wikipedia, no walk required |
| **Answerable by observation**, at least partly | The walk has to be able to *move* it; otherwise the trip is decoration | "What is the meaning of progress?" — too abstract to be touched by a noodle shop |
| **Tied to friction** (Pattern 4) | The best questions sit on the gap between the official story and the ground | "What does this place advertise about itself that the street contradicts?" |
| Phrased as a question you'd be **embarrassed to answer glibly** | Forces real observation over confirming a prior | "Why is the metro so good here when nothing else works?" beats "Is the metro good?" |

> The Wang tell: the questions are why "there's no boring place." A place is only boring if you arrive with no question — then everything is undifferentiated scenery. Arrive with three questions and the most ordinary street corner starts answering them.

Write the questions down and **label them Q1, Q2, Q3**. Every note you capture will be tagged to one of these (or flagged as a new question the field surfaced). That tag is the whole game.

### Step 2 — Design the meal-structured day: anchors chosen for contrast, walks chosen for ground (Pattern 9 + Tacit 3)

Now build the route. Pick **3-4 eateries** (or anchor environments) and place them so the day is a series of *walks between meals*, not a meal between sights. The eateries do three jobs: they anchor the day in time, they reward and refuel the walking, and — chosen well — they are themselves contrasting environments that generate associative diversity (Tacit 3: the food becomes part of the creative substrate).

Choose the anchors for **spread** and **contrast**, then map the walks as the actual instrument:

| Anchor choice axis | What you want | Why (the Wang logic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Spread across the map** | Far enough apart to force a real walk (20-50 min on foot, not a 3-min hop) | The walk is the generator; a tight cluster gives you nothing in between |
| **Contrast in kind** | A hole-in-the-wall, a chain, a fancy place; old district vs. new | Environmental variation (Tacit 3) — contrast is what makes patterns visible |
| **Native to the place** | Where locals actually eat, not the TripAdvisor top 10 | The official-story restaurant is the painted village; the local one is the ground truth (Pattern 4) |
| **Crosses friction-rich ground** | The walk should pass through where the place's contradictions live | Chongqing's cliffs, the subway through the building — the in-between is where the idea ambushes you |

Then lay out the day as **walks, not stops** — because the walk is what you're actually here for:

| Leg | From → To | The walk crosses… | What I expect it to bear on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Anchor A → Anchor B | [the neighborhoods / transitions / contradictions the route passes] | Q_ |
| 2 | Anchor B → Anchor C | … | Q_ |
| 3 | Anchor C → Anchor D | … | Q_ |

> **The design discipline:** if your route is mostly *being at* the anchors and barely *walking between* them, you've designed a restaurant crawl, not an observation engine. Invert it: the meals are short (eat, watch, note, go); the walks are long and slow and eyes-up. Wang's insight bombards him *en route* — the noodles are the anchor, the city between is the text.

### Step 3 — Capture raw observations as TEXTURE, on the walk, in the moment (Pattern 3)

This is the legwork — the actual instrument running. While walking (and while eating, watching the room), capture observations as **specific, sensory, concrete texture** — never as conclusions. A note is a *thing you observed*, rendered precisely enough that you could rebuild the scene later. The interpretation comes in Step 4; here you are a recording instrument, and the discipline is to capture *more* and *rawer* than feels useful.

Capture each note in this unit — the raw observation first, naked, before any meaning:

| Capture field | What goes here | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **The observed thing** (texture) | The concrete, sensory, specific detail — what you actually saw / heard / ate / smelled | "Subway line 2 exits directly into the 6th floor of an apartment block; residents walk through the station to get home" |
| **Where / when** | Anchor leg, time, exact spot — so it's traceable | "Walk leg 2, ~3pm, Liziba" |
| **Why it caught me** | The flicker of interest — *don't resolve it yet*, just mark that it pulled your eye | "Felt impossible and totally normal at once — nobody else looked up" |

Three capture rules from the method:

- **Texture, not conclusion.** Write "two of the six factory lines were running and the lot was half-empty at shift change," not "the factory is struggling." The conclusion is reversible; the texture is evidence. (This is the difference between a note that survives and a take that you'll second-guess.)
- **Capture the friction when you feel it (Pattern 4).** When what you're walking past *contradicts* what the place says about itself — the gleaming metro above the crumbling street, the "luxury" district where locals don't shop — that note is gold. Flag it. Friction notes tag to the highest-value questions.
- **Over-capture. The bus, the plane, the hook-or-crook getting there counts.** The minced-pork noodles "bombarding" Wang with an idea wasn't planned. You cannot know in the moment which detail will ladder; capture the ones that flicker, and capture them raw. The deadline (Step 6) does the cutting, not the moment.

> **The single-beautiful-sentence catch (Pattern 6):** if, on the walk, a *sentence* arrives fully formed — the way to say the thing — capture it verbatim, immediately, in its own note. These are rare and you will not reconstruct them later. The anchor line of the eventual piece is often a note you caught on a sidewalk and didn't yet understand.

### Step 4 — Tag every note toward the analytical question it might serve (the AN-2 gate)

This is the step that turns a photo roll into an engine, and it is the deterministic check against AN-2 (self-indulgent texture with no idea). For *every* note, ask the question that travel writers never ask: **what could this be evidence of?** Tag it to one of your live questions (Q1/Q2/Q3), or to a *new* question it surfaced — and if it tags to nothing, it is, for now, just something you saw.

For each note, fill the tag fields:

| Tag field | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Serves question** | Q1 / Q2 / Q3 — or **NEW-Q:** [the question this detail just raised] |
| **The ladder** (one line) | The provisional idea this texture *might* be evidence of — "the metro-through-the-building is the city solving for vertical density the way the West never had to" |
| **Friction?** | Yes/no — does it sit on a gap between official story and ground truth (Pattern 4)? |
| **Disposition** | KEEP (ladders to a live Q) · PARK (interesting, no question yet — hold) · ECSTASY (pure personal pleasure, no idea — flag, likely cut) |

The **ECSTASY flag is the AN-2 trip-wire.** When a note is "the sunset was unbelievable" / "best meal of my life" / "the market was so charming" with no idea attached — that is the exact failure Wang names. You do not delete it (the food is substrate, Tacit 3), but you flag it honestly: it is personal ecstasy, not an observation tagged to insight. A note bank that is mostly ECSTASY means you sightsaw; a note bank that is mostly KEEP means the engine ran.

> **The PARK column matters too.** Some textures flicker hard but tag to no current question — *don't force them onto a Q* (that manufactures a fake ladder). Park them. Often a parked note plus a later note suddenly form a pattern that *is* a new question. Wang's year-long note file (Pattern 5) is one giant PARK lot where, at the deadline, parked notes finally find their questions.

### Step 5 — Scan the bank for laddered patterns: where do 2+ notes converge? (Pattern 8)

A single note is an anecdote; the insight-porn risk (AN-4) is building a whole take on one. The engine's real output is **convergence** — where two or three independent notes, from different anchors and different walks, ladder to the *same* question and start describing the same underlying pattern. That convergence is the zoom-out (Pattern 8): the texture you can trust because you saw it more than once, from more than one angle.

Group the KEEP notes by question, then look for convergence:

| Convergence test | Anecdote (weak) | Pattern (strong) |
|---|---|---|
| **How many notes ladder here?** | One note, one take | 2-3 notes from *different* anchors/walks pointing the same way |
| **Did the data and the walk agree?** | Only the walk, or only a number | The number *and* the observed scene both point at it (triangulated) |
| **Is it grounded in texture, not assertion?** | "The city feels stagnant" | "Half-empty lot + the migration number + the friend who left — all three" |
| **Does it answer a live Q, not just decorate it?** | Nice color, no answer | It moves Q1 toward an actual answer |

Keep the convergences that pass on **at least two** tests. These are the candidate insights — the few patterns the field actually surfaced, grounded in repeated texture. Note the **NEW-Q** entries here too: a question the field raised that you didn't bring is often the most valuable thing you carried home.

> **The triangulation discipline (AN-4 guard):** if every "insight" rests on one charming anecdote, you've got the Gladwellesque one-story-one-takeaway formula, not analysis. The note bank's job is to let convergence emerge across anchors so no single noodle shop has to carry a thesis alone. Read the literature and the data *too* — the walk confirms or complicates them; it doesn't replace them.

### Step 6 — Hand the bank to synthesis (what the engine becomes)

The note bank is the engine, not the essay — and crucially, you do **not** synthesize on the walk. You capture in the field; you let the notes accumulate; you force synthesis at a deadline (Pattern 5 / Tacit 2: haste creates coherence deliberation cannot). When it's time to write, hand the bank off:

- The **convergences** (Step 5) become the analytical points — each one a section, developed via the zoom (Pattern 8): open on the grounding texture, elevate to what it means, return to texture.
- The **grounding texture** (Step 3) becomes the openings — start each section on the noodle shop, the metro-through-the-building, the half-empty lot (Pattern 3, the grounding detail), never on the abstraction.
- The **friction notes** (Step 4) become the engine's edge — the gap between what the place says and what you walked past is the insight an insider would find uncomfortable but accurate.
- The **anchor sentence** caught on the sidewalk (Step 3's Pattern 6 catch) becomes the spine — the line the piece is built to earn.
- The **ECSTASY notes** stay in the drawer as substrate (the barbecue you ate while thinking) — they flavor the voice but do not get promoted to insights they cannot support.

Do **not** write the note bank into the prose as a literal field log ("on my walk I observed…"). The fieldwork is *felt*, never reported as itinerary. The reader walks the city with you and arrives at the idea; they never see the tagging matrix. Dissolve the engine into one coherent voice (the genius.md "do NOT label the moves" rule) so the noodles and the analysis read as one mind thinking.

### Worked example A — Travel essay (a second-tier Chinese megacity)

**Field:** a mountain megacity famous for being "the city of the future" / "the cyberpunk capital."
**Outsider angle:** I don't live here; I keep the boosters' narrative at arm's length and walk where the tour buses don't.
**Live questions:** Q1 — How does a city built on cliffs actually *work* day to day? · Q2 — Is the "cyberpunk future" branding the official story, and what's the ground truth under it? · Q3 — Why does the infrastructure feel a generation ahead of the institutions?

| # | Observed thing (texture) | Where/when | Serves Q | The ladder | Friction? | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Metro line 2 exits straight into the 6th floor of an apartment block; residents walk through the station turnstiles to get home; nobody looks up | Walk leg 2, ~3pm, Liziba | Q1 | The city solves vertical density by *fusing* infrastructure and housing — the West never had to | N | KEEP |
| 2 | The "cyberpunk skyline" photo spot is mobbed with tourists; 200m downhill, the actual streets are crumbling concrete and shuttered shops | Walk leg 1, noon | Q2 | The future is a *photo backdrop*; the lived city is ordinary and worn — the brand is one block deep | Y | KEEP |
| 3 | Minced-pork noodles, 9 yuan, a plastic stool on a 30-degree slope; the vendor takes mobile pay but the building behind has no elevator | Anchor B, lunch | Q3 | Leapfrogged *consumer* tech (mobile pay everywhere) bolted onto un-leapfrogged *physical* stock (walk-up tenements) | Y | KEEP |
| 4 | The river fog rolling up the cliffs at dusk — genuinely one of the most beautiful things I've seen | Walk leg 3, dusk | — | (none — it's just gorgeous) | N | ECSTASY |
| 5 | Three separate stair-streets where the "ground floor" of one street is the "6th floor" of the one below | Walk legs 1-3 | Q1 | "Ground level" is a fiction here — the city has no single datum; everything is relative elevation | N | PARK |

**Anchor-sentence catch:** "Here the metro runs *through* the apartments, the future runs *through* the slum, and nobody on the train looks up."

**Convergences:**
1. The city's "future" is a photo backdrop one block deep ← notes #2, #3 (different anchors) → moves Q2: the cyberpunk brand is the painted village; the ground truth is leapfrogged software on un-leapfrogged concrete.
2. Vertical fusion is the city's actual genius ← notes #1, #3, #5 (three anchors) → moves Q1: it solved verticality by abolishing "ground level" as a concept.
**NEW QUESTION the field surfaced:** if consumer tech leapfrogged but physical stock didn't, what *else* in China is one-block-deep future over un-renovated past?

**Handoff:** Spine = the "nobody looks up" line. Section 1 opens on the metro through the building (note #1), zooms to vertical fusion, returns to the stair-streets. Section 2 opens on the photo-spot mob vs. the crumbling street below (note #2), zooms to one-block-deep branding. ECSTASY note #4 (the fog) flavors the closing image — never carries an argument.

*Why this works:* every KEEP note ladders to a named question; the one pure-beauty note is honestly flagged ECSTASY (the AN-2 trip-wire caught it); the insights converge across *different* anchors, so no single noodle shop carries the thesis (AN-4 avoided); the friction notes (#2, #3) are the engine's edge.

### Worked example B — ICP / customer field research (a wellness app)

**Field:** the actual morning routines of three target users (a wellness app's churned segment), shadowed in their homes/commutes.
**Outsider angle:** I built the app picturing a calm, intentional morning; I've never watched a real user's actual 7am.
**Live questions:** Q1 — When in the morning is there *genuinely* a free slot for our 10-min session? · Q2 — What do users say they want (in research) vs. what they actually do? · Q3 — What's the real competitor for that slot of attention?

| # | Observed thing (texture) | Where/when | Serves Q | The ladder | Friction? | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | User opens our app at 7:02, sees the 10-min timer, closes it, opens it again at 7:04, closes it — never starts | Home, leg 1 | Q1/Q2 | The "free slot" is imaginary; mornings are interrupt-driven, not block-structured | Y | KEEP |
| 2 | The thing that *does* get 10 min every morning: scrolling Reels on the toilet | Home, leg 1 | Q3 | The real competitor isn't another wellness app — it's the bathroom doomscroll | Y | KEEP |
| 3 | In the exit survey this user wrote "I want a calm, intentional morning routine" | (prior data) | Q2 | Stated desire vs. observed behavior — classic said-done gap | Y | KEEP |
| 4 | Their kitchen is genuinely beautiful, lots of plants | Home, leg 1 | — | (none) | N | ECSTASY |
| 5 | All three users do *something* one-handed while a kid/coffee occupies the other hand | All three, mornings | Q1 | Our two-handed UI is dead on arrival; the morning is a one-handed environment | N | KEEP |

**Anchor-sentence catch:** "They told us they wanted a calm morning; we watched them give the only calm ten minutes to the toilet."

**Convergences:**
1. The morning has no free *block* — only interrupt-gaps ← notes #1, #5 (different users) → moves Q1: design for 90-second one-handed interrupts, not a 10-min two-handed block.
2. Said-done gap is total ← notes #1, #2, #3 → moves Q2/Q3: the real competitor is the doomscroll, and the stated "intentional morning" is aspiration, not behavior.
**NEW QUESTION:** can we *win the toilet scroll* slot instead of fighting for an imaginary calm block?

**Handoff (honesty spine):** the observed behavior is real, not the behavior we wished for. Spine = the "calm ten minutes to the toilet" line. The product convergence (one-handed 90-sec interrupts) goes to the build team; the copy convergence (stop selling "calm intentional mornings" no one has) goes to the messaging. ECSTASY note #4 (the pretty kitchen) is held as substrate — it tells us nothing about the slot.

*Why this works:* the engine generalizes off travel entirely — anchors are life-moments, walks are the in-between behaviors surveys miss; the friction notes (#1, #2, #3) catch the said-done gap that *is* the research payoff; the ECSTASY flag kills the irrelevant pretty detail before it becomes a fake insight; convergence across three users (not one) makes the pattern trustworthy.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Dan Wang Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Quality Rubric. Flag and fix before delivering.

- **Every note ladders to a question, or it's flagged ECSTASY (AN-2):** the deterministic check. A note bank dominated by ECSTASY entries means you came home with photos and personal pleasure, not observations — the exact failure Wang names ("people are really only thinking about what is in front of them"). Re-run with sharper live questions, or honestly mark the trip as sightseeing.
- **Questions were named FIRST (Q1):** the live questions existed *before* the route was designed. If the questions were reverse-engineered from the notes after the fact, the day was a tour and the "questions" are post-hoc rationalization. The magnets come first.
- **The day is walks-between-anchors, not anchor-hopping (Pattern 9):** the route forces real walks (the insight generator), with the eateries as anchors and rewards — not a restaurant crawl or a landmark checklist. The in-between is the text; if the plan skips it, rebuild.
- **Notes are texture, not conclusions (Pattern 3):** every observation is a specific, sensory, traceable detail ("two of six lines running"), not a reversible take ("the factory's struggling"). The texture is the evidence that survives second-guessing; the conclusion comes only at convergence.
- **Insights converge across anchors, not one anecdote (AN-4):** the candidate insights rest on 2-3 notes from *different* anchors/walks (and ideally the data too), not a single charming noodle shop carrying a thesis. One-story-one-takeaway is the Gladwell formula, not analysis. Triangulate.
- **Friction notes are present and flagged (Pattern 4):** the bank contains observed *gaps* between the official story and the ground truth — the painted village vs. the morning after. A bank with zero friction notes means you only saw what the place advertises (AN-3 risk).
- **The engine is dissolved, not narrated:** the handoff produces prose that *walks the reader to the idea*, never reports the itinerary ("on my walk I observed…") or shows the tagging matrix. The scaffold must not show — the reader feels the fieldwork, doesn't see the columns.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Coming home with a photo roll (AN-2, the headline failure).** The notes are gorgeous — the sunset, the market, the best meal — and not one of them bears on a question. You experienced the place; you didn't observe it. **Recovery:** this is decided *before* you leave, not after. Re-run Step 1 — name 2-3 contested, observation-answerable questions, make them the magnets, and on the next field day tag every note in the moment. A note that won't tag to a question is ECSTASY; flag it honestly rather than smuggling it in as insight.
- **The landmark tour wearing an engine's clothes.** The "anchors" are the famous sights and the day is hopping between highlights with cars/cabs in between — so there's no walk, and the walk was the whole instrument. You'll come back with the same photos everyone has. **Recovery:** Step 2 — invert the day. Pick eateries where *locals* eat, spread across the map; make the meals short and the walks long, slow, eyes-up. The insight ambushes you between the noodles, never at the monument.
- **Capturing conclusions instead of texture.** The notes read "the city feels stagnant," "customers seem frustrated" — reversible takes with no observed evidence under them. By the time you write, you can't trust them, because you recorded your *reaction*, not the *scene*. **Recovery:** Step 3 — for every note, render the concrete thing you actually saw/heard/ate ("the lot was half-empty at shift change"; "she opened the app twice and closed it twice"). The interpretation waits for Step 4; the field is for recording, not deciding.
- **Building a thesis on one charming anecdote (AN-4).** One vivid note becomes the whole insight — the single noodle shop made to carry a claim about an entire city or customer base. It reads compelling and it's structurally a guess. **Recovery:** Step 5 convergence test. Hold the insight only where 2-3 notes from *different* anchors (and, where possible, the data) ladder to the same pattern. If only one note supports it, demote it to color, or go back and gather more — convergence is what makes the texture trustworthy.
- **Forcing parked notes onto a question (manufacturing a fake ladder).** A note flickered hard but tags to nothing, so you bend a live question to "fit" it — and now you have a ladder that isn't real. **Recovery:** use the PARK column honestly. A parked note is not a failure; it's a note waiting for its question (Pattern 5 — the year-long file is a giant PARK lot). Often two parked notes later become a NEW-Q on their own. Don't force the rung; let the pattern arrive.
