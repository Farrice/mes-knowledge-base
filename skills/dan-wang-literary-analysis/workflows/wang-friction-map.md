# Wang Friction Map — Official Story vs. Ground Truth (Dan Wang)

The leader's motorcade passes through a village scrubbed immaculate for his pleasure — fresh paint, swept streets, planted flowers — and the morning after he leaves, the villagers haul the props back into the shed and get on with their actual lives. Both scenes are true. The press release describes the first one. The piece lives in the *gap* between them. That gap is not a flaw in your reporting to be smoothed over with "on the other hand"; it is the analytical engine — the reason the piece exists at all. Wang's entire method as a China analyst is to refuse the newscast view ("everything immaculate for the leader's pleasure") and instead map what officials *say* against what he *walks past*, then name the friction nobody else names because nobody else read both columns. This workflow runs that mapping on purpose, for any topic where the public story diverges from the lived one — and it does not let you stop until you can pass the test that separates analysis from stenography: **would an insider find this uncomfortable, and accurate?**

## Pre-Flight

Read before executing — load these `genius.md` sections (do not paraphrase from memory; the patterns are precise, and this workflow fails the instant you start restating the official story as if it were insight):
- **Pattern 4 — Formal/Informal Friction Mining** — the core device: map the official story against the ground truth; the friction between them *is* the story. The whole matrix is this pattern made operational.
- **Pattern 7 — Outsider Error-Correction** — your distance is the instrument. You see the dysfunction *because* you are not captured by the in-group that has normalized it. The wider gaps are visible only from the outside.
- **Pattern 21 — Disappointment as Engine** — the moral charge under the gap. The friction has teeth when it comes from someone who wants the thing to be *better* (this could be a Florence; why isn't it?), not someone scoring points. Wang's San Francisco: the city of the "chosen" where you hide your car keys in a drawer. Disappointment, not complaint.
- **AN-3 — Restating the Official Story as Insight** — the failure this workflow exists to prevent. If your right-hand column only repeats what officials want said, there is no friction and no insight. The output gate (the insider-discomfort test) is the deterministic check against AN-3.
- **Decision Framework — Q2 and Q4** — Q2 (where does the official story diverge from the ground truth? the gap *is* the engine) and Q4 (where is my outsider distance? what disappointment is driving this?). These are the two gates this workflow turns into a procedure.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` before building the matrix — specifically **Q2** (is there a genuine divergence between the stated story and the observed reality, or am I about to restate a press release? *no gap = no piece*) and **Q4** (do I have real outsider distance and a real disappointment driving this, or am I captured by the in-group / merely complaining?). Two hard stops: (1) if the official column and the ground-truth column say the *same thing*, there is no friction yet — keep observing, do not draft. (2) If you cannot ground the ground-truth column in something actually *observed* (a meal, a walk, a scene, a number that contradicts the narrative, a primary document), you are guessing, not mapping — that is AN-2 (self-indulgent) or AN-4 (single-source) wearing a matrix. Stop and go look.

## Input Required

- **The topic** — the subject, industry, institution, place, company, policy, or trend you're writing about. One line is enough.
- **The official story** — what the press releases / leadership / consensus / category / "everyone knows" version says is true. The newscast view. (If you don't have it crisply, that's Step 1.)
- **Ground-truth sources** — what you can actually *observe* against the official story: on-the-ground reporting, the walk, the meal, the line workers' account, the data that contradicts the narrative, the leaked memo, the customer's lived experience, the number nobody cites. Wang triangulates — read the literature, interview several, look at the data, *and* observe the ground (AN-4). At minimum, name what your right-hand column is grounded in.
- **Your outsider angle** — what makes you *not* an insider here, and therefore able to see what the captured can't (Pattern 7). Optional but load-bearing: the wider gaps surface from this distance.
- **Format** — essay / Substack / LinkedIn / X / marketing / copy / ghostwriting (drives how much of the matrix becomes prose vs. stays as the engine under the prose).

## Workflow

### Step 1 — State the official story cleanly, with no editorial (the left column)

Before you can map the friction, you have to render the official story *fairly* — at its strongest, in the words its own believers would use. Wang's move (AN-3, inverted): restate the consensus so accurately that an insider would nod, *then* find where it breaks. A strawman official story produces a fake gap; a steelman official story produces a real one.

Write the left column as a series of crisp **stated claims** — what the press release, the leadership deck, the category, or "everyone knows" asserts is true. No sarcasm, no scare-quotes yet. The contempt, if any, is earned later by the gap, not declared here.

| Render the official story as… | Why | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| The claim **its own believers** would sign | A strawman gap is a cheap gap; the friction only has force if the official story was stated at full strength | Caricaturing the consensus so your "insight" wins easy |
| **Specific** stated claims, not vibes | "The official story is good" isn't a column entry; "Q3 guidance projects 40% growth" is | Vague gestures at "the narrative" with nothing to push against |
| The version that is **partly true** | The best friction sits where the official story is *half-right* — true on the surface, false underneath | Picking a claim that's just a lie (too easy; everyone already knows) |

> The Wang tell: the official story is rarely a *lie*. It's the village painted for the motorcade — genuinely painted, genuinely there, and genuinely not how people live. State it as the true-on-the-surface thing it is.

### Step 2 — Map the ground truth beside it (the right column), grounded in something observed

For each stated claim, write what you actually *observe* against it. This is the column that requires legwork — the meal, the walk, the line worker, the number that doesn't match the narrative, the primary document, the customer who churned. The right column is where Pattern 3 (texture) and Pattern 4 (friction) meet: every ground-truth entry should point at a *specific observed thing*, not a counter-abstraction.

Lay the two columns side by side. This is the matrix:

| # | Official story (stated) | Ground truth (observed) | Grounded in (the texture) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [what they say is true] | [what you actually saw] | [the meal / walk / number / document / scene that proves it] |
| 2 | … | … | … |

- **The right column must be observed, not asserted.** A ground-truth entry with no "grounded in" is a guess. If you can't fill the third column, you don't yet have a gap — you have a hunch (Pre-Flight Gate stop #2).
- **Counter-abstraction is not ground truth.** "The official story says growth; actually it's stagnation" is two press releases arguing. "The official story says growth; the third factory I walked through had two of six lines running and a parking lot half-empty at shift change" is friction. Texture is the proof.
- **Let the data and the walk disagree with each other too.** Sometimes the numbers support the official story and the walk contradicts it (or vice versa). That internal tension is often the *most* interesting gap — note it.

### Step 3 — Measure each gap and rank by width (find the 3-5 that ARE the story)

Not all gaps are equal. A piece does not run on twelve small frictions; it runs on the few wide ones. Score each row, then keep the widest 3-5 as the engine and let the rest fall away (radical infrequency applied at the paragraph level — bring down the average).

Rate each gap on three axes:

| Axis | Question | Low (cut it) | High (keep it) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Width** | How far apart are the two columns? | A nuance; "mostly true, slightly off" | The columns describe two different worlds |
| **Stakes** | Does the gap *matter* to the audience's live question? | A trivia gap nobody acts on | The gap decides money, belief, or behavior |
| **Hiddenness** | Is the gap *unseen* — visible only from outside (Pattern 7)? | Everyone already knows this gap | Only the outsider's distance reveals it |

Keep the rows that score high on **at least two** axes. The ideal engine gap is wide *and* high-stakes *and* hidden — that's the one only you can write. Discard the narrow, the trivial, and the already-obvious. **3-5 ranked gaps** is the output of this step; if you have more, you're surveying (AN-4 formula risk); if you have fewer than 3, you may not have enough friction to carry a piece — go observe more or pick a topic with more divergence.

> **The ranking discipline (Pattern 20 at paragraph scale):** a piece built on the one widest gap, fully grounded, beats a piece that lists eight gaps and develops none. Cut to the few that matter. The discarded rows aren't wasted — they're the texture you didn't have room for, and they prove you triangulated rather than cherry-picked.

### Step 4 — Charge the gap with disappointment, not complaint (Pattern 21)

A gap stated flatly is a fact-check. A gap stated from *productive disappointment* is analysis with a moral spine. For your top 3-5 gaps, name the standard the thing is *failing* — the better version it could have been. Wang doesn't just observe that San Francisco is dysfunctional; he indicts it *because it's the city of the chosen, the Stanford kids, the ones who should have built a Florence and instead built a place where you hide your car keys.* The disappointment names the wasted potential. That's what gives the friction teeth an insider can't dismiss as mere negativity.

| For each top gap, fill | Example shape |
|---|---|
| **The standard it's failing** | "This could be a Florence" / "These are the people who should have gotten it right" |
| **Why it should be better** | The resources, the talent, the stated mission — all present, all squandered |
| **The disappointment (not complaint)** | Error-correction from someone who *wants the thing to be good*, not a critic scoring points |

If a gap produces only contempt or only complaint, either find the disappointment under it (what *should* this have been?) or cut it. The Wang charge is always: *I see how good this could be, and I'm telling you why it isn't.* That voice is uncomfortable for insiders precisely because it's not hostile — it's a fellow traveler who wanted more.

### Step 5 — Name the one insider-uncomfortable insight (the output gate)

The matrix has done its work when you can compress the widest gap into a single sentence that an insider would find **uncomfortable but accurate** — the line they can't refute and wish you hadn't said. This is the deterministic gate against AN-3 (restating the official story). If your candidate sentence is one an insider would happily quote in their own marketing, you've restated the official story and failed. If it's one they'd quietly admit is true and publicly resent, you've found the friction.

Run the **insider-discomfort test** on your candidate insight:

| Test | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Would an insider **agree it's accurate** (privately)? | Yes — they can't honestly deny it | No — it's wrong, or a strawman |
| Would an insider be **uncomfortable** with it said publicly? | Yes — it names what they don't say out loud | No — they'd happily put it on a slide |
| Does it come from **disappointment, not contempt** (Pattern 21)? | Yes — a standard they're failing | No — generic hostility / score-settling |
| Is it **grounded** in the observed column, not asserted? | Yes — traceable to texture in the matrix | No — a clever line with no proof underneath |

The insight that passes all four is the analytical engine of the piece. Write it as one beautiful, quotable sentence (Pattern 6 — the anchor line) — short hard true. Build the piece around it.

### Step 6 — Hand the engine to the draft (what the matrix becomes)

The friction map is the engine, not the essay. Now hand it off:
- The **anchor insight** (Step 5) becomes the spine — the line the whole piece is built to earn and deliver.
- The **top 3-5 gaps** (Step 3) become the section structure — each gap a movement, each developed via the zoom (Pattern 8): observed texture → what it means → back to texture.
- The **"grounded in" column** (Step 2) becomes the openings — start each section on the meal, the walk, the number, the document (Pattern 3, the grounding detail), never on the abstraction.
- The **disappointment** (Step 4) becomes the tone — the moral charge that runs under the analysis without ever tipping into rant.

Do **not** write the matrix into the prose as a literal two-column comparison. The friction is *felt*, never announced. Never write "the official story says X but the ground truth is Y" — that's the scaffold showing (the genius.md "do NOT label the moves" rule). Dissolve the matrix into one coherent voice that walks the reader from the painted village to the morning after, and lets them feel the gap close.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Format | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Essay / Substack** | Native habitat. Full matrix → 3-5 gaps → each a section opening on observed texture, zooming to meaning. The anchor insight is the line readers quote and the piece's reason to exist. You have the runway to develop the disappointment as a sustained moral undertone. This is the annual-letter form: the gap *is* the letter. |
| **LinkedIn** | One gap, not five. Open on the single observed detail (the painted village), name the friction the audience lives but hasn't articulated (Recognition Anchoring, Pattern 16 — "you've felt this"), land on the anchor insight as the last line. The insider-discomfort test still gates it — a post that flatters the in-group is a press release. One considered post beats ten reactive takes. |
| **X / thread** | The single widest, most hidden gap, compressed. Tweet 1 = the official story stated straight (the steelman). Tweet 2 = the observed contradiction with its texture. Tweet 3 = the insider-uncomfortable insight as the payoff. Irony over earnestness; the gap does the work, you don't editorialize it. |
| **Marketing / brand** | The gap between what the *category* says and what customers actually *experience* IS the campaign (genius.md cross-domain: "map what the category says vs. what customers actually experience; the gap is the campaign"). Steelman the category cliché, ground the customer's lived reality, and let the friction become positioning. The anchor insight becomes the line on the page. Honesty spine: the ground-truth column must be *true* of your product, not aspirational. |
| **Copy** | The friction the prospect *lives but the category won't admit* is the hook and the mechanism. Ground every claim in the observed moment (texture over feature-list). The insider-uncomfortable insight reframed for the prospect ("the thing your last three vendors won't say out loud") is the angle. Pairs with `godin-false-proxy-purge` — the official story is often a false proxy the whole category measures. |
| **Ghostwriting** | Run the matrix *in the client's outsider position* — what can *they* see that their industry's insiders have normalized? The disappointment must be the client's genuine standard, not yours. One coherent voice: the friction map gives the client a real position to hold, which is what separates ghostwritten authority from generic thought-leadership slop. |

### Worked example A — Essay (a celebrated tech hub)

**Topic:** the "innovation capital" reputation of a flagship startup city.
**Outsider angle:** I moved here from the provinces; I keep the locals' rituals at arm's length.
**Ground-truth sources:** the walk to three coworking cafés, the data on net out-migration, the friend who locks his catalytic converter, a city budget line.

| # | Official story (steelmanned) | Ground truth (observed) | Grounded in | W | S | H |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "The most talented, mission-driven people on earth gather here to build the future." | The most talented people on earth gather here and then *route around* the city — gated offices, private shuttles, keys hidden in a drawer. | The car-keys-in-a-drawer ritual; the shuttle fleet; the office with no street-facing door. | 5 | 5 | 4 |
| 2 | "Density and proximity create serendipitous collaboration." | The density is real; the serendipity has been privatized into invite-only dinners. The street is something to get *through*. | The walk between three cafés — no one looks up; the calendar of closed-door salons. | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 3 | "This is the city the chosen build because they can build anywhere." | They can build anywhere, and what they've built *here*, for here, is a place that disappoints its own residents into leaving. | Net out-migration data; the budget line for problems that money clearly hasn't solved. | 5 | 5 | 5 |

**Engine gaps (ranked):**
1. They can build *anything* and built a city you protect yourself *from* — standard it's failing: the people most able to fix a place have opted out of the place.
2. The future is built here and routed around here — standard it's failing: density without civic stake is just co-location.

**Anchor insight (insider-uncomfortable-but-accurate):**
> "The people who can build anything have built, for themselves, a city they hide their car keys from."

*Insider-discomfort test:* accurate ✓ (they can't deny the keys) · uncomfortable ✓ (it's not on any pitch deck) · disappointment-not-contempt ✓ (these are the *chosen* — that's the charge: it could've been a Florence) · grounded ✓ (the keys, the migration data, the shuttles).

**Handoff:** Spine = the keys line. Section 1 opens on the drawer of keys, zooms to the privatized serendipity, returns to the shuttle gliding past the street. Tone = a fellow traveler's disappointment, never a hater's glee.

### Worked example B — Marketing (a wellness brand)

**Topic:** a supplement brand whose category sells "clinically backed transformation."
**Outsider angle:** I read the actual studies the category cites; most customers don't.
**Ground-truth sources:** the cited papers (sample sizes, effect sizes), 200 churned-customer exit comments, the subreddit thread.

| # | Official story (steelmanned) | Ground truth (observed) | Grounded in | W | S | H |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Clinically backed formula proven to deliver results." | The study was real, n=24, funded in-house, effect indistinguishable from the placebo arm by week 8. | The cited paper's own tables. | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 2 | "Customers love the transformation." | Customers love the *ritual* — the morning scoop, the routine — and churn the moment they audit the results. | 200 exit comments: "felt good doing it," "couldn't tell it worked." | 4 | 5 | 4 |

**Engine gaps (ranked):**
1. The "clinical proof" the whole category leans on is, on its own numbers, indistinguishable from placebo — standard it's failing: an industry that sells rigor on the back of papers it bets you'll never read.
2. The product that "works" is selling a *ritual*, not a result — standard it's failing: it could just *say so* and keep the customers who churn on the audit.

**Anchor insight (insider-uncomfortable-but-accurate):**
> "Your category doesn't sell a result; it sells a morning ritual — and prices in the bet that you'll never read the study it's named after."

*Insider-discomfort test:* accurate ✓ (it's in their own tables) · uncomfortable ✓ (no brand will put it on a label) · disappointment-not-contempt ✓ (the ritual is genuinely *good* — sell *that*, honestly) · grounded ✓ (the n=24 paper, the 200 comments).

**Handoff (honesty spine):** Use this only if the brand's *own* ground-truth column is clean — i.e., if you flip the campaign to honestly sell the ritual and the felt experience, the friction becomes the positioning ("we sell the ritual, and we'll show you the study"). If the ground-truth column indicts your *own* product, the matrix just told you to fix the product before you write the copy.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Dan Wang Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Quality Rubric. Flag and fix before delivering.

- **Real friction, not restated press release (AN-3):** the right column genuinely *diverges* from the left. If the official and ground-truth columns say the same thing, there is no gap — you've stenographed, not analyzed. The insider-discomfort test (Step 5) is the deterministic check: an insight an insider would happily put on a slide has failed.
- **Ground truth is observed, not asserted (AN-2 / AN-4):** every right-column entry traces to the "grounded in" column — a meal, walk, number, document, scene. A ground-truth claim with no texture under it is a guess (AN-2) or a single-source take (AN-4). Triangulate: literature + interviews + data + observation.
- **Steelmanned official story:** the left column states the consensus at full strength, in its believers' words. A strawman produces a cheap, fake gap. The friction only has force against the official story's *strongest* form.
- **The charge is disappointment, not complaint (Pattern 21):** the top gaps carry a clear standard the thing is *failing* (this could be a Florence), not generic negativity or score-settling. The voice wants the thing to be better — that's what makes it uncomfortable *and* undismissable for insiders.
- **3-5 gaps, ranked, not a survey (AN-4 / Pattern 20):** the piece runs on the few widest gaps, fully grounded — not a list of eight developed-none. If you kept more than five, you're surveying; cut to the engine.
- **The matrix is dissolved, not announced:** the handoff produces prose that *feels* the gap (the painted village → the morning after), never labels it ("the official story says X but…"). The scaffold must not show — the reader feels the friction, doesn't see the columns.
- **The anchor insight is one quotable sentence (Pattern 6):** short, hard, true — the line the piece is built to earn. If you can't compress the widest gap into one uncomfortable-but-accurate sentence, the friction isn't sharp enough yet.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Two press releases arguing (counter-abstraction masquerading as ground truth).** The right column says "actually it's stagnation, actually customers hate it" — claims with no observed texture, just an opposite assertion. This is AN-3 in disguise: you've swapped one official story for another. **Recovery:** force the "grounded in" column for every row. If you can't name the walk, the number, the document, or the scene, you haven't observed the ground truth — you've theorized it. Go look, or cut the row.
- **The strawman official story (a gap that was too easy).** Caricaturing the consensus so your insight wins cheaply — "they say everything's perfect" when no one actually claims that. The resulting "friction" impresses no one because the left column was a punching bag. **Recovery:** rerun Step 1. State the official story so fairly that an insider would *nod* — at its strongest, partly-true form. The best friction sits where the official story is half-right, not where it's a lie.
- **Complaint wearing analysis's clothes (AN-3 / Pattern 21 breach).** The matrix becomes a list of grievances — contempt with no standard, score-settling with no disappointment. Insiders dismiss it instantly as a hater's take. **Recovery:** Step 4 — for each gap, name what the thing *should* have been and why (the Florence standard). If you can only find contempt, you're captured by the opposite in-group; find the disappointment (you wanted it to be good) or cut the gap.
- **Surveying instead of engineering (twelve gaps, no engine).** Listing every friction you found and developing none — the AN-4 survey, "here are all the open questions" with no answer. The piece reads as comprehensive and lands as nothing. **Recovery:** Step 3 ranking. Score on width/stakes/hiddenness, keep the 3-5 that score high on two axes, and cut the rest to texture. One widest gap fully grounded beats eight gestured-at. "Comprehensive" output = system failure regardless of how many gaps you cataloged.
