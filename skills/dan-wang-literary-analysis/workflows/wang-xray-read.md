# Wang X-Ray Read — Want-To vs. Have-To Reading (Dan Wang)

There are two kinds of pages in almost every serious book. There is the page the author *had* to write — the cultural-revolution recap, the reform-and-opening summary, the lit-review throat-clearing, rehearsed a million times by a thousand authors before this one, present because the genre demands it and absent any heat. And there is the page the author *wanted* to write — the one where the prose suddenly accelerates, the examples get oddly specific, the opinions sharpen, a tangent the editor probably flagged survives anyway. That second page is where the book actually is. Tyler Cowen reads with x-ray vision precisely here: *"here is the potted history, we don't need to get into that — find the key analytical phrase."* This workflow turns that instinct into a procedure. You do not read the book front-to-back at uniform speed. You map it — skip column for the obligated padding, fire column for the animated insight — and you pull the one load-bearing phrase off each fire page. The output is a fire map: a reading plan that lets you take the real signal out of dense material in a fraction of the time, and *better*, because you spent your attention where the author spent theirs.

## Pre-Flight

Read before executing — load these `genius.md` sections (do not paraphrase from memory; the patterns are precise, and this workflow fails the instant you start dutifully reading the potted history you were supposed to skip):
- **Pattern 10 — X-Ray Vision Reading** — the core device: ask "Did the author WANT to write this section or HAVE to?" Skip obligated sections, study animated ones. The entire fire map is this question made operational, page by page.
- **Pattern 18 — Genre-Incentive Mapping (Escape the Form's Pull)** — *why* the obligated sections exist. Trade books pad toward the airport-book; academic books pad toward tenure-defense and lit-review; reports pad toward CYA boilerplate and methodology recitation. Knowing the form's incentives tells you *in advance* which sections will be have-to filler — you predict the skip column before you open the book.
- **Pattern 12 — Genre Transcendence Identification** — the fire is usually where the author *transcended* the genre's pull: the scholar who wrote one narrative chapter instead of a survey, the analyst who staked a real answer instead of cataloguing open questions. Fire = the parts that don't read like the category's lazy default. That is your detection signal.
- **Decision Framework — Q7 (Am I escaping the genre's pull?)** — turned inward on *reading* instead of writing: what does this format usually reward into mediocrity, and where did the author write *against* it? The places they escaped the pull are the fire; the places they surrendered to it are the skip.

> **🔒 Pre-Flight Gate**: run the **Decision Framework** in `genius.md § Decision Framework` before mapping — specifically **Q7** (what is this format's incentive pull, and so where will the obligated padding be?) and the reading-incentive read of **Pattern 18** (a *trade* book and an *academic* book on the same topic hide their fire in different places — the trade book's fire is the reported anecdote and the staked thesis; the academic book's fire is the one chapter that abandons hedging for a real claim). Two hard stops: (1) **this is a reading-acceleration tool, not a comprehension shortcut for material you must master in full.** If you are studying for an exam, auditing a contract for liability, or reproducing a proof, do NOT x-ray it — read every word. The fire map is for *extracting signal from dense source you are synthesizing from*, where 80% is genre padding. (2) If you cannot articulate what this genre's obligated sections *are* (Pattern 18), you cannot mark the skip column with confidence — name the form's incentives first, or you risk skipping the fire and studying the padding.

## Input Required

- **The source** — the book, report, dossier, white paper, or long document. Title + format, and the table of contents / section headers if you have them (the TOC is half the map).
- **The genre/format** — trade book / academic monograph / industry report / consultancy deck / white paper / dissertation / long-form journalism. This is load-bearing: the genre *predicts* where the padding lives (Pattern 18).
- **The reading goal** — what you actually need to extract. "The 3 novel claims only this author could make." "Whether their China-supply-chain thesis holds." "The mechanism, not the market-sizing." The goal sets the relevance filter on the fire.
- **Your synthesis target** (optional but load-bearing) — what you're building *from* this reading (an essay, a brief, a decision). The fire map should feed it directly; the key-phrase pull becomes your quote bank.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Map the genre's obligated sections BEFORE you read (Pattern 18 + Q7)

Do not open to page one. First, predict where the padding lives. Every format has a pull toward genre-typical filler — sections that exist because the form demands them, not because the author had heat. Name the pull, and you have your skip column before you've read a word.

| Genre | The form's incentive pull (Pattern 18) | Predicted obligated / skip sections | Where the fire usually hides |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trade book** | The airport-book: written for a mass audience, padded to length, story-formula repeated | Potted history; the "throughout human history…" framing chapter; the recap of famous case studies everyone knows | The reported scene the author actually witnessed; the staked thesis (Pattern 17); the chapter that reads unlike the others |
| **Academic monograph** | Tenure-defense: lit review, hedging, defensive footnoting, peer-anxiety | The literature review; the methodology recitation; the "limitations and future research" coda; survey-of-the-debate chapters | The one chapter where hedging drops and a real claim gets staked; the original data/fieldwork chapter (Pattern 12 — transcended the form) |
| **Industry / consultancy report** | CYA + length-as-value: boilerplate, market-sizing, exec-summary hedging | Methodology appendix; market-sizing recitation; "macro context" preamble; the disclaimer-laden executive summary | The one slide/section with a contrarian number; the named primary source; the recommendation the author clearly believes |
| **White paper / vendor doc** | Sell-the-thing: feature recitation, problem-inflation, social-proof filler | The "the world is changing" intro; the feature matrix; the logo wall and testimonial block | The architecture/mechanism section; the honest limitation buried mid-document |
| **Long-form journalism / essay** | The nut-graf formula, the obligatory both-sides paragraph | The throat-clearing scene-set; the "to be sure" balance paragraph; the explainer recap of background | The reported detail with no obvious purpose (the texture, Pattern 3); the line the writer clearly wanted to write |

> The Wang/Cowen tell: **"here is the potted history" is a phrase you should be able to say out loud about a section *before* reading it.** The cultural-revolution recap, the reform-and-opening summary — rehearsed a million times, identical across a hundred books, zero heat. Mark it skip on sight. The genre told you it was coming.

### Step 2 — Read the conclusion and the TOC first (find the thesis, then route)

Before the linear read, read the **conclusion / final chapter / executive summary's actual recommendation first**, and the **table of contents** as a map. Two reasons: (1) if the synthesis is clear up front, you can read the supporting chapters *selectively* — you already know what they're supporting; (2) the TOC, read against your Step-1 genre map, lets you pre-mark each section skip/fire/skim *before* the first page.

| Read first | What it gives you | How it routes the rest |
|---|---|---|
| **The conclusion / final chapter** | The thesis the whole book exists to deliver | Now every chapter is either *proof of the thesis* (skim for the proof) or *padding around it* (skip) |
| **The table of contents** | The structural map | Cross it with the Step-1 genre table — pre-mark each chapter SKIP / FIRE / SKIM |
| **The index** (if dense source) | Where the author's specific obsessions cluster | High page-density entries = where the author lingered = candidate fire |

Mark each section now, provisionally: **SKIP** (obligated padding — confirm in 30 seconds, don't read), **FIRE** (where the heat is — read closely, pull phrases), **SKIM** (proof-of-thesis — scan for the one number/example you need). You will adjust as you go, but you start with a map, not a blank book.

### Step 3 — Fire detection: read for the heat signature (Pattern 10 + Pattern 12)

Now read — but read *for the question*, not for the words. On every section, the live question is: **did the author WANT to write this, or HAVE to?** You do not need to read the whole section to know. The fire has a signature; learn to spot it in the first paragraph and the prose tells you whether to slow down or skip.

| Fire signal (slow down, read closely) | Have-to signal (confirm skip, move on) |
|---|---|
| **Specificity spikes** — named people, exact numbers, the particular factory floor, a dish eaten | Generic abstraction — "throughout history," "experts agree," "it is widely understood" |
| **Opinion sharpens** — a real claim staked, a disagreement voiced, a judgment risked (Pattern 12) | Hedging thickens — "some argue / others contend," the both-sides paragraph, the survey-of-views |
| **A tangent survives** — a digression the editor surely flagged, kept because the author loved it | The recap you've read in ten other books — the potted history, the rehearsed background |
| **Prose accelerates** — sentence rhythm changes, energy rises, the author is clearly enjoying themselves | Prose flattens — committee-smooth, dutiful, the lit-review drone, the methodology recitation |
| **Primary observation** — "I walked through," "I asked," "the data shows X, which surprised me" | Secondary summary — citation-stacking of what others found, no original sighting |

> **The transcendence cross-check (Pattern 12):** the hottest fire is usually where the author *escaped their genre's pull* — the scholar who wrote one narrative, reported chapter; the consultant who staked a contrarian number against the house view; the trade author who included the real, unglamorous fieldwork. Fire = the parts that **do not read like the category's lazy default.** If a section reads exactly like every other book in the genre, it's have-to. If it reads like the author forgot what genre they were in, that's the fire.

### Step 4 — Pull the key analytical phrase per fire page (the Cowen move)

This is the payload. For each FIRE section, do not summarize it in your own flattening paraphrase — **pull the author's actual load-bearing phrase**, the one sentence or clause where the analysis crystallizes. This is Cowen's x-ray vision in its final form: *find the key analytical phrase.* One per fire page is the discipline; if a page has none, it wasn't fire — re-mark it skim.

| The pull discipline | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **The author's words, not yours** | Quote the exact phrase where the insight lands | Paraphrase into generic summary (you lose the precision that made it fire) |
| **The analytical phrase, not the topic sentence** | The clause that *does the work* — the surprising claim, the mechanism named, the number that contradicts the consensus | The section header or the dutiful first sentence |
| **One per fire page** | Force the compression — what is the *one* thing this page added? | Pull six quotes (you've stopped x-raying and started transcribing) |
| **Tag it to your goal** | Mark which pulled phrase serves your synthesis target | Collect quotes you'll never use (signal becomes a hoard) |

The pulled phrases, in reading order, become the **key-phrase pull** — a distilled spine of the book's actual insight, in the author's own voice, that you can synthesize from directly. This is the artifact you keep; the book goes back on the shelf.

### Step 5 — Assemble the fire map and verify the skip list (the comprehension check)

Lay the three columns into the fire map: **skip list** (obligated sections, marked for skipping), **fire sections** (page ranges where the author was on fire), **key-phrase pull** (the load-bearing phrase per fire page). Then run the comprehension check — the guard against x-raying your way past the actual signal.

| Verification | Pass | Fail → recovery |
|---|---|---|
| **Thesis reconstructable from fire alone** | The key-phrase pull lets you state the book's thesis without re-reading the skips | If you can't, you skipped fire — re-scan a "skip" section; the heat was hiding in the padding |
| **Skip list is genre-padding, not inconvenient fire** | Every skip is genuinely obligated (you could predict it from Pattern 18) | If a skip held a staked claim or a primary observation, it was fire mismarked — pull its phrase |
| **Key phrases are the author's, load-bearing** | Each phrase is a real analytical crystallization, quotable, specific | If a phrase is generic ("growth was significant"), it's not fire — that page was skim |
| **Reading-goal served** | The pull answers what you came for | If not, you read for the book's emphasis instead of your goal — re-scan fire for goal-relevant phrases |

> **The honest guard (Pre-Flight stop #1, restated):** x-ray reading trades completeness for speed-times-signal. That trade is correct for *synthesis-from-dense-source* and catastrophic for *material you must master in full*. The comprehension check is where you confirm you made the right trade: if reconstructing the thesis from fire alone leaves a hole, either the skip was wrong (recover it) or this was material you should have read in full (abort the x-ray, read linearly).

## Content Type Adaptations

This workflow is primarily a *reading/research* instrument — but its output (fire map + key-phrase pull) feeds production, so the adaptation is about *what you do with the pull* per downstream format.

| Downstream use | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Research / synthesis (essay, brief)** | Native habitat. The key-phrase pull becomes your quote bank and the spine of the synthesis; the skip list documents what you *responsibly ignored* (proof you triangulated rather than skimmed lazily). Cross with `wang-friction-map` — the fire sections often contain the ground-truth observations that contradict the source's own official framing. |
| **Social (X / LinkedIn)** | One fire section, one pulled phrase, one post. The author's load-bearing phrase + your reframe is a textured post that rewards a second read. Don't post the potted history you skipped; post the heat. Bring down the average — one phrase worth pulling beats a thread of summary. |
| **Marketing / competitive intel** | X-ray a competitor's white paper or a category report: skip the feature recitation and market-sizing, pull the one honest limitation buried mid-document and the architecture section. The fire is where they accidentally told the truth; the skip is the sell. |
| **Copy (research phase)** | X-ray the source material (studies, reports, founder interviews) for the one analytical phrase that becomes the mechanism or the proof. The pulled phrase, grounded in the author's specificity, beats a paraphrased claim — texture over feature-list starts at the reading stage. |
| **Ghostwriting (client's reading)** | Run the fire map on what the client *should* read but won't have time to — hand them the key-phrase pull, not the book. Their authority is built on the fire, not the padding; the pull is how you load their voice with real, specific source material fast. |

### Worked example A — Trade book ("400-page book on China's economic rise")

**Source:** a flagship trade book on China's rise. · **Genre:** trade book. · **Goal:** the 2-3 novel claims only this author could make (for an essay).
**Genre pull (Pattern 18):** airport-book — padded to length, potted history rehearsed, famous case studies recapped.

| Section | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ch. 1-2: "From Empire to Reform" | **SKIP** | The potted history — cultural revolution, reform-and-opening, rehearsed a million times. Predicted from the genre before reading. |
| Ch. 3: "The Shenzhen I Walked" | **FIRE** | Specificity spikes — named factories, a meal, the author's own observation. Reads unlike the recap chapters. |
| Ch. 4-5: "The Standard Models" | **SKIM** | Survey of the famous growth theories — read in ten other books. Scan for the one number the author disputes. |
| Ch. 6: "What the Models Miss" | **FIRE** | Opinion sharpens — a staked claim *against* the consensus (Pattern 12, transcended the genre). |
| Ch. 7: "Conclusion" | (read FIRST) | The thesis. Read before everything; routes the rest. |

**Key-phrase pull:**
1. Ch.3, p.71 — *"The factory wasn't a triumph of planning; it was the residue of a thousand improvisations the plan never sanctioned."* → serves goal: the central novel claim (improvisation over plan).
2. Ch.6, p.188 — *"Every growth model imported here arrived as theory and left as a local dialect."* → serves goal: the author's contrarian mechanism.

**Comprehension check:** thesis (China's rise was bottom-up improvisation, not top-down design) reconstructable from two fire chapters alone ✓; the two skipped history chapters added nothing the conclusion didn't ✓. **Reading economy:** ~25% of pages read closely; thesis + 2 novel claims extracted. The other 300 pages were the village painted for the motorcade.

### Worked example B — Academic monograph / report ("a 90-page consultancy report on EV supply chains")

**Source:** a consultancy report on EV battery supply chains. · **Genre:** industry report. · **Goal:** does their "China-dependency is irreversible by 2030" thesis hold? (for a decision brief).
**Genre pull (Pattern 18):** CYA + length-as-value — boilerplate, market-sizing recitation, hedged executive summary.

| Section | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Exec Summary | (read recommendation FIRST) | Hedged, but the actual recommendation routes the read. |
| §1 "Market Context & Sizing" | **SKIP** | Boilerplate market-sizing — every report in the category has this; no heat. |
| §2 "Methodology" | **SKIP** | Methodology recitation — the CYA section. Confirm in 30s. |
| §3 "The Refining Bottleneck" | **FIRE** | A contrarian number staked — a primary supplier interview, specificity spike. The one place the author believes something. |
| §4 "Scenario Models" | **SKIM** | Standard 3-scenario hedging — scan for the assumption that drives the thesis. |
| App. C: "Refining Capacity by Geography" | **FIRE** | The named primary data — the number the whole thesis rests on, buried in an appendix. |

**Key-phrase pull:**
1. §3, p.34 — *"The bottleneck was never the mine; it was the refinery, and refineries take seven years and a tolerance for pollution the West has legislated away."* → serves goal: the actual mechanism behind the thesis.
2. App.C, p.81 — *"Western refining capacity additions through 2030, even fully permitted today, close less than 18% of the gap."* → serves goal: the number that decides whether the thesis holds.

**Comprehension check:** thesis ("irreversible by 2030") holds *because of the refining-permitting timeline* — reconstructable from §3 + App.C alone ✓; market-sizing and methodology skips added nothing to the decision ✓. **Reading economy:** ~15% of pages read closely; the load-bearing number was in an appendix the linear reader reaches exhausted. The fire map walked straight to it.

## Quality Gate

> **🛡️ Anti-Pattern Check**: review against `genius.md § Dan Wang Would Never... (Anti-Patterns)` and § Quality Rubric. Flag and fix before delivering.

- **The skip list is genre-padding, not skipped fire (Pattern 10/18):** every SKIP is genuinely obligated — predictable from the form's incentive pull, identical across books in the genre, zero heat. If a "skip" contained a staked claim, a primary observation, or a transcended-the-genre passage, it was fire mismarked. The comprehension check (Step 5) is the deterministic guard: thesis must be reconstructable from fire alone.
- **The key-phrase pull is the author's words, load-bearing (the Cowen move):** each phrase is the actual analytical crystallization — quotable, specific, the clause that *does the work* — not your flattening paraphrase and not the dutiful topic sentence. A pulled phrase that reads generic ("growth was significant") means that page was skim, not fire; re-mark it. Avoid AN-6 (AI-flattened summary): the precision that made it fire is exactly what a paraphrase destroys.
- **One phrase per fire page, not transcription:** the compression discipline holds — if you've pulled six quotes off a page, you've stopped x-raying and started copying the whole book. Force the *one* thing each fire page added.
- **Fire detected by heat signature, not by position (Pattern 12):** fire is where the prose accelerates, specificity spikes, opinion sharpens, the author escaped the genre's default — *wherever that lands*, including buried in an appendix. Don't assume fire is in the "important-sounding" chapters; the consultancy report's load-bearing number hid in App.C.
- **The right trade was made (Pre-Flight stop #1):** x-ray reading was correct for this material (synthesis-from-dense-source, ~80% genre padding), not applied to material requiring full mastery (exam, contract liability, proof reproduction). If the comprehension check left a hole, either recover the mismarked skip or abort and read linearly.
- **Goal served, not just the book's emphasis:** the pull answers what *you* came for, tagged to your synthesis target. A fire map full of the author's favorite phrases that don't serve your goal is a hoard, not a tool. Re-scan fire for goal-relevant phrases.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Skipping the fire because it looked like padding (the costliest error).** The author's hottest claim is buried in a section that *looks* obligated — a contrarian number in a "scenario models" appendix, a staked thesis hidden in what reads like a recap chapter. You skip it, and your synthesis misses the one thing that mattered. **Recovery:** Step 5 comprehension check — if you can't reconstruct the thesis from the fire alone, the fire is hiding in a skip. Re-scan the skips' *first paragraphs* for the heat signature (specificity, sharpened opinion, primary observation). When in doubt, skim a skip; never skim fire.
- **Transcribing instead of x-raying.** Pulling five or six quotes per page because they all seem good — at which point you've read the whole book at full speed and gained nothing from the method. **Recovery:** Step 4 discipline — *one* load-bearing phrase per fire page. Force the compression: what is the single thing this page added that no other page did? If everything seems load-bearing, you've lost the relevance filter — re-anchor on your reading goal and pull only what serves it.
- **X-raying material that demanded a full read (Pre-Flight stop #1 breach).** Applying the fire map to a contract you're auditing for liability, a textbook chapter you'll be tested on, or a proof you must reproduce — and missing the clause/step that lived in the "boilerplate" you skipped. **Recovery:** the gate is upstream. Before mapping, confirm this is synthesis-from-dense-source (extract signal, skip padding) and not master-in-full material. If it's the latter, abort the x-ray and read every word — speed is the wrong objective when completeness is the requirement.
- **Marking skip from the genre table without confirming (lazy prediction).** Pattern 18 *predicts* where padding lives, but a given author may have hidden fire in the section the genre usually wastes — the scholar who made the lit review a real argument, the report whose methodology section quietly admits the thesis is shaky. Trusting the prediction blindly skips that fire. **Recovery:** the genre map is a *hypothesis*, not a verdict. Confirm each SKIP with a 30-second first-paragraph scan for the heat signature before you actually skip it. Predict, then verify — never predict and assume.
