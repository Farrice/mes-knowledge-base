## 1. The Constitution Principle

{{FOUNDER_NAME}} named this on the April 21 call. She called the BOS the *Constitution*. The principle is one sentence:

> *"We amend it. We don't rewrite it. Once a doc reaches a 'yes, this feels right' state, it stays that way."*
> — `deliverables/2026-04-11-monday-package/10-working-method.md`

Applied to the BOS, this means three things:

**Locked sections stay locked.** When {{FOUNDER_NAME}} has confirmed a section reads right (the Manifesto v2, the 12 Non-Negotiables, the spine, Profile #1), the section does not get reopened. New thinking gets layered as an amendment with a date and a reason; the original survives.

**Targeted edits, not blank-page redrafts.** Every change is downstream of a specific shift: a new decision, a piece of new information, an {{FOUNDER_NAME}} insight, a reality-check from a shipped event. If a doc needs a full rewrite, that is itself a flag — most quarters should have zero rewrites.

**Each decision lives in one file.** Brand Bible holds the spine and voice. Non-Negotiables holds the Lines. ICP Master holds the profiles. When a decision changes, the one file where it lives gets updated, and references in every other file get re-checked. No drift from forked truth.

The BOS is a living document. A living document is not the same as a document that is always in flux. Living means it absorbs reality; in flux means nothing is settled. The Constitution principle is the fence between the two.

## 2. When To Amend

Three triggers — and only three. Anything else is noise.

**Trigger 1: {{FOUNDER_NAME}} names a change in writing.** Voice memo, email, doc comment, DM. The form does not matter. *Written* matters. A verbal "I think X..." in a co-work call does not trigger an amendment until {{FOUNDER_NAME}} writes it down (or sends a voice memo Farrice transcribes back to her for confirmation). Talk is exploration. Writing is decision.

**Trigger 2: Reality contradicts the doc.** A Line bent. A metric was wrong. A creative brief produced bad output. A profile turned out to describe nobody who actually shows up. The doc updates to match what the room taught us. The room is the final editor.

**Trigger 3: Discovery surfaces something missing.** A new asset type (a podcast brief, a video pillar). A new failure mode (a venue type that always says no). A new audience segment that did not exist when the BOS shipped. New scope gets a new doc, not a rewrite of an existing one.

Amendments outside these three triggers are drift in disguise — usually a collaborator's preference, a tool's default, or a moment of self-doubt looking for permission to soften something. Decline those. The Constitution holds.

## 3. The Amendment Workflow

Six steps. Every amendment, every time. No shortcuts.

1. **{{FOUNDER_NAME}} names the amendment.** Channel: voice memo, DM to Farrice, direct edit comment on a doc. Voice memos are the default — see the working-method file. They are never "too raw."

2. **Farrice reads it within 24 hours.** Voice memos get transcribed and synthesized. Edit comments get triaged. DMs get logged. The 24-hour window is the floor; faster is fine.

3. **Farrice identifies which BOS doc(s) the amendment touches.** Some amendments are surgical (one file). Some cascade (see Section 4). The cascade map is checked before any edit lands.

4. **Farrice drafts the amendment in writing — flagged in chat with the diff.** Format: *"Updated `03-voice-document.md` Section 2 — added pattern X, removed phrase Y. Cascade impact: brief Z needs a 1-line review."* The diff is explicit; the cascade is named; nothing is hidden.

5. **{{FOUNDER_NAME}} confirms in writing.** Yes / no / refine. Verbal confirmation in the co-work call does not count until it is written. This is the firewall against {{FOUNDER_NAME}}-uninformed amendments (Section 6).

6. **Change-log gets updated.** Every confirmed amendment is logged in `05-ops/01-change-log.md` with date, file, what changed, why, {{FOUNDER_NAME}}-confirmation date, and cascade impacts. The change log is the audit trail; it exists so a new collaborator (or {{FOUNDER_NAME}} in 12 months) can answer "why does this doc say this?" by reading one file.

After Step 6: cascading references get a quick re-check (Section 4). Then the amendment is live.

## 4. The Cascade Rules

Some docs are upstream of others. An amendment to an upstream doc requires a downstream review — sometimes a full rewrite of a downstream doc, sometimes just a one-line check.

**Brand Bible amendments** → ICP master, voice document, positioning one-pager, and ALL creative briefs need a 1-line review. The spine lives in the Brand Bible; if the spine shifts, every downstream asset must re-prove it still honors the spine.

**Voice Document amendments** → ALL creative briefs need a 1-line review. Briefs inherit voice rules. New rule, new pattern, removed pattern — every brief gets checked.

**Non-Negotiables amendments** → entire BOS sweep. The 12 Lines are the floor. If a Line moves, every doc that mentions the Line, every brief that triages against the Line, every drift signal that references the Line — all of it gets reviewed. Rare, by design.

**ICP Master amendments** (especially when Profile #2 or #3 finalizes) → all briefs that target by profile get reviewed. The umbrella ICP holds; the sub-profiles refine. New profile detail = new precision in briefs.

**DESIGN.md amendments** → photography rules, component tokens, all briefs with visual specs. Visual decisions cascade through every asset that gets shipped.

**Per-asset brief amendments** (e.g., updating `ig-feed-post.md`) → no cascade needed. Briefs are leaf nodes. They consume upstream context; they don't produce it.

The cascade map exists so amendments don't silently leave downstream docs out of sync. A monthly drift-check (Section 6) is the safety net if a cascade is missed in the moment.

## 6. Failure Modes

Four failure modes have already been anticipated. Each has a structural mitigation.

**Silent amendments.** Farrice updates a doc without naming the change in chat. The doc drifts; nobody knows when or why. *Mitigation:* every doc-change gets a chat-message flag with the diff (Workflow Step 4). No edit lands without a flagged diff, period.

**Cascade gap.** An upstream amendment doesn't cascade to downstream docs. The Brand Bible says X; a creative brief still says not-X. *Mitigation:* the cascade map in Section 4, plus a monthly drift-check where Farrice grep-scans for stale references. Quarterly review (Section 7) is the second backstop.

**{{FOUNDER_NAME}}-uninformed amendments.** Farrice infers {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s intent from a half-finished thought and updates without confirmation. The doc now reflects Farrice's interpretation, not {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s decision. *Mitigation:* Workflow Step 5 — {{FOUNDER_NAME}}-confirms-in-writing. If {{FOUNDER_NAME}} hasn't written confirmation, the amendment is a draft, not live.

**Document staleness.** A doc hasn't been touched in 6+ months and reality has moved on. The doc still reads "Profile #2 pending" three quarters after {{FOUNDER_NAME}} closed it. *Mitigation:* quarterly review (Section 7), monthly drift-check, and post-event amendments after every event ship. The room is the final editor; if the room taught us something, the docs hear it.

A fifth failure mode lives in human dynamics, not infrastructure: **collaborator-driven softening.** A designer suggests a wording change that smooths a sharp edge. A future curator recommends loosening curation to fill a slow event. A press contact wants a quote rewritten "for accessibility." None of these reach the BOS. The Constitution holds because {{FOUNDER_NAME}} named the lines and Farrice's job is to defend them in writing.

## 7. Quarterly BOS Review

Every quarter — March, June, September, December — Farrice and {{FOUNDER_NAME}} run a structured review. It takes ~90 minutes. The agenda:

1. **Read the 5 foundation docs aloud.** `00-master-index.md`, `01-brand-bible.md`, `02-icp-master.md`, `03-voice-document.md`, `04-positioning-one-pager.md`, `05-non-negotiables.md`. Reading aloud ({{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s drift-signal ritual extended to docs) catches drift the eye misses.

2. **Read the 12 Non-Negotiables aloud.** The same ritual {{FOUNDER_NAME}} uses for any uncertain decision. Quarterly, applied to the Lines themselves: are any starting to feel softer than they should? Has reality pressured any of them?

3. **Read the 10 Drift Signals aloud.** From `05-ops/03-drift-signals.md`. Which signals fired this quarter? Which got named at the time? Which got missed?

4. **Audit 3 random briefs against the latest brand state.** Pull three briefs at random (`02-briefs/*.md`). Do they still reflect the spine? Are they referencing a stale ICP? Did a voice rule change without cascading? Fix what's stale; log it.

5. **Update the change-log with a quarter summary.** A 3-5 line entry naming the major amendments of the quarter, the drift signals that fired, and the docs that needed cascade-fixes.

6. **Flag any doc that needs a full re-write.** Rare. Should be 0 most quarters. If a full rewrite is on the table, {{FOUNDER_NAME}} adjudicates whether the brand has shifted enough to justify it — most "rewrite" impulses are actually amendments in disguise.

The annual reckoning ({{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s anniversary ritual from Drift Signals) is a separate, larger version of this review with the full Internal Anchor in scope. The quarterly review is calibration; the annual reckoning is reckoning.
