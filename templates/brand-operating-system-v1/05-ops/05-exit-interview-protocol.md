# 05 — Exit Interview Protocol

---

## Why Exit Interviews

Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **Truth capture at the moment of feeling.** What people say at the door is closer to ground truth than what they post 3 days later. The room is still in their body. The questions get clean answers because the experience is fresh.
2. **The data {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s success spec depends on.** The 25% real-connection rate, the room-feel rating, the repeat intent — all of these only exist if we ask. Without exit interviews, those metrics are ghost numbers and the kill condition (12 events of failure) becomes unmeasurable.
3. **Content with permission.** Quoted with consent, exit interviews become the proof in IG content, the testimonial bank for venue pitches, and the founding stories of the brand. Stories over metrics — but the stories have to be captured.

---

## The Format

### Logistics
- **Where:** at the exit, before people leave the room. Not after they've stepped onto the sidewalk — too late. Not at coat check — too transactional. A clearly-marked spot, ideally with seating, just inside the door.
- **When:** during the natural flow-out window — the last 30 minutes of the event + 15 minutes after the official end.
- **Duration:** 2-4 minutes per person. No more. People are coming down from the experience and don't want to be detained.
- **Voluntary:** not gated to leaving. Anyone can decline. But the offer is warm, in-person, and invitational.
- **Privacy:** capturable in audio (with consent) or written notes. People should know what they're saying might be quoted (with permission).

### Who runs it
- **Event #1-3:** {{FOUNDER_NAME}} runs it personally. The founder doing exit interviews signals the brand's posture (we care about your experience) and gives {{FOUNDER_NAME}} unmatched data + intuition for course-correcting.
- **Event #4-12:** {{FOUNDER_NAME}} + 1 trained curator. The curator follows the script verbatim until they've internalized the spirit; they don't improvise the questions.
- **Event #13+:** As the operation scales, the curator team handles primary interviews; {{FOUNDER_NAME}} floats and does select ones.

The interviewer's posture: warm, present, unhurried. Not a clipboard. Not a checklist. Like asking a friend at the end of a wedding how it was.

## The Question Bank

Eight questions. Six asked verbatim every event. Two adapted to the specific event.

### The Six Core Questions

1. **"How was the room for you tonight?"**
   *Open. Lets them name what mattered without leading. Listen for: emotional language, body language, comparison to other rooms.*

2. **"Did you make a connection with someone you'd consider seeing again?"**
   *The success metric. Yes / No / Maybe. If yes, optionally: "What was it about them?" (Don't push.)*

3. **"Was there a song or moment that changed the room for you?"**
   *Captures the music's role. {{FOUNDER_NAME}} uses this to calibrate the next set. If no answer comes, that's a signal too — the curation didn't land.*

4. **"On a scale of 1-10, how would you rate the room?"**
   *Quantitative anchor. Pair with: "What would have made it a 10?" (Only if they answered ≤8.)*

5. **"Are you thinking about who you'd bring next time?"**
   *Repeat-and-bring-a-friend signal. Yes / No / Maybe. If yes: "Tell me about them" (briefly, optional).*

6. **"Anything you want to tell us — about tonight, about {{BRAND_NAME}}, about us?"**
   *The open exit. Often the deepest data comes here. Listen for: drift signals (e.g., "It would be even better if there were drinks"), confirmation signals (e.g., "I haven't done anything like this in years"), and unprompted referrals.*

### The Two Adaptive Questions

7. **Event-specific question.** Tailored to the event's signature moment or experiment. Examples:
   - For Event #1: "What's something you didn't expect about tonight?"
   - For an event where {{FOUNDER_NAME}} introduced a new format element: "How did the [X moment] feel to you?"
   - For a venue-pivot event: "Did this venue feel right for the kind of meeting we're doing?"

8. **Permission question.** Asked last, only if the person seems open:
   - *"If something you said tonight ends up in our content — quoted and anonymous, or quoted with your name if you'd like — would that be okay?"*
   - Three-way answer: Yes anonymous / Yes with name / Prefer not to share publicly.
   - Capture this clearly. No quote leaves the room without explicit consent.

## Capture Method

### Per-attendee record
For each interview, log:
- Attendee name (if shared) or anonymous ID
- Event date
- Quick demographics (age range, profile fit — Profile #1 / #2 / #3 / Other)
- Answers to all 8 questions
- Direct quotes (verbatim where possible)
- Permission level (anonymous / named / no public share)
- Interviewer's read: any notable body language, energy, what wasn't said

### Tooling
- **Phase 1 (Event #1-3):** voice-memo on {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s phone immediately after each interview, transcribed within 24 hours. Spreadsheet for the structured fields.
- **Phase 2 (Event #4-12):** simple Notion database with the structured fields, voice-memo backup.
- **Phase 3 (Event #13+):** curator-team-shared system with permission-tagged quote bank for content team.

### Aggregation
After each event:
- Per-event debrief (one page) — see `05-ops/04-success-metrics.md` for the metric structure.
- Quote bank update — pull permission-cleared quotes into the asset bank for content use.
- Drift-signal scan — were any signals from `05-ops/03-drift-signals.md` named in interviews? If yes, surface in the debrief.

## What the Interviewer Doesn't Do

- **Doesn't lead the witness.** No "did you have a great time?" — leads to "yes." Open questions only.
- **Doesn't sell.** This is not the moment to upsell the next event or push membership. The interview is an act of attention, not conversion.
- **Doesn't fix.** If someone names a problem, the interviewer says "thank you for telling me" and moves on. Fixing happens after, not in the moment.
- **Doesn't extract more than offered.** If an answer is short, that's the answer. Don't probe.
- **Doesn't promise content visibility.** "If we share this, it's only with your permission" — never "we'll definitely use this."

---

## What the Interviewer Does Do

- **Names what they hear.** "It sounds like the X moment was the one for you." Reflection signals presence.
- **Holds eye contact.** People can tell if they're being listened to. The data quality scales with the listening quality.
- **Captures the body.** What did they look like saying it? Lit up? Tearing up? Quiet? Note it.
- **Thanks them.** Genuinely. Their willingness to give 3 minutes is part of the room's good citizenship.

## The Failure Modes (And What To Do)

### Failure 1: Nobody wants to do it
If <40% of attendees agree to be interviewed, the offer isn't warm enough or the placement is off. Adjust:
- Move the spot. Try seating instead of standing.
- Reframe the ask: "{{FOUNDER_NAME}} wants to know how tonight was for you" beats "would you mind a quick interview?"
- Lower the friction: "60 seconds at the door before you grab your coat" beats "a few minutes."

### Failure 2: Interviews skew positive
If everyone says 9-10 across the board, the interviewer is leading or the sample is self-selected (only happy people stayed to be interviewed). Mitigation:
- Capture from people leaving early, too — the data is more honest from the people who didn't love it.
- Use anonymous written supplementary cards for people who don't want to talk.

### Failure 3: Quote bank goes unused
If we collect quotes and they never make it into content, the interviewers will stop caring. Mitigation:
- One quote per event surfaces in the post-event content cycle (with permission).
- The quote bank is reviewed in monthly content planning.

### Failure 4: Permission ambiguity
If we use a quote that someone said anonymous-only, we lose trust forever. Mitigation:
- Permission tags are mandatory and color-coded in the database.
- One person reviews permission tags before any quote is published — never the same person who collected it (cross-check).

## Privacy & Consent

- All interviews are voluntary.
- Permission is per-quote, not per-event. Saying yes to one doesn't mean yes to all.
- Identifying details (name, profession, anything that locates them in {{CITY}}) are removed from anonymous quotes.
- People can withdraw consent at any time. If a quote has been published, it gets pulled within 48 hours of the request.
- This isn't a legal posture. It's the brand's posture.
