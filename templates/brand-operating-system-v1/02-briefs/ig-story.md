## Section 2 — What This Brief Is For

This brief produces Instagram Stories — the 24-hour ephemeral surface where {{BRAND_NAME}} does the **daily-rhythm and warm-pull** work that feed posts and reels are too high-stakes for. Its job is **presence + intimacy + practical logistics**: keeping the brand in followers' top-of-mind without the production weight of a feed post, pulling contemplation-state ICP into dialogue via stickers, delivering event-cycle countdown information in a register that stays warm, and giving {{FOUNDER_NAME}} a low-friction surface to be a person (not just a brand). Stories sit at the **mid-funnel + retention layer** — they keep people who already know {{BRAND_NAME}} close, and they pull contemplation toward preparation through repeated low-pressure exposure. The one thing every story set must do: **feel like a friend who is mid-thought, not a brand running ads. If a story sounds like a marketing department wrote it, kill it.**

## Section 3 — ICP Target

Stories are watched by everyone who already follows {{BRAND_NAME}}, which means the audience skews toward **contemplation and preparation states** — the people warm enough to opt into daily check-ins. Less effective for cold pre-contemplation reach (use reels for that). Strongest for:

- **Profile #1 — {{ICP_PROFILE_1_NAME}}, the arts worker (LOCKED).** 33, Pilsen, contemplation edging into preparation. Watches stories on her commute and during tech-week breaks. Bridge Message: *"A {{CITY}} dance room, in daylight, sober, where you meet someone the way you meet a song — your body knows before your mind does."*

- **Profile #2 — {{ICP_PROFILE_2_NAME}}, the helping professional (PROPOSED).** 35, Logan Square, contemplation. Watches stories with sound off between clients. {{ICP_PROFILE_2_NAME}} is the most likely sticker-engagement responder (poll, slider, question) — her register engages with reflective prompts. Bridge Message: *"A daytime room, in {{CITY}}, where the music does the emotional labor — so you don't have to."*

- **Profile #3 — {{ICP_PROFILE_3_NAME}}, the quiet man (PROPOSED).** 31, Bronzeville, pre-contemplation. Watches stories rarely; mostly via warm referral (a friend texts him *"watch {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s story today"*). When he does watch, he watches all of them. Bridge Message: *"A daytime room. Sober. The music is good. We curate the floor so you can show up as yourself — not as someone hunting, and not as someone hunted."*

Routing: most story sets work for the umbrella audience and don't profile-target sharply. The exceptions: *event-countdown stories* lean hardest on Profile #1 (her preparation state needs the practical info); *exit-interview-spotlight stories* lead with whichever profile the quote came from; *hell-yes-filter stories* serve all three but {{ICP_PROFILE_3_NAME}} reads them with the most relief.

## Section 4 — Voice Rules (compressed)

Inherit voice from `00-foundation/03-voice-document.md` Section 6 — the AI Handoff Block — verbatim. Compressed:

> Warm, direct, confident, never preachy, never corporate. Show > tell at sentence level. Heart-encounter language as headline, body-first only as explanation. Em-dashes ≤ 2 per piece. No "Here's what / why / how" openings. No "It's not X. It's Y." reveal patterns. Name the enemy by name when it clarifies. Never preach sobriety. "Community" only when meant. Voice test: would {{FOUNDER_NAME}} say this to a friend over coffee?

Patterns this asset leans on most: **out-loud-asking** (P4, as poll/question stickers), **mechanic-as-sentence** (P6, one-mechanic-per-story), **hell-yes filter** (P5, end-of-set close), **crystallized-phrase stack** (P3, for daily-rhythm small moments).

The story-specific voice rule: **stories are read fast and felt slow.** Lower text density than feed (people consume in 4-7 seconds per slide), but the *register* still has to hold. {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s voice off-the-cuff is a feature, not a bug — a slightly handwritten quality reads as *real person* and beats a polished brand-deck story every time. **Nothing in stories should sound like marketing. If a sentence could be in a press release, rewrite.**

## Section 5 — Format Spec

Hard, not soft.

- **Dimensions**: 1080×1920 (9:16). Full-bleed.
- **Length per story**: 5-15 sec (the IG default — 15s for video, photos auto-set to 5s). Photo stories with reading-heavy text need 7-10s manual hold.
- **Story-set length**: **3-7 stories per "set."** Sets land in batches; followers consume them as a sequence. A single story is rarely enough to do narrative work; eight is usually too many.
- **Cadence**:
  - **2-4 stories most days** (daily-rhythm, small moments, music, mechanic-reminders).
  - **5-7 stories around event cycles** (countdown sets at T-7, T-3, T-1, day-of).
  - **1-3 quiet days when there's nothing to say** — silence beats filler. The brand is not desperate.
  - **No more than one set per day** unless event-cycle.
- **Text density**: **lower than feed**. People skim stories at 4-7 sec each. Max 2 short lines of overlay text per slide for non-text-led story types; up to 4 lines for hell-yes-filter or mechanic-reminder stories where copy is the point.
- **Stickers (used purposefully, never for engagement-bait)**:
  - **Polls** (binary or 4-option) — for daily-rhythm questions that are *real questions {{FOUNDER_NAME}} wants to know* (not "are you a hell-yes? YES / DOUBLE YES").
  - **Question stickers** — for genuine open prompts where {{FOUNDER_NAME}} will read and sometimes respond.
  - **Sliders (emoji slider)** — rarely; only for spectrum questions ("how much do you trust your taste in music when sober? — slider").
  - **Link-stickers** — for application links, manifesto links, RSVP forms. The only direct-conversion sticker.
  - **Location tags** — for venue posts only when the venue is publicly named (post-Event #1 mostly).
  - **Mention tags** — for crediting collaborators (the photographer, the venue manager when public, a guest curator).
- **Brand mark placement**: small `{{BRAND_NAME}}` wordmark in cream serif, bottom-left or bottom-right corner, sized to clear UI but not dominate. ~ 32px tall. Story-set's first and last slide always carry the mark. Middle slides optional.
- **Banned**:
  - "Tag a friend who needs this" stickers.
  - Countdown stickers used for fake urgency on non-event content.
  - Question stickers used as *"any questions?"* (lazy; people ignore).
  - Multiple stickers stacked on one slide (visual chaos).
  - Trending-audio music behind every story (use sparingly; a quiet story is fine).

Six story types. Each with a GOOD example (full sequence with copy, image idea, sticker if any) and a BAD example plus a one-line diagnosis.

### Type 1 — Daily-rhythm story

A small moment — {{FOUNDER_NAME}} cueing a record, a Pilsen sunset, the venue at golden hour, a track she's loving. Low text density. The visual is the substance; copy is a frame, not a pitch. 1-2 stories per day. The trust-building work.

**GOOD (3-story set, target = umbrella, daily rhythm):**

| # | Image | Overlay text | Sticker | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Close of a record on a turntable, label visible (a real Roy Ayers reissue), afternoon light catching the vinyl. | *"Tomorrow's room opens with this."* | (none) | 7s |
| 2 | Wide of an empty wood floor in a venue, sunlight slanting across, no people yet. | *"2pm sun on wood. The room before the room."* | (none) | 7s |
| 3 | Close of {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s hand on a coffee cup at a Pilsen café, sketchbook open beside it with a few lines of handwriting (an event setlist draft). | *"Thinking about how a song lands at minute 47 instead of minute 12."* | (none) | 8s |

*Why this works*: zero pitch, zero CTA, three visuals that tell the brand story without selling. The viewer feels close to {{FOUNDER_NAME}} without being asked for anything. Builds trust capital that a future event-invite story can spend.

**BAD:**

> *Story 1*: Stock photo of a sunset with overlay text *"Good morning {{CITY}}! ☀️ Hope you're having an amazing day! 🥰 Don't forget to follow {{BRAND_NAME}} for daily inspiration on living your most intentional life ✨💕"*

> *Story 2*: Same stock sunset, different filter. Overlay: *"Comment below if you're feeling inspired! 👇"*

*Diagnosis*: Stock imagery, generic well-wishes, "intentional life" wellness vocabulary, "comment below" engagement-bait — the four horsemen of brand-account stories. None of this sounds like {{FOUNDER_NAME}} or means anything specific to {{BRAND_NAME}}.

**GOOD (2-story set, target = Profile #1 ({{ICP_PROFILE_1_NAME}}) primary, Profile #2 ({{ICP_PROFILE_2_NAME}}) secondary):**

| # | Image | Overlay text | Sticker | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s handwriting on a sketchbook: a list of songs scrawling down the page, the day's date at the top. | *"I read every one of these. Some end up in the room."* | (none) | 6s |

*Why this works*: {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s actual register (*"the song that wrecks you"* — {{ICP_PROFILE_1_NAME}}'s language), genuine question ({{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s curating {{BRAND_NAME}} music; this is research), follow-up that closes the loop honestly (*"some end up in the room"* — true; sets the stake without overpromising).

**BAD:**

*Diagnosis*: Abstract wellness-vocabulary question that nobody can answer specifically (*"authentic connection"* generates word-salad replies); engagement-bait emoji stack; the question is a prompt for a brand workshop, not for a real person. {{ICP_PROFILE_2_NAME}}'s wince reflex fires in 2 seconds.

### Type 3 — Event-countdown story

T-7 / T-3 / T-1 / T-0 — practical info packaged warm. One key fact per story slide. The countdown is informational, not urgency-manufacturing — {{BRAND_NAME}} is a 50-seat curated event that won't artificially gin up scarcity.

**GOOD (5-story set, T-3 days before Event #1, target = Profile #1 (preparation) primary):**

| # | Image | Overlay text | Sticker | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cream background, serif. | *"Event #1 — three days out."* | (none) | 5s |
| 2 | The venue exterior at 2pm, real daylight, building visible. | *"Doors at 2pm. Music starts at 2:15. We're done by 5."* | (none) | 7s |
| 3 | Close on the application form on a small table, pen beside it. | *"If you're confirmed, you got an email Sunday with the address. Reply if you didn't get it."* | (none) | 8s |
| 4 | A photo of the water/coffee setup at the venue, no bottles. | *"There's no bar. There's water and good coffee and a snack table that won't ruin you. Eat lunch beforehand."* | (none) | 8s |
| 5 | A small wooden bowl with a few phones in it (mock-up). | *"Phones go in the bowl at the door. You'll find yours when the music stops."* | (none) | 7s |

*Why this works*: every slide is one operational fact, said in {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s register. {{ICP_PROFILE_1_NAME}} reads this and feels prepared instead of pitched. The mechanics (no bar, phones in bowl) are repeated as practical info, not as virtue claims — which is the right voice posture (*"we don't need it here,"* not *"you shouldn't need it anywhere"*).

**BAD:**

> *Story 1*: *"OMG only 3 DAYS LEFT until our LAUNCH event! 🎉🥳 Are you READY?? 😍"* with a countdown sticker.
>
> *Story 2*: *"DON'T MISS OUT! Spots are filling FAST!! Limited availability!!! Tap the link in bio NOW! ⏰"*

*Diagnosis*: Manufactured urgency ({{BRAND_NAME}} has 50 seats and a curated process, not "filling fast"); hype emojis stacked; "DON'T MISS OUT" is the wrong register for a curated event that explicitly filters out the unsure. The countdown sticker on a non-time-sensitive informational story is engagement-bait.

### Type 4 — Exit-interview-spotlight story

Anonymous quote (with permission), photo of empty room or candid moment behind it. Lower text density than the feed-post version; the quote is the substance, the image is the room. 2-3 stories per spotlight set. Use sparingly post-Event #1; pre-Event #1, use only with consented quotes from {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s network of singles who attended a practice run or a related {{FOUNDER_NAME}} DJ set.

**GOOD (3-story set, post-Event #1 example, target = umbrella):**

| # | Image | Overlay text | Sticker | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wide of an empty wood floor in soft late-afternoon light, the venue after everyone has left. | *"From an exit interview after Event #1."* | (none) | 6s |
| 2 | Same room, slightly different angle, sun lower. | *"'I went home and texted my sister. I told her I'd just been in a room where I didn't have to be charming, and someone still saw me.'"* — *K., 34* | (none) | 12s |

*Why this works*: the quote is doing the proof (specific behavior — *texted my sister*, specific feeling — *didn't have to be charming*). The image is the room itself, breathing. The link sticker on the third slide is purposeful (someone who reads the quote and feels recognized has a clean path forward). Permission is on file — assumed not optional.

**BAD:**

> *Story 1*: A stock heart graphic with overlay text *"Such a beautiful event! 💕 Everyone left so happy and connected!! ✨"*
>
> *Story 2*: A blurry photo of people dancing with text *"The energy was UNREAL! 🔥 So many soulmates made!"*

*Diagnosis*: Generic vague claims, zero specificity, "soulmates made" is a quantitative claim with no proof; the brand is selling itself instead of letting attendees speak. No exit-interview audio actually exists in this bad example — it's the brand voice talking *over* a fabricated success. {{ICP_PROFILE_2_NAME}} sees this and unfollows.

One of the four mechanics (daytime / sober / curated music / curated crowd) with one image illustrating it and one short sentence. 1-2 stories per reminder. Used when the brand wants to gently re-anchor the format without launching a full feed post or reel.

Rephrasing *"if you're a hell-yes, the link's in bio. if not, this isn't your event."* The filter executed gently for the story surface. Used at end of event-cycle story sets and occasionally as a stand-alone reminder during open-application windows.

## Section 7 — Visual Spec

Cite `01-visual/photography-rules.md` and `01-visual/DESIGN.md`.

- **Photography rule (non-negotiable)**: *If a photo could have been taken at 11pm, it fails.* Same daytime-only rule as feed and reels. (Brand bible §8.)
- **More copy-led than feed**: stories are read, not just seen. Type can sit on solid cream/terracotta backgrounds for some slides without violating photo-led rules — stories are the surface where this is permitted.
- **Hand-script accent for human moments**: {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s actual handwriting (sketchbook, setlists, application form, a quote scrawled in margin) is allowed sparingly. Hand-script over digital sans for any human moment. Never use a "handwriting font" — only {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s actual handwriting captured by camera.
- **Brand mark**: small `{{BRAND_NAME}}` wordmark in cream serif, bottom-left or bottom-right corner. Sized ~32px tall. Always on first and last slide of a set; optional on middle slides.
- **Color palette** (cite `01-visual/DESIGN.md`):
  - Cream — breathable background, primary type-on-solid surface.
  - Warm terracotta — accent on type-led slides; never as full slide background.
  - Midnight blue — rare, used for nighttime-of-{{FOUNDER_NAME}}-not-the-event moments (her listening to records at home, her sketchbook lit by lamp at 10pm — these are *{{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s life*, not *{{BRAND_NAME}} the event*).
  - Gold accent — sparing, only for couples / the moment / the story.
- **Typography**:
  - Vinyl-serif for hero quotes and key sentences.
  - Clean sans (Inter / Plus Jakarta tier) for body, attribution, practical info.
  - Hand-script (real {{FOUNDER_NAME}} handwriting) for signature-moments.
- **Banned**:
  - Bright pink, neon, club lighting, strobe.
  - Stock photography (especially generic sunset / heart / silhouette stock).
  - AI-generated portraits.
  - Emoji-as-icon overlays stacked (☀️🚫🎵💕).
  - Filters that fake golden-hour or fake sunlight.
  - Multiple stickers on one slide.
  - "Handwriting" digital fonts (Pacifico, Caveat, etc.) — they read fake immediately. Only real handwriting.

## Section 9 — Self-Check Questions

Before any story set produced from this brief ships:

1. **Spine fidelity**: Does the set honor heart-encounter, daytime, sober, curated? Does any slide drift?
2. **Profile fit**: Would the targeted profile ({{ICP_PROFILE_1_NAME}}, {{ICP_PROFILE_2_NAME}}, {{ICP_PROFILE_3_NAME}}, or umbrella) recognize themselves? Or could this be any dating brand's stories?
3. **Voice match (mid-thought test)**: Does this sound like {{FOUNDER_NAME}} mid-thought, or like a brand? Read each slide's overlay text aloud — if any line sounds like marketing, rewrite.
