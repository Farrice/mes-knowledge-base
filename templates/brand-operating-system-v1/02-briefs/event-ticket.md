## Section 2 — What This Brief Is For (purpose)

This brief produces the {{BRAND_NAME}} event ticket — the small, named object that lands in an applicant's inbox after they clear the why-gate, and (Phase 2+) the printed cardstock ticket that lives in their wallet for the week before the event. Its job is to do the opposite of what most event tickets do: instead of being a transactional receipt with a barcode and a row number, the {{BRAND_NAME}} ticket is *a small, kept object* — something the holder might tape to their fridge, slip into a journal, or hold in their hand on the morning of. It sits in the funnel right after Acceptance and right before Pre-Event Anticipation. The one thing it must do: confirm the holder is in the room, by name, and signal — through its physical and verbal posture — that the room is something they are arriving to, not consuming.

## Section 3 — ICP Target

The ticket targets the **specific named applicant** who cleared the why-gate. Not a segment. Not an umbrella. One person. The ticket is personal in the way an invitation to a small dinner is personal — it has their name on it.

- **Profile #1 — {{ICP_PROFILE_1_NAME}}**: an arts worker who keeps a leather journal she's had for four years. The ticket needs to be something she would slip between the pages.
- **Profile #2 — {{ICP_PROFILE_2_NAME}}**: a clinical social worker who curates her physical space carefully. The ticket needs to read as something that wouldn't embarrass her if it sat on her dresser for a week.
- **Profile #3 — {{ICP_PROFILE_3_NAME}}**: a designer who reads books and notices typography. The ticket needs to read as well-designed without trying — the kind of object he'd pick up because it looks like it knows what it is.

**Audience State**: action (they applied, they were accepted, they're coming). The ticket reinforces the yes — it does not sell. It confirms.

**Bridge Message (anchored in the ticket itself)**: One rotating line from the manifesto/anchor sits as the focal text on the ticket — *the manifest line.* See Section 6 for the rotation.

## Section 4 — Voice Rules (compressed)

> Warm, direct, confident, never preachy, never corporate. Show > tell at sentence level. Em-dashes ≤2 (often zero on a ticket — every dash is a sentence the design isn't doing). No "Here's what/why/how" openers. No marketing language anywhere. The ticket carries one quote, the recipient's name, the date, the place, the protocol of the room — nothing else. Voice test: if the ticket were laid on a table next to a wedding invitation and a museum membership card, would it read as belonging to the same family of well-considered objects?

**Patterns this asset leans on most**:
1. **Quote-as-anchor** — a single manifesto line sits as the focal text (Pattern 3 in voice doc applied to print).
2. **Mechanic-as-sentence** — the protocol details (doors at 2pm, phones in basket, sober, ~50 people) appear as full sentences with stakes, not as a feature list (Pattern 6).
3. **Personal touch print** — the recipient's name is PRINTED, not handwritten. (Handwritten names at scale read as theatrical; printed names with care read as serious.)

## Section 5 — Format Spec

### Digital Ticket (Phase 1+)

- **Dimensions**: 4×6 in (landscape), 1200×1800 px at 300dpi.
- **Delivery**: JPG attachment in the acceptance email, plus a PDF version for printing if the holder wants to.
- **Color profile**: sRGB for digital; convertible to CMYK if the holder prints it.
- **File naming**: `{{BRAND_NAME_LOWER}}-event-1-ticket-[firstname-lastname].pdf` — the file itself is personal.

### Physical Ticket (Phase 2+, post-Event-#1)

- **Dimensions**: 4×6 in landscape (postcard format).
- **Stock**: 14–16pt textured uncoated cardstock. Minimum weight: 110lb cover.
- **Texture**: lightly toothy uncoated stock (Mohawk Superfine eggshell or Crane Lettra equivalent). Never glossy. Never soft-touch. The texture is the design.
- **Print method**: ideally letterpress or thick offset on uncoated; minimally a high-quality digital print on the right paper.
- **Color profile**: 2-color print at minimum (Midnight-900 + Terracotta-600 on Cream-100 stock). 3-color if Slate-500 metadata is included as a third pass.
- **Production tier**: small-batch printing (50 tickets per event). Cost-per-ticket target: $1.50–$3.50 depending on stock and print method. The ticket is a small line item in the event budget — not a gift, but not a cheap-feeling commodity.

### Hard rules

- **NO dominant QR code**. A QR may exist on the back at <0.5" — it is never on the front, never the visual center.
- **NO "ADMIT ONE"**. {{BRAND_NAME}} does not admit. {{BRAND_NAME}} invites.
- **NO barcode** of any kind on the front of the ticket.
- **NO seat number, NO row, NO section, NO price**. The ticket does not communicate transaction.
- **NO script-font name**. The recipient's name is set in GT Sectra or Inter, never in Caveat or any cursive face. (The handwritten name reads theatrical at scale; the printed name with care reads serious.)
- **NO emojis** anywhere on the ticket.

## Section 6 — Hook & Structure Patterns

### Pattern 1: Quote-As-Anchor (mandatory)

The focal text on the front of the ticket is a single rotating line — the *manifest line* — drawn from {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s manifesto and anchor docs. The line is set in GT Sectra at 32–40pt. It is the thing the holder looks at first, every time they pick up the ticket.

**Five manifest-line options (rotate per event)**:

1. *"You meet someone the way you meet a song. Your body knows before your mind does."* — Manifesto v2 + Profile #1 Bridge Message.
2. *"The music does the emotional labor so the people don't have to."* — Manifesto v2 + Profile #2 Bridge Message.
3. *"A real room. Real daylight. The rest is up to the song."* — Anchor §3 + Pattern 3 voice rhythm.
4. *"You said yes to the same thing as everyone else in the room."* — Manifesto v2 (the curation principle).
5. *"By the time you speak, your body has already made the introductions."* — Voice Doc Section 3.1.

{{FOUNDER_NAME}} selects the manifest line per event based on the room she's building. Event #1 defaults to line 4 (the curation principle). Event #2 onward rotates.

> **GOOD:** *"The music does the emotional labor so the people don't have to."* — set in GT Sectra 36pt, Midnight-900, on Cream-100 background.

> **BAD:** *"Welcome to {{BRAND_NAME}} — your unforgettable {{CITY}} experience awaits!"* (Marketing language. Not a manifest line. Reads as a hospitality industry email.)

### Pattern 2: Mechanic-as-Sentence For The Protocol

On the back of the ticket, the protocol of the room is named in three or four sentences — never as a bullet list.

> **GOOD:**
> *Doors open at 2pm in real daylight. There is no bar. The music is {{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s, beat to beat. Phones go in a basket at the door — you'll find yours when the music stops.*

> **BAD:**
> *• Start time: 2pm sharp*
> *• No alcohol on premises*
> *• Curated music throughout*
> *• Phones not permitted on dance floor*
> (Spec sheet. Loses every reason behind every choice.)

### Pattern 3: Ticket-As-Keepsake (the design discipline)

The visual posture of the ticket is *would the holder keep this?* If the answer is no — if the ticket reads as a Ticketmaster receipt — the design failed. Every choice (texture, weight, type, the personal-name print, the manifest line) is in service of *would she tape this to her fridge for the week.*

> **GOOD design test:** Lay the ticket next to a wedding invitation, a museum membership card, and a small letterpress poetry chapbook. Does it belong in that family?

> **BAD design test:** Lay the ticket next to a Ticketmaster QR-and-barcode email printout, a movie ticket stub, and a "VIP" wristband. Does it look like it belongs there? If yes, kill the design.

### Pattern 4: Personal Touch Print (the recipient's name)

The recipient's first name (or first + last) is **printed**, not handwritten, in the system type. This signals serious, not theatrical.

> **GOOD:** *{{ICP_PROFILE_1_NAME}}* — set in GT Sectra 24pt, ink-900, beneath the manifest line, with adequate spacing.

> **BAD:** *{{ICP_PROFILE_1_NAME}}* — handwritten in Caveat 36pt with a pen-stroke flourish. (Reads as a calligraphed wedding place card. The brand is not theatrical; it is disciplined.)

- **Color palette**: Cream-100 (#F5EFE3) stock/background. Midnight-900 (#0F1A2E) for type. Terracotta-600 (#B8492E) NOT used on the ticket — the ticket is restrained, monochromatic-warm. The terracotta lives in the flyer and the website; the ticket holds Midnight on Cream alone, with Slate-500 metadata as a third tone.
- **Typography**: GT Sectra for the manifest line (36pt) and the recipient's name (22pt). Inter 14pt for body protocol details. Inter label-caps 11pt for metadata. Caveat is **NOT** used on the ticket. The single Caveat use elsewhere in the system ({{FOUNDER_NAME}}'s signature on a thank-you card) does not appear here — the ticket is set entirely in system serif + sans.
- **Layout**: 4×6 landscape with 0.4" margins. 1px hairline dividers in Border-Hairline. No drop shadows, no gradients, no decorative ornaments beyond the optional small wordmark glyph in the corner.
- **Photography**: NO photograph on the ticket. The ticket is type-led — the photograph lives on the flyer and the IG feed. Adding a photograph here breaks the keepsake register.
- **Anti-patterns**: see Section 5 + the Don't list in DESIGN.md.

## Section 9 — Self-Check Questions

1. **Spine fidelity**: Does the manifest line honor heart-encounter, daytime, sober, curated?
2. **Manifest discipline**: Is the line one of the 5 rotating options, verbatim? (No paraphrasing.)
3. **Personal-touch test**: Is the recipient's name printed in system type (GT Sectra or Inter), not in script?
4. **Keepsake test**: Would the holder tape this to their fridge? Or does it read as a Ticketmaster receipt?
5. **Voice match**: Could {{FOUNDER_NAME}} hand this to a friend the morning of and feel proud of the object?
6. **AI tells**: No marketing language. Em-dashes ≤2 (often zero).
7. **Banned phrases**: No "ADMIT ONE", no "VIP", no "save the date", no emojis, no community/intentional/conscious.
8. **Anti-patterns**: NO dominant QR, NO barcode, NO seat/row/price, NO photograph on the ticket, NO Caveat font?
9. **Family test**: Lay the ticket next to (a) a wedding invitation, (b) a museum membership card, (c) a small letterpress chapbook. Does it belong?
