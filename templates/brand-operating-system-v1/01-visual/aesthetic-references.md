## Group 1 — Daytime Dance Floor (the literal mechanic)

The genre {{BRAND_NAME}} is in. Real bodies, real daylight, real movement. The reference set proves the format exists and is photographable on-brand.

1. **Daybreaker (early days, c. 2013–2016).** Pre-acquisition Daybreaker — the original Brooklyn morning rave, when the format was sober dancing in a real warehouse at 7am. Search "Daybreaker Brooklyn 2014" image archive.
   *Extract:* The proof-of-concept that a sober, daytime, music-driven room can hold real ecstatic energy. Crowd composition, light source, raised arms in actual sunlight.
   *Caveat:* Daybreaker post-acquisition (branded yoga mats, smoothie bars, hype graphics) is in the do-NOT list. Early-era only.

2. **Sunday Service / Kanye early services (2019, before the merch).** When the format was a circle of bodies in a field, dawn light, gospel choir, no stage.
   *Extract:* The reverence in body posture. Daylight on faces. The quietness of a crowd that came for the room, not for the spectacle.
   *Caveat:* Religious overtones not on-brand for {{BRAND_NAME}} — extract the *staging* and *light*, not the iconography.

3. **Daytime warehouse parties — Berlin & Brooklyn documentary photography.** Stock-archive search: "afternoon warehouse dance Berlin." Look for documentary photographers who shoot the matinee Berghain Klubnacht ends, where bodies leave the building into daylight.
   *Extract:* The look of dance that has been going long enough to feel earned, photographed in actual outdoor light. The weariness-into-joy posture.

4. **Sunday afternoon DJ-led rooftop sets (Brooklyn, Mexico City, Lisbon).** Documentary photography of curated daytime parties — Nowadays Brooklyn outdoor, Mezcalería Tlacolula, Tubarão Lisbon afternoon sets.
   *Extract:* The composition — DJ booth in the middle distance, bodies moving on a wood floor in actual sunlight, no club fixtures.

5. **Wellness-adjacent but not wellness — sound-bath community gatherings (the older variety).** Pre-2018 sound-bath or movement-meditation gatherings in lofts. Bodies on real floors, real natural light, no Goop branding.
   *Extract:* The bodies-as-protagonists posture. Hands on chests, eyes closed, real diversity in the frame.

## Group 2 — Photography Aesthetic (real-bodies, real-light photographers to study)

The shooters whose grammar {{BRAND_NAME}} borrows. Not for their subject matter alone, but for their *light handling*, their *cropping*, their *willingness to leave a face in shadow*.

6. **Tyler Mitchell** (American photographer, Vogue, *I Can Make You Feel Good* monograph). Mitchell shoots Black bodies in actual daylight with a softness that refuses both stylization and flatness. Search: "Tyler Mitchell Vogue 2018" or his Pace Gallery archive.
   *Extract:* The honesty of the daylight. The way he lets bodies be still and just be looked at without performing for the camera. Color palette — soft warm pastels against natural skin tones — directly informs the cream-and-terracotta system.

7. **Petra Collins** (early work, c. 2014–2017, before the maximalist phase). Her early photography of teenage girls in real bedrooms with window light — the pinkness was *real light bouncing off real walls*, not a filter.
   *Extract:* The principle that warm tones come from real environments, not from post. Soft-gaze portraiture.

8. **Sandro Miller** ({{CITY}} portrait photographer). His {{CITY}} commercial editorial work — particularly his daylight portraiture on real city locations — is the local reference.
   *Extract:* {{CITY}}-specific environmental context. Brick, wood, window light from a real {{CITY}} window. Dignified portraiture.

9. **Annie Leibovitz — Vanity Fair editorial portraiture (the quiet ones, not the production-heavy ones).** Specifically her single-subject natural-light portraits where the room is the second protagonist.
   *Extract:* The placement of the body in the frame. The ratio of body-to-room. Decentered composition.

10. **Documentary wedding photography from the high end (Junebug Weddings annual best-of).** The photographers who get hired to document actual quiet ceremonies in actual rooms with actual light.
    *Extract:* Candid posture. Eyes-on-each-other framing. The grammar of two people noticing each other in a real room.

11. **Nan Goldin — *The Ballad of Sexual Dependency*** (use the daytime frames only — the ones in apartments and at kitchen tables, not the night ones).
    *Extract:* The "no permission asked" closeness. The willingness to shoot a real moment with no styling. Not the subject matter, the *gaze*.

## Group 3 — Color Palette in the Wild

Real-world examples of cream + warm terracotta + midnight blue + gold — proving the palette lives outside Pantone swatches.

12. **1970s record-jacket design — Blue Note Records reissues, ECM Records covers.** The Blue Note design archive (Reid Miles era, c. 1957–1967) — terracotta + cream + black-on-cream typography. ECM's later jacket design — restrained palette with a single saturated accent.
    *Extract:* The color discipline. Cream as canvas. One accent color held back. Type that earns gravity through scale.

13. **Mexico City contemporary interior design (Casa Wabi, hotels like Círculo Mexicano, Casa Bosques).** Terracotta walls, midnight blue tile, cream stone floors, gold detailing, all rendered in actual daylight.
    *Extract:* The palette in 3D, in real rooms, in actual sunlight. Proves it isn't a screen color — it's an architectural color.

14. **Jazz album art c. 1960–1980.** Specifically the *daytime* covers — Coltrane's *A Love Supreme* booklet, Miles Davis *Kind of Blue* alternate art, Pharaoh Sanders *Karma*. Warm-saturated color in honest light.
    *Extract:* The literary weight of a record-jacket serif against cream. The use of midnight blue as photographic background, not as design block.

15. **NYC daytime party documentary — Public Records Brooklyn afternoon programming, Nowadays daytime sets.** Photography from these venues during daylight hours — they intentionally book daytime DJ slots.
    *Extract:* The palette in motion. Warm wood, daylight through windows, real bodies in honest tones.

16. **Aimé Leon Dore (lookbook photography, c. 2018–2021 — before the over-stylization).** ALD's New York documentary lookbooks — old men in cafés, real Queens light, terracotta brick against cream walls.
    *Extract:* The brand grammar. Real environments. Real bodies. Restrained palette. Editorial sensibility on consumer goods.

## Group 4 — Typography References

The lineage of the GT Sectra display + Inter body pairing — and where the 1978-vinyl-jacket discipline comes from.

17. **Massimo Vignelli — {{CITY}} Subway map era / Knoll archive.** Vignelli's mid-century {{CITY}} design work has the structural confidence the system borrows. His typography is sterner than {{BRAND_NAME}}'s, but the *discipline* — fewer weights, generous leading, hairline rules — is the model.
    *Extract:* The principle that headlines earn gravity through size and color, not through bolding. The use of a single typeface at high disciplinary range.

18. **Reid Miles — Blue Note Records typography (1957–1967).** The serif-on-cream + photograph compositions that are the spiritual ancestor of the {{BRAND_NAME}} hero layout.
    *Extract:* The relationship between display serif and photograph. Type as caption to the image, not type as billboard.

19. **The New York Review of Books (long-form layout, current).** The NYRB body type — the way a 16px serif at 1.6 line-height earns long-form reading attention — is the body-text register {{BRAND_NAME}} approximates with Inter.
    *Extract:* The line-height discipline. The 65–75-character measure. The willingness to let the page breathe.

20. **The Gentlewoman / Fantastic Man magazine layouts.** The contemporary editorial design that nailed the "luxury without ostentation" register. Generous whitespace, single accent color per spread, photography as protagonist.
    *Extract:* The composition discipline. The trust in a single photograph + a single line of body type to carry an entire spread.

21. **GT Sectra in the wild — the typeface specimen and existing usage.** The Grilli Type specimen for GT Sectra, plus its use in contemporary editorial (search "GT Sectra editorial").
    *Extract:* Direct reference for the headline register the system uses.

## Group 5 — The vibe-NOT-this list (kill-on-sight)

Things that look like {{BRAND_NAME}} from across the room but aren't. Each is a documented failure mode.

22. **Daybreaker post-acquisition (c. 2018–present).** Branded yoga mats, smoothie sponsors, hype-bro morning-rave graphics, fluorescent green-and-purple gradients.
    *What's wrong:* The daytime mechanic is preserved but the *posture* is wellness-tech-bro. {{BRAND_NAME}} is more grown, more disciplined, more rooted in actual music culture. Avoid.

23. **Hum / The Class / Modern Wellness studio aesthetics.** Linen-clad practitioners, all-white interiors, minimalist sans typography, "self-actualization" vocabulary.
    *What's wrong:* Reads as corporate wellness. {{BRAND_NAME}} is not selling self-improvement. It is selling a room.

24. **Singles-event flyer design — Eventbrite mass-market dating events.** Pink-purple gradient backgrounds, photoshopped silhouettes of dancing couples, wineglass illustrations, sans-serif headlines screaming "MEET YOUR MATCH."
    *What's wrong:* This is the visual grammar of the *enemy* (Manifesto v2: "speed dating dressed as a party"). Avoid every element — the colors, the type, the subject matter.

25. **Sober-rave branding — *Daybreaker but harder*** (Morning Gloryville post-2017, the various "sober raves" that came and went).
    *What's wrong:* Insists on its sobriety. The visuals scream *"WE'RE NOT DRINKING."* {{BRAND_NAME}}'s posture is positive — *"clean meetings, in daylight, sober, with your full self in the room"* — not anti-alcohol-as-identity.

26. **Coachella / Bonnaroo branded festival aesthetics.** Hot pink + neon green, lens-flare graphics, "festival vibes" merchandise, group-of-friends-with-arms-around-each-other photography.
    *What's wrong:* Festival grammar. {{BRAND_NAME}} is a curated room, not a festival. Avoid the hype, the color palette, the maximalist composition.

27. **Speed-dating-event branding.** Stock-photo handshakes, name-tag illustrations, color-blocked checklists, friendly-corporate sans typography.
    *What's wrong:* This is the *exact enemy* the manifesto names. The visual language of a speed-dating event must never appear in any {{BRAND_NAME}} asset.

28. **AI-rendered "wellness retreat" stock imagery.** The Midjourney aesthetic of perfectly-lit, ethnically-diverse-but-airbrushed adults laughing in a perfectly-staged loft. Generic "lifestyle" output.
    *What's wrong:* Reads as AI on contact. Front-of-house imagery is real photography, period.

## How to Use This Document

**For a designer:** Use Groups 1–4 as a positive mood board. Reference 2–3 entries per design decision. Group 5 is the kill list — when a design starts drifting, check it against Group 5 first.

**For a photographer:** Groups 1 and 2 are the binding aesthetic. Bring 3–4 reference images from these groups to the pre-shoot scout. The Event #1 shooter brief in `photography-rules.md` §5 names this explicitly.

**For an AI-image generator:** Group 5 is the prompt-banned list. Group 1 is the prompt-anchor list. The AI image rules in `photography-rules.md` §6 enforce both.

**For {{FOUNDER_NAME}} or Farrice in a design review:** Run the three tests from `photography-rules.md` §7. Then ask: which entry from Group 1–4 does this image extract from? If you can't name one, the image is decorative, not aligned.
