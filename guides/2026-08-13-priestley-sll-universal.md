- Short form uses Pain / Prize / Problem, optionally multiplied by verified News.

## The mental model

### 1. The framework owns demand generation, not the whole business

SLL is universal only inside a clear boundary. It connects content to a measurable commitment. That makes it useful for a consultancy seeking assessments, an ecommerce brand seeking guide completions and purchases, a software company seeking activations, a nonprofit seeking event registrations, or an internal initiative seeking enrollment. It does not become an accounting, delivery, hiring, or product-development framework merely because those functions exist in the same company.

This boundary protects usefulness. A framework that claims every business task becomes too vague to make decisions.

### 2. The kernel stays fixed; five adapters change

The kernel is:

1. short-form content makes the right person recognize their current state;
2. long-form content proves and explains the path;
3. a lead form captures the next commitment;
4. a named owner responds within a stated time;
5. actual commitments feed the next content cycle.

The adapters are the stakeholder, desired change, obstacle, credible proof, and next commitment. “Process” also adapts: consulting delivery, software onboarding, product selection and use, a store visit, event participation, a donation journey, or an internal rollout can all occupy the same structural slot.

### 3. Universal does not mean evidence-free

The recommendation engine can only match what the content makes legible. Pain, Prize, and Problem therefore need real customer or stakeholder language. Reviews, interviews, sales notes, support logs, search behavior, and prior outcomes are valid sources. An invented avatar is not.

The same rule applies to proof. Credentials, testimonials, measured outcomes, demonstrations, public guidance, and research must be real and permission-safe. Missing proof is marked as missing; it is never filled with plausible copy.

## Capability 1: Install the system map

### What it is

Workflow 01 creates the shared operating map used by every recurring workflow: buyer connection, at least five entries in each content lane, three proof stories, principles and delivery process, an offer shelf, cadence dates, and the downstream handoff.

### When to reach for it

Use it at the beginning of a new business, offer, client engagement, campaign, cause, or internal adoption effort. Also use it when content is being published but no one can explain how a post leads to a measurable next step.

### When not to

Do not reinstall it every week. Recurring production should use the existing map and update only the evidence that changed. Do not run it when the task has no demand-generation job.

### Worked example

The verification run mapped a workflow-automation consultancy. The buyer was an operations leader managing a fragile manual handoff. Pain centered on reconciliation burden; the prize was reliable flow; the obstacle was proposals that replaced the entire stack before diagnosing one handoff. Proof used measured before-and-after steps with human-review boundaries. The Product for Prospects was a Workflow Friction Score.

### Honest edges

The map is locally validated as a complete output shape. It has not yet been installed against one of Farrice's current offers in this session, and it has no new market event attached.

## Capability 2: Produce the weekly and monthly content layers

### What it is

Workflow 02 turns the lane bank into daily short-form pieces. Each piece occupies one lane and bridges toward the monthly converter. Workflow 03 creates that converter using a real proof story, three to five principles, the actual delivery or adoption process, one closed awareness gap, and one commitment.

### When to reach for it

Use short form when the weekly queue is thin. Use long form when short posts attract attention but there is no stable asset that explains why the approach works and what happens next.

### When not to

Do not draft from a generic topic list when the System Map is missing. Do not attach current-news language without checking the event. Do not make every post carry Pain, Prize, and Problem at once; lane separation is what keeps the targeting signal clear.

### Worked example

The ecommerce control used a candle buyer who could not judge scent strength online. Pain was an overpowering room; the prize was the right atmosphere; the problem was that poetic product names hid practical decision information. Long form became a room-size selection and safe-use guide. The commitment was a scent-strength quiz, not a sales call.

### Honest edges

The generated examples prove adaptability, not audience resonance. Actual platform and buyer response still determine which lane language deserves reuse.

## Capability 3: Capture and audit the commitment

### What it is

Workflow 04 selects a Special Offer, Product for Prospects, or time-bound Promotion, then writes Hook → Value Proposition → Credibility → CTA and assigns the follow-up. Workflow 05 counts what shipped, detects orphan posts and forms, checks offer age, and weights the next cycle toward content that produced commitments.

### When to reach for it

Use the form builder when attention has no hand-raise or the offer is stale. Use the audit monthly and at quarter turns. For noncommercial work, explicitly rename the commitment: register, attend, donate, volunteer, subscribe, apply, approve, or activate.

### When not to

Do not default to “book a call.” A product guide, assessment, trial, sample, event registration, or application may fit better. Do not optimize from likes when the declared outcome is a form completion, activation, attendance, or purchase.

### Worked example

The nonprofit control replaced LAPS with commitment follow-up. Short form clarified the pain of wanting to help without accepting an indefinite obligation. Long form explained the one-day project journey. The lead commitment was event registration or project sponsorship. The audit measured registrations, attendance, repeat participation, and donations.

### Honest edges

Priestley's original LAPS language is commercial. Noncommercial adaptations are valid only when labeled, because their downstream process and success measures differ.
