# AI Slop Detector — Prose-Level Pattern Awareness

> **Purpose**: Develop creative instinct for recognizing and avoiding AI-shaped prose. This is ear training, not a compliance checklist.
> **Activation**: Embedded in Step 5d of the content creation gate. Internalize these patterns — the goal is to stop generating them, not to mechanically scan for them after the fact.
> **Trigger**: Any content production task where the output will be read by humans (copy, posts, emails, scripts, guides, profiles, headlines, etc.)

---

## Why This Exists

People's AI detection instincts are now razor-sharp. A single "delve" or one too many em-dashes and your audience mentally files the content as "AI slop" — regardless of how good the underlying thinking is. Credibility dies on contact.

The existing quality system prevents slop at the *methodology* level (forces expert framework loading). This directive prevents slop at the *prose* level — the vocabulary, rhythm, punctuation, and structural patterns that make writing feel machine-generated.

**The real test is always**: "Would a human who writes well for a living have written it this way?"

**The principle** (borrowed from Dr. Kriukow): AI text is the most statistically predictable version of any given idea. Every intervention should increase unpredictability — in word choice, sentence length, structure, and emotional texture.

---

## 1. Structural Tropes

Formulaic reveals, throat-clearing, and canned transitions that signal "AI wrote this before thinking about it."

**The "Here's what..." family** — the single most recognizable AI pattern on social media:
- "Here's what no one tells you:"
- "Here's what broke it:"
- "Here's what that got me:"
- "Here's what I learned:"
- "Here's the thing:"

**Throat-clearing openers**:
- "Let me be honest:"
- "The truth is:"
- "Let's face it..."
- "It's no secret that..."
- "In today's fast-paced [world/landscape/environment]..."
- "As [industry] continues to evolve..."
- "Now more than ever..."

**False pivots**:
- "But here's where it gets interesting..."
- "Something shifted."
- "Everything changed."
- "And honestly? It's been a wild ride."

**Canned structures**:
- "It's not X, it's Y." (the negation-reveal — one of the most copied AI structures, now migrating into student essays and video scripts)
- "[Problem]? Meet [solution]."
- "X is more than just Y. It's Z."
- "Whether you're a [persona A] or a [persona B]..."

**Meta-commentary** (AI narrating its own structure):
- "In this article, we will explore..."
- "Let's break it down."
- "Let's unpack that."
- "As mentioned earlier..."

**The fix**: If you catch yourself using any of these, ask: Am I using this because it's the *right* move here, or because it's the *default* move? If you can't articulate why this specific structure serves this specific moment better than any alternative, replace it.

## 2. Punctuation Tells

### Em-Dash Overuse

This is the single biggest mechanical fingerprint. AI uses em-dashes every 50–80 words. Human writers average one per 500 words.

**Rule**: If a 500-word piece has more than 2 em-dashes, audit each one. Most can be replaced with commas, periods, or parentheses. Em-dashes are a spice, not a staple.

**Why AI does this**: Training data includes 19th/early 20th century prose that used em-dashes 30% more than modern writing. The pattern is baked in. Even when explicitly told to stop, models struggle to reduce usage.

### Other Punctuation Tells
- **Colon-before-list habit**: AI mechanically introduces every list with a colon. Humans often flow into lists conversationally or with a dash.
- **Bold-everything habit**: When everything is bold, nothing is. Bolding should create hierarchy, not decoration.
- **Missing punctuation variety**: Humans use semicolons, parentheses, question marks mid-paragraph, and ellipses for texture. AI sticks to periods, commas, and em-dashes. If your punctuation palette is three marks wide, it reads as AI.

## 3. Vocabulary Tiers

### Tier 1 — KILL ON SIGHT

These words are so AI-correlated that their presence alone flags content. "Delve" saw a 900%+ spike in academic papers post-ChatGPT. Always replace:

> delve, tapestry, landscape, leverage, robust, pivotal, realm, multifaceted, comprehensive, meticulous, beacon, commendable, intricate, intricacies

### Tier 2 — HIGH ALERT

Acceptable in rare, precise contexts where no better word exists. Default action: replace:

> showcase, underscore, noteworthy, paramount, vibrant, unparalleled, unprecedented, garner, accentuate, pioneering, transformative, seamless, groundbreaking, innovative, cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changer, empower, streamline, elevate, optimize, scalable, synergy, foster, democratize, reimagine, disruptive

### Tier 3 — LATINATE BIAS

AI defaults to the Latinate form; humans default to the Anglo-Saxon. Always prefer the shorter word unless the Latinate carries genuine precision:

| AI Default | Human Default |
|-----------|--------------|
| utilize | use |
| facilitate | help |
| commence | start |
| approximately | about |
| purchase | buy |
| demonstrate | show |
| endeavour | try |
| ascertain | find out |

### Inflated Significance Phrases

Phrases that inject fake weight without adding meaning:

> "stands as a testament to," "plays a vital/significant role," "underscores its importance," "continues to captivate," "leaves a lasting impact," "watershed moment," "enduring legacy," "rich cultural heritage," "breathtaking," "steadfast dedication"

### Promotional Hype Phrases

> "unlock the power/potential of," "supercharge your," "future-proof your," "stay ahead of the curve," "harness the power of," "push the boundaries," "at the forefront of," "pave the way for," "embark on a journey," "navigate the complexities," "master the art of," "foster a culture of"

## 4. Rhythm Tells

### Sentence Length Uniformity

AI averages 15–25 words per sentence with a standard deviation of 3–5 words. Human writing swings from 3-word punches to 40-word constructions.

**The test**: Read the piece aloud. If every sentence takes the same breath, it's AI-shaped. Good writing has a heartbeat — short, long, medium, short-short, long. Not metronome.

### Paragraph Uniformity

AI makes every paragraph the same length, following topic-sentence → supporting evidence → summary sentence. Same pattern, every time. Human paragraphs are uneven — some are one sentence, some are eight.

### "-ing" Phrase Pileup

AI ends sentences with participial phrase chains at 2–5x the human rate:

> "...improving outcomes while building trust and fostering engagement."

One "-ing" phrase is fine. Three in a row is a fingerprint.

### The Treadmill Effect

Circling the same idea with different words without advancing the argument. A 500-word section that contains 100 words of actual information and 400 words of restatement. Each paragraph must MOVE — introduce something the previous paragraph did not contain.

### Rule-of-Three Compulsion

AI groups everything in threes to fake comprehensiveness: "Speed, accuracy, and reliability." Appears in nearly every AI paragraph. Real lists are messy — sometimes two items, sometimes five, rarely three.

### Synonym Cycling

AI has a repetition penalty that forces unnatural synonym rotation. A person might be called "the CEO" three times; AI calls them "the executive," then "the business leader," then "the visionary founder." Humans naturally repeat names and terms. Forced variety sounds stilted.

## 5. Emotional Tells

### Performative Empathy
Expressing understanding without naming a specific cost, failure, or moment. "I understand how challenging that can be" says nothing. "I wasted $4,000 learning this the wrong way" says everything.

### Sycophantic Glazing
"Great question!" or any variant. "Absolutely!" as an opener. Excessive validation even when the idea needs pushback.

### Fence-Sitting
Refusing to commit to a position. Presenting both sides of everything, even when one side is clearly right. The "complement sandwich": say something nice about A, nice about B, conclude with "both have strong points." Humans pick sides.

### Motivational Poster Tone
Every challenge reframed as an "opportunity." Every failure as a "lesson." Relentless positivity with zero frustration, doubt, or genuine negative emotion. Real writing has texture — not everything is uplifting.

### Ghost Citations
"Studies show..." / "Research suggests..." / "Experts agree..." without naming the study, researcher, or expert. If you can't name it, don't cite it.

### Missing Personal Stakes
No mention of what something actually cost — time, money, reputation, relationships. No specific dollar amounts. No named failures. No embarrassment. Content without skin in the game reads as AI-generated because AI has no skin in the game.

## 6. LinkedIn-Specific Tells

*Load this section for LinkedIn content tasks.*

### The AI LinkedIn Formula (now widely recognized)
1. Bold punchy hook (1 line)
2. Line break
3. Two-to-three word sentences stacked vertically
4. Line breaks between each point
5. Closing engagement bait: "Agree?" / "Thoughts?" / "What do you think?"

This format was effective in 2023. In 2025–2026, it's a neon sign that says "AI wrote this." LinkedIn's algorithm now actively deprioritizes detected AI content.

### LinkedIn Phrases That Signal AI
- "Thrilled to announce..."
- "Here's what I learned:" followed by a numbered list
- Non-controversial opinions positioned as "hot takes"
- Achievement posts: announce milestone → express gratitude → end with motivational statement
- "I hope this message finds you well" (left in from ChatGPT drafts)

### Engagement Bait Closers
- "Agree?"
- "Thoughts?"
- "What would you add?"
- "Drop a [emoji] if you relate"
- "Repost if you agree"

## 7. The Fix Protocol

This is not "search and destroy every flagged pattern." It's a set of lenses to look through before delivery.

### 1. Read Aloud Test
Read the piece out loud. If it sounds like a TED Talk transcript or a motivational poster, it's AI-shaped. Good writing sounds like a specific person talking — with their cadence, their pauses, their rough edges.

### 2. Prediction Test
Read one sentence. Can you predict the next sentence's structure? If yes — if the writing is that predictable — it's too statistically smooth. Surprise the reader at least once per paragraph.

### 3. Fingerprint Scan (Quick Mechanical Checks)
- Count em-dashes (max 2 per 500 words)
- Scan for Tier 1 vocabulary (kill on sight)
- Check sentence length variance (must swing between short and long)
- Count structural tropes (max 1 per piece, and only if it genuinely serves the moment)

### 4. The Human Question
"What did this actually cost me/them? What went wrong? What was embarrassing? What specific moment changed something?" If the piece contains none of this, it lacks the human stakes that separate real writing from generated content.

### 5. The Specificity Test
Replace every abstract noun with something concrete. "Visibility" → "your ICP finding you on page one." "Growth" → "going from 200 to 2,000 followers in 6 weeks." "Impact" → "three clients who told me this changed how they sell." Abstractions are where AI hides.
