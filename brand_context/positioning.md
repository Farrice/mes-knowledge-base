## Brand hierarchy (the container outlives any one offer)
| Level | Job |
|---|---|
| **Farrice Cain** | Durable trust container: the person, lived experience, judgment, point of view |
| **Category point of view** | Reason to follow: honest products, real proof, choose the argument before producing more creative |
| **Flagship paid expressions** | The Control-Beater, The Angle Map |
| **Deliverable parts** | Decision Book, Angle Cards, Build First Card; never separate brands |

Offer-removal test: if every offer disappeared tomorrow, a cold visitor should still know whom Farrice helps, what he believes, and why his judgment is relevant.

## One-line position
Creative strategist for supplement and performance brands. Campaign angles in 48 hours and claim-safe ad creative in 72, from someone who spent 18 years training the people who buy the product.

## Internal strategy line (not a public tagline)
Stronger arguments for honest products.

## Approved public anchors (his words)
- "18 years training the people who buy your supplement. Now I write your ads."
- "I don't make weak products louder."
- "I don't turn a fairy-dust dose into a heroic headline."
- "The best product almost never has the best marketing."
- "Supplement ads that don't die in Meta review."
- A new hook or format is not a new campaign angle.
- More creative can become a socially safer substitute for choosing one message direction.

## Why him and not an agency or a $5 AI-ad gig (About, verbatim)
"I spent 18 years as a personal trainer before this. I know what your customer mutters at 3pm when they're negotiating with themselves about the gym, why the drawer of half-finished supplements exists, and which promise makes them roll their eyes. That's where converting angles come from — and it's why I don't write miracle claims, the kind that get supplement ads killed in review before they spend a dollar."

## The three doors (cheapest first; pointer, verify in the profile package)
1. **The Control-Beater** — $500, 72 hours. 5 statics + 1 video to test against the current control. Problem-callout hooks; every claim screened against published Meta health policy and FTC guidance. Any Meta policy rejection fixed or replaced free.
2. **The Angle Map** — $750, 48 hours. 60-minute live read, three named campaign angles (belief, hooks, proof direction, claim-safety line) and which to build first.
3. **The Proof-to-Market Sprint** — $2,500, 10 business days, for a dated occasion (launch, retail push, reformulation, new ingredient). Third door only; not the front of the brand.

Shadow test running in parallel (not on the profile until a paid event): the 15-Ad Creative Test Pack, $1,000 prepaid, five materially different claim-aware hypotheses for a brand already running Meta ads.

## Authority posture
Bounded strategic authority as a category practitioner, never a campaign-performance celebrity. Lived facts: five years at The Vitamin Shoppe, coaching since 2008, 1,000+ people coached, Tier X at Equinox. These establish fluency in buyer desire, labels, doses, promises, and behavior. They do not establish ROAS, revenue, medical, legal, or compliance authority. Claims screening is editorial; the client's compliance owner keeps final approval.

## Until permissioned client evidence exists
Teach from lived observation. Make the reasoning inspectable. Use labeled demonstrations and teardowns. Leave public proof positions empty. No testimonials, client logos, results, or unconfirmed credentials. AI stays backstage: "Production runs on an AI system I built myself" is the only mention, and it explains speed, not quality.

## Explicit exclusions
Pre-revenue idea seekers · buyers wanting finished campaign production without a decision · unsupported-product promoters · performance-guarantee seekers · anyone asking him to replace legal, scientific, medical, or compliance review.

## Rules that outrank craft
- Send-before-build. Only sent, held, sold, collected validate anything.
- Never lead with AI capability claims. Lead with judgment and receipts.
- Never manufacture urgency. Not a fit → say so.
- One indefensible client kills the positioning.
