# Ghostwriting Voice Engine — Genius Context

> Load before executing any workflow. Full extraction intelligence.

## Core Operating System

GVE synthesizes 5 expert domains into a single deployable ghostwriting system: voice extraction (Lara Acosta), character embodiment (Mitch Albom), voice crystallization (Erica Mallet), sentence craft (Nicolas Cole), and rhetorical elevation (Ward Farnsworth). The pipeline flows: Extract -> Embody -> Crystallize -> Refine -> Elevate. Voice lives in the "imperfections" — the unusual word choices, rhythm breaks, personality quirks that most ghostwriters optimize OUT.

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### Voice Extraction (Lara Acosta)

### GP1: 5-Dimension Voice Scan
Map voice across 5 orthogonal dimensions: (1) Vocabulary Patterns — signature phrases, technical/conversational balance, metaphor preferences. (2) Sentence Architecture — average length, simple/complex/fragment mix, rhythm. (3) Emotional Range — default mode, passion triggers, intensity markers, humor style. (4) Perspective Markers — I/you/we ratios, question frequency, direct address. (5) Unique Identifiers — phrases only THEY use, unusual word combos, recurring metaphors.

### GP2: Signature Phrase Extraction
Collect 3-5 samples. Highlight phrases appearing 2+ times. Categorize as openers ("Look--"), transitions ("Here's the thing--"), intensifiers ("Dead wrong"), closers ("Your call."). Build library of 10-15 tagged phrases.

### GP3: Rhythm Fingerprinting
Map dominant rhythm pattern (e.g., "Short-short-short-LONG explanation-short punch"). Count average sentence length. Measure fragment usage. Note where they accelerate (passion) and decelerate (teaching).

### GP4: Emotional Range Mapping
Identify default emotional mode, passion triggers, intensity markers (ALL CAPS, dashes, clustering), humor style. Map full range from calm authority to urgent intensity.

### GP5: Voice Discovery Interview
Structured interview: "What do you believe your industry gets wrong?" "How do you explain what you do to a stranger?" "What topic could you rant about for an hour?" "What phrases do friends mock you for repeating?" Use when client has <3 writing samples.

### GP6: Dual-Persona Lens
For every voice element, ask: "Would this resonate with someone who PAYS them?" AND "Would this make someone SHARE their content?" Voice only for buyers lacks viral reach; voice only for entertainment lacks conversion.

### Voice Embodiment (Mitch Albom)

### GP7: Voice Archaeology
Study samples for recurring argument structures, belief assumptions underlying word choices, what they emphasize vs. skip, where language becomes most precise (passion zones) vs. vague (discomfort zones).

### GP8: Belief Mapping
Map what they believe about domain, audience, success, failure. A tech CEO who believes "execution beats strategy" writes differently than one believing "systems beat effort." Goal: finish their sentences.

### GP9: BECOME, Don't Imitate
Like an actor preparing for a role: absorb worldview, not just word patterns. Before writing, spend 5 minutes re-reading their best samples until you hear their voice. Write from inside their perspective, not while looking at a checklist.

### GP10: Creative Latitude Doctrine
You are not a transcription service. Capture their ideas and express them better than they would on their own. Find what they're TRYING to say and say it with their voice at its sharpest. Client reads it and says "I wish I'd written this."

### GP11: Contrast Mapping
Compare client samples against their stated influences. Where they sound like Naval Ravikant = derivative. Where they sound like nobody else = original. Amplify original, reduce derivative.

### GP12: Validation Through Anticipation
Before delivering content: "Could I predict their response to a controversial take in their domain?" If yes, embodiment is deep enough. If no, return to Belief Mapping.

### Voice Crystallization (Erica Mallet)

### GP13: 6-Component Voice DNA
Document: (1) Sentence Architecture. (2) Vocabulary Fingerprint — signature phrases, preferred verbs, jargon integration. (3) Emotional Range — calibrate on 4 spectrums: Reserved<>Expressive, Clinical<>Personal, Serious<>Playful, Detached<>Vulnerable. (4) Humor Integration type. (5) Structural Signatures — setup/payoff, list builder, story frame, myth bust, direct hit. (6) Specificity Patterns — Abstract<>Hyper-Specific.

### GP14: 4-Axis Voice Spectrum
Position on: Formality (Academic<>Conversational), Intensity (Understated<>Emphatic), Pacing (Deliberate<>Rapid), Intimacy (Professional Distance<>Personal Closeness). Each with measurable indicators.

### GP15: Do/Don't Guardrails
ALWAYS DO list (contractions, specific numbers, metaphor family, rhythm). NEVER DO list (corporate jargon, hedging language, engagement bait, off-domain topics). Scannable in 60 seconds.

### GP16: Voice Drift Warnings
Document 4-5 most common drift patterns per client. Examples: formality creep (dropping contractions), specificity loss ("many people"), teaching mode creep, over-qualifying ("I think maybe...").

### Voice Refinement (Nicolas Cole)

### GP17: Voice-First Optimization
Before ANY edit, identify voice markers — unusual word choices, rhythm breaks, quirks. Tag as PROTECTED. Then optimize everything else. Voice markers survive even when they violate "rules."

### GP18: Terminal Word Power Placement
Scan every sentence for terminal word weakness (ending in: is, are, was, it, this, that, the, a, an). Restructure so final word is power noun or strong verb.

### GP19: Three-Pass Economic Compression
Pass 1: Write freely. Pass 2: Circle every word under 4 letters, remove as many as possible. Pass 3: Reorder for flow. Target 30-50% reduction preserving meaning and voice markers.

### GP20: Rhythm Engineering
Map client's rhythm pattern from profile. Restructure paragraphs to match. Where sentences cluster at same length, introduce variation. Respect fragment preference. Last thing to tune.

### Voice Elevation (Ward Farnsworth)

### GP21: Saxon Punch
Build 1-2 sentences using Latinate, multi-syllabic words (intellectual tension). End with short, stony Saxon words (emotional release). "We obsess over optimization of methodologies... But eventually, you must sit down, shut up, and work." Max 1-2 per post.

### GP22: Chiasmus Reversal (ABBA)
"[Concept A] does not dictate [Concept B]. [Concept B] dictates [Concept A]." Creates closed logical loop reader cannot escape. Max 1 per post.

### GP23: Epistrophe (Apistrophic Echo)
Repeat same word at END of 2-3 consecutive clauses. "...of the people, by the people, for the people." Concept lingers after reading. Best in closings.

### GP24: Anaphora (Structural Priming)
Repeat phrase at START of 3+ consecutive clauses with escalating content. Primes reader's ear for crescendo. Like sports announcer building toward the goal.

### GP25: Two Languages Translation
State idea twice: first in conceptual language (intellect), then visceral language (gut). Connect with conjunction, dash, or hard period. Intellectual validation + emotional gut-punch.

## Signature Moves

1. **5-Dimension Deep Scan** — Analyzes all client content across Vocabulary, Sentence Architecture, Emotional Range, Perspective Markers, and Unique Identifiers. Deploy at client onboarding.
2. **Belief-System Archaeology** — Excavates core beliefs, assumptions, and argument structures to ensure writing from internal logic. Deploy before any significant content generation.
3. **Creative Elevation Protocol** — Expresses client's best thinking in most refined form, identifying what they intend to say and articulating with enhanced clarity within voice boundaries. Deploy during initial drafting.
4. **6-Component Voice Crystallization** — Deconstructs voice into 6 engineerable components for consistent deployment or team handoff. Deploy after initial profiling.
5. **Voice-Guarded Refinement** — Applies sentence-level optimization after tagging core voice markers as "protected." Deploy during final editing.
