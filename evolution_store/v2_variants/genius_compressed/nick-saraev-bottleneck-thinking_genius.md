## Core Operating System

Every business is a pipeline; the narrowest point (the bottleneck) dictates the speed of the entire system. The only work that matters is identifying and widening it — everything else is a strategic error. This is Goldratt's Theory of Constraints compressed for solopreneurs and run as a continuous flywheel, not a one-time audit.

---

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: Pipeline Visualization
Force any business into a left-to-right sequential pipeline (Lead Gen > Sales > Onboarding > Fulfillment > Delivery > Retention > Advocacy) before analyzing anything. Zoom into each stage as its own sub-pipeline with concrete steps. You can't diagnose what you can't see.

### GP2: Fluid Dynamics Constraint Model
Business growth isn't "add more things" — it's "find the narrowest pipe and widen it." Improving non-bottleneck stages has literally zero impact on total output, no matter how fast or wide those stages are.

### GP3: The Business Improvement Flywheel (4 Steps)
1. Identify the constraint (slowest part). 2. Widen it (invest ALL resources here). 3. Find new constraints (different stage becomes bottleneck). 4. Repeat. The advantage is cycle speed: people who run this in days win against those who take months.

### GP4: Revenue-Tier Constraint Mapping
<$10K/mo = Lead Gen bottleneck (100% focus on lead gen). $10K-$25K/mo = Fulfillment/Delivery bottleneck. >$25K/mo = Hiring/People bottleneck. These are heuristics, not absolutes, but they eliminate "what should I work on?" paralysis.

### GP5: The "Strategic Error" Frame
Non-bottleneck work isn't "less optimal" — it's a strategic error. Every hour on non-constraint work actively reduces business output by diverting resources from the one place they'd have impact. Kills shiny object syndrome dead.

## Signature Moves

1. **Pipeline Deconstruction Sprint** — Forces the business into a simple left-to-right pipeline visualization immediately, often on a whiteboard, to reveal stages and choke points
2. **The "Strategic Error" Hammer** — Labels any non-bottleneck work as a "strategic error" (not just "suboptimal") to cut through indecision and redirect energy
3. **"Widen or Replace?" Interrogation** — Asks whether the bottleneck can be optimized or needs fundamental replacement, preventing endless low-impact tweaking
4. **Oscillation Expectation Setting** — Proactively warns that widening one bottleneck will surface a new one, often in a previously "fixed" area
5. **Sovereignty Choice Point Inquiry** — Before widening major bottlenecks (especially hiring), explicitly asks if the resulting business aligns with the owner's desired lifestyle
