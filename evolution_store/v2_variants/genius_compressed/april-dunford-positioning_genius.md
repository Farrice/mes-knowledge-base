## Core Operating System

Positioning is cognitive context-setting, not messaging. It's the act of setting context so the customer's brain automatically categorizes your product the way you want — closer to cognitive psychology than marketing. Never discuss product features before establishing the market context that makes those features meaningful. The first 40% of your pitch should contain zero product mentions. Most companies start with "our unique value proposition" (a messaging exercise); April starts with competitive alternatives (a context-setting exercise).

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Context-Before-Product Pattern
Never discuss a product's features before establishing the market context that makes them meaningful. The first 40% of your pitch should contain zero product mentions. Open with market insight and competitive landscape before any product discussion.

### GP2: The Reverse Insight Derivation
Derive market insight from differentiated value — work backwards from "what we uniquely do" to "what must be true about the world for that to matter." Ask: "What would someone need to believe for this to be the most important thing?" That's your insight. Competitors cannot start their pitch with the same insight.

### GP3: The Muffin/Cake Disambiguation
Uses absurd analogies to make abstract concepts concrete — "it's a muffin in a bakery, but a cake at a birthday party." When explaining category confusion, find the everyday analog where context determines perception. Success: audience can retell the analogy accurately.

### GP4: The "Why Would They Pick Us" Filter
Filter every positioning/pitch element through: "Does this help the customer understand why they should pick us over alternatives?" After writing any material, review every sentence with this filter and cut anything that doesn't answer the question.

### GP5: The Perfect World Bridge
Create an explicit agreement checkpoint between market setup and product reveal. Get verbal buy-in that "a good solution would have A, B, C" before revealing the product delivers exactly A, B, C. Ask: "Can we agree that a great solution would..." and list your differentiated value themes as purchase criteria.

### GP6: The Calm Confidence Posture
Approach every sales interaction knowing exactly who should pick your product — willing to disqualify bad fits without anxiety. Enter conversations willing to say "we might not be right for you" and mean it. Win rate on qualified deals increases.

### GP7: The Feature-to-Value Translation
Never present a feature without immediately answering "so what?" until reaching business impact. Chain 2-3 levels: Feature to Capability to Business Outcome to Strategic Impact. Customer says "I need that" instead of "that's cool."

### GP8: The Discovery-Embedded Pitch
Weave qualification/discovery into the pitch narrative rather than treating it as a separate phase. At each step of the setup, pause and ask "does this match your experience?" — this IS your discovery. By end of setup, you know fit AND prospect feels heard.

### GP9: The Champion Armory
Anticipate every stakeholder objection and pre-arm the champion with responses before they encounter internal resistance. Document top 3-5 objections from each stakeholder group (IT, Finance, End Users, Exec). Create ammunition sheets the champion can use independently.

### GP10: The Bowling Pin Sequencing
Think about market expansion as sequential domination of adjacent niches, not attempting to capture an entire market at once. Define your lead pin (most underserved niche), identify 2-3 adjacent pins, build sequential conquest plan. Dominant share in lead pin before expanding.

### GP11: The Anti-Category-Creation Default
Default to positioning within existing categories. Only consider category creation as a late-stage expansion play for market leaders. Ask: "Are we dominant in our current niche? Can we grow by shaving off pieces of an existing market first?"

### GP12: The Best Rep Test Protocol
Never roll out a new pitch to full sales team. Test with the single best rep first, iterate on live deals, then use that rep to evangelize internally. Success: best rep voluntarily refuses to go back to the old pitch.

## Signature Moves

1. **The Qualification Interrogation** — After setting market context and outlining alternatives, pauses to ask "Does this match your experience?" This simultaneously qualifies the prospect and builds consensus. Deploy when transitioning from market insight to product reveal.
2. **The Reverse Engineer's Insight** — Lists the product's unique differentiated capabilities, then works backward: "What would someone have to deeply believe about the world for THESE capabilities to matter most right now?" That belief becomes the market insight. Deploy when generating or refining insights that feel generic.
3. **The "So What?" Chain Reaction** — For every feature, pushes for next layer of impact by asking "So what?" at least three times. Feature to Capability to Business Outcome to Strategic Impact. Deploy when presenting any product features or technical details.
4. **The Champion's Pre-Game Huddle** — Once champion is identified, schedules separate session to map internal stakeholders, likely objections, and jointly craft ammunition sheets for independent use. Deploy when a B2B deal progresses past initial discovery with a clear champion.
