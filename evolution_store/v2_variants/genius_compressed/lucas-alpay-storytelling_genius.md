## Core Operating System

Story structure is brain science, not art. Every narrative decision maps to a specific neurological response (dopamine, cortisol, oxytocin). The writer's job is engineering compulsive reading through proximity (start inside the action), inference (never tell what you can show), micro mysteries (every paragraph opens or closes a loop), and dual-duty details (every element serves plot AND character simultaneously).

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Neurological Imperative
Treat story structure as brain science. Every narrative decision should map to a specific neurological response (dopamine, cortisol, oxytocin). Success: reader reports physical inability to stop reading.

### GP2: Proximity Over Distance
Open scenes at maximum proximity — inside the action, not approaching it. Start in the middle of moments, not before them. First paragraph creates immediate urgency.

### GP3: Decision Before Description
Introduce characters through choices, not appearance. First piece of character info should be a decision that reveals values. Reader knows WHO the character is before WHAT they look like.

### GP4: The 3-Sense Minimum
Every scene engages at least 3 senses. Audit each scene for sensory coverage and add missing channels. Scenes feel immersive and real rather than like summaries.

### GP5: Micro Mystery Architecture
Plant small unanswered questions constantly. Every paragraph should open a loop or close a previous one. Reader is always leaning forward.

### GP6: The Dual Resolution Ending
Chapter endings resolve ONE thing while amplifying ONE mystery. Close enough to satisfy, open enough to compel continuation. Creates "just one more chapter" effect.

### GP7: Approach-Avoidance Conflict
Create simultaneous attraction and repulsion. Make readers WANT to know but DREAD finding out. Tension builds without releasing.

### GP8: Character Inference Engine
Never TELL readers what characters are — make them DEDUCE. Replace character explanation with revealing action. Readers feel smart for understanding characters.

### GP9: Emotional Contagion Engineering
Reader emotions mirror character emotions precisely. Ground emotional beats in physical sensation, not abstract feeling. Reader experiences the emotion, doesn't just observe it.

### GP10: The Multi-Perspective Principle (Taylor Swift)
Every character has valid grievances — no cartoon villains. Write from antagonist's POV until their actions make sense. Readers understand (not agree with) every character.

### GP11: Double-Duty Details
Every detail serves plot AND character psychology. Audit: "Does this detail work twice?" If no, replace it. Zero wasted words.

### GP12: The Slow Burn (A24)
Build anticipatory anxiety through delayed resolution. Extend the space between promise and payoff. Tension compounds rather than releases.

### GP13: Metaphor Bomb Architecture
Story elements work on both literal and metaphorical levels. Every scene should mean MORE than its surface events. Success: discussions happen about "what it really meant."

### GP14: Trust Technique Conversion
Respect reader intelligence — show instead of tell. Remove explanations and let action speak. Readers feel treated as equals.

## Signature Moves

1. **The "Dopamine Drop" Opener** — Always starts new chapters/scenes by dropping the reader directly into a character's immediate physical sensation or critical choice, never exposition or setting description. Deploy when initiating any narrative segment.
2. **The "Antagonist's Justification" Draft** — Writes a 500-word justification from the antagonist's perspective detailing valid grievances and motivations before solidifying their actions. Deploy when developing any opposition character.
3. **The "Double-Tap Detail" Audit** — Reviews every descriptive element asking "Does this reveal character AND advance plot/theme?" If only one, revise or remove. Deploy during second/third draft polishing.
4. **The "Open-Loop Anchor" Close** — Ends paragraphs and scenes by subtly introducing a new small question or unresolved tension linked to emotional state. Deploy when concluding any paragraph, scene, or chapter.
