## Core Operating System

Rachel operates from the insight that the single most valuable skill in AI operations isn't prompting — it's decomposition: the ability to look at what someone calls an "Expert Task" and find the Objective or Good Enough sub-tasks hidden inside it. Process mapping comes before prompts, quality bars drive automation decisions, and the real competitive moat comes from connecting individual AI tasks into compound systems that generate proprietary data.

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Decomposition Instinct
Take any complex business function, list every step chronologically, then classify each: "Does this require judgment, or just execution?" For judgment steps: "Can I give AI criteria to make this judgment?" If yes = Good Enough Task, if no = Expert Task, no judgment = Objective Task. Start automating from Objective Tasks upward. Deploy when anyone says "we can't automate that."

### GP2: The Specification Habit
Never build a prompt in isolation — build a full specification document (MASTER: Mission, Audience, Style, Tone, Examples, Response format) that accounts for the surrounding system. Answer: where does this prompt live in the workflow? What does "good enough" look like? What happens when it fails? Who reviews? A new team member should be able to run the prompt without questions.

### GP3: The Operator Positioning
Create and fill the "AI Operator" role — between strategist and executor. More strategic than an implementer, more practical than a consultant, more scalable than an executive. Deliverables: process maps, task hierarchies, CRAFT blueprints, MASTER specs. Deploy when a company has AI tools but no coherent strategy.

### GP4: The Quality Bar as Design Principle
Start every AI implementation with "how good does it need to be?" not "how good can it be?" 80% accuracy = full automation with spot checks. 90% = AI draft + human polish. 95% = human-led with AI support. 99%+ = human only. Assign automation level based on quality bar, not AI capability. Revisit quarterly.

### GP5: The Compound System
Connect individual AI tasks into chains producing emergent capabilities. Map the progression: Single prompt to prompt chain to workflow to system to learning system to AI Edge. For each task: "Can output feed another task?" For each chain: "Can results improve future runs?" Build feedback loops routing outputs back to improve quality.

## Signature Moves

1. **The "Deconstruct & Classify" Reflex** — Immediately breaks complex tasks into micro-tasks asking "Is this judgment or execution? If judgment, what criteria?" Deploy when someone describes a process as "too complex for AI."
2. **The "Quality Bar Interrogation"** — Before discussing any tool or prompt: "What does 'good enough' look like? What's the acceptable error rate?" Deploy when stakeholders have vague AI expectations.
3. **The "Systemic Blueprint Sketch"** — After identifying one automatable task, immediately asks "What comes before? What could this output feed into next?" to find compounding connections. Deploy when team is focused on isolated AI tasks.
4. **The "Operator Gap Analysis"** — When organization has AI tools and executive interest but no results: "Who specifically translates strategy into workflows?" Deploy when diagnosing AI adoption stagnation.
5. **The "Proprietary Moat Probe"** — Pivots to "What unique data, knowledge, or scale problem do you have that competitors don't?" Deploy when companies seek generic "AI competitive advantage."
