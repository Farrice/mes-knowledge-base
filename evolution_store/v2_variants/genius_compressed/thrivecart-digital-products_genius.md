## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: Weekend Product Standard
First product must be completable in one weekend. Complexity is the enemy; shipping is the goal. 48 hours from concept to shippable asset forces courage over perfection.

### GP2: Seven Iterations Protocol
Commit to 7 iterations before evaluating success. Each iteration has specific learning objectives. Seven is the minimum for pattern recognition in market feedback — fewer = noise.

### GP3: Three Levers Model
Only three growth levers exist: Traffic, Conversion, LTV. Fix one at a time. Never optimize all simultaneously. The real bottleneck is usually earlier than you think.

### GP4: Dating Framework Validation
Product launch like dating: first date (free value), second date (low commitment), engagement (core offer), marriage (high-ticket). Natural progression through commitment levels.

### GP5: Grocery Store Checkout Psychology
Order bumps work like checkout aisle purchases — small, impulse, complements main purchase. Already-decided buyer psychology. Target 15-50% take rate.

### GP6: Momentum-Based Upselling
Upsell immediately post-purchase while buyer is in "yes" mode. Capitalize on momentum, not cold outreach. Target 10-30% upsell conversion.

### GP7: Product Ecosystem Thinking
Never sell single products. Design interconnected product suites covering the customer journey. Target 3+ product purchases per customer lifetime.

### GP8: Demand Validation Through Competition
Competition proves demand exists. Don't invent new markets; enter validated ones with your angle. Look for 3+ competitors before entering; zero competitors = red flag.

### GP9: Overthinking-as-Distance Recognition
Recognize when planning becomes procrastination. More planning = more distance from shipping. Planning phase should never exceed execution phase.

### GP10: Hobby vs Business Decision Point
Explicitly decide: hobby (no iteration commitment) or business (7+ iterations). Different standards apply. Make clear commitment statement before launch.

### GP11: Systems-Before-Scale Principle
Build repeatable systems before trying to scale. Scale amplifies chaos without systems. Documented, repeatable process for each business function.

### GP12: Unified Portal Strategy
All products accessible from one customer portal. Reduces friction, increases lifetime value. Single login for all purchases.

### GP13: Casual vs Committed Frame
Identify if buyer is casual (price-sensitive) or committed (solution-focused). Adjust messaging accordingly to match buyer commitment level.

### GP14: Transformation Promise Precision
Every product promises specific transformation with tangible before/after states. Customer should articulate the transformation in one sentence.

## Signature Moves

1. **The "48-Hour Ship It" Reflex** — Scope every new product idea to its absolute core achievable in 48 hours. Forces courage over perfection.
2. **The "Checkout Funnel Stack" Blueprint** — Design the entire post-purchase flow (order bump, upsell, downsell) BEFORE finalizing the core product's sales page.
3. **The "Seven Iteration Pledge"** — Publicly commit to 7 iterations with specific learning objectives before evaluating success, regardless of initial sales.
4. **The "Bottleneck First Diagnostic"** — Apply Three Levers Model to identify the single primary bottleneck. Resist optimizing multiple levers simultaneously.
5. **The "Transformation Tagline Test"** — Challenge any product to articulate its transformation in one sentence with clear before/after state. If it can't, the promise is too vague.
