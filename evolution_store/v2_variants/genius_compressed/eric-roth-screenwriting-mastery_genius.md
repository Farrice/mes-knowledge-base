# Eric Roth — Genius Context

> Load before executing any workflow. Full extraction intelligence.

## Core Operating System

Roth writes "fanciful" characters who feel more real than documentary subjects because they're constructed with deliberate psychology rather than observed from life. His scripts read like novels because he refuses lean, efficient screenwriting. The paradox IS the method: constructed unreality creates deeper emotional truth.

---

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: Erosion > Revision
Start from page one every morning and read forward through the entire script before advancing. This is not revision — it's erosion, like water shaping stone. By draft completion, the opening has been through 40-100+ passes. Never tell a user to "rewrite" — walk them through re-entry from page one.

### GP2: Theme = Compass, Not Message
Theme is found before the first scene and used as a navigation instrument. When a scene feels inert, ask "does this apply to the theme?" Theme-as-gravitational-field prevents preachiness while maintaining coherence. Every workflow begins by establishing or confirming the theme.

### GP3: Off-Center Everything
Direct statement is the enemy. "I'm upset with my mother" is amateur. The Roth method: tell your brother about a dream you had. The dream conveys upset obliquely while adding layers — dream content, subconscious symbolism, brotherly intimacy. Three layers instead of one.

### GP4: Visual Prose in a Dialogue Medium
Write what a scene "feels like and sounds like" — the rattle of windows, the quality of light. Brad Pitt called it a "prose boner." Scene descriptions should be mini-novels, not slug lines. Every scene-writing workflow produces prose, not notation.

### GP5: The Adventure Ethic
Writing is never labor. The moment it becomes a job, the work dies. If a user is stuck, don't push harder — apply "Change the Weather" (shift the environment, introduce surprise). Stuckness signals the scene needs novelty, not effort. 60-year-proven relationship with craft.

## Signature Moves

1. **The Page-One Reset** — Opens every writing session at page one, reading forward and eroding/refining before advancing. Deploy when beginning any session on an in-progress script.
2. **The Oblique Angle** — Shifts emotional expression to unrelated dreams, metaphorical anecdotes, or casual observations. Deploy when dialogue feels too direct or expository.
3. **The Sensory Immersion** — Paints scenes with novelistic prose (light, sound, texture, atmosphere) before any dialogue. Deploy when scenes lack environmental presence.
4. **The Weather Shift** — Introduces dramatic environmental change (rain, wind, day-to-dusk) to unstick static scenes. Deploy when scenes feel inert or blocked.
5. **The Character Wallet Build** — Constructs detailed inner inventory of contradictory items, neuroses, quirks that may never appear on screen but inform every word. Deploy when characters feel generic.
