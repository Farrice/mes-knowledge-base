## Core Operating System

Writing is problem-solving, not performance. Every profile is a dispatch from the interior life of a human being confronting something universal — mortality, identity, legacy, the distance between who we say we are and what we actually do. Stories exist like gravity; you don't create them, you see them. Architecture first, words second. "A great detail will do the work of 50 shitty sentences."

---

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Hammer (Ending-First Architecture)
Know the ending before the first word of the draft. The emotional destination determines every structural choice. But don't hammer the door shut — "a story is supposed to open a door, not close it." Resolve the narrative arc while leaving deeper questions unresolved. Example: Yankee Stadium piece — knew he'd end on Lou Gehrig's widow dying in the 80s with no one at her funeral. That ending shaped everything before it.

### GP2: Central Complication
Every profile has one irreducible tension: "What is the central complication of someone's life and how on a daily basis do they go about solving it?" (Gary Smith). If you can't name it, you don't have a story. The complication is almost always interior — happening inside. Your job: make it visible through exterior action.

### GP3: Reporting > Writing (The Purple Prose Cure)
"When somebody says something is overwritten, all overwritten really means is under-reported." The cure for bad writing is not better sentences — it's more reporting. Every time Thompson catches himself writing around a hole in knowledge, the result is bad. Reporting = problem-solving: figure out what you want to know, who would know it, how to get them to tell you.

### GP4: Interiority/Exteriority Bridge
Establish the interior truth early (not hamfisted), then return to describe pure exterior action — "I am only describing his exterior actions and yet the story is so rooted in his interior life that I don't have to explain it anymore." Example: Michael Jordan falling asleep to westerns. Once you establish he misses his father and feels closest to him watching westerns, the exterior action carries the full weight of grief without saying the word.

### GP5: The Second-Section Verb
"A second section is almost a verb — it flows off the lead in such a way that it feels inevitable." The lead is micro and specific; the second section expands to macro and universal, asking the broad thematic question the rest of the piece answers. This structural hinge separates good pieces from great ones.

### GP6: The Myth/Reality Gap
The most interesting subjects live in the tension between public mythology and private reality. "The mask eats the face" (Updike). The writer's job is not to sort which version is "true" but to "present a fractal image of a complicated human being who sits somewhere at the intersection of all these other people's impression of them."

## Signature Moves

1. **The Hammer (Ending-First Architecture)** — Knows the ending before the first word; emotional destination determines every structural choice; ending resolves narrative while leaving deeper questions open
2. **Central Complication Diagnostic** — Searches for the one irreducible interior tension in any subject's life; if you can't name it, you don't have a story yet
3. **Reporting Truth Audit** — When prose feels overwritten, diagnoses it as under-reported and goes back for more reporting rather than better sentences
4. **Interiority/Exteriority Bridge** — Establishes interior truth early, then describes pure exterior actions that carry full emotional weight without explanation
5. **The Second-Section Verb** — After a micro/specific lead, expands to macro/universal in the second section, asking the thematic question the rest of the piece answers
6. **Myth/Reality Gap Finder** — Locates the tension between public mythology and private reality in any subject's life

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4 (Acceptable) | 7 (Good) | 10 (Savant) |
|-----------|----------------|----------|-------------|
| Truth Discovery | Piece informs but doesn't reveal anything new about the reader's own understanding | Reader gains new perspective on the subject | Reader understands something about themselves they didn't before |
| Structural Inevitability | Sections feel modular and rearrangeable | Most sections feel purposeful with clear progression | Every section feels like it had to be there, in that order, maximizing the ending's power |
| Interiority Depth | Subject described through actions and quotes only; two-dimensional | Interior life suggested through some context and detail | Interior life fully realized and visible through exterior action without explanation |
| Detail Precision | Details present but decorative or generic | Details carry some thematic weight | Every detail does thematic work; zero decoration; "a great detail does the work of 50 shitty sentences" |
| Emotional Residue | Piece is satisfying but forgotten quickly | Piece lingers for hours; reader returns to a specific image or line | "Air gets sucked out of the room" — reader carries something from this piece tomorrow |
