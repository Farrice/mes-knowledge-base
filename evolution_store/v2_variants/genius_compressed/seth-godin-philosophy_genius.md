# Seth Godin — Genius Context

> Load before executing any workflow. Full extraction intelligence.

## Core Operating System

Action creates identity, not the reverse. Every obstacle is either a solvable problem or an unchangeable situation — classify before strategizing. Ship generous work for the smallest viable audience, name your fears out loud, and replace perfectionism with consistency.

---

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: Resistance as Navigation Compass
When resistance appears, move that task to the TOP of your priority list. Resistance is information telling you the work matters — not a signal to retreat.

### GP2: The Fear-Status-Affiliation Triad
Decompose any stuck behavior into three forces: fear (of rejection/death), status (comparison), or affiliation (fitting in). Fear gets named. Status gets quit. Affiliation gets a scene change.

### GP3: Problems vs Situations Triage
Classify every obstacle: "Does this have a solution I don't want to execute? = Problem. Is this unchangeable? = Situation." Problems get action plans. Situations get acceptance. Zero energy on fighting situations.

### GP4: But vs And Diagnostic Reframe
Replace "but" with "and" in any complaint or excuse. "I want to grow AND my spouse doesn't like it" holds both truths without cancellation, turning excuses into design constraints.

### GP5: Smallest Viable Audience First
Ask "What's the smallest group where impact would be enough?" then "What's the smallest piece of work worth doing?" Start microscopically — one person, two hours, a PDF to 20 people.

### GP6: Pick Yourself Protocol
Stop waiting for authorization. Write it, email it to 20 people, post it tomorrow. "Here, I made this." Time from idea to public should be days, not months.

### GP7: Intentional Design (Who/What)
Every project starts with two written questions: "Who is this for?" (by name if possible) and "What is this for?" (what change does it create?). If the answer is "everyone," you're hiding.

### GP8: Perfectionism as Hiding
Define the spec. Three quality types: meets-spec (attainable), luxury (unnecessary), zero-defect pursuit (trap). Once spec is met, ship. Perfectionism's purpose is to prevent shipping, not improve quality.

### GP9: Consistency Over Authenticity
"Authenticity is a crock." Nobody wants you authentically cranky. Define "the best version of me" and play that role every day. Authenticity is for your best friend; everyone else wants consistency.

### GP10: The Generous Ruckus Frame
"Go make a ruckus" = generous work for people who care. Not attention-seeking. "Here I made this" is a gift, not a performance. Evaluate by "did it serve?" not "did it perform?"

### GP11: Where Do You Put the Tired
Accept fatigue upfront: "I will get tired AND I need to do both." Like a marathon runner — don't eliminate tired, plan where to put it. Optimize for endurance, not painlessness.

### GP12: Name It to Defuse It
Say hidden motivations out loud: "I'm buying $400 sneakers because I'm afraid of being left out." Named fears lose power. The more absurd it sounds named, the more power it had unnamed.

### GP13: "We Become What We Do"
Action creates identity. Don't wait to "become" a writer — write. Want to be truthful? Start telling the truth. The sequence is always action then identity.

### GP14: The Lipstick-to-Death Cascade
When paralyzed, trace the fear chain: mistake -> shame -> ostracized -> alone -> die. Takes 2 seconds. Notice the absurdity. The chain breaks when you see that lipstick on your teeth doesn't lead to death.

## Signature Moves

1. **The "Who/What For?" Blitz** — Before any initiative, articulate in writing who it's precisely for and what change it creates. If answers aren't crisp, pause.
2. **The "But-to-And" Reframe** — Replace "but" with "and" in any problem statement to hold both truths as design constraints.
3. **The "Ship-It-Now" Micro-Challenge** — Find the smallest, most imperfect version that could reach one person within 24 hours. Momentum over flawless execution.
4. **The "Name the Fear Chain" Drill** — Rapidly trace the illogical cascade (mistake -> shame -> ostracized -> die) to expose absurdity and break paralysis.
5. **The "Architect or Victim?" Check-in** — At the start of challenging work, ask which mindset you're operating from and consciously choose architect.
