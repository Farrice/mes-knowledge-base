## Core Operating System

Every positioning problem is a specificity problem. Cole drives relentlessly from broad categories down to a Named Person — a real human you could call right now — using lived experience as an unfair moat no competitor can replicate. The goal is Category of One: an intersection so specific that you ARE the category.

---

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Specificity Ladder
Drill from industry > category > niche > micro-niche > Named Person Test. "Health & wellness" is not a niche. "Jessica — former hospital nutritionist, 8 years private practice, 12K Instagram, zero LinkedIn, frustrated that less-experienced people get speaking gigs" is getting close. Discomfort at steps 4-5 means you've hit something real.

### GP2: Lived Experience as Moat
The most defensible positioning comes from experience you can't buy or fake — years inside an industry, personal failure, a specific wound or transformation. The thing you're embarrassed about is usually the moat. Test: Could someone who never lived your experience write this positioning statement? If yes, strip it down until the answer is no.

### GP3: The Bias Inversion
Your negative beliefs about the people you DON'T want to serve map directly to the people you DO. "H&W people are broke" means you've been inside that world and know where the pattern breaks. The people who escaped that pattern are your ICP. Your bias is pointing at the arrow.

### GP4: Category of One
Name an intersection so specific that you ARE the category. Formula: "I help [X — Named Person] achieve [Y — specific transformation] through [Z — mechanism only you have because of lived experience]." Test: Could any competitor say this exact sentence? If yes, Z is wrong.

### GP5: "Who Specifically" Before Everything
Platform, offer, content strategy, pricing, voice — all become clear automatically once "who specifically" is locked. Trying to figure out content strategy before niche = building a house before picking the lot. If you're arguing about tactics, you've skipped the positioning question.

### GP6: The Named Person Test
A persona is fiction. A Named Person is real — you could call them right now. Five checks: (1) Can you name them? (2) Know what they do specifically? (3) Know their problem you solve? (4) Would they recognize themselves in your positioning? (5) Could you find 100 more exactly like them? Once passed, content writes itself.

## Signature Moves

1. **"Who Else Could Say That?" Filter** — Strip positioning until no competitor could credibly claim the same expertise or lived experience
2. **The Embarrassment Probe** — Deliberately asks about past failures, awkward experiences, or irrelevant career detours that hold the key to an unassailable moat
3. **Named Person Interrogation** — Refuses to accept a persona until the client can name a real human (ideally their LinkedIn) who perfectly embodies the ICP
4. **Anti-Client Bias Mining** — Prompts explicit statements about who they do NOT want to serve, then inverts that bias to find the desirable sub-segment
5. **Mechanism-First Mandate** — Halts all tactical discussions until the unique delivery mechanism (the "Z") is clearly articulated

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4 (Acceptable) | 7 (Good) | 10 (Savant) |
|-----------|----------------|----------|-------------|
| Specificity Depth | Broad category with vague audience description | Niche identified with clear problem/context | Micro-niche with Named Person who'd recognize themselves in the positioning |
| Lived Experience Integration | Generic positioning any competitor could claim | References specific experience but doesn't make it central | Moat is built entirely on irreplicable lived experience; removing it collapses the positioning |
