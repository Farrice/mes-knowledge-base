When AI agents fail, the problem is missing context, not model capability. Remove manual escape hatches to force innovation within AI-native paradigms. ## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Escape Hatch Removal Principle
When adopting AI-first workflows, deliberately remove the ability to fall back to old methods. This forces you to solve problems within the new paradigm. OpenAI maintains a 100% Codex-written codebase with no manual coding allowed — problems become "how do I get the agent to do this?" not "I'll just do it manually."

### GP2: The Sorcerer's Apprentice Awareness
Hold two truths: AI tools provide extraordinary leverage AND require mature oversight. Maintain the "wizard, not apprentice" posture — know what each agent is doing at all times. Don't dispatch and sleep. The moment you lose track, you've become the apprentice (SICP's 1980s sorcery metaphor).

### GP3: Context-as-Bottleneck Diagnosis
When AI agents fail, audit what information was missing, not what the agent did wrong. Check: (1) What context was available? (2) What tribal knowledge was in your head but not the codebase? (3) What docs/comments/MD files could bridge the gap? Fix context, then re-run.

### GP4: The Scaffolding Impermanence Principle
All current tooling and frameworks are temporary scaffolding models will consume. Ask: "Will a smarter model make this unnecessary?" Build for where models are going. Invest only in capabilities models can't replicate: data access, distribution, domain expertise, UX.

### GP5: The Surgeon Management Model
Treat every engineer as a surgeon — spend 50%+ of time with top 10% performers. Anticipate tools they need before they ask. Pre-clear blockers around organizational corners. Top performers exploring and evangelizing creates org-wide multipliers.

### GP6: The Bottom-Up AI Adoption Flywheel
Successful AI deployment requires BOTH top-down executive buy-in AND bottom-up practitioner evangelism. Find "technical-adjacent" enthusiasts (not engineers), form a tiger team, let them explore and knowledge-share. Pull adoption beats pushed mandates.

### GP7: The N-Order Effect Thinking
Don't stop at first-order predictions. Force 4 orders: (1) What happens first? (2) What does that cause? (3) What does that unlock/destroy? (4) What new equilibrium emerges? Real opportunities are at orders 2-4 where competition is drastically lower.

### GP8: The Bitter Lesson Applied to Product
Extend AI's "bitter lesson" (less architecture, more compute = better) to product building. Default to simplest scaffolding, give models tools not constraints, assume current "right way" obsolete in 12-18 months. Bet on model capability improvement.

## Signature Moves

1. **Contextualize-First Debugging** — When AI fails, pause before blaming the model. Audit available context, identify missing tribal knowledge, encode it into reusable artifacts, then re-run.
2. **Scaffolding Sunset Planning** — For every AI feature, ask "Will a smarter model make this unnecessary in 12-18 months?" If yes, label as temporary scaffolding with deprecation plan.
3. **Pre-emptive Blocker Scan (Surgeon Mode)** — Spend disproportionate time with top performers in informal check-ins, probing for potential blockers before they surface.
4. **N-Order Opportunity Mapping** — For any AI trend, cascade thinking to 2nd, 3rd, and 4th-order effects to identify non-obvious opportunities and competitive moats.
5. **Wizard's Eye Oversight** — Maintain constant high-level awareness of each AI agent's activity. Never set-and-forget; actively steer and verify output.
