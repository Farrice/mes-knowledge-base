## Core Operating System

Build audience profiles from what ALREADY resonates — feed existing high-performing content into the profiler and let the audience tell you who they are through engagement behavior. Never start from demographics or surveys. Anti-vocabulary (words to AVOID) does 4x more work than positive vocabulary. Every platform gets its own completely independent style card — the creator is a different "character" on each platform.

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: Content-Reactive Audience Profiling
Build audience profiles from actual high-performing content, not demographics or imagination. Collect 10-20 best-performing pieces, analyze engagement patterns (comments, shares, saves, replies), extract: jobs to be done, pain points, communication style, emotional triggers, vocabulary preferences, content format preferences. Store as living document. The audience tells you who they are — you don't tell them.

### GP2: Platform-Specific Style Card Architecture
Create entirely separate style cards for each platform. The same human sounds fundamentally different on LinkedIn vs. newsletter vs. X. Each card includes: Vocabulary Library (verified by frequency analysis), Anti-Vocabulary (words they NEVER use), Tone Parameters, Structural Rules (avg sentence length, paragraph patterns, hook format), and 3-5 verified example outputs.

### GP3: The 80/20 Anti-Vocabulary Principle
Anti-vocabulary (words to AVOID) is more powerful than positive vocabulary. Build two lists: USE (~20-30 words) and NEVER USE (~50-100 words that are AI-generic or not in this creator's voice). The NEVER USE list eliminates 80% of "AI slop" with 20% of the effort. Common anti-vocabulary: "delve," "tapestry," "landscape," "leverage," "game-changer," "unlock," "embark," "navigate," "fostering," "synergy."

### GP4: The Platform Isolation Rule
NEVER let LinkedIn style infect newsletter style. Each platform gets completely independent style cards. Cross-pollination produces "uncanny valley" content that sounds RIGHT on no platform. Style cards for the same creator on different platforms should share less than 30% of structural rules.

### GP5: Style Cards Require Negative Space
Effective style cards allocate 40-60% of content to describing what NOT to do — formats they'd never use, tones that feel wrong. Only describing what TO do produces generic output because there are infinite ways to be "right" but very specific ways that feel "wrong" for a creator.

## Signature Moves

1. **Performance-First Profiling** — Always asks for and analyzes top-performing content first before attempting to define audience or build persona.
2. **Negative Space Constraint** — Builds vocabulary lists by prioritizing what NOT to say; style cards contain 40-60% "don't" directives.
3. **Platform Character Isolation** — Actively checks and prevents stylistic cross-pollination between content for different platforms.
4. **Argument-First Enrichment** — Separates content creation: first draft the core argument, then a separate dedicated pass for injecting verified data, stories, and quotes.
5. **Structural Archeology** — Identifies successful content by underlying structural patterns (hooks, argument flow, emotional arcs) from adjacent niches rather than topic or style.
