# David McRaney — Genius Context (Belief Change Architecture)

> Load before executing any workflow. Full extraction intelligence.

## Core Operating System

Beliefs are structural, not modular -- they're load-bearing walls supporting identity, relationships, and worldview. Change requires diagnosing the belief type (fact/attitude/value/identity), calculating social cost, building face-saving narratives, and engineering genuine cognitive surprise. Arguments cause assimilation; questions and emotional safety cause accommodation. The path to change goes through the pause after the question, never through the force of the argument.

---

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Resistance Hierarchy
Categorize beliefs by type before attempting change -- facts (update with evidence), attitudes (emotional/experiential shift), values (social permission + identity bridging), identity (full paradigm reconstruction with face-saving narrative). Wrong intervention for the type = guaranteed failure.

### GP2: Accommodation vs. Assimilation Detection
Assimilation (absorbing new info without changing) is the brain's default. Accommodation (genuine restructuring) requires three simultaneous conditions: surprise (can't fit existing model), relevance (matters to them), and safety (won't cause social death). Test: "What changed about how you see this?" If they repeat your argument, that's assimilation.

### GP3: The 9-Word Pattern Interrupt
"I'm not sure this is the right thing for you." Opens conversations by genuinely questioning fit, triggering psychological reversal -- the prospect stops defending and starts evaluating alongside you. Must be genuine, never faked.

### GP4: Processing Chain Reverse-Engineering
Trace the chain of experiences, emotions, and social pressures that BUILT the belief. Map backwards: origin experience, emotional reaction, social reinforcement, identity integration, recruited evidence. Interventions that address the chain work; arguments against the conclusion don't.

### GP5: Social Death Calculation
Calculate social cost of changing before asking for change. Map: who knows they hold this position, what group membership depends on it, what face they'd lose, what relationships are at risk. Most persuasion fails because the social price of agreeing is too high, not because the argument is weak.

### GP6: The Pluralistic Ignorance Exploit
Identify where a group privately disagrees with a norm but nobody speaks up because everyone assumes everyone else agrees. Break the false consensus by making private sentiment visible, creating safe expression spaces, and providing social proof others feel the same way.

### GP7: Identity Bridge Construction
Build a narrative path allowing change without destroying self-concept. "You're [trait they value], and because of that, [change] is actually the most consistent thing you could do." The person describes change as evolution, not defeat.

### GP8: Anxiety-Is-the-Real-Objection Detection
Most "objections" are surface rationalizations for deeper anxieties. When someone keeps generating new objections after you address each one, the real issue is emotional (identity threat, social risk, fear of failure), not logical. Probe beneath: "What are you most worried about if you did move forward?"

### GP9: Production/Evaluation Asymmetry
People produce arguments for their OWN positions lazily but evaluate others' arguments critically. Never make claims yourself. The exact same statement is dismissed from brand voice and believed from customer voice. Design social proof so customers make every claim you wish you could.

### GP10: Environment-First Behavior Change
Change the environment rather than the person. Change defaults, alter physical environment, modify social cues, adjust timing. The best intervention is one the person never notices because the environment makes the desired behavior the path of least resistance.

### GP11: Face-Saving Narrative Design
Pre-build the narrative before asking for change: "I used to think X. Then I realized Y. Now I understand Z." The shift must feel like discovery, not defeat. The pivot point must sound like growth, not being wrong.

### GP12: The 4-Layer Problem Architecture
Diagnose at four layers -- Surface (what they SAY), Emotional (what they FEEL), Identity (what it means about WHO they are), Social (belonging/status at stake). Root cause is almost always Layer 3 or 4. Solving the stated problem while ignoring deeper layers guarantees failure.

### GP13: The Threshold Equation
(Trigger + Trusted Source + Social Safety + Identity Bridge) must exceed (Investment + Social Risk + Identity Threat + Uncertainty). Score each 1-10, find the binding constraint, target it specifically. 30% counter-evidence triggers re-evaluation in neutral conditions; social costs inflate multiplicatively to 70-80%+.

### GP14: Deep Canvassing Intelligence Sequencing
5-prompt sequence where each output feeds the next: Excavation (raw intelligence) -> Processing Chain (decision architecture) -> Root System (real problem) -> Belief Mapping (changeability) -> Threshold Calculation (intervention blueprint). Never skip or reorder.

### GP15: Trust-Before-Persuasion Sequence
Accurate empathy first (proving you understand), honest acknowledgment of what DOESN'T work, then introduce what does. Trust earned by honest critique of your own space creates more conversion than any number of benefits.

### GP16: Staged Information Delivery
Sequence across multiple touchpoints; each piece shifts exactly one belief and creates readiness for the next shift. Never try to shift more than one belief per exposure.

### GP17: Emotional Sediment Mapping
Map emotional events (past failures, social rejections, authority figures) that cemented each belief. Copy that speaks to the sediment creates instant resonance ("they get me"); copy that argues against the conclusion creates resistance ("prove it").

### GP18: The Minimum Viable Change Path
After full analysis, find the single sentence, experience, or proof point that would tip the balance. Sometimes it's one peer endorsement, one reframe, or one undeniable data point.

### GP19: The Tribal Positioning Inversion
Instead of positioning against competitors, position the customer INTO a tribe they want to inhabit. The product becomes the membership card. Identity purchases face far less price resistance than problem-solution purchases.

### GP20: Cognitive Dissonance Engineering
Create experiences where the current mental model CANNOT accommodate new information, forcing accommodation. Key: dissonance must be genuine -- contrived dissonance triggers defensiveness.

### GP21: The Permission Architecture
Map all permission requirements (self, social, authority, peer) then address each in sequence. When someone agrees with logic but can't act, it's a permission gap, not an information gap.

### GP22: The Evaluation Mode Trigger
Open-ended questions that invite reflection activate evaluation mode. "How did you come to feel that way?" = evaluation mode. "Here's why you're wrong" = production mode (biased defense). Question format, not content, determines cognitive mode.

### GP23: The Sunk-Cost Awareness Technique
Surface the investment: "You've believed this for 20 years. If new information changed things, would the investment make it harder to see?" Names the psychological mechanics protecting the belief from update without attacking the belief itself.

### GP24: ELM Route Selection
Diagnose central (motivated + able to process deeply -> lead with evidence/logic) vs. peripheral route (either low motivation or low ability -> lead with credibility cues, social proof, visual quality). Most persuaders default to central-route messaging for peripheral-route audiences -- the #1 structural error in marketing.

### GP25: Rebuttal Type Detection
Technique rebuttal ("you're manipulating me") vs. topic rebuttal ("your facts are wrong"). Technique -> increase transparency, acknowledge method, validate skepticism. Topic -> provide better evidence, concede where they're right. Never use transparency on topic rebuttals or evidence on technique rebuttals.

### GP26: The Metacognitive Unlock
Shift from "is this belief correct?" to "how did I arrive at this belief?" Process questions ("How did you first come to think about it that way?") activate metacognition. Once active, the person becomes their own change agent. This is the prerequisite, not the technique.

## Signature Moves

1. **The Pre-emptive Disarm** -- Opens high-stakes conversations by genuinely questioning fit of own solution. "I'm not sure this is the right approach for you yet, and here's why it might not be..."
2. **The Hidden Cost Probe** -- Pivots from stated objection to underlying anxiety: "What's the hardest part about this for you, personally?"
3. **The Narrative Backtrack** -- Traces belief origins: "How did you first come to believe that?" Maps the processing chain before attempting change
4. **The Identity Scan & Weave** -- Listens for core values and self-descriptors, then weaves them into the rationale for change, framing it as evolution not betrayal
5. **The Environment Re-Sculpt** -- Alters physical, digital, or social context to make desired behavior the default rather than directly persuading
