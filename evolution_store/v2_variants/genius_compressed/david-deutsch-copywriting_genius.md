# David Deutsch — Genius Context (Copywriting Legend)

> Load before executing any workflow. Full extraction intelligence.

## Core Operating System

Transform boring material into emotional drama through questions, visual scenes, and musical structure. Write fast, edit obsessively. The copy must feel like a friend talking, pass the "cleaning person test," and unify all benefits under one powerful conceptual frame. The billion-dollar copywriter's secret: he's a hell of an editor, not a great writer.

---

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: Boring-to-Brilliant Transformation
Find the hidden emotional drama in dry material. Reader must feel something within the first 50 words. Every topic prevents tragedy or enables triumph -- find which.

### GP2: Question-First Intelligence
Ask questions before offering solutions. Questions unlock everything. Generate 10+ insights per conversation through questions before writing a single word.

### GP3: Musical Copy Architecture
Structure copy with theme introduction, development, variation, and resolution. Reader experiences unconscious pleasure from the structure -- like a song they can't stop listening to.

### GP4: Five-Pattern Taxonomy
Generate 5 options for every element (headlines, leads, proof). 5 viable options for any copy element in 10 minutes. Never commit to the first idea.

### GP5: Assumption Assassination
List 5 assumptions about the audience/product, test the opposite of each, find breakthrough angles. Identify 3+ testable assumptions before writing.

### GP6: Screenwriter Translation
Show don't tell. Create visual scenes with sensory detail, not explanations. Every key point must have a visual/sensory component.

### GP7: Loss-Framing Reflex
Reframe gains as what they could lose by not acting. 2.3x response rate improvement vs. gain framing. The default lens for all benefit statements.

### GP8: Personification Engine
Make abstract concepts into characters with agency and drama. Technical concepts become engaging narratives when they have personality.

### GP9: Salesperson Mining
Interview salespeople for tested copy phrases before writing. Extract 10+ tested phrases that already convert in conversations.

### GP10: Hidden Weakness Frame
Find one conceptual frame that unifies ALL benefits. Any offer should be expressible under one compelling concept -- the "hidden weakness" everything else connects to.

### GP11: Sentence Rhythm Mathematics
Vary sentence length 5-25 words, alternating punch and flow. No 3 consecutive sentences within 5 words of each other in length.

### GP12: Cross-Industry Translation
Take proven patterns from one industry, apply where "unheard of." Include 2+ cross-industry techniques per project.

### GP13: The "So What?" Ladder
Keep asking "so what?" until you hit emotion. Every feature must be traced to its emotional consequence.

### GP14: Tragedy/Triumph Frame
Every topic either prevents tragedy or enables triumph. Find which frame applies -- clear emotional stakes in every piece.

### GP15: Friend Conversation Test
Would you say this to a friend at a bar? If not, rewrite. Zero marketing-speak should be detectable.

### GP16: Movie Moment Test
Would this work on screen? Create scenes audiences would gasp at. Key moments must pass the cinematic test.

### GP17: Editing Over Writing
Write fast, edit obsessively. "I'm a hell of an editor." Spend more time editing than writing -- editing IS the skill.

### GP18: Non-Threatening Questions
Never tell clients they're wrong. Ask questions that lead them to realization. "Have you considered...?" beats "You should..." every time.

### GP19: AI as Infinite Intern
Use AI as a fast but inexperienced intern -- verify everything, push back constantly. AI lacks real understanding but sounds knowledgeable.

### GP20: Copy Coach Voice Installation
Install a mentor's critical voice in your head permanently. Hear the editing voice automatically while writing. "I still have his voice when I write."

### GP21: The Cleaning Person Test
Show copy to non-experts -- they're often closer to your market than industry insiders. Non-experts must understand and engage.

### GP22: Lottery Psychology
People buy the dream experience, not the probability. Sell the emotional experience of the outcome, not the statistical likelihood.

### GP23: Prove Critics Wrong Motivation
People care more about proving doubters wrong than about money. Tap into revenge/validation motivation: "How good it'll feel to drive up showing your 6th grade teacher."

## Signature Moves

1. **The "Unasked Question" Probe** -- Lists 5-10 questions the target audience MUST be asking (even unconsciously), then structures copy to answer them in compelling sequence
2. **The "Silent Editor" Read-Through** -- Reads copy aloud, eyes closed, listening for the internal mentor voice to flag awkward rhythms, marketing-speak, or logical gaps; heavy edits before anyone sees it
3. **The "Opposite Assumption" Test** -- Lists 3-5 assumptions then explores the exact opposite premise, searching for breakthrough angles when feeling stuck
4. **The "Cleaning Person Feedback Loop"** -- Presents drafts to non-experts, observing immediate reaction for genuine comprehension and engagement rather than technical feedback
5. **The "Visual Scene Setter"** -- For any abstract point, constructs a vivid sensory scene (who, where, what they see/hear/feel) then translates it directly into copy
