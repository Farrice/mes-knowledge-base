## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Demand Inversion
Focus on manufacturing WANTING, not improving quality. Before any marketing, ask "How am I creating demand?" not "How am I explaining my product?" Success = waitlist longer than capacity; turning away qualified buyers regularly.

### GP2: The 96% Market Unlock
Problem-aware targeting captures the 65% with dormant frustrations vs. the 4% actively searching. Rewrite all headlines starting with a PROBLEM your audience has right now, not a SOLUTION you offer. 10-20x increase in addressable market.

### GP3: The Tension Triangle
"No tension, no sale." Current Reality + Desired Reality + Obstacles = Buying Tension. In every sales conversation, diagnose all three points before presenting solutions. 40-60% higher conversion.

### GP4: Signal Volume Engineering
50 signals needed for every slot available. At 10 capacity, you need 500 people aware of your work monthly. Calculate: Capacity x 50 = Required monthly signal volume. Build marketing to hit this number.

### GP5: The Stakes Lens (Esther Perel Pricing)
Value exists in the buyer's situation, not the seller's credentials. Same expertise = $0 to one person, $250,000 to another based on what's at stake. Before pricing, ask: "What is at stake if they DON'T solve this?" Price at 10-30% of cost avoided or gain realized.

### GP6: The Susan Transformation
KPI status claimed through strategic positioning, not earned through credentials. Add industry + problem + transformation to any generic title. Make it so specific you feel uncomfortable. "HR consultant" → "hypergrowth people architect for Series A tech companies."

### GP7: The 11/90 Visibility Threshold
Recognition requires being seen 11+ times by the same person within 90 days. Below this threshold, you're invisible. Design content systems ensuring 11+ touches in 90 days. Target: "I see you everywhere" feedback.

### GP8: The 1/9/90 Market Architecture
Every market: 1% with extreme problems (pay anything), 9% with expensive problems (pay premium), 90% with cheap problems (price-sensitive). Focus all marketing on the 9% tier for 10x pricing on same service.

### GP9: Assessment-Led Sales
"X-ray before prescription." Build 15-25 question assessment revealing problems prospects didn't know they had. Creates value, demonstrates expertise, and pre-qualifies simultaneously. 40-60% conversion from assessment to sale.

### GP10: The Path of Least Resistance
Make buying feel like the obvious next step, not a decision requiring persuasion. For every offer element, ask: "What obstacle does this remove?" If none, remove the element.

### GP11: Official Capacity Declaration
Scarcity manufactured by declaring capacity publicly. "I take 16 clients maximum" creates value; "I'm available" destroys it. Calculate true capacity for excellence. Publish everywhere. Never exceed it.

### GP12: The KPI 5-Part Framework
Key Person of Influence status: Pitch + Publish + Product + Profile + Partnership. Audit yourself against all five. Identify gaps. Close them systematically. Recognized as authority within 90 days.

### GP13: The Newbie-Worker-KPI Ladder
Everyone: Newbie (learning) → Worker Bee (trading time) → KPI (attracting opportunities). Your positioning determines your rung. Implement positioning changes to climb from "available for hire" to "sought after."

### GP14: Baggage Mining
Your hidden IP lives in your "baggage" — experiences, struggles, unique approaches. Document every methodology, framework, or approach you use unconsciously. Name them. Named things have value; unnamed things are commodities.

### GP15: The Origin Story Power
Your mess is their message received. Struggles create connection; transformation stories create trust. Craft your biggest professional struggle into 30-second, 2-minute, and 10-minute versions.

### GP16: The Fame Game Inversion
If you can't win the existing game, create a new game where you're automatically the leader. Define your own category where your unique combination wins.

### GP17: Worldview Alignment
Don't just speak to what they WANT — speak through what they BELIEVE. Messages confirming worldview feel TRUE, not sold. Map ICP's beliefs about success, suspicions about industry, sacred values.

### GP18: The Private Conversation
Your ICP has an internal monologue running 24/7. Your marketing should sound like that voice, not like an outsider. Document what your ICP thinks at 3am when they can't sleep. Build messaging matching those thoughts.

### GP19: Transformation Windows
Stakes highest during transformation moments: promotions, expansions, crises, pivots. Map the 3-5 transformation windows in your market. Target people IN those windows for 3-5x premium accepted.

### GP20: Visible Demand Engineering
Demand becomes contagious when visible. Public waitlists, visible testimonials, "limited spots" communications create social pressure manufacturing more demand. Social proof generates inbound.

### GP21: The Anticipation Architecture
Oversubscribed launches: 70% anticipation, 20% launch, 10% delivery. Build 3-4 weeks of anticipation content before any launch. Make the launch moment ceremonial. Sold out before launch ends.

### GP22: The "With or Without You" Energy
Desperation repels; abundance attracts. Build pipeline so full that losing any single prospect doesn't matter. Let this energy permeate all communications. Prospects pursue you; premium pricing accepted.

### GP23: Outcome-Focused Business Design
Start with the life you want, then design the business to deliver it. Define ideal week, income, impact first. Then ask: "What business model delivers this?"

### GP24: The IP Development Cycle
Pause → Reflect → Document. Expertise only becomes IP when named, systematized, and teachable. Weekly: notice what you did. Monthly: reflect on patterns. Quarterly: document into named methodologies.

### GP25: The Specificity Principle
Generic creates competition; specific creates monopoly. "Business coach" competes with millions. "Revenue acceleration architect for B2B SaaS at $3-10M ARR" competes with almost no one. Add specificity until you feel uncomfortably narrow.

### GP26: The Value Hierarchy
Value isn't absolute — it's hierarchical based on buyer situation. Same service is life-changing or irrelevant based on context. Understand where your offer fits in the buyer's value hierarchy before any proposal.

### GP27: The Compound Authority Effect
Authority compounds. Early KPI moves create opportunities creating more authority. Make aggressive positioning moves early. Accept feeling like a fraud. Do it anyway.
