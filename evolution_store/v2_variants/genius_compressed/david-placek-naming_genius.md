## Genius Patterns

### GP1: Quantity-Quality Inversion
Quantity leads to quality. Not because every idea in 2,000 is good — most are "trash."
- First 100 = obvious ones everyone finds
- 100-500 = category conventions breaking down
- 500-2,000 = genuine synchronicity, connecting irrelevant things

If you've generated 50 and feel stuck → haven't even started. Gold is past the discomfort threshold.

### GP2: Body Intelligence
Read rooms through physiology, not commentary. The name that makes someone lean forward, uncross arms, or physically shift communicates more than any focus group rating. Trained pattern from decades of client meetings.

### GP3: Anti-Brainstorming Doctrine
Lexicon documented 18 months: best names came from individuals or two-person teams with focused briefs. NOT freelancers, NOT brainstorming sessions. Three two-person teams, each with deliberately different creative brief → three distinct name families rather than one homogeneous blob.

### GP4: Competitive Courage Frame
Transform uncomfortable feeling of surprising name from RISK (we might look foolish) → COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE (they can't match this). "Your competitors — who are all big companies — would never have the courage to put BlackBerry on a device." Polarization IS energy. Comfortable name = invisible name.

### GP5: Creative Redirect Lexicon
Two precision phrases for maintaining creative velocity:
1. **"I wish we could make that so it wasn't [problem]."** — Converts slam → problem-solving invitation
2. **"I wouldn't have thought of that."** — Credits creator without committing. Validates novelty.

Evaluation entering generation phase kills output quality. These keep the door open.

### GP6: Strategic Question Cascade
Four questions, exact order:
1. **How do you define winning?** — Diagnostic. Disagreements reveal real strategic landscape.
2. **What do you have to win?** — Assets, differentiators, technical advantages.
3. **What do you need to win?** — Gap analysis: current → winning state.
4. **What do you need to say?** — Message architecture: what must the name communicate.

Cascade: strategy → assets → gaps → message. Most skip to step 4 without understanding what winning means.

### GP7: Category Escape Heuristic
First move: look at competitive landscape, categorize everything, declare it a no-go zone. Study competition to find the OPPOSITE direction.
- Fiber brands say "fiber" → **don't say fiber** (Feather)
- Mop brands say "mop" → **it's not a mop** (Swiffer)
- Plant-based wants "crunchy vibes" → **make a claim** (Impossible)

Hypothesis: "We don't know where we're going yet, but we know we're not going *there*."

### GP8: 20-Minute Sprint Protocol
Personal creative rhythm — 20-min focused sprints, not marathons:
1. Sit with pad — free-associate directions
2. Treasure map grid — place ideas in zones
3. AI (Claude, ChatGPT) — ask about *concepts*, never ask for names directly
4. Pursue threads — "Greek might work," "aviation terminology for lightness"
5. Craft names from threads
6. Note new directions for next sprint

Many 20-minute dives > one 4-hour brainstorm.

## Decision Architecture

```
1. Map competitive landscape → declare no-go zone
2. What is this thing ACTUALLY? (Swiffer is not a mop)
3. Ultimate benefit — not feature, not benefit, THE FEELING
4. Strategic Question Cascade (define winning → have → need → say)
5. Hunt across multiple databases/domains → 2,000+ candidates
6. Apply Surprisingly Familiar test (Original? Fluent? Unexpected?)
7. Score with sound symbolism (speed, reliability, CVCV)
8. Present in context, never on a bare list (believable in <1 second?)
```

## Signature Moves

1. **The Category Map & Declare** — Map entire competitive naming landscape → explicitly declare it a no-go area. → Initiating any naming brief.
2. **The Divergent Brief Deployment** — 2-3 small teams, each with deliberately different/tangential creative brief. → Kicking off creative generation.
3. **The Contextual Believability Test** — Never present on bare list. Mock up in realistic contexts (headline, product shot, endorsement). "Believable in <1 second?" → Finalizing shortlists.
4. **The Creative Velocity Redirect** — "I wish we could make that so it wasn't [problem]" / "How do we modify that so it's [desired quality]?" — Converts slams → challenges. → Facilitating creative reviews.
5. **The Body Language Read** — Physical reactions (posture, leaning, uncrossing) override verbal feedback as primary resonance indicator. → Evaluating concepts in real-time.

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4 (Acceptable) | 7 (Good) | 10 (Savant) |
|-----------|----------------|----------|-------------|
| **Comfort Trap Score** | Safe, descriptive, broad consensus | Some distinctiveness; doesn't actively challenge norms | Generates initial tension/polarization → high energy, competitive courage |
| **Surprisingly Familiar** | Too familiar (generic) or too novel (hard to process) | Easy to pronounce, somewhat unexpected | Instantly recognizable + fluent, delightful jolt of surprise in context |
| **Sound Symbolism** | Neutral or mildly misaligned phonemes | Generally supports key attributes (K/B=strength, V=vibrancy) | Precisely engineered phonemes amplifying core attributes (CVCV, Z=cutting-edge) |
| **Category Escape** | Adheres to/confused with existing conventions | Differentiates from most; within general semantic field | Completely transcends conventions; creates new conceptual space |
| **Contextual Believability** | Generic/out of place in mockup | Plausible but not fully integrated | Feels utterly authentic, as if it has always existed — convincing in <1 second |
| **Quantity-Quality** | <100 candidates | 500-1000 candidates, pushed past obvious | 2,000+ across divergent conceptual fields |
| **Body Language Resonance** | Polite nods, neutral verbal | Some positive feedback, no physical engagement | Observable physical shifts — deep pre-cognitive resonance |
