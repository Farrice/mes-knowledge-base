# Luke Iha — Genius Context (Vicious Hooks)

## Core Operating System

A polite hook is a dead hook. You spend 70-80% of your writing time on the hook because if you can't get someone to open, your ad is dead on arrival. Most copywriters start their real ad 3-4 sentences in — they hedge, warm up, dribble the ball. A vicious hook goes right for the gut in the absolute first line. No hesitation, no breathing room, no throat-clearing.

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: Relevant in the First Line
Must connect to something the reader already cares about within 10-20 words. Three relevance categories: **Pain/Condition Callout** (call out a pain they already understand), **Belief Callout** (take a stance on something they debate), **General Openness** (pure transgressive intrigue — hardest to write, highest failure rate, grand slam when they win). Relevance is NOT always a pain callout.

### GP2: Emotionally Charged Language
Use visceral, earthy, concrete words with Germanic/Anglo-Saxon roots over Latin/French equivalents. "Sweat" not "perspire," "cloth" not "fabric," "bad" not "severe." Power words to inject 1-2 per hook: banned, sexless, stink, lying, die, naked, killing, terrifying, beg, murder, rotting, screaming. Every word must earn its place.

### GP3: Natural Intrigue & Open Loops
Don't give away the answer. The Prediction Test: if someone reads the hook and can guess what comes next, the loop leaks. "The #1 thing to avoid with AI tools" = leaky (they can guess). "When I cleaned out my father's guest bedroom, I realized I inherited his sexless marriage" = airtight.

### GP4: Stakes & Rubbernecking
Something heavy must be at risk — death, identity, marriage, health, money, reputation. The reader should feel the compulsion to look, like passing a car wreck. If nothing real is at stake, the hook has no gravitational pull.

### GP5: Specificity as Spice
Add concrete details but treat them like seasoning, not the main course. "After 26 years of marriage" (grounds in reality). "My father's guest bedroom" (the detail makes it feel true). Don't overload with 5 specific facts in one sentence.

### GP6: Doesn't Read Like an Ad
Hook should read like a confession, gossip, story, breaking revelation, or information promising insight. Never like marketing material. Test: cover the brand name — if you can tell it's an ad, rewrite.

### GP7: Caveman Language
Write at the simplest possible reading level. Short words. Short sentences. Earthy. Raw. Read it to a 12-year-old — if they can't understand every word, simplify.

### GP8: Consequence First, Mechanism Later
Lead with the consequence they already care about. "This weird morning habit spikes cortisol" (they know cortisol = bad) not "This weird morning habit causes electrical imbalance" (they don't know/care about electrical imbalance). Only use mechanism in hook if it's already widely known AND believed.

## Signature Moves

1. **The Hedge Execution** — Delete first 3 sentences of any draft. If sentence 4 is stronger, those lines were filler.
2. **The Flinch Test** — Read hook aloud. Does it make you slightly uncomfortable? If not, rewrite.
3. **The Germanic Swap** — Post-draft, find every Latinate/French word in hook, swap for Anglo-Saxon equivalents.
4. **The Prediction Block** — Show hook to a stranger, ask "What comes next?" If they guess, loop leaks, rewrite.
5. **The Consequence Flip** — If any hook leads with mechanism, flip to lead with the consequence the reader already fears/desires.

## Anti-Patterns

| # | Pattern | Why It Kills |
|---|---------|-------------|
| AP1 | The Warm-Up Intro ("Hey guys, so I've been thinking...") | 3-4 sentences of nothing. Real hook buried. DOA. |
| AP2 | The Polished Professional (clean, LinkedIn-appropriate) | Reads like an ad. No flinch. Sanitized = invisible. |
| AP3 | The Mechanism Lecture ("Our proprietary bio-cell...") | Leads with mechanism they don't understand. Zero relevance/stakes. |
| AP4 | The Loose Loop ("The one thing every entrepreneur...") | Predictable. If they can guess, they won't read. |
| AP5 | Word Snobbery ("exacerbate," "facilitate," "paradigm") | Latinate, abstract, high-grade-level. Kills visceral impact. |
| AP6 | Generic Stakes ("This could hurt your business") | Abstract, low-emotion. No rubbernecking. |

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4 (Acceptable) | 7 (Good) | 10 (Savant) |
|-----------|----------------|----------|-------------|
| First-Line Compulsion | Relevant but feels like strong headline, not MUST-read | Stops scroll with genuine curiosity, but pull weakens after first sentence | Physically impossible to scroll past — thumb freezes, brain demands next line before conscious choice |
| Emotional Charge | Some weighted words but overall tone stays safe/professional | Visceral and earthy with 1-2 power words, occasional Latinate dilution | Every word Germanic, guttural, concrete — hits like body blow. Zero abstraction. FEELS before thinks. |
| Open Loop Architecture | Implies topic but reader can predict general direction | Tight enough that casual readers can't guess, but sophisticated readers might | Airtight — no human can confidently predict what comes next. Prediction Test yields zero consensus. |
| Stakes Communication | Mildly at risk (convenience, minor frustration) | Real stakes (health, money, identity) but communicated indirectly/abstractly | Visceral, immediate, undeniable — death, marriage, identity destruction. Gravitational pull of consequence. |
| Anti-Ad Authenticity | Good marketing copy — polished, purposeful, clearly selling | Could pass as organic content in most contexts | Indistinguishable from confession, gossip, or breaking personal revelation. Cover brand = nobody knows it's an ad. |
| Caveman Language | Most words simple but 2-3 multi-syllable/Latinate create friction | Consistently simple, readable by 12-year-old, rare exceptions | A caveman could grunt-translate every word. Monosyllabic where possible. Zero cognitive friction. |
| Specificity | General claims or round numbers feeling manufactured | 1-2 concrete grounding details ("26 years," "guest bedroom") | Telling details doing double duty as grounding AND emotional charge — story feels undeniably true |
