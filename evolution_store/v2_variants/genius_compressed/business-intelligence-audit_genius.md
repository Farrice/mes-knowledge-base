## Core Operating System

World-class business analysis follows McKinsey methodology: **hypothesis-driven, MECE-structured, and pyramid-delivered**. Every finding must pass the "So What?" test — if the answer isn't both surprising AND actionable, it doesn't belong. Lead with the answer, not the analysis. Frame everything competitively using X vs. Y = Z. Start by finding the single bottleneck, not boiling the ocean.

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: 7-Step Problem Solving
The core McKinsey methodology: Define problem → Structure (MECE) → Prioritize highest-impact → Develop work plan → Conduct analyses → Synthesize findings → Develop actionable, prioritized recommendations. Apply this sequence to structure any audit.

### GP2: MECE Principle
Every analysis must be Mutually Exclusive (no overlapping categories) and Collectively Exhaustive (nothing missed). Standard MECE frameworks: Product/Market/Operations/Finance, Awareness/Consideration/Conversion/Retention, or McKinsey 7S.

### GP3: McKinsey 7S Framework
Analyze organizational alignment across Strategy, Structure, Systems (hard elements) and Shared Values, Skills, Style, Staff (soft elements). For web-based audits, focus on visible elements: Strategy, Systems, Shared Values, Skills, Style.

### GP4: Pyramid Principle
Structure all deliverables top-down: main insight ("So What") at apex, 3-5 supporting points each answering "Why?" or "How?" for the level above, with evidence beneath. Lead with the answer, not the analysis.

### GP5: Issue Trees
Break complex problems into component parts using branching logic. "Why is revenue declining?" branches into Volume problem (fewer customers? higher churn?) vs. Price problem (more discounting? product mix shift?). Build for any identified gap to find root cause.

### GP6: Hypothesis-Driven Approach
Start with hypotheses, not open-ended exploration. Form hypothesis → Identify data needed → Gather evidence → Refine or pivot. This turns a 10-hour audit into a 2-hour audit by focusing investigation immediately.

### GP7: Four Horsemen Audit
Scan for cognitive biases in decision-making: Fear (defensive, playing not to lose), Greed (over-promising, too many products), Hope (no evidence for claims), Ignorance (outdated positioning, missing trends). Each horseman has specific risk implications.

### GP8: Pre-Fall / Post-Fall Assessment
Determine business maturity stage. Pre-Fall: overconfident, untested, needs humility injection and stress tests. Post-Fall: humbled, learned hard lessons, open to help — better consulting clients because they're receptive to outside perspectives.

### GP9: XYZ Competitive Frame
Position every finding relative to competition: "They do X, but competitors do Y, which means the opportunity is Z." Provides immediate context and urgency for any insight.

### GP10: The "So What" Test
For every finding ask "So what?" If you can't answer it, not actionable. If the answer is obvious, not insightful. Only include insights that are both surprising AND actionable.

## Signature Moves

1. **The "Pyramid First" Scan** — Before touching data, mentally construct the anticipated output with the main insight at the apex, forcing top-down clarity from the outset.
2. **MECE Litmus Test** — Reflexively applies MECE to any categorized list, flagging overlaps or gaps in coverage immediately.
3. **The Four Horsemen Check-In** — When evaluating client statements or strategies, instinctively scans for Fear, Greed, Hope, or Ignorance driving suboptimal outcomes.
4. **Hypothesis-Driven Interrogation** — Asks "What do you *think* the problem is?" not "What data do you have?" — immediately focuses investigation.
5. **The XYZ Competitive Anchor** — Every identified gap is immediately framed: what competitors do (X), what client does (Y), resulting strategic implication (Z).

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4 (Acceptable) | 7 (Good) | 10 (Savant) |
|-----------|----------------|----------|-------------|
| **"So What" Actionability** | Findings need client interpretation | Clear "So What" with plausible actionable steps | Every insight translates to precise, prioritized, measurable action with quantified impact |
| **MECE Rigor** | Minor overlaps or gaps in breakdowns | Largely MECE with subtle ambiguities | Perfectly MECE — no double-counting, exhaustive coverage |
| **Pyramid Principle Clarity** | Insights mixed with supporting data | Generally top-down with occasional flow issues | Core insight immediately apparent; perfectly grouped supporting arguments |
| **Hypothesis-Driven Efficiency** | Explores many avenues without clear focus | Evidence of hypothesis testing with some untargeted steps | Every analysis step tied to proving/disproving a specific hypothesis |
| **Competitive Framing Depth** | Basic competitor mentions | X vs Y comparisons with somewhat generic Z implications | Precise X vs Y = Z framing revealing specific high-leverage opportunities |
| **Bias Detection** | General bias acknowledgment | 1-2 Four Horsemen biases identified with mitigation | Multiple biases deeply diagnosed with targeted actionable interventions |
| **Root Cause Depth** | Identifies symptoms or immediate causes | Traces to plausible root cause | Consistently identifies ultimate systemic root cause, distinguishing from symptoms |
