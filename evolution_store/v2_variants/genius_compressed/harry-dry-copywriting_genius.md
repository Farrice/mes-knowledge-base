## Core Operating System

Copy must pass three simultaneous gates — visualization (can I picture it?), falsifiability (can it be proved?), and uniqueness (could a competitor sign it?) — applied with 20-rewrite ruthlessness where every word is either working or subtracted. "Simple" is the result of extreme precision, not its absence.

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Three Rules Test
Every sentence must pass three gates: (1) Can I literally picture this? (2) Could this be proved true or false? (3) Could a competitor sign this? Three yeses = deploy; any no = rewrite. Applied simultaneously, these create exponential compound quality.

### GP2: The Zoom-In Technique
When encountering abstract language, keep asking "what do I actually mean?" until arriving at something you can drop on your foot. Draw a vertical line — abstract at top, concrete at bottom. "Regain fitness" becomes "Couch to 5K."

### GP3: The Falsifiability Filter
Replace every adjective with a pointed fact. "Intelligent" becomes "reads on the tube." "Good-looking" becomes "looks like Ryan Gosling, 6'2"." Facts persuade; adjectives assert.

### GP4: "Don't Talk, Only Point"
Eliminate persuasion through assertion — replace with persuasion through evidence. For every benefit claim, identify something external you can point to: a chart, a quote, a number. If you can't point to it, don't say it.

### GP5: The Uniqueness Test
Mentally place competitor logos beneath your copy. If it works for them, it's generic. Dig for what only YOU can say. This is the final gate that separates good copy from category-defining copy.

### GP6: The One Mississippi Test
Copy must communicate in under 2 seconds. Show to someone for 2 seconds, hide it, ask "What was that about?" If they can't tell you, the copy fails. Instant comprehension is non-negotiable.

### GP7: Juxtaposition Architecture
Pair opposites to create memorability and tension. Supermodels/dads. London/Ohio. Forever/2 months. Place contrasting elements in direct proximity — the brain files contradictions as important.

### GP8: Visual-Verbal Synthesis
Never separate copywriting from design. Write in the exact environment where it will be consumed — newsletter copy in ConvertKit, ads in Figma. Never isolated Word docs. Copy-design is one discipline.

### GP9: The Conflict Injection Method
Structure every piece around an implicit "but" — conflict creates narrative momentum. Before writing, identify: Before/After, Problem/Solution, Common Approach/Our Approach. No conflict = no reader engagement.

### GP10: Parallel Structure Obsession
Create rhythm through deliberate parallelism. Enforce identical grammatical structure in lists. "Raise twice the cash, hire twice the staff, spend twice as much." Rhythm makes copy memorable.

### GP11: Kaplan's Law Ruthlessness
Every word is either working or against you. No neutral words exist. Review each word asking "Is this word WORKING?" If not actively contributing, subtract it. Maximum density always.

### GP12: The Two-Line Maximum
Any paragraph exceeding two lines gets split or trimmed. Three lines = not clear enough. Simplify. This constraint forces maximum conciseness.

### GP13: The 20-Rewrite Standard
Set minimum rewrite number (10-25). Don't evaluate until minimum reached. Early versions are "dirty water" — quality comes from writing badly long enough that the dirty water clears.

### GP14: Time-Place-Action Openings
Open with immediate specificity — exact time, physical location, sensory details. Start with the literal current moment: time on clock, what you see, physical sensation. Ground the reader in YOUR reality.

### GP15: The Enemy Identification
Position products by defining what they're against. Three enemy types: Type A (different approach), Type B (different belief), Type C (direct competitor). Build copy around the contrast.

### GP16: The Metonymy Move
Substitute technical terms with visual/emotional ones. "1000 songs in your media player" becomes "1000 songs in your pocket." Find what's more visual, tangible, surprising than the technical term.

### GP17: The Single-Element Ad Test
Great ads succeed with minimal elements — ideally one. After creating an ad, try removing each element. Can it work with just the headline? Just the image? Fewer elements = stronger elements.

### GP18: The Feedback Versioning System
Generate 3-5 versions before seeking feedback. Present all simultaneously. Ask "which is best?" and "which elements would you combine?" Multiple versions prevent attachment to any single draft.

### GP19: The Facts-First Philosophy
Start with verifiable facts, build narrative around them. Before writing, gather 3-5 specific facts — these become anchors. All narrative emerges from these pegs. Facts first, story second.

### GP20: The Garden Walk Test
After believing done, physically leave for 15-30 minutes. Return and ask: "Can I remove anything?" If no, ready. If yes, continue. Fresh eyes are the final quality gate.

## Signature Moves

1. **The "Drop-It-On-Your-Foot" Test** — Before writing, challenge every abstract concept with "What do I actually mean?" until it becomes a tangible object or verifiable action.
2. **The Competitor Logo Swap** — Mentally swap the client's logo with a competitor's on completed copy. If the copy still works, discard it as generic.
3. **The Two-Second Interrogation** — Show headline to a non-expert for exactly 2 seconds. If they can't articulate the core message, completely rewrite.
4. **The Pre-Flight Conflict Map** — Before any writing, explicitly identify the inherent opposition (before/after, problem/solution, old way/new way).
5. **The Word Weightlifter** — During editing, subject every word to "Is this word WORKING?" Any non-contributing word is immediately removed.

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4 (Acceptable) | 7 (Good) | 10 (Savant) |
|-----------|----------------|----------|-------------|
| Visualization Density | Abstract terms dominate; difficult to form mental images | Mix of abstract and concrete; some parts clear, others vague | Every sentence evokes a specific, tangible image; no abstractions remain |
| Falsifiability Quotient | Relies on adjectives and assertions ("best," "high quality") | Some assertions backed by facts, many remain subjective | Every claim is a pointed fact; verifiable or demonstrably true |
| Uniqueness Signature | Generic; could apply to multiple competitors | Some unique elements, core message still broadly applicable | Irreducibly unique; only THIS brand could make these claims |
| One Mississippi Impact | Requires multiple reads to grasp the point | Understandable quickly, could be clearer | Core message instantly clear within 1-2 seconds |
| Conflict Architecture | Flat, descriptive; no tension or opposition | Some implicit tension, not clearly structured | Explicitly structured around compelling conflict creating momentum |
| Word Economy | Contains filler, redundancies, non-working words | Mostly concise, few trimmable words | Every word essential, actively contributing to meaning or rhythm |
| Paragraph Constraint | Frequent paragraphs exceeding two lines | Mostly two-line max with occasional slips | Strict two-line adherence forcing maximum conciseness |
