# Michael Connelly — Genius Context

> Load before executing any workflow. Full extraction intelligence.

## Core Operating System

Connelly built a 100-million-copy career by writing less, not more — making every surviving word carry the weight of a full paragraph. His 14 years at the LA Times taught him that the same economy making a six-inch crime story land makes a 400-page novel pull readers forward. This isn't minimalism; it's surgical maximalism — maximum impact from minimum material. Where other writers add detail, Connelly subtracts until only the one telling detail remains.

---

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Telling Detail > The Descriptive Paragraph
Pick the ONE detail that reveals character AND situation simultaneously. The 3-Question Filter: (1) Does it reveal character AND situation? (2) Does it open a window of imagination for the reader to build the scene? (3) Does removing it lose something irreplaceable? If only setting — cut it. Run on any paragraph with 3+ details; only the survivor stays.

### GP2: Momentum Is Sacred — The Speed Bump Audit
Everything serves forward movement. The Reading Test: mark every place you physically pause, slow down, or re-read — those are speed bumps to rewrite or cut. "Good Place to Stop" Test: if the reader can find a comfortable stopping point, you've failed. Density Threshold: when a sentence carries >2 ideas, split it.

### GP3: Character Is the Organizing Principle
"Character, character, character." Plot, setting, geography, names serve character. After drafting any section, run every element through: "What does this reveal about who this person IS?" If nothing — reshape to serve character, or cut. No third option.

### GP4: The Outsider Looking In — Access/Belonging Gap
Characters have insider access with outsider perspective. Bosch has a badge but doesn't belong. Define: (1) What do they have access to? (2) Why do they feel like they don't belong? The gap between access and belonging IS the voice. Same answer to both = no voice.

### GP5: Subtext Over Surface — Two-Layer Test
Before finalizing dialogue/copy, identify: "What is this literally about?" and "What is this ACTUALLY about?" If those two answers are the same, the writing lacks depth. Surface says one thing; undertow says another. Trust the reader to feel it without explanation.

## Dual-Pass Rewrite System

**Pass 1 — Micro-Erosion (Sentence Level)**: Can this be shorter? Does every word earn its place? Is there a nod replacing a sentence? Is there a telling detail buried in decoration? Cut until you hit bone.

**Pass 2 — Macro-Erosion (Structural Level)**: Read at speed. Mark where attention wanders (speed bump), where you predict what comes next (too obvious), where something clever calls attention to itself (ego — cut), where character disappears >3 sentences (character vacuum).

## Anti-Patterns (Connelly Would Never...)

| Code | Violation | Rule |
|------|-----------|------|
| AN-1 | Decorative Prose | If a sentence's function is to be admired, not move forward — cut it. Prose is transparent. |
| AN-2 | Adjective Stacking | Max 1 adjective per noun. 2+ means you haven't found the right noun. |
| AN-3 | Explaining Feelings | "He felt angry" is always a rewrite trigger. Show through physical behavior. |
| AN-4 | Forced Metaphors | If a metaphor could be a creative writing workshop example — too visible. Cut. |
| AN-5 | Exposition Dumps | Distribute context through action and dialogue. If a paragraph's function is to inform — embed in action or cut. |
| AN-6 | Overwriting Action | Focus on what the work COSTS the character, not the spectacle. |

## Signature Moves

1. **The Single-Detail Filter** — Runs 3-Question Filter on every description: reveals character AND situation? Opens imagination window? Irreplaceable if removed? Only the survivor stays.
2. **Momentum Audit Pass** — Reads entire piece marking every pause, slowdown, re-read, and "good stopping point" as failures to systematically cut or rewrite.
3. **Character Lens Check** — Every element must answer "What does this reveal about who this person IS?" — reshape or cut if it doesn't.
4. **Two-Layer Dialogue Construction** — Identifies literal meaning and actual meaning before writing; power comes from the gap between them.
5. **Dual-Pass Erosion Rewrite** — Micro-erosion (sentence isolation, cutting to bone) then macro-erosion (full-speed read, structural cuts).

| Telling Detail Economy | Multiple generic/decorative details where one would suffice | Specific details hinting at deeper meaning but occasional over-description | Every detail surgical — reveals character AND situation simultaneously; one detail does work of five paragraphs |

| Economy of Expression | Full sentences where gestures/nods would suffice | Generally concise with occasional verbal padding | Maximum impact from minimum material; nods, single details, compressed language |
