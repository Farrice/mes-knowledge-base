## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Constraint Telescope
Before solving any problem, zoom to the single bottleneck gating everything else. Ask: "If this business grew 3x next month, what's the first thing that would break?" Fix only that. Repeat. Spend more time analyzing the game than executing scattered actions.

### GP2: Subtraction Before Addition
Default to removing complexity before adding capability. "You can't pick something up until you put something down." List all processes/products/systems — cut the bottom 80%. See what breaks. Add back only what breaks. If you didn't break things, you didn't remove enough.

### GP3: The Bonus Bank Architecture
Never discount — it signals you don't believe in your product. Pre-load 5-10 valuable bonuses (product extras, support upgrades, exclusive access) and deploy situationally in sales conversations. Apple never discounts; they add features.

### GP4: Flywheel Over Motivation
Motivation fades; momentum is contagious. Design a 4-6 step business flywheel (Jim Collins's *Good to Great*): content → customers → results → referrals → repeat. Every step must make the next easier. Follow the system whether inspired or not.

### GP5: Price Anchor Engineering
Always present highest-priced option first. $200 steak on the menu makes $30 feel cheap. Create 3 tiers: lead with VIP at 3-5x middle tier. Present middle as "where most people start." Increases AOV without additional marketing.

### GP6: Partner-at-Point-of-Sale
Borrow credibility instead of building from zero. Find 3 businesses selling to your dream customers who don't compete. Offer 10-50% revenue share. Find the "Amazon" in your niche — the entity with trust + traffic — and plug in.

### GP7: Output-Only Measurement
Never measure hours, effort, or time. Measure only results. Tie compensation to measurable output: lower base + variable on results. Track revenue per team member. Daily tracking prevents small problems becoming massive disasters.

### GP8: The Vacation Test
Imagine taking a 4-week vacation — no Slack, no email. What would break? That list IS your systemization priority queue. Real wealth = business generates income without your presence. Great leaders run as fast as they can to not be needed.

## Signature Moves

1. **The "What Breaks?" Interrogation** — Starts by asking "If your business grew 3x next month, what's the single first thing that would break?" Cuts through symptoms to the true bottleneck.
2. **The "Put Something Down" Audit** — Before any new initiative: "What are we going to stop doing to make space for this?" Enforces subtraction before addition.
3. **The "Vacation Test" Thought Experiment** — Imagine 4 weeks no-contact; the "what would break" list becomes the systemization priority queue.
4. **The "Echo Marketing" Drill** — Ask customers about deepest pains and use their exact words verbatim in all sales and marketing materials.
5. **The "Three-Tier Anchor" Setup** — Always leads with significantly higher VIP option (3-5x middle tier) so the desired middle tier feels like exceptional value.
