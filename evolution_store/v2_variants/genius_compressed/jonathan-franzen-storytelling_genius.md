## Core Operating System

The best characters are defined by problems that are simultaneously small AND desperate — the embarrassingly specific neurosis that reveals deeper truths. Comic distance is the truth detector: if you can't find anything funny about your character's situation, you're too close. Every scene is powered by incompatible wants, every page is finished before the next begins, and the material we're most ashamed of becomes the best material once we can laugh at it.

## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Comic Problem Genesis
Start with the embarrassingly small thing that matters too much — the man who can't sleep unless the house is locked exactly right. Build the character outward from that specific itch. The best characters are defined by problems that are simultaneously small AND desperate.

### GP2: Comic Distance as Truth Detector
When you can laugh at a character, you've found truth. Victim-positioned writing (too close, too protective) produces sentimental, manipulative prose. Comic distance produces work that feels REAL. If you can't find anything funny about the situation, step back until you can see the absurdity.

### GP3: Shame-to-Comedy Conversion
The material we're most ashamed of — ugly thoughts, embarrassing desires, petty grievances — becomes the BEST material once we can laugh at it. Shame piles on shame, making prose heavy. Comedy liberates. The cringe-worthy becomes the thing that makes readers feel SEEN.

### GP4: Minimal Detail Maximum Impact
A 500-page novel might have less than one page of weather. Minor characters need only 2 sentences. Identify the 3 details that do the most work. Cut everything else. Trust the reader's imagination to do the heavy lifting.

### GP5: Want-Collision Scene Architecture
Every scene is powered by incompatible wants. Person A wants X, Person B wants Y, both can't happen. The collision generates drama without forced "conflict." Before writing any scene, articulate what each character desperately wants in that moment.

### GP6: The Iron Bridge Page Building
Every page must be FINISHED — publication-ready — before moving to the next. This prevents the "I'll fix it later" trap that produces drafts full of weak links. The novel is built like an iron bridge, link by link.

### GP7: Organic Plot Development
Know your destination, but discover the route. Sterile outlining produces predictable plots. Organic discovery — following character wants to their natural conclusions — produces surprising-yet-inevitable story. If you're surprised, the reader will be too.

## Signature Moves

1. **The Micro-Obsession Probe** — Begins by identifying the character's most embarrassing, disproportionate, yet deeply felt "small problem."
2. **The Absurdity Frame Shift** — Steps back from emotionally charged situations until inherent absurdity or dark humor becomes visible.
3. **The Unspoken Want-Clash Setup** — Before writing any scene, articulates the specific, often petty, incompatible desires of each character present.
4. **The Three-Detail Filter** — Ruthlessly trims all but 2-3 most impactful and revealing details, trusting the reader's imagination.
5. **The Page-Level Perfection Lock** — Refuses to proceed until current page is polished to near-publication standard.
