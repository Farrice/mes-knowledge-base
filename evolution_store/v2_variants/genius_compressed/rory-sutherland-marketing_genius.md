## Genius Patterns (Compressed)

### GP1: The Doorman Fallacy Detection
Before approving any cost-cutting or automation initiative, list 10+ functions the human/process performs beyond its stated purpose: security, status signaling, personalization, exception handling, emotional labor, relationship building, information gathering, brand perception. If you can't find at least 3 hidden value-creation functions, look harder.

### GP2: The Psychological Reframe
When presented with any business problem, generate 3 alternative framings: (1) What if customers don't want what we think they want? (2) What if the feeling matters more than the feature? (3) What if the problem is trust/status/identity, not utility? At least one reframe should reveal a solution 10x cheaper or more effective.

### GP3: The Human Proxy Heuristic
Humans evaluate sellers as proxy for evaluating products they can't directly assess. The vicar vs. underpants-guy car sale: we evaluate the person to evaluate the product. Optimize for winning the proxy evaluation (trust signals), not just the nominal one (features).

### GP4: The Transaction Utility Lens
Every purchase has two values — utility of the thing AND utility of the transaction itself. The "beach beer" paradox: people pay $7 for hotel beer but not shack beer. HOW something is bought affects perceived value. Design purchase experience to maximize feeling of good deal and appropriate treatment.

### GP5: The Costly Signal Detector
Certain goods MUST be expensive to perform their function. Louis Vuitton's price IS the product. Identify which costly signal function is served: status display, self-affirmation, generosity signaling, commitment signaling, quality proxy. Then ensure price and presentation support the signal. Price increase can IMPROVE demand.

### GP6: The Choice Architecture Awareness
People can't choose in isolation — they need comparison sets. Never present a single recommendation. Structure 3-5 options including a decoy (slightly inferior, slightly more expensive) to make preferred option obvious. Give customers the psychological satisfaction of choosing.

### GP7: The Brand Quake Recognition
Certain customer interactions are multiplicative, not additive — they can multiply brand perception by 10x or reduce it to zero. A single call center experience overrides years of advertising. Invest disproportionately in multiplicative moments: problem resolution, exception handling, personal recognition.

### GP8: The Private Company Advantage
Companies optimizing for customer value (private/founder-led) systematically outperform those optimizing for shareholder metrics (public/finance-controlled). Identify competitors' effective time horizons and exploit gaps created by their short-termism.

### GP9: The Fat-Tail Opportunity Spotter
Marketing is fat-tailed: 5-10% of ideas generate 110% of value. Resist averaging. Ask: "What's the maximum upside if this works spectacularly? Is this a baseball opportunity (max 4 runs) or a business opportunity (potential 1000)?" Allocate budget to preserve potential for breakthrough.

### GP10: The Evolutionary Psychology Anchor
Ground every insight in evolutionary psychology: conflict detection, trust assessment, status evaluation, loss aversion, social proof. For any customer behavior, trace it to evolutionary origins. Use deep drives, not surface preferences, to shape messaging. Marketing that aligns with evolved instincts works better and lasts longer.
