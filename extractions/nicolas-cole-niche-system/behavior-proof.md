# Behavior Proof — Synthetic Cold-Start Case

## Fixture

Maya is a physical therapist with ten years of clinical experience who wants to build a creator-led consulting business. She has published six unrelated posts, has no reliable performance baseline, no paid consulting clients, and can sustain one post per day. This is synthetic test data, not a market claim.

## Expected Route

`COLD START + HYBRID` → creator discovery first, while holding the service hypothesis open. The system must not force a Named Person or assert a permanent niche.

## Produced Decision

- `VERIFIED` within fixture: clinical experience, six mixed posts, no baseline, no paid event, daily capacity.
- `DERIVED`: test three territories—return-to-running education, clinician communication, and evidence-led fitness myths—with ten pieces each.
- `UNTESTED`: return-to-running may become the tip of spear.
- `NO EVENT`: no sales conversation, deposit, or payment.

The plan keeps format and cadence stable, records relative lift on qualified replies/saves/completion rather than views alone, and advances only the strongest topic into audience × problem × outcome variants. A service specialization is considered after repeated questions or buyer conversations reveal a deliverable problem.

## Negative Controls

1. **False demand:** six mixed posts cannot validate a market. PASS.
2. **Forced specificity:** no invented named buyer is required before publishing. PASS.
3. **Permanent identity:** the niche is framed as a 30-day vehicle. PASS.
4. **Premature service industry:** healthcare is experience context, not proof of a paying vertical. PASS.
5. **Pivot anxiety:** a weak first week triggers quality/distribution review, not an automatic niche change. PASS.

## Human Gate

Structural behavior is proven. A-tier judgment remains pending until a real operator or client case tests whether the recommendations feel sharp, surprising, and commercially useful.
