# Extraction Analysis

## Core Mechanism
Suzuki's public method is not "write one magical tweet." The repeated pattern is:

1. A high-specificity post stops qualified buyers.
2. The post offers a concrete free asset or proof bundle.
3. Comments become permission to send a DM or link.
4. The link moves people into a trust container: free material, community, email, Telegram, Discord, or sales page.
5. Low-ticket buyers can purchase directly. Higher-ticket buyers need a qualification and conversation path.
6. Early replies, reposts, and audience-fit comments feed the next reach cycle.

## Hidden Mechanics
- The "one post" is only the visible event. The machine includes account warmup, profile trust, free assets, buyer comments, DM follow-up, a checkout or application path, and fast reply handling.
- Specific numbers work as pattern interrupts because they imply proof. When numbers are unverified, the safer build is to use owned, provable numbers from the product or audience.
- Comment keywords are not only engagement bait. They create a list of people who took a public micro-action around the offer.
- The free asset must preview the paid outcome without giving away all implementation. It should make the next step feel obvious.
- Audience quality matters more than raw impressions. The system should reject broad posts that attract spectators but not buyers.
- The system uses a proof ladder: post proof -> free material proof -> community/social proof -> checkout/application proof -> guarantee or objection handling.

## Signature Moves
1. Specific result hook: number, time frame, buyer type, and product category.
2. Compact case breakdown: problem, actions, deliverable, result.
3. Multiple concrete freebies in one CTA.
4. Comment keyword tied to the buyer's desired identity or problem.
5. Reply sprint in the first 10 minutes after posting.
6. DM handoff into free trust container.
7. Direct checkout below the deliberation threshold; application/call above it.
8. Repeat winning post formats with new proof, angle, and buyer situation.
9. Turn the free asset into a decision tool, not a neutral question list.
10. Give ready buyers a separate link/application/conversation lane.
11. Show the first-use or first-7-day path so purchase feels like the next step in a plan.

## Failure Modes
- Hook names the product but not the buyer outcome.
- CTA asks people to "check it out" instead of offering a concrete reason to comment.
- Free asset is vague, generic, or unrelated to the paid product.
- Free asset only creates caution or more questions without a next move.
- Product has no believable proof path.
- Audience is large but low-fit.
- Price is high while the funnel stays self-serve.
- Ready buyers have no permission-based link, checkout, application, or conversation path.
- The funnel has no first-use plan after purchase.
- Platform adaptation copies the X post word-for-word instead of matching native behavior.
- Results are implied as guaranteed.

## Finished Asset Standard
Every Suzuki OS run should produce at least seven things:

1. A sales post or thread with a qualified-buyer hook.
2. A purchase-adjacent free asset that helps the buyer decide.
3. Buyer readiness diagnosis for cold, warm, ready, and wrong-fit buyers.
4. A funnel map with trust container, checkout or conversation path, and follow-up.
5. A first-use or first-7-day plan.
6. A proof-gap list that marks what must be true before publishing.
7. A 7-day launch or replay sprint.
8. One next action that can be done today.

## Floor-Raise Repair
V4-level behavior is now the minimum quality bar. The OS should fail and repair any output that is only a question map, caution asset, neutral report, or single-keyword freebie with no purchase-intent lane.

The behavior proof suite now tests:
- `$200` ebook.
- Affiliate course like DWA.
- High-ticket coaching/community.

Each fixture must include a post/thread, purchase-adjacent free asset, value/purchase-intent routing, DM scripts, checkout/application/conversation path, first-use plan, 7-day replay sprint, and proof/risk ledger.
