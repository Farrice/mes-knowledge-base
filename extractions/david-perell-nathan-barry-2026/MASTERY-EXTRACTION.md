## Hidden Knowledge

1. **Weak prose can be a false symptom.** Revision wastes effort when the real defect is an empty claim or a conviction the writer will not state.

2. **Public output is an upstream sensor.** Distribution practice can change what the writer notices before it changes audience size.

3. **Constraint prevents premature selection.** One sheet, no phone, and a finish condition keep the writer from choosing the first respectable thought.

4. **Compression needs conceptual mass.** Starting short often produces slogans because nothing substantial has been developed enough to distill.

5. **Response is confounded evidence.** Craft, packaging, current strength, audience fit, and reach must remain separate before a result can teach anything.

6. **A current changes salience more readily than substance.** The most honest current lift leaves the argument intact and changes only the entrance.

7. **An archive can hold future options.** Durable material gains new distribution windows when it is mapped to predictable cultural moments.

8. **Practice creates the right to be opportunistic.** Fast response at the right moment is often the visible return on invisible repetition.

9. **Historical contrast diagnoses the present.** The past matters here because it exposes what current systems have normalized.

10. **Placelessness reveals template dependence across media.** Prose, interiors, interfaces, and content packaging can all lose the trace of a maker.

11. **Brand fit is a separate judgment.** Nathan introduces the explicit distinction between a current that raises the brand and one that harms it, so the architecture preserves his attribution.

12. **Audience response cannot be literalized as truth.** David supplies immediate exceptions and current caveats, so response is evidence rather than an oracle.

13. **The soul boundary changes over time.** A tactic can be healthy during an earning phase and corrosive when it becomes permanent identity.

14. **Hold is a safety rule, not a David quote.** The transcript supports searching for a better current, while Antigravity adds the refusal state when evidence, fit, or truth is missing.

## Integrated Methodology

### Stage 1: Diagnose Substance Before Prose

Ask whether a worthwhile idea exists, whether the writer is withholding the real claim, or whether execution is the constraint. Do not send an idea problem into a line-editing workflow.

### Stage 2: Mine What the Writer Actually Notices

Use constrained private observation first. Build a record of concrete objects, incongruities, tensions, and reactions. Public practice can follow only with approval and with a learning purpose.

### Stage 3: Develop the Bit

Express the idea at full length before compression. Compare the 60-, 20-, and 10-second versions against the same invariant claim. Strip jargon where it hides inherited thought.

### Stage 4: Separate Quality From Current

Evaluate idea, execution, current velocity, and audience or brand fit independently. Missing or expired current evidence produces a hold.

### Stage 5: Reframe Without Corrupting

Freeze the timeless core. Build the why-now shell only after the current passes. Preserve an evergreen fallback and record the current's expiry.

### Stage 6: Use Structure to Reveal

For historical material, braid back to the present. For generic prose, identify what is missing from the supplied evidence of person or place. Route deeper writing repair to the established craft owners.

### Stage 7: Learn Without Worshipping Metrics

Treat authorized public reps as observations. Record what changed in the writer's attention and what the audience response can support. Do not turn reach into truth, conversion, or revenue.

### Stage 8: Protect the Work Behind the Tactics

Name the work the creator wants to be known for. Use currents when they fund or widen that work. Reject them when they steadily replace it.
