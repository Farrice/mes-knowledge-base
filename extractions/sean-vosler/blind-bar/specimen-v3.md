Subject: the market keeps renaming your ad problem

Even if you've already deleted three of these this week — read the quote in the middle, then decide.

I've spent a few months now reading founder threads in r/PPC and r/ecommerce sorted by TOP, never by new, mostly because I wanted to see what actually gets upvoted when a supplement founder asks why growth went flat. There's a pattern in there I can't unsee. Someone posts an ad-cost problem. And the reply that wins — the one the room votes up — renames it.

Here's the one that got me. October 2024, a 128-point r/PPC thread, top-voted reply, thirteen upvotes, from an operator doing 100+ sales a day in beauty and supplements:

"High ad cost is a symptom of an undifferentiated product."

Anonymous commenter, not an audited source, and I'd rather say that now than have you find it out later — I'm quoting it because it's the market's top-ranked verdict, not because anyone credentialed said it. Fourteen of the twenty-two threads I logged do the same thing. The founder brings a spend problem, the room hands back a positioning problem.

Then there's the half that I like to think no dashboard shows you. Over in r/Supplements, 133 points: "When you're marketing spend, is as big as theirs then you know you should be suspicious from the get go." Your buyer reads the volume itself as evidence against you. So your cost per order is a verdict on your differentiation, and your ad volume is your buyer's case against you, at the same time... Spending more makes both worse.

Which puts a very specific founder in a very specific bind. The one who wrote, verbatim: "The product works, the reviews are great, and churn is super low on subscriptions." Every input right, and the number won't move. It isn't that nobody buys it. It's that nobody believes it yet.

And the part that actually costs you shows up somewhere your reporting doesn't go. Another founder, another thread: "in the last 30 days I can't tell you what I know now that I didn't know a month ago." I've read that line many many times, it's the whole thing in one sentence. Multiply it by four quarters, then by however long you've been at this. That's a long stretch of your life funding an experiment nobody wrote the results of down anywhere. Imagine what those years were supposed to be for.
