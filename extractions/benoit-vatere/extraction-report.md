## Executive Summary

- **Core Genius**: Treats media buying as an engineered full-funnel system — every dollar categorized by funnel stage, judged by *incremental* signal (not platform-reported ROAS), tested only for home runs, placed only on platforms whose levers he controls, and fed creative with exactly one job per unit.
- **What Makes Them Different**: Everyone talks creative at Liquid Death; Benoit built the distribution machine underneath it. He is a founder-engineer who built attribution SaaS for CPG, then ran the doctrine at hypergrowth scale. ## Genius Patterns

### 1. Categorize Before You Optimize
- **Unconsciously**: First order of business in any account — map where money is spent, how, at what funnel stage. Only then judge over/under-investment.
- **Executable**: Build a spend map: every line item tagged awareness / consideration / conversion / retention before touching a single campaign.
- **Deploy**: Inheriting any account; quarterly reviews; client audits.
- **Success metric**: You can state the % of spend per stage from memory; imbalances are visible before performance data is.

### 2. Signals Over Perfection
- **Unconsciously**: "I don't need perfect, I need signals." Rejects the right-metric/right-confidence chase: "by the time you get it, it's over."
- **Executable**: For every measurement question, ask: what's the fastest directionally-reliable signal? Ship decisions on signal strength, not statistical ceremony.
- **Deploy**: Any measurement decision in a growth-stage company.
- **Success metric**: Decision latency in days, not quarters.

### 3. Fake-ROAS Detection
- **Unconsciously**: Assumes platform-reported ROAS is contaminated: "I can as a media buyer drive very high return, but those high returns are actually fake" — the $3 Meta claims would have happened without the ad.
- **Executable**: Never rank spend by platform ROAS alone. Always pair with an incrementality proxy.
- **Deploy**: Every budget-allocation meeting; whenever a channel "prints."
- **Success metric**: You can say, per channel, how much revenue would survive turning it off.

### 4. New-to-Brand % — the Poor Man's Incrementality
- **Unconsciously**: Couldn't run incrementality tests on historical data ("an MMM of two years… will never get me where I need to be within weeks"), so: high ROAS + high new-to-brand % ≈ probably incremental. Triage all spend through that filter.
- **Executable**: Pull new-to-brand rate per campaign/channel (Amazon and retail media expose it). Quadrant: ROAS × NTB%. Fund the high/high, interrogate the high-ROAS/low-NTB.
- **Deploy**: Day one at any brand, before any test infrastructure exists.
- **Success metric**: Spend shifts within two weeks of arrival, not after a measurement build-out.

### 5. Geo Holdout Fundamentals
- **Unconsciously**: Simplifies incrementality to matched markets: spend in New York, go dark in LA; if NY sales rise and LA doesn't, it worked. Self-aware: "people deep in incrementality will say 'what is he talking about?' — but this is the fundamentals."
- **Executable**: Pick two matched regions, spend in one, hold out the other, read retail sell-through delta. Even small budgets can run small geo tests.
- **Deploy**: Validating a channel before scaling; annual truth-check on the big channels.
- **Success metric**: A causal read on at least your top channel without vendor black boxes.

### 6. Incremental ROAS as North Star
- **Unconsciously**: The metric he's "paying a lot of attention on right now" — models/platforms now return incrementality reads in weeks, not years. Also probing whether social commerce (TikTok Shop) moves *scalable* retail sales or is discovery-only.
- **Executable**: Blend platform ROAS with test-based incrementality factors per channel; re-derive quarterly as models improve. (Fidelity: low — he names the destination, not his stack.)
- **Deploy**: Once NTB triage and a first geo test exist.
- **Success metric**: Budget ranked by iROAS, not ROAS.

### 7. Cost-per-PDP-View — the Mid-Funnel Chain
- **Unconsciously**: Decomposes consideration efficiency into a lever chain: CTR alone misleads → CTR × CPM = CPC → click-to-product-page drop-off (leaving-site popups, loops) → **cost per product page view** is the metric. Tests each lever separately.
- **Executable**: Instrument the full chain ad → click → PDP landing. Optimize whichever lever is weakest; a great CTR on a high CPM is still an expensive PDP view.
- **Deploy**: All consideration-stage buying, especially social → retailer .com.
- **Success metric**: You quote cost-per-PDP-view per channel; CTR is never cited alone.

### 8. Home-Run-Only Testing
- **Unconsciously**: "I'm only looking at big gains… doubledigit… more than 20% every time." Big effects reach significance fast — "if you're four days in and the data is still not conclusive, the outcome will never be big. Just move on."
- **Executable**: Design tests only where the hypothesis could plausibly move a metric ≥20%. Kill anything inconclusive at day 4. 5% winners are for multibillion incumbents defending share, not hypergrowth.
- **Deploy**: Every test roadmap; every test review.
- **Success metric**: Test velocity up, test count down; every completed test changed a decision.

### 9. Channels Are Lever Sets, Not Audiences
- **Unconsciously**: Never asks "where are my customers?" (they're everywhere); asks "what levers does this platform let me control for the job I need?" Awareness requires **frequency control** — if the platform dictates frequency, walk. Hence: social is NOT for awareness, period (1–1.5x/week algo-fed impressions inside a thousand-content feed = nobody remembers you); CTV, radio, podcast = awareness. Consideration requires click-out optimization + retargeting = Meta is good there. Conversion lives where people convert: amazon.com, walmart.com.
- **Executable**: For each stage: name the required lever (frequency / click-out optimization / conversion proximity), then admit only platforms that surrender that lever. "If they don't let me control it, I won't touch it."
- **Deploy**: Channel planning; every media-salesperson pitch.
- **Success metric**: Every channel on the plan has a stated job and a stated lever.

### 10. One Job Per Creative
- **Unconsciously**: "You cannot have everything in a single creative — who you are, how much you cost, the RTBs… You need one focus." Awareness creative's only job: remember the brand ("that we are a beverage and we're called Liquid Death" — not zero sugar, not the retailer). Liquid Death picked its lane: comedy — hard lane, but memorable. Consideration creative: link the now-remembered name to benefits + where to buy. Never both in one unit.
- **Executable**: Every brief opens with the unit's ONE job (remember-name / link-benefit / re-angle / win-back) and the funnel stage. Reject any unit carrying two jobs.
- **Deploy**: Every creative brief and creative review.
- **Success metric**: A stranger can name the unit's single job in 3 seconds.

### 11. Message Laddering by Buyer State
- **Unconsciously**: Retargeting never repeats the first message — "if they clicked and didn't convert, why didn't they convert?" → touch new, more precise objections. Lapsed one-time buyers get yet another message. Creative taxonomy = buyer-state taxonomy.
- **Executable**: Maintain a message map keyed to states: never-seen → seen-not-clicked → clicked-not-bought → bought-once-lapsed → repeat. Each state gets a distinct angle; test within state.
- **Deploy**: Retargeting and retention flows; CRM/audience architecture.
- **Success metric**: No audience segment sees the same message twice as its default.

### 12. The Golden Core (THE Failure Metric)
- **Unconsciously**: The teaser from the intro — the metric that destines marketers to fail: **CAC:LTV chased from day one.** Every product has a "golden core" of fast converters with a fixed revenue size ($500K, $1M, $3M…). Bottom-funnel spend harvests it cheaply; past it, CAC rises, the ratio inverts, and the marketer blames the algorithm ("the meta algorithm is not working anymore" — no). Everyone outside the core needs the full funnel. Conversion-only spend "is counterintuitively false — you'd get a lot of that revenue anyway"; unmeasurable awareness spend is what actually builds the base. Liquid Death ran remember-the-brand virality from the very beginning.
- **Executable**: Diagnose CAC trajectory against cumulative revenue. Rising CAC + flattening NTB = core exhaustion → shift budget up-funnel, don't retune the conversion campaigns.
- **Deploy**: Any brand whose "Meta stopped working"; early-stage budget design; investor conversations.
- **Success metric**: Awareness line items survive budget cuts with a stated reason.

## Hidden Knowledge

- **The funnel never collapsed**: "People trying to sound cool" say the funnel is shorter/dead. Aware → consider → buy always exists; speed varies per person. Some convert fast, some take months — which is exactly why single-ad-unit-full-funnel thinking fails.
- **Meta sees one fragment**: The deep reason to refuse algo-picked creative (Advantage+-style "throw creatives at the algo"): the platform optimizes its fragment; it cannot see your funnel. Letting it choose "completely distracts my full funnel." Consistency of creative-to-stage matters more than per-impression optimization.
- **Big-brand measurement is anti-startup**: 2-year MMMs and 95%-confidence rituals are for share-defenders. Adopting incumbent measurement at a growth brand is a category error, not diligence.
- **Investor pressure manufactures the failure**: "Investors are good investors, not good marketers." CAC/ROAS interrogation pushes founders into golden-core strip-mining exactly when full-funnel is needed.
- **The system is tedious, the shoots are a rounding error**: Host's observation, Benoit's confirmation — the glamorous celebrity shoot is a small part; the machine is "the collision point of data and taste." Campaign thinking vs. system thinking ("I know my place in the world; I need a system that marches day after day").
- **Control-comfort bias**: Marketers gravitate to what they control (their Shopify, their site traffic). "Where you get the most scale is mostly where you get the least amount of control." First-step thinking picks the wrong first step; pick steps by where you want to end.
- **Platform incentives are conditionally aligned**: Use platforms only in the ways your incentives and theirs coincide (Meta for click-out: aligned; Meta for awareness or creative choice: not).
- **Frequency is the physics of awareness**: Memory formation needs several exposures per week; an algorithm feeding 1–1.5x/week inside an infinite feed cannot create it at any budget. This is arithmetic, not opinion.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

### Exemplar 1: The NY/LA Geo Holdout (10:14–10:48)
- **Context**: Asked to make incrementality concrete for the audience.
- **The Example**: "I look at two regions… let's say LA and New York, and they are very similar in terms of shoppers. I'm going to spend money in New York and not spend money in LA. And I'm going to see if my sales go up in New York and don't go up in LA. And if it does, that means that what I did in New York works… If people are very deep in incrementality, they'll say 'what is he talking about?' But this is the fundamentals."
- **What makes this excellent**: A C-suite operator voluntarily downshifting to first principles, naming that experts will scoff, and doing it anyway. The show's graphics mirrored it (NY / LA word-clouds on screen). Calibration anchor for stakeholder communication: fundamentals stated plainly beat sophistication performed.

### Exemplar 2: The CTR Decomposition (12:30–14:10)
- **Context**: Explaining mid-funnel efficiency; warns "maybe I'm going into too much detail" — host: "we want all the details."
- **The Example**: Clickthrough rate is important, "but if you have a good click-through rate on a high CPM, it still drives a pretty expensive cost per click… and in the current world there's a loop — a popup that says you're leaving the site — people click but don't land. So I look at the cost per product page view… My lever is the clickthrough rate. My lever is the CPM… and I measure the drop-off from click to product page."
- **What makes this excellent**: Live decomposition of a vanity metric into a causal lever chain ending at the metric that actually prices the funnel stage. The template for auditing ANY composite metric.

### Exemplar 3: The Golden Core Monologue (26:33–27:45)
- **Context**: Advice for early-stage founders; the promised "metric destined to fail."
- **The Example**: "If you chase conversion from day one, you're going to fail… You're always going to have a certain amount of people that convert pretty fast — this golden core has a certain size: a million dollars of revenue, $3 million, $500,000. As you get out of this, there's no more scale. Your CAC starts rising, your CAC-to-LTV goes upside down, and now you're saying 'what am I doing? oh, the Meta algorithm is not working anymore.' No, no, no. The people outside the golden core need a full funnel."
- **What makes this excellent**: A complete failure mechanism — cause, curve, the exact words the failing marketer says, and the misdiagnosis — in under 150 words. Anchor for diagnostic writing.

### Anti-Exemplar: The Algo-Delegated Buyer
- **What mediocre looks like**: "All my customers are on Instagram, I'll spend there." Throws 50 creatives into Meta and "lets the algo figure it out." One ad unit carrying brand story + benefits + price + retailer. Reports platform ROAS to the board. Runs tests hunting 5% lifts that never reach significance.
- **Why it fails**: Every move surrenders a lever (frequency, creative-to-stage consistency, incrementality truth) to a platform whose incentives only fragmentarily align — while strip-mining the golden core and calling the inevitable CAC inversion an algorithm change.

## Signature Moves

- **The Spend Map First**: Before optimizing anything, tag every dollar by funnel stage and read the imbalance. → **Deploy when**: touching any account for the first time (his literal "first order of business" at Liquid Death).
- **The ROAS × NTB Cross**: Plot ROAS against new-to-brand % to find fake winners before any test exists. → **Deploy when**: budget triage with no measurement infra.
- **The Day-4 Kill**: Inconclusive at day 4 = the effect was never big; kill and move on. → **Deploy when**: every test review, no exceptions.
- **The Lever Interrogation**: First question to any platform/channel pitch: "what lever do you let me control?" No lever, no dollar. → **Deploy when**: channel planning, sales pitches, new-platform hype.
- **The One-Job Brief**: Write the creative's single job (remember-name / link-benefit / re-angle / win-back) at the top of the brief before anything else. → **Deploy when**: every brief; reject two-job units on sight.
- **The Fundamentals Downshift**: Explain measurement at NY-vs-LA level even when you know experts will scoff. → **Deploy when**: any stakeholder/board/client conversation about attribution.
- **The Algo Refusal**: Hand-pick which creatives serve which audience; never enable platform creative selection. → **Deploy when**: platforms push automated creative distribution (era-bound: Advantage+-style products, 2026).

## Methodology (progression)

1. **AUDIT** — spend map by funnel stage (produce: categorized spend sheet + imbalance read).
2. **MEASURE** — layer signals: platform metrics → NTB cross → geo holdout → iROAS (produce: channel truth table).
3. **TEST** — home-run-only roadmap with day-4 kills (produce: test charter + kill log).
4. **PLACE** — lever-audited channel plan per stage (produce: channel × job × lever matrix).
5. **SPEAK** — one-job creative briefs + buyer-state message ladder (produce: brief headers + message map).
6. **SCALE** — golden-core diagnostic + control allocation + retail media (CPG) (produce: budget-shift memo).
