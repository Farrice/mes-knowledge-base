# Reader Access Rewrite — The Seeds

## Private signal

The narrator cannot bear to discard a choice her mother left unfinished.

## Causal diagnosis

The problem is not vocabulary alone. The scene explains its emotional conclusion before any consequential action earns it. The jar should become the carrier; uncertainty about what it contains should remain real.

## Protected elements

- The mother is dead.
- The seeds are unlabeled.
- Planting is possible, not promised healing.
- Grief receives no compensating lesson.

## Finished rewrite

The kitchen had been emptied in sensible groups.

Pans for Ruth. Plates for the charity shop. The cracked blue mug in my bag, though I had no use for it and never drank tea.

At the back of the spice cupboard I found a jam jar filled with seeds. No label. Mum labeled everything. Lentils. Batteries. String too short to keep, which was a joke until she kept it for twelve years.

I held the jar to the window. The seeds were small and pale and could have been flowers or weeds or something that would never come up at all.

“Bin,” my brother said from the hall.

He was right. We had been right all day.

I put the jar in my coat pocket.

In March, I tipped half of it into the strip of dirt behind my flat. I kept the other half in the cupboard. That felt important, though I could not have said why.

For three weeks there was only soil.

Then one morning, beside the wall, something green had made a mistake.
