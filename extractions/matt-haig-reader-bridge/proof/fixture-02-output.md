# Child-Story Reset — The Last Memory

## Complexity diagnosis

The seven agencies and legal taxonomy explain a system before a person has something to lose. The ownership question needs one object, one forbidden action, and one relationship.

## Four-sentence reset

1. Mara’s father is dead, but his last memory is stored in a glass bead.
2. She wants to watch it once before she destroys it for work.
3. The bead belongs to the city, and opening it will erase its evidence seal.
4. If she watches, she may lose her job; if she does not, her father’s last hour will be deleted unseen.

## Generative object

**The sealed memory bead.** It turns ownership, grief, evidence, and temptation into one thing Mara can hold and choose to open.

## Adult-depth rebuild

The legal question remains, but it enters through consequence: the city calls stored memories property, families call them remains, and Mara has enforced the city’s rule on strangers for nine years. Her father’s bead makes professional certainty personal without making either side simple.

## Revised premise

A municipal archivist who has spent nine years destroying the unclaimed memories of the dead receives her father’s sealed final hour and has one night to decide whether to obey the law she built her life around.

## Selected title

**The Last Memory** — clear object and finality, with ownership and contents left unresolved.

## Opening movement

The bead arrived in a grey evidence box at 4:12 on a Thursday, between the memory of a dentist falling off a ferry and a child’s first lie.

Mara signed for it before she saw the name.

Her father had always written his sevens with a line through the middle. The clerk had copied the habit onto the label.

She turned the box over. The seal was clean.

For nine years she had told families that clean seals were a kindness.

**CONTINUE INTO SCENE TWO.** The engine now creates choices. World rules should enter only when they change what Mara can do.
