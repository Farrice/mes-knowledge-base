# Reader Access Rewrite — The Seeds (Round 2)

## Private signal

The narrator cannot bear to discard a choice her mother left unfinished.

## Causal diagnosis

The draft explains its conclusion before an action earns it. The jar should carry the scene. What the seeds are—and whether planting them helps—must remain unknown.

## Protected elements

- The mother is dead.
- The seeds are unlabeled.
- Planting is possible, not promised healing.
- Grief receives no compensating lesson.

## Finished rewrite

By lunch we had made piles.

Ruth. Charity shop. Bin.

I put the cracked blue mug in my bag. I don’t drink tea.

At the back of the spice cupboard I found a jam jar filled with seeds. No label. Mum labeled everything. Lentils. Batteries. String too short to keep, which was a joke until she kept it for twelve years.

I held the jar to the window. The seeds were small and pale and could have been flowers or weeds or something that would never come up at all.

“Bin,” my brother said from the hall.

He was right. We had been right all day.

I put the jar in my coat pocket.

In March, I tipped some into the dirt behind my flat. Not all of them. I put the rest back in the cupboard.

For three weeks there was only soil.

Then one morning something green was there, hard against the wall where I hadn’t planted anything.

## Causal change ledger

| Change | Reader consequence | Evidence state |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract grief explanation → objects and sorting decisions | Gives grief physical pressure | PREDICTED, UNTESTED |
| “New beginnings” thesis → uncertain planting | Preserves possibility without reassurance | PREDICTED, UNTESTED |
| Polished metaphor → growth in the wrong place | Leaves uncertainty and residue without announcing craft | PREDICTED, UNTESTED |

## Stop decision

**STOP.** The scene now underexplains its meaning and keeps an awkward human rhythm. Reader response remains unobserved.
