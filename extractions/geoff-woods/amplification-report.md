## A. GAP SCAN

### MISSED — in the source, absent from the extraction

**A1. The AI-Driven Leadership doctrine (the biggest miss).**
Roughly the final third of the interview is not about how one person prompts AI — it is about **leading people in an AI-driven org**, and it has concrete, repeatable moves. genius.md #15 records the content; **no workflow lets a practitioner RUN any of it.** The named moves in the source:
- The **thinking-leverage coaching protocol**: *"It is your job to identify what you believe the 20% priorities are... I'm not going to tell you what to do this week. You're going to show up to our one-on-one and you're going to tell me... Then it's my job to help you see what you don't see so that we can bridge those gaps."*
- The **EA-training reps**: *"Are those in order of priority?"* / *"This is your meeting. I'm going to go get a coffee. When I come back, you're driving the meeting."*
- **Own 100% of your job** (Keller lineage): *"If your boss has to follow up with you to ask if you got something done, you are not owning 100% of your job."*
- **Superpowers-matching**: *"What are your god-given superpowers?... This is probably three things that truly make you you... here's the 20% of this role these two things got to match up with."*
- **Org design**: *"AI-driven organizations are far more like networks and swarms rather than this hierarchy of top down."* / **department-chair vs. daycare employees.**
Why it matters: Farrice's niche is coaching *invisible experts* to own and articulate their value. Woods' thinking-leverage protocol IS a coaching methodology — it maps directly onto the client work. Workflow potential: **HIGH.**
Proposed: **`/gw-thinking-leverage`** — coach a person (report, client, or self) to own 100% of their job by bringing their own 20% plan, not a task list. Optionally a second, **`/gw-ai-org-design`** — superpowers → 20% match → agency-to-build-agents, networks-not-hierarchy.

**A2. Global custom-instructions profile builder.**
Woods installs a persistent operating profile *once*, across every tool: *"Mine knows I'm a high-D. I have zero patience... Give me the 20% that's going to drive 80%... don't fluff me up... be the challenger, red-team everything, fact-check everything so that what you send me is solid... I can do any prompt and it'll give an answer and then say here's my red team automatically."*
The extraction captures only the **anti-sycophancy slice** (persona-flip Phase 5). It misses the broader deliverable: a portable custom-instructions profile encoding communication style, 20%-delivery preference, format prefs, fact-check defaults, and the em-dash ban — installed into ChatGPT/Claude/etc. so *every* session inherits the operator. Workflow potential: **MEDIUM-HIGH.**
Proposed: **`/gw-custom-instructions`** — build the operator's once-and-done global instruction block (persona-flip's anti-sycophancy block becomes a component of it, not the whole).

**A3. Environmental trigger design (the sticky notes as a behavior-change tool).**
*"What's missing is a trigger. A trigger in their environment that will cause them to change."* — the two desk stickies (*"How can AI help me do this?"* + *"CRIT"*). genius.md #9 records it; no workflow builds a trigger system. The insight is generalizable ("knowledge doesn't change behavior; triggers do") and is exactly the kind of thing Farrice could productize for clients who *learn* a method but never *deploy* it. ## B. DEPTH SCAN — workflows compressing multiple tools

**B1. persona-flip (06) → split out the Challenger red-team as `/gw-challenger`. (STRONGEST split.)**
persona-flip serially rotates three genuinely distinct personas: Challenger (find cracks/biases/assumptions), ideal customer (model rejection), aggressive board member (demand ambition). These attack *different axes*. A practitioner very often wants **only** the Challenger — "red-team this deliverable" is a high-frequency standalone reach that does not need the full rotation, and does not need a real audience input (which the customer pass requires). Splitting the Challenger into an atomic workflow makes it reachable in one move; persona-flip stays as the full ceiling-breaker. **Bar met: a practitioner would reach for the split-out piece independently — daily.**
(Secondary note: the *ideal-customer pass* overlaps heavily with simulate-room (07). Worth a cross-reference so they don't drift into two half-versions of the same simulation.)

**B2. ai-board (05) → split out the future-self advisor as `/gw-future-self`.**
The future-self persona is Woods' *"most valuable board member"* and is conceptually independent of building a whole board — an operator would plausibly want *just* "build me a future-self advisor to consult daily" without the gap-derivation, culling, and exemplar-casting of a full board. It is also the most **sellable** standalone client deliverable (see D). **Bar met: reachable and wanted on its own.**

**B3. twenty-percent (03) → consider splitting the AI-vs-human handoff sort as `/gw-ai-handoff`.**
The 50%-curveball sort (*"if AI is 50% as good as you at a 20% task, start handing it over"*) is a decision tool a practitioner would run *without* the full prioritization ritual — "which of my current tasks should I hand to AI now?" Weaker than B1/B2 (it leans on the star-test output), so **MEDIUM** — flag, don't rush.

Workflows that should **NOT** be split (splitting = inflation): CRIT (01, already atomic), simulate-room (07, the calibration loop is the point and must stay attached), thought-partner (02, the orchestration IS the deliverable), cognitive-guard (09).

## Honesty Section — over-reach & fidelity risks

**H1 (material). Biographical specifics that are NOT in this transcript.** SKILL.md and genius.md state Woods was **"Chief Growth Officer of Jindal Steel & Power (market cap growth $750M → $12B during his tenure)."** The transcript says only: *"I was a sea level exec for a public company based out of India."* It names **no company, no title, no market-cap figure.** The Jindal Steel identification, the CGO title, and the $750M→$12B number were imported from outside this source. Two problems: (a) for an extraction that explicitly *quarantines* claims, attaching an **unverified external market-cap figure** to the persona is inconsistent with its own discipline; (b) it is presented in SKILL.md line 15 as parenthetical fact ("— his stated figure"), but he did not state it *in this source*. Recommendation: either source-verify externally and relabel as **"external background, not from the interview,"** or drop the figure to the claims-quarantine block. The **"$300M saved in 10 minutes"** figure, by contrast, IS in the transcript and is correctly quarantined.

**H2 (minor). "Co-founded."** The extraction says Woods *"co-founded the training and consulting company behind The ONE Thing with Gary Keller and Jay Papasan."* The transcript says he was *"partners with Gary Keller"* and *"they hired me to turn their book, The One Thing, into this training and consulting company."* "Hired me to build it" is a slightly weaker claim than "co-founded." Roughly supported by "partners," but a shade generous. Low risk.

**H3 (managed, worth watching). Tier O billing.** The CO-CREATION-CARD is a deliberate, *internally well-labeled* fusion — Lineage A (Woods, source-grounded) and Lineage B (Claude-native, explicitly "mine, not Woods'"). This is handled honestly inside the card. The only residual risk is SKILL.md's front-matter and intro elevating "Tier O" to near-equal billing *under Woods' name* — a skimmer could conflate Claude-native operator doctrine with extracted Woods material. Keep the lineage labels loud anywhere the card is surfaced. Not an over-reach; a labeling-discipline note.

No fabricated techniques found in the 12 workflows — every workflow move traces to a specific transcript moment. The workflow layer is faithful.
