## 1. The business

- **Offer:** a ladder — free teardown (door opener) → **$750 prepaid Angle Map** (60-minute live read + three named campaign angles in 48 hours) → **$2,500 prepaid Proof-to-Market Sprint** (10-business-day launch-messaging intensive) → $3,000–$5,000/mo recurring implementation (parked until paid proof exists).
- **Price point:** $750 entry, $2,500 core. High-trust, low-volume: one Sprint at a time, two per month max.
- **How it's bought:** DM-led on LinkedIn, not feed-led. One-gap DMs to verified prospects; content is a proof asset sent after the DM, not the front door.
- **Who actually pays:** the founder (or Head of Brand / VP Marketing) at an already-selling supplement, performance-nutrition, recovery, or sports-science brand, generally under $15M revenue, with a **dated occasion inside 60–90 days** — a launch, retail push, reformulation, new ingredient, or repositioning.
- **Viewer = buyer check: PASSES.** The founder who would watch this content is the same person who pays. No split — everything centers on him.
- **Explicitly not the business:** content creation/ghostwriting, social-media management retainers, standalone GEO/AEO audits, solo-coach positioning, "AI-expert" branding. In his words: *"I don't build the ads, run the account, or write your calendar. You've got people for that. I decide what they should be making, and why."*

## 2. Ideal Viewer Avatar — "Founder Marcus"

**Portrait.** Marcus is 38. He's the founder and formulator of a performance-supplement brand doing about $4M a year — he built the product because he needed it himself, and he can talk substantiation science for an hour without notes. He has total decision authority and spends his own runway. He has near-zero time: he'd rather be in the lab or the gym than on LinkedIn. Right now there's a launch on the calendar inside 60–90 days, and he does not have a settled answer to what the campaign should actually say.

**Psychographics (the gold):**
- Midnight fear, verbatim from the research: *"I'm the most knowledgeable person in this category and I'm invisible — and some kid with half my expertise and twice the posting cadence is becoming the name people associate with this."*
- Identity: *"I'm the real one. I earned this the hard way."* Visibility feels like joining the influencers he built the brand against.
- What he's already tried that failed: scaled ChatGPT content ("volume and zero edge" — and the shame loop: *"we pay premium CAC to drive traffic to content a free model could generate in 10 seconds"*); 8–12 AI tools that don't replace the thinking; "testing" that's the same ad 15 times; agency ROAS he doesn't trust; rankings that stayed flat while clicks vanished.
- Buying behavior: pre-$10M founders are highly active buyers of tactical $1,000–$5,000 sprints to de-risk before retainers. Highest-reliability trigger: a new exec or funding announcement (response rates halve after day 90).
- Mis-self-diagnosis: he's Problem-Aware but thinks it's an AEO problem or a writing/volume problem. The belief every piece of content must shift: "our copy problem is a writing problem" → **"it's an unmade decision: what we prove, who must believe it, what we'll defend."**

**Secondary avatar (noted, not centered):** "Brand-Lead Dana" — Head of Brand / VP Growth at a $3M–$40M DTC brand, 34–44, owns $15K+/mo paid budget, retainer authority to ~$5–8K/mo, "carries the number… and is the one who will get blamed." Buys on a longer, committee-shaped cycle. Her midnight fear: *"What if I spent the last year optimizing a thing that doesn't matter anymore, and everyone's too polite to tell me?"* Content should let Dana in; it should speak to Marcus.

**Disqualifiers:** pre-launch/no revenue; >$1M/mo ad spend with in-house creative team; any product Farrice wouldn't defend.

## 3. Dream outcome

The founder launches on one settled, provable message — knowing what to say, what approved proof backs it, and why buyers should care — before funding a dollar of execution, so the product stays on the market and reaches the people who need it.

## 4. Pain point bank (ranked)

*Add to this forever; source = comments + DMs — yours and your competitors'.*

1. **"Sea of sameness"** — every ad library in the category looks like one brand wrote it. "I lined up nine supplement homepages last week. I couldn't tell you which brand was which. Neither can your buyer."
2. **Founder Fog** — 73% of DTC founders can't articulate their positioning in one sentence without category jargon.
3. **Compliance strangles claims** — FTC exposure $53,088/violation, warning letters routine. "The biggest risk isn't under-promising. It's over-claiming without the data to back it up."
4. **The honest-claim asymmetry** — the honest claim needs a paragraph; the competitor's lie fits on a banner.
5. **LTV:CAC collapse** — below 3:1 the model breaks; churn is a verdict on the promise.
6. **AI-content trust collapse** — consumer enthusiasm 60% → 26% (2023 vs 2025). "AI didn't flatten brand voice; it revealed that most brands never had one."
7. **Creative fatigue** — ads die in 7–14 days, down from 30+. "Your winning ad didn't die because the market got bored. It died because you did."
8. **AI search names competitors, not them** — "If you're not in the AI answer, you're not on the shortlist."
9. **Scaling wall** — hero-product brand at $1–5M can't expand portfolio/retail without real message architecture.
10. **The customer's BS-detector** — "You're paying more for the marketing than for what's actually in the bottle."
