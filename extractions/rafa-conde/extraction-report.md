# Rafa Conde - Mastery Extraction

## Content Assessment

- **Source**: Dive Club YouTube episode, "Rafa Conde - Make your designs memorable", 59:01.
- **Expert**: Rafa Conde, product designer, design engineer, and maker of Hand Mirror.
- **Domain**: Memorable software, feeling-led product design, prototype communication, first-run experience, creative thinking, product charm.
- **Depth Tier**: Deep.
- **Existing Overlap**: Adjacent to creative direction, messaging, taste, naming, video, and narrative skills. New category needed: product memorability.

## Executive Summary

Rafa's core genius is turning product work into emotional translation. He does not ask whether a feature can be shown. He asks whether the idea can be felt. The work moves from vision to feeling, from feeling to artifact, and from artifact to memory.

The extraction captures a system for creating product experiences that users understand quickly, feel specifically, and remember later.

## Genius Patterns

1. **Feeling Before Feature**: Define the target emotion and remembered sentence before UI or copy.
2. **Sell the Idea Artifact**: Make an ad, short demo, or narrative prototype instead of only sharing mockups.
3. **First-Run Stage Setting**: Treat onboarding as the emotional frame for the whole product.
4. **Tiny Signature Detail**: Add small, specific details that make a product feel authored.
5. **Scope as Charm Protection**: Keep small products simple enough to love and explain.
6. **One-Step-Ahead Magic**: Use smart timing and anticipation to make software feel alive.
7. **Pacing as Product Feeling**: Use rhythm, motion, cuts, and silence as part of the design.
8. **Device and Body Context**: Show software where it is used, not only as clean screens.
9. **Personality Through Defaults**: Let defaults, settings, labels, and unlocks carry taste.
10. **Remembered Feelings Library**: Mine old experiences for emotional mechanics.

## Hidden Knowledge

- Users remember feelings more reliably than UI structures.
- Product demos fail when they show mechanics before context.
- Charm is strongest when concentrated in a few moments.
- Scope discipline is an emotional design decision.
- Video is a product design medium, not only a marketing channel.
- Tiny details make simple products feel cared for.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

### First MacBook Setup
- **Context**: Rafa remembers the emotional impact of a first-run MacBook setup years later.
- **What it reveals**: First impressions can become product mythology.
- **Calibration**: Onboarding should communicate the world the user has entered.

### Feature as Ad
- **Context**: Rafa argues that a feature idea should be sold through an artifact that communicates why it matters.
- **What it reveals**: The designer's job includes making the idea legible to people outside the maker's head.
- **Calibration**: If the idea needs buy-in, build a persuasive artifact.

### Hand Mirror
- **Context**: A small menu-bar camera check app gains affection through a clear center and playful details.
- **What it reveals**: Small utility products can be loved when restraint and charm work together.
- **Calibration**: Add only what makes the product more itself.

## Signature Moves

- **Name the Feeling**: Choose one exact emotional target before building.
- **Make the Ad**: Convert a feature into a short persuasive product artifact.
- **Map the First Memory**: Design first use as a remembered scene.
- **Add One Tiny Signature**: Choose a small detail that users can retell.
- **Cut to Protect Charm**: Remove useful additions that weaken the center.
- **Show in Context**: Present prototypes on device, with pacing and human stakes.
