# Behavior Proof — Pursuit-to-Offer and Story Angles

## Proof boundary

These are deterministic constructed controls grounded in source mechanics. They prove routing and refusal behavior, not buyer demand, audience lift, or revenue.

## Positive: tacit pursuit becomes an inspectable method

Input: a consultant repeatedly converts broad health claims into one sourced claim, one explicit boundary, and one buyer-use case. The behavior is supplied, repeatable, and produces a scoped deliverable.

Before: the Jun engine could preserve this as Pursuit but stopped at a story packet.

After: the Pursuit-to-Offer Miner returns `METHOD_CANDIDATE` with inputs, steps, decision rules, output, failure modes, `DELIVERABLE` proof ceiling, and an `OFFER_HYPOTHESIS`. It then routes to `/ml-validate-offer`; it does not claim demand.

## Negative: advice is not a method

Input: “I stayed consistent and believed in myself.” There is no sequence, decision rule, correction, or inspectable output. Result: `NEEDS_SOURCE`, then `NO_OFFER` if the missing facts cannot be supplied.

## Negative: method is not market proof

Input supplies repeatable actions and a real sample deliverable but no sends, replies, calls, deposits, sales, or payments. Result: `METHOD_CANDIDATE`, proof ceiling `DELIVERABLE`, `MARKET PROOF: NO EVENT`, and validation handoff.

## Positive: one moment yields meaning-distinct angles

Input: a creator begins strength training and supplies changes in concentration, recovery routine, self-trust, and time with a training partner. The Angle Expander may produce mental, physical, values/identity, and relationship angles. Each keeps the same event but receives its own fact-traced 3P.

## Negative: paraphrases are not angles

“Training made me more focused,” “lifting improved my focus,” and “I concentrate better after the gym” share one meaning. The expander returns one angle, not three. It must not inflate a hook permutation into semantic breadth.
