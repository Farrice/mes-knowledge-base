# Ben Meer x HubSpot: 10 hook frameworks

Source: `ben-meer-10-hook-frameworks.pptx`, 13 slides.

1. **How to `[X]`. `[Action] [Y]`.** Combines a pain point with an immediate promise of relief; the colon/list cue sustains attention.
2. **It took me `[X years]` to learn this: `[truth]`.** Frames the post as a shortcut to hard-won experience.
3. **Give me `[X minutes]`, and I’ll teach you `[skill]`.** Makes the time cost and expected return explicit.
4. **`[Number]` free `[item]` to `[benefit]`.** Combines a count, no-cost resource, and desired outcome.
5. **I’ll let you in on a secret: `[counterintuitive truth]`.** Uses a true expectation break; the deck warns against edginess without substance.
6. **`[Number]` ways to `[result]` in `[timeframe]`.** Defines scope, benefit, and time boundary.
7. **Most people `[common behavior]`. The top `[X%]` `[contrasting behavior]`.** Creates identity contrast. Use only with defensible percentile evidence.
8. **`[Impressive result]`. The exact `[X]`-step system.** Leads with proof, then offers the process. Use only with owned or cited proof.
9. **If I had to start `[goal]` from zero, I’d do these `[X]` things.** Removes assumed advantages and turns experience into a restart plan.
10. **The `[X]`-second rule that changed how I `[area of life]`.** Makes the method small and testable.

The deck closes with the test: “Is this surprising or actionable?” It then points to the seven-part guide and repeats the source-attributed 1K-to-840K-in-under-three-years case.
