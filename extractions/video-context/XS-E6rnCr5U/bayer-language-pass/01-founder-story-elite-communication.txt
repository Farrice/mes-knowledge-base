In 2018, I sold a fitness offer called Zero to Hero.

The promise was blunt:

Help “single ordinary guys in their 30s become extraordinary” and get into the best shape of their lives.

I knew how to make a transformation sound close enough to touch.

Eight years later, one sentence governs my work:

“Real data makes you careful. Careful sounds like maybe, and maybe loses to a lie that will say anything.”

Those two documents make a clean before and after.

My life between them is not that clean.

I cannot point to one cinematic turn when the old language fell away. The record gives me something less dramatic and more useful.

Three years behind a vitamin-shop counter, where my recommendation could be the final checkpoint between a bottle and a buyer.

Eighteen years training more than 1,000 people, hearing the same human question in a thousand different forms:

What should I take, and is this worth it?

Years spent buying the same pre-workouts and fat burners people later asked me to judge.

I was the buyer first.

Today, I build health and performance messaging around a harder question:

What is the strongest argument the proof can actually carry?

That question protects the truth without making the truth apologize for itself.

I know the dialect of hype from the inside.

I also know the quieter failure that comes after it: good evidence made so careful that nobody can feel what it means.

Truth needs more than restraint.

It needs a spine.
