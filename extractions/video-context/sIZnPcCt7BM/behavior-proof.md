# Behavior Proof — Education-Business Scaling Decision

## Input Tested

A creator has one personal brand and one email list. A $299 course averages $35,000 monthly revenue over the last 12 months; the best month was $60,000. Delivery depends on one excellent account manager whose process and definition of done are undocumented. The founder has three contractors, has published four YouTube videos, and sees no consistent lead flow yet. They want to spend $20,000 on production, launch a paid community, and add a second cohort offer in the same quarter because the course feels plateaued.

## Weakness Diagnosed

Before this expansion, the closest Nicolas Cole route was the product-progression planner. It could map a community or cohort into a product ladder and gate advanced vehicles by operating capacity, but it did not force:

- a concentrated-traffic cannibalization decision;
- subjectivity unbundling before scale;
- a More → Better → New plateau test;
- cheap proof before premium production;
- trailing-average protection against a peak-month anchor;
- one priority plus accepted atrophy.

The structural risk was a plausible-looking product roadmap that still multiplied the wrong engine.

## Source Mechanics Used

- Subjectivity does not scale (01:26-03:10).
- Concentrated traffic scales vertically (03:10-05:11).
- More → Better → New and the three-month better search (11:07-17:27).
- Budget version before premium production (17:27-20:25).
- Attention is zero-sum (27:42-30:07).
- Customer outcome compression (30:07-32:01).
- Rolling 12-month base instead of peak month (32:01-33:22).
- Scale can be the wrong economic choice (33:22-35:33).

## Output Produced

### Verdict

**STABILIZE, THEN SCALE VERTICALLY.** Do not launch the community, second cohort, or $20,000 production upgrade this quarter. The proposed “horizontal growth” still depends on one brand and one list, while core delivery is subjective and YouTube has not passed the budget proof.

### Traffic Topology

| Source | Offers dependent on it | State | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder personal brand | $299 course, proposed community, proposed cohort | Concentrated | New offers risk choice overload and cannibalization; deepen the course engine first. |
| YouTube | Proposed premium production | Unproven | Four videos are not a credible traction test. Keep production cheap. |

### Subjectivity Repair

Document the account manager’s recurring decisions, escalation triggers, customer-success actions, completion standards, and incentives. Run the documented version with a second operator on five customer cases. No automation or additional account manager until the second operator reaches the agreed quality threshold without founder rescue.

### More → Better → New Gate

- **More:** publish 24 low-cost YouTube videos over 12 weeks and run the current course sales path consistently.
- **Better:** improve one bottleneck at a time—topic selection, opening, CTA, landing-page education, onboarding, or completion—not all at once.
- **New unlock:** only reconsider another offer after 90 days if the current engine has hit its stated rep target, the delivery role is objective, and the chosen improvement metric has not moved across three review cycles.

### Economic Base

- Operating base: $35,000 monthly trailing average, not the $60,000 peak.
- Premium-production budget test: four videos is `NO EVENT`; 24 consistent low-cost videos must produce a defined lead or customer threshold before production spend.
- Fixed-cost rule: the proposed $20,000 cannot be justified from the peak month.
- Rollback point: stop the YouTube test after 24 videos if the predefined qualified-lead threshold is missed; do not reinterpret views as sales proof.

### Customer Outcome Compression

Before adding a community, audit the current course for the smallest completion path. Replace information-only modules with checklists, templates, or prompts where the customer can get the same result faster. Use completion, time-to-first-result, and customer effort as the product metrics.

### 90-Day Compounding Bet

- Owner: founder.
- Priority: make the current course acquisition and delivery engine repeatable.
- Weekly actions: two budget YouTube videos; one conversion/retention review; one account-manager process extraction session; one customer-outcome cut.
- Metric: qualified leads and course sales from the existing engine, plus second-operator delivery accuracy.
- Stop condition: 24 videos completed with the predefined lead threshold missed, or five delivery cases fail the quality threshold after one repair cycle.
- Accepted atrophy: no community build, no second cohort, no premium studio project for 90 days.
- Review date: 90 days after the plan begins.

## Behavior Delta

The source changes the route from **product expansion** to **engine stabilization and vertical depth**. It also changes the proof burden: a new product is no longer unlocked by an attractive roadmap; it requires independent traffic or evidence that the current vehicle has exhausted more and better. Premium production is no longer a brand-quality preference; it follows a cheap behavior test. The result is one decision and one compounding bet rather than three simultaneous builds.

## Validation Run

- Positive control: selects `STABILIZE` and produces all required decision fields.
- Negative control 1: concentrated traffic prevents horizontal scale without an anti-cannibalization mechanism.
- Negative control 2: subjective delivery prevents automation and hiring.
- Negative control 3: four videos and no lead threshold count as `NO EVENT`, not traction.
- Negative control 4: the $60,000 peak month cannot support fixed commitments without trailing evidence.

## Remaining Risk

This is a deterministic applied scenario, not a live business trial. Human review, customer outcomes, revenue impact, routing frequency, and real operator adoption remain `UNTESTED`. The source’s own business figures remain speaker-reported.
