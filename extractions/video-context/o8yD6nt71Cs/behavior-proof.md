# Behavior Proof: Seth Godin Problem-to-Action Loop

## Proof Type

Applied strategy scenario plus cold-start route check. This proves the workflow can change a decision artifact; it does **not** prove buyer response, revenue, or market effectiveness.

## Input Tested

> I keep refining a paid diagnostic because I want it to feel more distinctive, but I do not want to send it until the offer is undeniable. I have three versions, zero buyer conversations this week, and I am using how polished the PDF feels as my measure.

- Stakes: another 30 days of preparation without demand evidence.
- Constraint: no external send or outreach without explicit operator approval.
- Attempted fixes: more copy, more differentiation, more PDF polish.
- Feared downside: a warm contact is indifferent, which feels like proof the offer is weak.
- Current measure: internal polish confidence.

## Weakness Diagnosed

The apparent problem is offer quality. The load-bearing problem is an incompatible demand: obtain market certainty before allowing market exposure. “Undeniable” protects against rejection and preserves deniability; PDF polish is a controllable but commercially weak proxy.

## Problem-to-Action Memo

### Classification

| Obstacle | Type | Evidence | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| The offer may not be specific enough | Problem | It can be tested and revised | Test one promise with one qualified person |
| A buyer may say no | Situation | Buyer choice cannot be controlled | Accept refusal as possible |
| No external send without approval | Chosen constraint | Operator permission boundary | Prepare the test; pause at send |
| The offer must be undeniable before exposure | Problem | Certainty is being demanded from internal polish | Replace with a bounded evidence test |

### Root Post

- Load-bearing assumption: market exposure is allowed only after internal certainty.
- Symptoms organized: repeated PDF revision, zero buyer conversations, and polish used as the progress signal.
- Primary protection: **deniability** supported by **fear**.

### Truth and Tradeoff

- And-rewrite: “I want a distinctive offer **and** I need qualified buyer exposure before I can know whether the distinction matters.”
- Accepted cost: one qualified person may be indifferent or say no.
- Purpose exchange: for the next seven days, trade one short private conversation and 20 minutes of preparation for evidence about which promised change matters to one funded health/performance buyer.

### Actual-Risk Ledger

| Feared downside | Maximum loss | Who would know | Reversible? | Downside cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warm contact is indifferent | One conversation and mild social discomfort | Operator plus one contact | Yes | Ask for reaction, not purchase; stop after one conversation |
| Offer language is wrong | 20 minutes and one discarded version | Operator | Yes | Test a three-sentence promise, not a rebuilt deck |
| Rejection feels like category failure | One data point misread as a verdict | Operator | Yes | Pre-label result as one qualified response, not market proof |

### Smallest Useful Experiment

- Act: prepare a three-sentence offer statement and one question: “Which part would be worth solving now, if any?”
- Audience: one opted-in, relevant buyer or trusted buyer-proxy; exclude friends grading the prose and broad public audiences.
- Date and owner: ready today; external send waits for Farrice's approval.
- Decision informed: whether to keep the promise, revise the problem, or park the offer.
- Qualified signal: the person names a live consequence, asks a delivery/price question, or says no with a reason.
- Stop rule: after three qualified conversations, decide keep/revise/park; no fourth round of PDF polish without a repeated buyer reason.

### Feedback-Sponsor Audit

- Current measure: “the PDF feels polished.”
- Sponsor/beneficiary: the operator's desire for controllable certainty.
- Real behavior it weakly proxies: buyer comprehension and willingness to continue.
- Verdict: replace with qualified consequence language, questions, and next-step behavior from three conversations.

### Decision-Once Habit

- Cadence: one qualified offer conversation per weekday for four weeks, only after explicit approval for the outreach list/message.
- Minimum spec: one live consequence discussed and one response reason logged.
- Review date: end of week four.
- Maximum misses: one per week.
- Quit rule: park or materially reposition after three consecutive weeks with no consequence, question, referral, deposit, or repeated solvable objection.

### Verdict

Prepare the three-sentence test and approval-ready send list. Do not improve the PDF again before qualified response evidence exists.
