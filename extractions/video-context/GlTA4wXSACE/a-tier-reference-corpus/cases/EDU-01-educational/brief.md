# Blind Brief EDU-01

## Communication Job

Create a 700-900 word educational long-form piece that teaches supplement-brand founders how to distinguish ingredient evidence from evidence for the claim made by a finished product. The reader should leave with a six-rung diagnostic they can apply to a product page.

- Domain/truth risk: educational communication built on evidence-sensitive health and regulatory material.
- Destination: provisional LinkedIn article or Substack-portable education piece.

## Six-Rung Framework

1. The compound changes something in a cell or animal model.
2. The ingredient has a research literature.
3. The ingredient has evidence for the specific goal being claimed.
4. The studied dose matches the product dose.
5. The studied form and population match the product and buyer.
6. The finished formula itself has been tested.

## Supplied Evidence

- A public supplement-evidence operator reported that his map had scored 475 ingredient-goal pairings at zero; a better re-query found at least one real RCT for 277. This is verified as his first-party account plus a public dataset and site caveat, not a measured category-wide error rate.
- The public dataset contains 2,156 cleaned supplement-goal pairings. The published CSV is the pre-correction version.
- A 2022 JAMA Network Open analysis of 30 Amazon immune-support supplements found inaccurate labels in 17 and no third-party seals. It must always carry the 2022 date.
- FTC health-claim guidance expects competent and reliable scientific evidence and asks whether research is relevant to the specific claim. Do not state that FTC universally requires a fixed number of trials.
- Evidence generation closer to the finished formula is an emerging differentiator, graded `LIKELY`, not a universal legal requirement.
- A zero-result search may mean no trial exists or may reflect naming, outcome-language, or coverage failures. Those states must not be collapsed.
