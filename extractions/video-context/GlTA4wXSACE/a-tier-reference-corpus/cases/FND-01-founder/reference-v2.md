## Reference Asset

**I kept trying to become one thing**

I could build almost anything to 90%. Sometimes 99%. The work tended to stop right before full visibility.

For years, that pattern sat beside another one: I have never fit cleanly inside one lane.

I am half Black and half Filipino. I was bullied for being different and faster, and visible range made belonging harder. Being legible in pieces felt easier than asking people to understand the whole. Then the business world gave that old pressure a cleaner name: focus.

Pick one lane. Make the work simple to label. Keep the rest as hobbies.

The pick-one-thing advice is a cage with better lighting.

I still tried to live inside it.

At 37, my life does not read like a straight line. I am Jennifer's husband, JJ's father, and a stay-at-home dad. I also spent 18 years in strength, conditioning, and behavior change, helping more than 1,000 clients through transformations. That work taught me to pay attention to the gap between knowing and doing. A plan can be sound and still fail if it cannot cross that gap.

During COVID, I taught myself design. Design led to prompt engineering. Prompt engineering led to a working AI orchestration system with more than 90 agents, built solo during naptimes.

None of those moves erased the one before it.

Coaching taught me to watch behavior instead of assuming information was enough. Design taught me to make complexity visible. Writing taught me to find the clean sentence underneath a complicated thought. AI systems gave those disciplines a place to work together.

Strength training, psychology, anime, spirituality, fatherhood, writing, and AI systems keep meeting in the same work because they meet in the same mind. The range is the mechanism behind the work.

It also gave me more places to hide the old pattern.

Building the first 90% could still look like progress. Sometimes it was 99%. The work was real. The missing part arrived when another person could see the whole thing and decide what they thought of it. Research and preparation had to become release.

That is where many projects stopped.

Parallax became the first writing space where I felt truly expressive of the deep, nuanced material in my head. I let ideas stand beside each other that I had spent years separating: psychology beside Goku, fatherhood beside systems design, strength training beside the problem of being fully seen.

Reading those editions back energized me. I felt alive again.

The energy came from letting the parts reach the page together. Parallax gave me evidence that expression did not require compression. The system built during naptimes gave the same range an operating form: health-performance experience, design, writing, voice translation, and AI orchestration working as parts of one practice.

The same pattern sits under the work I do now. I help people pull the valuable thing out of their head, give it shape, and make it usable by someone else. I know the distance between having something real and making it legible because I spent years trying to shorten myself instead of translating what was there.

The system is working. Market adoption and client-performance proof remain unproven, so this story ends before the victory lap. The honest turn is smaller and more useful.

The thing I treated as a failure to choose has become the way I translate difficult ideas into useful form. Eighteen years with people taught me that transformation depends on translation. Design, writing, and AI expanded what I could translate. Together, those fields became the method.

Anyone who has spent years treating their own range as a defect may recognize this pressure. Different interests can look scattered from the outside even when each one is doing a necessary job. The demand to choose one thing can hide the way those parts compound.

Range becomes an advantage when the connection reaches a form another person can use. That is why the last 10% matters. It is the point where private coherence becomes public work.

This is a provisional story, written from the middle. I still know the 90% pattern. I am learning, in public, to cross the last 10 anyway.

That is the founder story I can tell honestly right now: a man who spent his life getting almost all the way there, learning in public to let the whole range be seen.
