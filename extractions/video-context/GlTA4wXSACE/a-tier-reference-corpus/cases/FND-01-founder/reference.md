## Reference Asset

# I kept trying to become one thing

I built 90-plus AI agents during naptimes.

That sentence sounds like a clean founder line now. It did not feel clean while I was living it. It felt like one more interest I would eventually have to explain.

Strength coaching. Psychology. Design. Writing. Anime. Spirituality. AI systems.

For most of my life, range looked like evidence that I had failed to choose.

I am half Black and half Filipino. I learned early what it feels like to be legible in pieces and harder to place as a whole. Later, business advice gave that same pressure a respectable name: focus.

Pick one lane. Make yourself easy to understand. Save the rest for hobbies.

The pick-one-thing advice is a cage with better lighting.

I still tried to live inside it.

I spent 18 years coaching strength, conditioning, and behavior change. More than 1,000 people taught me the same lesson in different bodies: knowing what to do and being able to do it are not the same thing. Transformation lives in the translation between them.

Then COVID pushed me into design. Design pushed me into prompts. Prompts pushed me into systems. Somewhere in there, while my son slept, I started building an AI operating system that could hold expert methods, remember decisions, and turn a loose thought into finished work.

None of those moves replaced the one before it.

The coaching taught me to watch behavior. Design taught me to make complexity visible. Writing taught me to find the sentence underneath the explanation. AI gave me a way to connect all three without flattening them.

The thing I kept calling a lack of focus was the mechanism.

But there was another pattern underneath it. I could build almost anything to 90%. Sometimes 99%. The work usually stopped when it was ready to become visible.

That last 10% felt different. It was no longer design or research or preparation. It was the part where another person could see the whole thing and decide what they thought of it.

Parallax became the place where I started crossing that line.

I wrote about the ideas that did not fit neatly together. Psychology beside Goku. Fatherhood beside systems design. Strength training beside the problem of being fully seen. When I read those editions back, I felt more alive. Not because I had finally found one topic. Because I had stopped removing pieces of myself before I reached the page.

That changed what I think a founder story is for.

It is not a polished explanation for why the business exists. It is the pattern that explains why this person could only have built this business in this way.

My work helps people pull the valuable thing out of their head, give it shape, and make it usable by someone else. I know that problem because I spent years trying to compress myself before I learned how to translate myself.

The system built in naptimes is real. So are the 18 years in the room. So is the page that made the parts speak to each other.

The last 10% is no longer where the work stops.

It is where the work finally becomes mine.
