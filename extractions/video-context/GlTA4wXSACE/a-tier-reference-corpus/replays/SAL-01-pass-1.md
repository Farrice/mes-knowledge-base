# You have a reader. We find the reader.

Parallax ICP Intelligence gives writers, consultants, founders, and mission operators a specific reader, the language that reader already uses, and a publishing plan built around that person.

Your work can be sharp and still arrive blurry.

In conversation, the fit can feel obvious because you hear the questions, adjust the words, and notice what lands. A page has none of that help. The language has to create recognition before your nuance has earned attention.

The homepage explains the mission. The newsletter shows how you think. The offer lists the work. A new reader still has to guess whether any of it was made for someone like them.

That guess gets expensive in small ways. One headline carries five audiences. A good idea needs three paragraphs of setup. The publication grows, but its center stays hard to name. You keep making stronger work while the reader remains a demographic, a job title, or a line in a strategy deck.

There is a real person on the other side of the page. Parallax finds the language that helps that person recognize themselves.

## The work starts with the reader

Every Parallax engagement begins with one free 20-minute ICP Tell.

We use that conversation to locate the people already implied by your work, the problem they can name, and the words they use when the polished language falls away. The paid engagement turns that material into an ICP document and a practical publishing system.

The output is meant to be used. It shapes the homepage, the hooks, the calendar, the voice, and the path from a reader’s first moment of recognition to a clear offer.

## One proof fragment

HVC’s actual March 2026 homepage headline was:

> “Parents and teachers working together to create courageous hearts in children.”

The mission was present. The reader was harder to see. Its recorded five-second test score was 1/3, and its SB7 score was 31/70.

Farrice’s documented rewrite was:

> “You want your child to be strong enough to face the world — and open enough to come home when it hurts.”

The associated subhead and CTA moved the surface from an organization describing its mission to a parent recognizing the stakes in plain language.

This is a copy and recognition example. No sales result, conversion lift, or case-study outcome is being claimed. Public use still requires Javier’s approval, and the proof spread needs visual production.

Two other real artifacts are available for that future proof spread: the Grace City Blueprint transit map with its five-rung revenue ladder, and the Family Conversation Card with its newsletter variant. They also require pre-publication client approval and visual production.
