# The six rungs between an ingredient study and a product claim

A supplement-evidence map had 475 zeros in it.

Then its operator ran a better query. At least one real randomized controlled trial surfaced for 277 of those ingredient-goal pairings.

That number is useful for one narrow reason: a zero can describe the search, not just the science. It is not an industry error rate. It comes from the operator’s first-party account, a public dataset, and a site caveat. The dataset contains 2,156 cleaned pairings, while the published CSV is still the pre-correction version.

“No trial exists” and “this search did not find a trial” are different findings. Naming differences, outcome language, and dataset coverage can create a search gap that looks like a research gap.

That same discipline belongs on the product page.

A common evidence section starts with a true sentence such as “this ingredient has been studied” and ends beside a much bigger claim about the finished formula. The citation may be real. The leap may still be too wide.

I use six rungs to see where the proof actually stops.

## 1. The compound changes something in a cell or animal model

This is a mechanistic signal. It can help explain why an ingredient is worth studying.

It does not establish that a person will experience the benefit named on the product page. The model, conditions, and outcome are still far from the buyer and the bottle.

## 2. The ingredient has a research literature

Now there are studies, reviews, or a broader body of work around the ingredient.

That matters. But “researched” is only an inventory statement. The literature may cover a different goal, a different form, a different group of people, or an amount the product does not contain.

A long bibliography can still sit on rung 2.

## 3. The ingredient has evidence for the specific goal being claimed

This is where relevance becomes visible.

Take the exact promise from the page. Then ask whether the research measured that goal, in terms close enough to support the words being used.

A real paper can be cited accurately and still fail this test. Evidence for one outcome does not quietly travel to another because the ingredient name stayed the same.

## 4. The studied dose matches the product dose

The study and the serving now need to meet.

If the research used a materially different amount, the paper tells you something about the ingredient under the studied conditions. It does not automatically establish the product’s claim at the amount inside the formula.

This is a claim-matching check, not consumer dosing advice.

## 5. The studied form and population match the product and buyer

Form matters. So does who was studied.

Ask which form of the ingredient appeared in the research and which population produced the result. Then compare both with the product and the people the page addresses.

A mismatch does not automatically make the evidence worthless. It sets a boundary on how confidently the result can be carried over.

## 6. The finished formula itself has been tested

This is the closest rung to the actual thing being sold.

Research on separate ingredients does not, by itself, demonstrate that the combined formula produces the claimed result. Testing closer to the finished product can narrow that gap.

That kind of evidence generation is an emerging differentiator. I would grade that `LIKELY`, not treat it as a universal legal requirement.

## Why the ladder matters

A 2022 JAMA Network Open analysis looked at 30 immune-support supplements sold on Amazon. It found inaccurate labels in 17 and no third-party seals.

That study does not give us permission to generalize across every supplement or every product page. It does force a useful separation: the ingredient literature, the label, the claim, and the finished formula are not interchangeable.

FTC health-claim guidance expects competent and reliable scientific evidence and asks whether the research is relevant to the specific claim. It does not universally require a fixed number of trials.

The six rungs are my operational synthesis of that evidence-matching problem. They are not an FTC rule, legal advice, or a legal conclusion.

## Put one product page through the six-rung check

Copy the main claim exactly as written. List the evidence cited beside it. Then mark the highest rung each source actually supports.

Do not average the gaps away.

If five papers reach rung 2, the page does not get rung 5 by volume. If the search returns zero, record whether that is a confirmed research gap or an unresolved search gap. If the formula reaches rung 6, say what was tested, in whom, and against which outcome.

Most pages do not need a taller pile of citations. They need a clean line showing where ingredient evidence ends and product evidence begins.

Compound. Ingredient. Goal. Dose. Form and population. Finished formula.

Six rungs. The claim should never stand higher than the evidence beneath it.

# Story Deployment Receipt

## Deployment decision

- **Narrative decision:** `STORY FRAGMENT`
- **Primary communication job:** Explanation and recall.
- **Audience relationship:** Supplement-brand founder, growth lead, or claims operator with four to five minutes of granted attention.
- **Selected mechanic:** One bounded, sourced real moment used as a concrete entry.
- **Story fragment used:** The evidence-map operator’s 277-of-475 re-query.
- **Production owner:** `narrative-script-optimization-audit`.
- **Voice:** Farrice Cain, BLEND mode, restrained educational register.

## Why this dosage

Health and regulatory evidence must remain the spine. The re-query gives the reader one concrete example of why a search result and a state of scientific evidence cannot be treated as the same thing. It supports comprehension without acting as proof for the six-rung framework or the supplement category.

## Rejected dosage alternatives

- **FULL STORY:** Rejected because the supplied material contains no supported protagonist arc with a want, obstacle, change, and located turn. Expanding the operator’s self-audit into a dramatic narrative would require invented chronology, motive, emotion, or scene detail.
- **NO STORY:** Rejected because a wholly procedural explainer would be truthful but lose the brief’s strongest recall aid. The bounded re-query demonstrates the research-gap versus search-gap distinction without displacing the evidence.

## Evidence sufficiency

The fact packet was sufficient for a direct educational article and one truthful fragment. It supplied the complete six-rung framework, bounded evidence-map example, dataset boundary, dated JAMA finding, FTC constraint, and required uncertainty labels.

It was not sufficient for a full story. No missing narrative texture was filled.

## Diagnosis and protected spine

**Diagnosis:** The main communication risk was evidence-layer collapse: moving from “an ingredient has research” to “this finished product supports this claim” without showing the intervening steps.

**Protected spine:**

- All six rungs remain present and in their supplied order.
- The 277/475 figure remains a bounded operator self-audit, not an industry statistic.
- The public dataset contains 2,156 cleaned pairings.
- The published CSV is identified as the pre-correction version.
- Research gaps remain distinct from search gaps.
- The JAMA Network Open analysis retains its 2022 date, 30-product scope, 17 inaccurate labels, and absence of third-party seals.
- FTC guidance is not converted into a fixed-trial rule.
- Finished-formula evidence as a differentiator remains graded `LIKELY`.
- The framework is identified as operational synthesis, not an FTC rule or legal conclusion.

## Frame options considered

1. **Evidence does not climb automatically:** Each rung marks a distinct transfer from mechanism to finished-product proof.
2. **A zero may describe the search:** The evidence-map correction teaches the research-gap versus search-gap distinction.
3. **The citation and the claim can both be real while the match is weak:** Relevance, dose, form, population, and formula still require inspection.

**Selected frame:** Option 1, with Option 2 used only as the opening fragment. This keeps the six-rung diagnostic primary.

## Opening options considered

1. “A supplement-evidence map had 475 zeros in it.”
2. “A long bibliography can still sit on rung 2.”
3. “A product-page claim can stand several rungs above the research beneath it.”

**Selected opening:** Option 1. It is concrete, fully supplied, and immediately establishes the search-gap boundary.

## Factual boundary and uncertainty

- The 277/475 correction is supported in the brief as a first-party account plus public dataset and site caveat. It is not a measured category-wide error rate.
- A zero result can represent either absent research or a failed search caused by naming, outcome-language, or coverage limitations.
- The 2022 JAMA finding applies to the 30 Amazon immune-support supplements analyzed, not the entire supplement market.
- Evidence closer to the finished formula is treated as an emerging differentiator graded `LIKELY`, not a universal legal duty.
- No claim was made about a universally required number of trials.
- The original named sources were not independently reopened during this blind execution.

## Truth checks

- No dialogue, chronology, motives, emotions, sensory details, outcomes, or causal claims were invented.
- No consumer dosing advice, disease claim, legal conclusion, or unsupported interaction or absorption claim was added.
- The ladder metaphor is a writing frame, not evidence.
- The six-rung diagnostic is explicitly labeled operational synthesis.
- The evidence fragment supports recall and does not serve as proof.
