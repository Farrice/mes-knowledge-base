## The last 10%

I read my own Parallax editions back and felt alive again.

For the first time, a writing space held the deep, nuanced material that had been living in my head. Not a cleaner little slice. Not the version trimmed down so somebody could place me faster. The whole mix was there: strength training, psychology, anime, spirituality, fatherhood, writing, AI systems.

Reading it energized me. The page could hold the range I had spent years treating like a defect.

I am half Black and half Filipino. I was bullied for being different and faster. For years, I treated visible range like one more thing that made belonging harder. A clean label had an obvious appeal. One lane. One answer when somebody asks what you do.

“The pick-one-thing advice is a cage with better lighting.”

Meanwhile, my work kept crossing the lines.

A strength program, a behavior-change plan, an anime transformation arc, a spiritual practice, a piece of writing, an AI system. I see the same questions moving through all of them. What changes? What gets in the way? What makes the change hold? How do you turn what lives in somebody’s head into something another person can actually use?

For me, those connections are the work.

I have spent 18 years in strength, conditioning, and behavior change, with 1,000+ client transformations. During COVID, I taught myself design. Then prompt engineering. At 37, I am Jennifer’s husband and JJ’s stay-at-home dad. The working 90+-agent AI orchestration system I built solo came together in naptimes.

That list can sound like a person refusing to choose.

I see a different pattern. Health-performance work gives me years of watching change up close. Design gives an idea form. Writing forces nuance into words a stranger can follow. Voice translation keeps expertise from losing the person who earned it. AI orchestration lets specialist parts work toward one outcome.

The range has receipts. It also has a job.

When a problem crosses domains, I have more than one way into it. I can see where behavior fights information, where good thinking gets flattened by bad language, where a tool ignores the human being who has to use it. One field catches what another one misses.

That is the advantage.

But range was never my only pattern.

I have a name for the other one: I build work to 90%, sometimes 99%, and stop before full visibility. The knowledge is there. The system works. The words exist. The last stretch stays unshipped.

I spent years with real range and almost-finished proof of it.

Parallax changed the shape of that problem. It became the first writing space where I felt truly expressive of the deep, nuanced material in my head. Then I read my own editions back. They energized me. I felt alive again.

The range already had a pattern. Shipping was the missing piece.

Now I am learning, in public, to ship the last 10%.

Maybe your range makes perfect sense inside your head and looks unruly on paper. Maybe you have been given kind, practical advice to simplify yourself until other people can repeat you. Maybe you keep waiting for all the pieces to resemble one conventional job before you let the work become visible.

I know the shape of that waiting. It looks productive from the inside because so much of the work is real.

Range still needs discipline. It needs practice deep enough to create judgment. Honest limits. A place for the fields to work together. Then it needs the point where my own pattern kept stopping: finished work that other people can actually see.

My range is not proof that every interest belongs in every project. The operating advantage comes from knowing which lens the problem needs, then bringing the others in only when they make the outcome clearer, more human, or more useful.

That is the work I am building now. Health performance, design, writing, voice translation, and a working AI orchestration system. Different tools, used by one person with intent.

Parallax gave the whole range somewhere to land. The page could hold the coach, the designer, the writer, the father, and the systems builder without asking one of them to leave.

I am still learning to ship the last 10%.

The cage still has better lighting. I am taking the whole range outside.

## Owner Execution Record

### Diagnosis

No draft was supplied, so the owner diagnosed the fact packet rather than rewriting an existing manuscript.

The main failure risk was a résumé-shaped manifesto: a list of domains and credentials that would make Farrice look accomplished without showing why the range matters. The second risk was abstract self-acceptance language that would claim permission for the reader without earning it.

The buried value was the relationship between two supported facts: Farrice’s range already had working proof, while his visibility repeatedly stopped at 90–99%. Parallax supplied the turn that connected range, expression, and public completion.

### Protected Spine

- Visible range made belonging harder for years.
- Farrice’s cross-domain practice is sincere rather than decorative.
- His range has grounded proof in health performance, design, writing, voice translation, and a working AI orchestration system.
- The 90+-agent system is proof of building and orchestration only, not market adoption or client performance.
- His named pattern is stopping at 90–99% before full visibility.
- Parallax was the first writing space where he felt fully expressive of the deep material in his head.
- Reading those editions energized him and made him feel alive again.
- The public-safe transformation remains in progress: learning to ship the last 10%.

### Frame Options

1. **The last 10%:** Farrice’s range already worked; public completion was the unresolved edge.
2. **The cage with better lighting:** Conventional legibility can reward people for reducing themselves.
3. **Range as an operating advantage:** Separate disciplines become valuable when they sharpen one another against real work.

**Selected frame:** The last 10%. It carries the supported transformation, prevents the piece from becoming a credential stack, and mirrors the reader’s possible gap between real ability and visible work.

### Opening Options

1. **Selected:** “I read my own Parallax editions back and felt alive again.”
2. “I have a habit of stopping when the work is 90–99% done.”
3. “The pick-one-thing advice is a cage with better lighting.”

The first opening was selected because it begins at the supported turn, opens low-status, and creates an immediate reason to keep reading without manufacturing stakes.
