## Treatment Output

# AI Creative Practice Design — Claim-Bounded Content Brief

## Project Ownership Lock

- **Desired change:** Enable a content lead to turn a supplied research packet into an inspectable, claim-bounded brief that a named human reviewer can approve or reject efficiently.
- **Smallest viable audience:** Five content leads at funded health-performance brands with a human claims reviewer. Not for creators without a claims owner or teams seeking autonomous medical advice.
- **Human outcome owner:** The brand’s named content lead; the named claims reviewer owns claim admissibility.
- **Human-only decisions:** audience, admissible claims, evidence sufficiency, public promise, taste, release, and stop/scale authority.
- **AI boundary:** AI may organize and draft from supplied sources. It may not select the project objective, invent evidence, make medical claims, approve itself, publish, contact anyone, or spend.
- **Publishing boundary:** Approval-before-public. Rung three is prepared, not executed.

## System Gap Map

| Element | Evidence state | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Current system | UNCONFIRMED | Research, content, and claims review appear to operate as separate handoffs with no shared evidence-state brief. |
| Underserved audience | UNTESTED | Content leads may need a faster path that preserves rather than bypasses senior judgment. |
| Repeated friction | PROVIDED, NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED | Dense sources are expensive to translate; reviewers re-check provenance and wording. |
| One leverage point | HYPOTHESIS | A compact brief that binds every candidate statement to source, evidence class, use boundary, and reviewer decision. |

## Proud Artifact Spec

- **Artifact:** One-page Claim Boundary Brief plus source-linked appendix and change log.
- **Moment of use:** A content lead prepares a draft; the claims reviewer approves, narrows, or rejects each statement.
- **Minimum spec:** desired audience/change; source list; `VERIFIED/LIKELY/UNCONFIRMED/UNTESTED` labels; permitted language; prohibited extrapolation; reviewer decision column; version history.
- **Proof required:** A reviewer can trace every candidate statement to a supplied source and complete the review without reconstructing the evidence chain.
- **Proud threshold:** The human owner can put their name on the brief because its claims are inspectable and its uncertainty is visible. It is not positioned as medical advice.

## Bounded AI Task Packets

### Packet 1 — Source Sorting

- **Input/source truth:** supplied source packet only.
- **Output/acceptance:** source inventory with title, date, type, relevance, and missing-access flag; every entry resolves locally or is marked unavailable.
- **Human judgment retained:** source admissibility and hierarchy.
- **Prohibited/stop:** no web supplementation, invented metadata, or claim drafting; stop on missing source access.

### Packet 2 — Evidence-State Extraction

- **Input/source truth:** accepted source inventory.
- **Output/acceptance:** candidate statements with exact source pointer, evidence state, limitations, and contradiction notes; zero unsupported statements.
- **Human judgment retained:** claim admissibility and interpretation.
- **Prohibited/stop:** no causal upgrade, clinical advice, or confidence inflation; stop on ambiguous attribution.

### Packet 3 — Brief Variants and QA

- **Input/source truth:** human-approved candidate statements and the fixed brief schema.
- **Output/acceptance:** two structural variants plus deterministic missing-source/unsupported-claim checks and a change log.
- **Human judgment retained:** final wording, taste, promise, approval, and release.
- **Prohibited/stop:** no new claims, audience expansion, publishing, or brand-promise change.
