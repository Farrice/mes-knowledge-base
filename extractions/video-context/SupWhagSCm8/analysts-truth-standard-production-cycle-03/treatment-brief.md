## Decision

Keep the Lead-to-Proposal Proof Sprint, its $1,500 price, bounded delivery, and
human-approval boundary unchanged through the first market test. The 4.33 to
4.30 tournament margin is too small to treat as proof of superiority. The
winner remains the best working hypothesis because it reaches the proposal
bottleneck, has a bounded demonstration, and already has a complete local test
surface.

Prepare the existing 30-message test. Do not send without separate approval.
Record five outcomes: sent, replies, calls, sold, and collected.

## Read The Result Without Moving Every Variable

| Observed result | What it most directly tests | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| No replies after the approved send set | List, trigger, or opening message | Repair targeting or message before changing the offer |
| Replies but no qualified calls | Problem recognition or call invitation | Repair the conversation bridge |
| Qualified calls but no deposits | Buyer fit, offer, price, or proof burden | Inspect call objections; change one supported variable |
| One $750 deposit collected | Exact-offer payment proof | Continue the primary and preserve the winning variables |
| Twenty qualified conversations and zero deposits | Current offer hypothesis failed its stated gate | Reopen the tournament; keep the supplement adapter parked unless its own proof improves |

## Evidence And Alternatives

The source ledger supports category activity, related buyer budgets, listed
supplier prices, and a buildable five-day demonstration. It does not prove
Farrice's exact offer will sell. Lead-response and booking remains a credible
alternative because it is easier to demonstrate, while the weighted scores
remain judgment calls rather than measured buyer preferences.

The strongest alternative explanation for failure is a bad list or weak
message, not necessarily the offer. The outcome table keeps those causes
from being collapsed into one verdict.

## What Changes

The baseline chooses the next hypothesis. This pass makes the next evidence
interpretable. It freezes approved variables, separates channel failure from
offer failure, and names the payment event and reversal threshold before any
external action occurs.
