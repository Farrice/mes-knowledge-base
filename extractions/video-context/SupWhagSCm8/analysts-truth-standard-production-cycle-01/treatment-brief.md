# Content-Ideation Brief: Three Words Are A Readiness Test

## The Decision

Develop one LinkedIn post around this concept:

> A three-word prompt tests whether the system is ready. If it fails, inspect
> the six decisions the prompt
> assumes have already been made: goal, context, permissions, boundaries,
> verification, and accountability.

Do not publish from the August 13 signal alone. Refresh the audience signal
first because the evidence is historical and comes from other creators' rooms.

## Why This Concept Earns Development

- **Proper TAM, LIKELY:** AI-enabled operators and expert-led brand founders
  recognize shortcut advice but are responsible for the operating layer that
  makes it work. The archived source does not verify that this is Farrice's
  current audience mix.
- **Specific psychology, VERIFIED in the sampled comments:** readers did not
  reject the three-word instruction; they supplied what it silently assumed.
  The tension is shortcut appeal versus implementation responsibility.
- **Unique angle, INFERENCE:** “context matters” is familiar. Treating a short
  prompt as a readiness check for six missing decisions turns the observation into
  an inspectable readiness test. The six-part list compiles language found in
  the sampled comments; it is not a validated universal taxonomy.

## Source Signal And Restraint

The August 13 brief reports that four of the five most-reacted sampled comments
under a 241-reaction AI-agent post named missing substrate such as context,
goals, permissions, boundaries, verification, or accountability. A separate
career thread contained the same visible-move-versus-underlying-support shape.

Plausible alternatives remain live: highly reacted comments may not represent
the whole room; the two creators had different audiences; the 553-comment post
may have used a question or comment mechanic; and none of the sampled people
were Farrice's audience. The evidence supports a content hypothesis, not a
claim that this framing will perform.

## Artifact Shape

Use a compact readiness-check post:

1. Open on the three-word prompt as the seductive visible move.
2. Reframe success as evidence that six prior decisions already exist.
3. Show the six decisions in one clean sequence.
4. Give one consequence: without them, autonomy becomes “chaos with better
   branding.”
5. Close with a readiness question: which of the six is actually missing?

The selected vessel is the readiness test. Preserve two defensible alternates
for later: a before/after teardown of the same task with and without substrate,
and a narrative built around the public correction “chaos with better
branding.”

## Smallest Useful Test

After refreshing the signal, show the concept card—not a finished post—to three
ICP-fit readers. Continue only if at least two can name a concrete operating
decision they would inspect next and no more than one reduces the idea to “add
more context.” If readers cannot distinguish the readiness test from generic
prompt advice, revise the mechanism or park the concept. A publish test remains
subject to Farrice's approval.
