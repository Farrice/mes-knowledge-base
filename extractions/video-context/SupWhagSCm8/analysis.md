## Seven Source Mechanics

1. **00:01:53–00:07:19 — Ad variations as strategic diversity.** Give the product, audience, platform, and campaign goal; require genuinely different headlines, hooks, emotional angles, CTAs, lengths, and formats. Follow with buyer-awareness, psychology, funnel-fit, and failure-condition analysis so selection is strategic rather than preferential.
2. **00:07:19–00:16:18 — Competitor messaging as a common comparison matrix.** Feed raw landing-page or ad copy; compare core value propositions, audience language cues, emotional territory, feature/outcome balance, and unclaimed gaps. Then identify crowded tropes and propose defensible positioning directions plus hero-copy starting points.
3. **00:16:18–00:22:10 — Calendar as campaign progression.** Give the brand, audience, objective, channels, and cadence; create a four-week progression rather than disconnected post ideas. Each entry needs platform, pillar, hook, format, and CTA, with one anchor asset adapted into channel-native derivatives.
4. **00:22:10–00:27:58 — Email sequence as one belief journey.** Supply the product, entry event, sequence objective, audience state, and voice; generate the sequence in one context so pacing, education, trust, objections, and conversion pressure develop coherently. Alternate angles are rewrites of the journey, not isolated subject-line swaps.
5. **00:27:58–00:32:25 — SEO brief as intent before outline.** Give the target query, audience, business objective, product context, and desired action. Diagnose search intent before building headings, questions, differentiation, proof needs, and CTA direction.
6. **00:32:25–00:37:22 — Voice blueprint as evidence-derived rules.** Use several successful real examples across formats. Extract personality, sentence rhythm, vocabulary, punctuation, channel adaptations, and explicit do/don't rules; validate the blueprint by transforming neutral corporate copy.
7. **00:37:22–00:42:00 — Campaign analysis as decision, not narration.** Supply spend, impressions, clicks, conversions, and revenue. Diagnose how CTR, CPC, CPA, and ROAS interact; separate observations from likely drivers; propose an ordered reallocation and measurement sequence rather than restating metrics.

## Common Operating Spine

The source's recurring brief has four explicit inputs: target audience, business objective, tone/voice, and required output structure. Its strongest additional move is the follow-up: ask for psychology, search intent, performance drivers, or strategic conditions after the artifact so the marketer can make a decision instead of copying text blindly.

Antigravity strengthens that spine with five missing controls:

1. Evidence provenance and uncertainty states.
2. One specialist owner for what good looks like.
3. Claim and causal restraint when data cannot support a conclusion.
4. A next-test design with a measurable signal.
5. Voice and permission boundaries before public or paid action.

## What This Source Should Become

This source should become a bounded conductor workflow inside the existing Oren one-person-marketer system. It should not become a Mikey persona, seven duplicate expert skills, a new universal marketing control plane, or an automatic routing rule.
