## Output Produced

### Library Unit Card

- **Working claim:** Treat a recovery score as a prompt to inspect context, not as a standalone verdict.
- **Audience problem:** Health/performance users may overreact to a single score without checking the behaviors and conditions around it.
- **Mechanism:** A score compresses selected signals; context restores the information the number cannot carry by itself.
- **Proof/status:** `UNCONFIRMED` for external publication. Requires device-specific documentation and appropriate health/performance sources.
- **Story/example:** A low score after an unusually hard session may require a different interpretation from the same score after several nights of poor sleep. Demonstration example only.
- **Reusable blocks:** "prompt, not verdict"; context checklist; device-data limitation; decision boundary.
- **Permissions:** Internal demonstration only. No external publishing.
- **Open risk:** Could be mistaken for medical guidance or a universal claim across devices.

### Primary Format Decision

- **Format:** Atomic essay.
- **Job:** Preserve nuance while practicing headline, hook, structure, rhythm, and skimmability.
- **Why not a tweet first:** The short contrast is memorable, but publishing it alone would overcompress the health caveat.
- **Why not a carousel first:** The idea is not yet a verified list/process, and the source cannot certify visual craft.
- **Reach expectation:** `UNTESTED`.

### Atomic Essay Draft — Internal Only

#### Your Recovery Score Is a Question, Not a Verdict

A recovery score can tell you that something changed.

It cannot tell you the whole story.

The same low number might appear after a harder training block, a disrupted night, an unusually stressful day, or a change in how a device captured its inputs. Those situations do not automatically mean the same thing.

So use the score to begin a check, not end one:

- What changed in sleep?
- What changed in training load?
- What changed in stress or routine?
- Is the pattern repeating, or is this one unusual reading?
- Does the device's own guidance support the conclusion you are drawing?

The useful skill is not obeying a number. It is combining a signal with context, then deciding whether you need rest, a training adjustment, more observation, or qualified guidance.

The score is data.

The decision still needs judgment.
