## Core Method

1. **Create the gap.** Break the pattern so the audience notices a meaningful contrast. The underlying move is relative, not absolute: the work must be different from what surrounds it.
2. **Make the gap matter.** Random eccentricity can win attention without winning preference. The difference must connect to a consequence the market values.
3. **Keep the audience guessing.** Leave a live question, implication, or next reveal. The open loop must promise substance rather than manufacture empty suspense.
4. **Do not be summarizable.** Layer the brand with real tensions, side interests, limitations, and imperfections. Over-polished one-note positioning becomes easy to classify and forget.
5. **Have the courage to make enemies.** State the behavior, assumption, or category norm the work rejects. Repulsion must be principled and aimed at ideas or patterns, not people or protected groups.

## Hidden Sequence

The five moves are not interchangeable. **Gap precedes meaning; meaning precedes intrigue; intrigue precedes depth; depth earns polarization.** Applying polarization first creates generic contrarianism. Applying open loops before meaningful difference creates tease without value. Applying the sequence after the multi-interest focus diagnostic turns a broad operator into a market-legible one without deleting the breadth.

## Operating Translation

For a focused health/performance offer:

- **Gap:** name the common category behavior the offer refuses, such as output without source receipts.
- **Meaning:** connect that refusal to claim risk, buyer trust, or wasted creative spend.
- **Open loop:** reveal the diagnostic finding before the full remediation architecture.
- **Unsummary:** preserve the real combination of research rigor, commercial judgment, visual craft, and systems thinking.
- **Enemy:** reject proofless growth theater and ungrounded health claims, not rival practitioners.

## Proof Gate

This method does not prove demand. A strategically interesting artifact still needs behavior evidence: response, use, a call, a sale, or collected payment. Treat attention as an intermediate signal and payment as the strongest commercial test.
