## Test purpose

This fixture tests whether the Dhar Transformational Content Factory changes behavior on a realistic creator idea while honoring a provenance stop.

It is a strategy test, not a finished Farrice script. The fixture contains no real Farrice scene, so the system must not invent one or write a first-person body.

### HEART Transformation Card

- **H, Honor the source:** The raw pattern is authorized. Specific personal details are not. `[PROVENANCE MISSING]`

### Viewer Transformation Contract

- Starting state: capable, busy, and increasingly ashamed that the work remains private.
- Private sentence: “If I keep organizing, I can call today productive without finding out whether the work is good.”
- Embodied conflict: begin another system or expose one imperfect proof to reality.
- Belief before: “My problem is that I lack discipline and need a better process.”
- Recognition hinge: the new system supplies certainty immediately; publishing supplies feedback but requires vulnerability.
- Belief after: “The next useful system may be one public proof and the feedback it earns.”
- After-state: understood, less ashamed, and able to choose one bounded act.
- Safe positive action: select one already-started asset and publish its smallest honest proof within 24 hours; opting out remains permitted.

### Packaging Integrity Packet

#### Three honest directions

1. **I Built Another System Instead of Shipping the Work**
   - Visual tension: a finished planning surface beside an unpublished asset.
   - Promise: expose the moment productive organization becomes protection.
   - Risk: requires a real, provenance-bearing scene.

2. **The Productivity Habit That Keeps Capable Creators Invisible**
   - Visual tension: polished tools surrounding one hidden piece of work.
   - Promise: reinterpret a respectable habit without calling the viewer lazy.
   - Risk: “keeps” must remain a pattern hypothesis, not a universal claim.

3. **Before You Build Another System, Publish This**
   - Visual tension: “new system” versus “one imperfect proof.”
   - Promise: provide a smaller decision the viewer can use today.
   - Risk: can become command-heavy unless recognition arrives first.

Selected direction: **I Built Another System Instead of Shipping the Work**. It remains provisional until the real scene is supplied.

Rejected high-click direction: **Your Productivity System Is Why You’re Failing**.

Rejection reason: it overstates causality, shames the viewer, and turns a context-dependent protective pattern into a universal accusation.

#### Tension-to-repayment covenant

- Literal promise: witness one moment when system-building displaced showing the work.
- Emotional promise: understand why that choice can feel responsible.
- Positive repayment: leave with a non-shaming interpretation and one bounded way to re-enter reality.

#### Thirty-second pitch

Capable creators often respond to feeling behind by building a better system. The system gives immediate order, but it can also postpone the moment another person sees the work. Through one real creator moment, this essay will show how respectable productivity can become protection, and how publishing one imperfect proof can create the feedback another private framework cannot.

#### Opening fulfillment

- **0–10 seconds:** `[Real scene required]` Show the unfinished asset and the exact system-building action that displaced it. On-screen contrast: “the work I could show” / “the system I built instead.”
- **10–30 seconds:** Name the private sentence: “If I keep organizing, I can call today productive without finding out whether the work is good.” Then state the consequential question: is the next system helping the work, or protecting it from reality?

Payoff:

- Literal: the opening’s work-versus-system choice is revisited with a different action.
- Perspective: the pattern may be self-protection, not proof of laziness.
- Action: publish one imperfect proof.
- Trust residue: “I was understood before I was asked to change.”

### Transformation Craft Packet

- Route: `educational`.
- Dependency: Kobi Brown Aha Engine.
- Belief before: “A better system will make me disciplined enough to publish.”
- Cognitive bridge: system-building offers an immediate closed loop: a choice, an organized surface, and a completed setup. Publishing opens an uncertain social loop. That makes the former emotionally easier even when the latter is strategically necessary.
- Belief after: “One imperfect proof can be the next system: action, feedback, adjustment.”
- Accuracy boundary: this is an interpretive model for the fixture, not a diagnosis or universal causal claim.
- Earned ending: return to the same work-versus-system choice and make the smallest visible choice differently.

### Audience Co-Greenlight Loop

Pre-production question:

> When you build another system instead of publishing, which part feels most true?
>
> A. I want the work to feel more complete.  
> B. Organizing feels safer than being judged.  
> C. I genuinely do not know what “small enough to publish” means.  
> D. This is not my pattern.

Decision rule:

- A wins: center the standard/completeness conflict.
- B wins: center the exposure/protection conflict.
- C wins: center the smallest-proof method.
- D or weak response wins: do not force the concept; test another premise.

Smallest complete production: one scene, one recognition hinge, one belief bridge, one public action.

Post-publication question: “Did this change what you chose to show this week?” Options: yes / not yet / no / prefer not to answer.

| Scoreboard | Signal | What it can prove | What it cannot prove |

| Discovery | Impressions, click-through rate, browse/search/direct source | Whether the package earned entry | Whether the viewer felt understood or acted |
