## Genius Patterns

### Pattern 1: Resistance as Navigation Compass
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Treats every creative block, procrastination, or avoidance as *information* — not failure
- **Executable Behavior**: When resistance appears, say "Thank you. This tells me I'm about to do something important." Then move the resistant task to the TOP of your priority list.
- **Deployment Context**: Starting any new project, creative work, difficult conversation, lifestyle change
- **Success Metric**: Resistance-triggering tasks are consistently executed first, not last

### Pattern 2: The Fear-Status-Affiliation Triad
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Decomposes all human motivation into exactly three forces — fear, status, affiliation — and diagnoses which one is driving any behavior
- **Executable Behavior**: When someone (including yourself) is stuck, ask: "Is this fear (of death/rejection)? Status (comparison/competition)? Or affiliation (fitting in/belonging)?" The answer determines the intervention.
- **Deployment Context**: Coaching, self-diagnosis, understanding audience behavior, relationship friction
- **Success Metric**: Root cause identified in under 60 seconds; intervention matched to actual driver

### Pattern 3: Problems vs Situations Triage
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Instantly classifies any obstacle as either a "problem" (has a solution you might not like) or a "situation" (must be accepted — no solution exists)
- **Executable Behavior**: For any stuck point: "Does this have a solution I just don't want to execute? → Problem. Is this like the laws of physics — unchangeable? → Situation." Problems get action plans. Situations get acceptance.
- **Deployment Context**: Decision-making, relationship conflicts, career crossroads, business strategy
- **Success Metric**: Zero energy wasted fighting situations; all problems have documented solutions (even uncomfortable ones)

### Pattern 4: But vs And Diagnostic Reframe
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Listens for the word "but" as a diagnostic signal that someone is using one fact to cancel another, then replaces with "and" to hold both truths simultaneously
- **Executable Behavior**: Scan any complaint, excuse, or internal narrative for "but." Replace with "and." Notice how the entire frame shifts — the second fact stops being an excuse and becomes a separate design constraint.
- **Deployment Context**: Content editing, coaching conversations, self-talk rewrites, sales copy, relationship communication
- **Success Metric**: "But" eliminated from problem statements; "and" holds contradictions without collapse

### Pattern 5: Smallest Viable Audience First
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Always starts with the smallest possible group that would make the work matter, then lets it grow organically
- **Executable Behavior**: Ask "What's the smallest group of people that if I made an impact on them, it would be enough?" Start there. One person. Three people. A household. Then ask "What's the smallest piece of art/work that would be worth it?"
- **Deployment Context**: Starting any new venture, content strategy, product design, community building
- **Success Metric**: First action is tiny, specific, and serves a named audience — not "everyone"

### Pattern 6: Pick Yourself Protocol
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Never waits for external authorization — acts as if he's already been chosen and then makes the work justify the choice
- **Executable Behavior**: Stop waiting for permission. Write the book and email it as a PDF to 20 people. Record the talk and put it on YouTube tomorrow. Start the blog under a pseudonym if needed. The four words: "Here, I made this."
- **Deployment Context**: Career transitions, creative launches, content publishing, entrepreneurship
- **Success Metric**: Time from "I want to do this" to "it's public" measured in days, not months

### Pattern 7: Intentional Design (Who/What)
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Before creating anything, locks two answers: "Who is this for?" (by name if possible) and "What is this for?" (what change does it create?)
- **Executable Behavior**: Every project starts with these two questions written down. If you can't answer them, you're floundering. If the answer is "everyone" — you're hiding.
- **Deployment Context**: Content creation, product design, business strategy, communication
- **Success Metric**: Both questions answered in writing before any production begins

### Pattern 8: Perfectionism as Hiding
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Reframes perfectionism NOT as quality pursuit but as fear-driven avoidance of shipping
- **Executable Behavior**: Define the spec. The spec is attainable. Once it's met, MERELY ship it. "The point of perfectionism is not to make it better. It's to keep you from shipping it." Distinguish: meeting spec (yes), luxury (irrelevant), zero-defect pursuit (trap).
- **Deployment Context**: Creative work, product launches, content publishing, business decisions
- **Success Metric**: Spec defined BEFORE work begins; shipping happens at spec, not at "perfect"

### Pattern 9: Consistency Over Authenticity
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Operates from a consistent professional identity rather than moment-to-moment emotional "authenticity"
- **Executable Behavior**: Define the role of "the best version of you." Show up as that role consistently, regardless of internal state. "If I were playing the role of the best version of me, what would that be like?" Authenticity is for your best friend. Everyone else wants consistency.
- **Deployment Context**: Personal brand, content creation, professional relationships, leadership
- **Success Metric**: Output quality doesn't fluctuate with mood; "bad days" don't reach the audience

### Pattern 10: The Generous Ruckus Frame
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Defines "making a ruckus" not as attention-seeking but as "work that matters for people who care"
- **Executable Behavior**: Strip the ego from creation. Don't do it for attention or likes. Do work that matters for a specific audience who cares. "Here I made this" is a gift, not a performance. Detach from outcome (the Buddhist "attachment" reframe).
- **Deployment Context**: Content strategy, personal brand, creative direction, professional standards
- **Success Metric**: Work evaluated by "did it serve?" not "did it perform?"

### Pattern 11: Where Do You Put the Tired
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Removes the expectation that important work should be effortless — instead teaches how to carry the inevitable fatigue
- **Executable Behavior**: Accept upfront: "I am going to get tired AND I need to be able to do both — the work and the tired." Like a marathon runner who doesn't eliminate tired but figures out where to put it. Don't optimize for painlessness. Optimize for endurance.
- **Deployment Context**: Long-term projects, lifestyle changes, creative endurance, business building
- **Success Metric**: Fatigue is anticipated and planned for, not treated as failure signal

### Pattern 12: Name It to Defuse It
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Forces explicit naming of fear, status-seeking, and irrational behavior to break its power
- **Executable Behavior**: Say out loud: "I am buying $400 sneakers because I am afraid of being left out." "I am procrastinating because I'm terrified of rejection." "I am comparing my kitchen to my neighbor's because of status anxiety." Named things lose power.
- **Deployment Context**: Self-coaching, consumer behavior analysis, copy that connects, audience empathy
- **Success Metric**: Hidden motivations articulated in plain language; behavior changes follow

### Pattern 13: "We Become What We Do"
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Inverts the typical "become then do" sequence — insists that identity follows action, not the reverse
- **Executable Behavior**: Don't wait to "become" a writer before writing. Write, and you become a writer. Want to be truthful? Start telling the truth. Want to be generous? Start giving. Action creates identity, not the other way around.
- **Deployment Context**: Identity shifts, career transitions, habit formation, personal development
- **Success Metric**: Identity statements follow completed actions, not precede them

### Pattern 14: The Lipstick-to-Death Cascade
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Maps the irrational fear chain: "I will make a mistake → cause shame → be ostracized → be alone → die" — completed in under 2 seconds
- **Executable Behavior**: When paralyzed, trace the fear chain. Name each step. Notice the absurdity of "lipstick on teeth → death." The chain is real but the conclusion is comic. This breaks the paralysis.
- **Deployment Context**: Pre-launch anxiety, public speaking, creative shipping, difficult conversations
- **Success Metric**: Fear chain traced and named; absurdity recognized; action taken anyway

## Hidden Knowledge

### Tacit Knowledge 1: The Architect vs Victim Story
We wake up every morning and the first noise in our head tells us which story we're in. The architect's story says "I can change the circumstances." The victim's story says "I can't change them." Both stories feel equally true. The choice between them determines everything.

**Deploy**: Before any strategic planning, name which story you're operating from. Architect mode = design the circumstances. Victim mode = exit that story first.

### Tacit Knowledge 2: "If No One Comes, No One Will Know That No One Came"
The downside of trying something new is almost always imagined. His mother's museum event story: she feared no one would come (5,000 showed up), but even if they hadn't, the failure would have been invisible. Most failures are invisible. We catastrophize visibility.

**Deploy**: Before any launch, ask: "If this fails, who will actually know?" Usually: almost no one. This neutralizes the failure catastrophe.

### Tacit Knowledge 3: The Seed-in-a-Bag Problem
Ideas and impulses kept inside you don't grow. "Most seeds don't bloom in a bag, in the cold, in a shed somewhere." They need to be planted in the open. The nagging ache of an unexpressed idea is a seed dying in a bag.

**Deploy**: Any idea that's been nagging for more than 30 days must be planted — even microscopically. Email it to someone. Write 200 words. Draft the first page. Exposure to air = growth.

### Tacit Knowledge 4: The System Wants You to Hide
High school taught us that the safest people weren't the ones who picked themselves — they were the ones hiding in uniforms, cliques, attitudes, mobs. The consumer system needs you hiding because hiding people buy armor (status goods, comfort goods). You're not failing to act — the system is working perfectly to keep you passive.

**Deploy**: When you can't seem to start, ask: "Am I choosing safety or am I choosing it because the system trained me to?" Name the system. Then defy it.

### Tacit Knowledge 5: The Safe Hiding Place Trap
"Congratulations. You've built a perfect place to hide." Every excuse structure — "I submitted to 20 publishers and they all turned me down," "I'm waiting for TED to call," "I just need 300K followers first" — is a carefully constructed hiding place. It LOOKS like effort. It IS avoidance.

**Deploy**: Audit your excuse structures. Are they actually barriers or carefully maintained hiding spots? If removing the excuse would mean you have to ship, it was a hiding spot.

### Tacit Knowledge 6: Action-First Identity Formation
"We become what we do. We don't do what we become." This inverts every self-help framework that says "believe it first." Godin says: act first, and the identity follows. Want to be brave? Do one brave thing. Want to be generous? Give one gift. The sequence is always action → identity, never identity → action.

**Deploy**: Replace "I need to feel ready" with one micro-action that proves the identity. The feeling follows the doing.

### Tacit Knowledge 7: The Cohort Multiplier
Everything works better with a truth-telling cohort. One to five people who hold each other accountable. This isn't optional support — it's structural infrastructure. "It will work every single time" if you find people who tell each other the truth and challenge each other.

**Deploy**: Before starting any major initiative, recruit 1-5 accountability partners. Share the podcast/book/idea. Say "Hold me accountable." The cohort is the infrastructure.

### Tacit Knowledge 8: The Dishwasher Truth
Most relationship friction (personal and professional) has a known solution — you just don't want the 10 minutes of discomfort to execute it. Your spouse loads the dishwasher wrong? You know the fix: have a 10-minute respectful conversation. You avoid it because the solution is uncomfortable, not because it's unknown.

**Deploy**: For any interpersonal friction, ask: "Do I know what would fix this?" If yes, the only question is: "Am I willing to endure 10 minutes of discomfort?" Usually the answer is "I should be."

### Tacit Knowledge 9: The Role of the Best Version
The mock turtleneck story. When Godin walks to work, he's "seth godin, small s, small g." When he puts on the mock, he's "the person who's written 10,000 blog posts and 20 bestsellers." The role is intentional. The role is service. The role is not fake — it's the best version made consistent.

**Deploy**: Define your professional role explicitly. What is the best version of you at work? Name it. Then ask every morning: "If I were playing this role today, what would I do?" Inhabit the role. Don't wait to feel it.
