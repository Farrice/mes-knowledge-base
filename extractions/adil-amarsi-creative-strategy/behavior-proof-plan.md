## Duplicate-System Baseline

The existing Luke Iha Creative Strategy Brief requires `[Target Market]` as Input 2. It then develops that supplied market into audience psychographics, positioning, category disruption, hooks, proof requirements, and an asset brief.

The existing contract does not independently compare candidate audiences or markets. It has no product-truth evidence ledger, current-strategy fingerprint, adjacent-community lane, geography/language readiness gate, rejected-hypothesis record, or single-variable market-learning test. It can sharpen a strategy inside a supplied target; it cannot decide whether the supplied target should change.

Before any Phase 5 mutation, run the frozen fixture through the existing prompt and preserve the output. If it already performs the full evidence-bounded triage below, cancel the new workflow and add only the smallest missing gate.

## Expected After Behavior

Lane | Expected decision | Evidence boundary
---|---|---
Product/use case | Select wheel-throwing ceramicists as a bounded test hypothesis | Twelve supplied review excerpts establish an account-level signal, not prevalence or demand
Adjacent distribution | `HOLD` pottery studios and creators | No channel access, permission, partnership, or native-community evidence
Language/geography | `HOLD` | No native-language VOC, demand, operating readiness, economics, or localization owner

The selected hypothesis changes the audience from home cooks to wheel-throwing ceramicists, the buying situation from standing in a kitchen to working at a pottery wheel, and the relevant product truths from generic cleanliness to split-front construction, tool pockets, and washable canvas.

The test holds product, price, offer, US geography, and initial media channel constant. It routes the selected hypothesis into the existing Luke Creative Strategy Brief. It does not produce hooks, ads, a concept grid, a persona deck, localization, or outreach.

## Whitespace Scope Gate

Scope | Evidence required | Fieldwell result
---|---|---
Account whitespace | The idea or use case is absent from the inspected brand assets | Supported: pottery is absent from the twelve inspected statics
Category whitespace | The idea is absent from a defined competitor sample | Unavailable
Market whitespace | Demand plus underserved supply is evidenced beyond the account and competitor sample | Unavailable

The output may say `account whitespace` or `candidate opportunity`. It may not say `market gap`, `underserved market`, `proven segment`, or `profit opportunity` from these inputs.

## Adversarial Tests

Test | Passing behavior
---|---

Destination test | The ceramicist hypothesis requires a materially different page frame from cooking
Proof-burden test | The use case requires the supplied pottery reviews and product-construction evidence
Learning test | The test asks whether pottery use merits further evidence, not which sentence gets more clicks
Label-swap test | Home cooks renamed food lovers remains cosmetic and fails
Translation test | English copy rendered in Spanish remains cosmetic without market, operations, economics, and cultural change
Format test | Story, meme, static, or UGC changes route to production owners and do not count as market-variable change

## Success Assertions

1. Classify all three baseline hooks as one strategy.
2. Label every fact `SUPPLIED`, `SOURCE-REPORTED`, `INFERRED`, `UNAVAILABLE`, or `PROHIBITED`.
3. Permit `NO EVIDENCE / HOLD`; never manufacture three positive opportunities.
4. Label pottery use as account whitespace or a candidate opportunity, not market demand.
5. Cite supplied product truths and review language in the selected hypothesis.
6. Preserve review language as individual evidence, not typical-results proof.
7. Name the changed variable, held constants, proof requirement, exclusions, and falsifier in the handoff.
8. Hold both adjacent distribution and localization lanes.
9. Design the smallest test without presenting it as executed.
10. End with explicit proof states for workflow behavior, runtime observation, market performance, and localization.

## Failure Assertions

Failure | Why it fails
---|---
The output merely improves the hooks | No new variable-triage behavior
Tone, format, image style, or direct translation is called strategic change | Cosmetic variation is misclassified
Twelve reviews become segment size or demand proof | Evidence is upgraded beyond its scope
The apron is described as designed for potters or universally non-bunching | Product intent or performance is invented
Spanish or Latin America is selected because the market is large | Demand, operations, economics, and cultural evidence are missing
Customer language, competitors, economics, permissions, or legal clearance are invented | Provenance gate fails
CTR, ROAS, CPA, conversion lift, or profit is predicted | No live event exists
A hook bank, ad slate, persona deck, localization package, or outreach plan is produced | The workflow absorbs another owner's function
File existence, prompt wiring, registry sync, or route discovery is called runtime proof | Structural proof is mislabeled

## Localization Hold Gate

A language/geography lane cannot become test-ready until it has target-market demand evidence, native-language VOC, native cultural review, translation and intellectual-property rights, product classification and claims review, privacy and platform review, fulfillment and returns readiness, duties and taxes, payments and currency, customer support, landed-margin economics, and a defensible acquisition ceiling.

Missing essentials force `OPPORTUNITY HYPOTHESIS: HOLD`. The workflow never presents itself as legal or regulatory clearance.

## Proof Ladder

Architecture approval proves a planned capability. Files and audits prove structure. Natural-language discovery proves routing. One detached Fieldwell pass proves runtime behavior on one bounded case. Repeated cases across distinct products can support behavioral-reliability evidence. Only a live controlled market event can support performance claims.
