## Executive Summary

Adil does not treat an angle as a sentence. He treats it as a coordinate system: product truth, buyer state, consequence, context, channel, and market. When a team is producing more words without learning anything new, his instinct is to change one of those coordinates first.

That instinct is the source's durable contribution. The current roster already knows how to research audiences, write briefs, create concepts, produce assets, and run client-acquisition demonstrations. It does not have one compact pre-brief gate that fingerprints the current strategy, distinguishes cosmetic from strategic variation, classifies the evidence scope, compares three opportunity lanes, and either selects one test hypothesis or returns `HOLD`.

The source therefore supports a **Profit-Finder Opportunity Scan** inside Luke Iha Creative Strategy. It does not support a second broad creative-strategy system.

## Genius Patterns

### 1. True-Variable Test

**Evidence:** `JOINT-SOURCE`, approximately 05:07–05:33 and 09:10–10:48. Adil frames strategy as finding a different part of the market; Matthew supplies the explicit cosmetic-rewrite anti-example.

**Executable behavior:** Fingerprint audience, triggering situation, desired outcome, product truth, channel/context, geography/language, offer, proof burden, and destination. Reject a variation when only wording, syntax, tone, headline formula, story format, or visual styling changes.

**Deploy when:** A team claims to have many angles but the work keeps speaking to the same person in the same situation.

**Success metric:** Every accepted hypothesis changes at least one strategic field, holds non-target fields constant, and creates a different falsifiable learning question.

**Disposition:** Core behavior for the new workflow.

## Hidden Knowledge

1. **An angle is a bundle of coordinates.** New phrasing does not create a new hypothesis when the audience, situation, channel, market, and proof burden stay fixed.

2. **The best opportunity often appears at an intersection.** A substantiated product property crossed with a neglected buyer situation is more defensible than freeform hook brainstorming.

3. **Distribution can perform differentiation.** The same product encountered inside an identity-native community may create a different competitive context, but adjacency still needs access and demand evidence.

4. **Specificity creates exposure as well as relevance.** A sharper segment can raise fit for one group while alienating another; segmentation and exclusions belong in strategy.

5. **Whitespace has three evidence scopes.** Absence from one account is account whitespace. Absence from a bounded competitor set is category whitespace. Market whitespace requires demand plus underserved supply evidence.

6. **Localization is operating design.** Language is only one component; fulfillment, support, rights, regulation, payments, economics, and native judgment determine whether the market is reachable.

7. **A strategist's value comes from interfaces.** Evidence, commercial choice, copy understanding, production handoff, and test design are more defensible together than any isolated asset skill.

8. **Client artifacts compress domain learning.** Analytics and prior creative provide faster account fluency than pretending niche familiarity.

9. **Live demonstration is both research and proof.** It reveals the account while making judgment observable, but it still does not establish market results.

10. **Anecdotes generate hypotheses, not causal laws.** The source's cases can inspire what to test; only evidence and a controlled event can establish what worked.

## Behavioral Signature Moves

1. **Fingerprint before ideation:** Record the current strategic coordinates before generating alternatives.

2. **Ask the unanswered-market question:** Search for the bounded absence before writing.

3. **Inspect product truth first:** Separate substantiated properties from assumptions and prohibited claims.

4. **Trace where else the buyer feels it:** Move from category pain to a specific triggering situation and consequence.

5. **Move the encounter environment:** Search communities, channels, and geographies only where an evidence path exists.

6. **Expose who the wedge excludes:** Name collateral, segmentation, and the stop condition.

7. **Select one or hold:** Compare lanes, reject weaker options, and allow `NO EVIDENCE / HOLD`.

8. **Lock the handoff:** Pass the changed variable, evidence, exclusions, proof burden, and falsifier rather than the whole transcript.

## Extracted Methodology

1. Record the current strategy fingerprint.
2. Classify every input as `SUPPLIED`, `SOURCE-REPORTED`, `INFERRED`, `UNAVAILABLE`, or `PROHIBITED`.
3. Diagnose whether proposed variations are cosmetic or strategic.
4. Generate at most one evidence-bounded hypothesis in each lane: product/use case, adjacent surface, and language/geography.
5. Label the whitespace scope as account, category, or market.
6. Compare evidence, upside, operational burden, proof burden, exclusions, and risk.
7. Select one test hypothesis or return `NO EVIDENCE / HOLD`.
8. Produce a compact handoff and smallest valid test; do not produce hooks, assets, localization, or outreach.

Steps 1–3 and the three source lanes are source-grounded. The evidence labels, whitespace scopes, hold state, selection matrix, and proof ladder are `OPERATIONAL-SYNTHESIS` added to keep the method honest and repeatable.
