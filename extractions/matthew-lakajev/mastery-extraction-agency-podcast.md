### Genius Patterns (new to this source)

#### 1. The Closed-Lost Campaign
- **What he does**: Monthly (minimum quarterly) offer campaign to *everyone the business has ever chatted to* — closed-lost deals, old prospects, past clients. Script: "Hey [name], we're looking to work with a couple more clients next month to solve [problem]. I remember we talked about this — are you still interested / do you still need help?" Origin story: his mate Jono at pre-COVID Zoom made President's Club purely by reopening year-old "no"s with "Hey, it's Jono from Zoom. You chatted to us about 6 months ago. I just want to know — where did we drop the ball?"
- **Why it works**: "The reason someone said no is not never — it's just not right now." People buy on compelling events; the ask *is* a manufactured compelling event (his dog-bed story: pee on the bed → purchase within 24 hours).
- **Execute**: Build the database (manually from calendar history if needed — "3 hours, do the hard work, there's no automated click thing"). Reach 500+ people. Monthly cadence. Client proof: Sydney client closed $45K in week one of running it.
- **Success Metric**: Immediate meetings from a list you already own. "If you've been in business 3-5 years and do this once a quarter, you will just immediately get meetings."

#### 2. The Opinion Ladder (never-pitch DM sequence)
- **What he does**: On any engagement signal (post like, profile view), open with an opinion ask about the thing they engaged with — never a pitch. Then ask their opinion on *their answer*. Three opinion asks total. Then offer something free ("I made a free 8-minute training that helped a client just like you — want me to send it?"). Then **stop talking for a week** ("they will think about you for a whole entire week"). Then invite: "I'm working with a few [niche] people next month on [thing] — interested?"
- **The exception**: If they raise their hand mid-ladder ("do you have a Sales Navigator course?"), capitalize immediately — speed to lead while the emotional charge is live.
- **Why it works**: "Every single salesperson pitches on the first or second message" — five exchanges with no ask makes you unclassifiable as a salesperson; the free asset beats everyone; the silence does the selling.
- **Success Metric**: Prospect self-converts (Candace asked to buy at opinion-ask #2; the furniture-SEO client's prospect flipped the conversation and booked the call himself).

#### 3. Quality-Time Prospecting (the anti-volume doctrine)
- **What he does**: Redefines volume: "Volume isn't how much you do, it's the amount of time you spend actually doing it." Five deeply personalized touches beat 100 generic ones. An hour writing 8 handwritten cards IS the hour of prospecting. Openly rejects Hormozi's 100-for-100: "the most garbage advice I've ever heard… I know people who've tried it and it destroys their soul."
- **The depth rule**: People suck at prospecting because they never go deep enough to get good — his own arc: heart pounding through early cold calls → 5-6 months at 2 hrs/day → 14 meetings booked in 80 minutes. "A lot of volume is masturbation for being insecure about not being good enough."
- **Success Metric**: Reply and meeting rate per hour spent, not touches sent.

#### 4. The 97/3 Conversation Market
- **What he does**: 97% of the market isn't ready; 3% is; only a sliver of the 3% will ever inbound. So he *starts conversations with everyone who engages* instead of waiting: "Most content creators — all they need to do is start conversations with the people that liked their content." Post-and-pray is "marketing to 0.1% of the market"; he markets to the 97 AND the 3.
- **The compounding**: Move people down the trust line for a year and "you have an inbox full of 1,000 people who all really like and trust you… when they have the problem, they're like, 'Sick.' That's how I booked 5,000 meetings."
- **Success Metric**: Conversations started per week; inbox depth of warm relationships — never impressions.

#### 5. The Six Gates of Trust (upgrade of the existing five-gate pattern)
Stranger → **Gate 1: "Is this real?"** (AI-era default is *negative* trust — "you're not starting neutral; no one believes anything") → **Gate 2: "Is this for me?"** → **Gate 3: The Snowflake Gate** — "I'm a special snowflake. Do you understand MY situation?" (where post-and-pray dies; where niching pays) → **Gate 4: The Risk Gate** — "if I do this, will it hurt me?" → **Gate 5: "Is it worth it / should I do it now?"** (the sale point) → **Gate 6: "Is this part of me?"** → **Identity Ally** — a client or friend who spreads word of mouth without you.
- **Two forces move people through gates**: familiarity (shared subculture, kids, CrossFit — "if someone's a CrossFitter, there's a 100% chance it will close") and value (real or perceived — the Akash Singh comedian example: trust that the click will pay off).
- **Three mechanisms carry the forces**: content (one-to-many, you control), chats (one-to-one, you control), word of mouth (you don't control — the only mechanism that builds trust *without* you).
- **Lane-scoping**: "I'm Alex Hormozi's gate-six identity ally… but would I take his restaurant recommendations? Maybe not." Trust is per-lane, not global.
- **Success Metric**: Diagnose where a funnel bleeds by finding which gate the prospect stalls at; count identity allies, not followers.

#### 6. Windows 95 Platform Arbitrage
- **The frame**: TikTok/Instagram = a brand-new MacBook; LinkedIn = the Windows 95 box next to it. "The platform actually sucks… that's your competition. Because there's no one there, it's so much easier to compete." Sales Navigator is "built on Windows 92."
- **The economics**: text-only platform (users open it primed to *read* — lowest content-production barrier); average user earns 2-3x other platforms; value-per-follower "disgustingly higher"; "a month of content in 5 hours… it's the Facebook of 2016."
- **The moat**: "It's never going to get better because it's not cool to use it" — the unfixable lameness is what keeps competition out (same logic as Cody Sanchez's boring businesses).
- **The truth serum**: the resume-graph can't be faked at scale — schools, employers, mutual connections. "On Instagram you don't know if someone's a billionaire or hired a fake jet."

#### 7. Twin-of-Best-Client Targeting
- **What he does**: "Your next best client is the twin of your current best client." Start from a named client, then Sales Navigator filters: same niche, connected to that client or to people you know, same city — even "within 5km of my house." Outreach leads with the twin: "Saw you're connected to Shawn — he's my other financial-planner client, we helped him grow XYZ. I documented exactly how in a two-page doc. Want me to send it?"
- **Why it works**: Never go cold — go *lukewarm* through mutual-connection familiarity ("just being familiar is enough"). And competitor curiosity: "Of course they're going to want to see what their competitor's doing." (Mundo: $128K in 6 weeks, now the #1 financial planner on LinkedIn.)
- **Success Metric**: Every outreach carries a mutual, a place, or a named twin — zero true-cold messages.

#### 8. Influence = Action Taken
- **The definition**: Influence isn't followers ("hot Instagram influencers have zero influence — people are objectifying them, not listening"). Influence is created when someone consumes your content, **takes an action**, and gets a positive return — "that's a sign your content is now within their identity." That's the Hormozi mechanism: he gets people to *do* stuff, reciprocity accrues.
- **The content consequence**: Speak only opinions-from-experience ("last Wednesday a client paid me $3,000 for a meeting — here's what I showed him"). The Warren Buffett quote on a billboard gets 14,000 likes and zero affiliation. Rip and grab client wins continuously — but never write a labeled "case study post" ("the worst thing you can say").
- **The polarity requirement**: contrarian-from-experience; viral leadership slop caters to "employees hungover on a Monday, scrolling on the toilet at 9:26am" — "I've had people with a million impressions a month who never had a lead."
- **Success Metric**: Actions taken by ideal buyers (profile views, replies, downloads, implementations) per post — never likes.

#### 9. Leads Fix the Founder's Nervous System
- **The model**: Maslow with a computer metaphor — "35 Chrome tabs open, CPU screaming." Founders stuck at security-stage (financial insecurity) make short-horizon decisions in a sympathetic state: bad clients, subpar hires, discounting. "Everything is downstream from lead generation" (cites Priestley). Demand surplus = choice: "so many leads you're saying no to people… you're always on the upper hand of the negotiation."
- **The founder's #1 job**: be the key person of influence for the brand; generate demand first — "it doesn't matter how good your system is; all they care about is how much demand you have."
- **Success Metric**: The founder is turning people away; pricing power and hiring quality rise as by-products.

#### 10. Self-Trust Erosion Mechanics
- **The mechanism**: every social scroll makes subconscious micro-comparisons; "every micro-comparison is a tear in your self-trust." Broken self-promises ("I'll start Monday") tear it further — but the cause is state, not character: in a sympathetic state, the brain drops the promise ("you didn't forget because you didn't care — you literally couldn't think of it").
- **The countermeasures**: **no-input mornings** (zero social before creative work — "the greatest don't consume while they do"); end-of-day **loop-closing walks** (speak the day's open loops into ChatGPT until thoughts shift from "urgent, urgent, urgent" to "random curiosity" — the state-shift tell); hire and act on self-trust (his 500-applicant screen selected for exactly this).
- **Why it's in a sales skill**: prospecting avoidance IS a self-trust deficit — "founders sit on the DM for two weeks because they know rejection will hurt." The fix is reps to numbness plus state repair, not scripts.

#### 11. Subconscious Model Mining (the IP factory)
- **What he does**: In a parasympathetic state (long walks, dog, no inputs), speak fluid stream-of-consciousness into Otter/AI for 10-30 min; paste; let AI reflect back un-crystallized thoughts ("that's what I was thinking"); go deeper, recursively — hundreds of hours. This is literally how the Six Gates were built: "I tried to map my own subconscious on how I trust things. It doesn't exist on the internet." He claims ~100 unpublished models, plus an AI second brain (every ChatGPT/Claude conversation + phone notes vectored — ~100K vectors) he queries as his own externalized mind. "AI is the ultimate mirror — a cathedral of mirrors. You just have to look."
- **Prerequisites** (his three mind-tools): meditation (witness thoughts without tagging emotion — "the thought isn't me"), CBT (challenge thoughts; the "make it get worse" technique that killed his 2019 panic attacks), metacognition (trace a thought to its cause chain — craving → bad sleep → sympathetic day).
- **Warning he gives**: "It's heavy. I've had people message me saying they were bawling their eyes out."
- **Success Metric**: A named, teachable proprietary model extracted from your own decision-making — IP no competitor can copy because it's mined, not researched.

#### 12. Puzzle-Piece Agency
- **The model**: Any new venture is a 250-piece puzzle where you're handed 100 pieces. Planning cannot produce the missing pieces — only action can. "Your thoughts aren't real… ideas are in the ether, the pieces are in reality."
- **The demonstrated behavior** (same-day workshop sequence): asked a group chat for help → "can we get on a call NOW?" → call in 45 min → whole page of notes → decided test event on the call → texted 3 invites *during the call* → afterwards: team dispatched to scout rooms, ChatGPT rebuilt an old offer doc, invites out — all same day. The adviser bought his program on that same call without asking the price.
- **The doctrine**: winning founders "take immediate action even when the action's 70%" — feedback is the payment for motion. High agency = "doing things immediately without double-guessing yourself."

### Hidden Knowledge

- **Trust is lane-scoped**: an identity ally in one lane won't follow you into another (Hormozi's restaurant recs). Never assume brand trust transfers across offers.
- **The AI era starts you at negative trust**: "no one believes anything… you must be a liar" — which is why in-person (run clubs, triathlons, events) is exploding as trust arbitrage, and why "is this real?" is now the hardest gate.
- **Referral-only sellers can't sell**: "I close 70-80% of my referrals" hides zero cold-selling ability — referral closes are pre-de-risked by word of mouth. Diagnose before believing a founder's self-reported close rate.
- **Compelling events can be manufactured**: nobody decides without one; a well-timed ask (closed-lost campaign, invite) *creates* the event instead of waiting for it.
- **Conversations produce feedback, not just pipeline**: his 8 months of $180K failure produced one asset — 150 in-person meetups — which produced both the offer insight ("people just need a few warm leads and want to do it themselves") AND the first 25 clients at $3.5K. The market conversation is the product-discovery engine.
- **The permission trap**: "people are stuck needing permission from society" — his phone number has been in his LinkedIn profile for 2 years; no one has ever called. The arbitrage on direct action is enormous precisely because nobody takes it.
- **Content is trust advertising for conversations** (reinforcement of existing insight, sharper here): "The go-viral crowd produces content because they're too chicken to talk to a stranger… they're trying to create demand so people speak to them."

### Signature Moves

- **Ask-opinion-never-pitch (×3)**: Every conversation opener and follow-up is an opinion request about something *they* engaged with. → Deploy on any engagement signal.
- **The Week of Silence**: After gifting the free asset, go dark for seven days before the invite — the silence does the selling. → Deploy after any value delivery; break only for hand-raises.
- **Free-asset-before-invite**: Even a warm prospect gets a gift before an ask (exception: live emotional charge = speed-to-lead, capitalize now). → Deploy at ladder end or any "struggling with X" admission.
- **Same-day engager outreach**: 10 DMs before 10am + message ~5 people who engaged with content that day. → Deploy as the non-negotiable daily rhythm.
- **Twin-first prospect search**: Every list build starts from a named best client and radiates outward through their graph. → Deploy before opening Sales Navigator.
- **Manufacture the compelling event**: Monthly closed-lost ask; scarcity-framed invites ("working with a couple more people next month"). → Deploy on any dormant database.
- **Think-do collapse**: "Can we get on a call now?" — action within the hour, invites sent during the meeting, 70%-ready is ready. → Deploy whenever the alternative is scheduling or pondering.
- **Go where competitors aren't**: offline visits, bad platforms, boring niches, rich non-tech buyers ("the partner at the legal firm, zero idea about tech, heaps of money"). → Deploy at every positioning choice.
