## The Frameworks Extracted (the IP)

**A. Principles of Messaging (the North Star)** — before any story: *What are you trying to say? Why? Who is it for? What are you promising the audience?* → governs every downstream cut.

**B. The 4 Pillars (Anatomy of a Story):**
1. **Get their attention** — 3 mechanisms: *Surprise* (break the pattern), *Emotion* (be the cause of it), *Call them by their name* (describe their exact thought/fear/dream so they recognize themselves — the "Just Do It" effect).
2. **Make them feel something** — the **3 C's** firing simultaneously: *Content × Character × Circumstance* (the Titanic test).
3. **Teach them something** — take a familiar world, reveal the parts they didn't know (the campfire-bear principle). Substance, not empty calories.
4. **Leave them wanting more** — not cliffhangers → **questions**. Open a loop they must close; everything prior promises the payoff is worth it.

**C. The Art of Subtraction** — master vs. good = knowing what to leave out (Twain: "sorry this is so long, I didn't have time to make it shorter"). Two detail buckets: *momentum details* (keep) vs. *withheld seeds* (plant → they lean in and ask → you get to show off).

**D. Pitch-Room Psychology** — infect them with the purpose; the test = they leave the room and immediately call their boss/spouse; read the mood as *opportunity* (be the best part of a bad day); mine overlaps and drop them at the right moment (nostalgia trigger); **truncate on emotional buy-in** — never overexplain.

**E. Ownership Engineering** — riff on their idea (needn't even use it) → they feel co-authorship → they fight for it.

**F. Applied layer (host's translation to business):** Save-the-Cat audience mapping · content-hook mechanics (contrarian truth, comment-bait emotion) · **but-and-therefore** escalation (Parker/Stone rule) · the **log line** (customer=hero, want, obstacle, you=the bridge) · **world-building for brand** (recurring characters/sets/formats) · character-canvas (you as protagonist: want/flaw/change).
