## Executive Summary

Rory’s deepest move is not “use behavioral science in marketing.” It is to treat adoption, expectation, attention, uncertainty, status, and response mechanics as part of the product itself. The nominal product can remain unchanged while behavior changes dramatically.

This source adds a more complete operating sequence:

1. Identify the behavior that must change.
2. Audit the journey edges before rebuilding the core.
3. Find the single psychological bottleneck.
4. Translate the offer into the buyer’s existing unit.
5. Make any sacrifice explicit and voluntary.
6. Test one variable or a genuinely different model.
7. Evaluate risky experiments at portfolio level.
8. Use intuition to search and evidence to verify.

The publisher sample reinforces the governing stance: logical conformity leaves profitable territory unexplored; context changes behavior; trivial variables deserve tests; and discovery may precede explanation. The sample does not supply the missing later chapters and is not treated as the full book.

## Hidden Knowledge

1. **Adoption is part of invention.** A technically complete object is commercially incomplete until people can translate, trust, and adopt it.
2. **The edges often govern the whole.** Arrival, waiting, payment, response, and exit are more likely to hide cheap behavioral advantage than the polished center.
3. **The buyer’s unit is infrastructure.** A memorable unit can outlive the campaign and become a category’s permanent decision language.
4. **Information design is emotion design.** More precision may produce more anxiety; the correct measure is the one that supports the desired behavior without deception.
5. **Restriction needs consent.** A limitation becomes positioning when the buyer understands and chooses the exchange in advance.
6. **Career incentives can defeat arithmetic.** A favorable firm-level bet will die when the individual sponsor absorbs the downside and the institution absorbs the upside.
7. **Response channels segment by psychology.** Removing a channel can erase a group whose barrier is procedural rather than attitudinal.
8. **Professional embarrassment can be a moat.** Effective practices discarded as unfashionable remain underused because peers, not customers, police the category.
9. **Focus creates adjacent demand.** Eccentric design can solve unnamed problems for people outside the intended segment.
10. **Reason belongs after search as well as before action.** Intuition can choose where to investigate without receiving permission to fabricate proof.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

These are transcript-attested demonstrations, faithfully summarized rather than treated as independently verified case studies.

### 1. Horsepower as Adoption Infrastructure, 02:13-04:03

Mine owners did not need boiler language. They needed to calculate the horses, food, and labor a steam engine displaced. The marketing unit made the innovation legible.

### 2. Oresund Self-Valuation Mechanism, 06:00-07:10

Cargo owners declared a value, but the operator retained the right to buy the cargo at that price. The mechanism aligned truthful reporting with self-interest.

### 3. Thaler’s Division-Head Problem, 24:43-27:15

Executives rejected favorable bets because a bad local outcome threatened their jobs. The chief executive wanted the bets pooled because the firm was likely to win in aggregate.

### 4. Uber’s Waiting Redesign, 29:41-33:37

The ride itself changed less than the booking, waiting, payment, and sense of progress. Expectations and agency expanded the market without relying only on a better vehicle.

### 5. Response-Channel Additivity, 42:21-44:58

Postal response and telephone response produced different rates; offering both approached the sum of their separate effects. The ordering mechanism mattered more than economists would expect.

### 6. American Express Rejection Removal, 49:18-54:24

The obstacle was not desire for the card but fear of rejection. Invitation and pre-approval language changed the social meaning of the action.

### 7. Explicit Minimalism, 60:05-65:32

Flat White or F-Off, Moxy, and the Slate Truck turned missing category conventions into a chosen bargain. The limitation worked because it was declared before the customer arrived.

### 8. Advertising Archaeology, 71:55-74:27

Long copy, direct mail, jingles, and comic-strip advertising were presented as mechanisms that became unfashionable rather than ineffective. A moving audience can experience an old form as new.

## Signature Moves

1. **Name the behavior:** Replace “improve the product” with the exact action, choice, or adoption that must change.
2. **Translate the specification:** Ask which cost, habit, fear, or labor disappears for the buyer.
3. **Audit the edges:** Walk arrival, ordering, waiting, payment, delivery, and exit as a first-time customer.
4. **Rewrite the brief twice:** Produce a physics brief and a psychology brief; test the cheaper credible path first.
5. **Invert the irritation:** Turn each hated category ritual into an opposite design possibility.
6. **Test atomically or radically:** Change one variable or replace the whole model; do not pretend a bundle teaches causality.
7. **Name the sacrifice:** State what the buyer will not receive and what that exclusion buys.
8. **Pool the bets:** Evaluate favorable experiments as a portfolio and protect local owners from rational failure.
9. **Excavate abandoned media:** Preserve the mechanism, discard the dated skin, and adapt it for today’s audience.
10. **Verify after searching:** Let anomalies direct attention, then demand evidence before scaling.

## Anti-Patterns

- Improving physics before locating the governing anxiety.
- Optimizing the polished center while ignoring journey edges.
- Asking customers to explain feelings they have not consciously formulated.
- Treating specifications as if they were already buyer value.
- Testing five simultaneous changes and claiming causal knowledge.
- Imposing an efficiency cut without making the trade-off visible and voluntary.
- Judging exploratory bets one manager or one quarter at a time.
- Fabricating progress instead of truthfully increasing milestone visibility.
- Discarding effective formats because peers consider them unfashionable.
- Using psychology to dismiss a real defect, false claim, safety hazard, or regulatory constraint.
