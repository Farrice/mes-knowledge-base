## Four-Mission Deployment

### ATTRACT — Recognition

**Job:** Help quiet experts recognize that the selling environment may be hiding their ability.

**Draft:**

I was bad at walking up to people on the gym floor.

The strange part was that training was where I was strongest.

Put me in front of a room and let me teach, and people listened. After the seminar, they came to me.

Same knowledge. Different room.

Sometimes “bad at selling” means you keep choosing a setting that hides the part of you people would trust.

**CTA:** Low-friction recognition: “Where does your expertise become easiest to see?”

### NURTURE — Pursuit and Method

**Job:** Reveal the method inside the story.

**Brief:** Teach the four moves of seminar-first proof: choose one problem from direct experience; put the teaching in an opt-in environment; demonstrate decisions and method; create a clear route for interested people to continue. Include the decision rule: this is useful when expertise is strong and interruption is the bottleneck, but it does not remove the need for follow-up or an offer.

**CTA:** Save or request the Method Card.

### POSITION — Judgment

**Job:** Demonstrate the distinction between describing expertise and making judgment inspectable.

**Draft:**

A bio asks people to believe you understand the work.

A useful seminar lets them watch you diagnose the problem.

That was the difference for me in training. I struggled to sell myself on the gym floor. In a seminar, the same knowledge became visible because people could hear how I thought.

The lesson was not “never do outreach.”

It was this: when trust is the constraint, build a format where judgment can be inspected before the sales conversation.

**CTA:** View a proof-bearing teaching artifact.

### CONVERT — Coherent Next Action

**Job:** Invite a fitting next step without pretending the story proves current demand.

**3P-to-Promise bridge — SYSTEM SYNTHESIS:**

- Problem selects quiet or under-visible experts.
- Pursuit supplies the mechanism: opt-in teaching that demonstrates judgment.
- Payoff supports only the claim that this worked as Farrice's historical client-attraction approach.
- Promise hypothesis: “Turn one area of deep expertise into a teaching asset that lets prospective buyers inspect how you think before a sales conversation.”

**CTA hypothesis:** Request an Expertise-to-Story diagnostic.

**Commercial state:** `OFFER_HYPOTHESIS`; `MARKET PROOF: NO EVENT`.

The same method supports a bounded workshop run-of-show:

1. Farrice's gym-floor/seminar story establishes the Problem.
2. The audience diagnoses where their expertise is currently invisible.
3. Teach the distinction between describing expertise and demonstrating judgment.
4. Transform one participant's supplied expertise into a 3P teaching asset.
5. Explain the implementation gap: most experts need source mining, message shaping, format selection, and proof boundaries—not more generic posting advice.
6. Introduce the diagnostic or service only if its real scope, price, terms, and proof are supplied.
7. Close with an authorized next action; no guarantee, scarcity, or invented client result.
