## New Genius Patterns (15-25, all primary-sourced with timestamps)

### Pattern 15: Visibility Split Diagnosis
- **What he does**: Decomposes "visibility" into traditional vs AI components before prescribing anything. JerkyGent: #3 Google organic, SPI 33/100 — Traditional 76%, Video 7%, **AI mentions 0%**, AI citations 37% (14:05).
- **Executable**: Benchmark both layers separately; never let a strong traditional rank mask AI absence. "Doing good SEO doesn't always drive more AI visibility" (0:00).
- **Success metric**: A per-category split scorecard exists before any work is prescribed.

### Pattern 16: Citation Autopsy First
- **What he does**: "My favorite thing to do right now is simply just go to the AI citations. This is most of the work that we do these days" (2:50). Counts brand mentions across all citations: 4 of 82, three self-serving → "they basically have one" (3:00-3:30).
- **Executable**: Extract every source AI cites for the target query set; count real third-party brand mentions; that count IS the diagnosis.
- **Success metric**: Mention count with self-serving citations separated out.

### Pattern 17: Consensus Engineering
- **What he does**: Models the AI's decision: "Imagine you're the AI… you've got this brand that no one ever talks about across any of your sources… It's working through these different sources and then looking for consensus" (3:40). Archer shows up everywhere because it's mentioned in almost every citation.
- **Executable**: The unit of AI SEO work is a third-party source mention, not an on-site optimization. Fix absence-from-sources before touching the site.
- **Success metric**: Rising mention share across the citation set over scan history.

### Pattern 18: Export-and-Mark Opportunity Mining
- **What he does**: Runs multiple keyword reports, exports ALL traditional + AI citations to one spreadsheet (schema: Keyword / URL / Platforms / Avg. position / Opportunity-Y), then hand-marks each URL: earned (blogs, press, affiliates), owned (FB/IG/YT), distribution (Amazon/Target/Walmart) (4:16-7:40). ~20 earned opportunities from 4 topics; "multiply by 10-20 topics, you'd have a couple hundred easily" (6:20).
- **Executable**: One export, one manual classification pass, one prioritized target list. "You can learn so much from this — people greatly underestimate this" (4:40).
- **Success metric**: Every citation URL classified; opportunity list in the hundreds at full topic coverage.

### Pattern 19: Retrieval-Informed Distribution
- **What he does**: Spots Amazon, Target, Walmart inside the citation set; checks — brand is on none of them. "Three massive e-commerce distribution engines… and they're actually being used in retrieval as well" (12:45).
- **Executable**: Retail/marketplace domains in the citation set = double-value targets (sales channel + citation source). Treat distribution gaps as SEO gaps.
- **Success metric**: Presence on every retrieval-cited marketplace relevant to the category.

### Pattern 20: Competitor Comparison Ladder
- **What he does**: Seeds with "[Competitor] alternatives," then expands to "[X] vs [Y]," then "[X] vs [Y] vs [us]" — "these are absolutely the best because it kills two birds with one stone: you rank for the competitor queries, then you slap yourself in there" (9:30-10:35). Template visible: honest Quick Picks (self takes ONE slot), competitor strengths conceded, "What We Looked For" criteria disclosed. Repeat across every competitor.
- **Executable**: Build the full ladder per competitor; self-placement is one credible slot, not a takeover.
- **Success metric**: Ladder complete for every retrieval-cited competitor.

### Pattern 21: Home Base → Echo Expansion
- **What he does**: "You've built out topic authority on your website, which is your home base… Once you've tapped it out, then expand. I recommend expanding into YouTube because YouTube has so much influence" (10:50-11:20); then Facebook/Instagram. "You just take the same topics, but make sure the content format matches whatever distribution channel you're using" (12:10). YouTube long-form > Shorts, though Shorts appear in retrieval (11:40).
- **Executable**: Same topic set, re-formatted per channel, sequenced website → YouTube → FB/IG. Influencer outreach on the same channels doubles the front (5:50).
- **Success metric**: Each tapped-out topic exists in channel-native form on every owned channel appearing in retrieval.

### Pattern 22: Benchmark-Annotate-Scan
- **What he does**: "A lot of people are just wasting money on tracking… there's no point tracking performance every day if you aren't working on that category" (13:40). Benchmark snapshot → annotate each shipped asset on the timeline (dated, categorized: "Content change") → run a scan after work → read movement against annotations (14:00-14:30).
- **Executable**: Track work-correlated, not calendar-correlated. "You're not going to be able to say one asset did it, but you can clearly see over time" (14:40).
- **Success metric**: Every scan preceded by ≥1 dated work annotation.

### Pattern 23: Category-Focused Linkbait Prompting
- **What he does**: "If you say 'give me link bait ideas' it's not that useful. Even 'we're a beef jerky company, give us link bait ideas' — not that useful. You want to get real focused on just that one category cuz you get way better ideas" (15:20). Verbatim prompt asks for data-driven/statistics-driven ideas serving backlinks + PR outreach + social distribution, 25 ideas. Then prioritizes 5 flagships.
- **Executable**: Prompt at category granularity, demand grounded research-backed angles, generate 25, build 5.
- **Success metric**: Ideas are research-seeded (named data sources) with explicit PR hooks, not topic titles.

### Pattern 24: Research → Asset → Design-Agent Chain
- **What he does**: "Conduct deep research on this topic" → 12m 10s run → reads limitations → adopts the "most defensible lead statistic" → "I would just use the research and convert it into a very structured asset" (16:40) → "Create a prompt for my design agent for the visual assets to support this data-driven study" → pastes into Claude design → full asset system (hero/OG/donut/timeline/map/infographic, brand design system, alt text) (17:00-18:00).
- **Executable**: Never publish raw research; never hand a generator a bare idea — chain research → structure → design brief → asset system.
- **Success metric**: Flagship asset ships with defensible lead stat + complete visual system in brand style.

### Pattern 25: Magic-in-the-Edit
- **What he does**: Skips the design agent's clarifying questions, lets it cook, then: "This is not going to be perfect out of the gate… you can start to just focus on editing. These days the key to using AI is you don't ever need to start with a blank canvas basically ever. The magic is in the edit… true with design, AI content, AI coding" (17:15-18:20).
- **Executable**: Generate fast at full scope, spend human time exclusively on the edit pass (hallucination check, refinement).
- **Success metric**: Human hours concentrated post-generation, not pre-generation.

## New Hidden Knowledge (7-11)

7. **One category, 90-180 days**: "We're usually focused on one category for anywhere between 90 to 180 days… to make sure we absolutely cover it to the fullest extent. Most businesses never do this. You have to go a mile deep" (0:55). Category focus is the enabling constraint for everything else — "If you try to be super broad, it's just too hard" (18:30).
8. **Press releases get retrieved**: A BusinessWire release appears in AI-mode citations — "We can run these ourselves, too. The fact that it's getting picked up as a citation tells us what we need to know" (5:06).
9. **The gaps are always more granular**: Brand covered sugar-free/keto/grass-fed; gaps were paleo, weight loss, bodybuilding, zinc/magnesium/vitamin D — "all separate dedicated intents" (8:58). GSC piping surfaces even richer granular intent when available (8:30).
10. **Strategy is easy; execution is the moat**: "You should be able to develop a strategy in one day… It's easy to develop the strategy. It's hard to execute" (0:40, 13:10). The one-page doc + one Canva map ARE the strategy artifact.
11. **Grounded ideation only**: "They're grounded, too, cuz you know that they're grounded because it actually did research" (15:55) — ideas without research seeding don't ship.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars (primary-sourced)

### Exemplar 1: The JerkyGent Citation Autopsy (1:30-3:45)
Ranks #3 in Google organic with strong 4-engine coverage, yet SPI 33/100: AI mentions 0%. Autopsy: 4 mentions across 82 AI citations, 3 self-serving → "they basically have one." Conclusion delivered as the AI's-eye view: no consensus, no recommendation, "this is going to hurt sales."
**Why it anchors**: The diagnosis is a counted number from the citation set, not a vibe — and it inverts the client's own success story ("doing really well in traditional search") in ninety seconds.

### Exemplar 2: The Archer Jerky Alternatives Template (9:37-10:35)
"5 Best Alternatives to Archer Jerky for 2026 Snacking" — intro concedes competitor heritage (People's Choice, family recipe since 1929), Quick Picks gives self ONE slot ("Best overall for craft discovery: JerkyGent") among four honest competitor placements, "What We Looked For" discloses criteria (flavor range, texture, ingredient fit, price per ounce, availability).
**Why it anchors**: Retrieval-credible self-promotion — the page earns the competitor query AND survives an AI's honesty sniff-test because it reads like a genuine roundup.

### Exemplar 3: The Linkbait Chain (15:20-18:20)
Category-focused prompt → 25 grounded ideas (e.g., "State of Healthy Beef Jerky Report" with PR hook "We analyzed 150 beef jerky products. Only X% met our healthy jerky criteria," seeded by USDA FoodData Central) → prioritize 5 → deep research 12m 10s → limitations review → defensible lead stat ("72% of beef jerky recalls came down to labels or inspection, not pathogens") → design-agent prompt → Claude design asset system in JerkyGent's design system → edit pass.
**Why it anchors**: Every step feeds the next; the human judgment moments (prioritize 5, adopt defensible framing, edit) are exactly where he spends time.

### Anti-Exemplar: "Why High Protein Snacks Are a Great Idea" (15:00)
The brand's own informational post, called out by name: "A lot of people get this messed up, including this brand… not just kind of generic stuff that AI could spin up in 2 seconds."
**Why it fails**: No data, no research seed, no PR hook, no link reason — topic support in name only.

## Signature Moves

- **Benchmark before touching anything**: First action on any category is a snapshot report — last week's AND today's (1:10). → Deploy at engagement start.
- **Citations first, content second**: Opens the citation set before generating a single content idea (2:50). → Deploy before any content planning.
- **Mark the export by hand**: Reads every citation URL and classifies it personally — the highlighted rows are his cursor trail (5:00-7:40). → Deploy during opportunity mining; never delegate wholesale to a script.
- **Concede strength before naming gaps**: "This is like really good. I'm impressed… But there are gaps still" (8:15). → Deploy in every audit; credibility of the gap list depends on crediting what works.
- **Prioritize 5 of 25**: Generates wide, builds narrow — "The five I'd prioritize" (16:15). → Deploy after any ideation burst.
- **Skip the clarifying questions, edit the output**: Let the agent cook at full scope, then concentrate on the edit (17:10-18:20). → Deploy on all AI-generation handoffs.
