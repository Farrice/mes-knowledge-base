## Genius Patterns Decoded

### 1. The Trust Header (WINDOW / LENS / SOURCES / COMPILED)
The brief's cover carries a 4-column mono metadata row: `WINDOW last 30 days · LENS marketing·creative·mcp · SOURCES ~120 posts + press · COMPILED jul 31, 2026` (t=00:33).
**Why It Works**: scope declaration before claims — the reader knows freshness, filter, and evidence volume before believing anything. It pre-answers "can I trust this?" which is the #1 abandonment reason for AI research output.
**How to Apply**: every generated brief renders window/lens/source-count/compile-date as a structural header, populated by the generator, never hand-typed.
**Success Metric**: 100% of briefs carry all 4 fields; zero briefs shipped without a dated window.

### 2. Claim-First Evidence Rows
Every insight is a bold lowercase verdict ("22 claude code skills grouped by job") + 1–2 line dense description + tiny mono source id, with a check control (t=01:25).
**Why It Works**: verdict-first inverts the AI default (context → maybe a point). Scannable in seconds; each row independently verifiable via its source link — "if I click on view, it takes me to that specific post and I can fact check everything" (t=01:10).
**How to Apply**: brief schema = list of {claim, evidence, source_url, confidence}; renderer forbids claims without a source field.
**Success Metric**: every rendered claim row has a working source link; spot-check 3 rows per brief → 3/3 resolve.

### 3. The Decision Layer Gets the Ink
Recommendations live in the document's only dark inverted section — `THE OPENING: where the lane actually is` — ranked "in order," each with the number that justifies it ("'ai seo' is past its peak… 'answer engine optimization' hit its highest month this month, at lower difficulty" — difficulty 74 vs 65, t=07:52).
**Why It Works**: visual contrast encodes information hierarchy — data is context, the decision is the product. Ranked ordering removes the reader's sequencing burden.
**How to Apply**: template reserves one inverted section for "what to do, in order"; generator requires ≥3 ranked recommendations each citing a stat from the evidence sections.
**Success Metric**: a reader can state the #1 recommended action within 30 seconds of opening the brief.

### 4. Built-In Epistemics (the "what this isn't" section)
The report ranks its own data reliability: "the trend shapes and the community sizes are the most reliable things here. the conversion percentages and revenue claims are the least. build on the first, quote the second carefully or not at all" (t=08:13).
**Why It Works**: pre-empting the fact-check converts skepticism into trust; it also inoculates against the AI-slop failure mode of uniform confidence.
**How to Apply**: our template makes this structural — VERIFIED/LIKELY/UNCONFIRMED chips per claim + a caveats section ranking source classes.
**Success Metric**: zero unverified claims rendered without an UNCONFIRMED chip; caveats section present in 100% of briefs.

### 5. One-Sentence Research, Tool-Discovery Delegated
His entire prompt names the *topic and the output standard*, not the tools' mechanics: "identify the tools we can use to conduct this research… turn that into a really good report using my Claude design system. The topic… is AI SEO" (t=04:54, verbatim in reference-corpus).
**Why It Works**: the agent's tool-discovery step (t=05:29) is more current than the human's memory of the catalog; the human's real job is topic choice + output standard.
**How to Apply**: standard prompt = topic + platform hints + "render with [our template]"; never enumerate endpoints manually.
**Success Metric**: research kickoff ≤ 1 sentence + template pointer; report lands in ≤10 min.

### 6. Ground Generation in Your Own Proven Wins
Before generating 30 video ideas, he has Claude read his channel: "there's things I've already proved like I made a video on this topic… other multiplier videos like AI blogging which did pretty good" (t=10:01–10:11).
**Why It Works**: this is the exact anti-"start from scratch" move — new ideas inherit priors from titles with demonstrated demand, so the calendar is an extension of a working strategy, not a cold ideation.
**How to Apply**: zeitgeist briefs join scraped market data with Farrice's own performance data (LinkedIn posts, Substack opens) before any ideation step.
**Success Metric**: every idea list cites ≥2 of the user's own proven pieces as anchors.

### 7. Arc-Structured Idea Calendars
The 30 ideas arrive in 3 narrative arcs — baseline-and-stakes → building-systems → money-proof-payoff (t=10:42–12:04) — with anchor terms to own, per-video talking points, and explicit caveats ("do not commit to 30 days… one term per video. US English only" t=12:07).
**Why It Works**: arcs turn a list into a campaign with escalating proof; caveats keep the plan falsifiable.
**How to Apply**: idea sections in briefs render as arcs with anchor terms, not flat lists.
**Success Metric**: idea output contains named arcs + per-idea talking points + a caveat block.

### 8. Cost Transparency as a Habit
He states the report's cost on screen: "$0.03 to actually create this report" (t=06:04) and shows "the entire stack that it used" (t=06:09).
**Why It Works**: per-run cost + tool provenance makes the workflow governable and repeatable — you can hand it to a VA with a budget (t=13:31) because spend is observable.
**How to Apply**: our briefs log run cost + tool stack into the source ledger; trackers already exist (.agent/*-usage.json).
**Success Metric**: every brief's ledger shows cost and stack; monthly spend reconciles against tracker.

### 9. Same-Scale Small Multiples
Per-keyword 12-month bar charts share one scale, captioned "same scale, very different shapes" (t=08:32).
**Why It Works**: shared scale is what makes shape comparison honest; separate auto-scaled charts would manufacture equivalence.
**How to Apply**: template chart component enforces shared y-scale across compared series.
**Success Metric**: any multi-series comparison in a brief uses one scale or is rejected.

### 10. The Document IS the Product (design system as personal infrastructure)
Brief, research report, and even his video scripts are all rendered in one personal design system (t=00:15, 01:52, 05:13) — cream paper, single blue accent, italic-serif accent words, mono labels, pull-quote bars.
**Why It Works**: a consistent personal document language collapses consumption friction to near zero — he reviews everything in one visual grammar; it also becomes brand equity ("what we built is something that a lot of people would want to pay for," t=12:46).
**How to Apply**: exactly our Phase C — Codex Antigravity tokens as the one grammar for research briefs, with the asset board as the library surface.
**Success Metric**: 100% of research output consumable in the house design system within one session of the template shipping.
