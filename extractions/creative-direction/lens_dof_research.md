# Lens, Focal Length & Depth of Field Research

## Focal Length Creative Effects
- 10-14mm (Fisheye/Ultra-Wide): Extreme distortion, surreal, immersive. Skate videos, action cams, experimental.
- 14-20mm (Ultra-Wide): Dramatic perspective, exaggerated foreground, epic landscapes, architecture, astrophotography. Distorts faces.
- 24mm (Wide): Environmental portraits, street photography, documentary, interiors. Slight distortion adds energy.
- 28mm (Wide): Classic street photography focal length. Natural but expansive. Garry Winogrand, William Eggleston.
- 35mm (Moderate Wide): "The journalist's lens." Natural perspective, versatile, street, documentary, environmental portraits. Henri Cartier-Bresson.
- 50mm (Normal/Standard): Closest to human eye. Natural, honest, versatile. "Nifty fifty." Portraits, street, product.
- 85mm (Short Telephoto): "The portrait king." Flattering compression, beautiful bokeh, shallow DOF. Fashion, beauty, headshots.
- 100mm (Macro/Medium Telephoto): Detail, texture, product photography, macro. Compression begins.
- 135mm (Telephoto): Strong compression, extreme subject isolation, dreamy bokeh. Fashion editorials, cinematic portraits.
- 200mm+ (Long Telephoto): Extreme compression, flattened perspective, voyeuristic, sports, wildlife. Subjects appear stacked.

## Anamorphic Lens Characteristics
- Wider aspect ratio (2.39:1 or 2.76:1 vs standard 1.78:1)
- Oval/elliptical bokeh (vs circular in spherical lenses)
- Horizontal lens flares (signature blue/amber streaks)
- Edge distortion and barrel distortion
- Shallower depth of field at equivalent focal lengths
- "Breathing" effect when focusing
- Rich background separation
- The "cinematic look" — used in most Hollywood blockbusters
- AI prompt keywords: "anamorphic lens", "oval bokeh", "horizontal lens flare", "2.39:1 aspect ratio", "cinemascope"

## Depth of Field Guide
- f/1.2-1.4: Razor-thin focus plane. Dreamy, ethereal, intimate. Single eye in focus.
- f/1.8-2.0: Very shallow. Subject sharp, background creamy. Portraits, product isolation.
- f/2.8: Shallow but workable. Standard for fast zooms. Good subject separation.
- f/4.0: Moderate. Enough depth for small groups, some background detail visible.
- f/5.6-8.0: Deep. Landscape sweet spot. Sharp across most of frame.
- f/11-16: Very deep. Everything sharp. Architecture, group shots, landscape.
- f/22+: Maximum depth. Diffraction softening begins. Star effects on point lights.

## Focus Techniques
- Rack Focus: Shift focus between two subjects. Redirect attention, reveal information, create tension.
- Pull Focus: Gradually change focus following subject movement.
- Split Diopter: Half-lens creating two focal planes simultaneously. Brian De Palma signature.
- Tilt-Shift: Selective focus plane tilted. Miniature effect, selective emphasis.
- Deep Focus: Everything sharp (Citizen Kane, Wes Anderson). Requires small aperture + wide lens.
- Soft Focus: Intentionally slightly out of focus. Dreamy, romantic, ethereal, vintage.
- Bokeh: Quality of out-of-focus areas. Creamy, swirly, busy, harsh. Lens-dependent.
