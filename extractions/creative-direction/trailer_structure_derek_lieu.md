# Derek Lieu — Trailer Story Structure (The Matrix Case Study)

## The Trailer Energy Curve
- Start strong (cold open) → calm down → build back up → climax
- "If a trailer feels high energy all the time, then none of it will feel high energy"

## Four-Act Trailer Structure:

### 1. THE COLD OPEN
- Hit the ground running with exciting, humorous, or dramatic scene
- Requires very little context to understand, or quickly provides its own
- Short, exciting, makes you want to know more
- Example: Trinity running across rooftops (3 shots) → Neo: "Whoa"

### 2. THE INTRODUCTION
- Premise of the story established via exposition or questions
- Music: light violins, "driving" tone — builds tension but unobtrusive to dialogue
- Production music term: "Driving" — a driving force
- Rhetorical questions paired with abstract imagery
- Keep it vague — hint at thesis without spoiling

### 3. THE ESCALATION
- Antagonist or central problem introduced
- Show what main characters will do about it
- Flurry of images, then a grounding statement
- Title card often appears here as a beat break

### 4. THE CLIMAX
- Protagonist does their thing
- Music swells
- Most interesting shots in rapid succession
- At peak excitement → cut to black → title card
- Can be purely visual with no dialogue (atypical but powerful)

## Dialogue Categories for Trailer Editing:

### Exposition
- Characters flat-out describing something
- Cut with shots of what they're talking about
- Bread and butter of trailers

### Rhetorical Questions
- Good for cutting with abstract imagery
- Keeps things vague while hinting at thesis
- People hate trailers that spoil — questions prevent that

### Straightforward Questions
- Set up lines of dialogue or actions
- Separate all questions, see what to pair them with
- Setup for grand answers

### Statements That Provide Own Context
- Short, set up moments of action
- Show character motivation
- Grand sweeping statements → end montages
- Monologuing villains = trailer gold

### The Perfect Meta Line
- "Unfortunately no one can be told what The Matrix is. You have to see it for yourself."
- Works in film context AND marketing context
- Category: PUT THIS INTO THE END OF THE TRAILER

## Key Editing Principles:
- Music is backbone, but dialogue helps editor choose visuals
- Match every line with a visual that makes sense (often INCREDIBLY LITERAL)
- Latch onto any noun in dialogue → find matching image
- After dialogue-visual pairs set, fill gaps with exciting visuals, accents, action
- Constraints help focus — music and dialogue provide a path

## Music Rule:
- "9 times out of 10 after logos, it'll be light violins"
- Purpose: build tension, unobtrusive to dialogue
- Production music libraries call this tone "Driving"
