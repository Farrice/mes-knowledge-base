# Cinematic Language & Visual Direction

When generating video, photography, or cinematic content, incorporate these core principles:

## Lighting Setups
- **High-Key:** Bright, even lighting with minimal shadows. Best for comedies, beauty, and upbeat moods.
- **Low-Key:** Dramatic shadows, high contrast. Best for noir, thrillers, and moody atmospheres.
- **Rembrandt:** A triangle of light on the cheek opposite the light source. Classic, dramatic portraiture.
- **Butterfly/Paramount:** Light placed directly above and in front of the subject, creating a butterfly shadow under the nose. Glamour and beauty.
- **Split Lighting:** Half the face is lit, the other half is in shadow. Dramatic, mysterious, and intense.
- **Chiaroscuro:** Strong contrast between light and dark, inspired by Caravaggio.

## Composition Rules
- **Rule of Thirds:** Placing subjects on the intersections of a 3x3 grid for dynamic balance.
- **Golden Ratio/Fibonacci Spiral:** Mathematical harmony (1.618) guiding the eye naturally through the frame.
- **Leading Lines:** Using architectural or natural lines to guide the viewer's eye to the subject.
- **Symmetry/Asymmetry:** Formal balance vs. dynamic tension.
- **Frame Within a Frame:** Using doorways, windows, or arches to create depth and focus.

## Focal Lengths & Lenses
- **14-20mm (Ultra-Wide):** Dramatic perspective, exaggerated foreground, epic landscapes.
- **35mm (Moderate Wide):** The journalist's lens, natural perspective, environmental portraits.
- **50mm (Normal):** Closest to the human eye, honest, versatile.
- **85mm (Short Telephoto):** The portrait king, flattering compression, beautiful bokeh.
- **Anamorphic Lenses:** Wider aspect ratio (2.39:1), oval bokeh, horizontal lens flares, edge distortion.
