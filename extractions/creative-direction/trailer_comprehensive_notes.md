# Comprehensive Movie Trailer Research Notes

## PSYCHOLOGY OF TRAILERS (The Backlot Company)

### 8 Psychological Principles:
1. First 5 Seconds — Reticular Activating System (brain's attention filter). Use immediate visual intensity.
2. Emotional Hook — Amygdala activation strengthens memory encoding. Emotion drives memory. Pixar masters this in 90 seconds.
3. Music & Sound — Dopamine release from music reinforces reward-seeking behavior. Rising tempo = adrenaline. Dissonant tones = tension. Align audio crescendos with visual climaxes.
4. Suspense / Curiosity — Information Gap Theory: curiosity sparked when gap between what we know and want to know. Tease, don't tell. Introduce stakes without resolution.
5. Rule of Threes — Three-act structure mirrors brain's preference for patterns. Setup → Confrontation → Escalation.
6. Visual Storytelling — Mirror neurons help us "experience" what we see. Show facial expressions, dynamic movement, high-stakes visuals early.
7. FOMO & Social Proof — "From the studio that brought you..." creates credibility and urgency. Brains wired to seek inclusion.
8. Closing Impact — Peak-End Rule: people judge experience by peak moment and ending. Memorable quote, final twist, or powerful musical punch.

## DEREK LIEU — TRAILER EDITING TRICKS
Three categories of trailer manipulation:
1. SHOW LESS — If something doesn't look good, show it as little as possible. Fast cutting hides weaknesses.
2. RAZZLE DAZZLE — Flutter cuts (black frames between footage), flash frames, jump cuts, aggressive sound design, slow motion, camera rotations, motion graphics.
3. MISLEAD — Make material look like something it's not. World's Greatest Dad trailer made a dark comedy look like a quirky Robin Williams comedy.

Key insight: "A fast cut trailer trying to obfuscate looks aesthetically the same as a fast cut trailer for the sake of style."

## 25 GREATEST TRAILERS (Mental Floss Analysis)

### Legendary Trailer Techniques by Film:
- Citizen Kane (1941) — Orson Welles breaks fourth wall, plays on radio/stage background, introduces troupe
- Psycho (1960) — Hitchcock gives personal tour of Bates Motel. Patience and charm before terrifying payoff. Long by modern standards but masterful tension.
- The Exorcist (1973) — Too intense for audiences. Strobing effects. Established expectations the film lived up to.
- Jaws (1975) — Percy Rodriguez voiceover + John Williams music. "It is as if God created the Devil, and gave him Jaws." First great summer blockbuster trailer.
- Alien (1979) — So iconic that Prometheus (30+ years later) reused the same sound effects. Pioneered the slow-build horror trailer.
- Friday the 13th (1980) — Advertised body count upfront. Anticipation from watching HOW it happens, not IF.
- The Shining (1980) — ICONIC: single shot of elevator doors, credits roll, blood tidal wave. That's ALL you need.
- The Abyss (1989) — Behind-the-scenes authenticity hooked audiences on the vision before VFX.
- T2: Judgment Day (1991) — Don LaFontaine "In a world..." voiceover. Shows NOTHING from actual film. Reminds of menace, never reveals twist that T-800 is now good guy.
- Pulp Fiction (1994) — Hit parade of songs that became synonymous with film. Multiple songs highlighting tone.
- GoldenEye (1995) — Reintroduced Bond after long delay. Casual flinch as bullets ricochet = one of great shots in Bond history.
- Independence Day (1996) — White House destruction shot. Single iconic image that defined the entire marketing.
- The Blair Witch Project (1999) — Found footage pioneer. Viral marketing before viral marketing existed.
- The Matrix (1999) — "Unfortunately no one can be told what The Matrix is." Perfect meta-marketing line.
- Spider-Man (2002) — Twin Towers sequence (later removed post-9/11). Showed cultural sensitivity in editing.
- Cloverfield (2008) — No title revealed. Mystery-box marketing. Statue of Liberty head rolling down street.
- The Social Network (2010) — Radiohead's "Creep" choir cover. Recontextualized a familiar song to create haunting tone.
- The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo (2011) — Led Zeppelin's "Immigrant Song." Aggressive, rapid-fire, felt like an assault.
- Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) — Pure kinetic energy. Let the visuals speak. Minimal dialogue.
- Get Out (2017) — Jordan Peele. Genre-defying trailer that felt like comedy then horror then social commentary.

## SOUND DESIGN — THE BRAAAM
- Originated from Inception (2010) by Hans Zimmer
- Long, low, brassy foghorn-on-steroids notes
- Imparts apocalyptic momentousness
- Became the most copied sound in trailer history
- Studio-recorded but samples sounds from the film's world
- Now considered overused but still effective when deployed sparingly

## KEY TRAILER ARCHETYPES (synthesized):
1. THE MYSTERY BOX — Cloverfield, Matrix, Alien. Show almost nothing. Create questions.
2. THE EMOTIONAL JOURNEY — Pixar, Schindler's List. Make them feel, not think.
3. THE SPECTACLE — Independence Day, Mad Max. One jaw-dropping image/sequence.
4. THE RECONTEXTUALIZATION — Social Network, Girl with Dragon Tattoo. Familiar song in unfamiliar context.
5. THE SHOWMAN — Psycho, Citizen Kane. Director/personality breaks fourth wall.
6. THE BODY COUNT — Friday the 13th, action films. Promise of escalating stakes.
7. THE SLOW BURN — Alien, The Shining. Patience, patience, patience... then IMPACT.

## MAPPING TO AI CREATIVE WORKFLOWS:
- Cold Open = Hook shot (first 3 seconds of any social content)
- Introduction = Context/premise establishment
- Escalation = Rising tension, faster cuts, music builds
- Climax = Peak visual moment, rapid montage, title reveal
- The BRAAAM = Sound design accent in AI video (Higgsfield audio prompts)
- Information Gap = "What is this?" curiosity in thumbnail/first frame
- Peak-End Rule = Final frame/title card must be the strongest impression
