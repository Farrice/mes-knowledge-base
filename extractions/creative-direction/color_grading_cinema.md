# Color Grading & Cinematic Color Research

## 10 Most Iconic Color Grades in Film (Filmworkz)
1. Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) — Hyper-saturated, graphic novel look. Desert warmth vs blue nights. Rotoscoped eyes for contrast. "Make it look like a graphic novel."
2. Blade Runner 2049 (2017) — Neon lights to orange smog. Yellow = information/enlightenment. Green = irony of life. Pink/purple = sensuality. Roger Deakins cinematography.
3. In the Mood for Love (2000) — Deep crimson and lush emerald. Wong Kar-wai + Christopher Doyle. Colors as characters. Period-specific 1960s Hong Kong.
4. Avatar (2009) — Bioluminescent blues, tropical greens vs brutalist grays. 70% CGI. UV-reactive paint.
5. Fantastic Mr. Fox (2009) — Wes Anderson's warm autumnal palette. Amber, ochre, burnt orange.
6. Vertigo (1958) — Green as obsession/death. Red as passion/danger. Hitchcock's color symbolism.
7. O Brother, Where Art Thou? (2000) — First major digitally color-graded film. Sepia-toned, dusty, Depression-era warmth.
8. The Matrix (1999) — Green tint for Matrix world. Blue tint for real world. Color as narrative device.
9. Amelie (2001) — Warm golden-green palette. Whimsical, fairy-tale warmth. Red as accent color.
10. Moonlight (2016) — Three-act color progression. Cool blues to warm purples. James Laxton cinematography.

## Common Cinematic Color Grade Styles
- Teal & Orange: Complementary colors, skin tones pop against cool backgrounds. Most popular Hollywood look.
- Bleach Bypass: Desaturated, high contrast, silver retained. Gritty, war film look (Saving Private Ryan).
- Cross-Processing: Unexpected color shifts, vintage, experimental.
- Day for Night: Blue-tinted, underexposed daytime footage simulating night.
- Sepia/Vintage: Warm brown tones, nostalgic, period feel.
- High Contrast B&W: Dramatic, noir, artistic (Sin City, Schindler's List).
- Neon/Cyberpunk: Saturated magentas, cyans, purples against dark backgrounds (Drive, Blade Runner).
- Pastel/Wes Anderson: Muted, controlled, symmetrical color blocking.
- Desaturated/Muted: Low saturation, realistic, documentary feel (Zero Dark Thirty).
- Warm Golden: Sunset warmth, nostalgia, romance (The Godfather).

## LUT (Look-Up Table) Basics
- LUTs are preset color transformations applied to footage
- Technical LUTs: Convert between color spaces (LOG to Rec.709)
- Creative LUTs: Apply aesthetic color grades
- Can be used as starting points, not final grades
- AI prompt equivalent: "color graded in the style of [film name]"

## Print Production Methods for Streetwear
- Screen Printing: Thick, durable, vibrant. Best for bold graphics, limited colors (1-6). Industry standard for streetwear.
- DTG (Direct to Garment): Digital inkjet onto fabric. Best for complex, multi-color designs. Photo-realistic.
- Sublimation: Dye infused into polyester. All-over prints, vibrant, no texture feel. Best for full-coverage designs.
- Heat Transfer/Vinyl: Cut vinyl pressed onto fabric. Best for simple logos, names, numbers.
- Embroidery: Thread stitched into fabric. Premium feel, dimensional, durable. Best for logos, small designs.
- Puff Print: Raised, 3D screen print. Tactile, premium, streetwear staple.
- Discharge Printing: Removes dye from fabric, replaces with new color. Soft hand feel, vintage look.
- Foil Printing: Metallic foil applied over adhesive print. Luxury, eye-catching.
- Flock Printing: Velvet-like raised texture. Premium, tactile.
- Plastisol: Standard screen print ink. Sits on top of fabric, vibrant, durable.
- Water-Based: Soaks into fabric. Softer hand feel, vintage look, breathable.
