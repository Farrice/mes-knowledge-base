# Creative Direction & Mood Board Methodology Research

## Creative Direction Process (from Wild Woman Haus + multiple sources)
1. Research & Discovery: Understand brand, audience, competitors, market position
2. Trend Forecasting: Identify current and anticipated design trends
3. Mood Board Creation: Visual exploration of direction, tone, aesthetic
4. Concept Development: Translate mood into specific design decisions
5. Guidelines & Specifications: Document rules for execution
6. Execution: Apply creative direction across all touchpoints
7. Evaluation & Revision: Annual review, audit, iterate

## Mood Board Methodology (from StudioBinder, Adobe, Figma)
### Purpose
- Communicate creative vision before production
- Align team/client on aesthetic direction
- Establish emotional tone and visual language
- Save time and money by getting alignment early

### Elements of a Mood Board
- Color palette swatches
- Typography samples
- Photography/image references
- Texture/material samples
- Pattern references
- Lighting references
- Composition/layout references
- Keywords/phrases for tone
- Film/video stills for motion reference

### Process
1. Define the brief (who, what, why, for whom)
2. Gather inspiration broadly (don't filter yet)
3. Curate ruthlessly (edit down to 8-15 images)
4. Arrange with intention (group by theme, create visual flow)
5. Add supporting elements (color, type, texture swatches)
6. Present and iterate (get feedback, refine)

### Pro Tips
- Create multiple mood boards for comparison
- Physical boards (corkboard, wall) allow tactile rearrangement
- Digital boards (Figma, Pinterest, Milanote) allow easy sharing
- Include "anti-mood board" showing what you DON'T want
- Poll your audience for validation

## Art Direction Framework (from Canva, SimplePlan Media)
1. Gather project information and creative brief
2. Research inspiration across multiple sources
3. Create mood board reflecting artistic direction
4. Define visual style guide (colors, fonts, imagery rules)
5. Develop creative concepts and present options
6. Execute and oversee production
7. Review, refine, and document for future use

## Typography Psychology
- Serif fonts: Traditional, trustworthy, authoritative, established (Times, Garamond, Baskerville)
- Sans-serif fonts: Modern, clean, approachable, minimal (Helvetica, Futura, Gotham)
- Script fonts: Elegant, personal, creative, feminine (Brush Script, Lobster)
- Display fonts: Bold, attention-grabbing, unique, expressive (Impact, Bebas Neue)
- Monospace fonts: Technical, retro, code-like, systematic (Courier, Fira Code)
- Slab serif fonts: Strong, confident, industrial, contemporary (Rockwell, Clarendon)
- Handwritten fonts: Casual, friendly, authentic, human (Comic Sans, Amatic SC)

## Typography Anatomy
- Baseline: Line where letters sit
- X-height: Height of lowercase 'x' (determines readability)
- Ascender: Part rising above x-height (b, d, h, l)
- Descender: Part dropping below baseline (g, p, q, y)
- Cap height: Height of uppercase letters
- Serif: Small decorative stroke at letter ends
- Counter: Enclosed/partially enclosed space (o, e, a)
- Kerning: Space between individual letter pairs
- Tracking: Uniform spacing across all letters
- Leading: Vertical space between lines of text
- Weight: Thickness of strokes (light, regular, bold, black)
- Width: How wide letters are (condensed, regular, extended)

## Font Pairing Rules
1. Contrast is key: Pair serif with sans-serif
2. Limit to 2-3 typefaces per project
3. Match x-heights for visual harmony
4. Use weight contrast (bold header + light body)
5. Consider historical context (don't mix eras randomly)
6. Establish clear hierarchy (display for headlines, body for text)
