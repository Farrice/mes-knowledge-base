**"Can I make Starbucks' most expensive drink for $1?"**

## 4. Progression Mechanism

**On-screen money counter.** Appears at 0:05 as **$0.00**, ticks up with every ingredient ($0.50 → $0.70 → $0.90 → $1.00), freezes at **$1.00** on the reveal. Lives bottom-left the entire video (0:05–0:40). The whole point of the cheapskate lens is *watching the dollar not get spent* — the counter is the progress bar that tells the viewer exactly how close the end is, and it doubles as the suspense ("is she gonna go over?").

---

## 5. Pacing Map

- **Fast (0:00–0:16):** hook, stakes, instant-coffee swap, Mom's dig — front-load the two strongest beats (the $1 reveal + the dollar-store coffee).
- **Medium (0:16–0:27):** the syrup fix and the milk pour. Steady build, counter climbing.
- **Slow (0:27–0:36):** deliberate deceleration. "Moment of truth," Mom lifts the cup, long silent sip, cut to Jenny's nervous face *before* showing the verdict. This is where engagement peaks — the held sip is the slow-mo.

---

## 6. Loop Design

**Seamless tail-into-hook.** The last frame (0:38–0:40) is Jenny holding up the giant $1-receipt cup — the *exact same cup and receipt* the video cold-opened on at 0:00. When the short loops, "...cost one dollar" flows straight back into "You've seen those hundred-dollar Starbucks drinks" over the identical visual, so the replay reads as continuous. The finished-drink cold open also carries it if the loop breaks (the finished giant drink is the peak visual up front). No flat-dead ending.

---

## 7. Delivery Note

Read every line like the money counter might tip over one dollar any second — quiet, fast, a little sneaky on the swaps ("*so* I'm using instant coffee"), then genuinely holding your breath on the sip. Never flat. The line "it tastes exactly like Starbucks" only lands if Mom sounds honestly surprised, not scripted.
