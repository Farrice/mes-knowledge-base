## Phase 1 — Idea Qualified

- **Question form:** "Can I make Starbucks' most expensive drink for $1?" — passes cleanly. It's an "Is it possible to ___?" gap a stranger wants resolved, and the price collision is a built-in **statement-under-the-question stack** (the drink is famously ~$100+ / the "most expensive" order; I'm doing it for a dollar). The gap gets *worse* the second you hear both numbers.
- **You-before-me:** The viewer's payoff (a cheat you can copy at the counter / at home) leads; my story trails as proof. The hack is the value — this is a *replicable-hack* idea, which is the rewatch-and-share ceiling (the $3-burrito lever).
- **Beat count:** ≤5 beats, one continuous kitchen moment (A→B→C). No split needed.
- **Challenge shrink:** Already the smallest resolvable problem — one drink, one dollar bill, ~10 real-time minutes at the counter/kitchen. Not a multi-day thing. Stays short-form.
- **Demand/supply + lens:** "Most expensive Starbucks drink" is a proven outlier topic; the **cheapskate lens + $0-budget-and-mom signature twist** is what converts the view to a subscribe. Avatar-fit: middle-schoolers who can't drop $100 at Starbucks but *can* recreate a hack in their kitchen — dead center.

### PROGRESSION MECHANISM
On-screen **money ticker** — `$1.00 left` → counts *down* with every ingredient (0:03 in, live through 0:37, freezes at the reveal). It doubles as the stakes: the viewer can see exactly how close I am to blowing the dollar, so the "end" is always visible. Matched to the challenge format; no slow-slope mystery about when it lands.

### PACING MAP
- **Fast [0:00–0:14]:** shock pour, price collision, ticker mechanic, first two pumps — front-loaded and dense.
- **Medium [0:14–0:26]:** mom's reaction beat, the pantry raid, the fifteen-cents-left constraint.
- **Slow [0:26–0:37]:** *the slow reveal* — deliberate stir, nervous face, mom leaning in, silent sip. This is the decelerated suspense zone; engagement peaks here because of the brake, not despite it.

### LOOP DESIGN
**Tail flows into the hook.** The final on-screen ticker (`$0.99`) and the toppling-whipped-cream drink in frame at 0:40 visually rhyme with the mid-pour drink at 0:00 — on replay, the last cup *becomes* the first cup, so it loops seamlessly. **Second-question backstop planted at 0:14:** mom's "that's not even coffee" seeds a *"wait — is it actually the same drink, or did she cheat the recipe?"* question that a viewer can only re-check by rewatching the pumps. Sub-30s-style rewatch trigger built in even at 40s.

### DELIVERY NOTE
Read every line like the price is about to be revealed *right now* — imminent-event energy, breath held on the fifteen-cents line, voice tightening into the sip. Never flat narration; even "one dollar, one drink" should sound like a countdown, not a caption.

### EDIT NOTES
- **0:00 (hook frame):** gen-fill / clean the background so nothing is "red" outside the drink and the dollar bill — the towering cup and the single bill are the only two focal objects. This frame is the thumbnail; QA it silent.
- **0:03:** the `$1.00 left` ticker must be legible on a TV screen (large, high-contrast) — a growing share of Shorts views land on TV; don't thumb-zone it.
- **0:08–0:14:** dual-narrative — audio talks about *cost* while the visual shows *pumps*, so sound and picture never say the same thing. ADR the "fourteen pumps / eighty cents" line clean over the fast-cut footage.
- **0:32–0:37:** ADR/lav the silent sip beat so the room tone sells "live"; let the faces do the work — no describing what we can already see.
- **0:37:** CTA card lands *before* the final word "ninety-nine." Nothing trails after the answer — cut hard on "nine."
