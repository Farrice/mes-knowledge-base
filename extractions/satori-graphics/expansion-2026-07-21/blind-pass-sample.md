## Three-Flow Spine — MyBPM Drop 001

### Essential Info
If they remember nothing else: **drops 08.01 · @mybpm**.

### The Spine
- **HOOK**: the tee itself, worn, shot low-angle against flat ground — bottom-right mass, largest element on the poster. Product brief → the product is the hook. Not a model's face: the face would win the fixation fight and this poster sells cloth, not a person. Crop at the collarbone.
- **SECONDARY**: "BPM" wordmark, upper-left, medium — the eye leaves the tee and climbs the diagonal to the mark.
- **FINISHER**: drop date + handle, small, bottom-left, sitting in the poster's one calm zone.

### Flow Line
Bottom-right (tee) → upper-left (mark) → bottom-left (date/handle). A diagonal climb, then a drop down the left edge — triangle path, no straight scan. The empty center-right is deliberate: it's the release zone the eye crosses between beats.

### Rent Test
- Background texture: PAYS — it's carrying a contrast job (see stack). 
- Secondary product flat-lay thumbnail: **EVICT** — second product = second journey. Week 1 sells one tee.
- Barcode/QR decoration: **EVICT** — pays no rent until there's a live shop link; decoration in week 1.

### Thumbnail Verdict
At 120px the tee reads as one dark mass, wordmark as one light mass, date zone as a quiet corner. PASS — no fix round needed because everything that competed got evicted, not quieted.

## Contrast Stack — MyBPM Drop 001

| Form | Zone | What it makes win |
|---|---|---|
| **Texture** | Concrete-grain ground vs. smooth cotton render of the tee | The tee — the only smooth surface on the poster, so the fabric reads as touchable |
| **Size** | Tee at ~55% canvas vs. wordmark at ~15% | Entry order: product first, brand second — nobody buys the logo |
| **Color** | Grayscale field + one saturated accent (the tee's print color) | The print IS the product's argument; it gets the only real color |
| **Psychology (advanced)** | Serif date-line in a streetwear layout that "should" be bold sans | The finisher — the one place the genre grammar breaks, so the date lodges |

**Advanced tier**: NORM — streetwear posters run condensed bold sans everywhere; the audience has seen a thousand of them this week. FLIP — the drop date set in a narrow serif, small, letterspaced. It reads like a gallery card in a gutter context. Service gate: it serves the message (the date is the conversion object and now it's the anomaly the eye files away); it transmits without narration (a date is a date); audience gate holds (the serif-in-streetwear move is an established high-fashion crossover code this audience already respects — validate against MyBPM's brand card before lock).

**Double-whammy**: the tee zone — smooth-vs-grain against the container, and the saturated print against the tee's own neutral cotton inside it.

**Restraint log**: no style contrast (photo + flat type is already two languages; a third would be noise); no emotion contrast (a drop poster has one feeling — want); shape contrast declined (the concrete grain would fight a geometric overlay).

**Audience gate**: the stack's net feeling is "hard, sparse, a little expensive" — correct for streetwear scarcity posture, wrong for a value brief. PASS for this audience.

**Zonal check**: center-right stays empty. Black tee shadow sits on the dark grain at the bottom edge — no contest there, on purpose.
