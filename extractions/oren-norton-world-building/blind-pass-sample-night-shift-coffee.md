## THE COUNTER SERVICE COFFEE CONCERN — Story page

**CHAPTER 1 — THE ROUTE**

Founded along U.S. Route 30 in the year of the interstate, the Counter Service Coffee Concern began as a supply operation for the establishments that never close. Its founder, one Dorothy "Dot" Malloy, a former switchboard supervisor with a doctorate in staying up, held one conviction above all others: that coffee is not a beverage but a shift schedule, and that no man or woman holding down the hours between midnight and six deserves anything less than a full pot.

Dot's first act of business was to certify, by hand, every diner on the route. Certification required three things: a counter you could put your elbows on, a cook who knew your order by your headlights, and coffee that arrived before you asked. Establishments meeting the standard received the Concern's seal — a chrome percolator rampant — and the right to advertise ONE PHRASE ONLY: "Refills are a right, not a favor."

**CHAPTER 2 — THE FORMULA**

The Concern's Night Formula was developed for the Route's truckers, nurses, printers, and dispatchers — the entire population of people awake on purpose. It was engineered to survive a thermos, a dashboard, and a double, and to taste like the moment the fry cook turns the radio down so you can talk.

It remains the only coffee formally banned from brunch.

**CHAPTER 3 — THE REVIVAL**

The Concern's route ledgers were feared lost until they turned up at a swap meet outside Toledo, purchased for four dollars by two night-shift ER nurses who recognized the percolator seal from a diner their grandmothers had been certified in. The Concern now operates from a former dispatch office, where it continues Dot's founding program: certifying the counters, honoring the refill, and keeping the lights on for the people who keep the lights on.

Applications for Route Certification are reviewed the first Tuesday of every month, at night.
