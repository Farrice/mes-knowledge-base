## THE OPENING

CHAPTER 1

Marcy Deaton had counted the money twice, and both times it had come out wrong.

Not short. Over. Eleven hundred dollars over, in a church collection account that on its best Sunday cleared nine hundred, and most Sundays didn't. She sat in the little office behind the sanctuary with the adding machine her predecessor had left behind in 1998 and ran the tape a third time, because at fifty-three, with a mortgage in its final year and a son she no longer spoke to, Marcy had learned that most problems in life were arithmetic problems, and arithmetic problems had the decency to stay solved.

The third tape agreed with the first two.

She looked at the deposit slips going back six weeks. The overage wasn't one mistake. It was a pattern, a hundred and change at a time, folded in with the loose bills where nobody would think to look for it. Somebody wasn't stealing from Calvary Baptist. Somebody was using it.

Her first call should have been to Pastor Riggs. Her hand was on the phone when she remembered the way he'd started locking his office in March, a man who for eleven years had never locked anything, who left his truck running outside the Food Lion. She put the receiver down. She was already building the case in her head the way her father the magistrate used to at the dinner table: opportunity, access, the recent change in behavior. It saddened her more than it scared her. She had known Dale Riggs since he was a boy.

The door opened behind her, and it was not Pastor Riggs.

It was the new organist, the quiet one, Wendell, who had come to them in the spring with his letter from a church in Ohio that, Marcy would learn much too late tonight, had burned down with its entire personnel file eight months before he mailed it.

"You found it," Wendell said, sounding relieved, and he shut the door with the soft click of a man who had thought about the door, and the lock, and the hour, well in advance. "I told them a bookkeeper counts. They never listen."

He sat down across from her and folded his hands, and the terrible thing, the thing that would keep Marcy from sleeping for many months of nights afterward, was how reasonable he made it all sound, and how the money, it turned out, was the least of what was hidden in that church.
