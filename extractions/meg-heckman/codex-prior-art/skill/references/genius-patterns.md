# Meg Heckman Buyer-Trigger Patterns

## Pattern 1: Self-Statement Product

**Move:** Translate a product from "what it is" into "what owning it lets the buyer say."

**Use when:** The product is useful, pretty, or clever but does not yet signal identity.

**Output test:** The buyer sentence starts with "I'm the kind of person who..." or "This is so me because..."

## Pattern 2: Recognition-Speed Gate

**Move:** Compress the joke, promise, or product hit until the right person gets it quickly.

**Use when:** The concept requires explanation, lore, or a long caption.

**Output test:** A niche insider can understand the hook at thumbnail, ad, first-scroll, or product-card speed.

## Pattern 3: Topic-To-Person Specificity

**Move:** Replace category labels with a vividly pictured buyer, situation, and private pain/laugh.

**Use when:** The idea targets "all moms," "all dancers," "all developers," "all EDM fans," or any broad group.

**Output test:** You can picture the person, their situation, and who they would send it to.

## Pattern 4: Future Reaction Design

**Move:** Design for the reaction the buyer wants after purchase.

**Use when:** The product looks good on a page but has no share, gift, comment, or public-use moment.

**Output test:** The product creates a plausible "that is so you" or "where did you get that?" moment.

## Pattern 5: Familiar/Twist Pairing

**Move:** Combine one familiar buyer-world element with one unexpected but true twist.

**Use when:** A concept is boring because it is too familiar or confusing because it is too strange.

**Output test:** The buyer decodes the familiar half instantly, then cares because of the twist.

## Pattern 6: Emotion-Then-Reason Purchase

**Move:** Name the feeling first, then supply the practical purchase reason second.

**Use when:** Copy leans on product specs, low price, or utility without emotional spark.

**Output test:** The buyer can feel the purchase before rationalizing it.

## Pattern 7: Conversation Starter Over Product Page

**Move:** Turn a static product into a portable conversation.

**Use when:** A product has no organic reason to be worn, gifted, shared, reposted, or mentioned.

**Output test:** The concept has a credible path through people, not only through ads.

## Pattern 8: Support Layer Discipline

**Move:** Let store, ad, email, and offer mechanics support the product feeling instead of trying to create it from scratch.

**Use when:** The funnel is carrying all the conversion burden.

**Output test:** The core product/design already makes the right buyer feel something before any funnel layer explains it.
