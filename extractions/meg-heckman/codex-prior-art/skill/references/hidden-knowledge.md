# Hidden Knowledge

## The Buyer Is The Medium

Meg's strongest transferable idea is that the buyer distributes the product by recognizing themselves and then imagining who else will recognize it. This applies to T-shirts, gifts, memes, offers, landing pages, lead magnets, service packages, and client-facing creative.

## Broad Appeal Is Often A Fear Response

Beginners make broad designs because broad feels safer to the creator. The buyer experiences that safety as blandness. The OS should ask what specific person becomes more visible, funny, smart, prepared, rebellious, competent, or understood by owning the thing.

## Cleverness Must Be Subordinate To Recognition

Creator cleverness can become a tax on the buyer. If the buyer has to admire the creator's mental move before they feel seen, the concept is probably upside down.

## Social Currency Is A Product Feature

For public or shareable products, "what will people say when they see this?" is not post-purchase fluff. It is part of the product.

## Emotion Is Not A Vibe Word

Emotion is the first conversion event. The reason comes later. The design, page, or offer has to create an affective yes before the buyer starts justifying the purchase.

## Specificity Needs Compression

Specificity does not mean adding details everywhere. It means selecting the detail that makes the person appear. One exact phrase can beat a paragraph of demographic traits.
