## The Seven Micro-Moments (genius patterns)

### 1. The Tattoo Test (design gate, pre-launch)
- **Behavior**: Before any design goes live: "Would our customer tattoo this on their body?" Not "is it pretty" — "would they look down and go, 'Yep, this design is me.'" Escalation ladder: "A bumper sticker says how you identify on your car. A t-shirt says it on your body, but a tattoo says it forever."
- **Executable**: Finish the sentence "My customer is the person who ___." If the design can't finish it, don't make it. ("Nobody tattoos a discount, but people will tattoo their identity.")
- **Proof shown**: a community seller's customer tattooed the brand's design (frame ~1:45). SHC bestsellers are behavioral confessions: "Bad Knees," "Pro Snacker," "Out of Breath."
- **Relation to existing OS**: this is the Identity Signal + mirror-vs-poster gate with a permanence escalation — fold in as sharpening, not a new trigger.

### 2. Reply to Every Ad Comment in Brand Voice
- **Behavior**: Answer every single comment under ads AS the brand — jokes, gifs, real conversation. Farmers-market frame: two stalls, same product; one vendor silent, one mid-conversation laughing — "you already know which line you are joining."
- **Mechanism**: "Every reply is written for one person and it is read by hundreds of thousands of strangers deciding if you are real." Bonus: engaged ads get platform-rewarded → cheaper CPMs.
- **Executable**: Define brand voice (spunky/zen/etc.), reply to every comment from the last week as the brand; ChatGPT/Claude in the brand voice makes it copy-paste cheap.
- **Frames**: real comment threads with SHC replies signed "-Eric" (~3:44).
- **Cited**: Sprout Social — "nearly half of shoppers actively want to buy from brands that respond on socials" (LIKELY, her citation).

### 3. Give Before You Ask (welcome email)
- **Behavior**: "The first email someone gets from you is a handshake. And most brands open that handshake with an invoice." SHC's welcome: hands the discount instantly (no games), talks like co-founder Eric actually talks, asks for nothing except a reply.
- **Why it's the leverage point**: welcome emails open at ~84% vs ~40% for everything else (GetResponse benchmark — LIKELY, her citation). "The one email everyone actually opens and most brands typically waste it."
- **Executable — the 3-line rewrite**: (1) deliver what you promised, (2) say hi like a human, (3) ask one small niche-specific question they'll actually want to answer. Works identically for subscriber #1 and #10,000.

### 4. Write Emails About Their Life, Not Your Product
- **Behavior**: The March email with no product in it: "Can we talk about the drive home after a hike?" — dead-silent first 5 minutes, jelly legs, the smell, windows down, McDonald's appearing on the highway after you swore you'd eat clean. Product enters only in the last line. Full email visible on screen (~7:45), signed Eric, Founder.
- **Principle**: "People read what feels like it is written for them. And they delete what feels written at them." It's a system, not a one-off: ranked everything that ruins a hike; "the lies every hiker tells" ("Yeah, I've been keeping up with my cardio, too"). "Week after week, it is about their world, not our shelf."
- **Precedent**: Trader Joe's Fearless Flyer since 1969 — a quirky magazine people WANT in their mailbox. Edelman: 81% need to trust a brand before buying (LIKELY, her citations).
- **Executable**: This week, write one email about a moment in the customer's life; product appears in the last line at most.

### 5. Let Customers Write the Products With You
- **Behavior**: "Need your help" email → a reply ("the plural of moose should be meese") → Eric builds the "Meese" tee → #3 bestseller. "A customer literally wrote our product in one sentence completely for free."
- **The mining system (email on screen ~10:20)**: subject "Need your help Megan?" — asks what belongs on a tee that doesn't exist yet ("Think: dumb phrases / something relatable / animals getting up to stuff. Doesn't need to be a finished idea"), promises: if yours makes it to the site, we send you one free. Plus standing signal: "we read your messages."
- **Mechanism**: IKEA effect — people fall in love with what they helped build. Lego Ideas: 10,000 votes → made → fan royalty (LIKELY, her citation).
- **Executable**: End the next email with one question. "When a reply makes you laugh out loud, build it," send that customer one free — "you just turned a customer into a co-founder." **"A cult brand isn't one that you follow. It is one that you've helped build."**
- **Context**: unprofitable first two months, "talking to a canyon" — replies were the free research that pulled them through. Testing period = calibration cost (matches existing hidden-knowledge #9).

### 6. Feature Your Customers, Not Your Products
- **Behavior**: "Sloths have been spotted" email (2x/year): real customers in tees on real trails. Whole message: "You wore them, we noticed. Tag us and you'll be featured next."
- **Principle**: "Your customer's photo is a review with a face." Nielsen: 92% trust recommendations from people over ads (LIKELY, her citation). GoPro's Million Dollar Challenge: 42,000 customer clips as the yearly ad (LIKELY). "You don't need 42,000 clips. You need one photo of one customer celebrated properly." Customers become the audience AND the marketing simultaneously.
- **Executable**: Message last 5 customers, ask for a photo with product; set review requests to reward photo/video; feature one this week (with permission + thank you).

### 7. Never Skip a Week (rhythm)
- **Behavior**: New designs drop weekly, zero missed weeks since launch — including weeks "the drop lands and nobody claps. But that doesn't matter. The drop happens anyways." Compounds: 250 live designs (claimed).
- **Principle**: "Trust is rhythm. A brand is like a heartbeat. Nobody notices it until it stops." A store dark for a month feels abandoned — "Would you put your credit card into an abandoned store?" This is the moment that can "quietly kill" the other six.
- **Anchor**: Costco's $1.50 hot dog, unchanged since 1985 — "one unbroken promise being worth more than any campaign" (founder's "I will kill you" quote shown on screen, ~13:25).
- **Executable**: Pick a day, calendar it, something ships every week on that day. "The size of the drop matters way less than the fact that it never misses."

## Hidden Knowledge

1. **The reply is the product pipeline** — email is framed as revenue (existing OS: "pre-paid traffic"), but she runs it as R&D: every reply is free research from the exact buyer; the ask-question→reply→product loop is how the catalog compounds without a creative team.
2. **Trust artifacts are written by a named human** — every on-screen email is signed "Eric, Founder"; comments answered "-Eric." The brand voice is a person's voice, not a persona document.
3. **One reader, mass audience** — replies and emails are written to ONE person and read by thousands deciding if you're real. The intimacy IS the broadcast strategy.
4. **The sequence is a trust ladder, not a list** — moments run in customer-lifecycle order: pre-product (design) → pre-purchase (comments) → post-optin (welcome) → relationship (story emails) → co-creation → celebration → rhythm. Each earns the right to the next.
5. **Consistency beats reception** — shipping the drop when "nobody claps" is the point; the audience watching you show up again and again is how a no-name brand becomes real.
6. **Free replaces famous** — all seven moments cost $0 and work at subscriber #1. The follower count objection is a category error: "They didn't bring an audience. They built one."
