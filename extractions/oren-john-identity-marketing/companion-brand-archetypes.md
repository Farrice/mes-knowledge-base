## 2. Named frameworks — THE FIVE CONTENT ARCHETYPES (flagship, new to this source)

Verbatim definitions + the operative question for each:

1. **The Oracle** [03:37] — *"best for brands who can teach people about the brand space... relies on a core expert or a team of several experts who can storytell."* Question: *"What does our customer need to understand about the world we inhabit that nobody else is explaining properly?"* Four content types: **historical explainers**, **category comparisons** ("is X or Y better for Z"), **why-things-are-the-way-they-are**, **predictions for the future**.
2. **The Performer** [10:10] — *"the content is a show. It's entertainment driven, personality first... built to be binged and shared rather than focused on selling. The product barely needs to be mentioned, it's often omnipresent throughout."* Question: *"If our brand had a show, what would it be? Would people watch it without knowing if it was ours?"*
3. **The World Builder** [13:08] — Oren's stated favorite. *"Building massive content that's interesting, far reaching and surprising, far beyond the world the brand inhabits."* Best for teams "willing to take a risk." Three content types: **short films**, **fictional character series**, **immersive intellectual content and curation**.
4. **The Catalyst** [15:31] — *"uses content to push people towards a better version of themselves. Your content is the bridge between who they are and who they want to be."* Three content types: **training/process documentation**, **inspirational/motivational text-overlay content**, **community highlights and challenges**.
5. **The Helper** [17:19] — *"brands that don't have a deep expert to put on camera, who want to lean on value content"* but it's *"practical," not motivational.* The explicit Catalyst-vs-Helper distinction: **"the catalyst is aspiring — there's a better life out there, it's shared because it's motivational. Whereas the helper is more practical."** Question: *"What are the little helpful things we can do that our customer will appreciate?"* Content types: **visual tutorials, carousel stories/light tips, story vlogs, interviews and curation**.

Closing diagnostic [20:53]: *"Do you have a full expert? Can you get just a creator? Can you be more helpful versus more aspirational? Do you want to take a real shot on goal and do some world building?"*

## 3. The resource-audit step (new mechanism, not in primary)

[02:24–03:37] Before picking an archetype, map: **who can be on camera**, **who can design** (internal/agency/budget-to-retain), **what's actually happening at the company that's filmable**. Oren's stated reason this matters: *"a lot of brands have an idea for some amazing thing they want to do on social, but rarely does anyone actually ever do the big idea... they're constrained."* This reframes archetype selection as constraint-first, not aspiration-first — pick the archetype your actual resources support, then build ambition inside it.

## 5. Prescriptive / operationalizable additions

- **Archetype-selection sequence**: pick archetype → resource-audit (talent/design/budget/filmable-reality) → brainstorm content list inside the chosen archetype → feed into an ongoing content-pillar system. Resource-audit-*before*-ambition is the key ordering insight.
- **The Catalyst/Helper distinction test**: is the content aspirational-and-shareable (Catalyst) or practical-and-relatable (Helper)? This is a sharper, two-question fork than anything in the primary source for diagnosing "helpful" content types.
- **Two-account method** (Kreiss): separate the founder-as-personality account from the brand-main account to avoid diluting either while still capturing personal-brand lift.
- **The "boring category" reframe**: for any category that feels too dull for content (industrial parts, manufacturing, B2B software), the fix is always the Oracle's "why things are the way they are" content type or the Catalyst's process-documentation — not a bigger budget.
- **Paid-organic bridge measurement** (Marpipe): informational creator content that never mentions the product directly still lowers CPA on paid — a concrete mechanism (not just an assertion) for why organic-first strategy pays off even for complex B2B products.
