## 2. Named frameworks / terms of art (new to this source)

- **Iconography as centerpiece** [02:59]: *"say you have iconography for your brand, it goes on your t-shirts etc, great, that's step one — but when you're able to permeate that into what you actually put inside the concepts of your campaigns, you're taking it to another level."* Distinct from and more granular than the primary's "signature designs" bucket — this is specifically about iconography *becoming the shoot concept itself* (hedges trimmed into a logo shape), not just appearing on the product.
- **"Cult of your logo" / logo worship** [04:49, 16:42]: *"are you building logo worship — are you making your logo a centerpiece that makes people want to have it on them?"* A named test the primary video doesn't have.
- **Lifestyle-adjacent** [07:11]: *"a lot of brands now whose lifestyle is basically being fashionable or being streetwear, but it was important to think about [Stussy] as adjacent to basically anything action related."* The word "adjacent" as the operative move — pick a lifestyle category wider than your product category.
- **The "story underneath" test for casting** [11:56–12:32]: *"it's not just hey I'm going to use my friends in my photos versus employing a real model — it's more of, is there a story that we can tell underneath that."* This sharpens the primary's "homie lookbook" into an actual selection criterion: cast people because of what they did/represent, not because of how they look. Concrete example: Brigade NYC designs a garment inspired by a specific friend's specific interesting activity, then builds a lookbook around that story.
- **The recurring-theme toolkit** [13:43–16:06]: three named, reusable *content signatures* distinct from visual signatures (colorway/pattern/tag from the primary): **surrealism** (an oddball mascot — the dinosaur), **urban action-and-people** (place/scene tells the story instead of a model), **the car/vehicle signature** (vehicles used as *framing devices* — through a car door, a truck bed — not as the subject of the photo).
- **The four-question personal playbook** [16:06–18:29] — Oren runs his own brand through it live:
  1. What is your iconography, and how can it last / scale (be fabricated big, become an archive)?
  2. Are you building logo worship?
  3. What lifestyles are you adjacent to? (his example: rodeo photography, ultra-car-enthusiast culture — not just "cars," but people who've spent time/money/identity on a specific build)
  4. What is your portrait philosophy — how are you capturing your people?
  Plus a fifth, unnumbered: what are your additional recurring themes (his own example: "heist/intelligent-crime" storytelling captions + a recurring horse motif).

## 4. Prescriptive / operationalizable additions

- **Iconography-first casting**: before choosing talent/location for a shoot, run the concept through "how could our iconography apply here" — this reverses the default brainstorm order (talent/location first, then branding).
- **Logo-worship audit**: ask literally whether your logo is something people *want on their body* — if not, that's a signal to invest in redesign/simplification before spending on campaigns.
- **Lifestyle-adjacency test**: define your category one level *wider* than the product (Stussy: not "streetwear," but "action sport"). Then source casting/collabs from that wider category.
- **"Story underneath" casting filter**: for any homie-lookbook-style casting decision, require an answer to "what did this person actually do that's interesting" — not just "do they look right."
- **Recurring-theme bank**: maintain 2–3 *non-serious* recurring visual bits (mascot, motif, running gag) alongside the serious brand work — gives a "flip side" that ages a brand's world without diluting the core.
- **Vehicle-as-frame, not subject**: if using cars/props as a recurring device, shoot *through* or *around* them rather than centering them — creates a repeatable visual signature with built-in intrigue.
- **Personal-playbook worksheet**: the five-question sequence (iconography/lastability → logo worship → lifestyle-adjacency → portrait philosophy → recurring themes) is directly reusable as a brand-audit worksheet — more concrete and sequenced than the primary video's "signature-design levers" list.
