## PRIORITY 1 — Stussy: the International Stüssy Tribe (IST)

**What actually happened.** Shawn Stussy began screen-printing his handwritten signature on tees ~1980 to promote his surfboard-shaping business (Laguna Beach, CA). Through the mid/late 1980s he and business partner Frank Sinatra Jr. (the accountant, not the singer) built the brand, and Shawn traveled internationally — meeting figures like Hiroshi Fujiwara, Nigo, Michael Kopelman, James Lebon, Goldie. From that network he assembled the **International Stüssy Tribe (IST)** — an invite-only crew of DJs, artists, designers, and shop owners representing key cities.

- **[VERIFIED]** IST was organized around ~5 anchor cities: **Los Angeles, New York, London, Tokyo, Paris.** NYC crew (1987) included Jules Gayton, [Andy] Mittleman, Ross, Kevin Williams, Jeremy Henderson; London crew (1989) included Michael Kopelman, Alex Turnbull, [James] Lebon, Fraser Cooke, Barnzley Armitage, Goldie. Source: [Reverie Page — The International Stüssy Tribe](https://reveriepage.com/blog/the-international-stussy-tribe-and-shawn-stussy-is-father-of-streetwear); [i-D](https://i-d.co/article/mark-lebon-international-stussy-tribe-london-bbq/).
- **[VERIFIED]** Members received **personalized varsity/tour jackets** with their name — the physical membership token. Source: Reverie Page (above).
- **[VERIFIED — art direction / "homie lookbook"]** Stüssy's imagery is overwhelmingly outdoor, in-motion, real-people-not-models: "99% of the photos are outside… catching some rays." Recall card [b1b32def "100 Art Direction Ideas"] (an Oren John video) — corroborates the "shoot your friends, not models" mechanism from a second Oren source.
- **[LIKELY]** The "friend-group ambassador / seed the right-fit micro-influencer in skate/surf/music" model is a fair characterization of IST: it was a curated peer network of culturally-credible insiders, not a paid mass-influencer program. This is well-supported by the member list (DJs/artists/shop owners) but the specific "ambassador" framing is Oren's gloss, not a term Stüssy used.

**Mechanism:** Top-down, *surgical* tribe engineering — hand-pick culturally-authoritative nodes in each city, give them a physical badge (the jacket) and creative latitude, let city chapters localize. The product becomes a recognition token between insiders. This is the canonical "pride in a subculture" + "belonging" archetype fused with **third-space hosting** (the crew *is* the congregation).

**The avatar-split ad-testing story (brand-team/pros vs customer look-alikes vs younger aspirational; ~50/50 "see-myself vs see-who-I-want-to-be"; route split audiences to different landing pages).**
- **[UNCONFIRMED externally / LIKELY as practitioner account]** This specific finding — that testing three avatar tiers surfaced a ~50/50 split between customers wanting to see *themselves* vs. a *younger aspirational self*, and that Lift routed the two audiences to different landing pages — appears **only** in Oren's own telling. Treat the *mechanism* as credible (Oren was the operator, so it's a firsthand claim) but treat the **"~50/50" figure and the "different landing pages" tactic as unverifiable specifics** — do not cite the number as fact.
- **[UNCONFIRMED] "Vervont" agency.** No verifiable web presence found for an agency named "Vervont" tied to Lift Foils. Likely a caption-mangled or small/defunct shop name. Do not assert the agency name. If a workflow needs it, label it "an agency Oren worked with / possibly mis-transcribed."

**Mechanism:** "Identity beyond affiliation" — the eFoil resolves an *internal* identity weak-point (aging athletic men: "I still think I'm 22"). Brees-on-Ellen worked because of *what it solved* (competence, vitality, play), not celebrity gloss. The avatar-split insight — customers bifurcate into "mirror me" vs. "show me who I want to become" — is the video's sharpest tactical idea and worth extracting *as a hypothesis-to-test*, not a proven benchmark.

## 1,000 True Fans (Kevin Kelly) — canonical math

- **[VERIFIED]** Kevin Kelly (founding executive editor of *Wired*) published **"1,000 True Fans" in March 2008** on his blog *The Technium*. Source: [kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans](https://kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/).
- **[VERIFIED] The math:** a "true fan" = someone who **buys anything you produce.** If you can net **~$100 profit per true fan per year**, then **1,000 true fans = $100,000/year.** Two load-bearing conditions Kelly states: (1) you must **create enough each year** that a fan can spend ~$100; (2) you must have a **direct relationship** (fans pay you directly) so you keep the full amount rather than an intermediary's small cut.
- **[LIKELY — caveat]** Modern critiques note the real per-fan figure is often lower after platform fees / that "buy anything" fandom is rarer than assumed ([Dave Karpf](https://davekarpf.substack.com/p/the-hollow-core-of-kevin-kellys-thousand)). Bind Oren's identity mechanics to the *principle* (depth of affiliation > breadth of reach), and flag the $100 as an illustrative unit, not a guarantee.

**Binding note for the OS:** Oren's five archetypes + "identity beyond affiliation" are the *why* a fan becomes a "true fan"; Kelly's essay is the *unit economics* of that depth. The clean join: identity mechanics manufacture true fans; 1,000-True-Fans prices them.
