# Behavior Proof Fixture B — Zero-Data Founder

> Synthetic evaluation fixture. It tests whether the system refuses to fabricate an ICP.

## Input

**Idea:** Clinic Ops Signal, a fictional AI intake assistant for independent physical-therapy clinics.  
**Founder belief:** “Owner-operated clinics are overwhelmed by leads and need AI follow-up.”  
**Evidence:** founder spoke with two friends who own unrelated local businesses; no clinic customers, interviews, or workflow data.  
**Constraint:** public research is allowed; outreach is not authorized.

## Naive baseline

“ICP: PT clinic owners with 5–25 employees and $1M–$5M revenue. Pain points: missed calls, slow follow-up, staff burnout. Position as a 24/7 AI receptionist.”

## Jordan-system output

### Current belief ledger

| Belief | Evidence | State |
|---|---|---|
| Independent PT clinics lose meaningful revenue from unworked inquiries | None provided | HYPOTHESIS |
| Owners personally feel the problem | None provided | HYPOTHESIS |
| AI follow-up is acceptable in this care context | None provided | UNKNOWN |
| 5–25 employees and $1M–$5M revenue predict the problem | None provided | REJECT AS UNSOURCED PROXY |

### Provisional known-good cases

Research three to five clinics that visibly show at least two of these public traces:

1. multiple locations or many therapist profiles;
2. paid-search landing pages or active lead-generation forms;
3. public complaints mentioning calls, scheduling, or delayed response;
4. after-hours inquiry routes without visible instant scheduling;
5. open front-desk/patient-coordinator roles.

These traces identify cases worth investigating; they do not prove lost revenue or buying intent.

### Five-Cs research matrix

| Lens | Question | Public evidence route | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer | What happened after the last inquiry? | Reviews and later permissioned interviews | Past-behavior sequence |
| Company | Which operating conditions create response delay? | Locations, hours, staffing roles, intake path | Candidate indicators |
| Category | What privacy/care constraints affect automated follow-up? | Authoritative regulatory and professional sources | Non-negotiable bounds |
| Competition | What do scheduling, answering, and intake alternatives promise? | Vendor sites and clinic workflows | Status quo/alternative map |
| Context | When does an inquiry become urgent or decay? | Interview evidence required | UNKNOWN until direct research |

### Interview guide — pending authorization

1. Walk me through the last new-patient inquiry that did not book.
2. What happened from first contact to the point it stopped?
3. Who touched it, and what else were they doing?
4. What have you tried to change that sequence?
5. Which inquiries should never receive automated follow-up, and why?
6. What would make you trust or reject an automated step?

### Prediction-saturation stop rule

Before each new case, predict the trigger, workflow break, current workaround, consequence, and objection. Stop only after predictions hold across at least three meaningfully different clinic contexts and new cases no longer alter a qualification or safety rule. The number is a diversity floor, not proof by quota.

### Verdict

`PROVISIONAL — NO ICP YET.` The next safe action is public known-good research. Interviews and any contact require separate authorization.

## Behavior delta

The baseline invented firmographics, pains, and positioning. The system protected the unknown state, identified observable traces, added category safety research, created a past-behavior interview design, and stopped before outreach or an unsupported ICP.
