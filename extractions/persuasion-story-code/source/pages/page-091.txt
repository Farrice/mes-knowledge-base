Most businesses and most sales are really about solving problems—if
you can stretch your mind a little to redefine not having what you want as
a problem. Buy that thing or learn something to get what you want, and,
shazaam! You've solved that problem.
And, the best-known and most commonly used persuasion stories are
problem/solution stories. By now you know there are many other types
to add dimension, appeal and effectiveness to your persuasion efforts.
But it's important to also include the "old standby" as we wrap up our
list of types of persuasion stories.
Think about what you see in TV commercials, hear from salespeople,
and read on websites. When there is an effort to persuade, most of the
time it's a problem/solution story. Weight-loss ads are a well-known
example. Someone was overweight (problem). Then they did something
that the advertiser would like you to buy (solution). And, wouldn't you
know it-they lost weight (result)!
In many online video sales letters, you'll find an epic story where
someone had a problem, they couldn't solve it, they happened upon a
strange and rare solution, they tried the solution, and, shazaam! The
problem is solved.
This includes elements of a Discovery Story, but at its core, it, too, is a
problem-solution story.
Here's one now for you:
Kim was having trouble closing sales with the pitch her sales
manager gave her. Customers would patiently wait until what she
had to say was over, then politely decline. She didn't understand
why, because she had a good product and she knew the product
inside-out.
