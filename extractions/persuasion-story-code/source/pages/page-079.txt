Here are four of the most effective Stories That Explain:
STORIES THAT SHOW HOW TO USE A PRODUCT
So one of the questions looming in the mind of anyone thinking about
buying something is so simple that many persuaders overlook it entirely:
"How does this work, and how can I use it?"
You may know the answer to this question so well that it may never
occur to you that your prospect doesn't know the answer at all.
But, think about it. If your prospect doesn't know, then all the big claims
you may be making could ring hollow and have no meaning. You believe
the claims are true. But your prospect is in the dark. And if you think
your prospect will figure this out on their own, you'll be wrong more
often than you'll be right.
That's where a Story That Shows How To Use A Product comes in.
This kind of story is not a mini-instruction manual. (That would be the
kind of "educating" I was warned against in my sales training.)
Instead of being a set of instructions, it's a narrative about how someone
else used the product to get results. It touches on some of the key points,
but does not need to spell out in hands-on detail how the results
occurred.
Your main goal with one of these stories is to create a realistic guided
visualization of your product in use (experience), ending in success
(results). This kind of story tells about a user of your product who your
prospect can identify with. As they hear or read your story, they start to
imagine themself going through the same experience.
Here is such a story from a 1921 ad written by copywriter (and later,
author) Victor Schwab. The story is about a secretary who took a course
and ended up becoming a professional writer of fiction. The headline
was very intriguing at the time:
How I Made $350.00 On One Short Story
And How I Learned to Write Stories That
Actually Sell Themselves.
