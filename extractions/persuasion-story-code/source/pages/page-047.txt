This story could be used in selling a supplement to get rid of heartburn:
No matter how bad your heartburn is today, I've been there. I
know what it's like walking on eggshells every time you are about
to eat certain foods. I even know-and I hope this isn't you, but if
it is, I've been there, too—what it's like to wait for the other shoe
to drop every time you eat something. I also understand the
frustration of not finding anything that stops the burning spikes of
indigestion, since I spent a lot of time trying things that didn't
work myself.
This story gives the prospect a lot of reasons to nod their head. It's very
visual, sensory and emotional: "walking on eggshells," "waiting for the
other shoes to drop," "the burning spikes of indigestion."
As the saying goes, misery loves company. And a person in pain will
instinctively trust another person who has gone through the same pain,
regardless of the other person's formal credentials.
The only real difference between a story about your prospect's pain and
the pain you know personally is the obvious one: This kind of story
comes from your experience.
Like the other story about pain, this story works well to set up the
presentation of a solution.
STORIES ABOUT PRESSURES FROM OTHER PEOPLE
When we tell stories, it's important to remember that most people care a
lot about what others think and say about them. Your prospects usually
like to see themselves as independent-minded and living by their own
lights. But reality is different.
