How To Create a I-couldn't-find-what-I-was-looking-for-so-I-created-
it-myself Story
Let's start by admitting that creating something new-anything new,
really—is usually far more complicated and detailed than what you want
to put in a story. For example: In a 2015 interview in Wired magazine,
AeroPress inventor Alan Adler revealed he tried 40 iterations before he
came up with the one that worked to his satisfaction. The Beatles did 102
takes of "Sexy Sadie" on The White Album before they got it right,
according to FarOutMagazine in the UK. Bestselling author Stephen King
rewrites every book multiple times before publishing it.
None of these facts make for persuasive marketing copy, though. Not for
most prospective buyers.
And there are many more nitty-gritty details that might be interesting to
some people, but probably not your prospects.
What's pretty much universally fascinating is the story of search...
frustration... and decision to come up with something new. And, the
process of creating it, with most of the gory or mind-numbing details left
out. Plus, of course, what you came up with!
So to write your invention story, start this way:
Make some notes about what you were frustrated with, and why.
Perhaps how you searched high and low, only to come up empty-
handed. When was the moment, and/or, what was the goal, that
prompted you to create something new? What had to be different about
it? What did you end up with? Why is it better than anything else out
there?
Get it all out on paper (or on your computer) before you put together a
story with this outline, where you'll choose only the most essential (and
exciting) facts:
1. What was the worst part of the way things were, regarding the
problem you eventually set out to solve?
What happened that made you decide to create something new?
3. What were the highlights of your invention / creation process?
4. What did you end up with, and what is its / are its / most
important unique benefit/benefits?
