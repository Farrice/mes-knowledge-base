STORIES ABOUT YOUR PROSPECT'S PAIN
When you are selling a product that relieves pain, you get instant
credibility with your prospect by describing that pain in a story the
prospect takes in as realistic and knowledgeable.
People are naturally suspicious when you promise you have a solution
that works, especially after they've tried other solutions that didn't work
for them before.
Again, remember, story like this does not promise the solution. Instead, it
makes the prospect much more receptive to a solution you will offer later,
because it shows you know what they are experiencing and how they
feel about it. Get this right, and you've gone a long way to dissolve
resistance.
Here's a story about what it's like to suffer joint pain. This could be part
of a larger sales message for a supplement to provide relief from joint
pain.
After a while, every morning you hate to get up. Not because
you're sleepy. You're well-rested enough. The problem is the pain
in your knees. You don't feel it much at first, but after that first
cup of coffee, every time you move your legs, it's like sandpaper is
grinding against your bones, underneath your skin.
Notice how this example moves, point-by-point, through time. At first,
it's just general and global: "every morning." But then it zeroes in. The
feeling is hardly noticeable at first. Then you drink a cup of coffee. And
after that, every time you move, you feel the agonizing pain.
This works in a couple of ways. First, obviously, if you have this kind of
joint pain yourself, the story brings it into sharp focus. But just as
important is the progression of time. The short sequence of events—
waking up, having a cup of coffee, and finally, feeling the pain full-
throttle—makes it more like real life.
