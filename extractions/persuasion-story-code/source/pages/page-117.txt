CHAPTER 10
HOW TO ADD POWER TO YOUR STORY
Until now, we've been focused on persuasion stories. I wanted you to see
how these types of stories are different in important ways from hero's
journey-type fictional stories—or the random recounting of life's events
that make up so many of the casual stories we hear every day.
Our focus on persuasion stories was not meant to disparage these other
kinds of stories. They are also important, and valuable. They serve a
purpose. And, let's face it—the entertainment stories are enjoyable. Since I
started writing this book, I've been watching a lot more movies and
series on Netflix, Amazon Prime Video, and Hulu. I could try to bluff
and say it was merely for "research purposes," but that's not true. In fact,
it was a lot of fun!
We've focused just on persuasion stories up until now in this book
because there are so many kinds, they're not all well known or fully
understood, and there are exact ways to use them. I don't know of
anyone else who has talked about those things this comprehensively
before. It's important to know what these stories are and how to put
them together, to become better at persuasion. It's also important to see
how they are different from other types of stories.
And there's a big payoff for knowing all this. Once you are familiar with
these basics, you know just how to get your point across and move your
persuasion efforts forward, story by story, when the time comes.
