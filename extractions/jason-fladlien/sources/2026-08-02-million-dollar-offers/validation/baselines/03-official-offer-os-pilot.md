# Clean-Room Baseline Decision Packet

## Verdict

**PRESERVE the core offer. PATCH one responsibility boundary before the next two-run internal pilot.**

The smallest defensible change is:

> Move diagnostic work from the offer owner to the operator. Retire the 14-question worksheet as required homework, reuse its prompts as the operator’s guide inside the existing 45-minute call, and make the existing recommendation plus `REBUILD / PRESERVE / HOLD` decision record the first item in the existing brief and review.

This is one coherent change: the owner supplies facts and remains accountable for the final decision; the operator owns classification, prioritization, and recommendation.

It does not rebuild the offer, add a deliverable, add a meeting, shorten the brief, add software, or claim improved results.

## Evidence Basis

- One of three owners abandoned the worksheet after question six and questioned whether they were answering correctly or creating extra work. This directly supports removing owner-authored diagnostic homework.
- One owner completed the worksheet and review but recorded no decision because the decision sequence was unclear. This supports placing the already-existing recommendation and decision record first.
- One owner completed the process and recorded `PRESERVE`. The core service can therefore produce its intended outcome; a rebuild is not supported.
- Three uncontrolled internal runs cannot establish causality, efficiency, commercial demand, or external performance.
- The pre-expansion system favors removing one demonstrated weight and avoiding asset overbuild before a proof loop.
- The pre-expansion incomparability workflows require competitor and pricing evidence that this fixture does not contain. Their redesign logic is therefore unavailable here.

## Pilot Variant

### Before

The owner completes a 14-question worksheet requiring them to:

- Classify 12 offer elements.
- Score six risks.
- Write three strongest objections.
- Choose a target promise.
- Identify every supporting asset.

They then attend the 45-minute call and later interpret an 18-page brief and supporting materials to determine what to decide first.

### For the next two runs

1. No required diagnostic worksheet is completed beforehand.
2. The operator uses the existing 14 prompts as an internal interview guide during the existing 45-minute call.
3. The owner supplies only available facts about the current offer, intended buyer, price, proof, delivery, constraints, and fixed elements. Missing facts are recorded as unknown rather than guessed.
4. The operator performs the classification, risk assessment, objection synthesis, and recommendation.
5. The existing 18-page brief begins with:

   - Operator recommendation: `REBUILD`, `PRESERVE`, or `HOLD`.
   - Owner’s one required final decision: `REBUILD`, `PRESERVE`, or `HOLD`.
   - Rationale and existing support materials afterward.

6. The existing 60-minute review opens with that decision and then examines the rationale.
7. No additional meeting, software, dependency, asset, or external action is introduced.

## Updated Internal Offer Description

> One accountable offer owner brings the available facts about an existing offer to one 45-minute operator-led interview, with no diagnostic homework. Within two business days, the owner receives one 18-page Offer Decision Brief whose first item is an operator recommendation and one recorded `REBUILD / PRESERVE / HOLD` decision, followed by the existing 60-minute review, spreadsheet scorecard, and 22-example library.

This is a pilot description, not an externally approved offer or claim.

## Two-Run Pilot Decision Rule

Record only internal feasibility evidence:

- Were the required factual categories captured or explicitly marked unknown within the existing call?
- Was an operator recommendation produced within the existing two-business-day delivery window?
- Did the owner record exactly one final decision by the end of the existing review?
- Was any extra meeting, homework, software, or dependency required?
- What actual operator time was required?

**Advance for further internal testing only if both runs produce a recorded final decision without added meetings, required homework, software, or invented inputs.**

If either run fails, mark the variant `HOLD` and identify the first failed stage. Do not respond by adding examples, software, or meetings without new evidence.

Even a two-for-two result would show only internal process feasibility. It would not establish external demand, pricing, conversion, revenue, capacity, or outcome improvement.
