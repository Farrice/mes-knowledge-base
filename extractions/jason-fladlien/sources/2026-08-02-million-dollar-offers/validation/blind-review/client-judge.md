## Material Decision Deltas

- **Weekly execution:** Both establish three foundational days as core and two trigger days as conditional. Orchid additionally defines when scheduling happens, how constraints affect it, and who activates trigger days.
- **First tangible value:** Orchid defines a truthful process milestone—the individualized plan exists and the core days are scheduled—while clearly stating that this proves no physical result. Cobalt supplies a sequence but does not make the milestone equally explicit.
- **Commercial unknowns:** Both correctly refuse to infer total price, charge count, or policies. Orchid converts these unknowns into a clearer owner-confirmation hold; Cobalt more usefully preserves intake as a safe non-checkout action.
- **Cross-burden discipline:** Orchid explicitly recognizes that scheduling adds a small kickoff action while reducing repeated interpretation. Cobalt identifies the reduced complexity but does not examine the introduced burden as fully.
- **Owner decisions:** Orchid's accept/offset/hold/reject decisions are more directly executable.

## Worse Decisions or Unnecessary Changes

- Orchid's blanket “no acquisition or copy handoff” is broader than necessary. A clearly non-purchase, intake-only presentation could potentially proceed if the unreviewed outcome claim were omitted. This is conservative, but not a material offer regression.
- Orchid's calendar-scheduling step adds a small procedure not explicitly stated in the fixture. It fits the existing individualized-plan and kickoff delivery, but operator ownership should still be confirmed.
- Cobalt's claim that the change lowers “actual default complexity” overstates the delta slightly; the source already calls the extra days optional or targeted.
- Cobalt's no-bonus/no-scarcity decisions correctly preserve locks, but they are protective refusals rather than material offer improvements.
- Calling Cobalt's draft “buyer-facing” while it contains an outcome still awaiting evidence review creates avoidable handling ambiguity.
