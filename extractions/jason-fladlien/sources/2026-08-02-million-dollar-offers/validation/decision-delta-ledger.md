# Offer TERMS — Decision-Delta Ledger

## Evaluation Question

Does the TERMS expansion make materially better, source-traceable, evidence-respecting offer decisions than the pre-expansion Jason system on the same frozen inputs?

The test rewards decisions, preservation, and useful restraint. It does not reward longer packets, framework vocabulary, source IDs, tables, or writing polish.

## Isolation Design

- Three frozen fixtures: personal, client, and official/internal.
- Three pre-expansion baselines generated from commit `6cf0bf6a3964679161924978632d7d4a35faa6a7` plus the matching fixture.
- Three enhanced runs generated from the fixture and the bounded installed TERMS path.
- Every generation agent launched with context fork `none` and was instructed not to read its paired output.
- Three separate blind judges saw only one fixture and two anonymized candidates.
- Candidate mappings were withheld until verdicts returned.
- Exact model identifiers and platform-level file-read transcripts were not exposed; the allowed-read boundary is supported by fork settings, task instructions, fixture hashes, and each agent's returned read list.

## Blind-Judge Decision Deltas

### Personal

The enhanced packet won because it made three consequential decisions the baseline left open:

1. The existing 90-minute audit produces a documented sprint fit/no-fit decision without inventing new duration or scope.
2. The first tangible win becomes a dated approval milestone before later sprint work.
3. Undefined guarantee language cannot be used publicly until the owner defines eligibility and “qualified conversation.”

The judge also identified real cautions: the fit/no-fit criteria still need owner definition, the dated milestone can create pressure, and the packet inferred buyer friction from an absence of timing evidence. Those risks were contained and did not materially regress the approved offer spine.

### Client

The enhanced packet won because it turned an already sensible three-day core into a more executable first week:

1. Core sessions are placed around the client's actual calendar during kickoff.
2. Trigger days stay inactive until a named owner activates them.
3. The individualized plan plus scheduled core days becomes a truthful process milestone that explicitly proves no physical outcome.
4. Missing commercial terms become explicit owner-confirmation holds.

The judge found two cautions: scheduling adds a small operator procedure not expressly supplied in the fixture, and the blanket hold on acquisition/copy handoff was broader than necessary. Neither was a material regression.

### Official

Both systems chose the same material pilot repair. The baseline was better at labeling it `PRESERVE core + PATCH` and supplied a crisper two-run advance/hold rule. The enhanced packet was better at surfacing operator-capacity and premature-decision risks and at protecting an informed choice.

These strengths cross-cancelled. The enhanced `REBUILD` label overstated a bounded process patch, while its held re-entry message and in-call repeat-back did not create material additional value. This fixture is a tie and must not be counted as a TERMS win.

## Calibration Rule Learned From the Tie

If the Revenue Offer Agent and TERMS converge on the same accepted changes, prefer the narrower change classification. Framework coverage, trace density, and additional held ideas do not count as material value. A bounded intake and sequencing repair remains a `PATCH` unless it changes the offer's core outcome, audience, scope, delivery model, or commercial architecture.

A sealed post-judge replay applied that rule to the unchanged official fixture and returned `PATCH`, two accepted changes, and no third proposal. This verifies the targeted calibration behavior without revising the original blind tie or the `2/3` comparative verdict.
