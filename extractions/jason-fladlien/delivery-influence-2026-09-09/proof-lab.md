## 3. Live sales / workshop invitation

Input: “My approach can completely change your business. Are you ready to take things to the next level?”

Scenario: a hypothetical copywriting workshop that actually includes a live section rewrite; price and commercial terms are not yet supplied. This is a teaching-to-offer transition, not a complete sales close.

Diagnosis: the promise exceeds the specified deliverable and the question cannot help someone evaluate fit.

Mechanics: D12 gives a usable free step and bounded paid scope; D07 connects the service to demonstrated work; D15 asks a real permission question; D17 ends with a clear action.

### Spoken module

“Before we talk about working together, take one sentence from your page and underline the result it promises.

“If it says something like ‘transform your marketing,’ put that sentence next to a blank line. On the blank line, write what the customer will actually leave with. Keep the scope small enough that you can show the work.

“That's the exercise. You can use it on the rest of the page yourself.

“If you'd like help doing it, the session we're discussing is for rewriting one section with the proof you already have. We would agree on the section and the terms first. A rewrite can't give you evidence you don't have.

“Would it be useful to look at the section you have in mind?”

### Observable branches

- Specific question: answer it using the supplied section and proof; if unanswered by available evidence, say what remains unknown.
- Confusion: ask which phrase needs clarification; show one input/output example again.
- Silence: allow space, then offer to leave the exercise with them. Silence is not evidence of fear.
- No: “Understood. You have the exercise if you want to use it.” End the invitation.
- Time short: omit optional examples; preserve the free exercise and the next-step invitation.
- Technical interruption: “The screen share stopped. I'll read the two sentences, then send them through the channel we've agreed on.” Sending still requires authorization.

Core ending: “Start with that one sentence. Make the result concrete enough to inspect.”

Optional extension: “If you have two more minutes, we can look at a second example.” Only proceed after agreement; end by returning to the same usable exercise.

Behavior delta: a vague transformation pitch becomes an inspectable exercise and a bounded invitation. Declining remains a valid outcome.

Proof gap: the service's real terms and delivery capacity are not provided. A public commercial version must use approved facts. Conversion: UNTESTED.
