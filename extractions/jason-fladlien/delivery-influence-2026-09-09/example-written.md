Jason Fladlien gets to the offer lesson before he gets to his biography.

Then, a few minutes later, he does something easy to miss. He reminds the room that the lesson applies to a VSL or an offer document too. Some of them don't run webinars. He gives them a reason to keep listening.

That's useful when you're writing about something you know well. The connection feels obvious to you because you already know where the point is going. Your reader may still be deciding whether it applies to them.

Take a line like “Make a smaller promise.” Fine advice. But the reader still has to figure out what to change.

For a copywriting service, that could mean replacing “Transform your marketing” with “Bring one landing-page section. We'll rewrite it together, using the proof you already have.”

Now there's something to evaluate. They know what to bring, what gets worked on, and what they'll leave with.

Before you add another benefit to your next draft, find the first place the reader has to make that connection alone. Write the example there.
