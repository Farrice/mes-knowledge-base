“Transform your marketing.”

Okay. What am I getting?

A rewritten page? A strategy? Someone to finally tell me which part needs fixing?

I can read that promise three times and still have to guess.

Jason Fladlien talks about finding the smallest promise that would excite someone enough to buy. I like that because it forces you to get specific about what you can actually do for them.

Say you're offering a copywriting session.

“Bring the section of your landing page you're stuck on. We'll rewrite it together, using the proof you already have.”

Now I can picture it. I know what to bring. I know what we're working on. And I know I'll leave with a rewritten section.

That might feel too small for everything you know how to do. There’s a temptation to squeeze all your expertise into the promise so nobody misses how much you bring.

But I'm still on the other side of the page trying to figure out what I'm paying for.

Give me that first.

Before you polish the promise, write what I'll have in my hands when you're done.
