# Buyer Psychology Decision Intelligence — Behavior Proof Lab

> **Portable simulated fixture evidence, not canonical production proof.** These examples were authored in the comparison workspace. They support replay design only; current canonical validation is reported in `canonical-deployment-receipt.md`.

## Verdict

**COMPONENT TESTED / PRODUCTION PROMOTION UNTESTED**

These are `SIMULATED_FIXTURE` comparisons. They test whether the component changes the right behavior while preserving facts, voice, options, uncertainty, and native ownership. They are not customer reactions, conversion evidence, publishability proof, or market outcomes.

Source anchors: [seed video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jbPNjNtQqk0), [choice-overload meta-analysis](https://scheibehenne.com/ScheibehenneGreifenederTodd2010.pdf), and the local corpus index beside this file.

## Evaluation Method

For each positive fixture:

1. freeze the same input and preservation lock;
2. produce a competent baseline without the overlay;
3. diagnose one primary decision and no more than one support decision;
4. produce a treatment through the native owner;
5. name the exact behavior delta as a hypothesis;
6. record the proof gap and next gate.

Negative controls must produce no overlay questions, changes, blocks, or factual dilution.

## P1 — Copy: Landing-Page Hero

### Frozen input

- Offer: private AI member-operations install for a fitness studio.
- Function: reviews incoming member communications and overdue follow-ups, then produces a daily staff action list.
- Setup fee: `$750`.
- Delivery: `14 days`.
- No verified revenue, retention, or time-saved claim.
- CTA: book a 20-minute fit call.
- Voice: direct, calm, useful.

### Preservation lock

Keep the offer function, `$750` fee, `14-day` delivery, CTA, quiet voice, and absence of outcome claims. Do not invent buyer research, staff pain, proof, urgency, or a guarantee.

### Baseline

> **AI Member Operations for Fitness Studios**
> We review incoming member communications and overdue follow-ups, then give your staff a daily action list. Private setup is $750 and takes 14 days. Book a 20-minute fit call.

### Weakness

The copy is accurate but gives the reader four equal facts before clarifying the operating change. It does not distinguish the offer from another reporting tool.

### Selected decisions

- Primary: **Focus** — the receiver must grasp one operating transformation.
- Support: **Congruence** — “member operations” should feel like an action system, not a dashboard claim.

### Source mechanic

`jbPNjNtQqk0` `03:54` (short named roadmap) and `21:52` (cross-surface congruence). Evidence: `SOURCE-OBSERVED`; the exact treatment is `OPERATIONAL-SYNTHESIS`.

### Treatment by /copy-engine

> **Know which member needs a reply today.**
> This private install reviews incoming member messages and overdue follow-ups, then turns them into one daily staff action list. Setup is $750. Delivery takes 14 days. Book a 20-minute fit call to see whether it fits your studio.

### Behavior delta

The first line now names the decision the staff receives, and the second line explains the mechanism. The reader-behavior hypothesis is faster classification of the offer before evaluating fee and delivery. No result claim or invented pain was added.

### Remaining risk

No live studio has confirmed that “which member needs a reply today” is its highest-value wording. The treatment still needs voice review and controlled buyer exposure before any performance claim.

### Next gate

Buyer-language validation, proof review, then Publishable Copy Gate if this becomes public.

## P2 — Content: Educational Post

### Frozen input

Teach this supported idea: adding more ideas can make a message harder to use; grouping the material around a short named roadmap can improve comprehension. Do not claim a universal ideal number of points or use generic personality flattery.

### Preservation lock

Keep the idea educational, evidence-bounded, and useful. No offer, fabricated story, unsupported statistic, or implied knowledge of the reader.

### Baseline

> Clear content should not have too many ideas. You should simplify it and make it easier to understand. Use a framework and focus on the most important points so people can remember what you said.

### Weakness

The baseline is true at a high level but generic, non-diagnostic, and hard to recall.

### Selected decisions

- Primary: **Recognition** — use an observable drafting scene, not a personality claim.
- Support: **Priority** — separate the consequential idea from supporting detail.

### Source mechanic

`jbPNjNtQqk0` `06:51` (recognition) and `11:51` (major versus minor). Evidence: `SOURCE-OBSERVED`; the scene and rule are `OPERATIONAL-SYNTHESIS`.

### Treatment by Content & Media Agent

> A draft often gets harder to use at the exact moment it starts looking more complete.
>
> The writer adds another lesson. Then another caveat. Then three examples. Every part may be good, but the reader no longer knows what to carry forward.
>
> Before adding another point, name the decision the piece is supposed to change. Keep that decision in the foreground. Group the rest as support—or move it to another piece.
>
> Clear content is not content with fewer thoughts. It is content with an obvious center of gravity.

### Behavior delta

The treatment supplies a recognizable, observable scene and one repeatable editing decision. The reader-behavior hypothesis is improved diagnosis and recall without implying a hidden personal trait.

### Remaining risk

No audience-retention or recall test has been run. Platform packaging and Farrice voice still need review for a real post.

### Next gate

Voice/taste pass, platform fit, and a frozen-input reader comparison.

## P3 — Marketing and Offer: Choice Architecture

### Frozen input

`SIMULATED_FIXTURE` offer set:

- Audit: `$250`, written diagnosis only.
- Sprint: `$750`, diagnosis plus a 14-day implementation plan.
- Install: `$2,500`, diagnosis, implementation, and 30 days of support.
- No verified results, scarcity, or segment research.
- All three options must remain visible with their material terms.

### Preservation lock

Keep all prices, deliverables, alternatives, and evidence limits. Do not create urgency, suppress the Audit or Install, or call the Sprint “most popular.”

### Baseline

> Choose the Audit for $250, the Sprint for $750, or the Install for $2,500. The Audit gives you a written diagnosis. The Sprint adds a 14-day implementation plan. The Install adds implementation and 30 days of support.

### Weakness

The baseline exposes every option but asks the buyer to construct the fit logic alone.

### Selected decisions

- Primary: **Choice** — preserve options while grouping them by use case.
- Support: **Fit** — recommend only against explicit delivery needs, not invented psychographics.

### Source mechanic

`jbPNjNtQqk0` `15:37` (fit under constraints) and `18:11` (decision burden). Evidence: `SOURCE-OBSERVED`; the use-case grouping is `OPERATIONAL-SYNTHESIS`. Choice-overload universality is rejected.

### Treatment by Revenue & Offer Agent, handed to Selected Campaign Owner

> **Pick the level of help you actually need.**
>
> - **Need the problem diagnosed?** Audit — $250. You receive a written diagnosis.
> - **Need a plan your team can implement?** Sprint — $750. You receive the diagnosis and a 14-day implementation plan.
> - **Need us to implement it with you?** Install — $2,500. You receive diagnosis, implementation, and 30 days of support.
>
> If your team can execute but needs a clear plan, start with the Sprint. If that is not your situation, the Audit and Install remain available on the terms above.

### Behavior delta

All material choices remain visible, but the comparison burden shifts from package names to delivery needs. The reader-behavior hypothesis is faster self-selection with less accidental overbuying or underbuying.

### Remaining risk

The recommended default has not been validated against real fulfillment capacity, buyer demand, or collection data. The fixture proves information architecture only.

### Next gate

Commercial truth review, fulfillment-burden check, buyer-language research, then controlled offer exposure.

## P4 — Bounded Knowledge Work: Client Decision Memo

### Frozen input

- Two landing-page variants are ready for a seven-day test.
- Variant A is 15% shorter and includes one verified testimonial.
- Variant B has the preferred visual direction but no claim proof.
- Test budget is `$500`.
- No traffic or conversion data exists yet.
- The client must choose which variant to test first.

### Preservation lock

Preserve all facts, uncertainty, budget, test duration, and the client's preferred aesthetic. Do not call either variant a winner or predict conversion.

### Baseline

> Variant A is shorter and has a verified testimonial. Variant B has the preferred visual direction but no claim proof. The budget is $500 and the test will run for seven days. There is no traffic or conversion data yet. The client should decide which page to test first.

### Weakness

The memo restates facts but leaves the consequential decision and rationale implicit.

### Selected decisions

- Primary: **Priority** — lead with the reversible decision that produces evidence.
- Support: **Congruence** — distinguish preferred visual direction from supported claim posture.

### Source mechanic

`jbPNjNtQqk0` `11:51` and `21:52`. Evidence: `SOURCE-OBSERVED`; the test recommendation is an `OPERATIONAL-SYNTHESIS` from frozen facts.

### Treatment by Selected Writing Owner

> **Decision: test Variant A first for seven days with the $500 budget.**
>
> A is the cleaner first test because it is shorter and already carries one verified testimonial. That does not make it the better page; it makes its current claim posture easier to defend while we collect the first traffic and conversion data.
>
> Preserve Variant B's visual direction as the next branch. Do not promote its claims until proof is added. At day seven, compare observed behavior and decide whether to retain A, test B with matched proof, or revise both.
>
> **Unknown:** neither variant has performance evidence today.

### Behavior delta

The memo now makes one reversible choice, gives a proof-bounded reason, preserves the preferred design branch, and keeps uncertainty visible. The receiver-behavior hypothesis is faster action without false certainty.

### Remaining risk

The recommendation depends on the assumption that obtaining first-party behavioral evidence is the current objective. A different objective could change the test order.

### Next gate

Client confirmation of objective, experiment setup, and post-test evidence review.
