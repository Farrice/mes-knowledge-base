## Deeper Pattern Extraction

### 1. Psychology works best as a decision map, not a decoration layer

The source corpus repeatedly moves from a receiver state to a response: defend → reflect/question; overload → chunk; vague recognition → specific scene; triviality → prioritize; excessive optimization → sufficient fit; too many paths → default; mismatch → align; affective distortion → pair feeling with evidence.

The pattern is conditional. Applying every mechanism would destroy the nuance the corpus is valuable for.

### 2. Jason's strongest move is often agreement with reality before redirection

Across belief perseverance, four communication modes, webinar transitions, and validate–reframe–close, he frequently acknowledges the current state before proposing a different frame. The ethically usable form is: validate what is true, clarify what remains unknown, then offer a reversible next step. Agreement may not be used to feign empathy or manufacture consent.

### 3. Subtraction is a cross-source invariant

Focus, Priority, Fit, and Choice all reduce decision work. This corrects a tension inside the existing Jason offer corpus, which often adds modalities to make offers incomparable. The combined rule is:

> Add modalities only when they increase a named buyer's fit; subtract presentation choices and redundant components when they increase comparison or delivery burden.

### 4. Congruence is the bridge between copy and the rest of the system

The beta example shows why buyer psychology cannot be confined to words. Claim, proof, visual treatment, price, maturity, delivery, and CTA form one inference. This makes Congruence the key handoff between Copywriting, Marketing, Revenue/Offer, Design, and proof owners—without giving Jason ownership of those crafts.

### 5. Defensive literacy is as important as persuasion

The loaded-language source explicitly teaches neutral substitution so a person can resist emotional hijacking. The system therefore uses a de-load → truth check → honest re-load sequence. The layer should make Codex harder to manipulate, not merely better at influencing others.

## Stacking Intelligence

| Diagnosed weak link | Primary owner | Optional bounded support | Skip support when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Message is mentally expensive | Current function owner | Low-Cognitive-Load Message Gate | The artifact is already clear |
| Evidence-based belief change | Current function owner | David McRaney | The issue is ordinary copy friction, not a held belief |
| Perceived value or behavioral strategy | Marketing/Revenue owner | Rory Sutherland | A simple offer fact or proof gap explains the issue |
| Content attention/retention | Content & Media | Kallaway | The problem is decision clarity rather than retention |
| Current identity or purchase trigger | Product/creative owner | Meg Buyer Trigger research lane | No current buyer evidence is needed |
| Direct-response mechanics | Copywriting | Sam Parr mechanics | The weak link is psychology diagnosis rather than copy execution |

Use one owner and at most one bounded specialist. Expert names are not integration evidence; the changed decision or artifact is.

## Non-Obvious Applications

1. **Client decision memos:** Priority and Congruence can turn a fact dump into one reversible decision without converting the memo into sales copy.
2. **AI review:** the negative controls tell an evaluator when persuasive optimization would be a regression.
3. **Offer simplification:** Fit and Choice can prevent incomparable-offer architecture from becoming fulfillment or comprehension bloat.
4. **Research interviews:** Four Modes can encourage reflection before challenge, improving understanding without diagnosing the participant.
5. **Red-team review:** Loaded-language deconstruction can expose emotional claims that outrun evidence.
6. **Product onboarding:** Focus and Choice can reduce first-action burden while preserving advanced capability in cold context.
7. **Design QA:** Congruence can detect when visual maturity contradicts an early-access, premium, clinical, or evidence posture.
