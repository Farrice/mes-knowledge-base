## Ordered Execution

1. **Source gate:** Confirm source receipt, evidence labels, and contradictions.
2. **Game gate:** Run play, pass, or wait when market viability or advantage is uncertain.
3. **Offer truth:** Separate facts, assumptions, proof, missing evidence, and preservation locks.
4. **TERMS audit:** Assess all five terms and 18 named levers.
5. **Primary-burden verdict:** Select one primary burden and no more than two supporting issues.
6. **Minimum redesign:** Change the smallest offer decision that materially reduces the primary burden.
7. **Targeted routing:** Invoke no more than one existing specialist per selected issue.
8. **Conditional adoption loop:** Run only for an early-win, non-use, retention, social-connection, or proof-capture problem. Activate only supported submodules, and count every returned offer change inside the same global three-change ceiling.
9. **Owner reassembly:** The public wrapper loads the Revenue Offer Agent. Diagnostic components recommend; the owner separately accepts, offsets, rejects, or holds each returned change and preserves one coherent offer.
10. **Change-class calibration:** Use `PATCH` for a bounded burden, sequence, responsibility, or safeguard repair that preserves the offer's core buyer, outcome, scope, delivery model, and commercial architecture. Reserve `REBUILD` for a material core change; convergence and framework detail are not uplift by themselves.
11. **Cross-term check:** Disclose any burden the redesign increases and offset or reject it.
12. **Result packet:** Return the offer delta, source trace, proof gap, validation status, and next commercial test.

## TERMS Diagnosis States

Each lever carries two independent axes: durable `source_status` plus one current-offer state:

- **SUPPORTED:** Current design already handles the lever with evidence.
- **FRICTION:** The lever adds avoidable buyer burden but is not the leading blocker.
- **BLOCKER:** Evidence indicates this lever can prevent purchase, adoption, or outcome.
- **UNKNOWN:** Required evidence is unavailable; do not fill with speculation.

Every adapted trace also names `adaptation_owner`. Primary-burden selection, the three-change ceiling, cross-term non-regression, permission stages, and market-state reporting are Antigravity controls rather than Jason claims.

The workflow must assess all levers but may not force a change in each. One primary blocker is the target. If no blocker is supportable, return an evidence request or no-change verdict.

## Prioritization Rule

The primary burden is chosen using three system-derived considerations:

1. **Buyer consequence:** Could it prevent purchase, adoption, or outcome?
2. **Evidence strength:** Is the diagnosis supported by the supplied artifact or buyer evidence?
3. **Operator control:** Can the offer owner change it without violating scope, safety, or proof?

This prioritization rule is an Antigravity integration decision, not a claim that Jason states this exact triad.
