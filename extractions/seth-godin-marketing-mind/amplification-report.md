### MISSED

**1. Employee/W2-career personal brand — "goal is to be somewhere we would miss if you were gone"**

> *"Whether you're a waitress at a cafe or you're making six figures on Wall Street, it's always the same. There is a shortage of people who make promises and keep them. There is a shortage of people who light up a room every time they walk into it... The goal is to be somewhere we would miss if you were gone."*

No workflow in the 4-skill stack (trio + planned 14) targets **career advancement inside a job** as its primary frame. `godin-personal-brand-role` (brand, Tier 3) covers the person-vs-ROLE distinction, but its worked example (Seth Godin, Patagonia) is founder/creator-facing — it never addresses a W2 employee trying to get promoted or become indispensable to a boss. Mel's own intro explicitly names this audience ("whether you're in a job right now and you want to make more money and get promoted") and Seth answers it directly — the stack has nowhere to catch that answer.

### LATENT

**1. "World's worst boss" = your own self-talk, undermining your work**

> *"I'm worried that you might have the world's worst boss. This person doesn't appreciate you, they're constantly nitpicking... You have probably guessed that that person is you... we can go find a friend or a group, start a circle, to change the way our boss talks to us."*

Partial coverage: philosophy's self-talk material (Tacit K1 Architect-vs-Victim, Pattern 12 Name-it-to-Defuse) diagnoses the same underlying phenomenon — a harsh internal narrator sabotaging output — but neither uses the "boss" frame, and neither prescribes the circle-based fix. **Missing**: the vivid "boss" personification (memorable, teachable) and the specific remedy (change how the boss talks to you via external accountability).

**2. Support-first circle-building — "find someone... support them... before the sun sets"**

> *"the most direct path is to find someone who's doing any kind of work online or just commenting interestingly online and support them. To find people who wish that someone like you would show up for them. Because if you do that, they will probably return the favor... Right now, before the sun sets, go find two or three people and support them. And that's how it begins."*

Partial coverage: philosophy Tacit Knowledge 7 (Cohort Multiplier) — "recruit 1-5 accountability partners... structural infrastructure" — covers the destination (a mutual-support circle) but not the entry mechanic (give first, unprompted, to strangers doing adjacent work) or the same-day urgency ("before the sun sets"). This is the *how you start* the circle Tacit K7 assumes already exists.

**3. Idiosyncrasy reframed as asset — "the normal spot is already taken"**

> *"When someone is called idiosyncratic, often it's viewed as an insult. But, it's the opposite of being average. It's the opposite of being the normal one. The normal spot is already taken... So, what makes you the idiosyncratic one that a few people would miss if it were gone?"*

Partial coverage: remarkability/exclusion-positioning material exists across all three trio skills (brand P5, philosophy P5/P7, gmind-03) — but none frame it as an *insult-to-asset inversion* tied to market-saturation logic ("the normal spot is already taken"). The vocabulary and the diagnostic question ("what makes you the idiosyncratic one") are net-new phrasing worth folding in, not a net-new concept worth a workflow.

**4. Authenticity trap / Patagonia badge — fresh exemplar for an existing pattern**

> *"if you're showing up saying, 'I am authentically me, please punch me in the face.' People who like punching folks in the face they're going to show up... The Patagonia logo isn't about the people who started Patagonia. It's about the badge the customer wears."*

Conceptually covered (brand Pattern 4 "Consistency > Authenticity," H5 Personal Brand Role Architecture) — but the current brand genius.md exemplar set (Nike/Hyatt, Carmine's, Gajist, boiler repairman) has no customer-badge case study. This is a sharper, funnier proof-case for an already-landed pattern.
