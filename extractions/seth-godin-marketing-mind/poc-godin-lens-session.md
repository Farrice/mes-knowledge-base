```
GODIN LENS SESSION
==================
Problem as stated: "How do I get more consistent on LinkedIn so more
fitness founders actually see the Authority Flywheel offer?"
Mode: EXECUTE
Stakes: the LinkedIn launch — the beachhead for the $2K-$5K Authority
Flywheel tier.

TRAP CHECK: Reach-marketing, named flat: consistency is a trap. People
don't hire a $2K-$5K authority partner because they've seen you around.
Familiar isn't hired. 40 million views sold four books.

THE REAL QUESTION: Six months of consistent posting from now, the block
won't be visibility — it will be that nothing in the feed creates
tension a specific fitness founder has to relieve by talking to you.
The real question: what story makes the right founder feel they're
falling behind until they book the call?

WORD SPLIT: "see" — viewers vs. buyers-in-tension. Viewers click
because the algorithm asked them to; the people who like you online
don't like you. A buyer-in-tension recognizes their own situation in
the story and needs it resolved. Test: would this person lose anything
by scrolling past? If no, they're a viewer, and more of them changes
nothing.

WHAT EXECUTE MODE WOULD HAVE ASKED: "Of your last ten posts, which one
was a founder unable to scroll past — and how do you know?" The likely
honest answer (silence, or a likes-count) exposes that the current plan
measures familiarity, not tension.

WHO'S IT FOR: The invisible expert in fitness — a founder or S&C coach
with real client results and no online authority, watching
worse-practitioners-with-better-content win clients they should have.
Has $2K-$5K/mo urgency money. Not for: new coaches without proof yet;
influencer-coaches who already own an audience; brands chasing
virality; anyone who wants it for $500.
WHAT'S IT FOR: To make their buried results impossible to ignore in
their niche — an experience worth more than it costs, measured in
inbound conversations they never used to get.
ENOUGH = 3-5 flywheel clients. That's a full calendar for one operator
at this tier. Not 10,000 followers — five right people.

THE ANSWER: Different question first, then we come back. Design the
tension before scheduling the calendar. The story: "your results are
invisible, and every week they stay invisible, a louder coach with
thinner results takes a client that should have been yours." That's
falling-behind tension — it spreads inside a niche because founders
check what their competitors are doing. Relief isn't following Farrice;
relief is the flywheel engagement. Which means the work this month is
not a posting cadence — it's making that tension land on 25 named
founders, supporting them first, and opening with a question every
invisible expert has an answer to.
Now the asked question, one line: post whenever there's tension worth
creating — the calendar follows the story, never the reverse.

CASES:
1. Jen Santulan (Farrice's own roster, real estate): she doesn't post
   "consistent SFV content" — she owns first-time-homebuyer-in-the-SFV,
   objection-in-eight-words hooks, ADU-as-bonus. Luxury buyers, don't
   come. Out-of-valley, don't come. The niche does the marketing her
   calendar never could.
2. Chip Kidd (book covers): roughly thirty covers a year, as Godin
   tells it, and no one goes to Chip and argues with him about
   typography. He never needed more art directors to "see" him — he
   needed to be the one and only for the few who get the joke. If you
   want to art-direct him, don't come.

THIS WEEK: Before the sun sets, pick 10 invisible experts in fitness
whose work deserves attention and support them publicly — a real
comment on the result they're proudest of, no pitch. Then DM three of
them one question everyone has an answer to: "What's the client result
you're most proud of that basically nobody knows about?" That's the
Girl Scout question — it starts the conversation the offer needs.
MEASURE: Real exchanges with fitting founders this week (target: 3).
Not impressions, not likes — conversations with people who match the
don't-come list's complement.
THE LESSON: The question asked for volume; the answer deleted volume —
when a plan's core verb is "be seen," replace it with "create tension
for a named few" before optimizing anything.
```
