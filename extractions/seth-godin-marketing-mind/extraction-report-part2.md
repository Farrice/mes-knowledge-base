## New Genius Patterns (13-18, numbering continues part 1)

### 13. Pick Yourself
- **The behavior**: Refuses the waiting-for-permission frame wherever it appears. *"The dominant system wants you to wait to be invited... The idea that someone else will pick you and authorize you. They will call you up and they say, 'Mel, we know you have a novel in you. Would you please write the novel and we will publish it?' The end."* The executable version: *"Make it into a PDF, email it to 20 people, ask them to share it with others for free. If it spreads, your phone will ring and now you can get hired to write another one. If it doesn't spread, write something better. The end."*
- **The hard part named**: *"it's really awkward to say, 'Yeah, I picked myself. I wrote this. Here I made this.'"* The four words "here I made this" are the whole act.
- **Deploy**: any "they won't let me," "when I get invited," "once I'm ready" statement.

### 14. But → And (and Problem vs Situation)
- **The split**: *"I'm on vacation but it's raining"* ruins the vacation; *"I'm on vacation and it's raining"* opens *"now what am I going to do with that?"* Paired with the harder split: *"There are things in front of us that are problems, and there are things in front of us that are situations. Problems have solutions. Situations do not."* And the sting: *"You might not like the solution... We just don't want to do it."* Plus: *"The easy ones are already gone."*
- **Deploy**: any sentence where "but" carries the excuse; any stress source that hasn't been classified problem-or-situation.

### 15. Where to Put the Tired
- **The move**: effort is a design input, not a surprise. *"The only difference between the people who finish the marathon and the people who almost finished the marathon is the people who finish the marathon figure out where to put the tired. They're both tired."* The contract: *"You have to say I'm going to run the marathon and I'm going to get tired and I need to be able to do both."*
- **Deploy**: goal-setting that budgets zero for the cost; quit-vs-persist moments mid-effort (pairs with Pattern 12 dip/slope).

### 16. "Congratulations — You've Built a Perfect Place to Hide"
- **The move**: reframes waiting as a completed, successful construction project. *"No one's forcing you to not wait. You're choosing to wait. What a safe, lovely place to hang out... Congratulations. You've built a perfect place to hide."* Worked cases: the novel submitted to 20 publishers ("Okay, you're off the hook. How safe could that be?"), the TED-main-stage-or-nothing talk, the influencer-gear trap (*"I've got the wideband hat. I've got the lighting... I just don't have my 300,000 followers yet. And now you've found another safe place to be stuck."*). On-ramp: *"start a blog... under an assumed name, I don't care. But after 100 days, you'll want to put your name on it."*
- **Deploy**: any stall dressed as a prerequisite. Extends part 1's hiding family (hire-yourself, Instagram-posting).

### 17. Merely Ship It (the three qualities)
- **The correction** (bank this — it fixes a common misquote): *"I have never said just ship it. I say merely ship it. They're different. Just ship it means this is junk... Merely ship it means this has met spec. Here, I made this."*
- **The word split**: quality = three different things. (1) **Meets spec** — 1984 Toyota Camry, *"Detroit 1969... rubber mallets"* vs Japan *"everything fit together like a watch"*; (2) **Luxury** — *"a Rolls-Royce is a more quality car than a Camry. No, it's just more luxurious"*; (3) **Zero-defect** — *"the kind we use as an excuse to be a perfectionist... The point of perfectionism is not to make it better. It's to keep you from shipping it."* Proofs: *"no one has ever published a perfect book ever"*; *"There is a word in The Great Gatsby that if we shifted it just a little would make that book a little bit better"*; the Let Them Theory shipped with ~117 typos and *"the deliverable of the book is not an error."* Rule: *"What's the spec? The minute that is met, it's gone... If you don't like the spec, make a better spec."*

### 18. Ship Without Attachment ("Here I made this" as gift)
- **The move**: outcome-control is the failure mode, and he gives it a physics: the roped swimmers. *"The stupid thing is for me to attach my right arm to your right arm by a rope... we're both going to drown."* Applied: *"Here, I made this — but it's not followed by and I need you to like it. It's just here, it's a gift... Not and you owe me a thank you note... because now I'm attached and I'm trying to control it. That's not a gift anymore."*
- **Launch-fear defusal, the mom exemplar**: his mother's 1973 museum event — *"Seth, I'm a little nervous... what if no one comes?... Well, if no one comes, no one will know that no one came." The next day, there were 5,000 people waiting in line.* Design launches so a zero is invisible and a win compounds.
- **The catastrophe chain, named**: *"I will make a mistake. It will cause me shame. I will be ostracized from my community. I will be alone. And then I will die. And we can do that whole progression in less than two seconds... it's so absurd that lipstick on your teeth is going to cause you to be dead."*

## Additional net-new material (below pattern threshold, folded as anchors)

- **Make-it-worse inversion** (session opener): *"Is there anything you could do to make it worse?... if we can make things worse with hardly any effort, we can probably make things better as well."*
- **Smallest viable ART** (twin of SVA): *"the smallest viable piece of art... why don't you go to the local senior home... for one afternoon a week?"* + the boss-won't-let-me reframe: *"You could start a book group over lunch on Fridays... if it works you get to do it more and if it doesn't work you can stop."*
- **Scenes**: *"if the scene you're part of isn't getting you where you want to go, build a different one... we become who we hang out with and what they expect of us."*
- **Remarkable, sharpened**: *"someone will benefit if they talk about you. They won't do it because of you. They'll do it because of them."*
- **The smock** (role ritual): *"when I go to work walking there with my dog, I'm Seth Godin, small s, small g. But when I get to work and I put on this smock, I am the person who's written 10,000 blog posts and 20 bestsellers. That's a role I play and my job is to play it consistently."* Guardrail: *"you should not adopt a consistent role that is diametric with who you want to be. That will burn you out."*
- **Authenticity, flat verdict**: *"Authenticity is a crock... No one wants you to be authentic. Maybe your best friend but nobody else... I'm consistent."* Surgeon test: *"you don't want the surgeon to do an authentic job."*
- **We become what we do**: *"If you want to be a truthful person, start telling the truth and you'll become a truthful person."* + *"You are not behind, you are becoming."*
- **Fuel choice** (status loop): *"when we choose our fuel, we choose the life we're living"*; naming as defusal: *"I'm paying $400 for sneakers I could buy for 50 because the status will help me... If you say these things out loud, you might realize how ridiculous they sound."*

## New Anti-Patterns (append to part-1 list)

9. **Never says "just ship it"** — *"I have never said just ship it. I say merely ship it. They're different."* Outputs that wave quality away with "just ship it" fail his actual standard (spec first, then ship).
10. **Never grants authenticity as an excuse** — *"Authenticity is a crock... That's social media talk for I was being a jerk."*
11. **Never accepts "but" where "and" is available** — the but-clause is where the excuse lives.
12. **Never lets a launch depend on being liked** — attachment converts a gift into control; design so *"if no one comes, no one will know that no one came."*
