## The Ring 5 trap list — personalized "looks reasonable, actually poison"

Ideas that would LOOK smart and could even get views — and why each one damages the channel:

1. **"5 AI tools every founder needs in 2026"** — you built a 90-agent system, so this feels on-brand. But it attracts AI-tool hobbyists, not supplement founders with launch budgets. The algorithm learns from whoever engages, so one hit here teaches it to send you tool-hunters forever.
2. **"How I run a business as a stay-at-home dad"** — a true and moving story, aimed at nobody who buys. A parenting-and-productivity audience will never book an Angle Map. (The story itself isn't banned — as a *chaos agent re-aimed at Rings 1–4* it becomes "I run a positioning practice from a nursery," told TO founders.)
3. **"3 marketing mistakes every business makes"** — pure Ring 5 generic. It competes with every marketer alive, showcases none of your category expertise, and tells the algorithm your channel is for "everyone" — which means no one.

The failure logic, once: these can get views, but the viewers they attract don't match the buyer — and the algorithm learns from whoever engages, so one viral Ring 5 video teaches it to send you more of the wrong people.

## Inspiration sourcing rules

- **TOPICS come from the ring your bucket sits on.** Narrow buckets: source Rings 1–3 (Ring 1 creators, if any exist, are topic gold even though you'd never build a bucket there). Broad bucket: source Ring 4, then re-aim delivery at Marcus. **Ring 5 is craft-only** — sourcing topics there is how channels drift generic.
- **CRAFT can come from anywhere** — formats, hooks, storytelling structures, editing, pacing. A cooking creator's format can carry a compliance topic.
- An empty ring isn't a problem, it's your opening: pull from the adjacent ring and re-aim.
