## The 7 dimensions, in plain language (the wheel's teaching panel, on paper)

*These are the same five beats that appear in the Positioning Wheel artifact — kept here so the teaching survives outside the visual.*

### 1. Topic selection
1. **What it is:** which subjects you make content about — and, just as loudly, which you never touch. (Example: one fitness channel only ever talks about knee pain; that's a topic selection.)
2. **The menu:** AI-search visibility · brand strategy/positioning · content & ghostwriting · claim-substantiation & compliance · the combined stack (positioning + proof + citation) · category culture critique.
3. **Your niche right now:** AI visibility 2 of 7 — *contested* · brand strategy 2 of 7 — *contested* · content/ghostwriting 2 of 7 — *contested* · claims & compliance 0 of 7 — **open** · the stack 0 of 7 — **open** · culture critique 1 of 7 — *open-ish*.
4. **The white space:** nobody makes claims-and-compliance content; nobody combines the three topics into one decision.
5. **For you:** this is your strongest opening — it's pain #3 in your own buyer research and it's empty. Unmeasured graveyard risk is the honest caveat: it could be empty because it's hard to make interesting. Your counter-stories are how it becomes interesting.

### 2. Substance depth
1. **What it is:** how deep the content actually goes — cheerleading ("you got this!"), tactical how-to (steps you can copy), or decision-level ("here's what to decide and how to know").
2. **The menu:** surface motivation · tactical how-to · credentialed practitioner depth · decision-level strategy.
3. **Your niche right now:** tactical how-to is the default (KeyGrow, Pennock) — *crowded for services*; practitioner-credentialed decision depth 0 of 7 — **open**.
4. **The white space:** depth that comes from having actually trained the buyer's customers for 18 years, pointed at what to say rather than how to write it.
5. **For you:** confirmed lane. The risk isn't competition, it's translation — decision-level content must still be concrete enough to feel usable in 45 seconds.

### 3. Unique stories & proof
1. **What it is:** the lived evidence a creator leans on so you believe them — results, scars, case studies, "I was there" stories.
2. **The menu:** client results/case studies · self-referential proof (be your own receipt) · credentials · lived buyer-side stories.
3. **Your niche right now:** mostly *unknown on this data*; Pennock's "we are cited by AI" self-proof is the one visible standout; lived buyer-side stories 0 of 7 — **open**.
4. **The white space:** nobody talks from behind the supplement counter. Nobody was the magazine-era buyer.
5. **For you:** your stories are uncopyable — but note Pennock's play: in a proof contest, a receipt beats a memoir. Build citation receipts of your own alongside the stories.

### 4. Avatar specificity
1. **What it is:** who the content is really for — "everyone in wellness" vs. one describable person. The narrower the address, the harder the right person stops scrolling.
2. **The menu:** any-brand generalist · health & wellness broadly · supplement brands specifically · the founder at a dated launch moment.
3. **Your niche right now:** health & wellness broadly 4 of 7 — *the default here, crowded* · generalist 1 of 7 · supplement-specific 1 of 7 (KeyGrow) — *contested* · launch-moment founder 0 of 7 — **open**.
4. **The white space:** the founder with a launch on the calendar in the next 90 days.
5. **For you:** contested at the category level — KeyGrow got to "supplement brands" first. Win by out-narrowing (the launch clock) and by carrying the claims layer they don't.

### 5. Delivery style
1. **What it is:** how it feels when they talk — casual friend, suit, professor, hype-machine, deadpan.
2. **The menu:** casual coach · polished consultant · hype energy · deadpan analyst · storyteller.
3. **Your niche right now:** **not enough data** — reading delivery style requires watching the content, and the fallback path has no archive. This slice is dark until the MCP run.
4. **The white space:** unknown.
5. **For you:** your interview suggests warm, direct, coaching-across-the-table — a real style. Whether it's differentiated in this niche is unverifiable today.

### 6. Storytelling format
1. **What it is:** the shape of how a piece is told — a countdown list, a myth getting busted, a teardown, a day-in-the-life.
2. **The menu:** countdown list · myth-bust · teardown/breakdown · story-with-lesson · comparison.
3. **Your niche right now:** **not enough data** — same reason as delivery style.
4. **The white space:** unknown.
5. **For you:** your teardown instinct (carousels) is a candidate signature. Validation waits on data.

### 7. Visual format
