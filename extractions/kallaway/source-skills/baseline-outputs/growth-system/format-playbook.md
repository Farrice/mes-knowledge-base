A principle to carry: viewers consume information most easily as **lists, bulleted points, and comparisons** — which is why countdowns, this-vs-that, and rankings dominate most niches.

## The test set — 2–3 formats to test per 7-video batch

Scored on the skill's four criteria. **Criterion 2 (proven in-niche) is unmeasurable on this data** — every pick is scored on vehicle fit, delivery/constraint fit, and bucket coverage only, and one pick is flagged as the deliberate bet the whitespace rule allows.

### Pick 1 — Busting a myth × You on camera, nothing else
- **Why:** the best vehicle for claims-and-compliance information is correcting a belief ("you think 'clinically proven' protects you — it doesn't"); the raw talking head is the cheapest layout that carries his strongest asset (he's a world-class speaker, uneven writer — speak it, don't script it). Carries Claim Autopsies and What To Prove directly; can carry all 3 buckets.
- **Skeleton:** (1) state the belief your founder holds, in his words → (2) break it with one specific receipt (a warning letter, a figure, a counter story) → (3) replace it with the decision to make instead.
- **Visual recipe:** phone at eye level, natural light, one take, captions on. No edit beyond trims.
- **Model videos to study:** none on file — no niche archive; when the scan runs, pull the top myth-bust talking heads from the watchlist.
- **Carries:** batch slots 1–2 (Claim Autopsies), 5–6 (AI Killed the Average Brand re-aimed as belief-correction).

### Pick 2 — Story with a lesson × You on camera, nothing else
- **Why:** the Behind the Counter bucket IS this structure; his proof is biographical and spoken. Same zero-production layout — two picks sharing one layout means one filming habit to build, not two.
- **Skeleton:** (1) drop into the scene ("a customer at the counter once asked me…") → (2) the tension (what the honest answer would cost) → (3) the one line that lands the lesson for the founder watching.
- **Visual recipe:** identical to Pick 1 — deliberately. Reps over variety.
- **Model videos:** none on file.
- **Carries:** batch slots 3–4 (Behind the Counter), slot 7 (the chaos confession video is a story-with-lesson).

### Pick 3 (the deliberate bet) — This vs. That × Words on the screen
- **Why:** comparisons are one of the two easiest shapes for viewers to consume, and his signature material is literally side-by-side (nine identical homepages; the honest paragraph vs. the lying banner; 1998 ad vs. 2026 ad). Words-on-screen needs no camera and no naptime filming window at all. Flagged as the bet: no evidence anyone in the niche does or doesn't do this — that's the risk and the reason to test it.
- **Skeleton:** (1) put the pair on screen → (2) strike/highlight the differences one beat at a time → (3) verdict card.
- **Visual recipe:** template in any caption tool; text animates in, strikethroughs land on the beat.
- **Model videos:** none on file.
- **Carries:** batch slots 1–2 and 5–6 as an alternate skin; the C2 chaos-reserve idea (silent ad-era comparison) lives here.

## The hero-format rule

Test these 2–3 for ~2–3 batches. Then the data — via the **channel-coach** — crowns the **1–2 hero formats** to double down on: testing ends, reps begin, and you get fast and excellent at making those. Don't marry a format before the data speaks; don't keep testing after it has.
