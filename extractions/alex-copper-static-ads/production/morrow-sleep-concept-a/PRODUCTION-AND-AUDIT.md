## What stayed locked

**Brand:** Morrow Sleep, a fictional composite.

**Product:** Adjustable cooling pillow.

**Persona:** A hot sleeper who flips the pillow looking for the cold side.

**Job:** Create immediate ritual recognition without assuming brand familiarity.

**Selling locus:** Clarity.

**Eye path:** Brand, ritual headline, product category, pillow, customer phrase, then action.

**Prohibited claims:** Measured temperature change, cooling duration, better sleep, medical benefit, typical result, or guaranteed performance.

The primary headline, “Still flipping your pillow at 2 a.m.?,” is inferred. The product descriptor and the customer line, “I stopped flipping my pillow to find the cold side,” came from the supplied fixture. “See the pillow” compresses the category CTA for layout without widening the promise.

## One-second audit

This was a simulated first-impression check plus direct visual inspection. It was not a stranger panel, and no human responses are claimed. A brief human test is still required.

### Cell 01: ordinary-product control

The five simulated reads were “cooling pillow,” “pillow for midnight flipping,” “sleep pillow,” “adjustable cooling pillow,” and “pillow ad.” All five identified the category; four caught the ritual or problem. The plain pillow does less explanatory work, which makes it a clean control. **Keep.**

### Cell 02: thermal-metaphor challenger

The simulated reads were “cooling pillow,” “hot side versus cool side,” “temperature pillow,” “cooling sleep pillow,” and “pillow with cooling zones.” All five identified the category and the hot/cool tension. Two reads could mistake the metaphor for technical performance. **Keep for a controlled test, but hold until the real product's substantiation and disclosure approach are approved.**

### Cell 03: generic headline challenger

All five simulated reads identified a cooling pillow for hot sleepers. None retained the more specific flipping ritual. The headline is clear but strategically weaker. **Kill.**

The visual hierarchy held across the batch. The headline lands first, one pillow carries the scene, the customer line stays secondary, and the action remains visible. There is no review collage, badge theater, unsupported number, clutter, or misspelling.

## What must change before launch

1. Replace the fictional pillow with rights-cleared product photography.
2. Confirm that “adjustable cooling pillow” accurately describes the SKU.
3. Supply the product substantiation and approved claim limits.
4. Confirm the customer line is real, permissioned, and shown in the right testimonial context.
5. Continue the same ritual and product-category frame on the actual landing page.
6. Show Cells 01 and 02 for one second to 3–5 unbriefed people and record their words.
7. Export to the exact platform dimensions chosen by the live account owner.

Do not use Cell 03. Do not let a later revision change headline, visual, proof, persona, and layout at once. Never present the thermal metaphor as measured cooling.

## Test order

1. Compare Cell 01 with Cell 02. Copy, layout, persona, action, and destination stay fixed.
2. Run the human comprehension gate before media spend.
3. After rights, substantiation, destination, and comprehension pass, traffic both cells under the same audience, placement, offer, and measurement window.
4. Record what the test can establish and what it cannot.

The route proved it can preserve a hypothesis, produce controlled files, catch a canvas failure, and reject a weaker challenger. It did not establish a winner, a typical customer result, product efficacy, or an improved business metric.
