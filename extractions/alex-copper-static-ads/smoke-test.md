# Behavior Proof: Morrow Sleep Static Acquisition System

## Verdict

**PASS: workflow behavior changed materially.**

Workflow 07 converted a generic product-first line into an evidence-labeled acquisition test slate with a named account gap, one persona, a post-click continuity rule, an explicit selling locus, controlled eye paths, isolated test cells, and a locked production handoff.

This is workflow and decision proof. It is not market-performance proof.

## Input Tested

- **Brand:** Morrow Sleep (fictional composite)
- **Product:** adjustable cooling pillow
- **Current account:** generic discount-led product shots dominate
- **Persona:** hot sleeper who repeatedly flips the pillow at night
- **Post-click destination:** persona-specific cooling-pillow landing page
- **Constraints:** no medical claims; no invented performance data; concept specification only

### Supplied Customer Language

1. “I stopped flipping my pillow to find the cold side”
2. “I woke up without a sweaty neck”
3. “My husband stole mine.”

## Before

> Upgrade your sleep with our advanced cooling pillow. Shop now.

### Weakness Diagnosed

- It assumes generic interest rather than creating immediate personal relevance.
- “Advanced” is unsupported and carries no customer meaning.
- It does not state who the ad is for or what account gap it closes.
- It contains no customer-language provenance, post-click continuity, eye path, or test variable.
- It cannot distinguish a messaging failure from a visual or format failure.

### White-Space Map

- **Overproduced:** discount-led product shots addressing a generic sleep audience.
- **Missing:** cold acquisition statics built around the hot sleeper's pillow-flipping ritual and supplied language.
- **Primary gap:** persona-recognition message for the hot sleeper before product detail.

### Persona, Funnel, and Destination Lock

- **Primary persona:** a hot sleeper who turns or flips the pillow while trying to find a cooler surface.
- **Acquisition job:** make that person recognize their nighttime ritual without requiring brand familiarity.
- **Assumed knowledge:** none about Morrow Sleep; only ordinary familiarity with a pillow and the act of flipping it.
- **Destination continuity:** the landing page must continue the pillow-flipping problem frame, show the adjustable cooling pillow, and use only substantiated product explanation. It may not widen back to generic “better sleep.”

### Proven-Format Selection

| Structure | Funnel job | Message fit | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline plus testimonial | Cold acquisition | Connects the ritual to supplied customer language | Format is source-supported; performance is unknown |
| Testimonial | Proof-oriented acquisition | Makes one customer sound like another customer | Phrase is supplied; representativeness is unknown |
| Persona static | Cold recognition | Names the hot-sleeper situation directly | Persona is supplied; market size is unknown |
| Post-it / native note | Feed-native message delivery | Carries short ritual language with low production complexity | Placement is a concept, not a winner |
| Meme | Broad cold recognition | Makes the repeated flip behavior immediately legible | Humor fit is untested |

### Message and Selling-Locus Ledger

- **Selling locus:** CLARITY.
- **Ad's job:** make the pillow-flipping problem and cooling-pillow category understandable before the click.
- **Destination's job:** explain the product truthfully and complete the evaluation without adding unsupported outcomes.

## Concept Slate

### Concept A: The 2 A.M. Flip

- **White-space gap:** no ritual-based cold acquisition static.
- **Format:** headline plus testimonial.
- **Headline:** “Still flipping your pillow at 2 a.m.?” (INFERRED).
- **Support:** “I stopped flipping my pillow to find the cold side” (SUPPLIED).
- **Selling locus:** clarity.
- **Eye path:** headline, then heat/cool pillow contrast, product, supplied testimonial, and “See the adjustable cooling pillow.”
- **Visual:** split pillow with a stylized heat-map glow on the used side and a cool-color treatment on the other; explicitly a visual metaphor.
- **Substantiation boundary:** no temperature, duration, or sleep-improvement claim.
- **Single test variable:** headline in Test 1; visual in Test 2.

### Concept B: Customer Said It Better

- **White-space gap:** no customer-language acquisition static.
- **Format:** testimonial.
- **Headline:** “I stopped flipping my pillow to find the cold side” (SUPPLIED).
- **Qualifier:** “A Morrow Sleep customer, supplied fixture language.”
- **Selling locus:** clarity.
- **Eye path:** quote, then product, hot-sleeper qualifier, and product-category CTA.
- **Visual:** unembellished bedside product image; quotation receives highest contrast.
- **Substantiation boundary:** one supplied testimonial, not a typical-results statement.
- **Single test variable:** testimonial-first versus question-first headline.

### Concept C: Hot Sleeper Recognition

- **White-space gap:** generic account does not qualify a hot sleeper.
- **Format:** persona static.
- **Headline:** “Hot sleeper?” (INFERRED).
- **Support:** “I woke up without a sweaty neck” (SUPPLIED and presented as one person's statement).
- **Selling locus:** clarity.
- **Eye path:** persona qualifier, then supplied quote, product, category explanation, and CTA.
- **Visual:** bedside crop with restrained warm-color cue near the sleeper and product shown separately.
- **Substantiation boundary:** no universal outcome or medical implication.
- **Single test variable:** persona qualifier versus ritual question.

### Concept D: Bedside Note

- **White-space gap:** no feed-native, low-production static built around the ritual.
- **Format:** post-it / native note.
- **Headline:** “Still flipping your pillow at 2 a.m.?” (INFERRED and handwritten on a bedside note).
- **Selling locus:** clarity.
- **Eye path:** note, then pillow, product name/category, and CTA.
- **Visual:** note placed on a nightstand beside the pillow, not on the pillow seam unless legibility can be proven.
- **Substantiation boundary:** the surface supplies context; it does not prove a benefit.
- **Single test variable:** conventional headline placement versus bedside-surface placement.

### Concept E: The Stolen Pillow

- **White-space gap:** no social-detail message drawn from supplied customer language.
- **Format:** testimonial / native text.
- **Headline:** “My husband stole mine.” (SUPPLIED).
- **Qualifier:** “An adjustable cooling pillow from Morrow Sleep.”
- **Selling locus:** curiosity, with the product category revealed before click and the landing page explaining the product.
- **Eye path:** social-detail headline, then paired pillows / missing-pillow scene, product qualifier, and CTA.
- **Visual:** one pillow missing from a two-person bed arrangement; no popularity claim.
- **Substantiation boundary:** one supplied anecdote, not proof of demand.
- **Single test variable:** social-detail headline versus ritual headline.

### Concept F: Flip Loop Meme

- **White-space gap:** no broad, instantly recognizable depiction of the repeated ritual.
- **Format:** meme.
- **Headline:** “Me, looking for the cold side again” (INFERRED).
- **Support:** “Adjustable cooling pillow” (SUPPLIED).
- **Selling locus:** clarity.
- **Eye path:** meme action, then problem label, product, and category CTA.
- **Visual:** three-panel flip sequence using the same pillow and sleeper position.
- **Substantiation boundary:** behavior depiction only; no product outcome claim.
- **Single test variable:** meme structure versus headline-plus-testimonial structure.
