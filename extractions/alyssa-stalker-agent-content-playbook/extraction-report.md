## Genius Patterns

### 1. "It's Just Data" — The Outlier Audit Comes First
- **What she does unconsciously**: Refuses to prescribe a format before reading the agent's own last six months. "Take a deep breath... it's just data" [01:58–02:04]. Looks for the one post that broke the flatline and names its attributes as "a leading indicator of a starting point" [02:39–02:41].
- **Executable behavior**: Pull 6 months of posts. Mark the flatline. Isolate outliers. Diagnose the attribute (topic, format, hook framing, who it spoke to, opinion strength). Build the next 30 days around that attribute.
- **Worked example**: Eric's "AI won't replace real estate agents, but agents who use AI will replace those who don't" → ~800 comments, most follows of the year → shifted to "70% more talking head yap style" with heavy opinions [03:43–04:20]. Alyssa's repeatable carousels outperformed everything for 18 months → she ran with it → 10k to ~15k [16:25–17:12].
- **Success metric**: A named attribute, not a vibe, drives the next test.

### 2. Person, Not Demographic
- **What she does**: Rejects "upsizer / downsizer" as "too broad" [05:09–05:23]. Asks life-stage and habit questions: "Where are they eating? Where are they hanging out?... Are they playing pickleball on a Friday and joining a social club or are they somebody who loves to couch rot on the weekend?" [07:47–08:01].
- **Executable behavior**: Write the person as a scene, not a segment. Then: "you have to start with one person to get to that point" [08:23–08:26] where people call because they like you.
- **Deploy when**: Agent says "I'll work with anybody" [08:10–08:11].

### 3. The One-Slide Reverse-Engineer
- **What she does**: Uses format compression as a niche-finder. "What can I fit in one slide, like one single image post and a caption — that's almost how you can reverse engineer who you're speaking to" [06:29–06:36].
- **Context**: Carousels "perform better than reels, by far" for her; "single image posts are back" [06:01–06:08]; the 2026 look is "butter yellow basic fonts... one message to one person" [05:44–05:51].
- **Deploy when**: A topic keeps needing seven slides or a talking-head to land.

### 4. Topic + Who + Lens — The Hook Framing Grammar
- **What she does**: Takes the broad default and appends the audience and the point of view. "Three things to do this weekend in city name" → "three things to do this weekend in city name that you've probably never heard of, especially if you're a mom of two" [12:12–12:27]. Eric's echo: "the three best things to do this weekend in Spokane if you're a mom with kids under five" [14:04–14:10].
- **Executable behavior**: Every local hook carries (a) the topic, (b) a who-it's-for clause, (c) the agent's take or framing. "Make sure you're trying it out with something interesting with local content that is specific to who you want to reach and has your personal point of view" [13:19–13:29].
- **Extreme form**: Gabe Einhorn's "if you're watching this video on August 15th or 16th" [14:46–14:50] — date specificity as a targeting lever; Eric flags it as "a cheap way to game the algorithm" that still produces the "camera in my room" effect [14:51–15:13].
- **Success metric**: The named person stops scrolling because the hook describes them.

### 5. Specificity Is Distribution — The 24-Hour Interest Test
- **What she knows**: "The way Instagram categorizes content now... I could be looking up one thing one time... and now for the next 24 hours it's testing similar type of content in front of me" [12:35–12:51]. "It's really an even playing field... the more specific you are, the more likely now you are to land in front of the right people. You don't have to fight for those views as hard" [12:54–13:05].
- **Why it matters**: Inverts the agent instinct that broad = bigger TAM. Eric: "so broad that no one actually cares... but it's going to land in the algorithm of those moms" [14:23–14:29].
- **Deploy when**: An agent resists narrowing because they "just want business."

### 6. The Personal-Lens Inventory — "What Do They Need to Know About Me"
- **What she does**: Flips the audience question. "It's not what does the audience want to see, but also what does the audience need to know about me in order to like me, trust me, and then work with me" [09:32–09:40]. "Sharing enough of yourself to where people feel like they get to know you" [09:50–09:52].
- **Worked example**: Eric's set — Star Wars, USC, baseball cards, the book he wrote, Yankees; the new baby and sports in stories; a bet mentioned at the end of broadcast-channel posts [08:47–09:18].
- **Executable behavior**: List 5–8 personal signals, then thread one into each week's content as a hint, not a feature.

### 7. Comfort Creator — Connection Over Transaction
- **What she does**: "Nobody comes to my account for tips anymore... they follow along my content because they actually feel seen" [16:35–16:43]. Names the term: "being a comfort creator" [21:10]. "It's connection content versus transactional content" [21:38–21:40].
- **Exemplars (her hooks, verbatim)**: "It's crazy what an 'I found you on Instagram' can do for my mental health as a real estate agent" [18:16–18:22]. "People only post their wins on social. So here are some of my recent flops" [18:39–18:42] — with the brutal receipt: "literally one person wanted my lead magnet the day before" [18:53–18:57]. "Self-care as a realtor... I don't have to earn my rest" [19:05–19:09].
- **Why it works**: "A lot of people are always yelling at real estate agents about what to do... I want people to feel comfort, or laughter, or yap when they come to my account... a space where they feel like they can grow without pressure" [19:15–19:32].
- **Deploy when**: The audience is over-lectured and under-recognized.

### 8. The Consumer Flip — "You're Not Behind. That's Exactly Who I Help."
- **What she does**: Ports comfort content to buyers. "Self-care as someone in their mid-30s who's yet to buy a house" → mindset, self-care, "start the process" → the payoff slide: "Don't worry, you're not behind. Lots of people are like that and guess what? That's exactly who I help" [20:10–20:51].
- **Structure**: hook names the private state → "oh, that's me, I'll keep swiping" → something funny/cheeky/supportive → solution last, framed as permission.
- **Contrast**: Eric: "That does so much better than FOMO or rent shaming or 'you have to do this now before school starts'" [20:52–20:59].

### 9. Create-Mode Text Post — The Ricky Carruth Format
- **What it is**: All-black background, text only, made in Instagram create mode. Eric reads three of Ricky's aloud: the 9 p.m.-Saturday agent post (11,000 likes), the confidence/market-cycle post, and "real estate agents wake up knowing they have zero guaranteed income, but they show up anyway. That's called courage" (12,000 likes) [22:04–22:38].
- **Production**: "If you have an idea, just one thing, tweet it out, screenshot it, and then post it" [23:09–23:13].
- **Why it works**: "This is really easy for an agent to share and kind of feel like, hey, at least someone kind of sees me" [22:49–22:53].

### 10. The Share-to-Stories Test — Write For Them, Through Their Lens
- **What she does**: "I think would a real estate agent want to share this to their stories or repost this so that their potential clients see it and it would make them look good" [23:36–23:46]. "The reason my content gets shared a lot by other agents is because I'm actually helping positioning them" [23:48–23:53].
- **Consumer version**: "meeting them where they're at, describing the feeling, and helping them connect with it" [24:19–24:23].
- **Eric's operationalization**: "What is the action someone's going to take with this? Are they going to share this in the DMs? Share to their story? Comment? Or is this just blasting them with information?" [24:28–24:42].

### 11. Goal-Tagged Content Mix — Local / Listing / Authority
- **What she does**: Coffee & Contracts' mix is "mostly local content, listing content because you got to show what it is that you sell, and then a little bit of authority building content and that's the smallest percentage" [25:59–26:10]. "Mostly for them with a little bit about you" [26:22–26:25].
- **Goal mapping**: grow → local ("fastest way to attract new followers" [26:47–26:51]); convert → listing [26:53–26:57]; nurture/trust → authority [26:59–27:01]. "Every post has a different goal... you're not going to get a ton of likes on authority-building nurture style content" [27:04–27:10]. Authority cadence: "pepper these in once every month or something" [28:04–28:06].
- **Success metric**: Each post is judged against its own goal, not against the local-viral post's likes.

### 12. Authority as Story, Never Billboard
- **Review**: "take one line out of the review, find the hook line, make that the hook of a post, and then break down the story of the transaction and why they said that about you" [30:52–31:04]. Screenshot reviews are "swipe, it's irrelevant to me" for anyone not hiring [31:28–31:31].
- **Award**: green-screen the article or make it "look like a news-based announcement... then tell a story about what you did differently to get there" [29:19–29:29]; "you want to know how I got on this list? Well, let me tell you" [29:49–29:51].
- **Breaking-news frame**: Tatiana Londono's "millionaire real estate agent gives her three social media tips" → "you won't believe what this client said about her agent" then swipe to the review [32:11–32:32].
- **Capture timing**: "do it when they're happy at closing time... when you're handing them the keys" [32:44–32:52]; ask questions on camera, clip later [33:05–33:17].
- **Comedic variant**: ask one question, cut in the answer to a different question (Katie Day / Tom Ferry room) [33:20–34:02].

## Hidden Knowledge

- **The misdiagnosis**: stuck agents believe they have a consistency problem; "most of the time it's not a consistency problem... they really aren't speaking to anyone specific" [02:44–02:57].
- **Narrow = reach**: 2026 Instagram distributes by interest test, so specificity buys distribution [12:35–13:05].
- **Reshare is the mechanism**: comfort content spreads because the reader uses it to position themselves to their own audience [23:36–23:53].
- **Authority is memory, not engagement**: "no one's going to share that post, but... it hits them in their head" [28:27–28:34].
- **Reviews are invisible to non-buyers**: disguise them as normal content to be seen and remembered [31:11–31:44].
- **The one-slide test is a niche-finder** in disguise [06:29–06:36].
- **An outlier line becomes a bit**: Eric's AI line "has been used now like as a joke in all of my comments moving forward" [04:03–04:08] — outliers can become recurring brand assets, not just one-offs.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

### Exemplar 1: The Hook Reframe (the host called it "the clip right there" [13:49–13:51])
- **Context**: Alyssa explaining why templates fail when the hook isn't adjusted [11:31–11:50].
- **The example**: "Most of the times people lead with 'three things to do this weekend in city name' and that's very broad... 'three things to do this weekend in city name that you've probably never heard of, especially if you're a mom of two.' So you've added additional context and framing of who this is for" [12:12–12:32].
- **What makes it excellent**: The reframe is additive, cheap, and carries all three components: topic (things to do), who (mom of two), lens (that you've never heard of = insider framing).

### Exemplar 2: Alyssa's Comfort Carousel Hooks
- **Context**: Eric asks for an example hook that reaches agents in their content journey [17:58–18:09].
- **The example**: "It's crazy what an 'I found you on Instagram' can do for my mental health as a real estate agent." / "People only post their wins on social. So here are some of my recent flops." / "Self-care as a realtor — I don't have to earn my rest." [18:16–19:09]
- **What makes it excellent**: None of them call out a content pain point directly. Each names a private feeling the reader already has, which is why "they follow along... because they actually feel seen."

### Exemplar 3: The Consumer Flip
- **Context**: Eric asks how to flip comfort content toward buyers who are 35 and haven't bought [19:55–20:09].
- **The example**: "Self-care as someone in their mid-30s who's yet to buy a house. And then you can talk about things that they can do to take care of their mindset... start the process... in the end you're offering them a solution by saying, 'Don't worry, you're not behind.' Lots of people are like that and guess what? That's exactly who I help" [20:10–20:51].
- **What makes it excellent**: Solution arrives last, as permission. The agent's offer is the natural conclusion of recognition, not the hook.

### Exemplar 4: Ricky Carruth's Create-Mode Posts (as read aloud)
- "If your real estate agent is still answering the phone at 9:00 p.m. on Saturday, you don't have an agent. You have someone who cares about your family's future." (~11,000 likes) [22:04–22:13]
- "Real estate agents wake up knowing they have zero guaranteed income, but they show up anyway. That's called courage." (~12,000 likes) [22:33–22:38]
- **What makes them excellent**: written FOR agents THROUGH the agent's lens so agents reshare them to look good to clients. Zero production.

### Anti-Exemplar: The Screenshot Review
- **What mediocre looks like**: "post a screenshot of a review of, you know, Zillow, Google review and it looks awful" [30:00–30:06].
- **Why it fails**: "I can just tell this is a review about someone and I'm not looking to even hire anybody. So, it's swipe. It's irrelevant to me" [31:22–31:31]. Authority content that looks like authority content is skipped by everyone not currently hiring.

## Signature Moves

- **Breathe, then grid**: opens a stuck-agent consult with "it's just data" and a six-month scan before any prescription [01:58–02:44] → **Deploy when**: any "I'm stuck at X followers" complaint.
- **Append the who**: never lets a local hook ship without a who-it's-for clause and a lens [12:12–13:29] → **Deploy when**: a hook reads like a listicle title.
- **Run the share test**: asks "would an agent share this to their stories to look good to clients?" before posting [23:36–23:46] → **Deploy when**: judging any comfort/authority piece.
- **Feeling first, offer last**: names the private state in the hook, holds the solution to the final slide [20:10–20:51] → **Deploy when**: any consumer-facing carousel.
- **Tag the goal**: labels a post grow / convert / nurture before reading its numbers [26:45–27:35] → **Deploy when**: reviewing performance.
- **Pull the hook line**: extracts one sentence from a review and makes it the hook [30:52–31:04] → **Deploy when**: a testimonial arrives.
- **Phone at the keys**: films the testimonial at handoff, not weeks later [32:44–33:17] → **Deploy when**: a closing is scheduled.

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4 — Acceptable | 7 — Good | 10 — Savant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience specificity | Names a segment ("first-time buyers") | Names a life stage + habit ("mid-30s renter who still scrolls Zillow at midnight") | One person you could picture at a specific table; the post reads like it was made for them alone |
| Hook framing | Topic only ("3 things to do in Spokane") | Topic + who ("...if you're a mom with kids under five") | Topic + who + lens ("...that you've probably never heard of") in one breath |
| Feel-seen factor | Gives a tip | Names a shared struggle | Names the private feeling nobody says out loud; reader thinks "do they have a camera in my room" |
| Shareability | Informative | Reader would send it to one friend | Reader reshares to their own audience because it positions THEM |
| Lo-fi authenticity | Clean template | Camera-roll photo + text, one message | Looks like a tweet screenshot; unmistakably human, no AI residue |
| Goal clarity | Post has a goal somewhere | Goal stated (grow/convert/nurture) | Goal stated AND the performance expectation is pre-declared |
| Opinion / lens | Reports the news | Adds "here's what I think" | Stands for something the audience would defend on your behalf |

## Methodology (progression)

1. **Diagnose** — outlier audit → attribute → hypothesis (Pattern 1).
2. **Aim** — one person, one-slide test, personal-lens inventory (Patterns 2, 3, 6).
3. **Frame** — Topic + Who + Lens on every local hook (Patterns 4, 5).
4. **Connect** — comfort/connection content, consumer flip, create-mode text posts, share test (Patterns 7–10).
5. **Mix** — local / listing / authority by goal; authority as story (Patterns 11, 12).
6. **Learn** — one experiment, data window, pivot or double down [35:52–36:03].
