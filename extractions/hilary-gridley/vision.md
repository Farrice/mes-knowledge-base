## The One-Line Genius

Everyone else in the roster **detects slop after it's produced**. Gridley **prevents it upstream** by turning a manager's tacit judgment into narrow, deployable feedback tools — so the quality bar exists *before* the work does.

Her signature move is a complete, teachable pipeline for encoding taste:

> **Edit pairs → pattern extraction → plain-English pass/fail criteria → rubric → deployable evaluator tool.**
> ("Column A: drafts people sent me. Column B: my revisions. What's the difference? Turn those patterns into 5 criteria. Write passing vs failing in plain English. Now write that rubric as a system prompt.")

That is a **taste-encoding factory** — a meta-method that can mint an evaluator from ANY corpus of before/after edits. Nothing in the 222-expert roster does this.

## The Extractable System (7 load-bearing frameworks)

1. **The Three Slop Roots** — decentralized AI use, no canonical context, no articulated "what good looks like." Diagnosis layer: slop is a *leadership clarity failure*, not a tooling failure. "Slop is a function of a lack of depth."
2. **The Judgment Encoding Pipeline** (crown jewel, above) — plus the deployment doctrine: **dozens of narrow purpose-driven tools, never one "second brain"** ("I was NOT making a second Hilary").
3. **AI-Native Situation Redesign** — situation → map today's reactive workflow step-by-step → reimagine with two forcing rules: *nothing is a surprise* (every signal proactive) and *the AI always goes one step further* (flag → 3 angles → mockups → conversion estimates → human picks → system cascades the update). Painted concretely: data sources, exact next actions, the human's role.
4. **The Three-Layer Quality Stack** — manage what good looks like at every altitude: how people spend time → which 10 of 100 projects → what good looks like per artifact. The second layer names the *other* slop: "10 applications nobody will ever use."
5. **Accountability Reframe** — "The job has never been about the work; it's about accountability for the work." Conversation shifts from "how are you using AI" to "what are you accountable for and what does good look like."
6. **Iterative Feedback Culture** — "This is too AI-generated — keep going" as a legitimate, non-binary feedback move. Breaks the bad/good binary that makes people defensive; installs shot → feedback → improve loops without the manager becoming the bottleneck.
7. **The Kick-the-Crutch Test** — build tools that *teach* while they assist: if you removed the tool tomorrow, the team should be better than before it existed. Virtuous cycle (people → systems → people) vs the slop doom loop (cognitive rot → worse systems → worse people).

**Kipp/Loop sidebar (referenced, attributed)**: Taste Profile (non-demographic ICP: beliefs, feelings, thresholds + core brand narratives as AI instructions), Craftspeople vs Context Carriers, Months-to-Minutes constraint ("prototype to CEO in 30 minutes, and it can't be slop").
