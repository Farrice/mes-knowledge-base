# Behavior Proof — Brand Direction Decision Spine

## Cold-start fixture

**Brief:** A premium modular desk-lamp company wants to feel technically credible, calm, and human. Discovery says professional buyers distrust gadget hype, home users fear complicated setup, and the flagship web product page is the purchase-critical surface. Custom photography is possible but must be quoted before approval.

## Baseline behavior

The baseline produces one attractive moodboard with roughly twenty images: brushed aluminum products, minimalist workspaces, muted blue palettes, geometric sans-serif type, and cinematic closeups. It calls the direction “Quiet Precision.” References have no named roles, all carry similar visual weight, and no distinction exists between research and client commitment. The board is approved by general preference, then colors and type are converted directly into tokens.

**Failure:** The output looks plausible but cannot answer why a specific reference matters, which discovery tension it resolves, what the photography implies for cost, or whether the direction works on the product page.

## Upgraded behavior

### A4 design challenges

1. How might the identity signal engineering credibility without reproducing cold gadget-brand conventions? Proving surface: product-page hero. Falsifier: viewers read the product as complex or clinical.
2. How might setup feel calm and obvious without making the product look simplistic? Proving surface: configuration module. Falsifier: hierarchy obscures the modular steps.
3. How might human presence add warmth without turning the brand into lifestyle decoration? Proving surface: photography-led benefit section. Falsifier: the lamp loses product authority.

### Internal exploration

The working board contains categorized research across industrial detail, human use, editorial type, light behavior, material finish, and modular diagrams. One beautiful neon gaming-desk reference is rejected: its attraction is real, but it answers none of the challenges and introduces an unwanted performance-gaming signal.

### Three client directions

- **Measured Instrument** — prioritizes calibrated detail, restrained grids, and technical diagrams. Strong on credibility; risks clinical distance.
- **Domestic Daylight** — prioritizes human use, soft natural light, and tactile surfaces. Strong on warmth; risks weakening modular proof.
- **Quiet Assembly** — combines precise component rhythm with daylight photography and calm instructional hierarchy. Strongest combined answer to challenges 1 and 2.

Dominant reference card for Quiet Assembly:

| Field | Decision |
|---|---|
| Role | Photography and composition |
| Borrowed quality | Directional daylight revealing material joints while leaving generous negative space for configuration copy |
| Design challenge | 1 and 2 |
| Decision weight | Dominant commitment |
| Implication | Requires controlled daylight product photography and a repeatable negative-space shot list |
| Cost / rights | Custom shoot; quote required before client approval |
| Rejection test | Reject if joints disappear, copy cannot sit cleanly, or the scene reads as generic lifestyle décor |

### Selection and borrowing

Quiet Assembly is selected. It borrows the exploded-view diagram convention from Measured Instrument and rejects Domestic Daylight's soft serif because it reduces interface legibility. The founder owns the final decision to keep the palette almost neutral despite research offering no decisive color preference.

### Proving surface

The first product-page hero fails at mobile width: the exploded diagram competes with the configurator and the human crop hides the joint detail. Revision moves the diagram below the hero, tightens the product crop, and reserves the hero's negative space for one setup promise.

### Reusable rule

“Use controlled daylight and visible joint detail to pair warmth with engineering proof. Preserve one quiet information priority per viewport; exploded diagrams support configuration below the hero rather than competing with the primary promise.”

## Behavior delta

| Test | Baseline | Upgraded |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery trace | None | Three evidence-backed challenges with falsifiers |
| Reference discipline | Attraction sufficient | Attraction-only neon reference rejected |
| Direction range | One board | Three strategic alternatives |
| Cost visibility | Hidden | Custom photography consequence named before approval |
| Human ownership | Implicit preference | Neutral palette marked taste-owned |
| Application proof | Tokens locked immediately | Mobile hero failure revises the direction before tokenization |
| Reuse | Style description | Applied rule with mechanic and constraint |

## Negative control

The build must fail if any of these are removed:

- a named reference role and borrowed quality;
- a design challenge tied to source evidence or an explicit taste owner;
- a proving surface with an observed result;
- a rejected attraction-only reference;
- a source boundary that keeps commercial outcomes UNTESTED.

## Result

Behavior-changing proof: **PASS** for the local cold-start fixture. The upgraded system changes selection, rejection, feasibility disclosure, in-context revision, and rule formation. Real-world revision reduction and commercial impact remain UNTESTED.
