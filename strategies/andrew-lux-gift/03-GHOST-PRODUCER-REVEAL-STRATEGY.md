# The Ghost Producer Reveal Strategy

## Your Hidden Weapon

You've produced tracks that got released under other artists' names. You've been the invisible force behind music people have heard.

**This isn't a liability. This is leverage.**

---

## Why Reveals Work

When KSHMR revealed he made "Tsunami" for DVBBS, it instantly transferred all credibility from that hit to his own name. One reveal changed his entire career trajectory — Top 15 DJ Mag, own label, headlining festivals.

**The "I made that" moment creates:**
1. Instant credibility (proof of ability)
2. Emotional connection (underdog getting credit)
3. Shareability ("holy shit, HE made that?")
4. Press/interview opportunities

---

## Before Revealing: The Legal Check

**Most ghost production agreements include confidentiality clauses.**

Before ANY reveal:
1. Review every contract for NDA restrictions
2. Identify tracks with no NDA or expired NDAs
3. Consider reaching out to the credited artist for permission
4. Consult with a music attorney if major credits are involved

### If NDAs Block Everything

You can still:
- Say "I've produced for major artists" (general claim)
- Demonstrate the SKILL LEVEL without naming tracks
- Let industry insiders spread the word privately
- Show production techniques that signal professional-grade work

---

## The Five Reveal Strategies

### Strategy 1: The Single Bombshell

**Best for:** One major credit that will shock people

**Format:**
> "I produced one of the biggest [genre] tracks of [year]. No one knows."
> [Show session file with your name and date]
> "I sold it for [amount]. It has [X] million streams."
> "This is the first time I've ever said this publicly."

### Strategy 2: The Progressive Reveal

**Best for:** Multiple credits to reveal over time

- Week 1: Hint ("There's something I've never talked about...")
- Week 2: Context ("I spent years making music for other people")
- Week 3: First reveal (smaller credit)
- Week 4: Bigger reveal
- Continue escalating

### Strategy 3: The Festival/Big Moment Reveal

**Best for:** Maximum impact at a key career moment

KSHMR revealed himself during Tiësto's Ultra set while simultaneously dropping their collaboration. Created an event, not just a post.

### Strategy 4: The Documentary Approach

**Best for:** Telling the full story authentically

Create or participate in a longer-form piece that tells the full ghost production story. The emotional arc (feeling invisible → taking credit → solo career) is compelling content.

### Strategy 5: The Soft Reveal

**Best for:** Credits you can't fully claim but can hint at

Produce content in the style of the ghost-produced track. Let people speculate. Neither confirm nor deny.

> "Here's how I'd produce a track like [famous song]"
> [Proceeds to recreate it almost exactly because you actually made it]

---

## Proof Elements

What makes a reveal believable:
- **Session files** with your name and dates
- **Date stamps** showing files existed before release
- **Demo versions** of the track
- **Sonic fingerprints** — techniques that match your known style

---

## The Narrative Frame

### Not: "I was a victim, feel sorry for me"
### Instead: "I paid my dues, now I'm ready"

Position ghost production as:
- Part of the journey (not a tragedy)
- Skill-building period
- Proof that the talent was always there

---

## Case Studies

**KSHMR:** Ghost produced "Tsunami" (DVBBS), tracks for Tiësto. Revealed at Ultra Music Festival. Now Top 15 DJ Mag with own label (Dharma Worldwide).

> "Going into dance music was like a second try at having a music career, and this time I wanted to make sure it reflects who I am."

**Bruno Mars:** The Smeezingtons wrote "Right Round" for Flo Rida, then appeared as featured artist on songs he wrote. Used production success to launch solo career.

> "My ambition from day one has been to become a pop star; I developed a parallel career as a producer purely because the path towards stardom tends to be long."

**Sia:** Wrote "Diamonds" for Rihanna, "Titanium" for Guetta (her demo voice was kept). The invisible songwriter became a superstar.

## Your Path

### Step 1: Audit
List every ghost production credit. Check legal status. Rank by impact.

### Step 2: Choose Strategy
Based on what's legally available — bombshell, progressive, or soft reveal.

### Step 3: Prepare
Gather session files and proof. Draft the narrative. Plan the content.

### Step 4: Time It
Align with a new release if possible. Have follow-up content ready.

### Step 5: Execute
Post the reveal. Direct attention to your own music. Tell the full story.

## The Bottom Line

Most producers have to build credibility from scratch.

You already have it — it's just uncredited.

**"I made that" is the most powerful thing a producer can say.**

For you, it's true.
