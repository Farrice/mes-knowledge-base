who this is for

A 38-year-old product manager with two children, a standing desk they rarely use, and a back that feels fine until it suddenly does not. They have saved stretching videos before. The videos did not survive the week. They do not need more exercises; they need a small, credible routine they can keep.

The founder's supplied edge is unusually concrete: 11 years as a physical therapist and direct experience with roughly 1,000 desk-worker backs. That is authority material, not permission for the system to invent a clinical conclusion.

07

the 90-day pilot

Weeks 1–3

Run the starting trio

Three founder-shot pieces per week. Observe; do not judge a piece inside 24 hours.

End of week 3

Make the first keep-or-revise call

Use qualified replies, saves, page visits, inquiries, and sales. Raw reach alone does not decide.

Weeks 4–6

Repeat the best pair

Change either the topic or the format, never both at once.

Day 45

Replace the frozen snapshot

Run a real 10–30 channel scan and see whether the four-minute opening repeats.

Weeks 7–12

Build repetitions around what survived

Add volume only after the signal holds across three batches.

Limits

what this proves and what it doesn't

This proves the content system can turn a thin, frozen input into a usable and honest client strategy without filling gaps with confident fiction. It does not prove current demand, clinical efficacy, conversion lift, cohort sales, or revenue. The synthetic video links are validation receipts, not live market evidence. Before production, the founder must supply the clinical observation, exercise selection, contraindications, permission-safe story, voice, and final approval.
