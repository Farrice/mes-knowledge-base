**Synthetic client-output proof. Evidence snapshot dated August 27, 2026; YouTube only; six frozen fixture videos.**

## Executive verdict

**The call:** own minimum-effective mobility for desk workers: short routines that survive real work and family life. Earn authority by showing what an 11-year physical therapist has actually observed, without letting AI invent the clinical lesson.

The strategy has one evidence-backed opening: a four-minute mobility result that performed at 6.4× its synthetic channel baseline. Everything else is either supplied by the founder, transferred as format, or explicitly held as a test.

## 1 · Who this is for

A 38-year-old product manager with two children, a standing desk they rarely use, and a back that feels fine until it suddenly does not. They have saved stretching videos before. The videos did not survive the week. They do not need more exercises; they need a small, credible routine they can keep.

## 2 · The open lane

The measured opening is narrow: one four-minute mobility video outperformed its channel baseline by 6.4×. The strategic opening is stronger but still a hypothesis: combine minimum-effective routines with a practitioner's pattern recognition, aimed specifically at desk workers rather than the general mobility audience.

## 3 · Where to aim

Start with two Ring-2 buckets, Four-Minute Desk Reset and Fine Until It Isn't, plus one Ring-3 authority bucket: Desk-Back Clinic. Do not chase general fitness reach. The fixture's larger coaching-business winners belong to other buyers and were excluded despite stronger raw performance.

## 4 · What to make

1. **Replacement demonstrations:** a bounded short routine replacing an abandoned long one.
2. **Practitioner observations:** one real pattern from 11 years of treating desk backs.
3. **Qualified tests:** compare conflicting mobility rules and explain the decision boundary.

The first item is evidence-backed inside the fixture. Items two and three remain on HOLD until the founder supplies the actual clinical judgment.

## 5 · How to make it

Publish three founder-shot pieces per week: two direct-to-buyer shorts and one authority explanation. Use demonstrations, a whiteboard, or a single paper prop only when it reduces cognitive work for the viewer. AI may organize evidence, generate reaction questions, and structure options. The founder owns the exercise selection, clinical interpretation, story, voice, and final approval.

## The 90-day pilot

| Window | Action | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–3 | Three reps per week across the starting trio | Observe; no verdict inside 24 hours. |
| End of week 3 | Compare qualified replies, saves, page visits, inquiries, and sales | Keep, revise, or bench each bucket. |
| Weeks 4–6 | Repeat the best topic-format pair; replace the weakest from the bench | Do not change topic and format simultaneously. |
| Day 45 | Refresh the real market evidence | Decide whether the four-minute opening repeats beyond the fixture. |
| Weeks 7–12 | Build repetitions around the surviving pair | Add volume only after the signal survives three batches. |

## Human creative ownership

Before any piece moves into production, the founder must answer:

- What did I actually observe across desk-worker clients?
- What is the exact decision rule I would defend in front of another physical therapist?
- Who should not follow this advice?
- Which story do I have permission to tell?

Until those answers exist, the system has a research queue, not publishable content.
