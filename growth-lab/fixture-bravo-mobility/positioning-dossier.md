# Positioning Dossier — Fixture Bravo Mobility

## The buyer

A 38-year-old product manager with two children, a standing desk they rarely use, and a herniated-disc scare still sitting in the back of their mind. They do not want a new identity as a fitness person. They want their back to stop dictating the day.

## What they already say

- “My back is fine until it isn't.”
- “Stretching videos never stick.”
- “I don't have an hour a day.”

These are intake statements from the frozen fixture, not researched market prevalence.

## Credibility the founder actually supplied

- Physical therapist for 11 years.
- Direct experience with roughly 1,000 desk-worker backs.
- $89/month online mobility cohort.

## Positioning hypothesis

**The minimum-effective mobility coach for desk workers who need a routine that survives real work and family life.**

This is a LIKELY positioning hypothesis, not a proven market claim. Its strongest support is the buyer's time constraint plus the fixture's one direct outlier around a four-minute mobility routine.

## Human-owned material still required

- The clinical pattern the founder saw repeatedly across those 1,000 backs.
- The exercises and contraindications behind any “four-minute” promise.
- A real client story the founder has permission to tell.
- The founder's actual disagreement with generic stretching advice.
