## Verdict (lead with it)

**Lane: dual-track, priority = LinkedIn-native flagship-first, fitness prospects stay in but re-routed.** Don't re-aim the whole offer off fitness — split by per-prospect CHANNEL truth. The 15-name list gets re-sorted on two axes: (1) posted/engaged on LinkedIn in the last 30 days, (2) authority-asymmetry (big off-platform proof, thin LinkedIn presence). Prospects clearing both (Jonah Rosner, Jennifer Broxterman) become the day-1 flagship sends — anchor accounts, not one-of-fifteen. Prospects clearing only activity (Adriana Morrison) get the standard cold-DM motion. Prospects clearing only authority (Rusin, Kassem Hanson) don't get disqualified — they get re-routed to wherever they're actually alive (IG DM, their own coaching funnel), with LinkedIn demoted to "credibility page" they'll check after the first reply, never the delivery venue. For the fitness prospects overall: strip "your LinkedIn is neglected" from the pitch — lead with their own list (newsletter/email/clientele), let LinkedIn be the doorway to the DM, not the promised destination.

**Pricing: hold $2,000, reframe the language from defense to status claim.** "I don't want to be a cheap option" is a tell — it's justifying downward. Replace with: *"This is founding-rate pricing while the system is building its first track record — $2,000, three pilot seats, then it moves."* Anchor isn't Cooz's $3K (doesn't survive checking) — it's the buyer's OWN verified economics: Rusin sells at $2-4K, Broxterman ran a $42K launch. $2,000 is priced INSIDE what they already charge their customers, which reads as a rounding error to them, not a discount ask. Premium signal comes from the spec-sample artifact's craft (survives-forwarding test) and from capacity language (3 seats, not open enrollment) — never from moving the digit.

### The Moves (one per expert)

*Steal this*: When a prospect list is flat, pick the ONE name whose off-platform authority most exceeds their on-platform presence and over-invest there — a single landed flagship reframes every subsequent pitch's evidence class.

**Lara Acosta** — *Move in action*: Add a 6th qualification gate ahead of audience-size — "posted on LinkedIn in the last 30 days AND visible comment/reply activity" — re-rank all 15 prospects by it, promote Adriana Morrison/Jonah Rosner/Jennifer Broxterman to day-1 sends, pull Rusin/Hanson out of the cold-DM lane entirely (not wrong-fit, just a dead first-touch channel).

*Steal this*: When a channel diagnostic and a lane diagnostic get confused for each other, the fix is almost never "change lanes" — it's "route delivery to where THIS prospect is alive."

### The Collision

The exact spark: **Ross's flagship-concentration logic** ("put disproportionate weight on the single account with the biggest authority/presence gap — that gap IS the opportunity") met **Lara's SLAY-framework activity gate** ("virality/reach is downstream of the AUDIENCE's platform behavior, not the creator's follower count — a dead feed can't surface a DM no matter how good the target"). Alone, Ross's logic says: chase Rusin hardest, he's the best story. Alone, Lara's logic says: drop Rusin, his feed is dead. Collided, they produce a rule neither stated alone: the flagship-worthy account and the reachable-by-DM account are different populations, and a name can qualify for one without qualifying for the other — so route the SAME artifact through a DIFFERENT channel depending on which population the prospect belongs to (LinkedIn DM for the reachable ones, IG DM/their own funnel for the dark-but-huge ones). Oren's forwarding-artifact standard and Farrice's dispatch reframe then generalized this into the operating principle below.

---

### The Principle

**Reachability is a per-prospect CHANNEL variable, not a lane variable.** When a "is this channel wrong for this whole market?" question surfaces (fitness/IG-native vs. LinkedIn), resist collapsing it into a binary lane decision. Instead, run two axes simultaneously per prospect — (1) authority-asymmetry (how much off-platform proof exceeds on-platform presence — this tells you WHO'S worth pursuing hardest) and (2) live-channel test (where do they actually engage — this tells you WHERE to deliver). A prospect who fails axis 2 on your default channel isn't disqualified and doesn't indict the lane; the failure re-routes delivery to wherever they're demonstrably alive, with the "wrong" channel demoted to a credibility page they'll check after the fact, never the send venue. Applies anywhere you're tempted to ask "is this whole market reachable through channel X" — the real question is almost always "which prospects are alive on X, and where are the rest actually alive instead."

### Your Rep

Different problem, same principle: You're deciding whether to pitch the Proof-to-Market $2,500 sprint to supplement/performance brand founders via cold LinkedIn outreach, but your best-fit prospect list skews DTC operators who live on Twitter/X and IG, not LinkedIn. Before concluding "wrong lane, re-aim the offer" — run the same two-axis sort: which of these founders have real LinkedIn activity in the last 30 days (reachable-by-DM tier) vs. which have the biggest brand-authority/LinkedIn-presence gap (flagship-worthy but dark-feed tier)? Then dispatch: LinkedIn DM for the first group, X/IG DM (or a warm intro through a mutual) for the second — with LinkedIn demoted to the credibility page they check after replying, never the venue you're betting the whole outreach on.
