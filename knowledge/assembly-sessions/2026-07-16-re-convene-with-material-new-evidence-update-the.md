## The Collision

The spark: **Stockton's funnel-taxonomy read of Adam's audit** (a Clarifier lead-magnet that broke the "must be free" rule and built its own sales bridge) collided with **April's so-what derivation applied to Adam's own positioning** (his stated JTBD is narrow — brand marketers, copy-paste pain — not horizontal).

Two domains: *funnel mechanics* (does the lead asset terminate in its own sale?) and *positioning theory* (what does the market leader's actual language reveal about category size?). Neither alone resolves the Fork A/Fork B debate — Stockton's read says "steal the mechanism," April's read says "don't steal the ambition." Together they produce the reframe: copy the *SKU structure* from the vertical operator while explicitly rejecting his implied category size — take the audit-as-bridge mechanic, leave the horizontal inference on the table.

That collision is what let Tommy's sequencing (close warm, post after) and Chris's artifact-reuse (one deliverable, two jobs) snap into a single build instead of four competing recommendations — because once you know the audit must (a) be a Clarifier that bridges its own sale, and (b) NOT be read as proof of a horizontal category, the only sane build is: sell it now, warm, silently — and derive any bigger claim later, backward, from what the audits actually surface.

## The Principle

**Don't choose the position — manufacture the evidence, then let the position fall out of it.**

The net-new principle (nobody stated it alone, it only exists in the combination): warm audits aren't weak proxy-evidence to be discounted next to a "real" cold test — they're the evidence-manufacturing machine itself, running one artifact on two clocks simultaneously.

- **Clock 1 (this week):** sell the audit warm (Jen, Josh & Katie, Andrea) → cash + delivery proof + a self-referential receipt (Farrice's own 200-skill exhaust, no client names, ships on its own schedule regardless of sales cycle).
- **Clock 2 (day 60+):** the FIRST cold, arm's-length sale of whatever claim gets derived from the warm audits — not asserted up front — is the only event allowed to reopen the Path A decision.

The mechanism that makes this different from "just test the offer": run the audit across 3–4 domains Farrice already has trust in, and treat the *pattern of disconnects* across those unrelated businesses as raw material for a positioning insight — not the offer itself. If a real cross-domain pattern emerges, it's one a single-vertical marketer (Adam) structurally cannot see, because he only ever works inside one function. That's the only kind of insight allowed to earn a horizontal claim. Everything else stays Fork A, silently, with cash moving now.

Transferable frame: **when a competitor's success creates pivot pressure, separate "can I deliver this for money" (test now, cheaply, with people who already trust you) from "should I become known for this" (test later, expensively, only with a stranger who owes you nothing) — and never let the first test's success count as evidence for the second question.**
