## Pitch Bible: "The Forge" (1-Hour Drama)

### Logline
A retired Army demolitions expert turned high school shop teacher discovers that the failing vocational program she was hired to save is actually a front for a county commissioner's money laundering operation—and the only way to expose it is to teach her students to build something real enough to make the scheme visible.

### The Curse
Every compelling story starts with a curse—an internal flaw the hero can't see in themselves.

**Elena Voss's Curse:** She believes she can fix anything by breaking it apart first. In the Army, that made her exceptional. In demolitions, you solve problems through controlled destruction. But her personal life is wreckage—two ex-husbands, estranged daughter, apartment full of takeout containers—because she applies the same logic to people. She tears things down and calls it honesty.

The curse means: Elena *can* see the corruption. She has the eyes for structural failure. But she can't build the coalition to fight it because she keeps detonating the relationships she needs.

### Three-Act Gravitational Field

**Act I — The Hook (Pilot + Episodes 2-3)**
Elena takes the job at Northfield High expecting a paycheck and zero emotional investment. She discovers the shop program has no functioning equipment, no budget, and students who've been parked here because they "aren't college material." 

The INCITING INCIDENT: She stumbles onto purchase orders for $340,000 in CNC machines that don't exist. The money went somewhere. The program is a ghost.

The gravitational pull of Act I: Elena wants to *leave*. Every instinct tells her to report it and walk. But a student—Marcus, 17, juvenile record, gifted engineer—builds a working lathe from scrapped car parts, and Elena sees something she hasn't felt in years: someone worth teaching.

**Act II — The Struggle (Episodes 4-7)**
Elena starts teaching for real while quietly investigating the financial trail. The students begin producing actual work—furniture, metalwork, a motorcycle restoration—that attracts community attention. This is the *worst possible outcome* for Commissioner Dale Harding, because a visible, successful program makes the invisible money harder to hide.

The MIDPOINT REVERSAL: Elena discovers the corruption goes higher than Harding. The school superintendent signed off on the ghost purchases. Reporting it to the district isn't an option—the district *is* the problem. Elena must go outside the system.

Her curse activates: she pushes away the one ally she has (fellow teacher and potential love interest Ray Okonkwo) by "being honest" about his passivity. She's alone by design—her own design.

**Act III — The Resolution (Episodes 8-10)**
Elena weaponizes the shop program itself. The students enter their work in the State Vocational Competition—a public event that requires district accounting disclosure. If Northfield's "investment" in vocational education becomes visible, the gap between reported spending and actual equipment becomes undeniable.

The CLIMACTIC SEQUENCE: Commissioner Harding tries to shut down the program the week before competition. Elena has to choose: protect the students by backing down (safe, but the corruption continues) or push forward and risk exposing the kids to retaliation.

Marcus makes the choice for her. He enters independently.

**The Curse Breaks:** Elena learns that she doesn't have to demolish the system to expose it. She just has to build something so genuine that the fraud becomes obvious by contrast. The act of construction becomes the act of demolition.

### Why This Works (Pressfield Mechanics)
- **The professional world carries the theme:** Demolitions expert learning to build = the spiritual journey literalized through craft
- **Resistance is the antagonist beneath the antagonist:** Harding is the visible villain, but Elena's real enemy is her own pattern of destruction. The curse makes the external conflict an echo of the internal one
- **The climax is inevitable from page one:** Once you know an expert at breaking things must learn to build, the ending writes itself—you just don't know the specific shape until you get there
- **The student-teacher dynamic is the moral engine:** Elena can't save herself, but she can save Marcus. And saving Marcus forces her to confront why she couldn't save her own daughter
