## Cold Open: "Fault Lines" — Episode 1

INT. LOS ANGELES SUPERIOR COURT — HALLWAY — 7:42 AM

The hallway is empty except for DETECTIVE ANA REYES, 44, standing outside Courtroom 9B with a Styrofoam cup of machine coffee she hasn't touched. She reads the coffee's temperature through the cup—too hot seven minutes ago, perfect now, but she won't drink it. The ritual is holding it.

The courtroom doors are polished oak with brass push plates that have been touched by ten thousand palms. Near the bottom of the left plate, someone's ring left a scratch in the shape of a comma.

She notices this. She notices everything that doesn't matter and nothing that does. That's why she's good at this job and terrible at being married.

The doors open. DEPUTY DA KEVIN PARK, 38, white shirt, no jacket—he runs hot, always—steps out and doesn't look at her.

PARK
    They took the plea.

REYES
    Which version?

PARK
    Second degree. Twelve to fifteen. 
    He'll do eight.

REYES
    Loomis.

PARK
    What about him?

REYES
    Loomis was in that house for six 
    hours. He tied her to a kitchen chair 
    with an extension cord and he ate her 
    food while she sat there. He made a 
    sandwich. Second degree is a legal 
    fiction.

PARK
    It's a legal outcome. There's a 
    difference.

REYES
    Not for her.

Park finally looks at her. He takes the coffee from her hand—the one she was never going to drink—and takes a sip.

PARK
    It's cold.

REYES
    It's been cold.

He hands it back. They stand in the hallway with the scratch-mark comma on the door plate and the machine coffee that served its purpose the moment she picked it up.

PARK (CONT'D)
    You caught a new one?

REYES
    Griffith Park. Hiker found remains 
    in a drainage culvert off the Vista 
    del Valle fire road. Skeletal. ME 
    says minimum eighteen months in the 
    ground.

PARK
    So it's old.

REYES
    Everything under the ground is old. 
    Doesn't mean it's finished.

She drops the cup in a wastebin that's too full. The cup sits on top, balanced. She doesn't push it down.

REYES (CONT'D)
    I pulled the missing persons reports 
    for the area. Two possibles. A trail 
    runner named David Uribe, reported 
    2023. And a park maintenance worker, 
    Sarah Kang, reported same year but 
    different month.

PARK
    Which one do you like?

REYES
    I don't like either of them. They're 
    both somebody's worst day.

She walks toward the elevator. Park watches her go, then looks at the cup balanced on the overflowing trash can. He pushes it down.

CUT TO:

EXT. GRIFFITH PARK — VISTA DEL VALLE FIRE ROAD — DAY

The drainage culvert is fifteen feet below the road, accessible only by a steep path of loose decomposed granite that someone has recently marked with orange surveyor tape.

Reyes stands at the top, looking down. She doesn't go down yet. She memorizes the approach first—where a person carrying weight would have to step, where the footing gives, where you'd pause if you were hauling something heavy in the dark.

She's not looking at the crime scene. She's looking at the route *to* the crime scene. Because the body tells you how someone died. The path tells you who brought them here.
