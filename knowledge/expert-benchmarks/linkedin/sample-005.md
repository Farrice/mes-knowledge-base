# LinkedIn Carousel: "7 Sentences That Prove You're a Better Writer Than You Think"

## Nicolas Cole Sentence Craft — Applied to Carousel Format

---

### Slide 1 (Cover)
**7 sentences that prove you're a better writer than you think.**

Most people don't have a writing problem.
They have an editing problem.

---

### Slide 2
**Your sentences end on the wrong word.**

The last word echoes in the reader's mind.

If that echo is "is" or "it" or "the" — the echo is empty.

Fix: restructure every sentence so the final word is a noun of substance or a verb of power.

❌ "Writing well is something that most people struggle with."
✅ "Most people struggle with writing well."

The fix took 3 seconds. The impact lasts forever.

---

### Slide 3
**Your paragraphs repeat themselves.**

Read each paragraph and ask one question: "What NEW information does this add?"

If the answer is "emphasis" — that's a polite word for repetition.

The reader's subconscious registers it as: "This writer ran out of things to say."

Delete it. Or merge it. Never let it breathe.

---

### Slide 4
**Your commas reveal confused thinking.**

Count the commas in every sentence.

0-2 commas: controlled thought.
3-6 commas: tangled thinking.
7+: either a stylistic masterpiece or a disaster.

Most of us live in the 3-6 zone — the valley of death.

If you can't control your commas, you can't control your argument.

---

### Slide 5
**Your adverbs are training wheels.**

"He walked slowly" → "He crept."
"She spoke loudly" → "She boomed."
"They moved quickly" → "They bolted."

Every adverb is a confession that you chose the wrong verb.

Find the verb that needs no modification. That's the verb.

---

### Slide 6
**Your vocabulary is working against you.**

Write at a fourth-grade level. Add ONE elevated word per paragraph.

One = intellectual reward.
Two = friction.
Three = punishment.

The reader should feel smarter after each sentence — not exhausted.

---

### Slide 7
**Your first sentence is throat-clearing.**

"In this post, I'm going to share..." → DELETE.

The reader decides in 10 words. Those words can't be stage directions.

Lead with curiosity. Lead with specificity. Lead with a number.

Never lead with a map of what you're about to say.

---

### Slide 8 (CTA)
**The compression test:**

Take your last published piece. Read ONLY the final word of every sentence. Write them in a list.

If more than 20% are small words (is, it, the, was, a) — your writing is leaking power at every boundary.

Fix those endings. Watch everything change.

### Cole Craft Metrics:
- Terminal words on every slide: "word," "forever," "say," "argument," "verb," "exhausted," "say," "change" — zero weak endings
- Adverb count across 8 slides: 0
- Commas per sentence: 0-2 throughout (one intentional 3-comma list for demonstration)
- Flesch-Kincaid: Grade 4.8
- Information density: every slide introduces exactly ONE new concept — zero overlap
- Compression: each slide is under 60 words despite teaching a complete principle
