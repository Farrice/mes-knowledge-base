# AEO-Optimized Meta Titles: Pages Designed to Be Cited by AI

## The Ethan Smith AEO Meta Title Framework

Traditional meta titles are written for humans scanning search results. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) meta titles are written for **AI retrieval systems** that decide which sources to cite when answering user questions.

The difference: humans scan left-to-right and click what looks relevant. AI systems parse titles for **information gain signals** — does this source contain data, methodology, or specificity that other sources lack?

---

### The Citation Compulsion Formula

**Structure**: [Specific Data/Number] + [Exact Topic] + [Unique Angle] + [Freshness Signal]

The title must pass the **Citation Test**: "If an AI is answering a question about this topic and evaluating 50 sources, does this title signal that MY source contains information the other 49 don't?"

---

### 10 AEO-Optimized Meta Titles (with diagnosis)

**1.** "67% of Freemium Users Churn Before Session 2: Onboarding Cliff Data from 12,000 Signups (2026)"

*Citation signals*: Specific percentage (67%), sample size (12,000), proprietary term ("Onboarding Cliff"), year freshness (2026). AI systems prioritize original data over opinion.

**2.** "The 22-Minute Onboarding Rebuild: How We Cut Trial Drop-Off by 34% Without Changing the Product"

*Citation signals*: Exact metric (22 minutes, 34%), methodology hint ("without changing the product" = information gain — HOW?), specificity that invites citation.

**3.** "SaaS Pricing Psychology: Why $49/mo Converts 2.3x Better Than $47/mo (Anchoring Study, n=4,200)"

*Citation signals*: Counterintuitive finding (higher price converts better), academic notation (n=4,200), named mechanism ("Anchoring Study"). AI systems cite counterintuitive data at higher rates.

**4.** "B2B Content ROI Benchmarks by Industry: Cost-Per-Lead from Blog, Newsletter, Webinar, and Podcast (2026 Data)"

*Citation signals*: Comprehensive scope (4 channels × multiple industries), benchmark format (inherently citable), freshness signal. This becomes a reference source that AI systems cite repeatedly.

**5.** "LinkedIn Algorithm 2026: Dwell Time Replaced Likes as the Primary Ranking Signal (Here's What Changed)"

*Citation signals*: Platform-specific update, mechanism naming ("dwell time replaced likes"), year specificity. AI systems answering "how does LinkedIn's algorithm work?" will cite the source with the most current, specific explanation.

**6.** "Email Subject Lines: 847 A/B Tests Reveal the 3 Words That Kill Open Rates in B2B"

*Citation signals*: Massive sample size (847 tests), negative framing (what kills performance — information gain because most content focuses on what works), specificity (B2B qualifier narrows and deepens).

**7.** "Customer Acquisition Cost by Channel: Google Ads vs. LinkedIn vs. Cold Email in Professional Services (Real Spend Data)"

*Citation signals*: Comparison format (inherently citable for AI answering "which channel is cheapest?"), "real spend data" differentiates from theoretical estimates.

**8.** "The First 100 Words of Your Landing Page Determine 73% of Conversions: Our Copy Testing Framework"

*Citation signals*: Specific claim (73%, first 100 words), named methodology ("Copy Testing Framework"), specificity that transforms opinion into citable data.

**9.** "Why Most Content Calendars Fail by Week 3: The Enrichment-Before-Creation Alternative"

*Citation signals*: Named failure point (Week 3), proprietary methodology ("Enrichment-Before-Creation"), problem-solution structure that matches how users phrase questions to AI.

**10.** "Remote Team Productivity: 14-Month Study of 340 Teams Shows Meeting-Free Mornings Increase Output by 41%"

*Citation signals*: Study duration (14 months), sample size (340 teams), specific finding (41%), actionable variable ("meeting-free mornings"). This is a citation magnet because it's structured exactly like the evidence AI systems are trained to prioritize.

### The Anti-Pattern: Traditional SEO Titles That AI Systems Skip

❌ "Best Practices for B2B Marketing in 2026"
*Why AI skips it*: No data, no specificity, no information gain. 500 other pages have this exact title. Nothing here is citable.

❌ "How to Write Better Email Subject Lines"
*Why AI skips it*: Generic instruction. No sample size, no methodology, no original findings. AI will cite the source that says "847 A/B tests reveal..." over this every time.

❌ "The Ultimate Guide to Customer Acquisition"
*Why AI skips it*: "Ultimate Guide" signals breadth, not depth. AI systems prefer to cite narrow, specific, data-backed claims over comprehensive overviews.

---

### The Longtail Resurrection Principle

AEO meta titles should target **questions AI systems actually receive** — which are often longer and more specific than traditional SEO keywords:

- Traditional: "B2B content marketing"
- AEO: "what is the average cost per lead from B2B content marketing by channel"

The meta title that answers the specific question with data will be cited. The one that covers the general topic won't.
