## Consumer Posture Profile: Premium Online Fitness Coaching ($297/mo)

### The Radical Individual (Not a Demographic)

We're not selling to "men 25-40 who want to lose weight." That's census data, not psychology. We're selling to one specific human—and then finding more people who share his interior architecture.

**Meet James.**

### THREE-DIMENSIONAL POSTURE SCAN

**Dimension 1: Occupation Posture**
James is a senior product manager at a mid-stage startup. He makes decisions all day—roadmap priorities, feature cuts, stakeholder management. By 6 PM, his decision-making capacity is depleted. This is why he orders the same dinner three nights a week (chicken, rice, broccoli from the Thai place) and wears the same rotation of four outfits.

What he *doesn't* realize: his fitness inconsistency isn't laziness. It's decision fatigue expressing itself as physical neglect. He has bandwidth for exactly zero more decisions about sets, reps, meal prep, and supplement timing.

**Dimension 2: Activity Posture**
James played lacrosse in college. He still identifies as "an athlete who got busy." Every January, he buys a gym membership. By March, he's going twice a week. By May, he's going when he "feels like it." By July, it's a line item on his credit card he ignores.

The pattern isn't failure—it's the wrong *type* of engagement. James thrives on external structure and team accountability. Solo gym sessions feel like homework. He needs a coach the way he needs a product deadline: someone who makes the decision for him and holds the timeline.

**Dimension 3: Thought Process Posture**
James consumes fitness content the way he consumes product research: in intensive bursts. He'll spend 90 minutes on a Sunday reading about creatine loading protocols, then do nothing with the information. Knowledge acquisition *feels* like progress. It isn't.

His internal narrative: "I know what to do, I just need to execute." This is the most dangerous sentence in consumer psychology because it's partially true—he *does* know a lot—but knowledge without structure is decoration.

### THE KRISTEN STEWART TEST

*Would James feel embarrassed if a peer saw him engaging with our content?*

**FAILS the test:** "Transform your body in 90 days!" — James would never share this. It signals desperation. A product manager doesn't "transform"—he optimizes. The language of transformation assumes something is broken.

**FAILS the test:** Shirtless before/after photos — James doesn't want to be the guy who posts body shots. His identity is competence, not aesthetics.

**PASSES the test:** "Your operating system is your body. Most founders skip the firmware update." — This maps fitness to his *occupational identity*. He can share this because it positions self-care as professional infrastructure, not vanity.

**PASSES the test:** "I outsource my fitness decisions the way I outsource my accounting. Same logic." — This reframes coaching as delegation, which is a *status behavior* in his world, not a weakness signal.

### WORLD DESIGN: What Does James's Life Look Like AFTER?

Don't describe what the product does. Describe the world he lives in once he's a client:

- He wakes up at 6:15 and checks his phone. The workout is already programmed. He doesn't decide anything—he just starts.
- His co-founder notices he looks different and asks what changed. He says "I got a coach" the same way he'd say "I switched to a standing desk." Casual. Integrated. Not a confession.
- On Sundays, he doesn't spend 90 minutes researching. The coach sent the plan. He meal preps because the list was texted to him. The cognitive load dropped to zero.
- He's not "fit." He's *capable*. He did a company 5K without training specifically for it and finished in the top third. He didn't post about it.

### MESSAGING ARCHITECTURE (Posture-Aligned)

**Headline:** "Accept the body your calendar built."
**Subhead:** *Most fitness programs add complexity. This one removes it. One coach. One plan. Zero decisions.*

**Proof Frame (Posture-Matched):** Don't use testimonials that say "I lost 30 pounds!" Use testimonials that say "I stopped thinking about fitness. It just happens now." The outcome James wants isn't a body—it's a system that runs without his attention.

**Pricing Frame:** $297/mo positions this as "less than the gym membership + the supplements + the app subscriptions he already pays." It's not an expense—it's consolidation.
