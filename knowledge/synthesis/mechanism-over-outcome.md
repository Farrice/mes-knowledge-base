# Mechanism Over Outcome

## Cross-Domain Pattern
HOW beats WHAT in every domain — but each domain calls it something different. A "mechanism" in copywriting is a "world" in brand strategy is a "professional world" in screenwriting is "context architecture" in AI strategy. The underlying principle is identical: audiences don't buy outcomes — they buy the explanation for WHY the outcome happens. The mechanism is the bridge between promise and belief. Without it, every promise is just another claim in a sea of identical claims.

## How It Manifests

### In Copywriting (Luke Iha)
"The mechanism makes the promise believable." The Million-Dollar Mechanisms System distinguishes UMP (Unique Mechanism of Problem — why the problem exists) from UMS (Unique Mechanism of Solution — why THIS solution works). The SIN Filter (Simple, Intuitive, New) validates mechanisms before deployment. Critical insight: "Most mechanism failures are complexity failures, not accuracy failures. The audience doesn't need to understand the SCIENCE — they need to understand the CONCEPT intuitively." And the most powerful finding: "The best mechanisms are already suspected. Peak resonance occurs when the audience already SUSPECTS the mechanism but hasn't articulated it." The mechanism names what they already feel.

### In Brand/Sales (Dai Media)
World Design: describe the client's life AFTER the transformation, not the product features. This is mechanism deployed as identity projection. The consumer doesn't need to know how the product works — they need to see themselves in the world the product creates. The Kristen Stewart Test (identity alignment) is a mechanism check: would this person inhabit this world? If not, no feature list will compensate. The mechanism isn't the product. The mechanism is the world the product enables.

### In AI Strategy (Dan Koe)
"AI quality is a direct function of context and constraints YOU provide, not model intelligence." The 4C Cognitive Architecture (Context → Clarification → Creation → Concerns) is a mechanism for thought — the structure of how you frame a problem determines the quality of the output. The model is the engine, but the context is the fuel. This is mechanism thinking applied to human-AI interaction: the HOW of prompting beats the WHAT of the model.

### In Screenwriting (Pressfield + Connelly)
Pressfield: the professional world carries the theme. A story about a detective isn't about crime — it's about the mechanism of justice confronting the mechanism of chaos. The environment IS the mechanism of character transformation. Connelly: showing via telling detail IS the mechanism of belief. A reader who sees one precise detail believes the entire world is real. The mechanism of fiction is the same as the mechanism of copywriting — give the audience one irreducible piece of evidence that the world you're describing exists.

## Actionable Implication
- **For routing**: When a task requires persuasion (copy, content, sales, brand), the system should check: "Is there a mechanism?" If the output is all outcome and no mechanism, it fails regardless of quality score. This should be a routing-level check, not just a quality gate item.
- **For unified vocabulary**: Create a cross-domain translation table. When Luke Iha says "mechanism," when Dai Media says "world design," when Pressfield says "professional world," and when Koe says "context architecture" — they mean the same thing. The system should recognize all four terms as triggering the same pattern.
- **For content creation**: Every piece of persuasive content needs a "mechanism sentence" — one sentence that explains WHY the outcome happens. If you can't write that sentence, the content doesn't have a mechanism yet and shouldn't be produced.
- **For skill evolution**: Test mechanism-injection across skills. Does adding a UMP/UMS check to LinkedIn content (Lara Acosta) improve adversarial resilience? Does adding World Design to copywriting (Luke Iha) improve identity resonance?
