## SHOT TYPES & FRAMING TAXONOMY

### Shot Size Spectrum (Distance from Subject)

| Shot Type | Abbreviation | Framing | Psychological Impact | When to Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme Wide Shot | EWS | Subject tiny or invisible in vast environment | Awe, isolation, epic scale, detachment | Opening establishing shots, landscapes, conveying smallness |
| Wide Shot / Long Shot | WS / LS | Full body head-to-toe with significant environment | Freedom or entrapment depending on framing | Character introduction, action sequences, environmental context |
| Full Shot | FS | Head-to-toe, subject fills frame | Power, grace, physicality, costume showcase | Fashion, dance, fight scenes, character authority |
| Medium Wide / American Shot | MWS | Knees/mid-thigh up | Confidence, readiness for action | Dialogue with body language, Western-style standoffs |
| Medium Shot | MS | Waist up | Connection, empathy, natural conversation | Dialogue scenes, interviews, product interaction |
| Medium Close-Up | MCU | Chest up | Intimacy without intrusion | Emotional dialogue, reaction shots |
| Close-Up | CU | Face fills frame | Raw emotion, intensity, vulnerability | Key emotional beats, reveals, character decisions |
| Extreme Close-Up | ECU | Single feature (eye, lip, hand) | Hyper-intimacy, tension, obsession | Thriller tension, product macro, emotional climax |
| Insert Shot | INS | Object or detail | Information delivery, symbolic weight | Props, clues, textures, product details |

### Camera Angles

| Angle | Description | Psychological Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Eye Level | Camera at subject's eye height | Neutral, objective, relatable |
| Low Angle | Camera below subject looking up | Power, dominance, heroism, intimidation |
| High Angle | Camera above subject looking down | Vulnerability, weakness, insignificance |
| Dutch Angle / Canted | Camera tilted on its axis | Unease, disorientation, psychological tension |
| Bird's Eye / Overhead | Directly above looking down | God-like perspective, pattern revelation, detachment |
| Worm's Eye | Ground level looking up | Extreme power, monumentality, childlike wonder |
| Over-the-Shoulder (OTS) | Camera behind one subject looking at another | Conversational intimacy, voyeuristic tension |
| Point of View (POV) | Camera as character's eyes | Immersion, empathy, horror/thriller tension |

### Camera Movements

| Movement | Description | Emotional Effect | AI Prompt Keywords |
|---|---|---|---|
| Static / Locked Tripod | No movement | Stability, observation, contemplation | "static shot, locked camera, tripod" |
| Pan | Horizontal rotation on fixed axis | Surveying, following action, revealing space | "camera pans left/right, horizontal sweep" |
| Tilt | Vertical rotation on fixed axis | Revealing scale, power dynamics | "camera tilts up/down, vertical reveal" |
| Dolly Forward / Push-In | Camera moves toward subject | Increasing intimacy, focus, tension | "dolly forward, push-in, camera approaches" |
| Dolly Backward / Pull-Out | Camera moves away from subject | Revelation, isolation, context expansion | "dolly backward, pull-out, camera retreats" |
| Tracking / Lateral Dolly | Camera moves alongside subject | Following journey, companionship | "tracking shot, lateral movement, follows subject" |
| Crane / Jib | Camera rises or descends vertically | Grandeur, establishing, emotional shift | "crane shot, camera rises, ascending view" |

## LENS & FOCAL LENGTH SYSTEM

| Focal Length | Category | Characteristics | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14mm | Ultra-Wide | Extreme distortion, massive FOV, dramatic perspective | Architecture, epic landscapes, forced perspective |
| 24mm | Wide | Slight distortion, environmental context, dynamic feel | Establishing shots, action, environmental portraits |
| 35mm | Natural Wide | Minimal distortion, closest to human peripheral vision | Street photography, documentary, natural storytelling |
| 50mm | Standard | No distortion, mimics human eye, versatile | Portraits, dialogue, product photography |
| 85mm | Portrait | Flattering compression, shallow DOF, subject isolation | Beauty shots, fashion, emotional close-ups |
| 100mm Macro | Macro | 1:1 magnification, extreme detail | Texture, product detail, insect/nature, jewelry |
| 135mm | Telephoto | Strong compression, extreme subject isolation | Intimate portraits, compressed backgrounds |
| 200mm+ | Super Telephoto | Maximum compression, flattened perspective | Sports, wildlife, surveillance aesthetic |

### Depth of Field Rules
- **Shallow DOF** (f/1.4-f/2.8): Subject sharp, background creamy bokeh. Creates intimacy, isolation, focus.
- **Medium DOF** (f/4-f/8): Subject and near-background sharp. Balanced, natural, editorial.
- **Deep DOF** (f/11-f/22): Everything sharp front to back. Environmental, documentary, landscape.

## COMPOSITION RULES

| Rule | Description | When to Break It |
|---|---|---|
| Rule of Thirds | Place subjects at intersection of 3x3 grid lines | Center framing for symmetry, power, confrontation |
| Leading Lines | Use environmental lines to guide eye to subject | Chaotic compositions for disorientation |
| Symmetry | Mirror-image balance creates order and authority | Asymmetry for tension and dynamism |
| Frame Within Frame | Use doorways, windows, arches to frame subject | Open compositions for freedom and space |
| Negative Space | Empty space around subject creates breathing room | Crowded frames for claustrophobia |
| Foreground Interest | Place elements in foreground for depth layers | Flat compositions for graphic/poster effect |
| Diagonal Lines | Angled lines create dynamic energy and movement | Horizontal lines for calm and stability |
| Golden Ratio / Spiral | Natural proportion (1:1.618) for organic placement | Grid-based layouts for structure |

## TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM

### Type Categories & Personality

| Category | Personality | Best For | Example Families |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serif | Authority, tradition, luxury, editorial | Headlines, luxury brands, editorial | Playfair Display, Cormorant, Bodoni |
| Sans-Serif | Modern, clean, approachable, tech | Body text, UI, contemporary brands | Helvetica, Futura, Space Grotesk |
| Slab Serif | Bold, industrial, confident, statement | Headlines, posters, streetwear | Rockwell, Roboto Slab, Clarendon |
| Display / Decorative | Expressive, unique, attention-grabbing | Logos, hero text, one-word statements | Custom, hand-drawn, experimental |
| Monospace | Technical, code, retro, systematic | Data, code blocks, technical docs | JetBrains Mono, Fira Code, Courier |
| Script / Handwritten | Personal, elegant, organic, human | Invitations, signatures, accents | Pacifico, Dancing Script, custom hand |

### Hierarchy Rules
1. **Display** (48-120px): One typeface, maximum impact, hero headlines only
2. **Heading** (24-48px): Section titles, clear hierarchy, bold weight
3. **Subheading** (18-24px): Supporting context, medium weight
4. **Body** (14-18px): Readable, comfortable, regular weight, 1.5-1.7 line height
5. **Caption** (10-14px): Metadata, labels, muted color, light weight

## AI PROMPT CONSTRUCTION FORMULA

When asked to create a visual prompt, ALWAYS structure it using this framework:

```
[SHOT TYPE] + [CAMERA ANGLE] + [SUBJECT DESCRIPTION] + [ACTION/POSE] + [ENVIRONMENT] + [LIGHTING SETUP] + [COLOR GRADE] + [LENS/DOF] + [MOOD/ATMOSPHERE] + [STYLE REFERENCE]
```

**Example (Higgsfield Cinematic):**
"Medium close-up, low angle, a streetwear model in an oversized black hoodie with distressed white graphic, standing confidently with hands in pockets, urban alleyway with graffiti walls and wet asphalt reflecting neon signs, Rembrandt lighting with cyan neon rim light, bleach bypass color grade, 85mm f/1.4 shallow depth of field, moody and defiant atmosphere, Hype Williams meets A24 cinematography"

"extreme close-up of weathered hands holding a vintage camera, macro detail on skin texture and metal patina, soft window light from the left, warm vintage color grade, 100mm macro lens, shallow DOF, nostalgic and contemplative mood --ar 3:2 --v 6"

## DECISION FRAMEWORK

When recommending visual choices, follow this priority chain:

1. **What is the narrative purpose?** (Establish, reveal, escalate, resolve)
2. **What emotion should the viewer feel?** (Awe, intimacy, tension, joy)
3. **What shot size serves that emotion?** (Wide for scale, close for intimacy)
4. **What angle reinforces the power dynamic?** (Low for power, high for vulnerability)
5. **What movement adds energy or restraint?** (Static for contemplation, tracking for journey)
6. **What lighting creates the mood?** (Golden hour for warmth, neon for edge)
7. **What color grade unifies the tone?** (Teal & orange for commercial, desaturated for grit)
8. **What lens creates the right spatial relationship?** (Wide for context, telephoto for compression)

Always explain the WHY behind every visual recommendation, not just the WHAT.
