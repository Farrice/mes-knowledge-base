### The Universal Streetwear Prompt Formula

This formula is your master key, designed to be adapted across any AI platform. It forces a focus on the core elements that define iconic streetwear visuals.

`[SCENE/SUBJECT] + [ACTION/VIBE] + [ARTFORM/MEDIUM] + [TECHNICAL STYLIZATION] + [BRAND AESTHETIC/REFERENCE] --no [EXCLUSIONS]`

- **Scene/Subject:** What is the core focus? (e.g., "A single, limited-edition sneaker," "A model wearing a graphic hoodie")
- **Action/Vibe:** What is happening or what is the feeling? (e.g., "levitating against a concrete wall," "captured in a moment of quiet confidence")
- **Artform/Medium:** What is the output format? (e.g., "lookbook cover photograph," "vector logo treatment," "risograph-printed poster")

- **Brand Aesthetic/Reference:** The creative anchor. (e.g., "in the style of 90s Supreme skate videos," "with the minimalist typography of Stussy," "inspired by the deconstructed look of Off-White")
- **Exclusions:** What to remove to refine the output. (e.g., "--no clean, --no corporate, --no generic models")

4.  **Negative Prompt Sculpting:** Use negative prompts (`--no`) as a sculpting tool. Instead of just excluding things you don't want, use it to refine the *feeling*. *Example:* `--no clean, --no perfect, --no corporate, --no symmetrical, --no smiling` immediately pushes the AI towards a grittier, more authentic streetwear aesthetic.

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1.  **Generic Subject Descriptions:** Avoid prompts like "A person wearing streetwear." It's too broad. The AI will default to generic, stock-photo-like models. Be hyper-specific about the model's look, attitude, and context to get a unique result.

2.  **Ignoring the Medium:** A prompt for a vector logo should be fundamentally different from a prompt for a lookbook photo. Failing to specify the `[ARTFORM/MEDIUM]` will result in outputs that are stylistically confused and unusable in a real-world production pipeline.

3.  **Forgetting the Brand Anchor:** Without a `[BRAND AESTHETIC/REFERENCE]`, the AI is just guessing. You'll get a cool image, but it won't feel like it belongs to a cohesive brand world. Always anchor your prompt in a specific, real-world aesthetic (e.g., "Palace Skateboards lookbook," "Off-White industrial design").

### Pro Moves

1.  **Master Materiality**: Use hyper-specific keywords to define textures. Instead of "fabric," use "heavyweight 250 GSM cotton," "slub-knit texture," or "pilled fleece." For packaging, specify "uncoated recycled paper stock," "embossed lettering," or "spot UV varnish." This grounds the image in reality.

2.  **The "Human Element" Trick**: Even when the product is the hero, adding a subtle, out-of-focus human element (a hand reaching, a person walking by) makes the scene feel more authentic and less sterile. It implies the product is being used and lived with.

3.  **Negative Prompting for Cleanliness**: When you need a clean, studio-like shot, use negative prompts to remove unwanted visual noise. On Midjourney or Kittl, use `--no text, logos, clutter, distracting elements` to ensure the focus remains solely on your product and its immediate setting.

4.  **Chain Prompts for Cohesion**: For a lookbook or campaign, start with a master prompt to define the core aesthetic (e.g., model, lighting, location). Then, create variations by only changing the action or product, keeping the rest of the prompt identical for a cohesive set of images.

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1.  **Generic Lighting**: Avoid simply saying "good lighting." Be a director. Is it "soft, diffused morning light," "harsh direct midday sun," or "dramatic, high-contrast chiaroscuro lighting"? The type of light dictates the entire mood.

2.  **Forgetting the Background**: A product floating in a void is a missed opportunity. The background and setting should reinforce the brand's story. A minimalist brand might use a clean, architectural background, while an outdoor brand needs a rugged, natural setting.

3.  **Ignoring the Lens**: Different lenses create different feelings. A wide-angle lens (e.g., 24mm) can create a sense of scale and drama, while a portrait lens (e.g., 85mm) creates intimacy and beautiful background blur (bokeh). Specify your lens choice to control the composition and focus.
