# Visual Language Encyclopedia

## Shot Types & Their Psychological Impact

| Shot Type | Frame | Psychological Effect | When to Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme Wide Shot (EWS) | Subject tiny in vast environment | Isolation, insignificance, epic scale | Establishing worlds, showing vulnerability |
| Wide Shot (WS) | Full body + surrounding environment | Context, geography, spatial relationship | Scene establishment, showing action in space |
| Full Shot (FS) | Head to toe, minimal environment | Character introduction, body language focus | Fashion, product reveals, character entrances |
| Medium Wide (MWS) | Knees up | Casual observation, natural conversation | Dialogue scenes, group interactions |
| Medium Shot (MS) | Waist up | Neutral engagement, balanced intimacy | Standard dialogue, interviews, tutorials |
| Medium Close-Up (MCU) | Chest up | Heightened attention, emotional engagement | Emotional dialogue, reaction shots |
| Close-Up (CU) | Face fills frame | Intimacy, emotion, psychological intensity | Emotional peaks, important dialogue, reveals |
| Extreme Close-Up (ECU) | Single feature (eye, lip, texture) | Obsessive detail, tension, abstraction | Tension moments, texture reveals, luxury detail |
| Insert Shot | Object or detail | Information delivery, symbolic weight | Product focus, clue reveals, texture emphasis |
| Over-the-Shoulder (OTS) | Subject framed past another's shoulder | Voyeuristic intimacy, conversation POV | Dialogue, confrontation, following action |
| POV Shot | Camera IS the character's eyes | Immersion, subjective experience | Horror, action, unboxing, first-person narrative |
| Dutch Angle | Tilted horizon line | Unease, disorientation, psychological tension | Villain reveals, chaos, psychological thriller |
| Bird's Eye | Directly overhead | God-like perspective, pattern revelation | Flat lays, choreography, establishing geography |
| Worm's Eye | Ground level looking up | Power, dominance, monumentality | Hero shots, architecture, streetwear power poses |

## Camera Movements & Their Narrative Function

| Movement | Technique | Narrative Function | AI Prompt Keywords |
|---|---|---|---|
| Static/Locked | No movement | Contemplation, observation, tension | "static camera," "locked-off shot," "still frame" |
| Pan | Horizontal rotation on axis | Surveying, revealing, connecting elements | "slow pan left/right," "panoramic sweep" |
| Tilt | Vertical rotation on axis | Revealing scale, power dynamics | "tilt up to reveal," "slow tilt down" |
| Dolly In | Camera physically moves toward subject | Increasing intimacy, drawing viewer in | "dolly forward," "push-in," "approaching" |
| Dolly Out | Camera moves away from subject | Revealing context, creating distance | "pull back," "dolly out," "retreating" |
| Tracking/Lateral | Camera moves alongside subject | Following action, maintaining energy | "tracking shot," "lateral movement," "following" |
| Crane/Jib | Vertical elevation change | Establishing grandeur, emotional lift | "crane up," "rising shot," "ascending" |
| Steadicam/Gimbal | Smooth handheld following | Immersion, following through space | "steadicam follow," "smooth tracking," "floating" |
| Handheld | Deliberate camera shake | Urgency, documentary realism, chaos | "handheld," "shaky cam," "raw movement" |
| Whip Pan | Extremely fast pan | Energy, surprise, time compression | "whip pan," "snap turn," "fast swish" |
| Zoom (vs Dolly) | Lens focal length change | Psychological compression, revelation | "slow zoom," "crash zoom," "zoom punch" |
| Orbit | Camera circles subject | Examination, heroic emphasis, 360 reveal | "orbit shot," "circling," "rotating around" |

## Composition Rules

| Rule | Description | When to Break It |
|---|---|---|
| Rule of Thirds | Place subjects at intersection of 3x3 grid | Center framing for symmetry/power |
| Leading Lines | Use lines to guide eye to subject | When you want the eye to wander |
| Frame Within Frame | Use doorways/windows to frame subject | When you want open, expansive feeling |
| Negative Space | Leave intentional empty space | When density/chaos is the message |
| Symmetry | Mirror balance for formal composition | When organic/natural feel is needed |
| Golden Ratio | 1.618 spiral for natural placement | When mathematical precision feels cold |
| Diagonal Tension | Place elements along diagonals | When stability/calm is the goal |
| Depth Layering | Foreground/midground/background elements | When flat graphic impact is desired |
| Pattern + Break | Establish pattern, then disrupt it | When uniformity is the message |

### Typography Hierarchy Rules
1. **Display:** 48-120px — Headlines, hero text. Maximum 6 words.
2. **H1:** 32-48px — Section headers. One per page/section.
3. **H2:** 24-32px — Subsection headers. Supporting hierarchy.
4. **Body:** 16-18px — Paragraph text. Optimize for readability.
5. **Caption:** 12-14px — Labels, metadata, fine print.
6. **Contrast ratio:** Minimum 2 weights apart (e.g., Light 300 + Bold 700).
