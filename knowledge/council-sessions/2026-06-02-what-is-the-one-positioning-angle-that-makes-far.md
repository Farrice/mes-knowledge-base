# Council Session — 2026-06-02

## How the Masters Thought — What is the ONE positioning angle that makes Farrice's 'invisible expert' coaching brand impossible to ignore, and the single launch move to prove it?

> Net-net: For an ICP built from guru-distrust (Maya, the Invisible Expert who has been burned by people who *claimed* to be good), every declared positioning angle is pre-discounted. The only angle that survives is one where the proof and the pitch are the same object. The launch move is not downstream of the angle — it *is* the angle, executed in public on someone else's brand.

---

### The Moves (one per expert: the SIGNATURE MOVE shown in action + 'the move you can steal')

**Seth Godin — "Better, not louder."**
*In action:* Refuses the volume game the whole category is playing. Doesn't ask "how does Farrice shout over the noise of coaching Twitter?" — asks "what is so remarkable that not-spreading-it costs the viewer something?" Lands on: a teardown that delivers a stranger more avatar insight in 4 minutes than the brand has on itself. Remarkable = literally worth a remark.
*The move you can steal:* **Don't compete on volume in a saturated channel — compete on a thing so useful that withholding it would be the strange choice.** Remarkability is a property of the artifact, not the megaphone.

**Chris Do — "Demonstrate, don't claim."**
*In action:* Treats the portfolio problem (Farrice has no logos, no testimonials, the cold-start credibility hole) as a permission to *build the proof live*. The teardown is a billable-quality audit ($1,500 rung) given away as content — so the free version and the paid version are the same artifact at different resolutions. The viewer watches the deliverable get made.
*The move you can steal:* **When you have no track record, manufacture proof by doing the paid work in public on a target the audience already recognizes.** Recognition transfers; you borrow the target's salience to carry your competence.

**Donald Miller (StoryBrand) — "Position as guide, never hero."**
*In action:* The teardown casts the recognizable brand as the flawed protagonist and Maya-watching-from-the-feed as the hero who learns. Farrice never appears as the hero of his own story — he appears as the guide standing beside a brand bigger than him, calmly showing the map. The "What They Did Right / What They Missed / What This Means For You" triad is pure guide-grammar.
*The move you can steal:* **Make a brand bigger than you the protagonist so you get to be the guide — guides are trusted precisely because they have nothing to prove and aren't asking for the spotlight.**

**Luke Iha (Avatar Machine) — "Know the customer better than the brand does."**
*In action:* Supplies the actual differentiator under the theatrics — grounded VOC research (≥15 real source URLs, zero modeled language) surfaces the target customer's *core wound* that the brand's own copy never touches. The teardown leads with this, not with the rewrite. The insight is the flex; the rewrite is just the receipt.
*The move you can steal:* **Open with the buried customer truth the incumbent missed — demonstrated knowledge of someone's customer is the most expensive thing to fake and the fastest trust-builder there is.**

### The Collision (the exact moment two domains met and produced the net-new principle)

**Domain A — Identity-Safe Positioning (the *buyer's* psychology):** Maya is paralyzed by the Authenticity Paradox. She wants visibility but is allergic to performance; to her, "getting visible" = "becoming a content guru," which is the identity she refuses. This is the wound the *offer* is built to dissolve: visibility without becoming an influencer.

**Domain B — Proof-as-Distribution (the *seller's* go-to-market):** Godin's remarkability + Chris Do's demonstrate-don't-claim + the public teardown mechanic. This is a channel/asset decision about *how Farrice gets attention*.

**The spark:** These were treated as two separate decisions — "what do I stand for" (A) and "how do I launch" (B). The collision is the realization that **the launch move dissolves the buyer's paralysis by modeling the exact behavior the offer sells.** When Farrice does a constructive teardown of someone else's brand, he is *himself* performing "visibility through demonstration, not performance." He becomes the living instance of his own product — the Invisible Expert who got visible without becoming an influencer, by showing his work *on someone else's brand* instead of broadcasting himself. The angle and the move stop being two things. The proof IS the pitch.

A second, sharper resolution fell out of the same collision — the **loud/quiet tension nobody resolved as compromise.** The instinct is "be louder than the gurus" (B) vs. "don't trigger the guru-allergy" (A). The council didn't split the difference on *volume*. It split the *channels*: **loudness lives entirely in the choice of target** (a name people already know = borrowed salience grabs the feed), **quietness lives entirely in the tone** (constructive, evidence-led, "what they got right and what I'd improve"). The teardown is *loud in WHO it's about and quiet in HOW it speaks.* That single configuration is the only one that captures attention without firing the buyer's defenses.

### The Principle (the transferable mental model)

**Collapse the proof and the pitch into one artifact — and when a tension looks binary, don't compromise the variable, split the channels.**

Two parts, both reusable far outside positioning:

1. **The Proof-Is-The-Pitch test.** Before claiming you're good at X, ask: *can the audience watch me be good at X, on a target they already care about, before I make any claim?* If yes, build that — it doubles as marketing, lead-gen, and product demo, and it's immune to the discount applied to all self-claims. If you can only *tell* them, your positioning is pre-discounted by everyone who's been lied to before.

2. **Channel-split over volume-compromise.** When you're caught between two seemingly opposite settings of one dial (loud/quiet, fast/careful, bold/safe), don't average them. Find two *different* variables and assign each extreme to its own — be maximal on one axis (the target), minimal on another (the tone). Compromise blurs; channel-splitting lets you be fully both.

3. **The behavior you model in your launch should BE the behavior your offer sells.** If your product is "visibility without performance," your go-to-market must itself be visible-without-performing. The launch is the first delivery of the product. Misalignment here is the tell that gets you discounted; alignment is the proof that can't be faked.

### Your Rep (one prompt: a different problem, same principle)

**Jen's first-time-homebuyer lead engine (SFV).** First-time buyers distrust realtors the same way Maya distrusts gurus — every agent *claims* to "have your back," so the claim is worthless. Apply the principle: instead of Jen posting "I help first-time buyers" content (telling), build the **Proof-Is-The-Pitch artifact** — publicly tear down a real, recognizable SFV listing's marketing and re-do it the right way (loud in the target = a property/agent people in the valley recognize; quiet in the tone = "here's what this listing's photos and copy are costing the seller, and what I'd change"). The behavior it models — *I will out-work and out-think the market on your behalf, in public, before you've paid me a cent* — is the exact behavior the buyer is hiring for. Ask yourself the same two questions: (1) can the buyer *watch* Jen be good before any claim? (2) where's the binary tension I'm about to compromise, and which two channels can I split it across instead?
