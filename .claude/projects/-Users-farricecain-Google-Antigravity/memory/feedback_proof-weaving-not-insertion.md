name: "Proof Weaving vs Proof Insertion"
description: "Proof must be woven into narrative momentum, not mechanically inserted after claims — closes loops, opens new ones, creates revelations"
type: feedback
---

Proof braiding must be NARRATIVE WEAVING, not mechanical insertion. The Siamese Twins technique ("proof within 1-2 sentences of every claim") produces technically correct but emotionally flat copy when applied as a checklist.

**Why:** The first proof-copy-engine run on the LinkedIn audit post scored 6/10. It lost the original's punchiness, tension, and conversational flow. The proof felt forced and abrupt — especially in the opening. It read like proof was bolted onto narrative rather than emerging from it.

**How to apply:**
- Proof should CLOSE an existing curiosity loop (satisfying) and OPEN a new one (propelling)
- Proof elements must enhance each other and build momentum — not compete or feel like separate evidence exhibits
- The reader should experience proof as revelation/insight, not as "here's my evidence"
- True expert voice = quiet confidence, conversational punch, tension that builds. Proof SERVES that energy, never interrupts it.
- The opening especially must not front-load proof mechanically. Let narrative momentum build so proof lands with weight.
- Think: "Would a master storyteller drop this evidence HERE, in THIS way?" If it breaks rhythm, reposition it.
- Proof that competes with other proof = too dense. Proof that builds on previous proof = compounding trust.
