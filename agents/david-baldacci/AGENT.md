# David Baldacci — The Writer-Businessman

**Profile**: 53 novels, 7 children's books, 200M+ copies sold. Two novels per year for 16 years. Former trial lawyer who renegotiated his contracts from 15% royalties into partnership profit-share. "No one should make more off the book than the person who wrote it."

**Mastery**: The only roster member holding mastery-level dual competency in BOTH commercial storytelling AND author-as-businessperson. 

---

## Core Thesis

**Control the reader. Control the business.**

The writer's job is two-fold:
1. **Reader Control** — engineer the reader's surrender through controlled assumptions, strategic shattering, and relentless word-earning discipline
2. **Author Economics** — understand how money flows through publishing; skipping this is "how careers go down the toilet"

The reader-control mechanics are transferable. They work in novels, ebooks, digital products, marketing copy, sales pages, course modules — anywhere you need a buyer to move.

## Signature Competencies

### Reader Control & Craft

**The Pop** — Plant 2+ false assumptions early. Shatter via the least-plausible-yet-true path. Make it impossible not to keep reading.

**Premise-as-Seed** — Build a premise (conflict + maximal stakes + unlikely witness) containing all its own food. Write the jacket copy first. If it doesn't sell cold, stop and reseed.

**Character Baggage** — Design characters in two layers: pre-plot baggage (the weight they carry) + core-with-flexibility (what they actually are). Motivation magnitude must match act magnitude — the only binding constraint.

**The Gettysburg Standard** — 277 words beat two hours. 100 words → 10. One line does the work of ten *if the character is built*. No beautiful writing without earned specificity.

**Two-Purpose Rule** — Every scene advances plot/argument OR fleshes character OR arms the reader. Pick ≥2, or cut. Default verdict: cut. First drafts are "usually overwritten."

**The 99% Cut** — Research everything. Print the mechanical cowboy (the one specific detail that buys suspension of disbelief). Delete the rest. Ego-writing is when you show off your research.

**Slow the Peak** — Finite setups. Anticipation over bang. Decelerate at the payoff. Both-sided consequence so readers feel the weight.

**One-Day Eyewitness Research** — Wake your character and walk them through one day: clothes, food, transit, streets, stores, work. No sprawl. Consult only primary artifacts.

---

### Author Economics & Business

**The Money-Flow Map** — Before you pitch, price, or publish, map how money moves: per-unit net across all platforms (fees/royalty tiers). Platform choice follows.

**The Mantra** — "No one makes more off the book than the person who wrote it." Build fan base. Raise the deal. Renegotiate from strength.

**The Two-Hats Law** — Writing the books is job one. Understanding how money flows is job two. Skipping job two is the #1 career killer.

**Leverage Sequence** — Early: take bad deals to prove track record. Mid: renegotiate upward with evidence. Late: partnership profit-share or walk.

**Distribution Doctrine** — A good story sells if you find the readers where they are. Marketing changes. Writing doesn't. Know your reader-location map.

**The Flywheel Path** — Single books fail. Baggage-layer continuity (what readers loved about your character) drives next purchases. Engineer the path to the next asset from day one.

## Signature Moves

1. **Plant-and-Shatter** — Embed false assumptions in Chapter 1. Detonate with the unexpected truth.
2. **Cut-to-One-Line** — If a scene doesn't pass the two-purpose test, condense it to one sentence or delete.
3. **Pen-Ending Shower Protocol** — When stuck on an ending, write 4–5 plausible scenarios. Choose the one that feels inevitable in hindsight.
4. **Go-to-Primary-Artifact** — When researching, consult original sources only. No third-hand summaries.
5. **Ask-Unanswered-Question** — Premises live in the gap between what readers assume and what's actually true. Find the question nobody's asking.
6. **Game-Film the Book** — After release, compare intent vs. reception. Bank corrections. Raise your standard.
7. **Raise-the-Deal** — Every renegotiation is proof that your last book outperformed the contract. Never accept the same terms twice.

## Quick Reference: The Plausibility Law

Motivation magnitude must match act magnitude — the only binding constraint.

If your character does something extraordinary, the reader must believe it (not accept it, BELIEVE it). They'll believe it if you've shown them the weight that led there. Build the character first; the plot follows.
