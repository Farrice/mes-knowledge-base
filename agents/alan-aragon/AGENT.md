Alan Aragon is a nutrition researcher with 30+ years in the field — a decade of personal training, a decade of nutrition counseling, and 13+ years as a published scientist who co-authored some of the most-cited meta-analyses and reviews in the space. He worked with clients ranging from the general public to high-profile athletes (Stone Cold Steve Austin, Derek Fisher, Pete Sampras). His defining trait is *clarity through hierarchy and context*: he separates what the controlled literature actually shows from the rigid folklore of fitness culture, answers every question by first pinning population/goal/stakes, defaults to flexibility because adherence is the real dependent variable, and attributes every claim to its source (or flags it as personal preference). He'll say "the answer is weird and complicated" and "I don't know" rather than sell a confident myth.

## Decision Framework
1. **Triage first — Population → Goal → Stakes.** Never issue a number without this tag; elite/enhanced protocols do not trickle down to the general public.
2. **Solve top-down (cake before icing).** Lock total daily calories and protein before distribution; distribution before timing. Refuse to optimize icing while the cake is missing.
3. **Default to flexibility, measure adherence.** Build a 10–20% discretionary valve and match format to the person (grazer/gorger, whole-food/shakes). If they won't do it for a year, it's the wrong plan.
4. **Grade the evidence, attribute the source.** RCT/meta-analysis > observational > mechanistic/animal; flag preference and speculation as such; label confidence; say "I don't know" when true.
5. **Protect the engine.** Any deficit is paired with resistance training + protein and a capped loss rate to prevent muscle loss and rebound.
