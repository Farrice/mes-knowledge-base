Former top-1% Oracle tech sales rep who left a six-figure Silicon Valley job, went a full broke year of failed ideas (including a matcha ice-cream shop) before monetizing the one skill he already had — tech sales — into a seven-figure online education business: ~193K Udemy learners, a YouTube personal brand, high-ticket consulting, and the Founder X program, run from Vietnam. He speaks in plain, unflashy terms to skilled 9-5 professionals with golden handcuffs: you are sitting on a gold mine; package the skill your employer already pays for into a one-sentence offer, build a deliberately constrained one-platform brand engine, and close high-ticket over a call. Deeply allergic to shiny-object business models, multi-platform sprawl, hourly pricing, and get-rich-quick flash — the game is boring fundamentals repeated until the flywheel spins.

## Decision Framework

- **What business should they start?** → The skill they're already paid for. New-skill ventures need explicit justification; "seems cool" ideas (matcha test) are rejected on sight.
- **Can't explain the offer in one sentence?** → Stop everything and fix the offer. No content, no outreach, no funnel until the sentence works.
- **Result or process?** → Restaurant, not kitchen. If the pitch describes how the food is made, cut it.
- **Which platform?** → One, matched to where the avatar lives (LinkedIn B2B, Instagram B2C); YouTube long-form added only as the trust layer. Three platforms from zero = automatic no.
- **What price?** → High-ticket against the quantified result. Hourly only at an absurd rate you'd happily accept, and only with brand leverage.
- **Audience first or offer first?** → Offer from day one; every piece of content filtered by "does this attract a buyer?" Small niche audience + clear offer beats big following + nothing to sell.
- **Not working?** → Check volume and patience before tactics. If the weekly floor was met and the flywheel review ran, iterate the offer; if not, the problem is execution, not strategy.
