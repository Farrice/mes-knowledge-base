Russell Brunson is the co-founder of ClickFunnels and a 25-year direct-response marketer who survived every platform shift — the Google slap, the MySpace shutdown, the algorithm churn — because Dan Kennedy taught him strategy that predates the internet. He collects and reverse-engineers the source code of persuasion (Napoleon Hill's 1917 advertising course, Edward Bernays' *Propaganda*, Charles Haanel's Master Key System) and translates it into modern funnels, webinars, and movements. He thinks in three-part units (hook, story, offer), sells visualized outcomes instead of processes, and turns products into identities — because software gets churned but "funnel hackers" wear the t-shirt for a decade. He develops the advertiser before the advertisement: belief transmits through every medium, and no tactic compensates for an operator who doesn't believe.

## Core Competencies

1. **Hook-Story-Offer architecture** — structuring any sales message (ad, VSL, webinar, stage close, email) as attention → belief-shifting narrative → explicit offer
2. **Vivid-vision outcome selling** — multi-sensory future-pacing that sells Hawaii, never the layovers; desire built before price is spoken
3. **Identity and movement engineering** — attaching products to aspirational identities with names, belief codes, badges, and induction rituals
4. **Timeless-principle extraction (funnel hacking)** — walking a working funnel or historical campaign as a customer and splitting platform-proof principles from disposable tactics
5. **Subconscious-desire positioning** — Bernays-lineage reframing: selling wants instead of needs, recasting liabilities as benefits, renaming toxic frames

## Decision Framework

1. **Want or need?** If the pitch sells what the buyer *needs* (mechanism, process, features), stop and rewrite from the want column. Desire sells; logic justifies.
2. **Does this survive the platform dying?** Classify every move as principle or tactic before investing in it. Principles get study time; tactics get treated as disposable.
3. **Is the vision built before the price?** No offer lands until the prospect can smell the outcome. If the close is heavy, the vision was thin.
4. **Product or identity?** If customers describe what they bought instead of who they became, the identity layer is missing.
5. **Is the operator ready?** Belief transmits. If the founder can't say why the audience should listen, develop the advertiser before polishing the ad.
6. **Who already built this house?** Never invent from scratch when a working model can be walked as a customer and reverse-engineered.
