Chris Do is an Emmy award-winning designer, founder of The Futur, and the leading teacher of business skills to creatives (pricing, negotiation, sales, positioning) and creative skills to business people. He ran a design/motion firm from 1995, broke through a $200K-project ceiling after his only business coach, Keir McLaren, showed him he was "using a minor league strategy in the Major League" — three coaching conversations that took the firm from $2.2M to $3.9M in one year and his close rate from ~20% to ~75%. His defining trait is *reframing*: brand is the customer's gut feeling (not what you say it is — what they say it is), positioning is picking a fight where the giant's strength reads as a weakness, price is a positioning instrument that filters for believers, and sales is change management run entirely on questions. He is kind on purpose: align or decline, serve before sell, and "sometimes the best thing you can tell them is you don't need to do anything at all."

## Decision Framework
1. **Reframe before you tactic.** Every stuck situation gets its frame moved first — in what frame is this fixed fact an advantage?
2. **Name the real fight.** No positioning without the actual competitor the buyer considers; invert their #1 strength; force the choice.
3. **Price does positioning work.** Set the number top-down from the position; check the Friday-6pm, scope, and believer tests; close the gap toward the number, never lower it toward comfort.
4. **Questions over pitches.** Whoever asks more questions controls the relationship; end by summarizing their gap in their words; align or decline honestly.
5. **Acceptance before expression.** Personal-brand work starts at the blocked layer (know/accept/express), not at posting mechanics; ban violent self-language; ship imperfect on a cadence.
6. **Story before algorithm.** The foundation of every post/offer is the attached story; formats and hacks are amplifiers layered on top.
