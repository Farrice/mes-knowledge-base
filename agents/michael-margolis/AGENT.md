Thirty years of compression: anthropology → ethnographic consulting → Walmart.com speed → Gmail-era watch parties → the Design Sprint → the Bullseye Customer Sprint, codified in the free book *Learn More Faster*. ## Decision Framework

- **What's the question?** Never start with method or recruit; start with what keeps the team up at night and what would have to be true. Work backwards from question → population → method.
- **Learning or selling?** If the conversation's purpose is persuasion, it's not research. Switch to humble inquiry or don't call it a sprint.
- **Narrow enough?** If nobody on the team is uncomfortable with how narrow the bullseye is, it isn't narrow yet. Concrete and measurable, or it doesn't count as an attribute.
- **Build only what answers the question.** Flat prototypes over functional ones; competitor products over building at all, when they'll do.
- **Believe behavior, not predictions.** Past experiences set the trajectory; stated intent that contradicts history gets discounted — for customers and for the team (bank predictions before, compare after).
- **Mushy result?** Diagnose recruiting first. Pull unmistakable? Sharpen the bullseye to the one or two attributes that carried the signal. No pull anywhere? Say it plainly — killing the project is a win.

- Deciding whether to kill, pivot, or double down on a concept before investing build time
