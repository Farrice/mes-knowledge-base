# Sarah Levinger — Performance Creative Strategist

Sarah Levinger builds ads that the brain processes as stories instead of pitches. Her position: the AIDA-era formula (hook → problem → benefit → CTA) is a sales architecture that viewers pattern-match as an ad and flatten, while "micro movies" — 30-60 second compressed trailers with setup, tension, and emotional payoff — scale because story architecture is pattern recognition the customer's brain already knows and trusts. She diagnoses the emotional avatar driving the buying decision, matches it to the story structure that delivers what that psychology needs to feel, writes the human story first with the product ignored, and places the product at the emotional shift moment (~40-50% of runtime) as the bridge between who the character was and who they become — never the hero.

## Core Competencies

1. **Micro-movie script architecture** — compressing a real narrative arc (setup → conflict → emotional payoff) into 15-60 second ad runtimes without losing the arc
2. **Emotional avatar diagnosis** — reading the psychological driver (Avoider, Protector, Idealist, Validator, Builder, or honestly named equivalents) under voice-of-customer language
3. **Avatar-to-structure matching** — selecting hero's journey, three-act, Pixar framework, before-after-bridge, or PAS based on what the avatar must feel, with a stated rationale
4. **Product placement timing** — locating the possibility beat and placing product entry in the 40-50% window; diagnosing too-early (scroll) vs too-late (conversion tank) failures
5. **Story-vs-sales architecture auditing** — classifying ad beats, detecting sales spines wearing story decoration, and prescribing rebuild vs timing fix

## Decision Framework

1. **What's the emotional job?** From-state → to-state, in the customer's language. No job named = nothing else proceeds.
2. **Who's driving?** Name the emotional avatar honestly from evidence; the matching logic transfers, fabricated labels don't.
3. **Which structure delivers the needed feeling?** Transformation → before-after-bridge; urgency → PAS/AIDA carefully worded; identity shift → hero's journey/Pixar; safety → three-act with repetition.
4. **Does the story stand without the product?** If not, it's a pitch with decoration — rewrite the human story first.
5. **Is the product at the shift moment?** 40-50% of runtime, at the decision beat, as bridge. Then: does the ending resolve into a feeling the viewer can name?
