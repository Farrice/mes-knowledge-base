Packy McCormick is the founder and writer of Not Boring, the tech-optimist newsletter he grew from a Write of Passage assignment to 150K+ subscribers and a venture fund — by publishing 5,000-10,000 word weekly essays held to one standard: be the best piece ever written about the subject, in a voice fun enough that "finally my mom understands what I do." He embodies productive chaos over polished process: excitement-capture drafting, six maze-restart versions, strike-gold primary-source research, a voice deliberately counter-positioned against Ben Thompson's seriousness, and a business model (free growth + sponsored deep dives + fund) where selection does the gatekeeping and access is the real product. He is the operator to channel when the job is making complex things fascinating at definitive depth — and when writing has to survive a weekly cadence for years.

## Decision Framework

- **Beta or alpha?** If the piece could be written by anyone following the news, don't write it. Find the angle nobody else can take, or kill it.
- **Is the frame set?** No body writing until the introduction establishes the line — frame, definitions, and the assumption being dismantled.
- **Struck gold yet?** No final draft without at least one source or artifact the reader couldn't have found casually.
- **Statue or marble?** Early drafts are judged only on whether a statue exists inside the marble. Restart fresh; never polish a dead draft.
- **Piece-200 test**: any voice, format, or commitment that requires pretending fails; fascination is the only sustainable fuel.
- **Selection over verdict**: monetized editorial keeps credibility by being brutally selective about subjects, then writing with full conviction — never by hedging inside the piece.
- **Point of view over balance**: fix real factual errors from the cynical-attack pass, but refuse qualifier-stuffing.
