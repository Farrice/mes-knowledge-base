# Buyer Panel — MyBPM Streetwear (EDM/Rave)

Source: `_active/mybpm/mybpm-merch-os-run-1/01-ground.md` (Phase 0 GROUND, the Raver-as-Identity-Keeper sub-identity) + `00-run-config.md` brand context. Detail below marked LIKELY is inferred from the ground doc's behavioral-moment research, not from real customer interviews or Shopify order data — the honest fix is to run a `mcclain-dai-audience-persona` pass against actual reviews/Discord threads/repeat-buyer data once MyBPM has post-launch traffic to mine.

---

## 1. The Identity Keeper (PRIMARY buyer)
The core ICP from Phase 0 GROUND: attends 2-5 events/year as pilgrimage, wears PLUR values year-round (not just at festivals), remembers the exact moment PLUR clicked for them, still thinks about kandi-trade strangers months later. Defends: that the shirt reads as a values statement, not a costume. Triggers yes: design that signals "I know what this actually means" via specific behavioral truth, not generic neon/trippy tropes. Objection voice (LIKELY): *"Every rave brand does the neon-skull thing — does this one actually get it, or is it another costume?"*

## 2. Economic Gatekeeper — Budget-Conscious Raver
Young (early-to-mid 20s), spends discretionary income on festival tickets, travel, and kandi supplies before merch; a $30-40 tee competes directly against festival-fund savings. Defends: not blowing the budget on "stuff" when the real spend is the next event itself. Triggers yes: a specific piece worth wearing daily (cost-per-wear logic) rather than a one-off festival costume. Objection voice (LIKELY): *"I'd rather put $35 toward my next ticket than a shirt I only wear once — does this actually get worn Tuesday at my desk job too?"* — **Confidence note**: LIKELY; ground this in real price-objection data from Shopify cart abandonment or post-purchase surveys once available.

## 3. PLUR Veteran / Authenticity Skeptic
5+ years deep in the scene, has watched dozens of brands try to cash in on rave culture and fail the sniff test. Defends: the community's trust that PLUR isn't for sale. Triggers yes: specificity that only an insider would write (genre-as-identity distinctions, the dawn-after-the-set disorientation, kandi ritual detail) — anything generic reads as exploitation. Objection voice (LIKELY): *"This is the fifth brand this year selling 'PLUR' back to us — what makes this one not a cash-grab?"*

## 4. NO-FIT — The Aesthetic-Only Trend Buyer
Follows rave/EDC visuals on social media, likes the neon/festival look, has never actually been to a rave or doesn't identify with PLUR values — buys rave-adjacent streetwear purely for the aesthetic. Per Phase 0 GROUND, this is explicitly the buyer MyBPM does NOT want to signal to ("generic design tropes... signal 'I think raves look cool'"). Tests self-selection: does the Week-1 asset's restraint (no neon, no trippy visuals) correctly fail to convert this buyer, or does the streetwear framing accidentally pull them in anyway? Objection voice (LIKELY): *"This doesn't even look like rave merch — where's the neon and the trippy stuff?"* — correctly walking away is the WIN here, not a loss.

## 5. Genre-Specific Purist
Identifies primarily with one genre subculture (e.g., Hardstyle or Techno) over "EDM" broadly, and reads generic "rave" framing as evidence a brand doesn't know the difference between House, Hardstyle, Dubstep, Trance, and Techno communities. Defends: genre identity as sharper than the umbrella "raver" label. Triggers yes: genre-specific product lines/language, not one-size-fits-all rave branding. Objection voice (LIKELY): *"This screams 'generic EDM brand' — do you even know Hardstyle heads and House heads aren't the same crowd?"* — **Confidence note**: LIKELY; MyBPM's existing genre-collection structure (Dubstep/House/Hardstyle/Techno/Trance per `00-run-config.md`) suggests this tension is real but unconfirmed by direct customer feedback.
