## Dominant worldview

**Saturation is the master frame, not differentiation.** These founders don't diagnose their problem as "our positioning is unclear" — they experience it as *invisibility inside noise*: "INSANELY crowded" (#12), "a shit ton of noise" (#21), "selling the same powder everyone else is" (#13). The felt problem is *nobody sees us*, not *our message is muddled*. A pitch that leads with "fix your positioning" risks not landing — a pitch that leads with "get seen through the noise" speaks their actual frame.

**Compliance is the load-bearing constraint, always in the room.** Every theme touches the FDA/FTC ceiling: "may help" not "will help" (#20), "TONS of things you can not say" (#19), "ad platforms are aggressive about flagging health claims" (#22), accounts getting shut down over wording (#22). This is not a side-issue — it's the mechanism forcing generic language across the whole category. Any offer promising sharper language must show it survives the claims ceiling, or it reads as naive to this audience.

**Agencies are pre-distrusted as extraction, not partnership.** The single sharpest sentiment in the corpus: "I wasn't paying for elite marketing performance. I was just funding their sales machine" (#6). The pattern described — slick pitch, $5K/mo retainer, founder/VP vanishes, work handed to a junior buyer juggling 14 clients (#5) — is a specific, repeated shape, not a vague complaint. A $2,500 bounded sprint (not a retainer) directly answers this scar tissue; naming the retainer trap explicitly (rather than avoiding the topic) will read as insider knowledge, not a sales pitch.

## Live objections (verbatim-derived, not paraphrased into categories)

- *"I already know my product works — why do I need someone to tell me how to say it?"* (existing panel voice, corroborated by #15's founder confidently listing his own differentiation angle — these founders believe they've already found their angle even when the language is generic)
- *"Has anyone else fallen into this agency trap?"* (#7) — the retainer-fear objection, needs to be pre-empted, not just avoided
- *"[Will this] burn through your budget getting ads rejected and figuring out compliance on your dime"* (#10) — fear that any outside help adds compliance risk rather than removing it
- *"Unless your product is half the price or 10x the quality of the other guy... dead in the water without a big name"* (#13) — skepticism that *any* messaging fix matters without celebrity/authority backing

## Live vocabulary (use their words, not synonyms)

"crowded" / "saturated" / "noise" (never "competitive landscape") · "agency trap" · "funding their sales machine" · "burn through your budget" · "magic numbers" (agencies promising) · "lean and scrappy" (self-description) · "money waste" · "first sale is expensive as hell" · "may help" vs "will help" (the exact compliance-language distinction) · "big name" (authority/celebrity proof, not "social proof") · founders describe hiring outside help as "outsourcing something you don't understand well enough to evaluate" (#8) — trust is earned by demonstrated category fluency, not credentials.

## What's changed recently (2025→2026 drift visible in the corpus)

- **Retention over acquisition, explicitly.** #26 (May 2026): "a lot of smaller supplement brands survive on creator content + retention more than cold ads now... the first sale is expensive as hell." This is a 2026-dated shift in how founders talk about their own funnel — cold acquisition is described as a losing game, not just expensive.
- **Claims-avoidance is now a stated strategy, not just a legal footnote.** #26 again: brands "avoid hard claims in ads and push more lifestyle/problem aware content instead to stay alive on Meta" — compliance has moved from "thing we deal with" to "thing that shapes our whole creative strategy."
- **Founder-as-face is treated as near-mandatory.** #25 (Sept 2025): "Very little gets out of the gate these days without a consistently visible and energetic founder." This raises the stakes on *founder-voice* positioning work specifically (vs. generic brand-voice work) — directly relevant to a founder-authorized sprint offer.
