# JJ Developmental Playbook — Age 2.5
**Your concrete, ready-to-use menu for presence-based development.**

---

## Core Principle
JJ is autonomy-loving, an empath, and fast-learns then bores. Your job: **give him choice within your intent, move with what he picks, and keep it fresh once he masters it.** This isn't about "screen time is bad" — it's about screens as TOOLS for his interests, not substitutes for you.

**Your role: the person who sees him fully and moves with him.** This is the anti-suppression model. He learns what it looks like to have someone present.

---

## Morning (Before Work Starts — 15-30 min)

**Why this slot:** You're freshest, he's eager, this sets the day's tone of presence.

### Option Set A: Autonomy Building (pick one, let HIM choose which)

**1. "What Should We Do?" — The Choice Menu**
- Lay out 3 options: (1) make a "movie" with toys (narrative play), (2) build something with blocks then knock it down (cause/effect), (3) dance party to his choice of song
- **His job:** point or say which one
- **Your job:** no steering, no "wouldn't you rather." Follow his pick 100%
- **Why:** Autonomy lover practices decision-making. You model: "I hear you, we're doing what YOU picked"

**2. Balance Bike Parade**
- Take the bike to a new route (driveway → end of block, backyard → living room obstacle course)
- Follow behind, narrate what you see: "You're pedaling with both feet now" (not instruction, observation)
- If he falls: matter-of-fact "oops, you're back up" (not coddling, not dismissing the feeling)
- **Why:** Mastery + novelty (same activity, new context keeps it fresh for fast learners)

**3. "Teach Me" — Reversal Play**
- Ask him to teach you something he knows: how to make the Mario sound, where the Lightning McQueen car goes, how to do the Paw Patrol dance
- **His job:** show/tell
- **Your job:** mess it up slightly on purpose ("wait, I did it wrong, show me again") so he gets to be the expert
- **Why:** Empath loves being seen; expert role feels big; builds competence narrative

---

### Option Set B: Screens as Engagement Tool (pick one, 10-15 min MAX)

**Why this feels different:** You're watching WITH him, asking one specific question, moving on. Not background ambient screen time.

**1. Danny Go Challenge**
- Pick a Danny Go video (he outgrew the baby stuff; he's into the challenge/game ones now)
- Watch it together, then: "Can you do that?" (the thing he saw)
- Example: if it's a dance, dance it together. If it's a game, you play it together after
- **Why:** Screen becomes springboard to physical play, not end state

**2. Mario Kart Sidekick Role**
- He plays Mario Kart, you sit next to him narrating like a sports commentator: "Look at that turn! You're in second place! You're catching up!"
- No backseat driving. Just celebration of what he's doing
- **Why:** Screen + competence + your attention all at once; he's performing for an audience he loves

**3. "Let's Find It" — YouTube Scavenger Hunt**
- Tell him you're looking for something specific: "Let's find the video where the car goes REALLY fast" or "let's find the one with all the colors"
- He helps you search/navigate
- Watch the first minute together, then "okay, I found it, you were right, let's go do something"
- **Why:** Agency in the process, not passive consumption; screen is a tool to find something, not the destination

## Midday (During Solo Parent Tuesday / Work-From-Home Windows — 20-45 min)

**Why this slot:** You're cognitively bogged. This needs to be EASY and double as Jen-coverage time.

### Structured Play (low cognitive load for you)

**1. "Store" / "Restaurant" Role Play**
- You set it up: line up his toys, give them price tags (just numbers, no math), arrange a "checkout"
- **He's the shopkeeper or customer** — your job is to play the role he gives you, ask "how much is this?" and follow his pricing/rules
- **Why:** Empath practice (he's managing a relationship), autonomy (he sets the rules), imaginative (zero prep, infinite play)
- **Duration:** He'll tell you when he's done (usually 15-30 min if you let it be his world)
- **When bored hits:** "We're closing this store and opening a new one" — new location, new rules, same setup

**2. Music/Movement Free Riff**
- You pick a song (Danny Go, "Baby Raves," toddler techno rotation) or he picks
- Play it, move however you feel
- **He does his thing, you do yours** — not instruction, not performance. Just moving bodies in the same room
- **Why:** Family rave culture you already have; movement anchors his big feelings; 0 prep
- **Variation:** You move like an animal, he copies, then he picks the next animal

**3. Sensory/ASMR Inspired Activity**
- He loves ASMR toy videos → bring that home: water table, kinetic sand, pasta box (dried pasta to dig in), bubble wrap stomping
- Sit with him, narrate the sounds/textures: "that's a crunchy sound," "look at how that falls"
- **Why:** Matches his documented interest in ASMR; solo screen-free version of the content he gravitates to; very low cognitive load for you

---

### Developmental Skill Practice (disguised as play)

**1. Back Float Confidence Building (in bath or water table)**
- He refuses on command — autonomy issue, not fear
- Setup: "Let's see how long we can float without kicking" (you float first, demonstrate)
- **His choice:** to try or not
- **Key:** no pressure language. "You're trying something new" vs. "you need to do this"
- **Duration:** 2-3 attempts max, then pivot to something else
- **Why:** Respects autonomy, reframes skill as interesting experiment, you model the behavior first

**2. Breath-Holding Game**
- Turn it into a game: "Let's see if we can say the alphabet before we need a breath" (exaggerated, not hard)
- You go first, he copies
- He's competing with himself, not following instructions
- **Why:** Fast learner gets bored with drills; game format keeps it fresh + his autonomy intact

**3. Kicking Pattern Play**
- Shallow water: "Let's make the biggest splashes" (no instruction, just do it together)
- Balance bike: "Can your feet go faster than your hands can push?" (observation + question, not command)
- **Why:** Skill practice embedded in play, not extracted into "practice time"

## Evening (After Jen Home / Between Work & Bedtime — 30-60 min)

**Why this slot:** Transition time from work-mode to family-mode; he needs your presence reset before sleep.

### Collaborative Creation (autonomy + mastery)

**1. Build & Narrate**
- Blocks, Legos, or Hot Wheels track: **he builds**, you narrate what he's making
- "Oh, you're making a tower. It's getting really tall. What happens next?"
- **Key:** you don't build it; you're the audience + question-asker
- **When he's done:** ask "should we keep it or build something new?" (his call)
- **Why:** Autonomy + competence + your attention all at once

**2. Story Co-Creation (from his toys)**
- Grab his Disney/Mario/Cars figures: "Tell me a story with these guys"
- He narrates or acts it out, you listen and ask: "What happens next? Why did he do that?"
- **Length:** however long he wants; your job is just to be the audience
- **Why:** Builds narrative thinking + language; empath practice; you're genuinely interested

**3. "Teach Me" Your Song/Dance**
- He knows Danny Go dances, toddler techno moves, Disney songs
- "Show me how YOU do it" (not copying you, him teaching you)
- Get it "wrong" on purpose: "wait, show me that again, I'm messing up"
- **Why:** Reversal of student/teacher dynamic; builds his authority + your genuine engagement

---

### Screen-Integrated Evening (10-15 min, if needed for wind-down)

**1. Curated Playlist Wind-Down**
- Family rave culture but slower: pick 3-4 songs (Danny Go, toddler techno, Disney soundtrack)
- You both move, sing along, no screen required — but if screen helps him settle, play the video
- **Key:** YOU singing + moving with him, not him alone with screen
- **Why:** Transition to sleep; family time + screen together, not screen time alone

**2. "One More Video" Agreement**
- Negotiate: "We can watch ONE more Danny Go or Blippi, then we're turning it off"
- Watch it together, then: "Screen's sleeping now, we're brushing teeth"
- **No negotiation after.** Clear boundary, delivered matter-of-fact (not punitive)
- **Why:** Autonomy lover respects clear rules; empath appreciates honesty

## Weekly Progression (Keep It Fresh)

JJ is a **fast learner who gets bored after mastery.** Use this template to avoid repetition:

| Week | Core Shift | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Week 1-2** | Repeat same activities in same contexts | Balance bike driveway, Store play kitchen, Danny Go videos |
| **Week 3** | Same activities, new locations | Balance bike to different route, Store play in bedroom, Blippi (new channel) |
| **Week 4** | Combine activities into sequences | Balance bike → obstacle course → dance party to cool down |
| **Week 5** | Introduce new activity in his interest area | Cars: Hot Wheels track building (new), Mario: Mario Kart championship tournament (format change), Disney: act out a scene he picks |

**When boredom signals hit:** He'll tell you. "No more cars" or just walks away. **That's the time to pivot, not push.** Autonomy-lover teaches you the pace.

## Screens: The Honest Framework

**What research says:** Screens aren't evil; quality of engagement matters. His ASMR interest, Danny Go challenge format, Mario Kart gameplay — these aren't passive. They're cognitive.

**Your deployment rule:**
- ✅ **DO:** Screens that require his action (Mario Kart, challenges, games, YouTube scavenger hunts)
- ✅ **DO:** Screens you watch WITH him + convert to physical play after
- ✅ **DO:** Screens matched to his documented interests (Danny Go, Disney, Mario, ASMR-adjacent)
- ❌ **DON'T:** Screens as babysitter while you context-switch
- ❌ **DON'T:** Passive video-watching with no follow-up for >15 min

**The operating principle:** Screens as TOOLS for his interests, not escapes from your presence.

## What NOT to Do (Anti-Patterns)

- ❌ **"Wouldn't you rather...?"** — Don't steer his choices. He picked it; you follow.
- ❌ **Instruction voice on everything.** — "Do it this way" kills autonomy. Narrate instead ("I see you kicking faster").
- ❌ **Cognitive load on yourself.** — This playbook is READY MENUS so you don't have to think when bogged. Pick one, follow his lead.
- ❌ **Screen time as solution to your work pressure.** — That's different from using screens as engagement tools. Know the difference.
- ❌ **Abandoning an activity he's mastered too fast.** — Mastery feels good to him. Do it in new contexts (rotation rule above) before replacing it.

## The Deeper Reason You're Doing This

Your father wasn't present. You're building the opposite model — not by being perfect, but by being THERE and seeing JJ fully. That's the whole thing. He learns what it means to be seen, to be safe with his bigness, to have someone move with him instead of against him.

This playbook is permission to stop overthinking it. **He doesn't need a perfect parent. He needs you present.** The menu is just so you can stay present even when work has your brain.
