## Hypothesis

Adding a "Transformation Isomorphism" pre-selection layer — where you explicitly map the reader's current state, desired state, and internal obstacle BEFORE selecting which story to tell — will produce stories that feel "written for me" rather than "well-crafted." The story's protagonist becomes a proxy for the reader's own journey, making the transformation feel inevitable rather than manufactured.

## Benchmark

**Prompt**: "Write a 150-word story for a LinkedIn post about why the best S&C coaches are invisible online"

### Control (Current genius.md — Intention + Obstacle, Yin-Yang, 5-Second Moment)
- Approach: Third-person narrator tells story of mentor with 200 followers turning away NFL athletes
- Reader position: Observer learning from narrator's experience
- Strengths: Vivid 5-second moment ("I don't. They find me."), good yin-yang contrast
- Weaknesses: Story is ABOUT the narrator, told TO the reader. Reader is audience, not protagonist.
- Composite score: 6.6/10

### Variant (+ Transformation Isomorphism)
- Pre-map: Reader current state = "invisible online, feeling left behind." Desired state = "confident invisibility = excellence." Internal obstacle = "belief that online presence = professional legitimacy."
- Approach: Opens INSIDE reader's head ("You've watched coaches with half your experience..."), protagonist's arc mirrors reader's desired transformation
- Strengths: Reader IS the protagonist. Resolution ("Their hands are full.") functions as personal permission. Surgeon analogy reframes invisibility as proof.
- Composite score: 7.9/10
