# /spiegel-bottleneck — Design-as-Bottleneck

> Use a strategic quality gate to ensure every output feels like one product. The slowdown is the feature.

## When to Use
- Output quality is inconsistent across channels, products, or team members
- Things "feel" incoherent but you can't pinpoint why
- Scaling has created a Frankenstein of different team outputs
- Before any major brand or product expansion

## Inputs Required
1. Current quality control process (who approves, what criteria, how long)
2. Sample outputs from different channels/team members (3-5 examples)
3. Brand/product identity aspirations (what it should feel like)
4. Current shipping cadence (how often things go out)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Cohesion Audit (The Stranger Test)
Take 5 outputs from different channels, teams, or time periods. Ask:
- Would a stranger correctly identify all 5 as coming from the same source?
- Score cohesion 1-10 across: visual language, tone, structural patterns, quality level
- Identify the outputs that "break" cohesion — what's different about them?

### Step 2: Bottleneck Identification
What single gate would ensure cohesion if ALL output passed through it?
Options:
1. **Design review** — Visual and experiential cohesion (Spiegel's default)
2. **Voice/tone review** — Written and verbal consistency
3. **Brand standards review** — Guidelines compliance
4. **Founder review** — Taste and judgment filter
5. **Quality score** — Quantitative rubric with minimum threshold

### Step 3: Gate Design
For the chosen bottleneck:
1. **Criteria**: What specifically gets checked? (List 5-7 checkpoints)
2. **Speed**: Maximum turnaround time for approval (24h recommended)
3. **Authority**: Who has final say? (Single person, not committee)
4. **Override**: Under what circumstances can the gate be bypassed? (Almost never)
5. **Feedback**: How does the gate provider give actionable notes?

### Step 4: Cohesion Standards Document
Create a reference document with:
- 3 "north star" exemplar outputs that define the standard
- 3 "reject" examples showing what fails the gate
- Checklist for self-evaluation before submission to the gate

### Step 5: Shipping Cadence Calibration
Accept that the bottleneck slows you down. Redesign shipping around it:
- What's the new realistic output volume?
- Where can you parallelize work upstream of the gate?
- How do you prevent the bottleneck from becoming a true blocker?

## Output Format
```
## DESIGN BOTTLENECK — [Product/Brand]

### Cohesion Audit Score: X/10
[Evidence from stranger test]

### Chosen Bottleneck: [Type]
- Authority: [Who]
- Criteria: [5-7 checkpoints]
- Turnaround: [Time]
- Override conditions: [When]

### Cohesion Standards
- North stars: [3 exemplars]
- Reject examples: [3 anti-exemplars]
- Self-evaluation checklist

### Adjusted Shipping Cadence
[New volume, parallelization plan, blocker prevention]
```

## Quality Gate
- Stranger test must use real outputs, not hypotheticals
- Bottleneck must have a SINGLE authority (not a committee)
- Must include specific reject examples (not just aspirational standards)
- Must explicitly acknowledge speed tradeoff and design around it
