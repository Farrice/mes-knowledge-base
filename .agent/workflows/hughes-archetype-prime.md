# Story-Archetype Priming (`/hughes-archetype-prime`)

You are operating from Chase Hughes's claim that **~7-12 story archetypes are wired into human cognition older than language**, and that effective persuasion = identifying which archetype the situation maps to, then priming its environmental components without ever naming it. The audience's own story-completion drive does the persuasion. Naming the archetype breaks the spell.

## When This Fires

Run this workflow whenever:
- A founder/brand/client needs a strategic narrative (origin story, pitch, "about" copy)
- A trial argument needs structural persuasion beyond the facts
- Content needs to feel inevitable rather than argued
- A difficult relationship is stuck and you suspect mismatched story arcs are the cause
- An audience is upset/disappointed and the disappointment looks like *Spielberg-ending frustration* (their archetype was primed by reality but reality won't complete it)

## Execution

### Step 1 — Diagnose the Available Archetypes

Map the situation against the canonical archetype set. Hughes's working list (drawn from Jung/Campbell/folk-narrative tradition):

| Archetype | Natural Resolution | When to Prime It |
|---|---|---|
| **David vs. Goliath** | Small wins through asymmetric leverage | Underdog client, scrappy startup vs. incumbent, individual vs. system |
| **Hero's Journey** | Departure → trial → return with the elixir | Founder narrative, transformation case study, founder-led brand |
| **Wounded Healer** | The wound becomes the source of the gift | Coaches/therapists/teachers selling expertise that came from their own collapse |
| **Tragic Comedy** | The funny mess that won't resolve cleanly | Diagnosing situations where audiences are demanding closure that won't come |
| **Redemption Arc** | Fall → reckoning → restoration | Comeback stories, second-act founders, rehabilitated brands |
| **Fall From Grace** | Rise → hubris → collapse | Cautionary positioning, contrast frames against bloated competitors |
| **Mentor & Apprentice** | Wisdom transmission earns its successor | Premium services where the offer is access to someone who has done the thing |
| **The Outsider Returns** | Exile carries back what insiders cannot see | Founders who left an industry, then re-entered with a fresh frame |
| **The Reluctant Champion** | Power refused, then accepted under necessity | Premium pricing, leadership of unwanted responsibility |
| **The Long Wait** | Patience rewarded by inevitable arrival | Slow-build brands, compounding-asset narratives, anti-hustle positioning |
| **The Reveal** | Hidden truth surfaces, breaking false reality | Investigative content, exposé pieces, paradigm-shift frames |
| **The Test Failed** | The failure was the lesson, not the loss | Resilience narratives, post-mortem framing, "I should have listened" content |

Identify **the archetype whose natural resolution will move the audience toward the action you need.** Pick exactly one. Multi-archetype constructions confuse the brain's completion engine.

### Step 2 — Inventory the Archetype's Environmental Components

For each archetype, the brain recognizes it via a small set of environmental signals — not the title, not the resolution. Examples:

- **David vs. Goliath**: Disproportion. Indifferent giant. Dispatched challenger. A smooth stone. A walk down a hill. Years of being ignored. The size mismatch as visible fact.
- **Hero's Journey**: A threshold. A refused call. A mentor figure. A descent. A lonely test. A return.
- **Wounded Healer**: The original wound, witnessed. The years of working alone. The first patient who arrived for the same reason. The healer's own scar.
- **The Long Wait**: A specific number of years. A repeated discipline. A slow accumulation. A door that finally opens by itself.
- **The Reveal**: The thing everyone walked past. The detail no one was supposed to notice. The pattern that, once seen, cannot be unseen.

Generate **3-5 environmental components** for the chosen archetype that are specific to the actor/situation in front of you.

### Step 3 — Prime the Components, Never the Title

Build the narrative using only the environmental components. Discipline:
- **Never name the archetype** ("This is a David and Goliath story" — fail)
- **Never name the resolution** ("And in the end, the underdog wins" — fail)
- **Never explain the priming** ("Notice how this is structured around a classic story arc" — fail)
- **Do** stage the components in the order the original archetype sequences them
- **Do** make components vivid, sensory, and specific to the actor

### Step 4 — The Negative-Space Test

Before finalizing, ask:
- Could a literate audience member identify the archetype from the components alone?
- Could they predict the resolution without reading the rest?
- Would naming the archetype add nothing they didn't already feel?

If yes to all three: the priming is working.
If they couldn't identify the archetype, the components were too generic. Sharpen them.
If naming the archetype WOULD add information, you haven't primed deeply enough — go back and add concrete environmental detail.

### Step 5 — Diagnostic Use (Conflict / Disappointment)

When the workflow is run for diagnosis instead of construction:
- Identify which archetype the audience/individual has been primed to expect
- Identify which archetype reality is actually delivering
- Name the mismatch. The pain often dissolves on naming.

Example: "You're upset that the Epstein situation isn't resolving. You've been primed for a Redemption Arc with the bad guys going to prison. We are, in fact, in a Tragic Comedy. The work is to update your archetype, not to demand the wrong story complete."

## Example Output

**Context**: A boutique copywriter has spent 7 years grinding before getting their first $20K client. They need an "About" page. The audience is high-end founders who distrust hype and respond to earned expertise.

**ARCHETYPE DIAGNOSIS**:
- Situation: copywriter introducing themselves to skeptical premium buyers
- Archetype selected: **The Long Wait** — natural resolution is "patience rewarded by inevitable arrival." Tells the buyer they're hiring someone whose craft was earned, not claimed.
- Archetypes considered: Hero's Journey (rejected — too founder-narrative-flavored, the audience is suspicious of triumphs), Wounded Healer (rejected — implies the buyer is in pain, which is wrong frame for B2B premium)

**ENVIRONMENTAL COMPONENTS**:
1. The seven-year span (concrete, specific number)
2. A specific repeated daily discipline (writing one practice headline a day, every morning, no audience)
3. The first email that came in unsolicited
4. The detail of how that first client found them (a buried Reddit comment from 2021 they'd forgotten writing)
5. The unchanged morning desk, before and after

**NARRATIVE CONSTRUCTION** (delivered as the About page):

> For seven years I wrote one headline a day before coffee. Not for clients. For nobody.
>
> The first email came on a Wednesday in May. A founder had read a Reddit comment I'd written in 2021 and forgotten about, traced it to a portfolio I hadn't updated in two years, and sent a one-line message: *"Are you taking work?"*
>
> The desk hasn't moved. The discipline hasn't changed. The clients now arrive on their own.

**NEGATIVE-SPACE TEST**:
- ✅ "Long Wait" never appears
- ✅ Resolution ("you should hire me because I'm the rare earned-craft option") never stated
- ✅ Architecture not explained
- ✅ A reader feels the arc — patience, accumulation, eventual arrival — without being told
- ✅ Components are sensory (Wednesday in May, one-line message, unchanged desk)

**What elevates this**: The Reddit comment is more persuasive than any testimonial because it primes a different archetype — *The Reveal* — nested inside *The Long Wait*. The buyer's brain runs both completions and concludes "they're real."
