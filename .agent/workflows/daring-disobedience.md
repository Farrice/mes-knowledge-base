# /daring-disobedience — The Skater's Mindset

Deploy Ocean Vuong's Daringness as Disobedience protocol. Every competent writer is held back not by skill but by the refusal to be called "too much." This workflow diagnoses self-censorship and prescribes the creative courage to produce work that DARES.

## The Chain

1. **SCORE**: Intent Score 5
2. **ROUTE**: Ocean Vuong (perceptual writing)
3. **LOAD**: Tier 2 — `skills/ocean-vuong-perceptual-writing/SKILL.md` + `genius.md` + Pattern 13 (Daringness as Disobedience)
4. **PRODUCE**: Execute phases below
5. **FINALIZE**: chain_runner.py

## Execution

### Phase 1: The Self-Censorship Audit

Read the draft. For every moment where you sense the prose pulling back — where you ALMOST said something but retreated to safety — mark it with [SC]:

| Signal | What It Sounds Like |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Generalization retreat** | You had a specific image but replaced it with a safe generality |
| **Tone flattening** | You wrote something bold/weird/uncomfortable and immediately added a qualifier |
| **Metaphor timidity** | You reached for a daring comparison and settled for a conventional one |
| **Ending retreat** | The piece was heading somewhere uncomfortable and you steered back to comfort |
| **Voice suppression** | Your natural rhythm appeared and you "fixed" it to sound more professional |

### Phase 2: The Eight-Stair Diagnosis

Vuong's metaphor: "The skater who falls on an eight-stair still gets respect. The one who nails a three-stair gets nothing." Rate your current ambition level:

- **3 stairs** (safe): Everything is competent, polished, risk-free. Nobody will criticize it. Nobody will remember it.
- **5 stairs** (moderate): Some risks taken, but hedged. Daring ideas presented in safe packaging.
- **8 stairs** (daring): Genuine risk. Perceptions that might confuse. Sentences that might be "too much." Images that make you uncomfortable to publish.

**If you're at 3-5 stairs**: Identify the 3 moments where you retreated from 8. Rewrite those moments at full daring.

### Phase 3: The "Too Much" Prescription

For each self-censored moment, write the version you were afraid of:
1. The image you almost used but thought was "too weird"
2. The sentence you almost wrote but thought was "too literary"
3. The perception you almost shared but thought was "too personal"
4. The structure you almost tried but thought was "too confusing"

**The rule**: If no one might call it "too much," it isn't daring enough.

### Phase 4: The Disobedience Test

- [ ] Does this piece disobey at least one writing convention you were taught?
- [ ] Is there at least one moment where you're genuinely uncertain if it "works"?
- [ ] Would you feel slightly nervous publishing this?
- [ ] Could someone call this "too much"?
- [ ] If all four are NO — you haven't dared enough. Return to Phase 3.

## Output

1. Self-censorship audit results
2. Eight-stair rating (before and after)
3. The "too much" versions of censored moments
4. Disobedience test results
5. Revised draft at 8-stair level
