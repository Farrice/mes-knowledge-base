# /literary-edging — Strategic Denial Engine

Deploy Pattern 8 from Ocean Vuong's extraction as a standalone composition tool. Every piece of content builds expectations. The question is: do you satisfy them boringly, or do you deny them brilliantly and deliver something the reader didn't know they wanted?

## The Chain

1. **SCORE**: Intent Score 5
2. **ROUTE**: Ocean Vuong (perceptual writing)
3. **LOAD**: Tier 1 — `skills/ocean-vuong-perceptual-writing/SKILL.md` + Pattern 8 (Satisfying vs. Denying Expectation)
4. **PRODUCE**: Execute phases below
5. **FINALIZE**: chain_runner.py

## Execution

### Phase 1: Map the Expectations

Read the piece (or outline it). At each key transition, note what the reader expects:

| Section/Paragraph | What the Reader Expects | Currently Delivered | Satisfy or Deny? |
|-------------------|------------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| After opening | The thesis or "point" | [what you wrote] | |
| After problem statement | The solution | [what you wrote] | |
| After buildup | The climax/payoff | [what you wrote] | |
| After vulnerable moment | The lesson/redemption | [what you wrote] | |
| Closing | The neat conclusion | [what you wrote] | |

### Phase 2: The Satisfy/Deny Decision

**Not every expectation should be denied.** The ratio matters:

- **All satisfied**: Boring. Predictable. Reader finishes feeling "that was fine" but carries nothing.
- **All denied**: Exhausting. Reader feels like the piece is being deliberately difficult.
- **Strategic denial (2:1 or 3:1)**: Build trust through satisfaction, then deny at the KEY moment. The denial lands BECAUSE trust was built.

Vuong's formulation: like a DJ. You give them the beat, you give them the beat, they start dancing... and then you DROP the beat. The exhilaration is "embedded in linearity" — it only works because of the pattern that preceded it.

### Phase 3: Design the Denial Moment

Select 1-2 moments for strategic denial:

1. **The Expectation**: What does the reader think is coming?
2. **The Denial**: What do you NOT give them?
3. **The Delivery**: What do you give them INSTEAD — that's more true, more surprising, more useful than what they expected?
4. **The Exhilaration**: Why is the unexpected delivery better than the expected one?

**The key**: The denial is NOT a bait-and-switch. The reader should feel: "I didn't know I wanted that, but it's better than what I predicted."

### Phase 4: Timing Architecture

Where the denial lives matters enormously:

| Placement | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| **Early denial** (25-30%) | Signals "this piece won't go where you think." Sets the reader to alert mode. |
| **Middle denial** (45-55%) | The classic DJ drop. Maximum exhilaration because maximum expectation buildup. |
| **Late denial** (75-85%) | Devastating. Reader was SURE of the conclusion. The pivot changes everything retrospectively. |
| **Final sentence denial** | The reader leaves carrying something different from what they came for. Strongest haunting potential. |

### Phase 5: The Exhilaration Test

- [ ] Does the denial create genuine surprise — not confusion?
- [ ] Is the delivered alternative MORE true/valuable than the expected delivery?
- [ ] Does the reader feel rewarded, not tricked?
- [ ] Would the piece be worse if you satisfied the expectation instead?
- [ ] Does the denial change how the reader perceives everything that came before it?
