# The 11th Prompt Can Wait

Nine workflows. Ten prompts.

Nothing from the factory had met an audience.

I built all nine before this story.

It could map the viewer's shift, carry it into production, and flag missing proof. There were three component receipts and a verifier. Every local check passed.

No person had decided whether a real Farrice piece meant anything to them.

System work is real work. It is also safer than letting a person decide whether the story landed.

You can spend the whole week tightening a prompt or rerunning the scorecard. The system never goes silent, and it cannot misunderstand you the way a person can.

Creators know the private sentence:

“One more pass. Then I'll publish.”

Another pass becomes a week. Soon the system is excellent and the work is still sitting there.

A factory earns its name when something leaves it.

The smallest honest test is one complete piece before one more component. A real source. A clear shift. One useful move. Then the audience gets a vote.

Local checks can tell you the machine runs.

Only a person can tell you whether the work meant something.

The 11th prompt can wait.

This story needed to leave first.
