## Operating Verdict

This series begins with a content factory that can prove nearly everything except the thing it was built for: whether the work helps a real person see or do something differently.

The higher-ceiling move is to let that practical contradiction grow into a question about authorship:

> Can a creator build a powerful system without using the system to avoid audience contact, then learn from the audience without handing over the pen?

The sequence is not seven versions of “publish sooner.” Each installment gives the reader a different job:

**distinguish, diagnose, design, risk, measure, decide, integrate**

Every installment also delivers one named Field Card. A reader can use any card alone. Together, the seven cards become the **Audience Contact Kit**.

This is a campaign plan, not campaign proof. FT-001 has no human acceptance or audience receipt yet. The current plugin verdict remains **54/100, keep as workflow**.

## Series North Star

- **Reader:** Creators and AI operators whose systems keep improving while contact with a real audience keeps moving one task away.
- **Starting belief:** “A more complete system will make me ready.”
- **Private sentence:** “One more pass. Then I’ll publish.”
- **Antagonist:** Productive certainty: work that is useful and also protects the creator from being misunderstood.
- **Cost:** The system accumulates capability while its most important uncertainty remains untouched.
- **Series promise:** Give the reader a way to distinguish internal readiness from audience evidence, design a valid field test, interpret response without category errors, and preserve authorship while learning.
- **Final after-state:** “I know what the system can prove, what only contact can reveal, and what I will change next.”
- **Safe positive action:** Release one complete, source-grounded piece; collect bounded evidence; change one primary variable.
- **Climax:** The factory is redefined as a completed release-and-return loop, not a larger inventory of prompts.
- **Sequel question:** Can meaning become repeatable without making the work generic?

## The Three-Act Arc

### Act I: The Comfortable Factory

Parts 1–2 expose the gap and identify why it persists. The reader first separates local proof from audience meaning, then sees how avoidance can wear the clothes of serious work.

### Act II: Contact Costs Something

Parts 3–6 move from advice into authorship. The reader designs an honest test, accepts that clarity cannot eliminate all misunderstanding, discovers that a full dashboard can still miss the answer, and learns how to let the audience inform a decision without surrendering taste.

Part 5 is the planned All Is Lost turn. The creator has published and gathered numbers, yet still cannot honestly claim the work meant something. Victory has to be redefined.

### Act III: The Return Loop

Part 7 integrates the cards into one operating loop. The proof of a factory is no longer content volume. It is whether a complete artifact reached a person, returned with usable evidence, and changed the next creative decision.

### Smallest Complete Test Spec

1. **Real source:** Name the lived scene, observed event, or evidence that earns the piece.
2. **Recognition:** State the exact private sentence that should feel understood.

5. **Decision rule:** Name the response that could change the next production decision.

### Four-Scoreboard Card

| Scoreboard | Question | Valid evidence | Does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Did the package enter people’s feeds? | Impressions, reach, qualified entry | Attention quality or meaning |
| Retention | Did people stay? | Completion or dwell signals when available | Why they stayed |
| Connection | Did they feel recognized enough to respond or return? | Substantive comments, saves when exposed, privacy-safe DMs | Behavioral change |
| Transformation | Did someone report a relevant perspective or action change? | Direct self-report tied to the piece | Clinical change, causality, or broad impact |

**All Is Lost rule:** If exposure is insufficient, mark the result INCONCLUSIVE. Do not punish the body for a distribution failure or turn a small sample into an audience truth.

### Co-Greenlight Matrix

| Observed pattern | Decision |
|---|---|
| Connection is strong; discovery is weak | Keep the body. Test the package only. |

### Release–Return Protocol

**Source to artifact to person to signal to judgment to one change to the next artifact**

1. Ground the piece in a real source.
2. Build one complete transformation contract.
3. Put it before a real person with permission.
4. Separate discovery, retention, connection, and transformation signals.
5. Apply Farrice’s felt verdict and the bounded audience decision.
6. Change one primary variable.
7. Preserve the accepted artifact and issue the next version separately.

**Climax gate:** Part 7 can explain the protocol before full series proof, but it may not say the series built community, transformed viewers, or proved repeatability without accepted audience receipts.

## Format System

The mapping below is a **DESIGN HYPOTHESIS**, not a claim that one format universally performs better.

| Format role | Best use in this series | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|
| Text recognition post | Interior contradiction, private sentence, and hard bookend | The value is mainly a diagram or sequence |
| Talking-head mirror | The voice and emotional precision carry the recognition | No real language exists yet and performance would fake certainty |
| Document carousel | The reader needs a saveable decision tool or specification | The slides would be a bullet dump with no reader decision |
| Field-receipt story | A real response, silence, or production event changes the meaning | The event is private, weakly sourced, or being inflated |
| Whiteboard explainer | Several proof categories must stay visually distinct | The concept can be said more clearly in one paragraph |
| Flagship synthesis video | Prior evidence and assets need integration | The campaign has not earned a climax |

### Format Routing Questions

1. Define the reader’s after-state.
2. Identify the real evidence this installment can show.
3. Name what the reader should be able to use or decide afterward.
4. Choose the surface that carries that job with the least production theater.
5. Confirm that the available owner can produce it without losing the transformation invariant.

There is no mandatory format rotation. Repeating a format is allowed when it remains the best carrier. Producing six adaptations of the same unproven idea is not a series.

## Angle Engine

Build each angle from five parts:

> **Concrete operating fact + private sentence + consequential uncertainty + perspective shift + usable decision**

If any part is missing, the result is a topic or opinion, not yet a field-series angle.

### Selected Angle Path

| Part | Angle class | Old belief | New decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Proof boundary | Local completion proves meaningful readiness | Separate operational proof from human evidence |
| 2 | Productive hiding | Useful work cannot also be avoidance | Ask which uncertainty the task closes |
| 3 | Test validity | Publishing automatically creates a useful test | Define source, shift, action, and decision rule first |
| 4 | Authorship risk | Perfect clarity can eliminate misreading | Clarify duty; accept bounded interpretive risk |
| 5 | Measurement | A full dashboard settles whether the work worked | Read four different scoreboards |
| 6 | Taste governance | Feedback must be ignored or obeyed | Give evidence a bounded vote |
| 7 | Operating system | A factory is its volume and capability | Measure completed release-and-return loops |

### Reserve Angle Bank

Use these only when real evidence earns them:

1. **System debt versus audience debt:** Which missing capability blocks the test, and which merely delays exposure?
2. **False completion:** Why a finished post without a learning decision can still be unfinished work.
3. **Silence is not one thing:** Low exposure, weak recognition, and no reported action support different interpretations.
4. **Creator overfitting:** When audience language improves precision and when it begins erasing authorship.
5. **Frozen winners:** Why an accepted artifact should be preserved before one-variable experiments begin.
6. **Freshness debt:** When repeated production starts copying its own past success instead of the source.
7. **The first honest no:** A rejected piece can produce better evidence than another internal score.
8. **Scale without flattening:** What the system must preserve as output volume grows.

### Gate 2: One-Part Publication

- Freeze and hash the accepted Part 1.
- Publish only with explicit approval and manual owner action.
- Do not produce a carousel, thread, or video adaptation before signal.

### Gate 3: Seven-Day Receipt

Review the four scoreboards separately.

- Insufficient exposure: mark the result inconclusive. Do not diagnose the body.
- Weak recognition: revise the private sentence or reader payoff before the hook.
- Recognition without reported action: revise the shift-to-action bridge.
- Three recognition signals plus one reported action: the next angle or adaptation may be greenlit under FT-001’s local rule.

That local threshold is not a platform benchmark and does not transfer automatically to later campaigns.

### Gate 4: Act Checkpoints

- **After Part 3:** Are the Field Cards independently useful, and do callbacks make earlier cards more useful?
- **After Part 5:** Is the main dramatic question still alive, or has the series become an analytics tutorial?
- **After Part 7:** Which observations repeat, which remain hypotheses, and what single question deserves the next field series?

### One-Variable Learning Rule

Format variation makes campaign comparisons directional, not causal. For an actual repair, change one primary variable:

| Weak signal | Primary variable to test |
|---|---|
| Discovery | Package |
| Retention | Opening or beat continuity |
| Connection | Private sentence or recognition hinge |
| Transformation | Perspective shift or safe action |
| Production friction | Format or capacity |

Freeze the source, audience, mission, arc position, and accepted voice decisions unless the evidence shows the premise itself is broken.
