# Parallax Transformation Post

## Publishable LinkedIn body

Somewhere around the $14,000 mark in courses I'd never finish, a business coach looked me in the eye and said, “Farrice, you need to niche down.”

I nodded. I paid him. I tried.

For the next two years, I built around “just fitness.”

I buried the psychology. Hid the spirituality. Never mentioned anime to a client.

The content worked, at least in the ways I knew how to count. Engagement. Likes.

I didn't want to open the laptop the next day.

That should have been the clue.

The coach was right about one part. A business needs a clear promise. People should know why they are there.

I turned that into an identity decision.

I let that promise decide which parts of me were allowed near the work.

The first Parallax edition opened with Neville Goddard's imagination doctrine, the biomechanics of a Romanian deadlift, and Goku's training arc on King Kai's planet.

All in the same paragraph.

I left it in.

Then I published it.

The work still needed focus. Focus had a different job now: help the reader understand why all of those things were in the room together.

If you keep deleting part of your mind because it looks off-brand, try this before you cut it again.

Put that interest next to the work people already know you for. Finish one sentence:

“Because I know both, I notice ______ that people in either field miss.”

That sentence is a clue to the connection you've been hiding.

A focused promise does not require a smaller person.
