# Proof-to-Market Source-Enriched Branch

## Publishable LinkedIn body

Your copy got prettier and kept saying nothing.

By the time a writer sees it, the sentence has already passed through the whole building.

The study holds. Legal cuts the claim. Creative flattens what survives. The agency sends another batch that clears and says nothing.

Every version sounds finished.

None gives the buyer a reason to choose.

Then you open a competitor's ad and feel two things in a row.

First, disbelief.

Then the worse one: their ad is working.

The problem gets renamed “creative fatigue,” and the next brief asks for more hooks.

More copy multiplies whatever decision is already there. When the decision is foggy, a folder full of variants gives you a folder full of ways to sound unsure.

The decision is plain enough to fit on one page:

What can we prove?

Who has to believe it, and what will they still doubt after they see the proof?

What will we still say when legal, creative, and media are all in the room?

I learned what that gap sounds like from the other side of a vitamin-shop counter.

First, I was the kid buying the loud pre-workouts, fat burners, and whatever the bodybuilding magazines said would deliver the physique, the look, the feel, the confidence.

Mostly the confidence.

For three years, I answered the questions the label had skipped: will the pills go down, does it mix with my medication, whey or isolate, is any of this worth my money.

Half the job was telling somebody to put it back.

After the counter, the question followed me through 18 years of coaching more than 1,000 people: what should I take, is this stack worth it, does any of it work. Different setting. Same doubt about trusting a product with your body and your money.

One useful test is to have legal, creative, and media answer those questions separately. If the answers split, the decision has not been made.

Until it is, the buyer is left choosing between your caution and somebody else's confidence.
