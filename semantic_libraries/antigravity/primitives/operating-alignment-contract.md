# Operating Alignment Contract

Use this contract when Farrice asks for the Codex system itself to feel more
coherent, powerful, repeatable, global, less noisy, or better orchestrated.

The goal is not to summon every expert. The goal is one operating layer that
turns raw intent into the right owner, bounded support gates, proof, feedback,
and the next action.

## Contract

1. Raw intent enters Autopilot or Virtuoso.
2. Co-Creative Launchpad identifies predicted need, center, edges, questions
   that change execution, and route bias.
3. Meta-intent is classified before expert routing.
4. One owner is selected.
5. Expert composition is bounded to contribution slots when needed.
6. Proof runs through verifiers, receipts, read-only status, or before/after
   behavior checks.
7. Feedback capture is prepared through routing intelligence or the friction
   ledger.
8. The closeout ends with one next action, not a menu of possible systems.

signals. They do not replace the control-plane owner.

## Orchestration Receipt

Every meaningful operating-alignment run should expose:

- objective
- meta_intent
- owner
- composition_owner
- route
- support_gates
- expert_lenses
- subagent_boundary
- verifier_results
- feedback_hook

If the run is trace-only or risk-blocked, verifier results may be `planned`.
Do not claim execution proof until commands, checks, or artifact changes have
actually run.

## Automation Boundary

Recurring loops should be useful and quiet:

- daily cockpit: status, critical failures, activation queue, next action
- weekly pulse plus supervised queue: stale systems, sleeping assets, routing
  evidence, and queue-only improvements
- monthly hygiene/evolution review: due-gated cleanup and evolution
  recommendations

Dry-run means no writes: no reports, queue entries, routing logs, activation
records, external writes, or global mirrors.

No automation deletion, disabling, broad archive, or destructive cleanup happens
without an explicit approval list.
