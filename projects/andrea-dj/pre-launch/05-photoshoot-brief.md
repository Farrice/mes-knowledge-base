## Treatment Doctrine

This is not a shot list. This is a treatment — the visual world Resonance lives in, expressed as specific frames a photographer can execute against.

**The room we're photographing into**: 2pm-5pm. Wood floor or terracotta tile. A single window doing all the lighting work. A single human or a single object — never a crowd, never a stage, never a club. Warmth that came from the room, not from a filter. The viewer should feel they could walk in and the air would smell like coffee and old vinyl.

**The visual contract**: every shot in this package must pass the daytime test (if it could have been taken at 11pm under fluorescent lights, it fails) AND the room test (if the room could be any room, it fails — the rooms have to feel like THIS brand's rooms). Reference shoots that get this right: Saul Leiter color work, Vivian Maier street, Tina Barney intimate scenes, Annie Leibovitz portrait studies. Reference shoots that get this WRONG: any nightclub editorial, any influencer-portrait pack, any wellness-brand campaign.

**The voice of the photographer**: a craftsperson, not a content creator. We are not making "Instagram aesthetic" — we are making images that ONLY work for Resonance. If a shot could be Lululemon or Equinox or a Costa Rica tourism board, rewrite it. The shoot proves the brand is for adults who notice what nobody else notices.

**Color and light grade**: cream-and-terracotta palette per DESIGN.md. No cool blue casts. No clinical white. Shadows live — keep them; don't crush them. Highlights breathe — keep them; don't blow them. The grade should feel like the light at 2pm in a room someone furnished slowly.

---

## ⚠️ Identity vs. Performance Framing (READ FIRST)

Shots involving turntables, headphones, and records are **IDENTITY** shots — they show who Andrea is (a DJ by craft + lineage, the curator behind the music). They are NOT "here's Andrea performing at Event #1." At Event #1, Andrea hosts and works the room — mic in hand, welcoming guests, between-songs energy. She is not behind the decks. A separate DJ (TBD, hire by Week 4) plays.

This shoot therefore needs **two distinct posture categories**: (a) identity shots — DJ-by-craft, records-and-headphones, music-as-lineage; and (b) host/MC shots — mic-in-hand, welcoming, room-managing, attentive listening. Both are Andrea. Both are real. The photographer should understand the distinction before the shoot starts.

See `08-andrea-event-role-doctrine.md` for the full doctrine.

## What This Shoot Is FOR

Three jobs:

1. **Founder content** — Andrea's face, hands, posture in daylight. Carries Founder Pillar content (origin story Reels, founder voice IG posts).
2. **Brand atmosphere** — Hands on records, vinyl, daytime room textures, candle, terracotta-on-cream still lifes. Carries Music Pillar, Curation Pillar, Spine Pillar visuals.
3. **Anticipation content** — Empty rooms with daylight, doorway shots, "the room you're walking into" framing. Carries Event #1 anticipation content as launch nears.

**What this shoot is NOT**:
- An event capture (Event #1 day handles that — different brief)
- A singles-event-promo flyer pack (banned aesthetic — these read as nightclub or wedding-industry, both wrong)
- An influencer-portrait studio session (off-brand — Andrea is a craftsperson, not a creator)
- A wellness brand campaign (banned — no soft pastel mood boards, no "be your authentic self" gestures)
- A Costa Rica tourism shoot (banned even when we're invoking Costa Rica — we're invoking the lived specificity, not the postcard)
- Anything that could be shot in a studio with infinity backdrop (the rooms matter — they are the proof)

## Section 1 — Shot List (12-15 Hero Shots)

Organized by content use case. Photographer brings this list. Andrea reviews on-site.

### Founder shots (4-5)

1. **Andrea profile at golden hour, real daylight** — three-quarter portrait, eyes calm, not posed-smiling. The kind of photo that reads as "she's in mid-thought." Background: window light + a wall (terracotta accent or natural wood preferred).

   **Story** — evokes the founder who has already decided what kind of room she's making, photographed in the second after she stopped thinking about being photographed. Not "the founder shot." The shot of someone who knows her own taste.

   **Reference** — Annie Leibovitz's 1996 portrait of Joan Didion (eyes off-camera, mid-thought, window light camera-left, body slightly turned). Cross with Tyler Mitchell's Vogue 2018 portrait of Beyoncé in the field — the daylight honesty, the refusal to flatten skin.

   **Execution notes** — 50mm or 85mm lens. Single-source window light camera-left at 7:00-7:30pm. Subject in the right third of the frame; her eyeline carries the negative space on the left. Crop to chest-up or three-quarter (never headshot-tight). AVOID: direct eye contact with lens, full smile, hand-on-chin gestures, any pose that reads as "press photo."
2. **Andrea's hands on records** — close crop, no face needed. Vinyl in frame, hands placing or pulling a record from a sleeve. Daylight hitting the record's grain.

   **Story** — evokes the curator's hands doing the work nobody sees — the music-school discipline rendered as a single gesture. The hands that picked the set, not the hands of a content creator pretending to DJ.

   **Reference** — Reid Miles's Blue Note Records album covers (1957-1967) — particularly the close-crop hands-on-instrument photographs. Cross with Saul Leiter's late color work for the way warm tones come from the room, not from post.

   **Execution notes** — 50mm or macro. Single window-light source raking across the vinyl at a low angle so the grooves catch a glint. Frame the hands in the left two-thirds; let the record's curve carry the eye out of frame. Shoot from slightly above to honor the working-with-the-record posture. AVOID: jewelry catching the light (kills the texture story), perfectly clean nails (the hands should look used), a sleeve placed for camera (it should look mid-action).
3. **Andrea at the decks, from behind** — silhouette + shoulder + headphones around her neck. Daylight through a window in the background. Reads as: she's at work, you're seeing her presence not her face.

   **Story** — evokes the lineage shot — the DJ-by-craft posture inherited from the music-school cafeteria and the orchestra rehearsal room. Not "DJ at work." The shot of a person at the center of something they built.

   **Reference** — Roy DeCarava's Harlem jazz photography (1950s-60s), particularly the from-behind musician shots where the room is doing the speaking. Cross with William Eggleston's domestic interiors — the way an ordinary scene becomes load-bearing when the light is correct.

   **Execution notes** — 35mm or 50mm. Camera positioned behind and slightly to the side of Andrea so we see shoulder, neck, the curve of headphones, and the window beyond. Window provides the rim light on her hair and shoulder; the decks fall into mid-tone shadow. Shoot at f/2.8-4 so the decks soften but stay readable. AVOID: any silhouette so dark it reads as nightclub stage, lens flare smears, the decks lit equally with Andrea (the photograph is about her presence at them, not a product shot of the gear).
4. **Andrea walking the lakefront** — wide-ish shot, real daylight, candid posture. Carries founder-content cards ("the room I came to Chicago looking for"). She's walking toward camera or perpendicular, not posing.

   **Story** — evokes the founder in transit, mid-thought, the city as the second protagonist. The shot that says she came here looking for something and is still walking toward it. Chicago is the room behind her.

   **Reference** — Vivian Maier's Chicago street photography (1950s-60s) — the way she captures a single body in motion against an architectural plane. Cross with Saul Leiter's New York color work for the chromatic restraint and the way a single figure carries the frame.

   **Execution notes** — 35mm. Andrea walks perpendicular to camera at normal pace; photographer captures the gait, not the pose. Frame her in the right third with Lake Michigan / city / a wall taking the rest. Late golden hour 7:45-8:15pm so the light rakes across her without flattening her face. Shoot at f/4-5.6 for environmental sharpness. AVOID: Andrea looking at camera, the over-shoulder glance, the contemplative-by-the-water trope, anything that reads as engagement photography or tourism board.
5. **Andrea's hands holding a coffee cup (or kettle pour)** — sense-detail anchor for the conversational register of the story. Ceramic, cream-toned mug. Hand close-up.

   **Story** — evokes the grandmother's coffee table from Andrea's founder story — the room that held you because the room was already set up to hold you. A single object proving the brand has its own gravity. Sense-detail for the heart-encounter frame.

   **Reference** — Tina Barney's domestic interior work — particularly the way she photographs hands and small objects with the same dignity as faces. Cross with Wolfgang Tillmans's still life photography for the willingness to let a small thing carry an entire mood.

   **Execution notes** — 50mm or macro. Hands and mug in the lower third of the frame; daylight from a window camera-left providing the warm-side glow on ceramic. The mug should be visibly used (no staged-perfect placement). Shoot at f/2.8-4 with the steam soft-focused if there's a pour. AVOID: latte-art close-ups, perfectly-styled flat-lay composition, any framing that reads as Sunday morning lifestyle blog or coffee brand campaign.

### Brand atmosphere (5-6)

6. **Vinyl record on turntable, needle drop** — close-up. Daylight from one angle (camera-left or right). Slight shadow from the tonearm.

   **Story** — evokes the second the song starts — the bodily moment before any music has played, when the room is about to change and only the curator knows what's coming. The mechanic of the brand rendered as a single object in motion.

   **Execution notes** — 50mm macro or 90mm. Single window-light source from a low angle so the tonearm casts a real shadow across the vinyl groove. Frame tight enough that the platter dominates but the needle is the focal point. Shoot at f/4-5.6 for groove sharpness. The platter does not need to be moving — capture the moment before. AVOID: artificial backlight glow, tonearm in pristine product-shot position, anything that reads as turntable manufacturer ad. The shadow has to feel like the room cast it.

   **Reference** — Reid Miles's Blue Note Records cover photography — particularly the way album jackets become objects of design discipline. Cross with Tina Barney's domestic still-life work where personal objects carry the room's whole sociology.

   **Execution notes** — 35mm or 50mm. Records arranged on a wood or terracotta surface; daylight raking across them from window-left at a 30-45 degree angle so the jackets cast soft shadows on each other. Shoot from above at a slight angle (not pure top-down). One or two records pulled slightly out of the stack to show edge depth. AVOID: perfectly stacked alignment (reads as record store display), records facing camera dead-on, any framing that suggests this is a music-blog editorial.    **Reference** — Luigi Ghirri's Italian interior photography (1980s) — the way a quiet room with daylight becomes the entire story. Cross with William Eggleston's color work on empty spaces where the light is the only event.

   **Execution notes** — 24mm or 35mm (the only wide-end lens permitted in this brief). Camera positioned at standing eye-level, centered on the strongest window light source. Frame the floor in the lower third, wall and window in the upper two-thirds. Let the shadow geometry from the window do the composition. Shoot at f/8 for depth-of-field sharpness across the room. AVOID: any visible event setup (no chairs arranged, no decks, no signage), interior decorator staging, anything that reads as Airbnb listing or real estate photography. The room is empty because it's waiting, not because it's for sale.

   **Story** — evokes the ritual object before the ritual — the small physical decision that signals someone is going to do this right. The candle unlit at 2pm is more honest than the candle lit at 11pm. Daylight earns this object back from every wellness brand that has misused it.

   **Reference** — Wolfgang Tillmans's still-life work on single objects — the patient, anti-decorative gaze. Cross with Robert Polidori's interior photography for the way a single domestic object holds historical weight.

   **Execution notes** — 50mm macro or 85mm. Single object on a wood surface; daylight from window-left providing the warm side-light. Candle in the right third of the frame; let the surface texture (wood grain, terracotta tile) carry the left two-thirds. Shoot at f/4 with shallow depth so the wick stays sharp and the background softens. AVOID: candle lit (kills the daytime test instantly), candle styled with eucalyptus / linen / spa accessories, anything that reads as Goop product photography or wellness retreat lookbook. The candle is unused; it's waiting for a room that hasn't filled yet.
10. **Coffee + book + window light** — sense-detail still life. Cream mug, a book (Andrea picks — Pallasmaa, Vuong, anything in her actual reading life), morning light. Anchors founder content.

   **Story** — evokes the founder's interior life as material objects — the literary register of the brand made physical. The shot that says Andrea reads, thinks, and chooses with care. Anchors the founder content without ever putting Andrea in the frame.

   **Reference** — Saul Leiter's New York still-life work — books, coffee cups, the geometry of objects on a table at the hour the light is best. Cross with Junichiro Tanizaki's *In Praise of Shadows* as a written reference for the visual register of warm soft daylight on tactile surfaces.

   **Execution notes** — 50mm. Book open or face-down on the surface; mug positioned in the lower third with steam if hot. Daylight from window-left providing soft side-light. Shoot from a 45-degree angle (not flat-lay) so the objects have dimension. f/2.8-4 for the soft falloff behind the book spine. The book should be visibly read — dog-eared, opened — not pristine. AVOID: flat-lay overhead composition (reads as Instagram lifestyle blog), perfectly aligned objects, latte-art focus shot, any branding visible on the mug. The book is the brand's interior life made object.
11. **Andrea-from-back at a window, holding headphones** — pensive but not posed. Daylight on her hair. Carries "the room I'm building" content.

   **Story** — evokes the founder facing the work — looking out at the city she came to and the room she's making. The contemplative back-shot that earns its place by NOT being a contemplative back-shot. She's holding the headphones because she just took them off, not because they're a prop.

   **Reference** — Annie Leibovitz's portrait studies where the subject's back carries the frame — particularly her work on writers and musicians where the room behind the subject is the second protagonist. Cross with Edward Hopper's painted compositions of single figures at windows for the staging logic (subject in negative space, light source as compositional anchor).

   **Execution notes** — 35mm or 50mm. Andrea stands at the window, three-quarters back to camera, headphones loose in one hand at her side. Daylight rims her hair and shoulder. Frame her in the right third with the window taking the upper-left. Shoot at f/4 with focus on her hand and headphones, body softening slightly. The pose has to come from her actually doing this — not from direction. AVOID: hand-on-window, head-tilted-thoughtfully, anything that reads as inspirational quote graphic background. The shot must feel like she forgot the camera was there.

### Host / MC posture shots (4-5) — NEW

*Critical for Event #1 content. These show Andrea in her actual Event #1 role: hosting, welcoming, room-managing. Not behind the decks. Mic in hand or in receptive-listening posture. Daylight always. Warm but composed (curator-who-runs-the-door, not host-who-welcomes-everyone — per voice doc).*

D1. **Andrea with mic in hand, daylight, mid-sentence** — three-quarter portrait, mouth slightly open mid-word, eyes connected to an off-frame person. Mic held low (talking to a small room, not performing on a stage). Reads as: she's running this room.

   **Story** — evokes the curator-on-the-room posture of someone who has already decided who gets in — speaking to a person she knows is listening, not addressing a crowd. The mic is at her hip because the room is small enough to hear her without it being raised.

   **Reference** — Annie Leibovitz's portraits of conductors and bandleaders (specifically her Quincy Jones and Wynton Marsalis portraits) — the way a microphone or instrument is held low when the subject has authority. Cross with Sandro Miller's Chicago portrait work for the directness of gaze and the documentary daylight register.

   **Execution notes** — 50mm or 85mm. Andrea three-quarter to camera, mic held at hip or chest level. Eyeline goes off-frame to camera-right (suggesting an attentive listener she's speaking to). Window light camera-left provides the soft modeling. f/2.8-4 with mic sharp and background falling off. Catch her mid-word — the slight mouth opening reads as alive, not posed. AVOID: mic held to mouth like a performer, eyes to camera, hand on chest, anything that reads as TED talk staging or wedding officiant.

D2. **Andrea welcoming a guest at the door** — warm posture, eye contact with someone just off-frame (the arriving guest), hand extended or near their shoulder, daylight from outside through the doorway. The "you're walking into the right place" moment. Can be staged with a friend.

   **Story** — evokes the threshold moment — the single second when an attendee crosses from the street into the room and Andrea decides their first impression. The host doctrine made photograph: warm, present, eye contact, no performance.

   **Reference** — Tina Barney's *Theater of Manners* series — particularly her photographs of arrival moments in domestic interiors where the host's posture carries the entire social architecture. Cross with documentary wedding photography from the Junebug Weddings best-of archive for the threshold-and-welcome staging logic.

   **Execution notes** — 35mm. Andrea positioned just inside the door frame; daylight from outside backlighting her shoulder and providing the contre-jour edge. Guest is off-frame to camera-left (we see Andrea's gaze and gesture toward them, not the guest). Frame her in the right two-thirds with the doorway opening carrying the left. f/4-5.6 for environmental sharpness. Hand position should be open and natural — not extended for handshake unless that's the actual moment. AVOID: full handshake captured mid-grip (too transactional), eyes-to-camera grip-and-grin, hostess-greeting-clients energy. The shot has to feel like she does this every day, not like she's posing as a host.

D3. **Andrea managing the room from a corner** — Andrea visible at the edge of the frame, room (or implied room — chairs, table, records visible) in the foreground/center. Reads as: she's present but not central. Curator posture.

   **Story** — evokes the curator-not-the-performer doctrine made spatial — Andrea is in the room, but the room is the protagonist. She's reading who's there and what the floor needs next. The shot proves she's working without working for the camera.

   **Reference** — Diane Arbus's environmental portraits where the subject is off-center and the room carries the frame. Cross with Henri Cartier-Bresson's documentary work for the geometric staging where the human is decentered but compositionally inevitable.

   **Execution notes** — 35mm. Camera centered on the room (chairs, records, decks, whatever furniture). Andrea visible in the right or left third of the frame, leaning against a wall or doorframe, eyes scanning the room (not the camera). Window light is the only source; let the room's natural shadow geometry build the depth. f/5.6-8 for environmental sharpness. AVOID: Andrea in the dead center of frame, anything that reads as "founder portrait in workspace," staged casualness. The shot has to read as photojournalism, not portraiture.

D4. **Andrea in attentive-listening posture** — head tilted slightly, eyes on someone off-frame, half-smile. Hand on cheek or holding a glass of water (not alcohol). The "she actually listens" moment.

   **Story** — evokes the curator's deepest competence: paying attention to one person at a time in a room where everyone wants attention. The shot proves the brand's claim that someone is actually noticing — Andrea's listening is the proof.

   **Reference** — Roy DeCarava's portrait work in Harlem — particularly his photographs of conversations where the listener carries the emotional weight. Cross with Sally Mann's Southern portrait series for the way she captures the half-second BEFORE a response, where the listener is still receiving.

   **Execution notes** — 85mm. Andrea three-quarter to camera, eyes off-frame to camera-right (the speaker). Half-smile, head tilted slightly. Water glass held loosely in one hand at waist level. Window light camera-left for the soft modeling. f/2.8 for the shallow depth that isolates her listening face. AVOID: posed contemplative gaze, hand-on-chin "thinker" pose, anything that reads as therapy session or coaching brochure. The listening has to look like she's mid-receiving, not mid-modeling-listening.

D5. **Andrea between-songs energy** — Andrea checking the room (not the decks), maybe walking, scanning who's there. Mid-stride if outdoor location. Reads as: she's working, not performing.

   **Story** — evokes the curator's pacing — the way the host moves through a room she's responsible for. Captures the rhythm Andrea has on the day: 60 seconds at the door, 60 seconds at the bar, 60 seconds reading the floor. The shot of a person tracking the room.

   **Reference** — Vivian Maier's Chicago street photography for the mid-stride compositional logic and the way a single moving figure carries an entire frame. Cross with Garry Winogrand's social documentary work for the way the photographer captures someone mid-thought in transit.

   **Execution notes** — 35mm. Andrea walks at normal pace through the room or across an outdoor space; photographer captures the natural gait. Frame her in the left or right third with the room/environment in the rest. Window light or open-sky light (no direct overhead sun). f/4-5.6 with 1/250 or faster shutter to keep her sharp. Multiple frames — pick the one where her eyeline is mid-scan, not focused on camera. AVOID: power-walk pose, fashion-editorial stride, anything that reads as runway. The stride is the working stride of someone with a job, not a model's stride.

**Wardrobe for Category D**: warmer / slightly more put-together than the DJ shots. Could be the same as the founder portrait wardrobe (Category A). She's the host now — read as the woman who owns this room, not the woman behind the decks.
