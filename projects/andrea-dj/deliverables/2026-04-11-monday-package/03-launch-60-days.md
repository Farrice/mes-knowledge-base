### Revenue scenarios (using the tiered model from the research doc)

| Attendees | Mix | Revenue | Net (vs $400 cost) |
|-----------|-----|---------|---------------------|
| 30 (low) | 10 @ $25 + 15 @ $35 + 5 @ $45 | $1,000 | **+$600** |
| 40 (target) | 15 @ $25 + 20 @ $35 + 5 @ $45 | $1,300 | **+$900** |
| 50 (full) | 20 @ $25 + 25 @ $35 + 5 @ $45 | $1,500 | **+$1,100** |

**Reality check:** At 40 attendees and bootstrap costs, Andrea nets roughly **$900.** That's not a side-hustle number — that's real money for a first event. More importantly, it proves the unit economics work, which makes Event #2 venue conversations radically easier ("our last event sold out, here's the revenue, let's talk").

### The break-even line

**12 tickets at $35 average covers all costs.** That's the floor. If Andrea can sell 12 tickets (which is half of her own 20-person invite list), Event #1 does not lose money. That's the psychological baseline for her to hold through the nervous middle weeks.
