07 — Claude Pro Setup Guide for Andrea

How to turn your $20/month Claude account into a creative partner that already knows Pulse

Why This File Exists

You have a lot of context in this package. Seven files, 1,400+ lines of strategy, voice guides, timelines, and content. Nobody is going to hold all of that in their head — not you, not me, not anyone.

Claude is going to hold it for you.

The idea here is simple: instead of trying to remember everything in the package every time you want to work on Pulse, you upload the whole package to a Claude Project once. Then every conversation you have in that project starts with Claude already knowing who you are, what Pulse is, what your voice sounds like, and what you're trying to build.

You walk up to Claude, say "help me write the DM for inviting Sarah to Pulse," and it does it — in your voice, with your context, without you having to re-explain anything.

This file walks you through doing that in about 20 minutes. And it gives you a library of prompts you can copy-paste whenever you need them.

The Five Things You Need to Understand About Claude Pro

Before you set anything up, know this. It'll save you hours and keep you from burning your $20/month quota.

1. You are on the $20/month tier.

That means you have limits. Not huge ones — you'll have more than enough for Pulse work — but you can hit them if you chat for hours with long back-and-forths. The limits reset every ~5 hours. So if you ever see a "you've reached your limit" message, just wait. You didn't break anything.

Rough rule of thumb: You can send ~40-80 messages in a 5-hour window before you'd hit limits. The longer each message (or Claude's reply) is, the fewer you get. For Pulse work, you'll rarely come close.

2. Projects = persistent context.

A Claude Project is a folder where you upload files once, and Claude reads them automatically in every chat you have inside that project. You'll have one Project called PULSE. It will contain this whole package. Every time you open a chat in that project, Claude already knows everything we've built.

This is the single most important thing about Claude Pro for you. Don't skip it.

3. Don't re-upload the same files to every new chat.

Before Projects existed, people used to paste their context into every conversation. Don't. That's slow, wastes your quota, and leaves room for mistakes. Once files are in your Project, they're there forever.

4. Short, specific prompts beat long, vague ones.

Claude is much better at "Draft a DM to invite my friend Lauren to Pulse. She's 31, a yoga teacher, single, exhausted by dating apps. Keep it warm and personal, 4 sentences max" than at "can you help me with my social strategy?"

The pre-built prompts below are all specific for this reason. Copy them, fill in the brackets, send.

5. Start a new chat when the topic changes.

If you've been writing a Substack essay for 30 minutes and you want to switch to writing a venue email, start a new chat inside the same project. Don't try to do both in one conversation. It costs more tokens, Claude gets confused, and you lose quality. Projects make this painless — new chats still have all your context.

Step 4: Add the Project Instructions

This is the most important step. Project instructions tell Claude how to behave whenever you chat inside this project. You paste them once, they apply forever.

Look for "Project instructions" or "Custom instructions" (the exact label varies — it might be under a settings icon inside the project).

Paste this, verbatim:

I am Andrea, founder of PULSE — a daytime, sober, curated dance party in Chicago for people who are done with dating apps and want to meet the old way, through music and movement. I am a DJ. I am launching my first event in June 2026. I am working with a creative strategist named Farrice who has given me the full Pulse package that you can read in this project.

Your role:
- Help me execute against the plan in the Pulse package.
- Write in my voice — warm, direct, confident, a little poetic when the moment calls for it, never preachy, never corporate. Use "I" when I'm speaking as myself and "you" when addressing my audience.
- Never start sentences with "Here's what..." or "Here's why..." — that's an AI-writing tell I want to avoid.
- Use em dashes sparingly — maximum 1-2 per piece of copy.
- Show, don't tell. Replace abstract descriptions ("an intimate atmosphere") with concrete images ("a song that holds you there long enough to walk across the floor").
- Never describe Pulse as primarily a "house music" event. Pulse is shaped by my curation across genres. House is in the mix, not the cornerstone.
- When I ask for content or copy, draft it in my voice first, then tell me what you'd change on a second pass.
- When I ask for advice, push back if you think I'm making a mistake. Don't just validate me.
- When I'm stuck, help me find what I actually want to say, not what I think I should say.

Pulse's enemy (what we stand against):
- Hookup culture disguised as singles events
- Swipe culture
- Bars as the only "permission slip" for meeting someone
- Speed dating pressure
- Performance over presence

Pulse's values:
- Clean meetings (daytime, sober)
- Curated rooms (who's in matters)
- Movement before words
- Stories over metrics (we care more about couples who form than follower counts)
- Chicago

Treat every question I ask inside this project as a question about Pulse unless I tell you otherwise. If I need space to process something personal, I'll say so — and then your job is to listen, not solve.

Save. That's it. From this point forward, every chat you open in the PULSE project starts with Claude already knowing all of this.

The Prompt Library

These are copy-paste prompts built specifically for you. Use them whenever the situation fits. Always use them inside your PULSE project so Claude has context.

Each prompt is structured: what it's for, when to use it, and the actual prompt (in a code block) you copy-paste. You fill in the [brackets] with your details.

PART 1 — Deep Work on Yourself

These prompts are for moments when you need to think clearly — not to produce content for Pulse, but to work through something that's in the way of building it. Andrea, you told Farrice you lead with excitement and the excitement fades. These prompts are for the days when the excitement is quiet.

Prompt 1.1 — The Morning Reset

For: Days when you wake up and Pulse feels like a huge, overwhelming thing you don't know how to do. Use this before you start any Pulse work.

I'm feeling [overwhelmed / scared / uninspired / disconnected from Pulse] today. I don't want you to fix it. I want you to help me figure out what I'm actually feeling and what one small thing I could do today that would move Pulse forward without requiring me to feel excited.

Ask me three questions, one at a time, to help me get clearer. Don't give me advice until I've answered all three.

Why it works: Claude is going to ask you real questions instead of giving you a pep talk. You'll answer them one at a time. By the third question, you usually know what to do. The small action at the end is the key — it breaks the paralysis.

Prompt 1.2 — The Voice Calibration Check

For: When you write something (a post, a DM, a caption) and you're not sure if it sounds like you. Paste your draft after the prompt.

Here's something I'm about to post/send. Read it and tell me three things:
1. Does it sound like me, based on what you know from the Pulse project?
2. Is there any sentence that sounds like AI (starts with "Here's what...", uses an em-dash as a crutch, or feels generic)?
3. If you were me, what would you change before sending?

Do NOT rewrite the whole thing. Just point out what's off.

[PASTE YOUR DRAFT HERE]

Why it works: The rule is Claude doesn't rewrite — it points. You still make the call. This keeps your voice yours.

Prompt 1.3 — The Fear Excavation

For: When something about Pulse is making you anxious and you don't know why. Or when you're procrastinating on a specific action and you want to know what's really going on.

I'm avoiding [specific thing: "sending the venue outreach emails" / "inviting my 10 people" / "posting on Instagram"]. I'm going to tell you everything I'm feeling about it in one unfiltered paragraph. Your job is NOT to fix it. Your job is to reflect back what you hear, ask me ONE follow-up question, and help me name the actual fear underneath the surface resistance.

[YOUR UNFILTERED PARAGRAPH]

Why it works: Most resistance is a symptom. The fear underneath is usually something like "I'm afraid this will fail and people will think I'm a fraud" or "I'm afraid nobody will come and I'll have to face that I was wrong about myself." Naming it out loud removes 70% of its power. Claude is a safe place to do that because it doesn't judge.

Prompt 1.4 — The Decision Clarifier

For: When you're stuck between two options and can't tell which is the right move. (Example: "should I lock June 20 or push to July 11?")

I'm trying to decide between:

Option A: [description]
Option B: [description]

Don't tell me which one to pick. Ask me questions that help me figure it out myself. Focus on:
- What am I actually afraid of in each option?
- What would the "future Andrea who already launched Pulse successfully" say about this?
- Which option feels more aligned with what we've built in the Pulse project?

Ask me one question at a time. Wait for my answer before the next.

Why it works: Decisions get made faster when you're asked the right questions than when someone else picks for you. Claude is good at this.

Prompt 1.5 — The End-of-Week Check-In

For: Every Friday. Use this at the end of the week to process what happened and reset for next week.

It's end of Week [X] of the Pulse launch. Here's what happened this week:

- [bullet point about what you did]
- [bullet point about what you felt]
- [bullet point about what worked]
- [bullet point about what didn't]

Help me process this. Don't just summarize what I said. Ask me what I'm most proud of, what I'm most scared of for next week, and whether I'm being honest with myself about anything. Push back if something I said sounds like avoidance.

Why it works: Weekly reflection keeps the 90% pattern from running the show. You notice drift before it becomes distance.

PART 2 — Pulse Execution Prompts

These are for actually producing things. Content, emails, invitations, essays. Copy, paste, fill in the brackets, ship.

Prompt 2.1 — The Venue Email Writer

For: Writing a custom venue outreach email when the template from file 03 isn't quite right for a specific venue.

I need a venue outreach email to [Venue Name]. 

About the venue:
- [Neighborhood]
- [What I know about it — vibe, capacity, what events they usually host]
- [What specifically makes it good for Pulse]

Use the template from file 03 of the Pulse package as the starting point. Customize the "why your venue is perfect" paragraph to be specific to this venue. Keep the email under 250 words. Don't be cute — be direct and warm. Sound like me.

Bonus: tell me one thing I could research about this venue BEFORE sending the email that would make it 20% stronger.

Prompt 2.2 — The Warm Invite DM Writer

For: Writing a personal DM or text to invite a specific person to Event #1. Use this for each person on your 10-person invite list.

I want to invite [Name] to Pulse Event #1. Here's what I know about them:

- How I know them: [best friend / mutual friend of X / yoga class / etc.]
- What they're up to: [job, passions, relationship situation if relevant]
- Why I think they'd get Pulse: [the specific reason]
- What might make them hesitate: [if anything]

Write me a warm, personal text message (not a mass DM) inviting them. Keep it 3-4 sentences max. It should sound like something I'd actually send a friend, not marketing copy. The invitation should feel like I thought about them specifically, because I did.

After the draft, tell me one thing I could add or change to make it more specific to this person.

Prompt 2.3 — The Instagram Post Drafter

For: When you need a new IG post (Week 4 onward, after the 3 posts in file 04 are shipped).

I need an Instagram post for the Pulse account. Context:

- Week of the launch timeline: [Week X]
- Goal of the post: [e.g., "tease what to expect at Event #1" / "share that tickets are opening" / "drop a Pulse philosophy reflection"]
- Format: [carousel / single photo + caption / reel idea]
- Any specific idea I want to explore: [if applicable]

Draft the caption in my voice. Follow the voice rules in the PULSE project instructions. Give me the hashtag set at the end. Then suggest the visual direction for the post.

If the post idea needs a hook line, give me 3 options for the first line so I can pick the one that feels strongest.

Prompt 2.4 — The Substack Essay Drafter

For: Writing a Substack essay. Best used if we've merged Substack with Pulse (see file 05).

I'm writing Substack Essay #[X]: [Essay Title]

What I want to say (in messy, unfiltered form, the way I'd say it to a friend):
[Record a voice memo and transcribe it, or type out what you want to say. Don't worry about structure.]

Your job:
1. Pull the real ideas out of what I said.
2. Structure them into a Substack essay of 800-1,200 words.
3. Write it in my voice (warm, direct, a little poetic, never preachy).
4. Use short paragraphs — Substack readers scan before they read.
5. Open with a line that makes someone stop scrolling.
6. End with a line that earns the click-through to follow Pulse.

Give me the full draft. Then tell me what YOU would cut in a second pass.

Prompt 2.5 — The Decline Script Writer

For: Someone has reached out to Pulse and you need to politely decline them (see file 02 — the Hunter, the Performer, the Recently-Heartbroken, the Tourist).

Someone messaged me about Pulse. I need to decline them politely. Here's the situation:

- Who they are: [brief description]
- What they said: [paste their DM]
- Which "Anti-Pulse" category they fall into, if I can tell: [Hunter / Performer / Recently-Heartbroken / Tourist / unsure]

Use the decline scripts from file 02 of the Pulse project as a starting point. Customize the tone to this specific person — firm but not cold for a Hunter, warm but deferred for a Performer, etc. Keep it short (3 sentences max). 

If I'm unsure which category they are, ask me one clarifying question before writing the script.

Prompt 2.6 — The Feedback Responder

For: When a venue, DJ, or prospective attendee pushes back on something and you don't know how to reply.

Someone just said this to me:
"[paste their message]"

Context:
- Who they are: [venue / DJ / potential attendee / etc.]
- What I was asking for originally: [brief description]
- What I'm feeling about their response: [honest — don't edit]

Help me draft a response that's firm about what Pulse needs but not defensive. If they have a legitimate point, acknowledge it. If they're trying to lowball or dismiss Pulse, hold the line without being rude.

Give me two options:
1. A firm-but-warm version
2. A more flexible version that finds middle ground

Tell me which one you'd send if you were me, and why.

Prompt 2.7 — The Exit Interview Questions Tune-Up

For: The week before Event #1, when you want to practice the exit interview questions (from file 04) and make them feel natural in your mouth.

I have 3 exit interview questions I'll ask attendees after Pulse Event #1:

1. "What was the moment that surprised you today?"
2. "Did you meet anyone you want to see again? You don't have to name them."
3. "If a friend asked you tomorrow what Pulse is, what would you tell them?"

Role-play this with me. Pretend you're an attendee leaving the event. I'll ask you the questions, you respond as someone who just had a moving experience. Then we'll debrief: what worked, what felt awkward, and what I should adjust.

Go first — start by telling me what kind of attendee you're going to role-play (nervous first-timer, confident regular, someone who cried during the slow dance, etc.).

Part 3 — When You're Stuck or Tired

Two last prompts for the bad days.

Prompt 3.1 — The Tired-But-Can't-Stop Reset

For: Days where you've been working on Pulse for hours and you're drained but something still needs to ship.

I've been working on Pulse for [X hours]. I'm tired. I still have to [the thing you need to do].

Tell me honestly: is this thing I need to do something I should push through right now, or is it something that will be better if I rest and come back to it tomorrow? 

If it's push-through — give me the shortest, lowest-effort version of doing it well.
If it's rest-and-return — help me write a 2-sentence text to [Farrice / whoever] so nobody's waiting on me, and tell me exactly what I'm going to do tomorrow morning when I wake up.

Prompt 3.2 — The "I Want to Quit" Prompt

For: The day when you wake up and genuinely consider giving up on Pulse. (This day will come. Every founder has it. It's not a sign you should quit. It's a sign the excitement has faded and the conditioning phase has started.)

I'm having a day where I want to quit Pulse. Don't talk me out of it. Help me figure out what's actually happening.

Ask me:
- What happened in the last 48 hours that might be under this?
- Is this about Pulse itself, or about something else in my life that's bleeding in?
- If I imagine NOT doing Pulse — really imagine it, a year from now, no Pulse, no event, no couples — what do I feel?

After I answer, reflect back what you heard. Don't tell me to keep going. Let me hear myself and decide.

Why this one matters: The days you want to quit are the days your body is telling you something real. Sometimes it's "this isn't the thing." Almost always, it's "I'm scared, I'm exhausted, and I need a day off, not a different life." Naming the difference changes everything.
