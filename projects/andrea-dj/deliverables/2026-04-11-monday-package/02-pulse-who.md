# 02 — Who Resonance Is For
## The ICP, the three archetypes, the filter, and why curation is the whole game

---

## Why This File Matters More Than You Think

Curation is not a vibe check. It's the entire product.

If the wrong ten people walk into Event #1, Resonance is dead. Not "struggling" — *dead*. One bad actor (a guy who treats it like Tinder IRL, a woman who came to show off, a group that came drunk) poisons the room, the stories go sideways, and the next 50 people who could have shown up to Event #2 don't.

If the *right* ten people walk in, Resonance is unstoppable. The stories they tell their friends on Monday are the marketing. The couples that form become your social proof. The energy in the room feeds itself.

This file tells you who the right ten people are. And who they're not.

---

## The Self-Selection Test (Before Everything Else)

The single filter that cuts through noise:

> **If you need to be convinced to dance, this isn't for you. The dance floor IS the event.**

Andrea had two real-world data points in the week before the Monday session:
- One friend, told about Resonance, said: *"I don't like to dance."* → not a Resonance person. Self-selected out. Saved us the trouble.
- Another friend, told about Resonance, said: *"When's the date? I want to be there."* → Resonance person. Self-selected in. Zero convincing required.

**Self-selection is the marketing.** Anyone who needs a long pitch is a leak. Anyone who hears "singles event where dancing is how you meet" and says *yes, that's it* is already on the list.

## The Three Archetypes (The People We're Building This For)

From the Resonance Brief. These three came alive for us when we imagined the room. Every piece of copy should feel like it was written *to* at least one of them.

### Maya — 34

Sober for two years. Her social life quietly shrank when she stopped drinking. She misses dancing but every venue that comes to mind is a bar. She's not lonely — she has friends — but she wants to meet someone and doesn't know how to do that without the social lubricant she gave up. She shows up to Resonance a little nervous. She leaves having talked to three strangers and danced for two hours straight.

**What the copy has to do for Maya:** Prove the room doesn't need alcohol to come alive. Show her that vulnerability is the natural state here, not a feat of bravery.

### Simone — 31

She goes to church. She has a full life. But there's a gap between her values and most of the spaces where single people meet, and she's never found anything that fit in that gap. Resonance isn't a church event and it isn't a club. It's something she didn't know existed and immediately forwards to four friends.

**What the copy has to do for Simone:** Signal a posture — intentional, grown-up, not adolescent — without going religious or earnest-to-the-point-of-boring. She reads tone carefully. One false note and she's out.

### Darius — 29

Spiritual, works out, meditates. He's been on the apps and hates how transactional they feel. He wants to meet someone the way his parents did — in a room, through a real moment. He doesn't drink much but he also doesn't dance much, because he's never felt like there was a space where it was okay to be a Black man being soft and open in public. Resonance feels like that space.

**What the copy has to do for Darius:** Make it safe to be soft. The room has to read as a place where emotional openness is protected, not where it'll be mocked or made into a spectacle. This is the hardest archetype to reach — the men we want in this room exist, we just haven't found them yet. Darius is the test of whether we have.

### The thread that unites them

> *People who are done with the transactional version of finding love. People who want to come back to themselves through the body. People who feel, in Maya's case the sobriety gap, in Simone's case the values gap, in Darius's case the safety gap, that no existing room holds them.*

Resonance is that room.

## The Three-Dimensional Gut Check (Before You Invite Someone)

**Who they are in their day:**
- They go to the farmer's market, not because it's performative — because they actually cook.
- They have a yoga or lifting or running routine, even if it's inconsistent.
- They've been to therapy. Or they're in therapy. Or they know they should be.
- They've noticed alcohol isn't doing what it used to. Whether they've quit, cut back, or are just aware.
- They read books. Not just self-help — books.

**Who they are on dating apps:**
- They're *exhausted.* They've deleted Hinge at least twice.
- They're not cynical yet, but they're getting close.
- They've been on enough "coffees" that they can feel themselves going through the motions.
- They've started saying things like *"I just want to meet someone normally"* or *"I don't even know where to go anymore."*
- They tell their friends the real problem: **"I feel like I'm dating a screen, not a person."**

**Who they are in a room:**
- They're the person who dances when nobody's looking.
- They can feel a song change them. They have a song that wrecks them.
- They're not the loudest in the group. They're the one the loudest person is trying to impress.
- They can hold eye contact for longer than two seconds without making a joke.
- They want connection more than they want to be cool. They're embarrassed by that, but they mean it.

## The One-Sentence Test

> **A Resonance person is someone who, if you told them *"come dance for three hours with forty strangers, and the point isn't to drink, it's to meet someone from your body first,"* would say *"that sounds weird, but I'm in."***

If they'd say *"that sounds weird, so no"* — they're not your person yet.
If they'd say *"sounds great"* without hesitation — they might be too in.
The hesitation matters. The willingness *despite* the hesitation is the signal.

## The Anti-Resonance Person (The One We Filter Out)

This is harder to write but more important. Every person in this category is a threat to the room. Not because they're bad people — because their reason for being there contaminates the reason everyone else is there.

### The categories you decline:

**1. The Hunter.**
The guy (usually, not always) who shows up because he heard "singles event" and translated it in his head to "hunting ground." You can smell them in a DM within three messages. They ask about the ratio. They ask how many women are coming. They ask if they can bring their buddies. **Decline immediately.** Don't explain.

**2. The Performer.**
The person who treats every room as a stage. Came to be seen, not to see. Already has 40K followers, wants to come "to check it out" and post about it. They might look like a positive signal on paper, but if their energy walks in, everyone else dims theirs. **Decline politely, offer to host them at a future event once there's more social proof.**

**3. The Drunk Group.**
Any group that's already pregamed before walking in. Resonance isn't sober-as-a-brand, but Event #1 has no alcohol — the room is curated around presence, and a pregamed group is already using a crutch. One drunk group wrecks the floor. **Filter at the door. Full refund. No drama.**

**4. The Tourist.**
The person who just wants to see what a curated no-alcohol singles dance room looks like. Curious, not committed. They'll come once, not dance, leave early, and the story they tell is lukewarm. **Not a deal-breaker, but deprioritize.** Don't hand them a ticket before committed attendees.

**5. The Recently-Heartbroken-Hunter.**
Different from the Hunter. This is the person who's in a raw place and is using Resonance as a rebound mechanism. They're not predatory — they're unstable. They'll have a high of the night, latch onto someone, and when it doesn't work out, it gets messy. **Have a soft conversation. Ask what's drawing them. If the answer is *"I just broke up and I need something new,"* gently redirect them to come in a month or two when the dust settles.**

**6. The Dancer-Averse.** *(New)*
The person who's otherwise a great fit but has told you *"I don't really like to dance"* or *"I'd come but I'd probably just watch."* They're not bad — they're just not for this room. The dance floor is the protocol; they'd be the wall-hugger who sets the room's energy template in the wrong direction. **Decline kindly. Tell them you'll let them know when you do a non-dance-centered Resonance Series event in Phase 2.**

### The filter rule

> **If you can't imagine this person still being at Resonance six months from now as a regular, decline.**

One-time attendees are fine. Contaminants are not. The difference is the *spirit they walk in with,* not how many times they show up.

## How You Actually Filter (The Mechanics)

You don't have a tech platform yet, so the filter is manual. That's fine — it's actually better. Every "yes" feels intentional.

### For Event #1 at 50 attendees — the Curation Ratio

**Phase 1 (first 25 spots): Hand-picked from your combined network.**
Andrea, you invite people *by name.* Friends, friends-of-friends, people you've already clocked as *"oh, they'd love this."* No public ticket link yet. These are the seed crystals. Farrice contributes from his orbit too, flagged as *"Farrice's invite"* so you know.

**Phase 2 (next 15 spots): Warm referrals from the 25.**
Each seed can nominate *one* friend. The nomination comes through you — they text you *"hey, can I bring X?"* and you make the call. You say yes if X passes the gut check, no if they don't.

**Phase 3 (final 10 spots): Public, but gated.**
Open the ticket link on IG with a soft gate: *"Spots are limited. Tell us in one sentence why you want to be in the room."* Read every submission. Decline anything that feels off. Approve the rest.

**Total: 50 people for Event #1. Hard cap: 55 (buffer for over-RSVP).**

### If Confirmed Yeses Stay Under 35 by T-10 Days

Don't pad to 50 with weaker invites. **Tighten to 40 instead.** Better an intimate 40 than a padded 50 with filler. Quality over fill is non-negotiable — the floor dynamics at 40 with all high-intent attendees beat 50 with 10 lukewarm ones.

### For Event #2 Onward — The Resonance Couples Recommendation Flywheel

Once Event #1 has happened and you have stories, the model flips:

- **70% of Event #2 seats go to Event #1 attendees and *their* one-person invite.**
- **20% go to new warm referrals from your network.**
- **10% go to public applications with the same "why" gate.**

Every seat has a social proof chain behind it. Nobody walks in cold. The room pre-selects for shared values because the existing attendees are filtering.

## Scripts for Declining (Save These)

Declining gracefully is a skill. Four scripts in your voice.

### Script 1 — The Hunter (quick, firm, no explanation)

> *"Hey, thanks for the interest. Resonance isn't the right fit for what you're looking for. Best of luck out there."*

Do not elaborate. Do not apologize. Do not engage if they push back.

### Script 2 — The Performer (warm, deferred)

> *"Really appreciate you reaching out! Event #1 is fully curated and we're keeping it tight for our core community this first time. I'd love to have you at Event #2 — can I put you on the list and loop you in when it's closer?"*

Almost always, they never follow up. Problem solved.

### Script 3 — The Recently-Heartbroken (soft redirect)

> *"Hey, thank you for sharing that. I'll be honest — I want to have you in the room, but I also want it to be the right moment for you. Give it a few weeks, take care of yourself, and message me again when things feel more settled. I'll save a spot."*

People who respect this come back healthier. People who don't were never Resonance people anyway.

### Script 4 — The Tourist / The Dancer-Averse (no-pressure defer)

> *"Hey! The first event is small and we're keeping it to people who are really ready to be on the floor. Want me to add you to our list and let you know about Event #2? By then we'll have some stories to share and you'll know if it's your thing."*
