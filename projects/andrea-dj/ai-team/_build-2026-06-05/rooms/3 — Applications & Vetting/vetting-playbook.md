## Section 1: The Vetting Framework

### 1.1 How People Enter the Funnel

Resonance has four intake channels. Each signals different motivation and requires different handling.

| Channel | What It Signals | Vetting Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Referral from trusted source** | Pre-vouched, lower risk | Fast-track to call |
| **Direct outreach by Andrea** | Hand-selected, high intent | Priority vetting |
| **Interest form (organic)** | Self-selected, unknown quality | Standard queue |
| **Waitlist from prior events** | Already screened, proven interest | Fill gaps first |

**The interest form** lives on the Resonance site. It captures: name, age, what drew them, one sentence about what they're looking for, phone number. That's it. No essays. No resumes. The form is a triage tool, not a screening tool. Andrea reads every submission within 48 hours.

**Referral protocol:** When someone says "my friend should come," Andrea thanks them, asks for the friend's number, and says she'll reach out directly. She does NOT let the referrer do the vetting for her. A trusted person's referral gets a faster call slot. It does not get a yes.

### 1.2 The Vetting Touchpoint

**Format:** 15-minute phone call. Video is optional if the person prefers it. Coffee is too long for first-pass; save in-person for borderline cases where Andrea needs more data.

**Why phone:** Tone of voice reveals more than text. Pauses reveal more than words. A 15-minute call is long enough to be diagnostic, short enough that nobody resents it. It also signals that Resonance is serious — the call IS the first filter.

**Scheduling:** Andrea sends a Calendly link with 15-minute slots. She offers 2-3 days of options, never more than a week out. Urgency maintains momentum.

**Pre-call prep:** Andrea reads the person's form (or referral note) 2 minutes before the call. No pre-written judgments. The call is the data.

### 1.3 Who Does the Vetting

Andrea. Always. No delegation.

This is non-negotiable for Event #1-3. After three events with clear pattern recognition, Andrea may train one deputy using this playbook. Until then, every yes passes through her judgment. The room is her product. She owns the curation.

### 1.4 The Decision Rubric

Three piles. No numeric scores. No spreadsheets during the call. Gut-informed, pattern-tested.

| Pile | Action | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| **Hell Yes** | Offer spot immediately or within 24 hours | Send commitment sequence (see Section 6) |
| **No** | Reject within 48 hours using appropriate script (see Section 5) | Log reason, tag for future exclusion if warranted |
| **Maybe** | 24-hour hold. Andrea sleeps on it. May do 10-min follow-up call. | If still unsure after 48 hours → No. Uncertainty is a No. |

**The rule:** A maybe is a no unless something specific shifts. Andrea is not running a conversion funnel. She is curating a room. Indecision is data.

### 1.5 Funnel Math

**Target:** 50 attendees (approx. 25 men, 25 women)

**The numbers:**
- 120-150 people enter funnel (interest form + referrals + outreach)
- 80-100 get vetting calls
- 40-50 get "hell yes" offers
- 35-45 confirm with deposit
- 30-40 show up

**Why the drop-off is good:** A 60-70% show rate from confirmed attendees is healthy. It means the vetting is working — the wrong people self-select out, and the right people prioritize it.

**Buffer strategy:** Andrea should aim to have 55-60 "hell yes" confirmations by one week before the event. The 48-hour audit (see Section 7) is where she finalizes the 50.

**Gender balance rule:** Never let either gender exceed 55% of confirmed attendees. If men are oversubscribed, hold a waitlist. If women are oversubscribed, same. The 50/50 (or close to it) ratio is not cosmetic — it is structural to the experience.

## Section 2: The 15-Minute Vetting Call — Men

### 2.1 The Opening (2 minutes)

Andrea's goal in the first 120 seconds: establish warmth, signal seriousness, and set the frame that this is a conversation, not an interview.

**Exact opener:**

> "Hey [name], it's Andrea. Thanks for taking the time — I know 15 minutes is short, so I'll jump right in. I'm hosting this thing called Resonance on July 18th. It's a daytime dance party for single people who are serious about finding a partner. No bar, no phones, no forced mingling. I talk to everyone who comes because the room matters more than the venue. So I'm curious — what made you interested?"

**Tone:** Warm, direct, slightly conspiratorial. Not corporate. Not therapist. Like someone who knows exactly what she's building and wants to know if you're the right piece.

**What Andrea is listening for in the response:**
- Did he do any homework, or is he winging it?
- Does he sound nervous (fine), indifferent (bad), or over-rehearsed (suspect)?
- Does he answer the question, or does he immediately try to impress?

### 2.2 The Questions — Category A: Relationship Intent and Self-Awareness

**Question A1:** "When you think about the relationship you want, what's the thing that matters most that you haven't been finding?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "Someone I can be quiet with. The women I meet want constant entertainment." or "I want a partner, not a project. I'm tired of fixing people or being fixed." | He knows what he wants and what hasn't worked. He's dating from clarity, not desperation. |
| **Red flag** | "Honestly? Just someone hot who isn't crazy." or "I just want someone who appreciates me. Women these days don't appreciate good men." | Blames others, objectifies, no self-awareness. Pattern of externalizing. |
| **Maybe** | "I don't know, I haven't really thought about it that specifically." or "Someone kind, I guess? Smart?" | Generic, underprocessed. Probe: "Say more about your last relationship — what was missing?" |

**Question A2:** "How do you typically meet people now, and how's that going?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "Apps mostly, and they're fine for volume but terrible for depth. I want to meet someone in a context where we're both showing up fully." or "Through friends, but my circle is small and coupled." | Realistic, frustrated with current tools for specific reasons, not just bitter. |
| **Red flag** | "Apps are garbage. Women only want [height/money/status]." or "I don't meet anyone. Women don't go for guys like me." | Bitter, victim narrative, no agency. These men poison a room. |
| **Maybe** | "I'm not really dating right now, I just saw this and thought it sounded different." | Could be open-minded or could be non-committal. Probe: "What would make you actually show up?" |

**Question A3:** "What's something about yourself that you're actively working on?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "I tend to withdraw when I'm stressed instead of talking about it. I'm working on that." or "I used to think being a provider was enough. I'm learning to be present." | Specific, honest, shows growth orientation. This is the vulnerability question in disguise. |
| **Red flag** | "Nothing really, I've got my shit together." or "I work too hard, that's my flaw." (bragging disguised as flaw) | No self-awareness or performative humility. Either way, no depth. |
| **Maybe** | "I should probably be more outgoing." or "I don't know, maybe my communication?" | Vague, possibly true but underprocessed. Probe: "Give me a specific example." |

### 2.3 The Questions — Category B: Social Behavior and Floor Fit

**Question B1:** "Tell me about the last time you were in a room where you didn't know anyone. How did you handle it?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "I found one person to talk to, then another. I don't try to work the room." or "I was nervous, so I went to the bathroom, collected myself, came back." | Shows self-knowledge and adaptive strategy. The bathroom answer is gold — it shows he manages himself. |
| **Red flag** | "I dominate every room I'm in." or "I usually find the host and make them introduce me." or "I just stand there on my phone until someone talks to me." | Either performative, entitled, or helpless. None work on the Resonance floor. |
| **Maybe** | "I don't really go to things where I don't know people." | Honest, but probe: "How would you handle this event then?" |

**Question B2:** "When you're at a party or event, do you dance?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "Not usually, but I have before." or "Only when I'm drunk, which is why this sober thing is interesting to me." or "I love dancing, I'm terrible at it, I do it anyway." | Honest about his relationship with movement. The "terrible but do it anyway" answer is the ideal profile. |
| **Red flag** | "I don't dance." (flat, final) or "I only dance if I'm good at it." or "I dance to show off." | Rigid, image-obsessed, or performative. The floor will break him. |
| **Maybe** | "I haven't in a while but I'd try." | Fine if it sounds genuine. Concerning if it sounds like he's saying what she wants to hear. |

**Question B3:** "What kind of music makes you feel something?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | Specific answer with personal story. "There's this song by [artist] that I always come back to when [specific memory]." or "I don't care about genre, I care about whether it makes me want to move." | Has a relationship with music that's personal, not performative. |
| **Red flag** | "I don't really listen to music." or "Whatever's on the radio." or genre-snob answer with judgment about other tastes | Either disconnected from his own sensory experience or using taste as status weapon. |
| **Maybe** | "I like a little of everything." | Generic but not disqualifying. Follow-up: "Name the last three songs you listened to." |

### 2.4 The Questions — Category C: Resonance Fit

**Question C1:** "What drew you to this specifically? There are a lot of singles events."

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "The no-phone thing. I want to be present." or "Daytime, sober — that means people are actually choosing this, not just falling into it." or "The dance floor part scares me a little, which is why I need to do it." | Gets it. Understands that the constraints are the point. The "scares me" answer is ideal. |
| **Red flag** | "I heard there would be successful women there." or "My friend said it would be a good place to meet people." (no further thought) or "Sounds easier than apps." | Treating it as a convenience, a market, or a shortcut. Wrong motivation. |
| **Maybe** | "It just seemed different from the usual bar scene." | Not wrong, but surface-level. Probe: "What do you think would actually happen there?" |

**Question C2:** "The format is unusual — no forced introductions, no speed dating, just dancing. Some people find that uncomfortable. What do you think?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "That's exactly what I want. I'm tired of interview-style dating. I want to feel someone before I interrogate them." or "That does make me nervous, but I'm more nervous of another awkward cocktail party." | Understands that physical presence precedes verbal negotiation. The second answer is the uncertain man who moves anyway — ideal. |
| **Red flag** | "How are you supposed to meet anyone without talking?" or "That sounds inefficient." or "So I just dance and hope someone likes me?" | Doesn't trust non-verbal intelligence. Wants to optimize, control, or be chosen without risk. |
| **Maybe** | "I think I could do that." | Lukewarm. The right people say "I want that" or "that scares me but I'm in." |

**Question C3:** "What would make you leave early?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "I can't think of anything. I committed to being there." or "If I felt truly unsafe, but that's it." | Committed, grounded. Understands that leaving early breaks the container for everyone. |
| **Red flag** | "If it was boring." or "If nobody was talking to me." or "If the music was bad." | Conditional attendee. Will poison the room by checking out visibly. |
| **Maybe** | "If I had an emergency, I guess?" | Reasonable. Not a flag either way. |

### 2.5 The Close (1 minute)

Andrea ends every call the same way, regardless of her decision:

> "Thanks for taking the time, [name]. I really appreciate it. I'm making decisions over the next few days and I'll text you either way by [specific day]. If it's a fit, I'll send you everything you need to lock in your spot. If not this time, I hope our paths cross at something else down the line."

**Why this close works:** It sets a deadline (no limbo), promises communication either way (no ghosting — that's brand poison), and leaves a warm exit regardless of the decision.

## Section 3: The 15-Minute Vetting Call — Women

### 3.1 The Opening (2 minutes)

Same opener as men, with one added sentence after "the room matters more than the venue":

> "...the room matters more than the venue. And honestly, women set the tone of the room. So I'm especially thoughtful about who I invite. I'm curious — what made you interested?"

This signals: you matter, the standard is high, and she's not desperate for attendees. Women should feel chosen, not recruited.

### 3.2 The Questions — Category A: Relationship Intent and Self-Awareness

**Question A1:** "When you imagine being in a relationship that actually works, what's different from the dating you've been doing?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "I want to be chosen by someone who sees me, not evaluated by someone who's shopping." or "I'm tired of the three-date cycle where nobody actually opens up. I want depth faster." | Frustrated with superficial dating for specific, articulated reasons. Wants partnership, not just a boyfriend. |
| **Red flag** | "I just want someone who has their shit together. I'm done with man-children." (said with contempt) or "I'm not even sure I want a relationship, I just thought this sounded fun." | Contempt for men is a room-poisoner. "Just fun" is the wrong event — send her to a regular party. |
| **Maybe** | "I want something real, you know?" | Vague but directionally correct. Probe: "What does 'real' mean to you? Give me a specific." |

**Question A2:** "What's your specific frustration with how you're meeting people now?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "Apps feel like a second job and the men I meet aren't serious. They want to text forever and never actually plan anything." or "My friends set me up with guys who look good on paper and have no presence." | Specific complaint, not generic bitterness. She's tried the normal channels and found them lacking. |
| **Red flag** | "Men are intimidated by me." (said without irony, as primary explanation) or "There are no good men left in Chicago." | Externalizes all failure. Will view the room as another disappointment before she arrives. |
| **Maybe** | "I don't meet anyone, really. Work keeps me busy." | Could be genuinely selective or using busyness as shield. Probe: "When you have met someone recently, what happened?" |

**Question A3:** "What would a man need to do or be for you to feel genuine curiosity about him?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Green flag** | "Be present. Make me feel seen instead of scanned." or "Have some relationship with his own life — not perfect, just engaged." or "Take a risk. Show me something real before I have to earn it." | Has standards that map to the Resonance format. These answers describe the dance floor. |

| **Maybe** | "I don't know, I just feel it or I don't." | Trusts intuition (good) but hasn't examined it (concerning). Fine if other answers are strong. |

### 3.3 The Questions — Category B: Safety and Room Contribution

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |

When they're doing their own thing, I can relax." or "I watch how men treat the space and the other women. That's my signal." | Reads social dynamics. Understands that safety is behavioral, not just structural. |
| **Red flag** | "I don't feel safe anywhere with men I don't know." (flat, no nuance) or "As long as there's security and I can leave, I'm fine." or "I feel safe when men don't talk to me unless I want them to." | Either too guarded for this format (will leave early) or expects the event to manage all her discomfort. |
| **Maybe** | "It depends on the vibe. I can usually tell pretty quick." | Reasonable. Most women's answer. Not a flag. |

**Question B2:** "When you're in a room with other single women, do you feel competitive or collegial?"

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |

| **Red flag** | "Not my problem. I'm there for me." or "I'd tell the host." (only answer, no personal agency) | Either too individualistic or too passive. The right answer shows direct, low-drama intervention. |
| **Maybe** | "I'd probably notice but not know what to do." | Honest. Fine if other answers show social awareness. |

Nervousness + willingness is the ideal profile. |

| Response Type | What It Sounds Like | What It Means |

## Section 4: The Red Flag Catalog

### 4.1 Automatic Reject — No Exceptions

These are immediate nos. Andrea ends the call politely and moves on. If any of these surface, do not overrule.

| Red Flag | What They Might Say | Why It's an Auto-Reject |
|---|---|---|
| **Contempt for the opposite gender** | "Women these days only want..." / "Men are all man-children..." | Will poison the room. Other attendees will feel it within 10 minutes. |
| **Sexual aggression or innuendo in a vetting call** | "So we dance, and then what happens?" (with implied subtext) / "How far do people usually go?" | Dangerous. Auto-reject, tag for future exclusion. |
| **Pressuring or boundary-testing during the call** | "Can't you just tell me now if I'm in?" / "Why do you need to ask so many questions?" / "My time is valuable, let's make this quick." | Doesn't respect process → won't respect the container → won't respect other attendees. |
| **Admitted current relationship (non-monogamous or 'complicated')** | "I'm technically still married but we're separated..." / "My partner knows I'm here." | Wrong event. Send to a poly/non-monogamy event. Resonance is for people seeking committed partnership. |
| **Substance dependence as prerequisite for social function** | "I can't dance sober, but I'll try." / "I'll need a drink or two first." | The event is sober. If he can't imagine dancing without alcohol, this is the wrong format. |
| **Falsified information** | Claims on form don't match call (age, relationship status, how they heard about event) | Dishonesty is an automatic reject regardless of what they lied about. |
| **Referral for wrong reasons** | "My friend said this would be a great place to find hookups." | Wrong event, regardless of who referred them. |

### 4.2 Strong Reject — Need 2+ to Justify No

One of these is a yellow flag. Two is a no.

| Flag | What It Sounds Like |
|---|---|
| **Vague or evasive answers to direct questions** | "It depends" to everything, no specifics, deflects with humor |
| **Over-rehearsed or performative responses** | Sounds like he's reading from a script about what women want to hear |
| **Excessive focus on credentials/status** | Leads with job title, salary, neighborhood, name-drops within 2 minutes |
| **No curiosity about the event or Andrea** | Doesn't ask a single question. Treats it as an application, not a conversation. |
| **Recent traumatic breakup (within 3 months)** | "I just got out of a relationship and I need to meet someone new." — Too raw. Will be reactive, not present. Suggest they come to Event #2. |
| **Says yes to everything too quickly** | "Sounds great, whatever, I'm in." No hesitation, no questions. Either desperate or not actually listening. |
| **Dating as numbers game** | "I'll come to anything, I'm on every app, I go to every event." — Spray-and-pray approach. Not serious about THIS event. |
| **Excessive skepticism or interrogation of Andrea** | "How do I know this isn't a scam?" / "What are YOUR credentials?" — Can ask reasonable questions. But treating Andrea with suspicion while wanting her curation is a mismatch. |

### 4.3 Yellow Flags — One Is Fine, 2+ Is a No

| Flag | What It Sounds Like | When It's Okay |
|---|---|---|
| **Nervous to the point of rambling** | Talks a lot, loses thread, apologizes for talking too much | Nervousness is fine. Rambling without self-awareness is the flag. |
| **Extremely limited dating experience** | "I've never really dated, I was married for 12 years." or "I haven't dated since college." | Fine if self-aware. Concerning if combined with rigid expectations. |
| **Very specific physical/type preferences** | "I really only date [height/ethnicity/body type]." | One preference stated honestly is different from a checklist recited with entitlement. |
| **Works in a status-heavy field** | Finance, law, tech executive — leads with it | Fine if doesn't lead with it. Yellow only if credentials are offered unprompted as proof of worth. |
| **Asks about who else is coming** | "What kinds of people will be there?" / "How many women?" | Reasonable question. Yellow only if asked with a predatory or consumerist tone. |
| **Hesitant about the format** | "I'm not really a dancer..." | Fine if followed by "...but I want to try." Not fine if followed by "...so what else is there to do?" |

### 4.4 Green Flags — Say Yes Even If They Seem Uncertain

These are the signals of the right person who just doesn't know it yet. If Andrea hears one of these, bias toward yes even if other answers are uneven.

| Green Flag | What It Sounds Like | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **The honest fear** | "This scares me. That's why I need to do it." | The uncertain person who moves anyway. This IS the ideal profile. |
| **The specific frustration** | "I'm tired of [specific dating behavior] because [specific personal consequence]." | Has done the work of understanding their own experience. Not generic bitterness. |
| **The bathroom answer** | "I went to the bathroom, collected myself, came back." | Self-management in social discomfort. Will handle the floor well. |
| **The growth narrative** | "I used to [old behavior], and now I'm [new behavior]. Here's how I know." | Evidence of self-work, not just aspiration. |
| **The generous observation** | Talks warmly about friends, ex-partners, or strangers. Doesn't trash anyone during the call. | Will contribute to a room where people feel safe. |
| **The format believer** | "The no-phone thing is what got me." or "Dancing first makes so much sense." | Gets it without needing it explained. Will show up as a participant, not a consumer. |
| **The quiet depth** | Pauses before answering. Thinks. Doesn't fill silence with words. | Present. Self-aware. Comfortable with not performing. |
| **The referrer's humility** | "My friend said I should do this. I wasn't sure, but here I am." | Open, humble, trusting a relationship. Will bring that same openness to the floor. |
| **The relationship with their own body** | Any answer to the music/dancing/movement questions that is personal, specific, and non-performative | The floor is a body-first space. This person will be at home there. |

## Section 5: The Rejection Framework

A bad rejection creates an enemy. An enemy tells 10 people. This is brand poison. Every rejection must be handled with the same care as an acceptance.

**General principles:**
- Always reject via text, never ghost
- Always within the promised timeframe
- Always warm, always clear, never apologetic
- Never give detailed reasons (invites argument)
- Never say "you're not good enough" (even if true)
- Always frame as fit, not worth

### 5.1 The "Not This Time" (Wrong Fit, No Malice)

**The person:** Fine human. Wrong room. Maybe too cool, too cautious, too recently broken up, or just not quite the right energy.

**Exact text:**

> "Hey [name], thanks again for taking the time to talk. I've thought about it and I'm going to hold your spot for this one — I'm being really intentional about the mix for the first event and I don't think it's the right fit for where you are right now. This is absolutely not a reflection on you as a person. I'd love to keep you in mind for future events if you're open to it. Either way, I hope you find what you're looking for."

**Tone:** Warm, clear, not overly explained. The phrase "where you are right now" is key — it frames the rejection as temporal/situational, not personal.

**Follow-up:** Add to "future events" list. Do NOT promise a spot at Event #2. Just keep the door warm.

**If they ask why:**

> "It's really just about the mix — I'm balancing a lot of factors for this specific event. Nothing more than that."

Never elaborate. Elaboration invites debate.

### 5.2 The "Absolutely Not" (Predatory, Dangerous, Toxic)

**The person:** Crossed a line in the vetting call. Sexual innuendo, contempt, boundary-testing, or something that made Andrea's stomach turn.

**Exact text:**

> "Hey [name], thanks for your interest in Resonance. After our conversation, I've decided not to move forward with an invitation. I hope you find events that are a better match for what you're looking for."

**Tone:** Professional, minimal, zero warmth. Do NOT say "it's not you" or "maybe next time." Do NOT explain.

**Why this works:** It's unremarkable. There's nothing to screenshot and post. There's no detail to argue with. It's just a no. Boring rejections don't create enemies — defensive or overly explained rejections do.

**If they push back:**

> "I don't have anything to add. Best of luck."

Then mute the thread. Do not engage further. One reply maximum.

**Tagging:** Add to exclusion list with note: "[date] — [specific behavior]. Do not admit." If they reapply to future events, auto-reject.

### 5.3 The "Not Yet" (Almost Right, Maybe Event #2)

**The person:** Good energy, wrong timing. Maybe too raw from a breakup, maybe moving too slow on self-work, maybe just needs one more month of whatever they're working on.

**Exact text:**

> "Hey [name], I really enjoyed our conversation. I think you're a great person but the timing isn't quite right for this specific event — I'm getting a sense that you're in a transition period and I want this to land well for you. I'd love to check back in [specific timeframe, e.g., 'in a couple months' or 'for the fall event']. Would that work?"

**Tone:** Warm, invested, honest. This person should feel seen, not rejected.

**Follow-up:** Calendar reminder to reach out. When you do, reference the prior call: "Hey [name], we talked back in [month] and I said I'd check in. I'm planning the next Resonance for [date]. Are you in a different place now? I'd love to have that conversation again."

**Why this works:** It frames Andrea as someone who pays attention and cares about fit. Even if they never come, they'll speak well of the brand.

### 5.4 The "Your Friend Isn't a Fit" (Referral from Trusted Person, Wrong Candidate)

**This is the hardest rejection.** The referrer's trust is an asset. Handling this wrong damages two relationships.

#### Step 1: Tell the candidate first.

Use the "Not This Time" script above.

#### Step 2: Tell the referrer separately, same day.

**Exact text to referrer:**

> "Hey [friend], I wanted to let you know I talked to [candidate] and decided not to invite them to this one. I really appreciate you thinking of me — the referral means a lot. For this event, the mix just wasn't right. I hope you know I take every referral you send seriously. [candidate] seems like a good person, just not the right fit for this specific room."

**Tone:** Grateful, clear, not defensive. Do NOT throw the candidate under the bus. Do NOT share specific negative details from the call.

**If the referrer pushes:**

> "I hear you, and I know you care about them. I've learned to trust my gut on these calls — I've seen what happens when I override it. I hope they'll understand, and I hope you do too."

**If the referrer is a repeat referrer whose judgment keeps being wrong:**

Have a direct conversation: "Hey, I love that you send people my way. I'm noticing that the last few haven't been quite the right fit. No pressure at all, but maybe hold off on sending folks for a bit until I get the first few events under my belt. I don't want you to feel like your friends are getting rejected."

This preserves the relationship while protecting the funnel.

## Section 6: The "Hell-Yes" Commitment

A "hell yes" is not "I'll try to make it." A hell yes is a commitment with stakes. This section is about converting the vetting yes into a locked-in attendee.

### 6.1 The Offer Text

**Exact text:**

> "Hey [name], I'd love to have you at Resonance on July 18th. You've got a spot if you want it. To lock it in, I need two things: a $50 deposit (goes toward your ticket, fully refundable if you cancel 7+ days out) and a quick 'hell yes' reply to this text. No 'maybe,' no 'I'll try' — I need a real yes. The room depends on people who are fully in. You game?"

**Why this works:**
- It names the deposit as a commitment device, not a revenue grab
- It explicitly rejects weak yeses ("no 'maybe'")
- It frames the commitment as contributing to the room, not just securing a spot
- It's direct without being aggressive

### 6.2 The Deposit Mechanism

**Amount:** $50 (non-trivial but not prohibitive)
**Applied to:** Full ticket price (not an extra fee)
**Refund policy:**
- Full refund if cancelled 7+ days before event
- 50% refund if cancelled 3-7 days before
- No refund if cancelled <72 hours before (but credit toward next event)

**Why the deposit matters:** It creates financial skin in the game. Someone who pays $50 is dramatically more likely to show up than someone who just said "yes" to a text. The refund policy is generous enough to not feel punitive, structured enough to prevent flaking.

**Payment:** Venmo or Zelle to Andrea directly. Simple, personal, no Eventbrite friction.

### 6.3 Flake Prevention

**The confirmation sequence:**

| Timing | Action |
|---|---|
| **Upon acceptance** | Send offer text + deposit request within 24 hours |
| **48 hours after offer** | If no response, one follow-up: "Hey, still holding your spot but need to know by [date] so I can offer it to someone else if not. No pressure either way, just need a clear answer." |
| **7 days before event** | Group text to all confirmed attendees: "7 days. I'm so excited to see what this room becomes. Reminder: doors at 2pm, phones in the basket at 2:15, we start moving at 2:30. Dress code: wear something that makes you feel like yourself. See you Saturday." |
| **48 hours before event** | Individual text: "Hey [name], confirming you're still in for Saturday. Reply with a thumbs up so I know we're good." |
| **Day before event** | Final group text: "Tomorrow. 2pm. [Address]. Bring your fully present self. Everything else is handled." |

**The 48-hour individual check-in is critical.** It forces a moment of re-commitment. Anyone who ghosts this text is a flight risk. Andrea should have 2-3 standby people she can offer last-minute spots to.

### 6.4 Handling the "Something Came Up"

**If someone cancels before the 72-hour window:**

> "No problem at all, thanks for letting me know. I'll refund your deposit [or apply it to the next event, your choice]. Hope everything's okay."

**If someone cancels within 72 hours:**

> "Got it. I'll hold your deposit as credit toward the next Resonance — just let me know when you're ready. Hope to see you at the next one."

No guilt. No pressure. But no refund either — the policy is the policy.

**If someone no-shows without communication:**

Do not chase them. Do not ask what happened. Add a note to their file: "No-show [date], no communication." If they reapply to future events, require a higher deposit ($100) or decline.

**Why sub-types matter:** They help Andrea visualize the room's texture. 10 Discerners and no Warm Anchors makes a cold room. 10 Uncertain Movers and no Veterans makes a fragile room. She wants variety within each gender.

### 7.3 When to Close a Category

Andrea should set soft caps on sub-types to ensure diversity. These are not hard limits — they're signals to bias decisions.

| Category | Soft Cap | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Veteran men | 6 | Too many creates a competitive/status-heavy room |
| Discerner women | 6 | Too many creates a room where everyone is evaluating and no one is participating |
| Uncertain Mover men | 6 | Too many creates a room where no one takes the first step |
| Transitioner women | 5 | Too many recent-breakup energy shifts the tone |
| Any single sub-type | 8 | No matter what the type, concentration above 8 changes room dynamics |

**Closing a category:** When Andrea hits the soft cap, she can still admit someone in that category if they're a "hell yes" with exceptional green flags. But she should bias toward open categories.

### 7.4 The 48-Hour Audit

Two days before the event, Andrea runs through this checklist:

**Attendance audit:**
- [ ] Confirmed count is 48-55 (buffer for last-minute cancellations)
- [ ] Gender balance is 45-55% either way
- [ ] No more than 8 of any single sub-type
- [ ] At least 3 Warm Anchor women confirmed
- [ ] At least 3 Uncertain Mover men confirmed
- [ ] No one on the auto-reject list has slipped through
- [ ] Everyone has paid deposit
- [ ] Everyone confirmed via 48-hour check-in
- [ ] 2-3 standby people ready to fill last-minute drops

**Room-poison audit:**
- [ ] Review vetting notes for any "maybe" admits — are there 2+ yellow flags that should have been a no?
- [ ] Check if any two attendees have a prior conflict (exes, bad dating history) — Andrea won't know all of these, but if she does, one needs to be contacted and given an honest heads-up with option to bow out
- [ ] Confirm no one was admitted on referral alone without a call

**Final decision rule:** If the audit reveals a problem, fix it now. Better to have 47 right people than 50 with 3 room-poisons. A smaller room with the right energy beats a full room with the wrong energy.
