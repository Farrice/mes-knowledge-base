## The 12 Lines

These are the lines that do not get crossed, even when it's expensive not to.

### 1. Daytime
Resonance flagship events happen in daylight. **Variations on the format require a different name.**

### 2. Sober
No alcohol at flagship events. **Variations on the format require a different name.**

### 3. Curated admission
Every attendee is vetted. Hell-yes commitment to being an active participant is the floor.

### 4. No hookup culture
Resonance is for people seeking a committed partner. The room and the language reflect that.

### 5. Founder-curated, always
Andrea, or curators trained in her taste, decide who's in the room. Not an algorithm, not a venue, not a sponsor.

### 6. Music curated by Resonance
No sponsor stages. No "guest DJ from our brand partner." Andrea sets the sound.

### 7. No promoters paid per ticket
Commission promoters get butts in seats but not the right people. The room rots fast when that's how it grows.

### 8. Phones stay off the floor
The event protects presence.

### 9. Stories over metrics
Success is measured in couples formed and connections made, not attendance or follower count.

### 10. Chicago-first
No expansion to other cities until the Chicago model is undeniably working.

### 11. No already-coupled attendees as gatecrashers
Coupled people only enter the room as carefully invited community-builders, if at all.

### 12. No sponsor that requires compromise
**If the sponsorship requires drift, the sponsorship is wrong.** A sponsor that asks Andrea to soften any of lines 1-11 is the wrong sponsor, regardless of dollar value.

## How To Use This Document

### When to paste this in
- Any conversation with an AI about a Resonance decision
- Before responding to a venue offer, sponsor pitch, partner request, or media inquiry
- When ticket sales feel slow and the temptation to "make it more palatable" rises
- When a friend, advisor, or stakeholder proposes a "small adjustment" to the format
- During post-event reflection, to test which lines held and which bent

### How to triage a decision against the lines
1. Name the proposal in one sentence.
2. Read each of the 12 lines and ask: *does this proposal require softening this line?*
3. If yes to any line — the proposal is wrong, regardless of upside. Decline.
4. If no — proceed, document the decision, move on.

### Sponsor decision template (pre-built triage)
For any inbound sponsor / partner / collaborator request, paste this doc + the proposal into Claude and ask:

> *"Apply Resonance's 12 Non-Negotiables to this offer. Which lines, if any, does it touch? Recommend ACCEPT, MODIFY, or DECLINE with one-sentence reasoning."*

A clean accept honors all 12. A modify proposes specific edits to bring the offer into alignment. A decline names which line was crossed and ends the conversation cleanly.

### Variations are sub-brands, not exceptions
Lines 1 and 2 are explicit: variations on the format (evening events, alcoholic events) **require a different name**. Resonance Series, Resonance Retreat, Resonance Late, etc. — these are *named sub-brands*, not exceptions to the flagship lines. The flagship name carries the mechanic; sub-brands carry their own.

This is the moat. The competitive equivalent — a generic "spiritual singles event" — can host any version because it doesn't have these lines. Resonance is a *category of one* because it doesn't.

## What This Document Is Not

- Not a contract.
- Not a list of nice-to-haves.
- Not negotiable on a per-event basis.
- Not the sum total of the brand — it's the floor. The brand is also voice, visual, story, curation, music, room dynamics, and the people Andrea brings together. The lines just keep all of that from drifting.

## What Happens When a Line Bends

Honest truth: lines bend before they break, and most brands die when 2-3 lines have quietly bent and nobody noticed. The only protection is reading this list **before** every decision, not after.

If a line bends, the response is not punishment. It's surface and reset:
1. Name what bent ("we let alcohol in for one event because the venue required it").
2. Name the reason ("we needed a venue and were afraid we'd lose the date").
3. Decide: was this the right call given the lines, or was it a drift?
4. If drift — what's the structural fix that prevents it next time?
5. Update this document only if Andrea decides a line is permanently amending. Otherwise the line stands.
