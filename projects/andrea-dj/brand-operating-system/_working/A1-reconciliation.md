## Section 1 — The Unified Spine

The single canonical statement of what Resonance IS.

**One-line spine (Andrea, Manifesto v2 epigraph):**
> *"Heart encounters, not head encounters. A daytime, sober dance party in Chicago for people who want to meet a partner."*

**The frame: heart vs. head.**
Andrea's manifesto v2 sharpens the older "mind vs. body" framing to **heart vs. head**. The shift is canonical. "Heart" is warmer, more human, more legible to the ICP than "body," and it carries the seeking-a-partner stakes that "body" alone does not. (Andrea's anchor §1: *"To create heart encounters, not head encounters — between people who want to find a partner."*)

**The mechanism: body-first, dance as the protocol of connection.**
Body-first is the *how* of heart encounters, not a competing frame. The Monday Package's body-first thesis — *"dance as the protocol of connection, not the entertainment around it"* (01-pulse-brand.md, "The Spine") — is preserved as the mechanism beneath the heart-vs-head frame. The dance floor is how two people get to a heart encounter without performing through the head first. (Andrea's anchor §1: *"The dancing is the medium. The meeting is the point."*)

**The mechanic: daytime · sober · curated music · curated crowd.**
Four mechanics, all from Andrea's manifesto v2 paragraph 2:
> *"We hold our events during the day, sober, with carefully curated music and a carefully curated crowd."*

These are not branding choices. They are the **Lines** (anchor §3) — the non-negotiables Andrea will not cross even when it's expensive not to. Daytime is line #1. Sober is line #2. Curated admission is line #3. Music curated by Resonance is line #6.

**The enemy.**
Sharpened from Andrea's manifesto v2:
> *"We're not a hookup event in a singles-event costume. We're not speed dating dressed as a party. We don't believe a bar at 11pm is the only permission slip this city gives you to meet someone."*

The enemy is not a competitor. It is **the culture of head-first, transactional, alcohol-mediated meeting** — the apps, the bars at 11pm, the singles-mixer-in-disguise. Specifically named in Andrea's anchor §3.4: *"No hookup culture."* And in v2: *"You've left a thousand rooms with a phone full of contacts and no one to call."*

**The promise (what someone leaves with).**
Andrea's anchor §4: *"People leave lighter, smiling, and not wanting to leave. Roughly 25% of attendees make a real connection with someone they would consider seeing again."*
The promise is not "you'll meet your person tonight." The promise is **a real connection from a real room**, with a 25% floor and a best-case of *"at least one person leaves confident they've just met their person."* The Monday Package's earlier framing (*"the experience is the point; the couple is the residue,"* 01-pulse-brand.md) softens this — Andrea's docs sharpen it: *"We count the couples, not the followers."* (Manifesto v2.) The couple is not residue. The couple is the metric.

## Section 5 — Voice Signature Patterns

Named patterns extracted from Andrea's actual writing. These become voice-document anchors for B3.

### Pattern 1 — The Anaphora Pattern (You've-tried cadence)

Manifesto v2 paragraph 3:
> *"You've tried the apps. You've tried the bars. You've been to the blind dates your friends set up and the parties that promised connection. You've left a thousand rooms with a phone full of contacts and no one to call."*

Four sentences, three start with *"You've."* The repetition does emotional work — it makes the reader feel *recognized* by an exhaustive list. The fourth sentence is the **landing**: it sharpens the diagnosis (*"a thousand rooms… no one to call"*). Use this pattern when the goal is recognition + diagnosis.

### Pattern 2 — The Frame-Then-Sharpen Pattern

Andrea opens with a clean declarative, then immediately names the enemy in specifics. Manifesto v2:
> *"Resonance is for people who want a committed partner and are tired of formats that make that harder, not easier. We're not a hookup event in a singles-event costume. We're not speed dating dressed as a party. We don't believe a bar at 11pm is the only permission slip this city gives you to meet someone."*

Frame: *"for people who want a committed partner."* Sharpen: three specific enemies. The frame alone is generic (every dating brand says it); the sharpen is what earns the frame. Use this pattern in headlines, About copy, sponsor pitches, and venue pitches.

### Pattern 3 — The Crystallized-Phrase Pattern (3–4 word taglines stacked)

Manifesto v2 paragraph 5 (the *"What we believe in"* paragraph) is built almost entirely from short, stacked phrases:
> *"Clean meetings. In daylight. Sober. With your full self in the room. Curation. Of the music, of the people, of the room. Movement before words. Stories over metrics. We count the couples, not the followers."*

Notice the pacing: 2–4 word phrases, period-stacked, occasionally expanded into a longer line. The rhythm is **declarative-staccato**. It reads like a creed, not a paragraph. Use this pattern for hero copy, IG bios, opening sequences, manifesto reprises.

### Pattern 4 — The Out-Loud-Asking Pattern (rhetorical questions the ICP uses)

Anchor §2 quotes the ICP literally:
> *"Why is it so hard to meet a good, nice person?"* / *"Where are all the good guys?"* / *"There are no good women out there anymore."*

Andrea sources her ICP language by listening to what they're already saying *out loud*. This is a voice-research method, not just a voice pattern: when crafting hooks, drop in phrases the ICP literally says. Manifesto v2 closing reflects this: *"If you've asked yourself why it's so hard to meet a good person, the answer is not another app."* — uses the ICP's own question as the hook.

### Pattern 5 — The Hell-Yes Filter Pattern

Manifesto v2: *"If you're a hell-yes to this, we want you in the room. If you're not sure, this isn't your event."*

Two-sentence repel-and-attract. The first sentence invites; the second sentence filters. The filter sentence is what makes the invitation mean something. Use this pattern in any "who this is for" copy — bios, application gates, post hooks, RSVP confirmations.

### Pattern 6 — The Mechanic-as-Sentence Pattern

Andrea writes mechanics as full sentences with stakes, not as feature bullets. Compare:
- Feature-bullet (avoid): *"No alcohol. Daytime event. Curated."*
- Mechanic-as-sentence (Andrea, manifesto v2): *"We hold our events during the day, sober, with carefully curated music and a carefully curated crowd."*

The full sentence carries the stakes of the choice. Use this in any place where mechanics could collapse into bullet slop — hero pages, About sections, venue pitches.
