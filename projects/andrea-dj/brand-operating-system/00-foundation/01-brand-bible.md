## 1. The Spine

The frame is **heart vs. head**, not mind vs. body. Andrea's manifesto v2 sharpens the older framing — and the sharpening matters. *"To create heart encounters, not head encounters — between people who want to find a partner."* (Internal Anchor §1.) Heart carries the seeking-a-partner stakes that body alone does not. Body is anatomy. Heart is what someone is actually trying to find.

Underneath the heart-vs-head frame sits the mechanism: **body-first.** This is the *how*, not a competing thesis. Andrea names it directly: *"The dancing is the medium. The meeting is the point."* (Anchor §1.) Two people get to a heart encounter without performing through the head first because the dance floor lets the body make the introductions. The Monday Package's earlier framing — *"dance as the protocol of connection, not the entertainment around it"* (legacy `01-pulse-brand.md`) — is preserved here as the mechanism layer beneath the heart frame, not as the surface frame itself. In downstream copy, lead with heart. Explain with body.

The mechanic — the four operational choices that make the mechanism possible — comes from Manifesto v2 paragraph 2:

> *"We hold our events during the day, sober, with carefully curated music and a carefully curated crowd."*

Four words doing the work: **daytime, sober, curated music, curated crowd.** These are not aesthetic choices. They are Lines 1, 2, 3, and 6 of Andrea's anchor — non-negotiables she will not cross even when crossing them would be cheaper. They are why the brand can credibly claim heart encounters: every one of them strips out a head-first apparatus the ICP is exhausted by.

The promise is the simplest part. *"People leave lighter, smiling, and not wanting to leave. Roughly 25% of attendees make a real connection with someone they would consider seeing again."* (Anchor §4.) Andrea sharpened this from an earlier softer version. The Monday Package said *"the experience is the point; the couple is the residue."* That framing is retired. Manifesto v2 is canonical: *"We count the couples, not the followers."* The couple is not residue. The couple is the metric.

## 3. The Enemy

Resonance has no competitor. The enemy is the **culture of head-first, transactional, alcohol-mediated meeting** that the ICP is already exhausted by. Andrea names three forms of it directly in Manifesto v2, plus a silent fourth that lives in the ICP's actual life.

### Enemy 1 — The hookup event in a singles-event costume

The party that markets itself as a singles night but is structured to produce hookups, not partners. Dim lighting, dance floor, bar at the center, music tuned to suppress conversation just enough that a phone-number exchange feels like the win. The ICP has been to this and left with a phone full of contacts and no one to call. Resonance refuses the costume by inverting the structure: daylight strips the dim, sober strips the bar, curation strips the room of people who showed up for something else.

### Enemy 2 — Speed dating dressed as a party

The format that imports the speed-dating clock into the party's body. Name tags, rotation timers, icebreakers, "tell the group something interesting about yourself." It is the head-first mechanic with a dance soundtrack. The clock is the tell: speed dating treats connection as a yield problem you solve with throughput. Resonance answers with a 3-hour arc that has no rotation and no scripted introductions. The body does the introductions.

### Enemy 3 — The bar at 11pm as Chicago's only permission slip

The cultural assumption that the only legitimate way to meet someone in this city is at a bar, late, with alcohol doing the courage work. *"We don't believe a bar at 11pm is the only permission slip this city gives you to meet someone."* (Manifesto v2.) Daytime is the direct answer: you don't need to be off-shift, dressed for night, three drinks in, and operating on borrowed permission to meet someone real.

### Enemy 4 — The algorithm pattern (the silent one)

Andrea does not name this in the manifesto, but the ICP lives inside it. The apps, the swipe, the optimization loop. *"You've tried the apps. You've tried the bars."* The apps are not the manifesto's rallying cry because every dating brand attacks the apps. Resonance attacks them at a deeper level: the apps are the head-first failure mode the body-first mechanic is designed to bypass. By the time someone is in the Resonance room, they have already left the apps. The room is the answer; the apps are the problem the room solves.

### Enemy 5 — The performance demand (the meta-enemy)

This is the deepest cultural enemy. The four enemies above are formats; this one is the underlying assumption that connects them. The performance demand is the cultural assumption that adult social rooms require armor — that you can't show up as your full self in a room where strangers might be evaluating you, so you bring the armor: the app's curated profile, the bar's loose-but-defended posture, the speed-dating script, the wellness-brand vocabulary. Andrea has lived inside the performance demand twice — once as a bilingual private-school student trained to be "the nice, quiet, well-behaved little Andrea" (her words), once as a Chicago music student walking into US orchestra culture and finding "intense competition and hostile relationships, restraining orders between orchestra members" (her words). Resonance is built against both formats AND the meta-pattern underneath: the assumption that adults can't have a room where the armor comes off without earning permission through alcohol, status performance, or transactional self-presentation. The four mechanics (daytime, sober, curated, music-led) work because together they remove every load-bearing piece of the performance demand. Daytime removes the bar's permission slip. Sober removes the chemical mask. Curated removes the strangers-evaluating-strangers dynamic by closing the door on the wrong people before anyone gets in. Music-led replaces verbal performance with bodily presence. The performance demand is what makes the room feel like the answer. Naming it explicitly is what makes the answer feel obvious.

## 4. The Voice

Resonance sounds like a person who has been in the room and is telling you, not selling you, what it is like. Andrea's voice has two registers — the polished register of the manifesto (declarative, period-stacked, frame-then-sharpen) and the conversational register of her story (dictation-energy, bilingual touches, sense-detail anchoring like grandmother's coffee or empanadas at the cafeteria); brand voice work uses the polished register, founder-story and origin work uses the conversational register, and both are Andrea. Warm. Direct. Confident. Poetic when the moment earns it. Never preachy. Never corporate. Never apologetic. It assumes the reader is intelligent and has already lived through what the apps and the bars produce.

### The voice rules

1. **The body-first test.** Every line must honor dance as the protocol of connection. If a sentence could belong to a speed-dating event, a bar promo, or a wellness retreat, rewrite. Ask: *does this serve the heart-vs-head thesis with body-first as the mechanism underneath?*

2. **Show, don't tell.** Never describe what Resonance is "like." Show what happens in the room. *"The way they moved, the way they listened, the way they were standing when the song shifted"* beats *"an intimate atmosphere."*

3. **No AI writing tells.** Never start a sentence with *"Here's what..."* or *"Here's why..."* Cap em-dashes at 1–2 per piece. No structural reveals (*"It's not X. It's Y."*). No mic-drop endings. No anaphora that wasn't earned.

4. **Second person when the reader is the protagonist. First person when Andrea is telling her story.** Never *"one."* The reader is the main character of the room copy. Andrea is the main character of the founder copy. Do not confuse the two.

5. **Heart-encounter language as headline. Body-first as explanation.** Lead with the heart frame. Use body-first to explain *how*. Never lead with body-first — it strands the reader in mechanism without stakes. *"Heart encounters, not head encounters. The dance floor is how."* — that order. Not the inverse.

6. **Name the enemy by name when appropriate.** Apps. Speed dating. The bar at 11pm. Hookup-events-in-disguise. It is not rude. It is clear. The ICP has lived inside these — naming them is recognition, not attack.

7. **Chicago stays in the mouth.** Don't over-reference the city, but when you do, mean it. *"Chicago, which has always known how to dance"* is earned (Manifesto v2). *"Vibes in the Chi"* is not.

8. **Never preach sobriety.** No-alcohol-at-flagship is the line, not a sermon. The posture is *"we don't need it here,"* not *"you shouldn't need it anywhere."* Big difference.

9. **Use the word "community" only when you mean it.** Andrea uses it once in the anchor, surgically: *"They have community of some kind, but the partner they want isn't in it."* (§2.) That is the whole right pattern — community as a noun describing something the ICP already has, which is not the thing Resonance is. Do not call Resonance a community. Resonance is a room.

10. **Drop the alcohol-as-comparison framings.** The legacy *"nobody here needs to be drunk enough to approach you"* line leans on alcohol-as-the-other to make its point. The heart-vs-head frame transcends that comparison. The Resonance posture is positive: *"clean meetings, in daylight, sober, with your full self in the room."* Not "without the drunk."

### Andrea's voice signature patterns (named, deferred to the voice document)

These are the six patterns extracted from Andrea's own writing. They are listed here so the bible names them; full treatment lives in the voice document.

- **Pattern 1 — Anaphora (the "you've tried" cadence).** Repetition of a structure across 3–4 sentences, landing on a sharper diagnostic in the final clause.
- **Pattern 2 — Frame-then-sharpen.** Open with a clean declarative, immediately follow with three specific enemies named.
- **Pattern 3 — Crystallized-phrase stack.** 2–4 word phrases period-stacked into a creed.
- **Pattern 4 — Out-loud-asking.** Use phrases the ICP literally says out loud. *"Why is it so hard to meet a good, nice person?"*
- **Pattern 5 — Hell-yes filter.** Two-sentence repel-and-attract: *"If you're a hell-yes, we want you. If you're not sure, this isn't your event."*
- **Pattern 6 — Mechanic-as-sentence.** Mechanics get full sentences with stakes, never bullet-slop.

### The voice test

> *Would Andrea say this to a friend over coffee? If no, rewrite.*

This test is non-negotiable. If a sentence cannot survive Andrea reading it back to a friend in plain conversation, it is not Resonance voice — regardless of how clean it scans on the page.

> I grew up in Costa Rica, in a Latino family, the older daughter, which if you grew up in one of these families you already know what that means. My first memory of what a room is supposed to feel like is coffee at my grandmother's house. ## 8. The Visual Direction

Daytime is mechanic, not metaphor. The visual brief follows the mechanic, not the legacy "Chicago at golden hour" metaphor. Golden hour as an aesthetic vocabulary — warm light, dropped shoulders, late-afternoon body-state — is still useful. But the photography spec is anchored to a sharper rule:

> *If a photo could have been taken at 11pm, it fails.*

That rule rewrites the whole image bank. No dim-room shots. No bar-lit tones. No nightclub silhouettes. No "atmospheric" low-light dance frames that smuggle in the bar-at-11pm visual grammar Andrea is rejecting. Daylight in real rooms, with real people, in honest light. If a designer is unsure whether a frame is on-brand, the test is simple: could this frame have been pulled from a singles-event shoot at 11pm? If yes, kill it.

### Color palette guidance

The legacy palette holds: cream, warm terracotta, midnight blue, gold accent — tested against actual daylight, not stylized warm filters. Terracotta should read as light hitting a real wood floor at 2pm, not as an Instagram filter. Midnight blue grounds the warmth — the sky behind a room, not the room itself. Cream is the breathable background, never cold white. Gold accent stays sparing: couples' names, the moment, the story. Avoid hot pink and club neon (hookup culture), pure white minimalism (corporate wellness), black and chrome (nightclub).

### Typography direction

Serif for heat: weighted, the kind that belongs on a vinyl record jacket from 1978. Clean sans for body: Inter or Plus Jakarta Sans tier, readable, warm, not sterile. Hand-script reserved for human moments only — Andrea's signature, couples' quotes, anything that earns the human touch. Hand-script is rare punctuation, not a brand tone.

### What this section is

Strategic intent. The executable spec — exact hex codes, type stack with weights, component tokens, photography shot list, WCAG-validated palette — lives in `01-visual/DESIGN.md`. A designer cold-reading this section should produce on-brand mood frames. A designer reading the DESIGN.md should ship production assets.

## 9. Operational Spine

Resonance has a metric, a patience window, kill conditions, and drift signals. Together they are the steering mechanism. Documents in `05-ops/` carry the full detail. This section names them so the bible holds the whole brand in one frame.

The metric is **25% couple-formation** at every flagship event. *"Roughly 25% of attendees make a real connection with someone they would consider seeing again."* (Anchor §4.) Best case: at least one person leaves confident they have just met their person. This replaces the older "experience is the point; the couple is the residue" framing. Couples are the metric. Story-capture infrastructure exists to verify and amplify the rate, not to replace it.

The patience window is **12 events.** Andrea will not pivot or walk away until 12 events of clear failure on either kill condition: couple-formation well below the 25% floor across all 12, or attendance below a viable threshold across all 12. Twelve events accounts for normal variance, market education, and time for a community to form. Strategic recommendations that propose pivots before 12 events of clear failure are out of scope. (See `05-ops/04-success-metrics.md`.)

The drift signals are the steering wheel. Anchor §7 lists them: watching follower count more than the floor, music gone safer, the room becoming a scene, more energy on the brand than on the room, sponsor conversations eclipsing event prep, daytime/sober/curation softening under pressure. When any drift signal fires, stop and re-read the anchor. (See `05-ops/03-drift-signals.md`.)

The final operational anchor is Andrea's sentence on sponsorship, which generalizes to every external decision Resonance will face:

> *"If the sponsorship requires drift, the sponsorship is wrong."*

Apply it to every venue offer, partner request, media inquiry, advisor suggestion, and growth opportunity that comes through the door. If it requires drift, it is wrong — regardless of dollar value, prestige value, or relationship value. The lines hold.
