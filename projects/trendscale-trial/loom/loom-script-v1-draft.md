## [0:25–1:35] BRIEF 01 · JCKED — derivation

The carnitine category sells faster fat loss. Every ad. Same promise, two hundred times. My avatar has scrolled past all of them. He is thirty-six, in the gym four days a week, tracks his macros, and there is a layer around his middle that has not moved in three years. He tried carnitine once. Quit at week three because nothing happened. And he blamed himself.

He is wrong to. That is the opening.

There is a real reason his bottle did nothing. Two of them. The dose, most brands give five hundred milligrams when the studies that worked used four thousand. And the mechanism. Stored fat cannot be burned until it is carried into the cell, and the one enzyme that carries it, CPT-1, cannot do its job without carnitine. No carnitine, no key, no access.

So I had a mechanism. I needed a picture for it. I tried a furnace with no fuel line. I tried a clogged filter. Both made him feel broken. The vault won because the emotion is different. His fat is not gone and it is not broken. It is locked. And a lock implies a key. That reframes three years of stubborn fat from a willpower failure into a door he was never handed the key to. That is the unlock direct response is built on. You give him an honorable reason he has not won yet.

On awareness he is problem-aware. He sees the layer, he is out of explanations. The ad moves him to solution-aware by handing him a name, a mechanism, and a reason, so by the end he is not hunting for carnitine. He is hunting for the clinical dose. The category leads with a shirtless before-and-after. I lead with a textbook. The pattern interrupt is the format itself, and it clears Meta on its face, because I never name his condition. I describe the door.

## [1:35–2:45] BRIEF 02 · PURAVITA — derivation

Puravita is the harder problem. And the more interesting one.

Every magnesium ad sells sleep, energy, calm. To a man already typing "best magnesium for sleep." That man is twenty percent of the market. The man who needs it most is not searching magnesium at all. He is forty-seven, he sleeps eight hours and wakes up exhausted, and at 3am he is typing "why do I feel like garbage." His doctor ran his bloodwork and told him he is fine.

He is not fine. And here is the fact that runs the whole brief. A standard blood test sees less than one percent of the magnesium in your body. The other ninety-nine percent lives in muscle and bone, where the test cannot reach. He can be badly depleted and still test normal. His doctor is not lying. The test is just blind.

So the job is not to sell him magnesium. It is to move him from "I am just getting old" to "there is a fixable thing here." I needed a picture for an invisible problem. I tried a low-fuel light, but a fuel light is a warning, and his whole story is that he never got a warning. The phone battery won because that is the point. Your phone tells you at five percent. Your body does not. The metaphor and the mechanism are the same shape. A gauge you were never shown.

That is a Schwartz level-one play. Unaware to problem-aware. It is the expensive bet. Most magnesium ads live at level three because it converts faster. But level three concedes the eighty percent who do not know they have a problem yet. This brief goes and gets them.

## [2:45–3:40] CLOSE — why the method

Two things before I let you go.

First, every brief has an iterations page, and that page is the real strategy. Three variants queued behind the control, each one holding the picture constant and testing a different lever. Because week one is not about whether the ad works. It is about whether the angle works. I build the test to answer that question before the question gets expensive.

Second, the reason I work this way. Direct response is a behavior change discipline pretending to be a marketing discipline. The customer does not need a better feature list. He needs a new story about why his body is the way it is. One that does not require him to have failed. Give him a picture, give him a mechanism, give him a reason to act that is not shame. He will buy.
