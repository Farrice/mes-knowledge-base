The Big Idea

Your phone shows you when it's at 5%. Your body doesn't.

Every man over 40 has lived this scene. He slept eight hours. He ate clean. He didn't drink. He still feels like a low-grade flu. So he blames age. Then stress. Then his phone. His body has been screaming for two years.

The Research04 / 08

Three facts the avatar doesn't know. Each one rewires his story.

Fact 01 · The blood-test gap

Serum magnesium reflects less than 1% of total body magnesium, and serum is a poor predictor of true status. The other 99% lives in bone, muscle, and soft tissue. A man can be severely depleted and still test "normal." This is the foundational caveat in every clinical magnesium reference.

NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, Magnesium fact sheet (Health Professional).

Fact 02 · The 600 processes

Magnesium is a cofactor in over 600 enzymatic reactions (up from the older 300-enzyme estimate). Muscle contraction. Neurotransmitter synthesis. ATP production. Magnesium doesn't assist these reactions. They don't happen without it. When the body is low, the systems don't fail outright. They just perform worse, which is exactly the symptom pattern the avatar is living.

de Baaij, Hoenderop & Bindels, "Magnesium in Man," Physiological Reviews, 2015.

Fact 03 · The depletion curve

Nearly half of US adults, about 48%, ingest below the EAR (estimated average requirement), with the lowest intakes in men over 71 and adolescent males. Men 35-plus sit squarely on the curve. And refining whole wheat into white flour strips out roughly 80% of its magnesium, so the modern diet quietly removes it. This isn't a discipline problem. It's a food-supply problem.

NIH ODS (intake / NHANES); USDA FoodData Central (grain-refining loss).

The authority weave · cold-traffic anchor

Dr. Andrew Huberman recommends 145mg magnesium threonate, or 200mg magnesium bisglycinate, 30 to 60 minutes before sleep. Dr. Peter Attia says he aims for about a gram of elemental magnesium a day across several forms. The avatar has already heard the recommendation. He just hasn't acted on it. Our ad delivers the missing piece: the metaphor makes him feel why it matters, the science gives him permission to act, and the twelve-form Puravita formula goes further than the one or two forms the experts name.

Compliance note

Every claim above is structure-function: "magnesium supports the body's enzymatic processes." We never say "treats fatigue" or "cures insomnia." Aligned to Meta's long-standing Personal Attributes standard (last updated 2024).

Meta H&W compliance · what this brief honors

No diagnosis claims. Not "you have magnesium deficiency." We say "the body's magnesium stores are largely invisible to standard tests." Educational, not diagnostic.
No personal-attribute hooks. "You sleep eight hours and wake up exhausted" as a universal observation, never "are you tired?" Described from inside his experience, not by his condition.
No before/after. Anatomical glow metaphor, never body transformations.
No timeframe promises. If "6 to 8 weeks" appears, it's only because Puravita's own PDP cites it (94% report the change by week 8). Verifiable customer data, not a promise.

Do not · for the editor
  • Don't show a doctor in a white coat. Reads as an ad immediately.
  • Don't show a literal anatomical diagram. Stylized glow only.
  • Don't let the avatar smile through Beats 01 to 03. Restrained.
  • Don't use stock "tired guy at a desk" footage. Stay in metaphor.
  • Don't add urgency text ("Limited stock!"). Kills the editorial tone.
  • Don't put a person next to the Huberman quote. Let the waveform carry it.

Three iterations queued behind the control. Each holds the battery metaphor constant and tests a different lever. This page is the strategy — the control answers whether the picture lands; the iterations answer which lever moves it.

"My wife noticed before I did."

Open on the partner-noticed beat instead of the phone battery. The PDP testimonials are saturated with this exact phrase, which suggests it may be even more universal than the metaphor.

02 · Native advertorial

"The mineral your blood test missed."

Re-cut as an 800-word native article. The science weave and the 1% reveal work even better in long-form. Higher-LTV avatars convert through native at materially better margins.

03 · Female 45+

"My bloodwork looks fine. So why doesn't anything else?"

Female HeyGen avatar, same structure, perimenopausal symptom set in the resonance beat. Magnesium has a strong perimenopause story. Opens a major secondary segment.
