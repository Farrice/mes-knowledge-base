Section 01 · Ad Script05 / 12

The ad I'd ship on day 30, and why it opens with a barber, not a side effect.

Spoken hook · 0:00–0:03 · direct to camera, bathroom mirror, no filter

"My barber told me something last week that I genuinely cannot stop thinking about."

01 · Curiosity + authority transfer

The viewer doesn't yet know what the barber said. Open loop, and the brain is wired to close it. Authority borrowed from a non-salesy figure. A barber sees more scalps than any dermatologist, and the audience knows it.

02 · Bypasses the "this is an ad" filter

No product, claim, or urgency in the first three seconds. Pain-led hooks ("Losing your hair?") run into Meta's Personal Attributes enforcement. Curiosity clears it.

03 · Customer voice, not brand voice

This is how a real guy tells the story, not how a brand writes it. The strongest beat ("my girlfriend noticed before I did") lands because the ad refuses to sound like a pitch deck.

The rule

No two hooks pull the same lever.

Shipping ten curiosity hooks teaches you nothing about which lever the audience actually responds to. Spread the levers and Week 1 doesn't just find a winning hook. It tells you what kind of hook wins, so Week 2 compounds instead of guessing.

Section 05 · Teardown 01A · Keeps09 / 12

Keeps Commercial #04. What's working, and what's quietly costing them.

Verified · keeps.com/learn/keeps-hair-loss-commercials

"Yeah, hair loss runs in my family. But I use Keeps, the easiest way to keep my hair. Keeps sends me clinically-proven treatments that can stop my hair loss. Learn more at Keeps.com."

What's working

01 · The concession opener. "Yeah, hair loss runs in my family" → immediate pivot to "But I use Keeps." A one-beat hook with a hard turn at the conjunction.

02 · It walks past the defense. Most ads open with "Worried about hair loss?" and the viewer's guard is already up. Keeps agrees with the viewer first, then pitches.

03 · Proof compression. "Clinically-proven" carries the whole credibility load in 11 words, right before the CTA. And the "easiest way to keep my hair" line repeats across Keeps' library, compounding recall.

What's costing them

01 · It validates the fatalism. "Hair loss runs in my family" reinforces the genetic-helplessness belief that delays purchase. The avatar buys when he believes he has agency, not when he's told it's inherited.

02 · Convenience over outcome. "Easiest way" sells the subscription, not the result. In a category running high CPMs, the ad that sells the outcome wins the click.

03 · No mechanism. "Clinically-proven" is a claim, not a reason. Nothing teaches the viewer why it works, so nothing reframes his story.

Section 05 · Teardown 01B · The Inversion10 / 12

Same family reference. Opposite ending.

My counter · spoken hook · 0:00–0:04

"My dad went bald at 32. I'm 36. Here's what I did differently."

The same family reference, used to disprove fatalism instead of validate it. Genetic helplessness is the #1 mental block in male hair loss. Disproving it through a direct family contrast is the most powerful angle available, and counter-positioning against an established brand's stale message is one of the highest-leverage moves in DR.

01 · Inversion variant

Fatalism flipped to agency. Hypothesis: lower CPA on cold traffic. Agency-framing beats acceptance-framing in prospecting because it gives the buyer permission to act. Same creator, same length, only the worldview flips.

02 · Specificity variant

"Clinically-proven" → drug names + price. Hypothesis: higher CTR among the qualified avatar. "The same minoxidil and finasteride a dermatologist prescribes, $25/month." Hard specificity beats soft credibility.

03 · Anti-couch variant

Reject the convenience frame entirely. Hypothesis: novelty angle, fresh creative oxygen. A creator who got off the couch, went to a dermatologist, and came back with a result the subscription never promised.

Section 05 · Teardown 02 · Nutrafol11 / 12

Nutrafol owns women's by sub-segmenting it. Men's is wide open.

Verified · active Meta creative, June 2026

"GLP-1 associated hair thinning. When you have a significant amount of weight loss, it's stressful on the body, and what we see clinically is hair thinning. I often recommend a hair-growth nutraceutical."

Three moves you can verify by watching the ad

Authority frame. A clinician voice, not a model. The credibility comes from the delivery, not a before-and-after.

New-category naming. "GLP-1 hair thinning" is a sub-segment nobody else owns yet. Naming the problem is how you own the solution.

Implicit validation. "What we see clinically" makes the viewer's private worry feel observed and normal, which lowers the buying defense.

The extension · men's side

Don't ship one creative engine. Ship four.

Nutrafol's women's playbook isn't one ad. It's audience segmentation disguised as creative. The same logic is wide open on the male side, where every competitor still ships one undifferentiated message.

Segment 01 · Hairline recession
Segment 02 · Crown thinning
Segment 03 · Post-finasteride recovery
Segment 04 · Post-transplant maintenance

Extracted principle. Each segment gets its own hook bank, creator archetype, and CPA target. That's the moat. Authority AND UGC, not authority OR UGC.
