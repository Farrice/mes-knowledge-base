**The down payment is no longer the binding constraint for a prepared first-gen buyer — income and access are.** Assistance can get you in the door with little cash, but you still need ~**$150K household income** to carry an entry SFV condo. This is the OPPOSITE of how every "down payment assistance!" video frames it — and it's what makes Jen's content honest and different.

## Rent-vs-buy honest framing
2BR rent ~$3,000 vs entry-condo all-in ~$3,758 (more at low down once PMI+HOA stack). **Buying is ~$750–$2,000/mo MORE than renting in 2026 SFV.** Don't claim "buying is cheaper." The true frame: *"you pay a premium now to stop rent inflation and start building equity — and assistance solves the cash-to-close wall, not the monthly wall."*

## 2026 vs 2025 — what changed (the data-currency point)
- **Inventory loosening** — SFV condo listings **+31% YoY**; ~4.7 months supply (nearing balanced). Condos = where buyer leverage is. SFR listings down ~19.5% YoY.
- **Entry prices flat/softening** — LA County −0.6% YoY; SFV condo −5.5% YoY (Feb). High end is where strength is.
- **Rates stuck mid-6s** — the late-2025 dip (~6.0–6.2%) reversed; "wait for rates to drop" underperformed.
- **NAR commission rules now in force** — buyer-agent comp off MLS, written buyer-agency agreement required (CA AB 2992). First-timers may now **owe their agent's fee directly** if seller doesn't cover = a NEW cash-to-close line to budget.
- **CA insurance / FAIR Plan crisis** — +29.1% FAIR Plan hike approved for late 2026; fire-adjacent SFV foothills (Sylmar, Sunland, Chatsworth fringe) — insurance is now a **qualification risk**, not an afterthought.
