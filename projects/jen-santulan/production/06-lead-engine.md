#### 1. the rent-vs-buy truth (side by side, honestly)

| each month | renting your 2BR | buying an entry SFV condo |
|---|---|---|
| what leaves your account | ~$3,000 | ~$3,758 all-in (PITI) |
| where it goes | your landlord's equity | yours: principal plus a locked cost |
| next year's number | whatever they raise it to | the same payment, on purpose |

**the honest part most agents won't say:** buying costs about **$750 more per month** right now. that's real. anyone who tells you owning is cheaper today is selling you something.

and that $750 is buying you two things rent never will: your rent stops rising on you forever, and the money starts building something that's yours instead of someone else's.

*(figures: SFV entry condo ~$600K, 20% down, ~6.5% rate, per SRAR's own calc, Feb 2026. SFV 2BR rent ~$3,000, RentHop May 2026. verify your block same-day before you act, and i'll do that with you.)*

---

#### 2. the income to qualify (the number that actually gates you)

an entry SFV condo needs roughly **$150K household income** to carry comfortably.

that's the real wall, not the down payment. and it's exactly why **37% of buyers in 2026 are buying with a partner, sibling, or parent** to clear it *(U.S. News Spring 2026 survey).* teaming up isn't a compromise. it's how the math gets done now.

> the down payment is the solvable part. income and access are the real questions, and there are more ways through both than you've been told.

---

#### 3. the real program menu (with honest status — open AND closed)

| program | what it does | status right now |
|---|---|---|
| **CalHFA MyHome** | covers up to **3.5%** of your down payment | **open** — the workhorse door |
| **CalPLUS + ZIP** | helps with closing costs | open, stacks with MyHome |
| **LA City LIPA** | up to **$161,000** in help | open, but **City of LA only**, tiny capacity |
| **Dream For All** | first-*generation* buyers, up to 20% / $150K | **closed for 2026** (window ended Mar 16). watch for the next round, don't wait on it |

the agents who hyped "down payment assistance!!" skipped the part that matters: **assistance gets you past the cash wall. your income gets you the home.** that's the part you can actually plan around.

*(any program can shift mid-year. i confirm current status the day we run your numbers.)*

---

#### 4. two 2026 lines no one budgeted for (so you're not surprised)

- **the agent-fee line.** new commission rules mean in some deals a buyer owes their agent's fee directly if the seller doesn't cover it: a new cash-to-close number to plan for, not panic over.
- **insurance.** in the fire-adjacent foothills (Sylmar, Sunland, Chatsworth fringe), insurance is now a real qualification question, not an afterthought. it changes which homes pencil out. worth knowing before you fall for one.

---

#### 5. three questions to know if you're actually ready

not "do you feel ready," nobody feels ready. these are the calculating ones:

1. **between you and anyone you'd buy with, is combined income near $150K, or is there a realistic path there in 12 months?** (extra work counts. in that same U.S. News Spring 2026 survey, 57% of buyers said they were taking on additional work to afford it.)
2. **can you reach ~$20–25K in cash-to-close, counting a family gift if one's available?** about 1 in 5 first-time buyers used a gift or loan from family. almost nobody puts 20% down anymore.
3. **do you plan to stay in the place at least ~5 years?** that's the line where the $750 premium turns into equity instead of a cost.

**two or three yes-or-almost?** you're not disqualified. you're closer than you've been told.

> the typical first-time buyer in 1981 was 29. today it's **40, a record high** *(NAR 2025).* if you're 40 and renting, you didn't fall behind. the whole map redrew, and you're standing right where everyone else is.
