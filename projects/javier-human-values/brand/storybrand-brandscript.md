## STORYBRAND BRANDSCRIPT: HUMAN VALUES COLLECTIVE
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

---

### 1. CHARACTER (THE HERO)

**Desire:** To raise a child who is strong enough to resist what's harmful and open enough to come to them when something's wrong.

*The parent is the hero. Not the teacher, not the organization — the parent who lies awake at night wondering: "Will my kid be okay?"*

---

### 2. PROBLEM

**Villain:** The culture of silence and emotional shutdown between parents and children — the unspoken rule that families don't talk about what's really going on in their hearts.

**External Problem:** Parents don't have a practical way to have meaningful conversations about values, emotions, and real life with their children — especially as those children enter the most dangerous years (ages 11-24).

**Internal Problem:** "I'm afraid I'm repeating the same patterns I grew up with. I want to be close to my kids, but I don't know *how* to talk about the things that matter — and I'm terrified that if I get it wrong, they'll stop coming to me altogether."

**Philosophical Problem:** No child should grow up believing it's safer to suffer alone than to talk to their own parent.

---

### 3. GUIDE

**Empathy:** "We know what it's like to grow up in a household where nobody talked about the hard stuff — and to realize you might be repeating it."

**Authority:**
- Javier Payano — character education expert, 10+ years as a music educator at one of Chicago's top public schools, certified in Education in Human Values and Music for Social Change
- Implemented human values programs in Chicago classrooms with documented improvements in student happiness, emotional stability, and decision-making
- 2021 3Arts Make a Wave Award recipient
- Training at the Institute of Sathya Sai Education (Thailand) — the global center for Education in Human Values methodology
- Parent testimonials: *"I have found new ways to see myself as a human with equal value. This message had not reached my heart before I started practicing EHV."*

---

### 4. THE PLAN

**Process Plan:**

1. **Listen to the podcast.** Start learning what Education in Human Values looks like in real families — real conversations, real breakthroughs, real parents like you.

2. **Get the Value of the Week.** Sign up for a free weekly email with one human value, a simple family conversation guide, and starter questions you can use that same week.

3. **Join Sunday Night Dinner.** Use our done-for-you dinner scripts to start a weekly practice that builds a connection strong enough to last through the teenage years and beyond.

**Agreement Plan:**
- You don't have to be a perfect parent. You just have to show up one dinner at a time.
- This isn't therapy, and we're not telling you how to parent. We give you a simple practice rooted in universal human values — you adapt it to your family.
- If Sunday dinner doesn't work for your family, use the scripts wherever your child is most receptive — the car ride, the trampoline, the diaper-changing table. This is universal.

---

### 5. CALL TO ACTION

**Direct CTA:**
> "If you want your child to grow up with a courageous heart — strong enough to resist what's harmful and open enough to come to you when something's wrong — joining Sunday Night Dinner is the right next step."

**Transitional CTA:**
> "Start with the Value of the Week — a free weekly email with one human value and a family conversation guide you can use this Sunday."

---

### 6. FAILURE (STAKES)

• Your child learns early that small problems get big reactions — and they stop bringing you the big stuff. By the time they're a teenager, they've learned to suffer in silence.

• The patterns you swore you'd never repeat — the anger, the shutdown, the "don't talk about it" culture — quietly take root in your own home because no one gave you a different script.

• Your teenager pulls away (which is natural), but there's no connection strong enough to pull them back. When something really bad happens, they don't reach for you. They reach for their phone, their friends, or worse — nothing at all.

---

### 7. SUCCESS (TRANSFORMATION)

• Your child brings you the hard stuff — not because you're perfect, but because you've made it safe. When they're scared, confused, or hurting, *you're* the one they call.

• You break the cycle. The patterns that shaped your childhood end with you. Your kids grow up in a household where love, honesty, and courage are practiced out loud — not just hoped for.

• Your child leaves your home with a courageous heart — empathetic but not naive, strong but not hard, passionate about justice and peace in the world. You know you gave them everything they needed.

### The "Broken Vase" Hook — Special Asset

This is your most powerful single piece of messaging. Use it everywhere:

> *"If your child brings you a broken vase and you get angry, you've just taught them the biggest lesson of their childhood: it's not safe to tell you the truth. And if a broken vase isn't safe — depression won't be either."*

This hook works because:
- It's instantly visual (broken vase = concrete image)
- It activates guilt without blame (every parent has done this)
- The stakes escalate in one sentence (vase → depression → silence)
- It makes the solution feel urgent and achievable
