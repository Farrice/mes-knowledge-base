## 1. The Pinned Offer Post (spec)

This is the funnel. One post, pinned to the profile, doing the entire job of a five-step funnel because Threads keeps the original post overlaid when a link is tapped **[VERIFIED]** and reportedly doesn't throttle in-post links **[LIKELY — test on your own account, see Section 6]**.

**Structure (fixed order, do not skip a beat):**

1. **Value — open in the scene.** A statement, not a question. Give something real before asking for anything. This is where the credential and the behavioral insight live (Section on Frame: never introspection alone — pair it with the mechanic in the next beat).
2. **Answer.** Name the actual thing that fixes what beat 1 surfaced. Concrete, not vague ("here's the system," not "here's a mindset shift").
3. **Name DWA.** Say the product plainly. No euphemism ("this program," "the thing I use") — name it.
4. **One reason.** A single, honest reason to buy through him specifically, not forty thousand other links. This is the toolkit, stated as mechanic, not hype.
5. **Link in the post body + inline disclosure.** Not the bio. Not the first comment. In the post, next to the link, in the same breath.
6. **Toolkit bonus, spelled out as a mechanic** — the receipt-DM instruction (Section 2), so the reader knows exactly what to do next.

**Worked example (built on Angle E — First $1,000 = the Belief Unlock):**

> $1,000 won't change your life. But the morning it hits your account, your brain stops asking if you can even do this — and that question was the only thing standing between you and the next one.
>
> I spent eighteen years coaching people through exactly that gap. Knowing what to do was never the problem. Doing it was. So the fastest path isn't a new hustle, it's a beginner program: one product, one skill, repeated until it's not scary anymore.
>
> That program is Digital Wealth Academy. $497, one time, and I'm not going to pretend the internet doesn't already have opinions about it — look them up, then look at what you're actually getting: a system, not a promise.
>
> Affiliate link, I earn a commission if you buy through it, at no extra cost to you: [link]
>
> Buy through this link, DM me your receipt, and I'll send you the AI toolkit I built for exactly this stage — the one that removes the blank page, the tech overwhelm, and the "what do I even do next" that makes people quit in week two. It won't make you money. It removes the reasons you'd quit before you found out if it could.

Notes on why this passes both compliance and voice:
- Zero income claims — "$1,000 won't change your life" is a belief statement, not an earnings promise.
- Disclosure sits directly on the link, plain language, FTC-safe (Section 3).
- No question sign-off, no twin-reveal, no triple anaphora, one em-dash total.
- Behavioral insight (beat 1) is immediately paired with the money mechanic (beats 2-4) — satisfies the "not introspection alone" requirement.

## 2. The Receipt-DM Toolkit Mechanic

**The full instruction, exactly as it should appear wherever the offer is made:**

> "Buy DWA through my link. DM me a screenshot of your receipt. I'll send you my custom AI toolkit, free — no cost, no upsell."

**Why this single sentence is doing three jobs at once:**

1. **It's the only reason to click *this* link.** With 40,000+ affiliates selling the identical $497 product at the identical price, the toolkit is the sole point of differentiation available on session-only attribution. It converts a structural weakness (no cookie, no persistent tracking) into a reason to act now, without inventing fake urgency — the urgency is real and mechanical: click a different link and there's no toolkit waiting on the other side.
2. **It's attribution proof.** Session-only or short-cookie **[UNCONFIRMED — confirm in portal]** attribution means Farrice has no reliable way to know who bought through him versus a different affiliate unless the buyer self-reports. The receipt DM is that self-report. It's not a "nice to have" — it's the only confirmation mechanism that exists in this model.
3. **It's a warm contact and a future testimonial, for free.** Every receipt DM is a real name, a real conversation, and (with permission, later, honestly) a potential proof point. This is inventory Farrice cannot get from a cold click alone.

**What NOT to do with this mechanic:**
- Never call it a "bonus package" or stack it with implied value ("$997 worth of tools, free!") — that's the scarcity/fake-value theater this whole positioning is built against.
- Never gate the toolkit behind anything beyond the receipt. No email opt-in, no additional funnel step — the entire point is that it's frictionless in the same session.
- Never imply the toolkit makes money. State plainly, every time, what it actually does: removes blank-page paralysis, tech overwhelm, and the "no next step" quit point. That's it.

## 3. Affiliate Disclosure — Exact Lines (FTC-safe, plain language)

Use one of these, verbatim, **at or immediately before every link**, every time, no exceptions:

**Standard (default — use this one):**
> "Affiliate link — I earn a commission if you buy through it, at no extra cost to you."

**Short version (character-constrained contexts, e.g. a reply):**
> "Affiliate link, I get a commission if you buy — costs you nothing extra."

**DM version (conversational register):**
> "Quick disclosure since I'll drop a link: it's an affiliate link, I earn a commission if you buy through it, doesn't cost you anything more."

**Rules governing all three:**
- The disclosure comes **before or directly alongside** the link — never buried in a bio, never in a separate post, never only in a pinned comment.
- No hedge language ("just so you know," "full transparency," "I gotta say") — state it plainly and move on. Over-qualifying a disclosure reads as apologetic, which erodes the disqualify-honestly posture that's doing real conversion work elsewhere.
- Never substitute a vague variant ("sponsored," "partner link," "#ad" alone) — DWA-specific commission language is clearer and more defensible than platform-standard tags.

## 4. Warm-Adjacent Coach-to-Peer DM Script

**[LIKELY]** the highest-probability first sale comes from people who already trust Farrice as a coach — old S&C clients, dad friends — not a cold Threads stranger in week one. This script is honest, disqualifying, and leads with the toolkit as the differentiator, matching the brand's anti-hustle posture.

> Hey [name] — random, but wanted to run something by you specifically because I trust you'll tell me if it's a bad idea.
>
> I picked up an affiliate deal for a digital marketing course called Digital Wealth Academy. $497, teaches the actual skill stack for building an online income stream. I'm not going to sell you on it being magic — you know me, I don't do that. It's a real program with real homework, and most people who buy any course like this don't finish it. That's not a knock on the course, that's just what happens with courses.
>
> The reason I'm mentioning it to you and not posting it everywhere is I built an AI toolkit specifically for the part where people quit — the blank page, the "what do I even do first" — and I only send it to people who buy through me and DM me the receipt. It's not going to make you money. It removes the reasons you'd stop before you find out if it could.
>
> If that's not where you're at right now, genuinely no pressure, I'd rather you say no than buy something you won't touch. But if you've been sitting on wanting to build something on the side, happy to send you my link and walk you through what I built. Your call.

**Why this holds the brand:**
- Opens with disqualification ("most people don't finish," "no pressure") — the integrity screen (Angle G) as a functional DM move, not just a content angle.
- No income claim anywhere, including implied ("build something on the side" — not "make money").
- The affiliate relationship is disclosed by describing the mechanic plainly, not hidden.
- Ends by handing the decision back, but crucially — this is a *warm* DM, so "your call" here is fine because there's no pinned-offer-post's job of forcing same-session conversion. If they say yes, move immediately into the live-DM close in Section 5 rather than letting the conversation go cold.

## 5. Live-DM Conversion Script (ends with the link, right now)

Session-only attribution means a "I'll send it later" DM is a lost sale — the buyer's next session may click a different affiliate's link, or none. Once someone signals real interest in a live conversation, close in that message. Never say "I'll follow up" or "let me grab that for you in a bit."

**Script (drop-in, adapt the bracket):**

> Love it — here's exactly what to do right now so you don't lose the thread:
>
> 1. Click this: [link] — that's my affiliate link, I earn a commission if you buy through it, doesn't cost you extra.
> 2. Checkout is direct with DWA, $497 one-time. I don't touch your payment, no info goes through me.
> 3. Once you're through, screenshot the receipt and send it right back here in this chat.
> 4. I'll send the AI toolkit the second I see it — same conversation, no separate form.
>
> Takes maybe five minutes start to finish. I'm right here if anything's confusing on the checkout page.

**Rules for this script:**
- Numbered, mechanical, zero persuasion beyond what's already happened in the conversation — by the time this message sends, the selling is done. This is instructions, not a pitch.
- The disclosure is embedded in step 1, not appended as an afterthought.
- "I'm right here" replaces any sign-off question — it's a standing offer, not a prompt for a response, which keeps it clear of the banned cheap-question-signoff pattern.
- If they go quiet after this message, do not chase with urgency language ("still there?" "don't miss out") — that crosses into manufactured urgency. A single honest follow-up 24-48 hours later ("no rush, link still works whenever") is the ceiling.

## 6. Verification Checklist — Run Before Scaling Anything

Two categories: portal facts to confirm, and a link-throttling test to run on Farrice's own account. Nothing in this playbook should scale past a handful of posts until both are done.

### A. Verify in your affiliate portal (do this before the first post goes live)

- [ ] **Which DWA your link points to.** Confirm it's Rachel's flagship at `portal.digitalwealthacademy.com`, not Brad Williams' Grassroots-Wealth DWA. **[Currently LIKELY, not confirmed.]**
- [ ] **Payment processor and payout cadence.** Confirm Fanbasis and the ~2-day payout window, or note the actual processor/timeline if different. **[Currently UNCONFIRMED.]**
- [ ] **Attribution window.** Confirm whether tracking is truly session-only (zero cookie) or a short cookie window (24-72 hrs is common even on "session-only" claims). This changes nothing about the content strategy (build for session-only regardless, per the ground-truth doc) but changes what you tell buyers about timing if they ask.
- [ ] **Commission rate and price**, reconfirm $497 / 85% / ~$422 per sale still match what the portal shows — pricing and splits can change without notice on affiliate programs.
- [ ] **Guarantee language**, if any is offered to buyers — pull the exact official wording from DWA's terms. Never write your own guarantee copy; only ever restate theirs, verbatim, with a link to their terms if quoting it.

### B. Test link-throttling on your own account (run this in parallel with the portal check)

The whole pinned-post and in-post-link strategy rests on the claim that Threads doesn't throttle outbound links the way X and LinkedIn do. That claim is **[LIKELY, practitioner-confirmed, not dataset-proven]** — treat it as a hypothesis to test, not a settled fact.

- [ ] Post 5-10 original posts that are text-only (no link) and log reach/reply numbers for each.
- [ ] Post 5-10 original posts with the affiliate link in the body (same disclosure format each time) and log the same metrics.
- [ ] Compare average reach per post across the two sets after the full 5-10/5-10 run — don't judge off 1-2 posts, Threads reach is noisy post-to-post.
- [ ] **If link-posts consistently underperform text-posts** (meaningfully, not noise-level), treat throttling as real for this account and fall back to: text-only post with the value/answer/name-DWA/one-reason beats, then the link goes in the first reply, with the disclosure repeated in that reply.
- [ ] Re-run this test roughly monthly — platform algorithm behavior shifts, and a result that holds in month one isn't guaranteed to hold in month three.
- [ ] Log the pinned-offer-post's own conversion signal separately (profile visits vs. DMs/receipts) — since it lives in one fixed spot rather than the main feed, its throttling behavior may differ from timeline posts and deserves its own read.

**Standing rule:** until both A and B are confirmed, every post that references the affiliate program should carry the mechanics in this document exactly as written — the mechanics don't change based on the test outcomes, only the link *placement* does (in-post vs. first-reply).
