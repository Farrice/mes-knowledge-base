# DWA Threads — Hook Bank

_Organized by angle, then by awareness level. Every hook here traces to `../02-research/00-research-and-whitespace.md` (angle bank) and clears the voice/compliance rules in the other two anchors. Reply-sprint openers are called out explicitly — they carry no link, no pitch, and exist to borrow reach per `07-conversion-and-playbook.md`._

**Awareness ladder used below:** unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → (reply-opener, a separate no-pitch use-case that can apply at any awareness level).

## Angle A — The Adherence Coach

Belief: it's not the course or method that failed you — follow-through is a trainable skill you were never taught. Spine credential: 18+ years S&C / behavior-change coaching.

### Unaware
- "Eight years I sat across from people who quit their gym program in week two. Not because the program was bad. Because nobody ever taught them how to keep showing up. Your side hustle is dying from the same disease." — statement hook, opens in the coaching scene before naming any product.
- "The course isn't the problem. I know because I spent eight years watching people fail at a completely different kind of program for a completely different reason: they were never taught how to actually follow through." — names the belief-shift directly, no em-dash, no twin-reveal structure.
- "A guy told me once, 'I know exactly what to do, I just can't seem to DO it.' That was a client talking about his squat program. It's also every person who bought a course last year and never opened it." — cleared signature line, reader-as-protagonist bridge in sentence two.

### Problem-aware
- "Ninety percent of my clients quit before the transformation showed up on the outside. Not from laziness. From the exact same three reasons, every single time. Turns out those reasons don't care if you're trying to lose twenty pounds or make your first sale online." — universalizes the coaching pattern; sets up the mechanic reveal in a follow-up post.
- "You didn't fail the course. You failed the same way my gym clients failed for eighteen years: no cue, no plan for the bad day, no one checking if you actually did the thing." — targets buyers who already own a course and stalled.
- "Progressive overload works because you never ask a beginner to lift the max weight on day one. Every side hustle course skips that rule completely and wonders why 95% of buyers quit by week three." — bridges to Angle C mechanic without stating income figures.
- "I turned away clients who weren't ready. Real coaching means telling someone the truth before they hand you money, not after. Most people selling online courses have never said no to anyone." — integrity-screen crossover (Angle G) while staying rooted in Angle A.

### Solution-aware
- "The alignment gap is not a knowledge gap. You already know the ten things you're supposed to do today. The system that gets you to actually do them is the entire ballgame, and nobody selling courses is teaching that system." — uses the cleared "alignment gap" asset; sets up the toolkit/DWA mechanic in the next line.
- "Behavior-change coaching taught me one rule that applies here too: the plan matters less than what happens on the day you don't feel like doing the plan. That's the whole game with a side hustle. Same as the gym." — pair with the concrete money mechanic in the accompanying paragraph (Hard Rule 5).
- "I built an AI toolkit that does not make anyone money. It does the one thing eighteen years of coaching taught me actually matters: it removes the excuse you were about to reach for at 9pm tonight." — anti-hype AI framing, no income claim, honest limitation stated upfront.

### Product-aware
- "Every client I've coached who actually changed had the same morning: they did the small unimpressive thing before they felt ready. Today's small unimpressive thing is one post and three replies. That's it. That's the whole session." — for a warmer audience already aware of the offer; drives toward the daily 30-minute mechanic without hype.

### Problem-aware (bonus, ties to Angle E)
- "A client once cried in my gym not because the workout was hard, but because it was the first thing in years he'd finished. That feeling is the entire reason your first dollar online matters more than your fifth thousand." — ties to the belief-unlock/first-$1,000 mechanic; dramatizes a client's feeling, never Farrice's private family history (privacy boundary honored).

## Angle B — Stay-at-Home Dad, Not a Guru

Belief: you don't need a nap-time-mom schedule or a bro-with-a-Lambo hustle. A dad with 30 focused minutes counts. Texture, never the narrative arc (per frame verdict).

### Unaware
- "My son naps for exactly forty-seven minutes on a good day. Today I built an entire side project inside that window." — cold scroll-stopper, pure scene, no product mention.
- "Every 'work from home' post I scroll past is a mom with a smoothie and a laptop on a Tuesday morning. I'm a dad with a baby monitor clipped to his belt and thirty minutes on the clock." — white-space hook, names the SAHD absence without arguing for it.
- "The tantrum started at 1:47. My work day ended at 1:48." — shortest, most scene-forward hook; show-don't-tell in two clauses.

### Problem-aware
- "I coached people through changing their bodies for eighteen years and never once told a client to 'find two extra hours a day.' You don't have two hours. Neither do I." — pairs credential with the reader's actual constraint.
- "I don't have a home office. I have a hallway, a baby monitor, and thirty minutes before the nap ends." — contrasts SAHD reality against aspirational "desk setup" content.
- "Every side hustle guide I've read assumes I have a free evening. I get one half hour, once, if the nap holds." — reframes "not enough time" as a design flaw in the advice, not in the reader.

### Solution-aware
- "Stay-at-home dad. Thirty minutes a day, most days interrupted twice. That's the whole operation, and it still moves." — bridges into proof-of-process; sets up the daily 30-min split.
- "I run this business between one nap and one tantrum, and I've stopped apologizing for how small that window is." — confession-pivot; anti-guru stance: small window, real output, no hustle-speak.

### Product-aware
- "Forty thousand people are selling the exact course I bought. None of them built their version of it strapped to a toddler." — names the saturation directly per the ground-truth doc; turns SAHD identity into differentiator, not excuse.
- "Link's in this post. Affiliate link, I earn a commission if you buy through it, no extra cost to you. That part I'll say straight, because most people selling this course won't." — disclosure lands inside the hook itself (Compliance Rule 3); pairs honesty posture with the link mechanic in the same breath.

### Reply-openers (no pitch, no link — borrow reach only)
- On SAHM/side-hustle posts: "Stay-at-home dad here too. The math changes when your free time is a toddler's nap length, not a calendar block."
- On "quit my 9-5" posts: "The hard rep isn't quitting the job. It's showing up on the Tuesday three weeks later when nothing's working yet and no boss is making you clock in."
- On "I'm not consistent enough" posts: "Consistency isn't a personality trait, it's a schedule problem. Eight years of coaching taught me the people who 'lack discipline' almost always just picked a system too big for their actual day."

## Angle C — Progressive Overload for Income

Belief: stop trying to deadlift $5K/month cold — run a beginner program.

### Unaware
- "First week with a new lifter, I never touch the bar they think they're ready for. I put forty-five pounds on it and make them own that weight before we talk about anything heavier." — pure gym scene, no explicit money bridge yet.
- "Eight years of coaching taught me the injury never happens on the heavy lift. It happens on the lift right before it, the one somebody rushed because the heavy one looked close." — oblique, coach-voice-forward; works as a cold scroll-stopper before the reader knows it's about money.
- "Progressive overload only works because the body doesn't know the difference between too much and too soon. Neither does your motivation when the first month of a side hustle asks for more than a beginner program should." — most conceptual of the set, good for readers already nodding along to "start small" advice but missing the mechanism.
- "I coach people through the exact moment their form breaks down under too much weight too soon. It looks identical whether the load is a barbell or a bank account." — quiet, coach-across-the-table register.

### Problem-aware
- "Every side hustle I've watched implode had the same warm-up: zero. Straight to the heaviest plate they own, first day, no working sets."
- "A deload week isn't quitting. It's the only reason the next block of training works. I don't know why nobody applies that to a side hustle that's about to get abandoned in week two."
- "The bar that gets someone hurt isn't heavy. It's unfamiliar and heavy at the same time. Every dollar goal I watch people set for month one is both."
- "Nobody grabs a barbell for the first time and loads four plates a side. That's exactly how most people approach their first month trying to make money online, and it's why they quit by week three." — statement hook naming the exact failure timeline.

### Solution-aware
- "You don't program a beginner's first month around their goal lift. You program it around the weight that won't break them. Nobody selling online income teaches that part."
- "The bar doesn't care how bad you want the goal weight. Add plates faster than your body adapts and something tears. Income works on the same principle and almost nobody trains for it that way."

### Product-aware
- "I've never programmed a client's first session around the number they wanted. I programmed it around the number they could actually recover from. Somebody selling you a course should be doing the same math with your first month of income." — speaks to someone who already bought a course and is stalled; no course named, no income claim.
- "The heaviest weight in the gym isn't dangerous by itself. It's dangerous the day someone who's never trained decides that's where they start. Same rule, different bar, when the goal is your first $1,000." — ties to the $1,000 belief-unlock beachhead; references a target, not an earned figure, so it stays compliant.

### Reply-openers
- On burnout/side-hustle posts: "This is the part nobody in the side hustle space says out loud: the plan doesn't fail, the recovery time does. You added a business on top of zero sleep and called it a discipline problem."
- On failed-attempt/relapse posts: "Coached enough people through habit change to know this pattern by heart. It's never the strategy that quits first. It's the beginner load being wrong from day one."
- On "what course/plan should I follow" posts: "Following this because the actual bottleneck in month one is never information. It's that nobody programs a beginner's first thirty days like a beginner. Every plan in this space is written for someone six months in."
- On dad/parenting-time posts: "Stay-at-home dad here, same nap-window math you're describing. The thing that changed for me wasn't finding more time, it was stopping the habit of restarting from zero every time the week got messy."
- On generic motivation/discipline posts: "Real answer from someone who coaches behavior change for a living: motivation isn't the variable that predicts who follows through. Recovery capacity is. Most side hustle advice ignores that completely."

## Angle E — First $1,000 = Belief Unlock (pinned offer post)

Belief: the first grand won't change your life — the proof it gives your brain will. **This is the pinned post carrying the link + disclosure.** Every instance below must weld the insight to the concrete mechanic (DWA link / toolkit) in the same piece — never leave it as pure introspection (Hard Rule 5, and research counter-read #2).

### Unaware
- "Eighteen years coaching people through body transformations taught me exactly why side hustles fail, and it has nothing to do with the hustle." — opens on credential-as-scene, not status claim.
- "The reason you've bought four courses and finished none of them isn't discipline. It's a gap nobody ever named for you." — sets up the alignment-gap asset.
- "I put my son down for his nap at 1pm and spent the next thirty minutes doing the exact thing I used to coach gym clients through." — naptime-build asset as scene-open, no income figure.

### Problem-aware
- "Week two is where every side hustle I've watched actually die, not week one, and not from lack of effort." — follow with the concrete first-$1,000 mechanic, not just the diagnosis.
- "You know exactly what to do. You just can't seem to do it. I've heard that sentence a thousand times in a gym, and I hear it in DMs now too." — direct use of the documented spine line.
- "Nobody messages me about their side hustle strategy. They message me about the 11pm scroll where they wonder why they haven't started yet."
- "The three courses sitting unopened on your phone were never the problem." — single-statement hook, avoids the banned twin-sentence contrast form.

### Solution-aware
- "Everyone chasing $5,000 a month skips the one rep that actually matters: the first $1,000." — names the beachhead directly; $5K is the destination the niche talks about, not a claimed earning.
- "In a gym, nobody loads the bar to their max on day one. Somehow in side hustles, everyone tries." — native progressive-overload metaphor.
- "The first $1,000 will not change your life. It changes whether your brain believes the second one is coming." — extension of the anchor's approved seed hook; describes a hypothetical psychological effect, not a promise or a personal earnings claim.

### Product-aware
- "$1,000 is the number every guru skips on the way to the screenshot of $10,000." — positions against screenshot culture without showing one; body copy must carry the disclosure at the link.
- "Forget the finish line for a second. Nobody talks about what happens in your head the day the number goes from zero to something real." — closes the belief-unlock frame; pair immediately with the DWA + toolkit mechanic, never leave as pure introspection.

### Reply-openers
- On side-hustle/dad-finance posts: "This is the exact reason most side hustles die in week two, not the strategy, the follow-through. Worth separating those two problems before you pick a new one."
- Credential-led: "As someone who coached behavior change for eighteen years before I touched a side hustle: the plan was never the hard part for most people. Showing up on day 11 was."
- SAHD identity: "Stay-at-home dad here, thirty minutes between naps and tantrums most days. The thing that's kept me consistent isn't motivation, it's making the bar embarrassingly low on the hard days."
- On scaling-too-fast posts: "The $5,000 goal is doing you a disservice right now. Nobody hits that number before they've proven the first $1,000 is even possible for them."
- Generic side-hustle struggle: "This tracks with everything I saw coaching gym clients: people don't quit because the program is wrong, they quit because nobody built in a small enough win early."
- Closing on a statement, not a cheap question: "Following this because the discipline gap you're naming is the same one I watched for eighteen years on a gym floor. That gap is trainable, most people just never get taught how."

## Angle G — The Coach Who Tells You Not to Buy (integrity screen)

Belief: a real coach disqualifies people; a hustler sells everyone. Strongest available construction against the "guru with a face" risk (credential + disqualify + zero income claims, all three present).

### Unaware
- "I told a client to leave my gym once. He had cash in hand and everything." — pure scene, no product mention, earns the read before the pivot.
- "The best coaches I've worked with all had the same bad habit: turning away money." — statement hook, sets up the integrity-screen belief before any offer context.

### Problem-aware
- "You've bought the course, the templates, the calendar app. None of it touched the actual problem." — speaks to someone who's already tried and failed.
- "Every program you've quit died in the same week. Week two. That's not coincidence, that's a pattern I've coached out of people for eighteen years." — one comma-joined sentence, avoids the twin-reveal template.
- "Nobody ever quit a plan because the plan was bad. They quit because nobody built the part that catches them in week two." — behavior-change reframe, lead-in to Angle A.

### Solution-aware
- "A guy DMed me this morning wanting the fast version of this. I told him to close the tab." — signals disqualification without naming DWA yet.
- "Forty thousand people are selling the exact same course right now. Ask any of them what happens in your week two and watch them change the subject." — uses the LIKELY 40,000-affiliate saturation stat as social proof of the white space; pivots to the behavioral gap nobody else addresses.
- "You can spot the real coach in any comment section. They're the one telling half the room not to buy." — closest extension of the seed hook.

### Product-aware
- "The reviews calling this course a scam are onto something, just not the part they think. The product's fine. Most buyers were never going to make it work, no matter who sold it to them." — names the scam-review controversy directly, before the reader does; three plain statements, not a twin-reveal.
- "I watched every 'exposed' video on this course before I ever posted about it. Most of what they got right had nothing to do with the product." — no income claim, no defense of the product's earnings potential, addresses only the credibility gap.

### Reply-openers (genuine value, no link, no pitch)
- "This is the exact pattern I saw with clients who plateaued for years: the goal was too big to trigger a daily action. Shrink the target until it fits the 10 minutes you actually have, not the 2 hours you wish you had."
- "Coached behavior change for almost two decades. The number one killer isn't motivation, it's that the first rep is never small enough. What's the smallest version of this you could do today that takes under 5 minutes?" — ends on a genuine question directed at the ORIGINAL POSTER's content, not a cheap sign-off on Farrice's own piece.
- "Following because this is the actual problem, not the one everyone talks about. You don't have a discipline problem. You have a plan-requires-too-much-willpower-at-9pm problem. Redesign the environment, not the person."
- "Stay-at-home dad here, 30 minutes between naps and tantrums is my whole runway most days. What's actually moved the needle: doing the smallest possible version of the task the second I sit down, before I can think my way out of it."
- "This reads like a habit-stacking problem more than a discipline problem. Attach the new thing to something you already do on autopilot and the remembering-to-do-it problem mostly disappears."

## Usage notes (binding)

- **Em-dash budget:** max 2 per finished piece — count across the whole post/thread, not per hook.
- **Never publish a reply-opener as a standalone original post.** They're built with zero product mention and no mechanic pairing by design; running them as a first-touch post reintroduces the "introspection alone" failure mode Hard Rule 5 exists to prevent.
- **Every original post that leads with pure behavioral insight (Angle A/C unaware and problem-aware tiers) must be paired, in the same piece, with a concrete money/action mechanic** — either directly in that post or in the immediately-attached thread continuation. Never let an insight stand alone in the piece that carries maximum feed exposure (post 1).
- **UNCONFIRMED items never get asserted as fact in a hook**, even a good one: session-only attribution mechanics, Fanbasis payout timing, and Threads non-throttling all stay hedged until verified in the portal (see `00-ground-truth-compliance-and-offer.md`).
