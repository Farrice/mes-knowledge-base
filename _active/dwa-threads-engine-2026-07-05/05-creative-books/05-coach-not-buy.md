## Positioning line
Farrice is the credentialed behavior-change coach who tells DWA buyers why they'll quit in week two and screens out the ones who won't do the work — not another face chasing money in public.

---

## THE HOOK (line 1)
> I told a client to leave my gym once. He had cash in hand and everything.

**Why this one, over the other 15 candidates in the bank:** it's pure scene, no product, no coaching-lecture tone — it earns the read the way a friend earns your attention at a bar before he tells you the point of the story. Every other angle-G hook in the bank either states the belief upfront ("The best coaches... turning away money") or needs a beat of setup ("You can spot the real coach..."). This one just happens, cold open, and the reader has to keep reading to find out why a coach would refuse cash. That curiosity gap is what carries them into the disqualify pivot in post 2 instead of them bouncing at the first sign of a pitch.

## FULL POST (ready to paste — 4-part Threads chain)

### Post 1 (root — the hook)
```
I told a client to leave my gym once. He had cash in hand and everything.

He'd bought three programs in two years, quit every one around week two. He figured handing me the money was the commitment. I gave it back and told him to come find me when he had thirty honest minutes a day, not before.

Eighteen years of making that call: people ready to buy something new and people ready to actually do something new are two different groups. The second one is smaller.
```

### Post 2 (reply — the pivot + the screen)
```
I'm running that exact screen on the program I put my name behind, Digital Wealth Academy. Want money that shows up while you sleep? Close this. Can't give thirty honest minutes a day for a few months? Close this too. That's roughly what I get most days, between naps and everything else. I'm not asking you for more than I already give it.
```

### Post 3 (reply — the mechanic, behavior insight paired with the concrete tool)
```
Next to that course, I built a toolkit for the three things that actually kill week two: the blank page, the tech that eats an hour before you've typed a word, and not knowing what today's ten minutes should even be. It doesn't do the work for you, it just removes your excuses for stopping.
```

### Post 4 (reply — same-session path + close)
```
Buy through the affiliate link in my pinned post (I earn a commission, you pay nothing extra), DM me the receipt, and the toolkit's yours. And yeah, it's the same course a lot of people online have loud opinions about. Look them up, then look at who's actually telling you not to buy it unless you'll do the work.

If you've quit three of these already, I probably know why. The part that was supposed to catch you in week two was never built. So I built it.
```

## Why the behavior insight is never introspection alone
Post 2 delivers the belief (readiness-to-buy vs. readiness-to-act are different populations — the disqualify posture that IS the trust signal per the research doc's white-space map). Post 3 immediately converts that belief into a named, concrete mechanic: the toolkit that removes three specific frictions (blank page / tech overwhelm / no next step). The reader never sits in pure feeling — every behavioral claim lands next to something they can actually go do. This satisfies the ground-truth counter-read #2 mitigation directly (behavior frame pairs with a money/action mechanic in the same piece, never therapy alone).

## SAHD identity — texture, not arc
One clause only, inside the disqualify beat in post 2: "that's roughly what I get most days too, between naps and everything else." It does the differentiation work (male SAHD in a SAHM-coded niche) without becoming the story — the story is the integrity screen, the dad life is proof he's not asking for more than he gives.

## Visual / on-camera direction (shoot in the naptime window)
Format is photo + text, not video — this angle's power is the words doing the disqualifying, a talking-head risks undercutting the "quiet, not performing" register. Shoot ONE still:
- Kitchen table or the corner of the gym floor he coaches out of, natural window light, no ring light.
- Sitting, forearms on the table or crossed, steady/direct look into the lens — not smiling, not stern, just present. This is "the coach who just said no to your money," not "the guy hyping you up."
- No text overlay, no branded graphic, no gym-selfie flex. Plain. The photo's job is to make the words in the post feel like they came from a specific, real, tired-but-steady person — not a template account.
- Shoot during the actual naptime window so the environment (mess in frame, a small toy visible, the actual light at that hour) does the "this is real life" work without saying it.

## Same-session buy path
No link in this chain. Reader reaction → profile visit → pinned offer post (angle E, "First $1,000 = the Belief Unlock") carries the affiliate link + full disclosure + receipt-DM mechanic. This post's only job is trust + disqualify; the pinned post's job is convert. Post 4's parenthetical ("I earn a commission, you pay nothing extra") pre-disloses honestly even though no live link sits in this chain, consistent with the brand's transparency-as-moat stance.

Passes the coffee test: this is a story Farrice would actually tell a friend before getting to the point.
