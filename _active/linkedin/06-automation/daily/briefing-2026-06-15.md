## 6. Draft Post (voice-gated, paste-ready)

```
The people best at AI are the most afraid of it.

That's not a guess. Anthropic surveyed 81,000 people on how they actually use AI at work. The pattern that fell out was almost cruel: the workers getting the biggest speed gains were also the ones most sure they were about to be replaced.

Read it the usual way and it sounds like doom. The tool is so good it is coming for the people who use it best.

I read it the other way around, after a long career watching people misread their own fear before I ever touched any of this.

Fear like that measures how much of yourself you handed the tool.

Use AI to replace your thinking, and every good result is quiet proof you were not needed. Of course it scares you. You built the thing that does the part you thought was you.

Use it to extend your thinking, and the same good result feels like reach. The judgment stayed yours. The tool just carried it past where your hands could reach alone.

Same software, opposite feeling, and the whole difference was set before either of you started typing.

So if working faster keeps leaving you more afraid, the question was never whether the tool got too good. It is which half of the job you quietly stopped doing.

Put the part only you can do back at the front of the work, and the fear runs out of things to hold onto.
```
