# Your content got cheap. Your humanity just got expensive.
*Engine: /writers-room (faithful 3-layer: diagnose → compress → heartbeat → platform/voice, 9 expert lenses). NOTE: flagged by the voice gate — see gate results.*

A client read her own work back to herself last month, stopped, and asked who actually wrote it.

She runs content for three wellness brands, and she didn't have a clean answer. Forty posts deep, the voice had gone smooth in that faintly-too-polished way every brand now wears, and somewhere in there she'd stopped being able to tell where she ended and the tool began. Her content had gotten cheaper to make. That was the whole problem. When words cost almost nothing, sounding like everyone else is the most expensive mistake you can make.

That's the enemy, and it isn't AI. It's the average. AI didn't lower the value of a real, accountable human standing behind a claim. It made everything around that human cheaper, which leaves the human as the only scarce thing you still own.

Two things happened to your brand in the last eighteen months, and they run in opposite directions. Making content got close to free. Being believed got harder than it has ever been. Most founders only felt the first one, and quietly hollowed out the half that was still worth money.

There's a more useful way to see it. You are not running one funnel. You are running two, and AI is doing the opposite thing to each.

**The top of the funnel, discovery, now belongs to the machines.**

Someone wants to know what to take, what to buy, who to trust. They don't open ten tabs anymore. They ask a model and take the answer it hands back. That answer is the new shelf, and off it you don't exist, however good the product is.

This month gave the cleanest proof yet. On June 19, 5W published its Pharma AI Visibility Index. U.S. pharma spent an estimated eight billion dollars on direct-to-consumer advertising last year, the largest ad commitment of any category in the country. Ask an AI engine about a treatment, though, and it names Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk for reasons that have almost nothing to do with who spent the most.

We already watched the same physics in supplements. Earlier this year the same firm pushed 3,800 category questions through ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI answers. Four brands took more than 47% of the citations it tracked. AG1, reportedly the largest greens brand by revenue, came in third. What earned the shelf in both categories was the same: substance a machine could quote, dosing and mechanism and a claim it could repeat without hedging. Spend can't buy that shelf. Two of the highest-spending markets in the country just proved it on the record.

Fight this half with human craft and you lose. Feed it clean, quotable substance and you win. The brutal part is that reach and polish and ten more variants do almost nothing here, because the buyer got intercepted upstream by a model that never saw your homepage.

**The bottom of the funnel, the decision to trust you, runs on the opposite fuel.**

Your buyer arrives having read AI-written everything, and skepticism is their default setting. Ogilvy named this in January: the authenticity recession. As the machines flood the world with scalable perfection, visible human imperfection becomes the premium. This half rewards exactly what the top punishes. A real face. A named practitioner. A claim with a reputation attached.

Here's the part nobody says on the QBR. The fear was never that the product was replaceable. It was that you were. You watched a model write a competent post in nine seconds and felt the floor move, so you did the rational thing and made more, more volume, more variants, more proof that you were still useful, and every one of them sounded a little more like the machine and a little less like you. The machine didn't take your edge. It made it cheap to fake the thing that was never your edge in the first place.

So the scarce thing moved while everyone was watching the cost line. It used to be expensive to produce content and cheap to be a specific human. Now it's the reverse. The work you were proudest of, the angle you could smell before the data confirmed it, the take only you would have had, is the exact thing the machine is built to flatten. Which is also why it's the only thing left with pricing power. Taste didn't stop mattering. It became the part of the funnel nobody else can prompt.

The founders losing money optimized the wrong half. They poured their hours into the discovery layer the machines already flattened, then automated the trust layer, the one thing a machine cannot fake. They worked hardest at making themselves more replaceable.

Here is this week's tangible asset. Run it on your own brand, or a client's, in about twenty minutes.

### The Funnel Bifurcation Audit

**Part 1. Top of funnel: are you AI-visible?**
Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI mode. Paste your real category questions, one at a time: best [category] and why; is [product type] worth it and which brands are trusted; your customer's situation and what to consider; compare the top [category] brands; what the research says about your key ingredient. Score each: Does your brand appear at all? Is what it says accurate? Does it cite your actual proof, or someone else's opinion of you?

*If you're absent or misrepresented, the problem is rarely the product. Your proof lives in formats a machine can't parse, a PDF, an image, a paragraph of brand voice. Publish your dosing, mechanism, and study data as clean, quotable text. Boring to a designer, catnip to a model.*

**Part 2. Bottom of funnel: is your trust layer visibly human?**
Walk your own buyer's path, first click to checkout. Count the places a stranger could prove a real human stands behind the claim: a named person, not "our team," answering questions; a face on camera making the actual promise; a practitioner with a verifiable reputation; real, specific testimonials, not "Amazing product!!"; a claim a regulator could trace to a person.

**The read:** Tally both halves. A low top-of-funnel score means you're invisible to the machines now doing the recommending. A low bottom-of-funnel score means you get discovered and then disqualified at the door by a buyer who can smell automation. Almost everyone is lopsided, and the lopsidedness is the strategy you've been running by accident.
