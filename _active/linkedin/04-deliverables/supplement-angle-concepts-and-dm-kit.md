# Supplement Angle Concepts (×4) + DM Kit — The Control-Beater Sales System

*2026-08-28. Grounded in the research: problem-callout hooks appear in 4.2% of supplement ads but hit long-runner status 44% of the time (Evolut, 500-ad study); single-image statics = 40% of top performers; all claim language screened against published Meta health policy + FTC guidance ("supports/promotes," never "treats/cures/prevents/eliminates"). Reskin rule: each concept adapts to any brand in ~10 minutes — swap product, palette, and the one bracketed claim line.*

---

## Angle 1 — "The 3PM Crash" (energy/pre-workout/greens/B-complex)

**Problem-callout hook**: "It's 3pm and you're negotiating with yourself about the gym again."
**Static copy**:
- Headline: THE 3PM NEGOTIATION
- Body: You didn't lose motivation. You lost blood sugar stability four hours ago. [Product] supports steady energy through the afternoon — so the 6pm workout conversation never happens.
- CTA: Skip the negotiation →
**Visual brief**: Split frame — left: office desk, 3:07pm on screen, half-dead posture; right: same person mid-set at 6pm. Warm/cool color contrast. Product bottom-right third, small. No before/after body imagery (banned).
**Trainer authority line (optional overlay)**: "18 years of clients told me the same lie at 3pm." — F. Cain, CPT
**Reskin**: swap [Product] + palette + one mechanism phrase (e.g., "B12 + adaptogens" per label).

## Angle 2 — "Underfed, Not Undertrained" (protein/recovery/creatine)

**Problem-callout hook**: "You don't have a training problem. You have an eating problem you're calling a training problem."
**Static copy**:
- Headline: UNDERFED, NOT UNDERTRAINED
- Body: Four sessions a week and no progress usually means one thing — recovery never got fed. [Product] supports muscle recovery so the work you're already doing finally shows up.
- CTA: Feed the work →
**Visual brief**: Single strong image — loaded barbell racked, gym empty, harsh single light. Text-forward layout (typography-first). Product small, corner. Mood: verdict, not hype.
**Reskin**: mechanism line varies by product (protein timing / creatine saturation / sleep support).

## Angle 3 — "The Mechanism Explainer" (any hero-ingredient product: magnesium, ashwagandha, omega-3, probiotics)

**Educational hook**: "Here's what [ingredient] actually does — in one diagram, no miracle promised."
**Static copy**:
- Headline: HOW [INGREDIENT] ACTUALLY WORKS
- Body: 3-step visual chain (e.g., magnesium: intake → nervous-system regulation → supports deeper rest). No outcome claim beyond "supports." Footer: FDA disclaimer line.
- CTA: See the full breakdown →
**Visual brief**: Clean 3-node diagram, brand palette, ingredient macro shot as node one. This is the compliance flex — it LOOKS like the ad legal would approve, because it is.
**Why it sells the brand**: educational mechanism angles are the named compliant path that scales ("hooks around mechanisms, not miracles").
**Reskin**: swap ingredient + 3 nodes from the brand's own label claims.

## Angle 4 — "The Drawer" (adherence angle — any subscription product)

**Problem-callout hook**: "Somewhere in your kitchen is a drawer full of half-finished supplements."
**Static copy**:
- Headline: THE DRAWER OF GOOD INTENTIONS
- Body: The problem was never the product. It was the routine it never attached to. [Product] is built for the habit you already have — [one-line ritual fit: morning coffee / gym bag / bedside]. 
- CTA: The last one you'll abandon →
**Visual brief**: Overhead shot of an open drawer with 6-7 assorted half-used tubs (generic/unbranded), one branded product set apart on clean counter above. Honest, wry tone — the anti-hype ad supplement buyers screenshot.
**Reskin**: ritual line + product placement.

**Production notes (all 4)**: statics first (1:1 + 4:5 crops), AI stack for imagery/comps, no photoreal AI humans, no before/afters, no disease words, FDA disclaimer where a structure/function claim appears. Ship at 70% polish — these open conversations.
