## B. LinkedIn Long-Form #1 — "Who actually wrote this?"
*Angle: voice drift → funnel bifurcation. Strongest emotional hook. Run first.*

---

Who actually wrote this?

A friend got asked that by a client last month. She runs content for three wellness brands. She didn't have a clean answer, and the pause told the client everything.

The trust didn't break in that meeting. It had been breaking quietly for months, across forty posts that were 85% her and 15% nobody. That smooth, competent, faintly-too-polished voice every brand now shares.

Her content got cheaper to make. That was the whole problem. When content costs almost nothing to produce, looking like everyone else costs more than it ever did.

I keep saying this to founders in wellness and performance, and most don't want to hear it: you are running two funnels now, and AI is doing the opposite thing to each one.

At the top, discovery, the machines took over. Someone asks ChatGPT "what magnesium should I take," and an AI writes the answer. A 5W study run between January and April 2026 put 3,800 supplement prompts through the major models. Four brands took almost half the citations it tracked. AG1, reportedly the largest greens brand by revenue, landed third. The brands that won were the ones publishing proof a machine could actually read, budget be damned.

At the bottom, the moment someone decides to trust you with their body and their money, the opposite happened. Trust got expensive. Your buyer shows up having read AI-written everything, and they can smell it by the second line. The same tools that get you found make your prospect more suspicious by the time they reach you.

Now watch where the money leaks out.

Founders watched content get cheap and bought more of it. Daily posting, ten variations, "we've doubled our output." They poured effort into the half of the funnel AI already flattened, then quietly automated the one thing AI cannot fake: visible proof that a real person stands behind the claim.

The brands pulling ahead did it backwards. They let AI flood the top, where being machine-readable is the entire game. Then they spent the hours they saved making the bottom of the funnel unmistakably human. A named coach who answers her own messages. A face on camera. A claim with someone's actual reputation riding on it.

The cheaper your content gets, the more your humanity is worth. Most people are selling theirs at a discount without realizing the price went up.

So the question was never whether to use AI in your wellness brand. You already do. The real question is which half of your funnel you're hollowing out to save a few hours.

Run the test this week. Ask an AI model your top ten category questions and watch whether your brand shows up at all. Then read your last ten posts and count how many a stranger could prove a human made.

One of those numbers is too low. You already know which one, and you've known for a while.

## C. LinkedIn Long-Form #2 — "You're losing to not being cited"
*Angle: AI Visibility / the new shelf. Strongest authority signal. Run second.*

---

Your wellness brand is losing sales to a competitor you have never benchmarked against. It's the AI that answers before anyone reaches your site.

Watch how your customer actually shops now. They don't open ten tabs and compare. They ask ChatGPT or Perplexity, "is this category worth it, and which brand should I get," and they take the answer the machine hands them. That answer is the new shelf. If you're not on it, you don't exist, however good the product is.

A 5W study run between January and April 2026 tested 3,800 supplement prompts across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI overviews. Four brands captured more than 47% of the observed citation share. The category's largest greens brand by revenue came in third. What earned the shelf was structured, machine-readable proof: dosing, mechanism, the kind of claim a model can quote without hedging.

Most founders are still polishing a homepage for a shopper who never arrives. That shopper got intercepted upstream by a model that never saw the homepage, only the data it could parse.

Premium brands get hit hardest. You can be measurably better than everyone in your category and still be invisible, because a machine cannot read your superiority off a lifestyle photo and a founder origin story. If your edge isn't written somewhere a model can quote it, the model has no way to repeat it.

There's a second front. These models assemble their "is it worth it" verdicts substantially from community threads, Reddit most of all. So the skeptical buyer who trusts the AI is being handed a verdict built partly from strangers and partly from your own under-structured content, presented as neutral truth.

This is fixable, and most of your competitors haven't noticed yet. That gap is the window.

Two moves before the end of the month. Ask each major model your top twenty category questions and write down whether you appear and what it says about you. Then check whether your real proof, the studies and dosing and mechanism, lives anywhere a machine can read it, or whether it's trapped inside a PDF and a beautiful paragraph.

The brands that win the next two years are simply the ones the machine can read.

**The cheaper your content gets, the more your humanity costs**

Two things happened to your business in the last eighteen months, and they pull in opposite directions.

Producing content got close to free. And being believed got harder than it has ever been.

Most founders only noticed the first one. They saw the cost of a post drop to nothing, and they did the rational thing: they made more. More posts, more variants, more "we're shipping daily now." It felt like progress. It was actually the slow erosion of the only thing they couldn't replace.

No dashboard tracks the thing that actually moved. In a trust-based category like wellness, the value of your content was never the words. It was the proof that a specific, accountable human stood behind them. AI didn't lower the value of that proof. It made everything around it cheaper, which means the proof is now the only scarce thing you own.

I think about your business as two funnels.

**The top of funnel, discovery, now belongs to the machines.** When someone asks an AI "what should I take, what should I buy, who should I trust," a model writes the answer. A 5W study run between January and April 2026 put 3,800 supplement prompts through the major models and found four brands took almost half the citations it tracked. The category's largest greens brand by revenue came in third. The winners published proof a machine could read. This half of your funnel rewards being legible, structured, machine-quotable. Fight it with human craft and you lose. Feed it with clean data and you win.

**The bottom of funnel, the decision to trust you, runs on the opposite fuel.** Your buyer arrives having read AI-written everything, and skepticism is their default setting. Ogilvy named this the "authenticity recession": as AI floods the world with scalable perfection, visible human imperfection becomes the premium. This half of your funnel rewards exactly what the top punishes. A real face. A named practitioner. A claim with a reputation attached.

The founders losing money optimized the wrong half. They automated the trust layer to save time and flooded the discovery layer with content the machines ignore. They worked hard to weaken themselves at both ends.

So here is this week's tangible asset. Run it on your own brand, or on a client's, in about twenty minutes.

---

### 🔧 The Funnel Bifurcation Audit

**Part 1 — Top of funnel: are you AI-visible?**

Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI mode. Paste your real category questions, one at a time:

```
1. "What's the best [your category] and why?"
2. "Is [your product type] worth it? Which brands are trusted?"
3. "I'm [your customer's situation] — what [category] should I consider?"
4. "Compare the top [category] brands."
5. "What does the research say about [your key ingredient/mechanism]?"
```

Score each answer:
- ☐ Does your brand appear at all?
- ☐ Is what it says accurate?
- ☐ Does it cite your actual proof, or someone else's opinion of you?

*Commentary: If you're absent or misrepresented, the problem is rarely the product. It's that your proof lives in formats a machine can't parse: a PDF, an image, a paragraph of brand voice. The fix is to publish your dosing, mechanism, and study data as clean, structured, quotable text. Boring to a designer. Catnip to a model.*

**Part 2 — Bottom of funnel: is your trust layer visibly human?**

Walk your own buyer's path, from first click to checkout. Count the places a stranger could prove a real human stands behind the claim:

- ☐ A named person, not "our team," answering questions
- ☐ A face on camera making the actual promise
- ☐ A practitioner or expert with a verifiable reputation attached
- ☐ Real, specific testimonials (not "Amazing product!!")
- ☐ A claim a regulator could trace to a person

*Commentary: Most wellness brands score high on Part 1 effort and low on Part 2 reality, or the reverse. The number you want is balanced, with the human signals concentrated exactly where money changes hands.*

**The read:** Tally both halves. If your top-of-funnel score is low, you're invisible to the machines now doing the recommending. If your bottom-of-funnel score is low, you're getting discovered and then disqualified at the door by a buyer who can smell automation. Almost everyone is lopsided. The lopsidedness is the strategy you've been running by accident.
