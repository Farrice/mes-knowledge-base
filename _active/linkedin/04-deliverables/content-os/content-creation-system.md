## 1. THE SIGNATURE TEMPLATE — "The Physiology Misconception Correction"

This is the one post Farrice writes more than any other. It is, in a single shape, the edutainment engine, the aha engine, AND the AI-citation engine — and only an 18-year credentialed body-expert can write it. Run it ~3x/week. It carries both signature pillars and volume pillars without ever reading like commodity myth-busting, because the correction is delivered from inside the mechanism, not from a list.

### The 5-Layer Stack

**Layer 1 — STAKES OPENER (Kallaway's Stakes-as-Housing).** Open inside a scene where a real person did everything right and the floor moved anyway. Character + something at risk + urgency. Not life-or-death — *personally relevant to the reader's body or budget.* The scene IS the curriculum (Kobi: teach through the anomaly, not the lecture). Turn the prediction engine on before you teach anything.

**Layer 2 — BIG QUESTION (Kallaway's specificity calibration).** Load the curiosity gap. Specific enough that the reader forms a prediction, not specific enough that they can guess the answer. Front-load it — the earlier it lands, the longer the drip runs. This is the "see more" engine.

**Layer 3 — HEAD-FAKE / ACCOMMODATION SURPRISE (Kallaway × McRaney, the aha).** The corrected mechanism IS the head fake. The reader's current model genuinely can't explain the anomaly from Layer 1. You reveal the mechanism — unexpected but immediately logical. The test is McRaney's accommodation gate firing at once: **surprise** (their model breaks), **relevance** (it's their body/budget), **safety** (updating costs them no face). Response must be "Oh!" not "Huh?" If it's "yeah I knew that," you wrote assimilation — redesign for surprise. This is the undiluted physiology payload (Kobi's Vegetable Smuggle): the entertainment got them in the door; the real mechanism is the cargo, never watered down.

**Layer 4 — FACE-SAVING BELIEF SHIFT (McRaney's Face-Saving Narrative).** Give the reader a story they can tell themselves about why they believed the old thing — one that preserves dignity. The shape is "I used to think X. Then I realized Y. Now I understand Z." The pivot must sound like discovery, not being wrong. Build it so the reader sounds *smarter for having changed.* Use first person OR put the reader in it ("If you've ever..."); never lecture them into it.

**Layer 5 — RESTRAINT CUT CLOSE (Kobi's Restraint Cut + recognition close).** Do the honest version once and stop. No mic-drop, no milking, no cheap question. Hand the reader a tangible GET (Cole), then close on recognition — an image, a declaration, or a bookend back to the opening scene. Soft-CTA only ~1-in-5 (Section 6); default = none.

### Fully-Written Walk-Through — EVERGREEN MODE (correct a held myth)

*Pillar #1 (Supplement & Ingredient Truth), carried with the claim-safety overlay. Hook = Dense format, 152 chars.*

> She took magnesium every night for four months because three doctors and her sleep tracker all told her to. Her deep sleep never moved a single minute.
>
> Same dose. Same brand the whole time. So why did the most-recommended sleep supplement on earth do nothing for her?
>
> Because "magnesium" on a label is not a molecule. It's a marketing word.
>
> The bottle she bought was magnesium oxide. It's cheap, it's legal, and the body absorbs roughly 4% of it. The other 96% never reaches her bloodstream, let alone her brain. She wasn't supplementing sleep. She was supplementing her toilet.
>
> The form that crosses into the nervous system is magnesium glycinate or threonate. Different molecule, different price, different result. The label is allowed to print "magnesium" on both because the law cares about the element, not the form your cells can actually use. The claim is true. The product is useless. Both things at once.
>
> I spent the first decade of my career telling clients "take magnesium" and quietly wondering why half of them felt nothing. I thought they were non-responders. Then I started reading the certificate of analysis instead of the front of the bottle. Now I don't recommend a mineral. I recommend a form.
>
> Flip your bottle over tonight. If it says oxide, citrate, or just "magnesium," you're paying for the cheapest version that exists. Glycinate is the one your brain was promised.
>
> Four months of doing everything right, undone by one word the label was legally allowed to hide.

*Why it works: L1 puts a competent person on the failing floor (did everything right, nothing moved). L2 is predictable-not-guessable — you guess "wrong dose? tolerance?" and you're wrong. L3 is the head fake: "magnesium is a marketing word, not a molecule" — surprise + immediately logical, the absorption clue was always there. The claim-safety read ("the claim is true, the product is useless, both at once") is the moat overlay only Farrice can run. L4 is face-saving in first person — Farrice was wrong, so the reader doesn't have to feel dumb. L5 hands a tangible GET (flip the bottle, look for the form) and bookends to the opening scene. Zero em dashes in the hook, two in the body. No cheap question close.*

Oxide is the version your body absorbs about 4% of.

It's doing exactly what a system trained on marketing copy does: it reads the front of the bottle, where "magnesium" is a true word, and it never reads the absorption data, where the form is the only thing that decides whether the molecule reaches your brain. It inherited the label's blind spot at scale.
>
> I used to think AI search was a threat to credentialed experts like me. Then I watched it confidently recommend a mineral form I'd spend a session talking a client out of. Now I understand the opposite is true: the machine is fluent in claims and illiterate in mechanisms, and mechanism is the one thing 18 years of physiology buys you that a model trained on ad copy can't fake.

*Why it works: entity (ChatGPT) in line 1, riding 41% AI-shopping borrowed attention [VERIFIED]. L2 Big Q is the test setup — reader runs the prediction. ## 2. THE 8 BLEND PLAYS (usable mini-templates)

A niche = Market × Format. The market (physiology) is fixed; the format is the portable Trojan horse. **Play #1 is the spine (~60% of blended posts). Plays #2, #4, #5 are SEASONING — ~1-in-4 posts max, and only when Farrice can fill them with world-class substance.** The rest sit between.

### 1. DUE-DILIGENCE TEARDOWN — *PRIMARY / spine*
**Shape:** Borrow the equity-research / short-seller report. **Thesis → Bull case → Bear case → Verdict → Receipt.** Buyer-native (LinkedIn thinks in ROI and risk) AND the exact GEO-citable shape (stats + expert voice + source = the ~40% visibility lift).
**When:** The default for any "does this claim hold up?" post. Supplements, protocols, devices, longevity stacks.
**1-line example:** *"Due-diligence teardown: the magnesium-glycinate-for-sleep claim. Bull case, bear case, and the one piece of evidence the marketing leaves out."*

### 2. PATCH NOTES FOR YOUR BODY — *seasoning, 1-in-4, gaming-fluent only*
**Shape:** Nerf / buff / meta / S-tier language applied to physiology. The mechanic is universally legible; the credential makes it legitimate instead of cute.
**When:** A genuine shift in the evidence base, framed as a balance update. Only if you actually speak gaming.
**1-line example:** *"Patch notes for your sleep: magnesium glycinate got buffed to S-tier, oxide got quietly removed from the meta, and 'just take magnesium' is now a bannable build."*

### 3. TIER-LIST TEARDOWN
**Shape:** The S-to-F tier list, ranked by a rigorous axis — FTC-survivable evidence, not marketing. Ranking is dopamine-native; the credential legitimizes the format.
**When:** A category with many players and wildly uneven evidence (longevity supps, recovery gadgets, "clinically studied" claims).
**1-line example:** *"Every 'longevity' supplement, S to F, ranked by whether the evidence would survive an FTC substantiation review. Most are not where the marketing thinks they are."*

### 4. PARENTING FIELD-NOTES — *seasoning, 1-in-4, behavior-change payload*
**Shape:** Parasocial-confessional from real parenting, landing behavior-change psychology where B2B operators feel it.
**When:** A behavior-change or mental-game post (Pillar #4) where a domestic scene makes the mechanism land harder than a workplace one.
**1-line example:** *"My toddler taught me why your team won't adopt the new process: you can't reason someone out of a behavior their environment rewards."*

### 5. BOSS-FIGHT ARC — *seasoning, 1-in-4, anime/RPG-fluent only*
**Shape:** Training-arc / mini-boss / villain structure imports earned-progression narrative the dry niche never uses.
**When:** A psychology or recovery post about a process people misread as failure (burnout, plateaus, deloads).
**1-line example:** *"Burnout isn't your final boss. It's the mini-boss that exists to teach you the recovery mechanic before the fight that actually matters."*

### 6. RED-TEAM MY CLAIM
**Shape:** Investigative takedown pointed at yourself or the industry. Publicly perform a scarce capability — stress-test the most-cited claims until they collapse.
**When:** You can genuinely break a popular claim (or survive the attempt). Pairs natively with the AI-error overlay.
**1-line example:** *"I tried to get ChatGPT to defend the five most-cited supplement claims. Three collapsed under one follow-up question. Here's the autopsy."*

### 7. SERIALIZED SCOREBOARD
**Shape:** An ongoing public arc instead of static tips — a 90-day biomarker run, one variable a week, the data IS the post. Scoreboard tension + return-to-watch (steal Nippard's recurring-series Topic Lock).
**When:** You're willing to run something real over weeks and let the result be uncertain.
**1-line example:** *"Day 1 of a public 90-day resting-heart-rate experiment. One variable per week. I don't know how it ends — that's the point. The data is the post."*

### 8. MECHANISM-REVEAL
**Shape:** Satisfy the 2026 consumer shift from "does it work?" to "*how* does it work?" The dry niche states conclusions; nobody does satisfying mechanism reveals in a popular format — and physiology IS mechanism.
**When:** Any time the *why* is more gripping than the *whether*. Often the strongest version of the signature template's Layer 3.
**1-line example:** *"Why your magnesium does nothing: the absorption mechanism the label is legally allowed to hide from you."*

> **Routing rule:** spine plays (1, 3, 6, 7, 8) carry the operator/claim-safety/AI-error overlay by default. Seasoning plays (2, 4, 5) appear ~1-in-4 and only when fluent — a gaming wrapper on a topic Farrice can't fill reads as creator-for-creators and the buyer winces. When unsure, default to #1.

## 3. THE EDUTAINMENT + AHA + PSYCHOLOGY STACK (pre-build checklist)

Run this before drafting. It's the union of Kobi (doorway/payload), Kallaway (retention), and McRaney (real change). Don't enumerate these in the post — they're the water the writing swims in, not a fence it follows.

**☐ THE VEGETABLE SMUGGLE (Kobi).** Entertainment is the doorway; the undiluted physiology idea is the payload. Lead with appetite (anomaly, stakes, striking claim), never "Today I'll explain." Never water down the idea to make it accessible — the smart-beginner rewrite keeps the real mechanism, drops only the jargon.

**☐ ACCOMMODATION, not assimilation (McRaney).** Design for SURPRISE, not clarity. The post fires only if all three hit at once:
- **Surprise** — the reader's current model genuinely can't explain the anomaly. (If they can absorb it without changing, it's assimilation — "so true!" with zero behavior change. Redesign.)
- **Relevance** — it's their body, their budget, their job on the line.
- **Safety** — updating costs them no face. Pre-build the dignity exit before the challenge.

**☐ THE FOUR-STEP LOOP (Kallaway).** Stakes → Big Question → Head Fake → Rehook, running invisibly. No gap longer than ~2 sentences without an unresolved prediction. Every head fake passes "Oh! not Huh?" (surprise + understanding, never confusion). Transitions are relay-race handoffs ("Then I started reading the certificate of analysis instead..."), never dead air.

**☐ THE PSYCHOLOGY LEVERS (woven, never visible):**
- **Curiosity gap** = Kallaway Big-Question specificity (predict, can't guess).
- **Identity / tribe** (McRaney inversion) — position the reader into an identity they want: "the kind of operator who reads the evidence, not the label."
- **Cognitive dissonance** — present the experience their model can't explain *before* handing the resolving frame ("you did everything right and the floor still moved").
- **Pluralistic-ignorance exploit** — name what everyone privately thinks but won't say (the dossier wince lines: "you're paying more for the marketing than for what's in the bottle"). Relief response = "finally, someone said it."
- **The Pause** — change happens in the silence after the question. In writing, that's the line break before the turn. Don't fill it.
- **Production / evaluation asymmetry** — never claim your own authority. Deploy the 18-yr credential as evidence *inside a scene* ("I'd spend a session talking a client out of that"), never as a résumé bullet. Make the reader produce the conclusion.

**☐ TRUST-BEFORE-PERSUASION (McRaney's sequence).** Accurate empathy (prove you understand their situation) → honest acknowledgment of what does NOT work, even in your own space ("I told clients to take magnesium for a decade and quietly wondered why half felt nothing") → then what does. Honest self-critique converts more than any benefit. Target response: "the first honest one."

**☐ THE RESTRAINT CUT (Kobi).** Cover the hot take honestly ONCE. No follow-up milking, no "part 2," no mic-drop deflation. Name what you walked away from if it sharpens the close. This is the anti-grifter signal the founder avatar needs to see.

> **Anti-slop teeth:** a post can ace curiosity and clarity and still be NOISE if it produces no belief/perspective shift (Cognitive Change row) or rests on a copyable edge (Differentiation row). The defensible edge here is the mechanism + claim-safety + lived credential — the layer an AI or a marketer structurally can't fake this quarter.

## 4. THE HOOK SYSTEM (formats + char ceilings + domain templates)

Diandra's mechanics. The hook has ONE job with humans (earn the "see more" click) and ONE with the algorithm (360 Brew reads the first 40-50 words and tracks whether people stop and read). Every hook opens a **gap**. Pick exactly one format.

### The 4 Formats + Ceilings

| Format | Shape | Ceiling | Lean when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dense** | Continuous text, zero line breaks, full tension loop | **140–160 chars total**, spans 2–3 mobile lines | Text-only, data/proof-heavy, claim needs context to land |
| **Punchy + Context** *(workhorse)* | L1 bold claim/interrupt → blank line → L2 rehook | **L1 ≤50, L2 ≤50** | Default. Contrarian takes, headline-style openers. Rotate sub-variants |
| **Single-Line Bomb** | One charged sentence, "see more" cuts in fast | **≤50 chars, one line** | A take too good to dilute. High variance. Lean here with strong media/video |
| **Stacked** | 2–3 patterned lines, blank-separated, completion bias | **lines ≤60** (sub-variant E ≤50) | A natural series — before/after, escalating numbers, parallel regrets |

**Universal limits:** max total hook 210 chars · max single author-line 75 chars · a line break costs a full above-the-fold line · for posts >300 chars end the hook on `?` `:` `…` `.` so the cut feels intentional · final check is always the mobile previewer (mobile-optimized ⇒ desktop, not the reverse).

**Hard bans (non-negotiable):** no questions in hooks (only exception: Stacked-4C with data viz attached) · **zero em dashes** (Farrice's standing rule, natural stacking point) · no emojis · no LinkedIn clichés ("game-changer," "deep dive," "let that sink in," "read that again," "this changed everything," "they called me crazy," "here's the thing," "the truth is," "then it hit me," "I'm excited to share") · no filler (actually/basically/very/just/really/honestly/literally) · no stranger-blind hooks (must work with zero idea who Farrice is) · never fabricate numbers.

**Register:** Formal/B2B standard caps is the DEFAULT (data-first, operator audience). Lowercase only if the body is consistently informal — lowercase in B2B reads as careless.

**Newsjack rule:** the entity name appears in the **first 1–2 lines** (the borrowed-attention mechanism). Never open with the brand/product name on a *sponsored* post — but on a jack, the named entity IS the hook.

## 5. THE "GET" STANDARD (Cole) + Pre-Publish Checklist

**Every post hands the reader exactly one tangible thing** — a thing they can check, ask, run, or decide *tonight*, without buying anything. "Flip your bottle over and look for the form." "Run the 20-question test on your category." "Ask the brand for the certificate of analysis, not the claim." If the reader finishes and can't name what they got, the post is polished-but-flat — and polished-but-flat is a FAIL.

Cole's craft governs the sentence: terminal word power (end on a noun or power verb, never "is/it/the"), three-pass compression (cut 30–50%, signal over noise), fourth-grade base + one elevated word max per sentence, the New-Information filter (every paragraph advances or dies), comma control (0–2 per sentence). Density beats completeness — the reader leaves *smarter*, not buried.

### The 6-Item Pre-Publish Checklist

1. **THE GET** — Can I name in one sentence the tangible thing the reader walks away with? (No → not shippable.)
2. **THE AHA** — Does Layer 3 produce "Oh!" not "Huh?" and not "yeah I knew that"? (Surprise + relevance + safety, all three. If it's assimilation, redesign.)
3. **THE EDU-FIRST WINCE-TEST** — Does this sound like a smart operator naming the 2am thing, or like a growth-hacker? (Growth-hacker register = wrong. Entertainment is tone, never topic.)
4. **THE FACTS** — Every real-world claim labeled VERIFIED/LIKELY from the dossier/research. Zero UNCONFIRMED stats (no 60.7%, no 58%, no Medician CPM). Numbers stated as observed when they're observed, not dressed as published stats.
5. **THE BANS** — Zero "It's not X, it's Y," zero twin-sentence aphoristic endings, zero triple anaphora, zero "Here's what/why/how" openers, zero mic-drop, zero cheap-question signoff. ≤2 em dashes total, **0 in the hook**. No filler.
6. **THE RESTRAINT CUT** — Did I do the honest version once and stop? (No part-2 milking. The close is recognition — image/declaration/bookend — not a question.)

> **Deterministic backstop:** run `python3 execution/prose_classifier.py check <file>` before shipping. It flags reveal-leadins, em-dash overuse, contrast-reveal, question-close, and LinkedIn clichés at the gate. The checklist is the judgment; the classifier is the floor.

## 6. THE SOFT-CTA RULE

**Value-first. The offer is soft, occasional, and never a pitch.** Default = no CTA. Roughly **1-in-5 posts** carries a light discussion-CTA (Diandra's growth bucket: invite the conversation, never sell). The offer — GEO/SEO ghostwriting for health brands — is *earned by the body of work*, surfaced softly, never the reason the post exists. The Restraint Cut applies to selling too: name the thing once, don't hammer it.

**Exact soft phrasings (rotate; never more than one, never on a jack):**
- *"If you formulate or market in this category, run that test on your own claims this week and tell me what the machine leaves out."*
- *"Curious what form your magnesium is — flip the bottle and drop it below. I'll tell you if you're in the 4% club."*
- *"What's the most-recommended supplement in your stack you've never actually felt? I'll read the mechanism for the top one in the comments."*
- *"If your rank tracker says you're fine but the clicks vanished, that's the gap I spend my days on. Happy to point at where it's leaking, no pitch."*
- *"Disagree? Tell me the claim you think survives a receipt. I'll red-team it honestly."*

**Never:** "DM me to learn more," "book a call," "link in comments to my offer," any urgency-stacked pitch. Those activate the founder-avatar wince (influencer/growth-hacker register) and burn the trust the whole system is built to earn. The discussion-CTA's job is reach + conversation; the pipeline comes from being the source the operator-class quietly saves and DMs — not from asking.
