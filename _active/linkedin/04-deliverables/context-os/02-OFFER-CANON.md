# Offer Canon — verbatim promises, prices, boundaries

*Prices and promises come from this document only. No AI may discount, bundle, or invent guarantees beyond what's here.*

## The ladder

### Rung 0 — The free teardown (the door)
"Send me the live page or campaign. I will point to one consequential message or proof gap using your public words—nothing invented."
Protocol: one useful observation, privately delivered. No multi-page free strategy document, no legal diagnosis, and no automatic publication promise.

### Rung 1 — The Angle Map, $750 prepaid (the cash rung; wargame-ruled 2026-07-28)
**Promise:** a 60-minute live read of the brand's message, then **three named angles with core beliefs, hook directions, approved-proof direction, and an editorial claim-safety line under each, delivered inside 48 hours.**
Price is one number: $750, prepaid, no range, no discounting. Make-right clause: if the first read does not produce three usable angles within the agreed scope, he revises until it does.
Why it exists (context for the AI, not for copy): paid tactical strategy is an established category. Whether this exact Angle Map sells remains unconfirmed until payment clears.

### Rung 2 — The Proof-to-Market Sprint, $2,500 prepaid (10 business days)
For the authorized buyer with a dated occasion: launch, retail expansion, or repositioning. Deliverables: buyer-decision map, positioning and proof hierarchy, campaign-angle bank, launch messaging blueprint, and one priority campaign asset. AI-search is a supporting pattern read only. Capacity: one Sprint at a time, two per month maximum during the founding phase.

### Rung 3 — Recurring implementation (parked until the 14-day test passes)
Test at $3,000–$5,000 per month only after paid Angle Map and Sprint proof exists. Scope: research updates, new angles, message deployment, and evidence/quality review. Do not sell or build this rung during the initial validation window.

## The claim-safety wedge (embedded in every rung, never standalone)

He maps each claim to the evidence and boundaries the client approves, then flags language that needs qualified review. The client's attorney or compliance owner has the pen. Say it as: *"I read for signal, not law."* He never certifies claims or implies science the brand has not supplied and approved.

## Objections and the standing answers

- **"Why should I trust a stranger with no logos?"** → Lead with one specific observation from the buyer's live occasion, a bounded $750 paid scope, and the make-right clause. He has zero direct-response performance receipts for this offer—never fake them.
- **"Is this just another positioning deck?"** → Everything ends in named angles with hooks — the thing a media buyer can run Monday.
- **"We already have an agency."** → He doesn't replace the agency; he makes the decision the agency keeps asking them for. "I don't build the ads, run the account, or write your calendar. I decide what they should be making, and why."
- **"AI can do this now."** → Never argue AI capability. "Everyone has the same AI models. You have someone who's lived the physiology your ad is selling — so the angle is true before it's scaled."

## Hard boundaries (violating any of these is off-canon)

- No revenue guarantees, ever. Quality make-right only.
- No science claims the brand hasn't earned; no compliance certification.
- No work for products he wouldn't defend — the entire position is claim-honesty.
- Prepaid only on paid rungs (bank-clear lag makes invoiced work phantom revenue).
- Never lead outreach with "your better product is losing" (a seller's belief) — lead with the metric-shaped trigger the buyer already feels (angles fatigued, LTV:CAC, sameness).
