### 3.3 Researched struggles

- The team ordered 15 ads but received one belief dressed in 15 layouts.
- “Concept,” “hook,” and “variant” mean different things to the founder, creative strategist, and media buyer.
- The claim sheet enters after the headline is written. That timing creates avoidable review loops.
- Creative fatigue triggers more output before anyone identifies what the account has already learned.
- A new SKU or evidence event needs a bounded first test, but nobody owns research-to-concept translation.
- The account cannot give all 15 ads enough delivery at once, so launch volume starves the learning the pack was supposed to create.
- The media buyer asks for more creative while the organization still lacks a learning agenda.

### 3.4 Insider-language boundary

Use target-native language only where it sharpens the diagnosis: control, concept, variant, hook, creative fatigue, winner/loser set, substantiation, approved claim, reviewer, launch sequence, learning agenda, test cell, account signal.

Do not decorate the work with jargon. Do not use CTR, thumb-stop rate, CPA, or ROAS as proof unless the source and account context are available and authorized.

### 3.5 Four sandbagged content tests

#### Test 1: Account-learning teardown

**Target:** founder or growth lead who believes a 15-ad order created 15 tests.  
**Opening line:** “You do not have 15 ads. You have one idea wearing 15 shirts.”  
**Precision lines:**

- “A new color is not a new belief.”
- “If the account cannot learn a different answer, it is a variant, not a concept.”
- “The expensive failure is not a weak ad. It is buying the same uncertainty 15 times.”

**Proof object:** redacted map comparing one repeated hypothesis with five materially different bets.  
**CTA:** “If you are already spending on Meta for one health/performance SKU, send five ads that worked, five that did not, and the approved claim sheet. I’ll tell you whether the account contains enough signal for five real tests.”  
**Success threshold:** one qualified operator supplies or offers to discuss the required input set. Record likes and views in the separate growth ledger.

#### Test 2: Evidence-to-message demo

**Target:** growth owner or creative strategist losing time between product truth and reviewer feedback.  
**Opening line:** “The claim sheet should enter before the headline, not after the ad is designed.”  
**Precision lines:**

- “A reviewer cannot rescue a concept that was built on an implication the evidence never supported.”
- “Claim-aware does not mean timid. It means the evidence boundary becomes a creative constraint early enough to create something useful.”
- “The reviewer owns approval. The strategist owns making the approved truth worth noticing.”

**Proof object:** one source-to-message matrix with approved, reviewer-needed, and prohibited zones clearly separated.  
**CTA:** invite a qualified operator to compare the matrix with their current handoff.  
**Success threshold:** one qualified growth or reviewer-side response identifies a real handoff failure or requests a fit conversation.

#### Test 3: Launch-sequence specimen

**Target:** performance lead or media buyer who cannot give 15 ads meaningful delivery at once.  
**Opening line:** “A 15-ad pack may need to launch three to five at a time.”  
**Precision lines:**

- “Throughput is not a learning plan.”
- “If every concept enters at once, budget allocation can turn five hypotheses into one unreadable result.”
- “The sequence should protect the question the account is trying to answer.”

**Proof object:** redacted three-wave launch map with the learning question attached to each wave.  
**CTA:** ask the target operator which constraint (budget, control stability, reviewer timing, or offer change) would break the proposed sequence.  
**Success threshold:** one qualified media/growth operator starts a concrete sequencing conversation.

#### Test 4: Failure-proof workflow demo

**Target:** founder or growth owner accustomed to vendors accepting bad inputs and blaming execution later.  
**Opening line:** “Sometimes the most useful deliverable is a refusal to manufacture false confidence.”  
**Precision lines:**

- “No recent winners and losers means no account-learning claim.”
- “No approved claim source means the concept work is not ready to promise launch-ready copy.”
- “Rejecting an underqualified brief protects the buyer from paying for polished uncertainty.”

**Proof object:** fit-screen specimen showing accept, pause, and reject conditions.  
**CTA:** invite the operator to test one current SKU against the input gate.  
**Success threshold:** one qualified buyer asks about fit, scope, price, or the fit-review path.

### 3.6 Conversion path

1. A sandbagged post or private proof object surfaces a precise operating problem.
2. The CTA requests five winners, five losers, and the approved claim material for one priority SKU.
3. Farrice runs the fit screen: live Meta, sufficient signal, one SKU, decision owner, reviewer, inputs, launch owner, results-return commitment.
4. Qualified prospects enter a fit conversation; unqualified prospects receive a clean reason for the pause or decline.
5. Farrice presents the exact **$1,000 prepaid** Pack hypothesis. Interest without the price does not count as demand evidence.
6. Payment is recorded separately from proposal acceptance. Delivery, launch, and result states remain separate events.

**Collection constraint:** a working payment/collection rail must exist before any live proposal is sent. Payment setup sits outside this strategy map.

| Stage | Evidence required | PMF meaning |

| Qualified exposure | Correct role + account meets minimum conditions | Audience fit signal only |

### 3.8 Growth-lane protection

Continue broader content only when it strengthens category recognition without contaminating the conversion test. Growth content can earn reach; it cannot be allowed to redefine success for the sandbagged lane.

The conversion lane wins when the right operator acts. The growth lane wins when the right market remembers the point of view. Neither substitutes for a prepaid event.
