## The finding that shapes every scene below

**This ICP's imagery works better without a person in it.**

Their core wound is *invisibility* — "shouting into the void," "excellence in a soundproof room,"
"my work should speak for itself." Putting a recognisable human in frame resolves that wound
prematurely: a visible person is, by definition, already seen. The absence *is* the message.

So the inventory is objects, architecture and thresholds. Where a body appears it is partial,
turned away, or implied by what it left behind. This is a deliberate art-direction constraint,
not a stylistic preference, and it should be re-tested — not assumed — for any other ICP.

Second-order consequence worth knowing before the sweep: it removes the hardest failure mode on
a cheap draft model. `nano-banana-2` is explicitly not the photoreal-people lane; an inventory
with no faces in it never asks the model for the thing it is worst at.

## Fixed probe subject

> **a single closed door in a long corridor, light spilling underneath it and nowhere else**

**Why this one (P2):** it is the most-produced shape, not the most interesting one. It carries the
central metaphor of the entire ICP profile, it is architecture rather than incident so it recurs
without repeating, and it holds negative space on either axis — meaning a style that survives it
will survive most of the inventory.

Do not change it mid-sweep. Flynn's rule: everything learned is a *difference*, and a difference
requires a fixed baseline. Changing the probe voids the run.
