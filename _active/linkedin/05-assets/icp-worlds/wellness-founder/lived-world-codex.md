trust-collapse-era DTC wellness founder · **Built:** 2026-08-10 · **Craft:** Connelly Telling

**Purpose:** the ZOOM layer, not scenes. Insider micro-details that make this founder feel *"he
sees the world I'm living in"* — objects only someone who has shipped a supplement PDP, sat in a

## Day Arc

**1. 6:15 AM — phone in bed, before coffee.** Opens the ad account dashboard first, site analytics
second. Green arrows on one screen, a flat revenue line on the other — the gap he's learned to
distrust before he's out of bed.

**2. 8:30 AM — cold-eyes pass on their own product page.** Coffee in hand, reading the site like a
stranger would, because a founder who never does this is the founder who gets teardown'd. This is
where the buried claims live — the FAQ line, the footnote, the mismatched footnote, the review-count
seam.

**3. 11:00 AM — warehouse or 3PL video call.** Ops walkthrough: the tub coming off the line, the
QR sticker, the batch stamp, the binder of supplier COAs nobody outside manufacturing ever opens.

**4. 1:30 PM — compliance/legal review.** Counsel on the line or in the room. Red pen on a printed
page. The exact dollar figure per violation gets typed into a tracker, not estimated.

**5. 3:45 PM — growth/creative review.** Ad account open next to a browser full of competitor
tabs. The sameness is visible before anyone says it out loud. Margin math sits in the same
spreadsheet tab as the creative brief.

**6. 6:00 PM — the customer-voice reckoning.** End of day, the review widget gets read start to
finish, not skimmed. This is where the worst review of the week gets found and screenshotted for
the team channel — never the best one.

**7. 9:40 PM — closing the laptop.** One more scroll before bed: LinkedIn requests, a cold DM from
a stranger with an unfamiliar name and the word "teardown" in the preview line. Hovers. Doesn't
decide yet.

  replaced with the coffee-in-hand cold-eyes read of their own PDP (Beat 2), where the object that
  matters is the accordion line, not the mug.
- **Cut:** "sticky notes on a whiteboard" — generic startup-office cliché, zero category
  specificity; replaced with the claims matrix spreadsheet (Row 12), which is a document type only
  this exact founder maintains.
- **Kept and sharpened:** every row above required a specific document, screen state, or physical
