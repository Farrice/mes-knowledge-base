## Day arc

Six beats, kitchen counter to midnight — where they are, what's in their hands, what they're dreading or hoping.

1. **6:10am, kitchen counter.** Phone in hand before the coffee's poured, thumb stalled over another LinkedIn connection request promising to "transform your workflows with AI" — dreading it's the fortieth one this month, all the same pitch.
2. **7:45am, laptop at the counter.** Meta Ads Manager open before anyone else is awake, scanning the frequency column for anything crossing 2.0 — hoping the account made it through the night without a spike.
3. **9:20am, inbox.** The co-packer's COA email lands, a lot number sitting in the subject line, cursor hovering before opening it — dreading a retest flag more than an FDA letter, because this one they can't blame on anyone else.
4. **11:40am, second monitor.** The claims matrix spreadsheet, one substantiation cell highlighted red where a headline outran its evidence — dreading the exposure everyone's meant to fix for three weeks and hasn't.
5. **1:15pm, desk lunch.** The review widget scrolled past — twenty five-star tiles about taste, none about the dose that took six months to formulate right — a private, specific kind of shame nobody outside the company would clock.
6. **9:45pm, two tabs open at once.** The agency's green ROAS dashboard beside the actual bank balance, willing the two numbers to agree before finally closing the laptop — exhausted, still checking.

## The recognition test, applied

Every row above survived the question: *would an outsider think to include this?*

Rows that would have failed and were cut before this table was finalized: a generic "spreadsheet open on a laptop" (any founder, any industry — no insider signal); "a founder on a call" (no artifact, no zoom); "a warehouse" as a wide establishing shot (that's scene-inventory's job, not this codex's); coffee alone, anywhere, as its own artifact (explicitly ruled out — the ban is in the brief for a reason: caffeine is universal, the *specific object it leaves behind in a specific workflow* is not).

What survived shares one property: each artifact is legible only if you've done the job. A designer or a stock-photo buyer would reach for "lab coat," "warehouse shelves," "spreadsheet." An operator reaches for the retest flag in a subject line, the toggle that produces a filtered star count, the exact frequency number where fatigue starts, the column header that's actually FTC language. That gap — between the prop an outsider would pick and the artifact an insider would recognize — is the whole point of this layer.
