## Day arc

1. **7:40am — Cold reopen.** Laptop wakes to whatever tab was open at 11pm last night: Meta Ads
   Manager, Delivery column, same ad set that was already limping when the day ended.
2. **9:00am — Standup.** Reports blended ROAS off the agency dashboard because that's the number
   in the deck; the true channel CAC lives in a different tab nobody asked to see.
3. **10:30am — The angle-bottleneck ping.** Media buyer Slacks: account's ready to spend more,
   nothing new to feed it. No brief exists yet to answer with.
4. **12:15pm — Lunch-at-desk scroll.** Public Meta Ad Library, filtered to the category, nine
   thumbnails that could all have shipped from the same brand.
5. **2:00pm — Compliance pass.** New ad copy against the claims matrix, red pen circling the line
   that reads one word away from a disease claim.
6. **4:30pm — Screening call.** A resume with no dated, published work gets one sentence and a
   pass. The one that's left gets asked about CM3 — and the answer decides the call.
7. **9:45pm — Late-night ad review.** Home, laptop open again: the legal pad with one sentence
   crossed out three times, the frequency column that's crept past the number that matters, and —
   on the good nights — a phone lighting up with a single deposit while the room stays dark.

## Recognition test — what got cut

Applied to every candidate before it made the table above: *would a non-practitioner recognize
this as charged, or does it read as generic office set-dressing?* Cut for failing it:

- A whiteboard of OKRs/sticky notes — reads as "office," not as this operator's specific Tuesday.
- A wall of motivational posters or a standing desk — pure set-dressing, no researched anchor.
- A generic "47 browser tabs" gag — kept only once, in row 4, specifically because the *count
  range* (8–12) and the *reason it's charged* (paid tools that don't replace thinking) are sourced;
  a second version of the same joke elsewhere in the table would have been cut as redundant.
- A Zoom-call screenshot with faces — this ICP's world is documented as screens and interfaces,
  not identity theater (scene-inventory.md, "What shapes this inventory"); faces were dropped
  category-wide, not just per-row.
- A coffee cup with a wellness-brand logo — decorative, not evidentiary; no source ties a specific
  cup to a specific pain or number.

Every surviving row ties to a named number, a named UI label, or a documented deliverable format
that a non-practitioner would not know to look for or would not read as significant.
