## What shapes this inventory

This buyer's world is screens and documents, not identity theater — an operator drowning in
interfaces (ad libraries, dashboards, product pages, briefs), not a person performing a role. So
every scene below is a workspace object or interface in a specific, checkable state. No faces
required to carry the meaning; where a body appears it's a hand, a cursor, a phone screen at the
edge of frame — never staged activity. This also sidesteps the hardest failure mode for a cheap
draft model (rendering legible small text inside a UI screenshot): every scene's proof-point lives
in a physical object (a printed page, a sticky note, a taped statement) rather than on-screen copy
the model has to spell correctly.

## Fixed probe subject

> **A product page glows on a monitor, cursor resting on three manufacturing bullet points, and
> beside the keyboard a single printed page lies face-down, one corner dog-eared.** (P4)

**Why this one:** not the most interesting scene in the set — the most-produced. The Teardown
Wedge (free audit → paid pilot) is the live, weekly-cadence content motion for this segment,
independently verified as the working play [`ICP-BATTLE-CARD.md:64`], already shipping on a
numbered weekly schedule (`03-launch/teardowns/2026-07-27-teardown-02-momentous.md` is #2 of an
ongoing series). Every week's post needs this exact shape: a real PDP or hero, the shallow claim
under the cursor, the deeper proof one page away. A probe optimized for the rarer metaphor-scenes
(P1, D1) would select a style that fails on the asset this brand actually ships most weeks.

Flynn's rule: do not change the probe mid-sweep. Everything learned in `02` is a *difference*, and
a difference requires a fixed baseline.
