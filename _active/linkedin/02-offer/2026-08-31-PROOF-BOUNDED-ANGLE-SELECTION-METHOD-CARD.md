The repeatable method is not broad “claim-safe content.” It is a message-selection process that chooses the strongest campaign argument a Health Performance brand can support for one buyer and one live occasion before the team funds copy or production.

## Exact problem

A supplement or performance brand has product facts, claims, creative options, and an urgent occasion, but no settled answer to this question:

> Which argument should we lead with for this buyer, now, given the proof the brand can actually use?

Without a selection layer, the team tends to choose the most exciting claim, the loudest creative idea, or the safest generic wording. The result is either exposure, sameness, or expensive execution built around the wrong message.

## Intended user

The marketing owner, founder, or growth lead at a supplement or performance brand facing a current launch, retail expansion, reformulation, or repositioning decision.

## Required inputs

1. One active commercial occasion and decision deadline.
2. One named buyer or buyer state.
3. The live page, deck, campaign, or public language under review.
4. A client-approved source pack: product facts, studies, testing, certifications, reviews, and other permitted evidence.
5. Explicit claim boundaries and the person who retains legal or compliance approval.
6. Confidential-input handling terms and one final decision-maker.

If the source pack, permissions, handling path, or owner is unresolved, the process stays `HOLD`.

## Ordered actions

1. **Fix the decision boundary.** Name the occasion, buyer, deadline, and the single message decision the brand must make.
2. **Read the live surface.** Capture the brand's current language verbatim and state what it presently asks the buyer to believe or decide.
3. **Build the proof boundary.** Separate client-approved product evidence from ingredient evidence, reviews, public claims, interpretation, and unknowns. Mark claim-sensitive language `usable`, `needs support`, or `outside scope`.
4. **Find the message gap.** Identify the unresolved choice between the current language, the buyer's actual decision, and the strongest proof available.
5. **Draft five candidate angles.** Each candidate must name a distinct core belief, lead promise, proof direction, and best campaign use.
6. **Cut to three.** Reject paraphrases, unsupported claims, and directions that do not fit the occasion. Keep the three strongest combinations of buyer relevance, proof support, and strategic difference.
7. **Pressure-test the three.** Compare the belief each angle must move, the proof it can lawfully and credibly lean on, and the decision it helps the buyer make.
8. **Choose one lead angle.** State why it wins this occasion, what the team should ship next, and what the campaign must not claim or imply.
9. **Deliver the decision product.** Produce the three-angle map with hooks, approved-proof direction, editorial claim-safety line, lead recommendation, and next campaign decision.
10. **Capture outcome evidence separately.** Record the buyer's unresolved decision before the work, decision after the read, selected angle, reported brief/campaign change, exact feedback, and permission state. Never convert delivery into performance proof.

## Decision rules

1. **Approved evidence missing:** `HOLD`; do not invent support or start the paid clock.
2. **Claim exceeds the evidence:** remove it or mark it for qualified review. This includes disease framing, misleading net impression, and support stronger than the brand actually holds.
3. **Ingredient evidence only:** do not silently promote it to finished-product proof.
4. **Two angles share the same belief, proof logic, and campaign use:** merge them. Different wording does not make them different angles.
5. **Nothing survives buyer relevance and the proof boundary:** return `NEEDS SOURCE`, not three weaker options.
6. **The work expands into positioning, proof hierarchy, finished assets, production, or media:** stop the Angle Map and recommend a separately scoped implementation engagement.
7. **The buyer requests guaranteed compliance, revenue, retailer acceptance, rankings, or AI citations:** decline the guarantee.

## Observable output

One decision-ready Angle Map containing:

1. The occasion, buyer, deadline, and current message gap.
2. Three materially different campaign angles.
3. For each angle, a core belief and three to five hook directions.
4. Each angle carries a client-approved proof direction.
5. Add an editorial claim-safety line to each: what may be said, what needs qualified review, and what falls outside scope.
6. Finish with the recommended lead angle, the reason it wins, and the next campaign move.

The usability test is operational: the brand can begin the next creative brief without reopening the message-selection decision.

## Known failure modes

1. Generating angle volume without making a selection.
2. Treating a clever name or hook variation as a materially different angle.
3. Borrowing disease framing from a cited study into otherwise safe copy.
4. Treating ingredient evidence as proof of the finished product.
5. Flattening every claim into lifeless hedge copy instead of choosing a supportable mechanism, experience, or proof.
6. Inferring buyer motives, internal strategy, ROI, or performance from public surfaces.
7. Calling editorial screening legal clearance.
8. Allowing a second occasion, buyer, product, or newly introduced evidence pack to enter the fixed scope.

## Exclusions

1. Legal advice, regulatory approval, or platform clearance.
2. Finished copy, scripts, design, production, or media planning.
3. New scientific substantiation supplied by Farrice.
4. Revenue, conversion, retailer, ranking, or AI-citation guarantees.
5. Performance claims not reported by the buyer and separately permitted for use.
