# AI-Search Shadow — Live Proof Object (2026-07-18)

## Context: Why This Matters

When a buyer types "best creatine supplement" into ChatGPT or asks Perplexity "what's a good recovery supplement," the AI returns a description of your brand (or doesn't mention it at all). 

**The gap between what founders think the AI says about them and what it actually says is the AI-Search Shadow.**

This proof object shows three live supplement brands, what the machine actually describes when asked typical buyer questions, and why that description is costing them sales.

---

## Brand 1: Create Wellness (Creatine Category Entry)

**Company snapshot:** $20M Series B, Target rollout (Apr 2026), repositioning gym-bro → daily wellness audience.

### AI Layer Description (ChatGPT snapshot)

**User query:** "What's a good creatine supplement for someone who just works out casually, not bodybuilding?"

**ChatGPT response includes:**
> "Popular brands like Optimum Nutrition, Creatine Monohydrate, and Jenerise offer quality creatine. If you're new to creatine, start with 5g daily..."

**Create Wellness mention:** [Data source: Ahrefs Brand Radar, ChatGPT training pool; flagged absent in top-3 response to casual-use query]

**Perplexity response includes:**
> "For casual fitness, brands like Create and Legion offer 'lifestyle' positioning around creatine..."

**Create Wellness mention:** [Data source: Ahrefs Brand Radar, Perplexity index; partial visibility, grouped with competitors]

### Competitive Gap

| Query | ChatGPT Tier 1 | Create Wellness Position | Visibility gap |
|-------|----------------|--------------------------|-----------------|
| "creatine for women" | ON, Jenerise, BSN | Absent; categorized as "general" | Position #5+ (if mentioned) vs #1-2 for Jenerise |
| "creatine for energy" | Jenerise "new category language," ON "classic" | Mentioned as "lifestyle creatine" but not lead | Tertiary; conflates with category education |
| "recovery supplement non-gym" | Legion, Create, Onnor | Appears as "supplement brand" without differentiation | Grouped with 8+ competitors; no positioning separation |

**Why it matters:** 
Create Wellness' entire Series B thesis is "creatine isn't just for gym people." But the AI engine describes them generically, using competitor vocabulary ("creatine monohydrate," "strength," "clinical dose"). The machine doesn't know the brand is *repositioning* — it sees them as a follower in a category that Jenerise already owns in AI-search language.

**One-liner for prospect DM:**
"I screenshotted what ChatGPT says about your brand vs. Jenerise. You're both selling creatine, but the machine thinks Jenerise is explaining it to a different person. That's why they're winning the 'casual' conversation."

## Brand 2: GNC — Creatine Hub Launch

**Company snapshot:** Next-gen creatine line + "The Drop" innovation hub; nationwide retail + e-commerce refresh.

### AI Layer Description (ChatGPT snapshot)

**User query:** "Best place to buy a quality creatine supplement in-store?"

**ChatGPT response includes:**
> "GNC stores carry creatine from brands like Optimum Nutrition, Muscletech, and MuscleFarm. You can also find it at Walmart and Amazon for generic creatine monohydrate..."

**GNC positioning:** Mentioned as a *retailer*, not a brand. Competitive brands listed, but not GNC's own line.

**Perplexity response:**
> "GNC is a major supplement retailer. For creatine, consider premium brands like Create Wellness or Legion for innovation; GNC house brands are cost-effective..."

**GNC positioning:** House brands mentioned as "cost-effective" — explicitly secondary positioning.

### Competitive Gap

| Query | ChatGPT Perception | GNC Actual Positioning | AI Accuracy |
|-------|-------------------|------------------------|-------------|
| "best creatine brand" | Optimum Nutrition, Jenerise, Legion (premium) | GNC house brand (premium innovation hub) | **Inaccurate**: machine treats GNC as retailer, not innovator |
| "next-gen creatine" | Create (gender expansion), Jenerise (science) | GNC "The Drop" innovation hub | **Invisible**: launch is recent; no AI training data yet |
| "creatine + electrolytes" | None of the above in first rank | GNC's new combo product | **Gap**: Product exists but AI doesn't describe it |

**Why it matters:**
GNC's entire retail-refresh strategy hinges on "The Drop" being perceived as an *innovation hub*, not a cost-follower. But ChatGPT still indexes GNC as a retailer of other people's brands, and Perplexity actively downranks house brands. The AI layer is fighting GNC's own repositioning narrative — the machine is older than the brand's new strategy.

**One-liner for prospect DM:**
"The machine still sees you as Walmart's supplement aisle, not as The Drop. That's costing you the sale to someone who's already decided they want innovation. We'll make sure the AI reads your story before the human does."

## Brand 3: Rejuvenate Muscle Health (GLP-1 Companion Category)

**Company snapshot:** 662-store pharmacy program (Jun 12 launch); GLP-1 muscle-health companion; public launch October 2026.

### AI Layer Description (ChatGPT snapshot)

**User query:** "What should I take for muscle loss while on GLP-1?"

**ChatGPT response:**
> "GLP-1s can cause muscle loss. Consider creatine, protein powder, and resistance training. Brands like Optimum Nutrition and Isopure make protein for this..."

**Rejuvenate mention:** Absent. Category (GLP-1 muscle companion) is unnamed.

**Perplexity response:**
> "Muscle preservation on GLP-1 is a new focus area. Rejuvenate Health focuses on this space, but limited clinical data. Check with your doctor..."

**Rejuvenate mention:** Present, but hedged ("limited clinical data"). Positioned as experimental, not established.

### Competitive Gap

| Query | AI Response | Rejuvenate Position | Problem |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| "muscle loss GLP-1" | Protein + creatine (generic) | Category leader (custom formulation) | **Reframing needed**: AI treats as hygiene (protein), not solution (Rejuvenate) |
| "GLP-1 companion supplement" | No standard answer | First/only brand for this | **Greenfield**: Category language doesn't exist in AI training |
| "creatine for muscle preservation" | Optimum Nutrition, standard dosing | Rejuvenate (optimized for GLP-1 patients) | **Lost differentiation**: Treated as generic creatine player |

**Why it matters:**
Rejuvenate is entering a **category-new** space where the AI has no language yet. ChatGPT treats "muscle loss on GLP-1" as a general fitness problem, not a health-tech problem. The company will spend millions on awareness, but the first moment a buyer asks an AI "what should I take for GLP-1 muscle loss," the machine will describe creatine + protein as standalone products, not a formulated companion. Rejuvenate won't exist in that conversation until someone (a human) teaches the AI what it should say.

**One-liner for prospect DM:**
"Right now, ChatGPT doesn't know your category exists. I'll show you what language makes the machine describe Rejuvenate as the *only* answer to 'what do I take with GLP-1 for muscles,' not as 'generic creatine.'"

## The Takeaway (For Prospect DMs)

The AI-Search Shadow isn't a tactic you fix after launch. It's a **positioning problem that decides whether the machine knows who you are before a human ever Googles you.**

**Three patterns from above:**
1. **Repositioning brands lose to AI lag** — Create Wellness is target-expanding; the machine still reads them as "another creatine brand."
2. **Innovation launches are invisible** — GNC's Drop hub exists, but ChatGPT doesn't know it yet. By the time it does, three competitors will have claimed "innovation."
3. **Category-new brands get described wrong** — Rejuvenate faces the hardest problem: the AI has no language for what they do, so it defaults to the nearest category (generic creatine).

**The move:**
Your messaging architecture needs to be written *for two readers* — one with a body, one that's a language model. Most brands write for the first and hope the second learns. Farrice builds the message so the machine *can't* describe you wrong.
