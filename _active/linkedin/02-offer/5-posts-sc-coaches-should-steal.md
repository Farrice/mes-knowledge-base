## Post 1: The Methodology Post

**What it does**: Teaches ONE element of YOUR training system — not generic advice.

> **Hook architecture (Kallaway P28)**: Line 1 = Context Lean (what most programs do).
> Line 3 = Stun Gun ("Here's why that doesn't work"). The arrows = Haymaker.
> **Dual-Persona**: ICP reads for programming insight. IFP shares for the contrarian take.
> **Triangle check**: Pain ✓ (common approach fails). Solution ✓ (your alternative). Trust ✓ (specific results).

### Template:

```
Most [sport] programs still [common approach].

Here's why that doesn't work for [athlete type]:

[1-2 sentence explanation of the biomechanical/physiological reality]

What I do instead:

→ [Step/principle 1]
→ [Step/principle 2]
→ [Step/principle 3]

The result: [specific, measurable outcome you've seen]

This isn't revolutionary. It's just what happens when you stop copying
textbook periodization and start programming for the human in front of you.
```

### Example:

> Most NBA programs still isolate hip mobility from force production.
>
> Here's why that doesn't work for guards who need to decelerate at full speed:
>
> The hip doesn't care about your foam roller. It cares about whether
> your athlete can accept force at 3x bodyweight while changing direction.
>
> What I do instead:
>
> → Teach deceleration as a skill, not a byproduct
> → Program eccentric strength before plyometrics (always)
> → Test in chaotic conditions — not just controlled reps
>
> The result: Zero non-contact knee injuries in 4 seasons.
>
> This isn't revolutionary. It's just what happens when you stop copying
> textbook periodization and start programming for the human in front of you.

## Post 2: The Transformation Story

**What it does**: Shows your coaching in action. Builds trust through specificity.

> **Hook architecture**: Line 1 = Context Lean (athlete + problem). "First thing I noticed" = Stun Gun.
> Phased breakdown = Haymaker with nested curiosity loops (Kallaway P3).
> **Dual-Persona**: ICP reads for coaching methodology. IFP shares for the narrative arc.
> **Lara SLAY check**: S ✓ (raw story). L ✓ (the lesson). A ✓ (phased methodology). Y ✓ (the takeaway).
> **Why this format wins**: Transformation content creates believers, not just consumers (Kallaway P11).

### Template:

```
[Athlete/client type] came to me with [specific problem].

[1-2 sentences of context: what had been tried, why it wasn't working]

The first thing I noticed: [your diagnostic insight — the thing most
coaches would miss]

Here's what we did:

[Phase 1 — what and why]
[Phase 2 — what and why]
[Phase 3 — what and why]

[Timeframe] later: [specific result]

The lesson: [one-sentence takeaway that reveals how you think]
```

> **Note**: You don't need to name athletes. "A Division I point guard"
> or "a 35-year-old former pro" is specific enough. The coaching insight
> is what makes this valuable — not the name attached.

## Post 3: The Contrarian Take

**What it does**: Positions you as an independent thinker. Creates conversation.

> **Hook architecture**: "Unpopular opinion" = Context Lean. "Here's why" = Stun Gun.
> Counter-argument + refined position = Haymaker.
> **Algorithm gold**: Contrarian takes drive *substantive* thread-depth comments.
> LinkedIn's 2026 algorithm specifically rewards posts that generate back-and-forth
> replies, not one-off reactions. Thread depth > comment count.
> **Dual-Persona**: ICP debates the idea. IFP shares it for the hot take. Maximum reach.
> **Triangle check**: Pain ✓ (industry error). Solution ✓ (your alternative view). Trust ✓ (experience-backed).

### Template:

```
Unpopular opinion in S&C:

[Your contrarian belief — stated clearly, no hedging]

Here's why:

[Evidence from your experience — what you've SEEN, not what you've read]

[The common counterargument — and why it's incomplete]

[Your refined position — nuanced, not absolutist]

I know this will bother some people.
But I've been programming [timeframe] and the data doesn't lie.
```

### Example topics for S&C coaches:
- "Bilateral squats are overrated for [sport] athletes"
- "Most 'sport-specific' training isn't specific to anything"
- "GPP is the most undervalued phase in professional sports"
- "Velocity-based training is solving a problem most coaches don't have"
- "The reason your athletes break down isn't training load — it's coaching ego"

## Post 5: The "Field Note"

**What it does**: Gives a snapshot from your actual coaching day. Builds intimacy.

### Template:

```
Something happened today that I want to share.

[2-3 sentences describing the moment — specific, sensory, present tense]

[Why it matters — what it revealed about coaching, training, or athletes]

[The takeaway — not a lecture, but a reflection]

This is what [X] years in this field teaches you.
Not the textbook version. The real one.
```

### Why this format works:

LinkedIn rewards "authentic" content. But "authentic" for S&C coaches
isn't motivational quotes or gym selfies.

It's the quiet observation from the weight room floor.
The moment an athlete does something that makes you pause.
The insight you'd share with a trusted colleague over coffee.

Write those down. Those are your best-performing posts.
