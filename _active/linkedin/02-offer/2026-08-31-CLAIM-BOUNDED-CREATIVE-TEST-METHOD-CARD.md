# Claim-Bounded Creative Test Build: Method Card

## Verdict

**Method state:** `METHOD_CANDIDATE`  
**Packaging state:** `OFFER_HYPOTHESIS` as the Control-Beater  
**Proof ceiling:** `METHOD`  
**Market proof:** `NO EVENT`

The current Health Performance process is creative strategy that becomes testable ad creative for supplement and performance brands. It is not an Angle Map business, and Proof-to-Market is not the commercial owner.

The buyer-facing package currently attached to this method is the Control-Beater: five static ads and one production-ready video concept, delivered in 72 hours after complete inputs. The name does not mean the work is proven to beat a control. It names the test the assets are built to enter.

## Exact problem

A supplement or performance brand can make and run ads, but its next production round is at risk of becoming more wrappers around the same message. The team needs a small set of materially different creative tests grounded in buyer language, product truth, past-ad learning, and claims the brand is actually permitted to make.

The operating question is:

> What should the next ads test, and how do we turn those hypotheses into production-usable creative without inventing customer truth, product proof, or performance certainty?

## Intended user

The founder, growth lead, or creative lead at a supplement, sports-nutrition, recovery, or performance brand that:

1. already runs paid social;
2. names a current best-performing ad or incumbent creative;
3. maintains production capacity internally or through partners;
4. supplies approved product claims and supporting material; and
5. assigns final brand, compliance, and production approval to one person.

## Required inputs

1. One product, one customer segment, and one primary paid-social platform.
2. The current best-performing ad or incumbent creative, with whatever delivery and performance context the brand can responsibly share.
3. Product page, offer terms, approved claims, substantiation sources, required disclaimers, and known prohibited language.
4. Useful buyer language from reviews, comments, interviews, support records, surveys, or other permissioned sources. The deeper Creative Test Sprint standard uses at least 30 useful customer records; a thinner set must be disclosed as a limitation.
5. Past ads and known learnings, with observed results separated from team interpretation.
6. Brand system, available product assets, production constraints, and platform specifications.
7. One approval owner and the qualified legal or regulatory owner who retains final claim approval.

The 72-hour clock does not begin until the required inputs are complete enough to pass the input gate.

## Ordered actions

1. **Lock the test unit.** Name the product, segment, platform, incumbent creative, commercial occasion, and the exact learning question for the next round.
2. **Gate the inputs.** Confirm that buyer evidence, approved claims, product assets, past-ad context, production constraints, and approval ownership are usable. Mark the work `HOLD` if a missing input would force invention.
3. **Read the incumbent.** Identify its core belief, promise, proof, visual device, audience state, format, and known performance context. Separate what the data shows from what the team merely believes about it.
4. **Build the buyer-language bank.** Cluster repeated situations, objections, desired experiences, anxieties, and decision language. Preserve verbatim phrases and source locations. Do not turn frequency into causal truth.
5. **Build the claim boundary.** Label each proposed product or outcome statement `approved`, `conditional`, `hold`, or `outside scope`. Keep ingredient evidence separate from finished-product evidence and assess the whole ad's likely net impression.
6. **Diagnose the creative gap.** Write one sentence naming what the current creative overuses, misses, or leaves untested. The diagnosis must connect buyer evidence, product truth, and the next useful learning.
7. **Create materially different hypotheses.** Develop at least three distinct creative hypotheses. Each must specify the buyer belief to move, message, proof direction, visual telegraph, best format, and the question the test should answer.
8. **Allocate the six outputs.** Turn the strongest hypotheses into five static ads and one production-ready video concept. Multiple assets may explore one hypothesis only when they isolate a meaningful variable; decorative reskins do not count as new tests.
9. **Make the work production-usable.** Each static needs finished copy, layout direction, asset requirements, platform dimensions, and a claim note. The video concept needs a hook, script, shot sequence, on-screen text, asset list, and edit notes. A test map ties every asset to its hypothesis and intended learning.
10. **Run the five-pass review.** Check source truth, claim boundary and net impression, strategic distinctness, production feasibility, and testability. The internal ship rule is at least 4/5 for truth, production usability, and testability, an average of at least 4/5, and no unresolved hard red flag.
11. **Hand off the pack.** Deliver the creative, claim notes, production instructions, and test map to the approval owner. Describe every asset as worth testing, not as a winner.
12. **Capture learning separately.** After deployment, record what was actually served, comparable delivery conditions, observed results, production feedback, policy decisions, and the next iteration. Do not backfill a causal story from partial performance data.

## Decision rules

1. **Inputs are incomplete:** `HOLD`. Do not start the delivery clock or let AI fill the gap.
2. **No coherent incumbent or settled message exists:** do not manufacture six ads around confusion. Re-scope to a strategy-first Creative Test Sprint before production. This is a delivery gate, not a return to Angle Map as the business.
3. **The brand calls an ad the control but supplies no performance context:** treat it as the incumbent creative, not a verified control, and remove any control-beating implication.
4. **A claim is stronger than the evidence held:** weaken, replace, or hold it for qualified review. A disclaimer cannot rescue a misleading main impression.
5. **Only ingredient evidence exists:** do not present it as proof that the finished product produces the same outcome.
6. **Two assets move the same belief with the same proof logic and test question:** merge or revise them. A new layout alone is not a new hypothesis.
7. **An idea exceeds the brand's real assets, talent, timing, or budget:** redesign it before handoff.
8. **An asset has no explicit learning question:** remove it from the test pack.
9. **The buyer asks for guaranteed platform approval, ROAS, CPA, revenue, or a winning ad:** decline the guarantee. Farrice may offer the bounded policy-rejection fix or replacement that he controls.
10. **A new product, audience, platform, or evidence package enters after scope lock:** re-scope the engagement rather than quietly absorbing it.

## Observable output

One claim-bounded Creative Test Pack containing:

1. one-page strategy and incumbent diagnosis;
2. source-traced buyer-language bank;
3. claim map with approval ownership;
4. at least three materially different creative hypotheses;
5. five static ads with finished copy and production specifications;
6. one production-ready video concept with script, shot sequence, on-screen text, and edit notes;
7. an asset-to-hypothesis test map; and
8. QA and approval record.

The usability test is operational: the brand's designer, editor, or creator can produce or deploy the approved work without another strategy meeting, and the media owner can state what each asset is intended to teach.

## Known failure modes

1. Making six attractive variants that all test the same argument.
2. Using the Control-Beater name as a performance promise.
3. Beginning the 72-hour clock before the evidence and approval inputs are complete.
4. Treating a high-performing format as proof that its message caused the result.
5. Importing disease framing or unsupported outcomes from studies, reviews, competitors, or creators.
6. Treating public product language or ingredient research as independent finished-product substantiation.
7. Letting AI invent buyer language, proof, performance findings, or a legal conclusion.
8. Writing ideas that are strategically interesting but impossible for the actual production team to make.
9. Omitting the test map. Without it, the brand cannot tell what each asset was meant to teach.
10. Calling editorial claim screening legal clearance or guaranteed Meta acceptance.
11. Confusing adjacent spec work, category spending, or a polished portfolio with payment proof for the exact offer.

## Exclusions

1. Media buying, budget allocation, campaign operation, or attribution.
2. Creator sourcing, talent management, filming, or finished video editing unless separately scoped.
3. Legal advice, regulatory certification, or guaranteed platform approval.
4. New scientific substantiation created or supplied by Farrice.
5. Guaranteed control wins, ROAS, CPA, revenue, health outcomes, or commercial lift.
6. Unlimited revisions, additional products, additional audiences, or additional platforms.
