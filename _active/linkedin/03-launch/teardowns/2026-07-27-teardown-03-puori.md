## 1. CREDIT WHERE IT'S REAL

Puori does the single most concrete thing anyone in this category does, and I could not find another brand doing it.

*"Every single batch of every product is third-party tested against more than 200+ contaminants including heavy metals, pesticides and BPA."* Then: *"You can access the full test reports online, so you don't have to worry about what's in your supplements."* Then the part that matters: there is a QR code on the package, and it pulls the report for the batch in your hand.

Not a category average. Not a representative sample. Your tub.

The O3 formula is honest work too: 2,000mg of omega-3 per serving, 1,250mg EPA and 500mg DHA, from wild anchovy, and *"IFOS certified (5/5 stars) for freshness and purity."* Anchovy is the right call for heavy-metal load and they say why.

The founder line is plain and human: *"At Puori, we worry about what's in our supplements, so you don't have to."* — Oliver Amdrup-Chamby, CEO & Co-founder.

## 2. WHERE THE PROOF STOPS SHORT

**The hero is an adjective again.** *"One Ingredient. Unmatched Standards."* Unmatched by whom is never answered. This is the same unnamed comparative that Transparent Labs and Momentous both run, which tells you something about the category. Everyone claims a summit and nobody names the mountain.

**200+ contaminants is a number with nothing human attached.** It's an impressive fact and an empty feeling. Nobody has a relationship with contaminant number 147. The figure proves diligence to a buyer who already trusts you and does nothing at all to a buyer who doesn't.

**The QR code is one sentence.** The most remarkable asset the brand owns is described in a line on a secondary page. It is not on the hero, not in a video, not in the review flow, not in the post-purchase email as far as the public surface shows.

**The whey page is thin.** PW1 unflavored shows 11 reviews at 64% five-star. That's a visible weak spot on a hero product, and 64% reads worse than no rating at all next to the O3's 87%.

## 5. THREE ANGLES I'D SHIP

**Angle 1 — The scan is the campaign.**
One person. One tub. One phone. They read the batch number off the label, type it in, and the actual lab report for that actual tub loads on screen. Shot once, no script, no studio. That is the most concrete proof any brand in this category can put on camera, and Puori is the only one who can shoot it, because Puori is the only one who has it. Right now it's a sentence.

**Angle 2 — Answer Cher in public.**
She asked the question every single buyer has and almost nobody types out. Publish the comparison. Not against a named competitor, which is a legal fight nobody needs, but against publicly available category data on omega-3 purity and oxidation. Puori has its own published numbers and an IFOS rating to put on the other side of the line. A brand that publishes its test results and then declines to interpret them is doing the hard half and skipping the useful half.

**Angle 3 — Lead with the burps, close with the lab.**
The customers already wrote the hook eight different ways. No smell, no aftertaste, no burps. That is the felt benefit, it's free, it's in their own words, and it's currently buried under contaminant counts. Lead with the thing a person notices on day one and let the testing be the reason it's true. Purity is the receipt. It was never the pitch.

**And one repair:** the 11-review, 64% five-star whey page needs review volume before it needs anything else. A post-purchase ask on that SKU alone would move it more than any copy change.

---

## 6. THE COMPLIANCE LINE

The current claims are conservative and clean. *"Supports heart health," "Supports brain function," "Supports normal vision"* are textbook structure/function phrasing with the disclaimer attached. No exposure there.

The angle that carries real legal weight is Angle 2, so the shape of it matters. Naming Costco or Kirkland in a comparative purity claim is a Lanham Act invitation and a fight with a company that has more lawyers. Publishing your own COA numbers against publicly available category data is a factual statement about your own product, which is defensible and boring in the good way.

On Cher's study reference: the honest answer beats the confident one. If the evidence on a benefit has limits, saying so out loud is the most on-brand move a purity company can make, and it is the one thing a less careful competitor structurally cannot copy.
