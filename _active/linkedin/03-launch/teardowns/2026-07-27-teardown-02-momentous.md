## 1. CREDIT WHERE IT'S REAL

This is the deepest evidence surface of any brand I looked at this week, and it isn't close.

The creatine page cites six systematic reviews and meta-analyses, each with a plain-language pull-quote. It publishes the testing date and the testing house: *"Tested by Light Labs in NOV 2025. 13 substances tested for quality."* The standards page states the spend: *"We spend up to 2% of our revenue on independent third-party testing and certification."*

And this line, which is the best sentence on the entire site: *"Our relentless commitment to doing things differently has won us 10 U.S. government contracts as well as the trust of all 32 NFL teams and 200+ collegiate and professional sports teams."*

Thirty-two out of thirty-two. That is not a testimonial, it's a census. Nobody in this category can buy it, borrow it, or fake it.

Also real: *"Momentous never uses proprietary blends or mystery formulas."* And the framing line, *"As the anti-supplement supplement company,"* which is a genuinely good piece of positioning.

---

## 2. WHERE THE PROOF STOPS SHORT

**The three bullets under the buy button.** On the creatine page, the first thing a buyer reads after the price is:

> *"New Signature Spec creatine standard"*
> *"Ultrafine powder for exceptional mixability"*
> *"Unrivaled 6-stage testing & certification"*

Three lines, and all three are about the factory. Not one of them is about what creatine does to a person. On a creatine page, the highest-value real estate on the site is selling the plant.

**The hero.** *"Our Highest Quality Creatine, Ever."* Then: *"Superior quality, superior manufacturing, superior mixability."* Three superlatives, and the third one is mixability. Mixability is a convenience. It is being given equal weight to quality.

**The number that moves.** The homepage says *"10x fewer impurities, 2.5x stricter heavy metal limits, no PFAS or microplastics."* The product page for the same product says *"2–5x tighter heavy metal limits."*

Same site, same day, same creatine, two different numbers about the same test. For a brand whose whole argument is precision, a rounding error about precision is the one mistake that costs more than it looks like it costs.

**The unnamed baseline.** *"10x fewer impurities"* than what? *"Industry standard"* is never defined anywhere on the page. This is the same structural move the standards page correctly criticizes when the rest of the category does it: *"The supplement category is low-trust for a reason: overhyped claims, ineffective formulas, and trends prioritized over truth."*

**32 of 32 is on an interior page.** The strongest proof asset in the entire category is on /pages/momentous-standard. The homepage hero is about creatine mixability.

## 4. THE GAP IN ONE LINE

Purity is a floor. No one has ever felt a floor.

Momentous has bought the highest floor in the industry and is still standing on it in the marketing.

---

## 5. THREE ANGLES I'D SHIP

**Angle 1 — Lead with the locker rooms.**
32 out of 32 NFL teams belongs in the hero, not on a standards page. It is the one claim in this category that requires no explanation, no citation, and no trust from the reader. A person who has never heard of NSF understands a whole league. Everything else on the standards page becomes the proof underneath it instead of the argument itself.

**Angle 2 — Give the three bullets back to the body.**
Keep one manufacturing line. Make the other two about what happens to a person: strength and power under load, and mental fatigue when sleep falls short. Both are already substantiated on the same page by cited meta-analyses. This costs nothing, requires no new evidence, and changes what the buyer reads in the first four seconds.

**Angle 3 — Own the brain.**
Every creatine brand in America is fighting over the weight room. Momentous is one of the few with the citations already on the page to fight somewhere else: *"Creatine monohydrate in adults shows small but reliable improvements in memory and faster performance on attention and processing-speed tasks."* The buyer for that is not a lifter. It is a forty-two-year-old who slept five hours and has a meeting. That is a bigger market than the squat rack, it is currently unclaimed, and Momentous already paid for the evidence to enter it.

**And one repair, not an angle:** pick a single heavy-metal number and use it everywhere. Then define the baseline it is measured against, or drop the multiplier and state the actual limit. The absolute number is more impressive than the comparison anyway, and it can't be challenged.

---

## 6. THE COMPLIANCE LINE

The cognitive claims are the higher-risk surface, and they are already handled correctly: asterisked, structure/function worded, and supported on-page by named systematic reviews. That is the right architecture. Leading with cognition would not increase exposure, because the evidence is already sitting under it.

The real exposure is in the opposite place. *"10x fewer impurities"* and *"2.5x stricter heavy metal limits"* are comparative superiority claims against an undefined baseline. Unnamed-comparison claims are the standard challenge target for a competitor complaint, and the fix is cheap: define the baseline, or publish the absolute limits and let the reader do the math.

*"Unrivaled 6-stage testing"* has the same shape. Rival unnamed, claim unfalsifiable, therefore challengeable.
