## 1. CREDIT WHERE IT'S REAL

An outside lab tested six creatine gummies. Four failed. Two of them had no detectable creatine at all, and a third would have taken 800 gummies to reach a working dose.

Create came back at **4.59 grams against a 4.5 gram label.**

That is the whole ballgame in this category and they won it. Creatine degrades into creatinine in water and heat, which is exactly what making a gummy involves, and it is the reason most of the shelf is selling candy with a claim printed on it.

Their quality page is not decorative either. Creatinine capped at 100 ppm. Dicyandiamide at 50 ppm. Dihydrotriazine at 3 ppm. Mercury, cadmium, lead and arsenic each at 0.1 ppm. *"100% of Create's purity and potency testing is third-party"* and *"every batch is NSF Certified for Sport®."* Gummies pulled and retested at eighteen months, still meeting label.

Nobody publishes numbers like that unless they hold, because publishing them is how you get caught.

They also didn't round up. Three gummies is 4.5 grams and they printed 4.5 grams, in a category where 5 is the number everyone claims.

## 2. CLAIM → PROOF → GAP

**Claim A.** Homepage hero: *"Creatine for Everybody."* Under it: *"Save Big and Have the Hottest Summer Ever."*

- **Proven:** the sale is real and the mass-market read is right. Creatine is going mainstream and Target is rolling it out nationally.
- **Gap:** the mainstream buyer's first question in 2026 is not which flavor. Lab tests on gummies made national health press. The hero answers a question nobody is asking yet and skips the one everybody is.

**Claim B.** Product page FAQ, last item, below *"Are your gummies safe for children?"*: *"Yes, our core creatine gummies are the only NSF for Sport Certified® creatine gummies in the world! They've undergone extensive content verification and safety testing."*

- **Proven:** yes, and NSF for Sport is the most expensive certification in this business.
- **Gap:** the strongest sentence they own is the last answer in a collapsed accordion. Nothing on the site says it out loud where a buyer is deciding.

**Claim C.** Trust bar and review header: *"5.0 Stars"* and *"100K+ Reviews"* — sitting one scroll above the widget's own *"Customer reviews 4.82 · 4,675 reviews."*

- **Proven:** 4.82 across 4,675 is an excellent number, and the 100K may well be an all-channel count.
- **Gap:** two review numbers on one page, and the bigger one is rounder. Every other brand does this. Create is the one brand that can't afford to, because their entire position is that their numbers survive an outside check.

**Claim D.** Science page, the MEMORY section. Chart titled *"Memory Improvement With Creatine,"* plotting younger adults 11–31 against older adults 66–76. Footnote: *"Volek JS, Duncan ND, Mazzetti SA, et al. Performance and muscle fiber adaptations to creatine supplementation and heavy resistance training."*

- **Proven:** creatine and cognition is a live, real research area, and their named advisors — Dr. Abbie Smith-Ryan and Dr. Darren Candow — are among the actual people publishing in it.
- **Gap:** that footnote is a bench-press and muscle-fiber study sitting under a memory chart. The same citation also appears under the strength chart, where it belongs. On the one page carrying two real researchers' names, the memory claim is sourced to a lifting paper.

**Claim E.** Benefit bar, both product pages: *"BUILD LEAN MUSCLE · INCREASE ENERGY · IMPROVES RECOVERY · BOOSTS COGNITION."*

- **Proven:** structure/function territory, and the FDA disclaimer is in the footer where it should be.
- **Gap:** "BOOSTS COGNITION" is the strongest verb on the weakest-evidenced of the four, and it's the one their own memory chart is under-sourced on. See §6.

## 5. THREE ANGLES I'D SHIP

**Angle 1 — The number, in the hero.** Replace the seasonal sale line with the test result and the source. Four of six gummies failed an outside test; ours measured 4.59 against a 4.5 label. This is the only angle a competitor cannot copy this quarter, because copying it means paying for testing that might not go their way. Run it while the category's failure is still in the news.

**Angle 2 — Take the FAQ answer out of the FAQ.** "The only NSF Certified for Sport creatine gummy in the world" is a category-of-one claim buried under a question about children. It belongs on the pack, the PDP above the fold, the Target shelf-talker, and the first line of every retail sell sheet.

**Angle 3 — Fix the memory footnote before somebody screenshots it.** Then use the repair as content: a short piece from Smith-Ryan or Candow on what creatine and cognition research actually shows, with the real citations under the real charts. The brand that gets caught being careful about its own footnotes owns a position nobody else is even competing for.

---

## 6. THE COMPLIANCE LINE

Dose and purity are the safest claim surface in this category. Stating that a batch is NSF Certified for Sport, or that a serving measured 4.59 grams, is a fact about the product, not a health claim, and carries no substantiation burden.

The exposure is on the other side of the page. "BOOSTS COGNITION" is a structure/function claim, and the chart supporting it is footnoted to a resistance-training study. A claim is only as substantiated as the evidence actually cited for it, and a mismatched footnote is worse than no footnote, because it looks like a citation until somebody reads it. The disclaimer in the footer does not repair a citation that doesn't say what the chart says.

Cheapest fix in the whole teardown, and it's a fifteen-minute job.
