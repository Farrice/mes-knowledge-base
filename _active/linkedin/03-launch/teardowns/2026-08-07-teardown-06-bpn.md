## 1. CREDIT WHERE IT'S REAL

Two facts sit on BPN's product pages that most of their competitors cannot put on theirs.

The whey is *"NSF Certified for Sport® to verify it does not contain unsafe levels of contaminants or prohibited substances."* The creatine is NSF Certified for Sport and made from **Creapure®**, produced in Trostberg, Germany — the named source, not "premium creatine."

NSF Certified for Sport is the certification that matters when losing costs you a career. It is what a drug-tested athlete's compliance officer looks for. It is expensive, it is per-batch, and it is the reason a college strength coach can hand somebody a tub without taking on liability.

The rest of the business is real too. Since 2012. 920,000+ customers. Veteran-owned. 4.9 stars across 9,263 reviews on one protein and 3,304 on the creatine. That is not a brand with a marketing problem in any ordinary sense. People love it.

---

## 2. CLAIM → PROOF → GAP

**Claim A.** Homepage hero: *"GO ONE MORE."* Sub: *"JOIN THE 920,000+ CUSTOMERS Since 2012, BPN has been on a mission to equip people with the supplements, resources, and community to Go One More every day."*

- **Proven:** the community is real and the number is theirs to claim.
- **Gap:** the hero sells belonging. Belonging is rentable. Any founder with a camera and a decade of consistency can build it, several are building it right now, and the day the founder posts less, the moat drains. The unrentable asset is the certification, and it is not in the hero, the sub, or anywhere above the fold.

**Claim B.** Site architecture. There is no quality page. `/pages/quality` returns a 404, and no science, testing, ingredient-sourcing, or quality page is linked in the navigation or the footer. The strongest claims exist as badge text — *"THIRD-PARTY CERTIFIED,"* *"All BPN supplements are third party tested for banned substances, quality standards and label accuracy"* — and a slogan, *"THE BARE STANDARD: Built on quality and proven by results, without compromise."*

- **Proven:** the badges are accurate.
- **Gap:** "The Bare Standard" is a promise with no page behind it. A standard that isn't written down is a slogan. Competitors publish COAs, name their labs, and post certificate numbers; BPN holds a better certification than most of them and has nowhere to send anyone who wants to check.

**Claim C.** About page. *"Since 2012, BPN has been on a mission to equip people with the tools, resources, and community to Go One More everyday."* Testing and certification appear only in separate badge sections further down.

- **Proven:** consistent with the brand.
- **Gap:** the origin story is the product story. Nothing in it explains why the label is trustworthy, which is the question a first-time buyer who isn't already in the community is actually asking.

**Claim D.** Product claims: *"25 grams of protein per serving to improve recovery and build muscle,"* a *"whey-casein protein blend,"* creatine *"shown to increase strength, power and performance."*

- **Proven:** standard, defensible, correctly phrased.
- **Gap:** identical to every competitor's phrasing. The differentiated fact — Creapure from a named German plant, NSF per batch — is listed as a spec bullet next to "GLUTEN FREE."

## 4. THE GAP IN ONE LINE

The product is built for people who get drug-tested and the marketing is built for people who get inspired, and only one of those two things can't be copied.

---

## 5. THREE ANGLES I'D SHIP

**Angle 1 — Build the page that doesn't exist.** One URL: the labs, the certificate numbers, what NSF Certified for Sport actually screens for, what Creapure is and why the plant in Trostberg matters, what gets rejected and how often. "The Bare Standard" becomes a document instead of a slogan. Everything below depends on this existing.

**Angle 2 — Sell to the person who can't afford to be wrong.** Drug-tested athletes, military, first responders, anyone whose job does urinalysis. Their existing customer base already skews this way and the certification was bought for exactly them. This is a segment competitors can't chase without paying for the same cert, and the copy writes itself from the certificate.

**Angle 3 — Put the cert in the founder's mouth.** The community is the distribution and the founder is the channel; the missing content is the one nobody in the category makes. What per-batch testing costs, what it caught, why the company keeps paying for it when 920,000 people would buy on flavor anyway. That is the piece that converts belonging into a reason.

---

## 6. THE COMPLIANCE LINE

Little exposure here, which is worth saying plainly. Protein and creatine claims are the most substantiated in the category, and BPN phrases them conservatively — "shown to increase strength, power and performance" is supported and correctly hedged.

The one thing to tighten: *"third party tested for banned substances, quality standards and label accuracy"* is stated as a blanket across "all BPN supplements," while NSF Certified for Sport appears product by product. If the certification does not cover the whole catalog, the blanket sentence is the line to fix. Naming which products carry which certification costs nothing and removes the only claim on the site that outruns its proof.

## 10. ADVERSARIAL PASS (run before drafting)

**The founder's best rebuttal:** *"Community IS the moat. We built 920,000 customers on it and no certification page would have added one of them."* — True historically, and the piece concedes it plainly ("it works, genuinely"). The argument isn't that community failed; it's that it's the asset that can be competed away and the cert is the one that can't. Both can run.

**Second rebuttal:** *"Nobody reads quality pages."* — Buyers don't; procurement, retail buyers, team dietitians, and compliance officers do, and those are the doors Angle 2 opens.

**Where I could be unfair:** calling belonging "rentable" is a judgment, not a fact, and it's phrased as mine. I also can't see whether a quality page exists behind a login or in an app — checked the public site only, stated in §0.

**Where I stayed inside the fence:** no claim about product failures, revenue, or founder behavior; the catalog-wide certification question is raised as a question rather than an accusation.
