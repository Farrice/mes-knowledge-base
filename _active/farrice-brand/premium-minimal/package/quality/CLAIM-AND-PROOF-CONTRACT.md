# Claim and Proof Contract

## Governing boundary

The system may organize client-approved evidence and show editorial message boundaries. It does not provide legal, medical, scientific, regulatory, or compliance approval.

## Never add

- Invented science or substantiation.
- Product efficacy beyond supplied approved evidence.
- ROAS, conversion, revenue, reach, ranking, or performance results.
- Client logos, testimonials, quotes, or case-study language without explicit permission.
- Unconfirmed credentials.
- Compliance badges, green checks, legal seals, or other approval theater.
- Finished-campaign or guaranteed-outcome implications.

## Lived authority may establish

- Fluency in buyer goals and product choice.
- Familiarity with labels, formulas, doses, promises, and purchase behavior.
- Farrice's willingness to distinguish supported products from weak or overstated ones.

It may not establish campaign-performance, medical, scientific, legal, regulatory, or compliance authority.

## Fictional demonstrations

Every fictional asset must visibly state:

FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION · NOT CLIENT WORK

The label must remain legible when the asset is viewed alone. Fictional work may demonstrate format or reasoning; it may never occupy a public proof position or imply a paid client result.

## Proof-boundary language

Use plain fields such as:

- What supplied proof supports.
- What returns for qualified review.
- Outside supplied proof.

Use an editorial footer such as:

Editorial strategy is not legal, medical, scientific, regulatory, or compliance approval.

## Stop condition

If an asset requires a claim, credential, logo, outcome, or evidence source not supplied and approved, stop that field and mark it for qualified review. Do not create persuasive placeholder proof.
