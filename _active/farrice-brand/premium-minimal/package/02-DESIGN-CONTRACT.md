# Farrice Cain Premium Minimal — Design Contract

## Design intent

A commissioned decision object translated into a personal brand: quiet, exact, contemporary, and decisive. The work should feel expensive because the thinking has been edited—not because the surface is decorated.

The cultural reference is Kith-like restraint applied to business communication. This is a taste principle only. Never copy Kith logos, layouts, campaigns, photography, product styling, or fashion codes.

## Three mood words

**Restrained. Contemporary. Decisive.**

Every choice must contribute to at least one of these words. A decorative choice that contributes to none should be removed.

## Two modes, one system

### Master-brand mode

- Masthead: `FARRICE CAIN`
- Descriptor: `CREATIVE STRATEGY FOR SUPPLEMENT + PERFORMANCE BRANDS`
- Dominant content: category point of view, lived observation, teardown, or framework
- Offer reference: restrained footer or final frame only when commercially relevant

### Offer mode

- Masthead: `THE ANGLE MAP`
- Author: `FARRICE CAIN`
- Dominant content: three campaign arguments and one recommendation
- Route grammar: permitted only when the content genuinely compares choices

### Approved-banner exception

The current launch banner predates final token normalization and uses `#F3F0EA` and `#151514`. Preserve that approved file exactly. Do not treat its legacy values as new system tokens. Any future banner rebuild should use the canonical palette only after Farrice approves the new asset.

## Typography

- Family: **Helvetica Neue only** for approved final production.
- Weights: 400 regular, 500 medium, and 700 bold.
- Display tracking: `-0.025em`.
- Functional labels: uppercase with `+0.16em` tracking.
- Headlines: sentence case.
- Display leading: close and optically balanced; never touching.
- Secondary text: Graphite or reduced emphasis, never weak enough to become unreadable.

Do not introduce serif type, italics, outlined type, decorative quotation marks, faux small caps, or exaggerated luxury tracking.

## Grid and spatial law

- Twelve columns on every surface.
- Keep at least one-third of most compositions open.
- Maximum three hierarchy levels.
- One dominant idea per surface.
- Use alignment, rules, and whitespace before containers.
- Apply optical balance after mathematical alignment.
- At 320 px display width, the subject and dominant thought must remain understandable.

## Rules and lines

- Hairline: 1 px.
- Structural line: 2 px.
- Recommended route: 6 px.
- Quiet routes: 1–2 px.
- One dark interruption per sequence maximum.

## Components

### Functional masthead

A small uppercase identity label at top left. It is not a decorative logo.

### Field index

Use `FIELD / 01`, `NOTE / 02`, or a two-digit page number. Use Stone at large scale or Graphite at label scale.

### Decision line

A thin horizontal rule that establishes structure. It becomes heavier only when making a recommendation.

### Three-route grammar

Three parallel strokes differentiated by number, position, and weight. Never assign decorative colors to routes. Use the motif only when real choices are being compared.

### Proof boundary

Two or three aligned fields separated by rules: supported, qualified review, and outside supplied proof. Do not use warning colors, shields, checks, badges, or compliance theater.

### Dark recommendation

Ink background with Paper text. Reserve it for the consequential decision—not general drama.

## Portrait policy

- Use only the original supplied portrait pixels from the separately governed private identity add-on.
- Crop and uniform scale are allowed.
- Face regeneration, replacement, inpainting, reshaping, beauty filtering, face-specific color alteration, or AI upscaling are prohibited.
- Portraits are occasional authority or story devices. They are not required on every asset.

## Prohibited signals

- Serif type.
- Gradients, shadows, rounded pills, badges, seals, ornamental icons, contours, or card-heavy layouts.
- Black-and-gold luxury theater.
- Decorative route colors.
- Stock supplement imagery used as generic credibility.
- Stale approval labels on public assets.
- Logos for the Decision Book, Angle Cards, or Build First Card.
- Route lines used as decoration when no choice is being compared.
