# Farrice Cain — Brand Foundation

## Brand hierarchy

| Level | Job | Governing rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Farrice Cain** | Durable trust container | The person, lived experience, judgment, and point of view survive any one offer |
| **Category point of view** | Reason to follow | Honest products, real proof, and the campaign argument should be chosen before more creative is produced |
| **The Angle Map** | Flagship paid expression | One concrete way Farrice applies the point of view to a live message decision |
| **Decision Book / Angle Cards / Build First Card** | Functional deliverable parts | Never separate brands, logos, or public offers |

**Offer-removal test:** If The Angle Map disappeared tomorrow, a cold visitor should still understand whom Farrice helps, what he believes, and why his judgment is relevant.

## Strategic core

**Internal brand idea:** Stronger arguments for honest products.

This is a strategy line—not a newly approved public tagline.

### Approved public anchors

- “I don't make weak products louder.”
- “I don't turn a fairy-dust dose into a heroic headline.”
- Honest products with real proof deserve an argument strong enough to earn attention.
- A new hook or format is not necessarily a new campaign angle.
- More creative can become a socially safer substitute for choosing one message direction.

## Primary audience

Established supplement, recovery, functional-nutrition, or performance-brand founders and marketing leads who already have:

- a real product and approved evidence;
- a live launch, retail push, reformulation, new ingredient, repositioning, or creative-cycle decision;
- a team or agency ready to produce; and
- a need to choose what the product should argue before funding more creative.

## Explicit exclusions

- Pre-revenue idea seekers.
- Buyers seeking finished campaign production.
- Unsupported-product promoters.
- Performance-guarantee seekers.
- Anyone asking Farrice to replace qualified legal, scientific, medical, regulatory, or compliance review.

## Authority posture

Farrice speaks as an experienced category practitioner with bounded strategic authority—not a campaign-performance celebrity.

### Lived authority facts

- Five years at The Vitamin Shoppe.
- Coaching since 2008.
- More than 1,000 people coached.
- Tier X at Equinox.
- Independent personal-training and holistic-coaching experience.

These establish fluency in buyer desire, product choice, labels, doses, promises, and human behavior. They do not establish ROAS, conversion, revenue, medical, scientific, legal, regulatory, or compliance authority.

Until real permissioned client evidence exists:

- teach from lived observation;
- make the reasoning inspectable;
- use clearly labeled demonstrations and teardowns;
- leave public proof positions empty; and
- do not use testimonials, client logos, results, or unconfirmed credentials.

## Two public content lanes

### 01 — The decision before the creative

The difference between a genuine campaign angle and another hook, creator, format, or layout. Show the recognizable scene in which a team keeps producing because choosing one direction feels harder.

### 02 — Product truth a buyer can believe

Product truth, approved proof, claim boundaries, formula transparency, dose judgment, and the distance between what the buyer hopes and what the product can support.

Retail and coaching are lived texture inside these lanes—not a competing third identity.

## Voice invariants

### Use

- Spoken, direct, concrete language.
- A recognizable buyer scene before a framework.
- One thought per paragraph.
- Plain distinctions: hook versus angle, proof versus promise, product versus story.
- A clear recommendation and the trade-off behind it.
- Specific lived details Farrice can personally stand behind.
- The positive standard beside the refusal: honest products should not whisper.

### Avoid

- Strategy jargon and internal process language.
- Synthetic anecdotes or an AI imitation of Farrice.
- Generic prestige claims such as “world-class,” “premium,” or “top operator.”
- Endless options presented as expertise.
- Biography dumps with no link to the buyer's current decision.
- Inflating retail or coaching experience into campaign-performance proof.

Farrice owns the final voice pass. Writers and AI may structure and tighten; they may not fabricate personality.
