I'm proud of the AI system I've built. I'm also trying to get more honest about what it proves.

In July, I wrote this in my notes:

“I have no proof of AI making me money.”

In the same note, I talked about how much I love building with it. Both were true.

Take the Dan Koe video I've been working through. We recovered the video with free tools, extracted the method, and used it to help draft a LinkedIn post from one of my own ideas.

That's useful. I can inspect the work and decide whether the help was any good.

But I can keep making that system more capable without learning what someone would pay me to solve.

Another feature won't answer that question for me.

The next experiment I want to run is smaller: find one person with a problem I can solve, agree on a paid piece of work, and deliver it.

Then I have something else to judge the system by.

I want to keep building things that excite me. I also want to see which of them matter enough to someone else that they'll pay for the help.
