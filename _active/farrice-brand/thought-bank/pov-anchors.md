# POV Anchors — Voice Rules

These are **identity-level reframes** that anchor Farrice's voice across all content. Different from hooks (which are deployable specifics): POV anchors are general, durable rules that should pass through downstream workflows as filters.

When a content workflow produces a draft, the draft should be checkable against these anchors — does it honor them, or did the writer (AI or otherwise) soften past one?

---

## Active POV anchors

### 1. Empathy = doing what it takes for them, with positive intent

**Captured**: 2026-05-05 ([inbox source](inbox/2026-05-05.md))
**Theme**: [empathy-vs-manipulation](themes/empathy-vs-manipulation.md)

**The rule**: Saying things in the specific way the audience needs to hear them — even if it doesn't make me look good — is empathy when paired with positive intent and love. Without that intent, same techniques become manipulation. The line is intent, not technique.

**Implication for content**:
- Soft, hedged, "professional" copy that protects the writer's image at the audience's expense = anti-empathy
- Direct, stakes-raised, "this is what you actually need to hear" copy with care visible = empathy
- Performative ethics ("but I would never persuade!") = cowardice with extra steps

### 2. Marketers/strategists who refuse to read their audience are choosing comfort over outcome

**Captured**: 2026-05-05 ([inbox source](inbox/2026-05-05.md))
**Theme**: [parenting-as-marketing](themes/parenting-as-marketing.md), [empathy-vs-manipulation](themes/empathy-vs-manipulation.md)

**The rule**: "Everyone wants to say the pretty stuff that makes them look good, sound good, and feel good about themselves. Nobody wants to talk truly the way the audience or the ICP needs to hear you in order to get what you want from them."

**Implication for content**:
- Don't write to feel smart. Write to land.
- Pretty-sounding ≠ effective. Often inversely correlated.
- The writer's discomfort = signal you're closer to what the reader needs

### 3. Presence is the discipline; screens are the temptation

**Captured**: 2026-05-05 ([inbox source](inbox/2026-05-05.md))
**Theme**: [presence-vs-screens](themes/presence-vs-screens.md)

**The rule**: Work happens when JJ sleeps. Presence happens when he's awake. If screens enter the awake-time, the trade has to be honest: I better goddamn produce while he's on them. No half-presence — half-presence trains him to expect partial attention from people who claim to love him.

**Implication for content**:
- Cheap dopamine has a real cost; adults just stopped feeling it
- "I'm sacrificing for my family" is often the lie we tell to justify screens-as-babysitter
- Time-compression / work-window discipline is a content angle (especially for solopreneur audience)

### 4. No cheap question signoffs

**Captured**: 2026-05-05 — taste-pass calibration session ([source: variants file](../../content/linkedin-posts/2026-05-05-jj-manipulation-variants.md))
**Theme**: cross-cutting voice rule (applies to all content)

**The rule**: Generic question closes used to extract engagement (e.g., *"What's the X for you?"* / *"Who was it actually for?"* / *"What's your version?"*) are now banned. They feel cheap. They optimize for comment-count, not for actual recognition.

**Why this rule exists**: All three Variant A/B/C drafts of the JJ manipulation hook closed on a generic question. Farrice flagged this as voice-degradation: "asking questions is good, but it feels cheap now." Question-extraction at scale is an AI-style move that creators in our content ecosystem are starting to over-use; it telegraphs "I needed engagement so I asked for it" rather than "I had something to say."

**Implication for content** — voice-true closes invite recognition without extracting:

| Banned (cheap question signoffs) | Voice-true alternatives |
|---|---|
| "What's the last thing you softened?" | **Image close** — concrete final image that lands the whole piece (Cole atomic-essay move) |
| "Who was it actually for?" | **Declaration close** — clear statement of what's true / what changed / what to do |
| "What's your version?" | **Earned silence** — close on a fact and let it sit |
| "Sound familiar?" | **Naming what they feel** — the sentence that recognizes their internal state |
| "Drop yours in the comments." | **Mirror without question** — "you've felt this" type statement that recognizes without prompting |
| "Anyone else?" | **Litotes** (Sean Macintyre) — understated assertion that lands harder than direct |
| "What would you add?" | **Smallest action** — specific, non-generic next move (different from generic engagement bait) |
| "Curious what you think." | **Bookend admission** — return to opening hook with deepened understanding (the new A close pattern) |
| "Have you experienced this?" | **Concrete future image** — return to the world with a specific time/scene (the new B close pattern: *"Tomorrow JJ wakes up at 6:43"*) |

**Edge case — when questions ARE OK:**
A specific, content-driven question that *advances the post's argument* and could only be asked here (not transferable to any other post) is fine. Test: would this exact question make sense pasted onto someone else's post on the same topic? If yes = generic = banned. If no = earned = OK.

**How to apply**: When a draft closes on a question, paste the question onto a hypothetical generic post by another creator on a similar topic. If the question still works there, rewrite the close.

### 5. Speak their exact language, even when it sounds insane to anyone else

**Captured**: 2026-05-05 — V1→V2 revision pass on JJ manipulation post ([source: variants file](../../content/linkedin-posts/2026-05-05-jj-manipulation-variants.md), [worked example: V2 P3 + P9](../../content/linkedin-posts/2026-05-05-jj-manipulation-variants.md))
**Theme**: [empathy-vs-manipulation](themes/empathy-vs-manipulation.md), [parenting-as-marketing](themes/parenting-as-marketing.md)
**Relationship to other anchors**: This is the *prescription* to POV #2's *diagnosis*. POV #2 names the failure ("refusing to read your audience = comfort over outcome"). POV #5 names what the fix actually requires.

**The rule**: Speak the audience's exact private language. The specific words, framings, and internal logic that only land inside their world. Even when it sounds absurd, weird, or alienating to anyone outside that world. Especially then. Generic, broadly-palatable language is what writers reach for when they'd rather not land than risk landing wrong. Refusing to use the audience's actual language because it would isolate non-audience is choosing your own image over their outcome.

**Worked example (JJ post V2):** In Farrice's house, "everything goes to sleep when JJ does — the lights, the dog, his toys." That's how JJ understands the world. So the bedtime rule landed in JJ's economy: "if he wants his toy to play with later, he has to sleep now, because if he doesn't, the toy isn't going to wake up." To anyone outside that house, the framing sounds insane. To JJ, it's the only frame that lands. The principle generalizes: every audience has a private language, and the writer/marketer's job is to speak it — including the parts that read absurd from outside.

**Implication for content**:
- The audience's actual words, framings, internal logic > the writer's polished/professional/general translation
- "Will this isolate readers who aren't the ICP?" is not a problem to solve — it's the *signal you're closer*. Broad palatability across non-ICP and ICP simultaneously is usually the sign of a softened piece
- The trade is real and conscious: you choose landing for the one over palatability for the many
- Test for any line: *"Could a writer working for any audience in this category write this exact sentence?"* If yes = generic = soften failure. If no, only this audience's people would write it this way = on target
- Signature crystallized phrases, niche idioms, in-group references, and "weird" specificity are features, not bugs
- Pretty-sounding ≠ effective; this anchor + POV #2 together make the inversion explicit

**The trade-off, named**: Most marketers and writers won't do this because they're scared of isolating people who aren't their audience. Those people *will* be isolated. That isolation is the price. The math is: a piece that lands with 1,000 of your actual ICP > a piece that's faintly readable to 50,000 people who will never buy/follow/recognize. Broad palatability is what you choose when you'd rather not land than risk landing wrong.

**How to apply (draft check)**:
- Highlight every line that could appear, unchanged, in a generic LinkedIn post by any creator in your category. Those are softening points. Rewrite each in the audience's specific private language — even the "weird" parts
- If a draft has zero sentences that would feel jarring or absurd to a non-audience reader, the draft hasn't reached its audience yet
- If you find yourself flattening an idiom, in-group reference, or niche framing because "outsiders won't get it," stop. That's the line that was going to land

## How POV anchors get used

When running any content workflow:
1. The workflow produces a draft
2. Before delivery, check the draft against active POV anchors
3. If a draft contradicts or softens past an anchor → flag and rewrite
4. New anchors get added here as they emerge from inbox/themes captures

This file is also a **voice-recovery tool** — if a piece feels off but the writer can't name why, re-reading active POV anchors usually surfaces the violation.
