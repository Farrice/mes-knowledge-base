The complete brand system in one document.

The PARALLAX brand sits on a single design philosophy: the Virgil Abloh 3% Rule. Take the most familiar premium-newsletter format — clean typography on a solid background — and change exactly 3% of it. The 3% is the offset. One letter in violet. The visual hook of an editorial publication that doesn't apologize for being plural.

This document is the canonical reference. Everything else — assets, templates, voice, photography — derives from it.

01 · Brand Essence

The brand in one sentence.

Brand Essence
A polymath's newsletter that flatters the reader's intelligence through restraint — and quietly refuses to pick a lane.

What this is

PARALLAX is the publication for people who were told to pick one thing and never could. The visual identity is built on the premise that recognition is more valuable than sophistication at the early-brand stage. A wordmark with one violet letter is recognizable after a single exposure. Translucent geometric planes are not.

The cultural intersection is specific: Japanese minimalism meets hip-hop typography. Muji meets Madlib. Tadao Ando concrete walls with Griselda Records swagger. This is the founder's actual cultural register — biracial, polymath, technologist, modern philosopher, stay-at-home dad. Not cyberpunk. Not tech-startup. Not wellness-blog. Editorial gravitas with a quiet kind of confidence.

The emotional architecture

When a reader encounters anything PARALLAX, the emotional response should be — in order:

  • Recognition — "this is for people like me"
  • Relief — "I don't have to explain myself here"
  • Intrigue — "what's the slight shift I'm noticing?"

Never: "this looks cool." Never: "this is sophisticated." Never: "this is a tech newsletter."

02 · The Three Anchors

Visual hook. Emotional core. Cultural anchor.

Every PARALLAX asset, sentence, and decision passes through these three filters. If a piece doesn't carry one of the three, cut it or rewrite it.

Visual Hook
The Offset
Every typographic element carries a subtle, deliberate misalignment that creates visual tension. Not random. Not chaotic. The kind of thing a designer notices immediately and a non-designer feels without knowing why. The second L in violet — that's the offset.
Emotional Core
Quiet Defiance
The feeling of someone who stopped apologizing for seeing the world differently. Not aggressive. Not rebellious. Settled. The visual equivalent of a person who walks into a room and doesn't adjust themselves to fit it.
Cultural Anchor
Muji × Madlib
Japanese minimalism meets hip-hop album typography. Tadao Ando concrete walls with Griselda Records swagger. The restraint of editorial publishing with the swagger of confessional rap. This is the actual cultural intersection of the founder.
03 · Color System

Two colors. That's the whole system.

Every iconic publication identity uses 1-2 colors, maximum. Five colors is a committee decision that never punched. When everything is monochrome except one color, that one color becomes yours.

Ink
#1C1C1E
Near-black. Not indigo, not navy. The absence of pretension. The dark canvas on which Parallax lives.
Violet
#7B61FF
The 3%. The single point of attention. Used only where intent should land — never as decoration.
Warm White
#F5F0EB
Body text on dark. Softer than pure white. Reads warmer at long-form length. Carries craft.

Pure #FFFFFF never appears. Pure #000000 never appears. Both feel cheap by comparison. The palette commits to its specific darks and warms with conviction.

Color usage matrix

  • Ink — All primary surfaces (covers, banners, cards, web body). Default background.
  • Warm White — All body text on ink. Always. (Pure white if-and-only-if Substack's editor forces it.)
  • Violet — Reserved for: the second L of the wordmark, primary CTA buttons, edition numbers, divider rules under wordmarks/quotes. One violet element per composition is the maximum. Two is sprawl. Zero is acceptable.

Accessibility

Warm white on ink: 14:1 contrast — well over WCAG AAA. Violet on ink: 4.05:1 — passes AA for large/bold text only (≥18pt regular, ≥14pt bold). Violet on warm white: 4.4:1 — same large/bold restriction. Never use violet for body copy.

05 · The Wordmark

The wordmark is the logo. One artifact, not two.

PARALLAX does not have a logo mark. The "Shifting P" / "Prism Mark" / "Layered Lens" instinct is wrong — the word is the brand.

Wordmark · Dark Variant
Primary identity asset. Warm-white letters with the second L in violet. 1344×256.
Wordmark · Light Variant
Ink letters on warm-white surfaces. Violet L stays — that's the brand constant.

The rule of the violet L

Counting P-A-R-A-L-L-A-X, the 6th letter — the second L — is the one in violet. Every other letter is warm white (or ink, on light). Buried in the middle, the letter you don't expect to notice. The first letter would be obvious. The last would be try-hard. A letter that quietly breaks the pattern in the middle — that's PARALLAX made visible.

Wordmark specs

  • Font: Space Grotesk Bold (700)
  • Letter-spacing: +20 (tight and confident, not loose and airy)
  • All caps. Always.
  • Padding: minimum 40px on all sides
  • Never cropped. Never rotated. Never stretched.

08 · Voice & Tone

PARALLAX sounds like a friend who happens to be twelve people at once — and stopped apologizing for it.

Not a guru. Not a thought leader. Not a coach. A specific, plural, deeply curious person who writes the way they think — and who will tell you the embarrassing truth before they tell you the marketable insight.

The full voice & tone guide lives at voice-and-tone.md. The five non-negotiable rules:

  • Open with the thing itself, never the setup. No preamble. The first 7-10 words are the scene, not a runway to it.
  • Confession beats observation. When Farrice inserts himself ("I spent years…", "I have a son"), the content becomes unmistakable.
  • Rhythm varies intentionally. Short sentences land punches. Long sentences build pressure. Walls of text die in feed.
  • Never summarize what the reader will find. Make them feel something, then leave the door open.
  • Slot machine close. Every piece is satisfying on its own. The link is the full payoff, not the only payoff.

The narrative spine

Scene or confession → The reframe that changes how you see it → One quiet invitation

Banned phrases (do not use)

  • "Here's what/why/how…" — generic AI opener; rob the reader of the moment.
  • "Let's dive in" — performative.
  • "thought leader" — status word; brand is anti-status.
  • "It's not X. It's Y." — twin-sentence aphorism. AI structural tell.
  • "Picture this:" / "Imagine:" — cheesy hook stem.
  • Triple anaphora. Em-dashes >2 per piece. Mid-essay "But here's the thing:" bridges.
09 · Photography

Real faces. No AI humans.

Editorial author photography aesthetic. Think Ta-Nehisi Coates' New Yorker portrait, Ocean Vuong's bookjacket, Rick Rubin on The Creative Act — direct gaze, no smile or just the suggestion, soft natural light, neutral wardrobe, neutral background.

Full direction at photography-direction.md. The condensed version:

  • Frame: chest-up medium-close. 85mm equivalent at f/2.0-2.8.
  • Expression: direct eye contact, mouth at rest. The breath between sentences.
  • Wardrobe: solid black, charcoal, navy, or warm cream. No patterns, no logos.
  • Background: solid wall — concrete, plaster, painted neutral. No props.
  • Lighting: single window source or large soft key. No rim light. Slightly warm of pure daylight.
  • Output: one photo. Use it everywhere. Recognition compounds when the asset never changes.

Do-not-shoot list: stock-photo aesthetic, glamour-style portraits, group photos, brand-asset shots, studio backdrops, smiling-with-teeth headshots, AI-generated humans of any kind.

10 · AI Image Prompts

Seasoning, never the main course.

For special editions where content demands a visual metaphor, AI imagery enters the system. Default editions use the typographic edition cover template. Full prompt library at prompts/special-edition-covers.md.

The working formula

[Concrete physical subject], [specific lighting direction and color temperature],
[specific real-world surface or environment], [specific film stock reference],
editorial still-life photography, shallow depth of field, [mood],
no text, no logos, no humans
--ar 1:1 --s 200 --v 6.1

Aesthetic constants

  • Warm neutral palette — concrete grays, warm whites, ink shadows. No cinematic teal-orange grading.
  • Single directional light source — window, harsh sun, single Edison bulb. Long shadows.
  • Real materials — concrete, raw wood, matte paper, plaster, fabric.
  • Reference film stocks — Kodak Portra 400, Tri-X. Never digital VSCO/Instagram looks.
  • No people. No faces. No tech products. No screens. No branded objects.

The image goes at 20-30% opacity, bottom-right of the edition cover template. Typography stays primary; the image is texture. Reserve full-frame imagery for ~1-in-10 editions maximum (manifestos, milestone editions).

11 · Do's & Don'ts

The brand discipline in two columns.

Do

  • Use violet only where attention should land. One violet element per composition is the maximum.
  • Keep the wordmark identical across every surface — same font, same spacing, same color logic.
  • Use Space Grotesk Bold for all headlines and the wordmark. Always.
  • Use Source Serif 4 for all body text, taglines, italic emphasis.
  • Use JetBrains Mono only for edition numbers, technical labels, and the polymath stack.
  • Ship typographic-only covers as the default for editions.
  • Use generous padding. 80px on edition covers, 48px+ on email banners.
  • Maintain WCAG AA on every text/background pair.
  • Lock edition numbering: "EDITION 001" pattern. JetBrains Mono uppercase.

Don't

  • Generate a logo mark. The wordmark IS the logo.
  • Introduce a third color. Two colors. One dark, one accent.
  • Use AI image generation for the wordmark, banners, or core identity.
  • Mix rounded and sharp corners in the same view.
  • Use drop shadows, gradients, glows, or any depth effect.
  • Use violet for body copy on warm white (4.4:1 contrast — large/bold only).
  • Use more than two type weights on a single composition.
  • Describe parallax visually. The word carries the concept.
  • Use AI-generated faces or human imagery anywhere.
  • Ship without the wordmark. Every public asset carries it.
12 · Future Spec

What to add when growth demands it.

Deliberately out of scope for v1.0. Add when the surface becomes real, not before — premature spec is brand-bloat.

  • Motion / animation direction — for video Notes, motion graphics, animated banners. Currently zero spec; add when Farrice ships first video edition.
  • Print / merch direction — if Parallax ever ships swag (T-shirts, notebooks, stickers). The wordmark scales; the system needs guidelines for tactile materials.
  • Podcast cover system — if Parallax podcast launches. Square 3000×3000, leveraging the wordmark + violet L identity.
  • Email signature system — for Farrice's transactional emails (client work, partnerships). Wordmark + role + URL minimum.
  • Edition typology refinement — different edition types (Manifesto / Deep Dive / Case Study / Reader Response) may want subtly different cover treatments. Current system uses one cover template; subtype variants can extend it.
  • Lead magnet / Prompt Pack visual treatment — Prompt Packs currently inherit the publication design. A dedicated visual lockup can emerge once the Pack format matures.
  • Conference / speaking presentation deck system — if Farrice speaks publicly representing Parallax, a Keynote/Slides template carries the brand.
  • Custom domain — currently parallaxletter.substack.com. If a custom domain (parallax.[tld]) is acquired, update wordmark deployment, social previews, email banners.

The DESIGN.md file is the source of truth. Adding to this future spec means proposing additions to DESIGN.md first, getting them lint-clean, then producing assets.
