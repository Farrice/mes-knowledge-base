# Parallax — Photography Direction

**Version:** 1.0 (2026-04-27)
**Applies to:** Author headshot, B-roll for video Notes, Substack avatar source photo, future merch / podcast covers, any human imagery published under the Parallax brand.

The creative rebrief explicitly forbids AI-generated faces. This document is the spec for shooting the real thing.

---

## The Author Photo (the one shot to get right)

This is the most important visual asset in the brand. It will appear on the Substack profile, every Note byline, the email footer, every cross-platform repost, every podcast appearance, every conference badge — for years.

### The Reference

Think: **the editorial author photo aesthetic.** Look at:
- Ta-Nehisi Coates' New Yorker portrait
- Ocean Vuong's *On Earth We're Briefly Gorgeous* press photos
- Rick Rubin's photo on *The Creative Act* book cover
- Hanif Abdurraqib's bookjacket headshots

The pattern: real person, real face, **no smile** or only the suggestion of one, **direct gaze** at the camera, soft natural light, neutral wardrobe, neutral background. The portrait says "I have something to say. I'm done convincing you to listen."

### What we're NOT going for

- LinkedIn corporate headshot (too polished, too suit-and-blazer)
- Substack creator photo (too blogger-friendly, too smiling)
- Tech founder portrait (too "powerful pose," too studio)
- Influencer aesthetic (no ring lights, no gradient backgrounds, no "look candid")

### Composition

- **Frame:** Tight medium-close — chest up, shoulders included. Not a tight head crop. The shoulders carry a quiet authority that head-only crops lose.
- **Lens:** 85mm equivalent (full-frame) or 50mm on APS-C. Compresses features naturally. Avoid wide angles (24-35mm) which distort the face.
- **Aperture:** f/2.0 to f/2.8. Shallow enough to gently separate from background. Not so shallow the face plane goes soft.
- **Distance:** ~6 feet from subject to camera. This is the editorial portrait distance.
- **Angle:** Lens at the subject's eye height. Never below (heroic look — wrong register), never above (diminutive — also wrong).

### Expression

- **Direct eye contact** with the lens.
- **Mouth at rest, slightly closed.** No teeth. The suggestion of a smile is fine; an actual smile breaks the register.
- **Eyebrows neutral.** No raise (questioning), no furrow (intense).
- **Stillness.** Not the moment after a laugh, not the moment before a word. The breath between sentences.

The expression to aim for: *I'm not posing. I'm just here.*

### Wardrobe

- **Tone:** Black, charcoal, deep navy, or warm cream. Solid. No patterns. No logos.
- **Texture:** Slight texture is good — knit, cotton, wool. Avoid shiny synthetics, formal suits, branded apparel.
- **Layers:** A simple T-shirt or henley works. A black jacket over works. Avoid full suit-and-tie (wrong register) and avoid statement pieces (the photo isn't about the clothes).
- **Accessories:** Minimal. A single chain or watch is fine if Farrice already wears one daily. Don't add jewelry for the photo.

The wardrobe rule: **clothing the reader's eye scans past in 1 second to land on the face.**

### Background

- **Solid wall** — concrete, plaster, painted wall in a muted neutral. Beige, soft gray, deep charcoal, or warm white.
- **Mild texture** is good (a slightly weathered concrete wall, a textured plaster) — the brand's Tadao Ando reference. Pure flat painted drywall reads more sterile.
- **No props.** No bookshelves. No plants. No window light streaks. No gradient backdrops. No Substack logos.
- **Distance from background:** 4-6 feet. Enough that the background goes gently out of focus at f/2.0-2.8.

### Lighting

**Natural window light is the goal.** North-facing window in the late morning or early afternoon. Subject 90° to the window, light falling across one side of the face.

If artificial:
- One large soft source (octa, large softbox, bounced strobe) at 45° to the subject, slightly above eye height
- Optional fill: a 4×4 white bounce on the shadow side at +1 to +1.5 stops below key
- No rim light. No hair light. No background light.
- Color temperature: 4500K-5500K. Slightly warm of pure daylight; never cool.

The lighting feel: **window light in a thoughtful person's living room.** Not a studio. Not a basement. Not a rooftop.

### Tonality / Color Grade

- **Slightly desaturated.** Not black-and-white, but pulled toward neutral.
- **Skin tones honest.** Don't push the orange/teal cinematic LUT. Farrice is biracial — accurate skin tone matters culturally and aesthetically. The skin should look like skin, not graded plastic.
- **Mild contrast.** Lift the shadows just enough to read detail; keep highlights from clipping.
- **Reference grade:** Kodak Portra 400 emulation if shooting digital and grading. Editorial film stock baseline.

### What to avoid

- Beauty retouching. Skin texture is fine. Pores are fine. Slight asymmetry is fine. The reader trusts a face that looks like a face.
- Heavy vignetting.
- Crops at the eyes or mouth (only the chest-up frame, with full face in the rule-of-thirds upper third).
- Multiple "selects" published. **One photo. Use it everywhere.** Recognition compounds when the asset never changes.

### Output

- **Color, hi-res master:** uncompressed TIFF or PSD, 16-bit, sRGB, at full sensor resolution
- **Substack profile crop:** 800×800 square (face centered, slight room above the head)
- **LinkedIn / X / Instagram:** 800×800
- **Email signature:** 256×256
- **Notes byline:** 64×64 (auto-generated by Substack; use the same source)

### When to reshoot

Don't, unless:
- Significant physical change that no longer reads as the same person (different hair length doesn't count)
- 3+ years have passed and the photo starts to feel dated (rare with editorial-style shots)
- The photo was taken at a moment that no longer represents the brand (e.g., shot before Parallax existed and feels disconnected)

If reshoot — match the original framing, lighting, and wardrobe register so the brand recognition holds.

## B-Roll for Video Notes / Substack Videos

Substack now supports video Notes. If Farrice records video content, the visual register stays the same as the photo aesthetic.

- **Background:** Same wall, same lighting register
- **Framing:** Slightly looser — chest up to mid-bust, leaving room for hand gestures
- **Eye line:** Camera at eye height; speak to the lens, not over it
- **Audio:** Lavalier mic or shotgun mic close-positioned. Audio quality matters more than resolution.
- **Length:** Notes videos cap at ~90 seconds; Substack edition videos can be 5-10 min
- **Cut:** Single take preferred for Notes. No jump cuts that scream "I edited this." Light trim only.
- **Wardrobe rule:** Same as photo — solid, neutral, no logos.

## Lifestyle / Process Photography (optional, for special editions)

For editions that need a non-portrait visual (a desk shot, a writing-process photo, a photo of JJ's hand on a notebook), apply:

- Same color grade (slightly desaturated, honest skin/wood/paper tones)
- Real environments, not staged
- Hands and small details preferred over wide environment shots
- No "reading a book at a coffee shop" tropes
- If JJ appears, **back of head only** — protect the child's privacy. Hands and small body parts are fine.

These photos sit in the typographic system at 20-30% opacity, bottom-right corner of edition covers, never as the main visual. The typography stays the asset; the photo is texture.

## Do-Not-Shoot List

Do not produce or commission:

- Stock-photo aesthetic shots ("man at laptop in coffee shop")
- Heavily retouched glamour-style portraits
- Group photos (the brand is a single voice)
- Brand-asset shots (Farrice holding a Parallax mug, Parallax-branded laptop sleeve, etc. — corporate-content tropes)
- Studio backdrops (white seamless, gradient backdrops, branded backdrops)
- Smiling-with-teeth headshots (wrong register; reads sales-y)
- Conference / event photos as primary brand assets (use them in editions if relevant, never as profile)
- AI-generated humans of any kind (per the rebrief, non-negotiable)

## When Hiring a Photographer

If Farrice hires for the headshot, send the photographer:

1. This document
2. The reference list above (Coates, Vuong, Rubin, Abdurraqib)
3. The wardrobe rules
4. The output spec

Budget: editorial portrait by a competent photographer in a major city is $400-1500 for the session + delivery. Higher tier ($2K-5K) gets celebrity-portrait-level work. The brand is in the editorial register, not celebrity — the lower-tier budget is appropriate.

If Farrice shoots himself: tripod, 10-second self-timer, mark the floor for distance, take 50+ frames, pick one. The shoot above can be DIY in a single afternoon with a solid wall, a window, and a willingness to delete most frames.
