## The Decision

Build the public niche around one commercial tip of spear:

> **Proof-to-Market strategy for founder-led health and performance brands:** deciding which campaign arguments the brand's proof can actually carry before it spends on launch creative.

Use this positioning line for the test:

> **I help founder-led health and performance brands decide which claims and campaign angles are worth building before they fund the launch.** I combine 18 years of body-level experience with evidence-led research and AI-assisted content intelligence. Your attorney keeps the pen; I read for signal, differentiation, and proof fit.

This is a **reversible market vehicle**, not a permanent identity. It earns the lead because it concentrates Farrice's most credible intersections into a problem a buyer can recognize and a paid decision product the buyer can try.

The creator umbrella is broader but subordinate:

> **Evidence before hype:** how people and brands turn complex expertise into decisions others can believe, use, and buy.

That umbrella keeps AI systems, transformation psychology, and the personal “last 10%” story available without making a new visitor decode five unrelated identities.

## Evidence Language

- `VERIFIED`: inspectable workspace artifact or current primary/public source.
- `SELF-REPORTED`: Farrice's documented lived experience or stated preference.
- `DERIVED`: a conclusion drawn from evidence, not a direct fact.
- `UNTESTED`: plausible recommendation awaiting behavior.
- `NO EVENT`: the required market action has not happened, so no positive or negative inference is allowed.

## The Niche Architecture

### Primary audience

**Founder/CEO of a founder-led health or performance product company with a dated growth occasion**, such as a launch, reformulation, fundraise, retail expansion, or category repositioning, and enough budget to fund execution. “Funded” should mean **an active budget and a real decision clock**, not merely venture-backed.

**Secondary audience:** Head of Brand, Growth, or Integrated Marketing at a larger funded company who needs to defend messaging and creative decisions across clinical, legal, leadership, and production teams.

**Named-person test:** Tim Near of GateDrop is a usable current example: his role and distribution-expansion occasion are documented, while his exact messaging pain and willingness to pay are `UNTESTED`. This is the right level of honesty: a real person, a real occasion, and no invented inner monologue.

### Problem

`DERIVED`: The brand is about to spend on creative, media, retail, or a launch before settling which differentiated claim its evidence can support and its buyer can understand. The result is either cautious sameness or aggressive language that creates credibility and review risk.

### Desired outcome

`UNTESTED`: Before production spend, the buyer chooses three defensible campaign arguments, knows what proof supports each one, and can brief the internal or external team without reopening the strategy on every asset.

### Vehicle

1. **Cold decision product:** $750 prepaid **Angle Map**: one 60-minute live read, three named campaign angles, delivered within 48 hours. Each angle includes a core belief, hooks, proof direction, and an editorial claim-safety line.
2. **Warm implementation decision:** $2,500 prepaid **Proof-to-Market Sprint**: positioning, buyer-decision map, proof hierarchy, campaign-angle bank, launch messaging, and one priority asset over ten business days.
3. **Expansion:** recurring implementation stays `PARKED` until paid proof exists.

### Specialist advantage

Farrice can read the same claim through four lenses that are rarely housed together: what a buyer hears, what a practitioner knows about the body, what evidence can support, and what a creative team can actually make. The AI system increases research coverage and speed; it is not the promise.

### Information advantage

The health-performance library turns ongoing category activity into reusable evidence, objections, buyer language, and campaign angles. The compounding asset is not “more content.” It is a decision library connecting market occasions to claims, proof, and creative direction.

## Smallest Validation Sequence

### Test 0: Ignite the event (48 hours)

`NO EVENT` → create and test the existing $750 payment link, then send the five prepared, verified one-gap messages already in the campaign package. Do not build another offer or another content system first.

**Pass:** five messages are actually sent and logged.  
**What it proves:** only that the market test has begun.  
**Approval boundary:** payment-account setup and outreach are external actions; Farrice must execute or explicitly authorize them.

### Test 1: Problem recognition (first 50 qualified sends)

Lead with a dated occasion and one observed messaging/proof gap. Record replies in the buyer's exact language.

**Signal:** qualified replies, requests to see the read, or disagreement specific enough to sharpen the problem.  
**If zero replies after 50 well-targeted sends:** fix the list or opener; do not declare the niche dead.  
**If fewer than five qualified conversations after roughly 100 sends:** investigate channel, reach, and occasion quality.

### Test 2: Paid decision (up to 20 qualified, price-quoted conversations)

Name the $750 Angle Map no later than the second substantive exchange and quote it in every qualified sales conversation.

**First confirmation:** one prepaid Angle Map.  
Twenty qualified conversations with the price stated and zero sales is a strong reversal signal. Kill or materially reposition the Angle Map.  
**Seller diagnostic:** conversations with no price quotes are not offer rejection.

### Test 3: Repeatability (30 days after first payment)

Deliver the first Angle Map in 48 hours, capture client-approved outcome evidence, and offer the Sprint only where the implementation need is real.

Two paid Angle Maps plus one Sprint conversion within 30 days is the commit signal. At that point, commit to the niche for 90 days and standardize the repeatable delivery layer.  
**Do not count:** views, likes, compliments, free teardowns, payment promises, or unpublished assets.

### Parallel content signal

Publish the 12-piece test only if Farrice approves publication. Track role-fit comments, qualified DMs, conversations, and which proof objects get reused. Raw reach, saves, and follower growth help choose content formats; they do not prove paid demand.

## Proof Gaps

1. **Buyer language gap: `UNTESTED`:** Current pain language is inferred from regulation, hiring briefs, market artifacts, and Farrice's analysis. It does not come from completed conversations with the target buyers.
2. **Payment gap: `NO EVENT`:** No one has been sent, quoted, sold, or collected for the Angle Map.
3. **Outcome gap: `UNTESTED`:** No paid case yet connects the Angle Map to a better campaign decision, faster internal alignment, avoided wasted production, or a commercial result.
4. **Channel gap: `UNTESTED`:** LinkedIn contact routes are verified, but LinkedIn has not proved it can produce conversations in this segment. Keep public content as proof infrastructure while testing direct contact; do not make follower growth the business model.
5. **Scope gap: `UNTESTED`:** Larger regulated health companies may expect technical SEO, analytics, clinical review, publishing operations, or legal/compliance ownership. Preserve the boundary: Farrice decides what should be made and why; qualified reviewers approve regulated claims.
6. **Funded-buyer definition: `DERIVED`:** Venture backing is a weak qualification by itself. A dated occasion, accessible decision-maker, active budget, and unresolved message are stronger selection criteria.

## Risks and Reversal Conditions

- **Risk: the niche is right but the buyer is too large.** Start with founder-led companies where one person can say yes. Move upmarket only after the decision product has proof.
- **Risk: “claim-safe” sounds like legal compliance.** Lead with better campaign decisions and proof fit; repeat that the client's attorney or compliance owner retains approval.
- **Risk: AI attracts the wrong audience.** Keep AI behind the mechanism. If AI posts draw tool-seekers rather than health-brand buyers, demote the territory.
- **Risk: more production becomes avoidance.** The current bottleneck is exposure to a buyer. No new offer architecture before Test 0.
- **Pivot rule:** do not pivot because of low views, boredom, or five unanswered messages. Pivot the vehicle only after the price-quoted conversation threshold fails, or when another direction wins on comparable buyer and payment evidence.
