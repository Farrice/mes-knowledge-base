**Status:** PROVISIONAL WORLD CORE · DEPLOYABLE CONCEPT · HUMAN TASTE APPROVAL REQUIRED

**Public umbrella:** Farrice Cain remains the stable public identity. The Decision Table is a bounded health-performance offer world, not a rebrand.

**Deeper service path:** Source-to-Search Trust Layer

**Source basis:** current Farrice brand voice and visual canon; Health Performance IP service ladder, acquisition offer, Angle Map briefs, and weekly synthesis; Scott Norton × Oren John artifact mechanics.
**Proof state:** The current-market fit is `LIKELY`, based on the latest internal strategy evidence. Client outcomes, conversion lift, and buyer demand remain `UNTESTED` until live use.

## Decision

Build a working world around a real founder problem: a product can contain several defensible benefits while its campaign cannot give all of them equal billing. Farrice's role is to put the promises on the table, preserve their evidence, and issue one clear lead decision.

The world should feel like an editorial decision room with visible evidence—not a laboratory. A lab coat, faux certification seal, clinical white space, or invented scientific institution would overstate Farrice's authority and collapse into a familiar wellness cliché. The ownable tension is **crowded proof becoming one defensible lead belief**.

## Prop-Grade Briefs

### 1. Claim Card Deck

- **Issuing institution:** Farrice Cain's Angle Map practice
- **Recipient/owner:** Decision Owner and the working team
- **Moment of issue:** After source intake, before angle selection
- **Material/form:** Twelve uncoated index cards plus four role cards: `LEAD`, `SUPPORT`, `HOLD`, `CUT`. Each claim card carries claim language, buyer occasion, evidence state, and source pointer.
- **Typography/register:** Editorial utility; black text, one cyan evidence accent, red only for unresolved risk. No seals, faux science, or ornamental jargon.
- **Period detail:** Contemporary working-table object; handwritten intervention is allowed and preferred over artificial vintage styling.
- **Functional job:** Turn benefit crowding into a movable decision model without deleting valid secondary truths.
- **Use ritual:** Read each claim aloud, pair it to a buyer occasion, mark its proof state, then move exactly one under `LEAD`.
- **Display/share behavior:** Overhead tabletop image or short sorting sequence; publish only with public or permissioned source material.
- **Zoom-in proof:** The source pointer and evidence state must remain legible in a close crop.
- **Production constraint:** Prototype with plain cards first. No manufactured deck, paid print run, or client data without approval.
- **Derivation:** Fantasy → Decision Table → Angle Map practice → Decision Owner → choose one belief while preserving the rest.
- **Predicted behavior:** The team physically moves one claim into the lead position and stops debating all claims as equals.
- **Artifact-vs-merch:** `ARTIFACT` — removing Farrice's name leaves the sorting function and world logic intact.

### 2. Angle Map Decision Sheet

- **Issuing institution:** Farrice Cain's Angle Map practice
- **Recipient/owner:** Decision Owner
- **Moment of issue:** At the decision session
- **Material/form:** One-page landscape sheet with three candidate angles, one lead recommendation, evidence state, buyer occasion, support roles, and a decision line.
- **Typography/register:** Plain-language editorial hierarchy; the lead angle receives spatial priority, not decorative hype.
- **Period detail:** Resembles a serious creative-direction sheet, not a consulting slide or medical report.
- **Functional job:** Convert the analysis into an approveable choice with named downstream consequences.
- **Use ritual:** The Decision Owner initials the lead angle and names the next surface it governs.
- **Display/share behavior:** Kept beside the brief during the next homepage or campaign round.
- **Zoom-in proof:** A viewer can see why the lead won and what happened to every non-leading claim.
- **Production constraint:** No efficacy judgment; decisions concern message priority and source status.
- **Derivation:** Fantasy → Decision Table → Angle Map practice → Decision Owner → authorize one lead angle.
- **Predicted behavior:** The owner approves one angle and attaches it to a real creative surface.
- **Artifact-vs-merch:** `ARTIFACT` — its authority comes from the decision record, not a logo.

### 3. Decision Receipt

- **Issuing institution:** Farrice Cain's Angle Map practice
- **Recipient/owner:** Decision Owner and execution team
- **Moment of issue:** Immediately after the Angle Map decision

- **Typography/register:** Receipt logic without retail cosplay; terse fields, one decisive sentence, inspectable source pointers.
- **Period detail:** Dated and versioned as a living operating record.

- **Display/share behavior:** Forwarded internally and cited in reviews.
- **Zoom-in proof:** `What leads / what supports / what is held / why / who owns next` all appear without opening another document.
- **Production constraint:** No PDF by default; deliver as a native Codex-readable artifact or agreed client format.
- **Derivation:** Fantasy → Decision Table → Angle Map practice → Decision Owner → carry the choice into execution.
- **Predicted behavior:** The team attaches the receipt to the next brief instead of reopening the positioning debate.
- **Artifact-vs-merch:** `ARTIFACT` — it is a chain-of-custody object for the decision.

### 4. Evidence Intake Receipt

- **Issuing institution:** Farrice Cain's Angle Map practice
- **Recipient/owner:** Decision Owner and Farrice
- **Moment of issue:** After intake, before analysis
- **Material/form:** A compact checklist showing each received source, permission state, evidence state, missing item, and claim boundary.
- **Typography/register:** Operational, calm, exact. Missing evidence reads `NOT RECEIVED` or `UNVERIFIED`, never disguised by progress language.
- **Period detail:** A back-office dispatch that looks useful enough to keep.

- **Display/share behavior:** Stored with the project record; referenced when a risky claim reappears.
- **Zoom-in proof:** Source name, date, status, and permission boundary are visible.

- **Derivation:** Fantasy → Decision Table → Angle Map practice → Decision Owner → know what evidence is actually on the table.
- **Predicted behavior:** The owner supplies a missing source or explicitly accepts an uncertainty before strategy proceeds.
- **Artifact-vs-merch:** `ARTIFACT` — the mundane receipt is the world's truth boundary.

### 5. Decision Owner Assignment Card

- **Issuing institution:** Farrice Cain's Angle Map practice

- **Moment of issue:** Kickoff
- **Material/form:** Small digital or uncoated-paper card naming product, decision date, governed surface, owner, and the sentence: “Your job is not to keep every benefit equal. Your job is to make the lead belief clear.”
- **Typography/register:** Direct, adult, non-ceremonial. No medal, rank, or fake credential language.
- **Period detail:** Contemporary assignment slip; useful enough to pin near a working screen.
- **Functional job:** Prevent committee ambiguity by assigning decision authority.
- **Use ritual:** Owner confirms the governed surface and the decision deadline.
- **Display/share behavior:** Added to the kickoff record or placed at the table during the session.
- **Zoom-in proof:** One named person, one decision, one deadline, one governed surface.
- **Production constraint:** It confers workflow responsibility, not professional or scientific qualification.
- **Derivation:** Fantasy → Decision Table → Angle Map practice → Decision Owner → own the lead-angle decision.
- **Predicted behavior:** One person accepts responsibility for the final angle rather than deferring to a committee.
- **Artifact-vs-merch:** `ARTIFACT` — ownership changes the recipient's role and behavior.

### Reversible First Prototype: Claim Card Deck

**Prototype scope:** Use one current public product page. Create no more than twelve plain digital or index cards. Each card gets: exact claim language, source pointer, evidence state, buyer occasion, and provisional role. Add four role cards: `LEAD / SUPPORT / HOLD / CUT`. Arrange them on a real table, run the decision ritual, and capture one overhead proof frame.

**No-permission boundary:** This plan authorizes the local prototype specification only. It does not authorize publishing, product-image reuse, external contact, paid production, printing, or efficacy judgment.

**Promotion gate:** Promote the prototype only if all five pass:

1. One lead angle is selected for a named buyer occasion.
2. Every other claim has an explicit support, hold, or cut role.
3. Every visible claim has a source pointer and evidence state.
4. A second person can explain the choice from the cards without a long verbal rescue.
5. The output feeds a real brief, audit, or offer conversation; a beautiful photograph alone is a failed test.

**Kill conditions:** Kill or redesign if the cards feel like a workshop gimmick, imply scientific adjudication, require Farrice's monologue to make sense, or merely restate the product page.

## Decision Memo

**Do this:** Prototype the Claim Card Deck as the first physical-digital carrier of the Angle Map. It joins analysis, service delivery, visual content, and proof in one reversible object.

**Don't do:** Launch a faux lab, invent credentials, manufacture merch, rename the public umbrella, or imply that the current offer has already improved conversion, rankings, or revenue.

**We're wrong if:** the real buyer problem is not benefit crowding; Farrice wants Source-to-Search Trust Layer—not Angle Map—to be the public wedge; “Decision Table” feels too procedural in his mouth; or a live founder cannot use the cards to make a faster, clearer decision.
