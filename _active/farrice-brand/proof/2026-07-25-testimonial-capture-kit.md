## The positioning problem, and the fix

You trained them. You want authority as a creative strategist. The instinct is to steer them toward saying something strategy-flavored, and that instinct will sink you: they'd be describing work they never bought, and anyone reading it can tell.

What carries across is how you worked, not what field you worked in. Three things happened in the Carbon Torch engagement that a creative strategist does every day.

You went looking for the real constraint instead of taking the stated problem at face value, which is what sent you into Katie's labs and into the specifics of Josh's GLP-1 protocol.

You built for the person in front of you. Neither program is a template wearing a client's name.

And Katie's scale sat at 135 while she thought she was failing. You told her the number was measuring the wrong thing. That is positioning work. Same muscle as telling a brand their metric is lying to them.

So let the testimonials stay true to what happened. They describe how you think, in the room where it happened. You do the framing later, when you place them. Never coach a training client into calling you a creative strategist.

## The ask (text it, you see these people)

> Hey [name], quick favor, zero pressure.
>
> I'm putting my proof together for the next stretch of work and I'd love to use a few words from you about working with me.
>
> I know you're busy so I wrote a draft based on what actually happened. Change anything, add anything, or just tell me it's fine and I'll use it as-is.
>
> [paste draft]
>
> Sound right to you?

Writing it for them kills the blank page, which is the actual reason most testimonial asks die in the inbox. "Zero pressure" drops the defensiveness. "Change anything" hands them ownership without making them produce.

**Send Josh's today.** He just settled, he's satisfied, and he already said yes, so the goodwill is as high as it will get before it quietly decays.

## Follow-up cadence

| Day | Message |
|---|---|
| 0 | The ask above |
| 3 | "Hey, just bumping this up. No rush at all, whenever you get a sec." |
| 6 | "Floating this back up one more time. A 👍 works if the draft's fine." |
| 10 | "I know you're slammed. Last couple times I'll bring it up. If the draft looks good just say so and I'll use it." |
| 14 | "Last bump, promise. [re-paste draft] A 'looks good' is all I need." |

Around day 6 you will feel like a pest. You won't be one. People forget, so a bump reads as a reminder. Most of them carry a small guilt about not replying and your bump resolves it for them. Worst case available here is silence, and nobody torches a relationship over a testimonial ask.
