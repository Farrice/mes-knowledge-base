## ENVISION — The Individual

### Her name is Maya.

She runs a leadership coaching practice that mostly works through referrals from people whose teams she's transformed. If you pulled up her LinkedIn, you'd see 1,400 followers — most of whom are former clients, ex-colleagues from her corporate years, and a slow-accumulated network of fellow coaches she's collaborated with across a decade.

She has the kind of presence where people don't remember meeting her — they remember what she said. There's a half-second gap between when you ask Maya a question and when she answers, and in that gap you can feel her actually considering. That gap is part of what makes her valuable. It's also what makes her invisible online — the gap doesn't translate to a feed.

She wears the same three watches. Hair always up when she's working, always down when she's reading. There's a green Moleskine notebook open within arm's reach in any room where she's thinking.

She got her certification from a prestigious program ten years ago. She hasn't told anyone she's certified in three years because the certification doesn't actually matter — what matters is what happens in her sessions. But she also hasn't replaced the credentialing with anything else, which means strangers have no way to know what she does at her depth.

She has a husband who runs a different small business and they've made it work for eleven years. They live in a city you'd recognize but it doesn't define her. She doesn't post photos of dinner.

## OCCUPATION in Farrice's World

### Maya's role: She is a translator who's lost her audience.

In Farrice's brand world, Maya is the **expert who has the goods but lost the channel.** She has the intellectual property — the actual diagnostic skill, the framework she's built session by session over a decade — but she doesn't have a way to make that visible at scale. Her business runs on the very thin oxygen of word-of-mouth from past clients, and that's compressing.

**The energy she brings**: Quiet authority. Maya doesn't hype. She demonstrates. When she's in a room, the conversation deepens. When she's in a feed, she disappears.

**What makes Farrice's world tick because of Maya**:
- She is the *reason* the Oracle archetype exists. The diagnostic, the resonance work, the voice extraction — those services only matter if there's a Maya to deploy them on.
- She is the *test* of every workflow. If a piece of content doesn't make Maya feel seen, it's not Oracle-grade. It's noise.
- She is the *moat*. Anyone can produce content for someone who's already loud. Producing content for Maya — who is quiet by design — requires actual diagnostic skill.

## ACTIVITY in Farrice's World

### What Maya DOES (rituals, routines, identity-reinforcing behaviors)

- Reads two books at once: one trade, one theory. Always.
- Takes hand-written notes in client sessions. Types up themes after.
- Drinks her tea hot enough that it's a problem. Won't switch.
- Has a specific 4 PM walk. Even when it's raining. Even when she has back-to-back calls.
- Runs the same diagnostic question at the start of every session: *"What were you thinking about on the way here?"*
- Has rejected the same coaching-platform's affiliate offer three times because it would compromise her positioning, even though she could use the money.
- Saves articles to read later, in a Notion she calls "marination," not "reading list."
- Edits her own LinkedIn posts six times before publishing — and posts maybe twice a month because of it.

### What Maya REFUSES

- Won't post "5 lessons" carousels. They feel beneath her, but she can't articulate why.
- Won't go on most podcasts. The interviewer doesn't ask the questions she'd want to answer.
- Won't take on team-coaching clients with executives who don't read.
- Won't write a book yet because she doesn't think she's earned the title.
- Won't hire a ghostwriter from the obvious places because she's heard their work and it sounds nothing like her clients.
- Won't run paid ads. It would feel like begging.
- Won't say her certification anymore.

### What this reveals

Maya's identity is built on **earned authority + chosen invisibility**. She's not invisible because she lacks confidence. She's invisible because the available channels feel wrong. She'd rather be unknown than be misrepresented.

This is why Farrice exists in her field of vision the moment she encounters him: he is the first person who has named the *cost* of her chosen invisibility without prescribing the obvious cure (more posting, lower-quality output, "build a personal brand"). He doesn't ask her to compromise. He shows her there's a third option.

## THOUGHT PROCESS in Farrice's World

### Her internal logic

Maya thinks in arcs, not bullet points. When she's working through a problem, she traces it backward — what was happening 6 months before this got hard, what changed, what's the structural rather than surface cause. This is why most consulting content feels thin to her: it solves at the level of symptom.

She has a private theory that 80% of her professional peers are running on the inertia of past credibility, and she's terrified of joining them. She doesn't say this out loud. It would be petty.

She has another private theory that the AI revolution will hollow out coaching practices like hers, but only the ones that haven't already done the diagnostic work to know what's actually irreplaceable about their offering. She's not sure if she's done that work yet. This is her real anxiety.

### What feels aligned to Maya

- Long-form writing that respects the reader's time by EARNING it
- Specificity. Names, dates, the precise phrase, the actual thing said
- Frameworks that account for nuance rather than collapsing it
- People who say "I don't know" cleanly
- Work that survives slow re-reading
- Voices that have a private life — that don't say everything

### What feels INAUTHENTIC to Maya

- Anything that ends with "Drop a 🔥 if this resonates"
- LinkedIn posts that are clearly written by an LLM with em-dash density above 6 per piece
- Coaches who post their own quote-cards
- Anyone who calls themselves a "thought leader"
- Frameworks that are obviously the writer's own marketing in costume
- Performative vulnerability ("I'm being honest with you...")
- Confidence that hasn't been earned by getting things wrong publicly

### Shift from problem-solving to emotional outcome

**The wrong framing**: "Maya needs help with marketing."

**The right framing**: Maya needs to feel that her decade of careful work *can be made visible without being cheapened.* That's not a marketing problem. That's an identity-preservation problem.

When Farrice offers her voice-matched ghostwriting + content systems, what she's buying is **the relief of not having to choose between staying invisible and selling out.** That's the emotional outcome. The deliverable is incidental.

## STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS

### Content voice for Maya

- **Density of insight per sentence** matters more than total word count. Maya re-reads. Repay her.
- **No engagement bait.** Cheap signoffs ("What's your take?") read as breaking the contract. The contract is: I respect your attention. Don't ask for performative engagement.
- **Specificity over abstraction.** "The phrase a client said in session that made the room go quiet" beats "transformation breakthrough."
- **Frameworks must be load-bearing.** If the framework collapses under one specific case, Maya's done. She's seen too many.
- **Lineage over novelty.** Reference what you read. Show your inheritances. Maya distrusts un-sourced confidence.

### Product decisions for Maya

- **Voice-matched ghostwriting** is the single most important offer. Maya will pay $5K-$8.5K/mo for ghostwriting that actually sounds like her. She's been burned 2-3 times before. The diagnostic is the differentiator.
- **The $500 Proof Run** is the specific entry point that respects her caution. She would never start at $5K. She might start at $500.
- **Parallax paid tier** is the slow-trust mechanism. Maya pays $96/yr for a year before she ever inquires about service.
- **AI Brain Build** is for the version of Maya who's *already converted* and is now scaling — she might want one in year 2, not year 1.

### Experience design for Maya

- **Delivery cadence matters more than volume.** Weekly is enough. Twice-weekly feels like a fire hose.
- **Email > DM > slack.** Maya is on slack for clients only. Email is sacred. DM is for emergencies.
- **No video of Farrice's face on first contact.** Voice (audio) and writing first. Face on second meeting. This sequence respects how Maya processes.
- **Receipts.** Every claim Farrice makes about results — show the receipt. Maya verifies.
