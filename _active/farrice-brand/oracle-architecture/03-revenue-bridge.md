## Funnel Mechanic

> **Education → Trust → Product Credibility → Purchase**, with a Catalyst-colored emotional register.
>
> The Invisible Expert encounters Top-of-Funnel content that names their real problem ("you're an expert who can't articulate your value"), watches Mid-Funnel process pieces that prove the system works, then converts on Bottom-of-Funnel offers because the *framing* of their problem already pre-sold the solution. The Catalyst coloring means the conversion feels aspirational ("become the version of yourself that shows up online") even though the structural mechanic is expertise-driven.

**Conversion psychology** (the inner monologue of a converting prospect):

> *"Wait. He just named the exact thing I've been failing at. And he didn't blame me — he blamed the framework I was using. That coaching-psychology thing he mentioned, that's why his work feels different. Of course voice is a diagnostic artifact. I never thought of it that way. ...I want him to do this for me."*

Notice what the prospect didn't say: "What's the price?" Price is irrelevant once the framing fits. **The Oracle pre-sells through reframe, not promotion.**

## 4-Layer Revenue Architecture

### Layer 1 — Content-to-Attention (Top of Funnel)

| Element | Spec |
|:--|:--|
| Content type | Category-education (Resonance Gap, Remarkability, ICP-as-Media-Channel) |
| Primary platform | LinkedIn personal account |
| Secondary | Substack/Parallax + Notes |
| Volume target | 3-4 LinkedIn posts/wk + 1 edition every 2-3 wks + 5-7 Notes/wk |
| Risk control | Cap at 5/wk regardless of capacity (volume past threshold is anti-Oracle) |
| Key metric | Qualified impressions (impressions × % Invisible Expert match) |
| Month 6 target | 6,000 avg/post × 30% qualified = 1,800 qualified pixels per piece |

### Layer 2 — Attention-to-Trust (Middle of Funnel)

| Element | Spec |
|:--|:--|
| Content type | Process transparency (How I think, the diagnostic walkthrough, voice case studies) |
| Lead capture | Parallax subscription (free tier) — auto-CTA on every TOF post |
| Owned-audience strategy | Parallax = primary; LinkedIn = sub-rented; email = bridge |
| Volume target | 2 mid-funnel posts/wk + 1 deep process Parallax edition/month |
| Key metric | Free Parallax subscribers + click-through to landing pages |
| Month 6 target | 800 free subscribers; 40% open rate; 200 click-throughs/wk to landing |

**The Catalyst coloring lives here.** Mid-funnel pieces should feel aspirational — the reader sees the diagnostic process and thinks "if I had this system thinking through my work, I'd be different." That feeling is the bridge from attention to intent.

### Layer 3 — Trust-to-Intent (Bottom of Funnel)

| Element | Spec |
|:--|:--|
| Content type | Case studies, $500 Proof Run pitch, Engagement Anatomy, Why I Guarantee My Work |
| Offer presentation | Embedded in content (the case study IS the offer reveal) |
| Objection-handling | "Why I Guarantee My Work" + "Why I Don't Take Discovery Calls" pieces |
| Cadence | 1 BOF piece every 10 days (over-deployment kills the Oracle frame) |
| Key metric | Inquiries via DM/email + Proof Run signups |
| Month 6 target | 12 inquiries/mo, 8-12 Proof Run signups |

### Layer 4 — Intent-to-Revenue (Conversion)

| Element | Spec |
|:--|:--|
| Sales process | Self-serve for Proof Run + Parallax tier; lightweight intake form for retainers |
| LTV play | Proof Run → retainer ladder → AI Brain consulting → Parallax annual sub |
| Key metric | Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) + project revenue |
| Month 6 target | $10K-$15K MRR (mix of retainers + Parallax + Proof Runs) |

## Revenue Risks & Mitigation

### Risk 1: Founder-as-bottleneck

**Description**: All revenue funnels through Farrice's personal capacity. 1-3 hr daily window is a hard ceiling.

**Mitigation**:
- Productize ruthlessly. Parallax paid tier + AI Brain Build = leverage. Retainers = revenue.
- After M6, hire freelance editor + VA before adding retainers.
- Hard cap at 4-6 retainer clients until team is built.

### Risk 2: Audience fatigue from over-monetization

**Description**: Oracle archetype dies if audience smells the sales motion. Too many BOF pieces = dead Oracle.

**Mitigation**:
- 70/20/10 ratio enforced: 70% TOF/MID, 20% BOF, 10% Parallax tier promotion.
- BOF pieces never > 1 per 10 days.
- "Why I Guarantee My Work" runs once per quarter, not weekly.

### Risk 3: AI commoditization race-to-bottom

**Description**: Voice-matched ghostwriting using AI is not a moat by itself. Anyone can do it badly. Differentiation must be in the diagnostic.

**Mitigation**:
- Lead with coaching psychology + 18-year credibility, not "AI."
- The mechanism stack (coaching + perception + AI + Oren) is the moat. Repeatedly surface the *combination*, not any single component.
- Pricing reflects mechanism, not output volume. Don't compete on per-post price.

### Risk 4: Catalyst-coloring drift to Catalyst-as-archetype

**Description**: Aspirational content is easier to write than expertise content. Drift toward Catalyst feels good — and kills Oracle credibility.

**Mitigation**:
- Quarterly archetype audit: % of content that is mechanism/expertise vs. % that is aspiration/identity.
- Target ratio: 70% expertise, 30% Catalyst-coloring. Never invert.
- If the audit shows drift, run `/oren-archetype-diagnostic` again to re-calibrate.

### Risk 5: Time-to-first-revenue compression failure

**Description**: If M2 doesn't show $500+ revenue, motivation collapses, content cadence drops, the whole thing stalls.

**Mitigation**:
- Activate $500 Proof Run by Week 4 of M1. Don't wait for "perfect" content.
- Direct-message 5 warm contacts in M1 with personalized Proof Run invites. Don't rely solely on inbound.
- Treat M1-M2 as cash-flow priority over audience-growth priority. Brand-building happens in parallel, not first.
