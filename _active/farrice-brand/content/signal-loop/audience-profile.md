## Primary audience

Founders and authorized marketing leads at already-selling supplement,
wellness, recovery, and performance brands. The fastest current buyer
hypothesis is a founder at a roughly $1M–$15M brand, but revenue band is not
inferred from appearance and remains a working fit hypothesis.

## Audience jobs

1. Decide which product truth deserves to lead the next launch or campaign.
2. Turn approved evidence into plain, defensible positioning and usable angles.
3. Escape creative fatigue without producing more interchangeable variants.
4. Give internal writers, agencies, and media buyers a decision they can
   execute consistently.
5. Show claim fluency without making the brand sound frightened of its own
   evidence.

## Live tensions

- "We did everything right and we're losing to worse products."
- "The copy got prettier and kept saying nothing."
- "I can explain the product perfectly on a call; none of that survives into
  the marketing."
- The brand needs new concepts, but more AI-generated volume does not resolve
  what it should prove.
- The founder has authority but no single owner for the proof hierarchy and
  messaging decision.

## Reactive triggers

| Trigger | Content implication |
|---|---|
| LTV:CAC pressure | Show how unclear angles create waste before discussing more volume |
| Scaling wall | Expose where founder intuition failed to become a usable brand decision |
| New launch, ingredient, or retail expansion | Demonstrate proof hierarchy and claim-safe direction |
| New marketing executive or funding event | Show a compact decision read that reduces career risk |
| Angle bottleneck | Make the difference between more variants and a new decision visible |

## Anti-triggers

- AI theater or tool-first claims
- Generic marketing instruction detached from supplement-category reality
- Invented case studies, revenue claims, or implied client outcomes
- Legal-certification language
- Advice for pre-revenue products with no real customer evidence
- Content that mistakes a copy symptom for the root problem

## One belief shift

From: "Our copy problem is a writing or volume problem."

To: "It is an unmade decision: what are we proving, who needs to believe it,
and what will we say plainly and defend when pushed?"

## Platform behavior

LinkedIn content must stay inside supplement, claims, positioning, DTC, and
performance-brand language. The early job is credibility for qualified
conversations, not broad reach. The two approved pillars are:

1. Named public-surface teardowns with credit first, then a verbatim gap and
   claim-safe direction.
2. The core thesis argued from Farrice's lived experience at the vitamin-shop
   counter, across eighteen years of coaching, and as a buyer of the category.
