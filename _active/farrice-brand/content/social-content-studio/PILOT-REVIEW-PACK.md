## Review Artifact 1 — Authority Post

Status: **HUMAN_REVIEW_PENDING**  
Business job: Authority  
CTA: None

Creative fatigue has become the cleanest diagnosis in marketing. Sometimes it hides a simpler problem: nobody chose what the campaign needs the buyer to believe.

The team adds hooks, edits, creators, and versions. But if the ingredient, outcome, delivery format, and belief behind the product are all competing to lead, every new asset has to guess:

- Lead with the ingredient?
- Lead with the outcome?
- Lead with the format?
- Lead with the belief behind the product?

By round three, the variants look different and say the same thing.

I learned this from the other side of the counter. People didn't buy a better adjective. They looked for a reason they could understand and believe.

Before another creative round, name the product truth, the buyer tension, and the proof that can carry the argument. Then production has something worth multiplying.

A hook changes the doorway. The angle decides the argument.

### Draft Notes

- The vitamin-shop counter is verified lived authority; no client room or campaign result is implied.
- The four lead choices are strategic categories, not a claim that every campaign must use one exclusively.
- If the counter line feels bolted on, cut it rather than manufacturing a deeper scene.

## Review Artifact 2 — LinkedIn Carousel

Status: **HUMAN_REVIEW_PENDING**  
Business job: Authority / offer education  
Format: 8 pages, 1080 × 1350

### Slide Architecture

1. **Some “creative fatigue” is an unmade decision.**
2. **The symptoms look like production.** More hooks. More edits. More creators. Another round.
3. **Ask the question underneath it:** What exactly are we trying to make the buyer believe?
4. **A hook changes the doorway. An angle changes the argument.**
5. **Without the argument, every asset has to guess.**
6. **Which truth leads?** Ingredient · outcome · delivery format · belief behind the product.
7. **Choose three things before production:** product truth · buyer tension · proof that can carry it.
8. **Then build. Before another creative round, choose the argument.**

### Visual Brief

- **Mode:** Angle Map offer mode because slide 6 presents real strategic choices.
- **Canvas:** 1080 × 1350; 12-column grid; minimum one-third open space.
- **Type:** Helvetica Neue only; sentence-case headlines; uppercase functional labels with `+0.16em` tracking.
- **Palette:** Canvas `#F3F3F0`, Paper `#FAFAF8`, Ink `#101010`, Graphite `#555553`, Line `#D8D8D3`, Stone `#8C8C82`.
- **Hierarchy:** One dominant sentence per slide, maximum three levels.
- **Route grammar:** Use parallel monochrome strokes only on slide 6, where choices are actually compared.
- **Dark interruption:** Slide 7 may use the single Ink field with Paper type to mark the decision.
- **Prohibited:** gradients, shadows, pills, badges, ornamental icons, generic supplement stock imagery, proof seals, and decorative route colors.
- **Adapter handoff:** `ai-carousel-content-engine`; copy and visual direction are approved separately before rendering.

## Review Artifact 3 — Short Video Concept

Status: **HUMAN_REVIEW_PENDING**  
Business job: Conversion  
Target: 35–50 seconds  
CTA: **DM ANGLE** once

### Spoken Draft

You can spot an unmade campaign decision pretty quickly. The team keeps adding hooks, edits, creators, and versions.

Ask what the buyer needs to believe. If nobody owns the answer, the team is producing more guesses faster.

Before the next brief, write down the product truth you want to lead with, the person who needs to believe it, and the proof that can carry it.

Those choices form the angle. The hook opens the door.

Choose the argument first, then make the variants. If your team is stuck at that decision, DM ANGLE.

### Shot and Visual Brief

- **Performance:** Direct-to-camera, quiet conviction, no guru cadence. Farrice should rewrite any phrase that does not sit naturally in his mouth.
- **Opening:** Medium close-up; no animated hook text beyond a restrained caption of the first line.
- **Supporting cards:** Three full-frame text cards for product truth, buyer, and proof; one idea per frame.
- **Visual system:** Master-brand mode until the final Angle Map card; Canvas/Paper field, Ink type, Helvetica Neue.
- **Motion:** Hard cuts and restrained type movement; no AI avatar, stock bottle montage, fake dashboard, or generated face.
- **Adapter handoff:** `video-studio` only after voice/taste approval; no paid render is authorized.
