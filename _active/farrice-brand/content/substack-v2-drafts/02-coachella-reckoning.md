# Edition 02: The Coachella Reckoning

**Publication**: Parallax
**Subject line**: I took my two-year-old to Coachella and my judgment of it broke.
**Preview text**: A Madeon moment I didn't see coming, a two-year-old asleep in the wagon, and what I was looking at wrong.
**CTA type**: Reply
**Prompt pack**: The Presence Audit
**Arc position**: Edition 2 — Power Demonstration (pivoted from Ed 04 for cultural timing — Weekend 2 ended 2026-04-19)

---

Saturday evening at Coachella, Madeon played the opening of "Shelter" during his set.

I wasn't expecting to hear it. Madeon had said farewell to that song a while back and aged it out of his sets. It's a fan favorite. One of my most cherished songs. When the intro started building, I got so excited.

My wife Jen screamed "BABE!" I grabbed JJ, pulled him up on my shoulders, and we got to share a moment I wasn't expecting. Especially at Coachella of all places.

---

My wife walked out to "Shelter" at our wedding. She'd had someone play it acoustically, on keyboard, the whole song, as she came down. I didn't know it was coming. It's carried a specific weight for me ever since.

I had it filed under songs I'd have to wait fifteen years to share with my son. EDC maybe, when JJ was eighteen. If they still played it by then.

I've been judgmental of Coachella for a long time. Not the music. Some of my favorite DJs play this festival every year, and I've been a Madeon fan since before most of that crowd could spell his name. What I couldn't stand was the culture around it. Outfits as a whole personality. Strangers pushing past you to get the photo. The festival feeling less about what was on stage than about being seen near it.

We live 150 miles away. Every year I'd scroll past the posts and think: not for me.

We went to Weekend 2 for Laarni. It was her birthday, she was celebrating at Coachella, and we showed up for her. Me, Jen, JJ in a wagon, and our best friend Ryan. Ryan is JJ's godfather. He'd been with us at Palm Tree Festival it was JJ's first major festival ever. Coachella was JJ's first big one.

Friday and Saturday. Not the demographic they put on the flyer.

---

We had a wagon. A stroller. Snacks. A phone.

When music hit JJ, he'd want up. Arms out. Eyes on the stage. Moving with the bass. When it didn't, he'd point at the phone. His shows. YouTube. Ten minutes of whatever two-year-olds watch to refill, then he'd look up, hear something he liked, and want back in.

Nothing about it was performance. He was there when he was there, and checked out when he wasn't, and nobody was grading him on the transitions. By 10:20 Saturday night he was already out cold in the wagon as we walked toward Bieber.

---

The internet was deciding, in real time, that Bieber's set was a disaster.

"Phoned in." Lazy. Disrespectful. He wasn't giving people what they came for.

Most of my attention that set was on JJ asleep in the wagon. Jen's a Bieber fan. I'm not. I don't know his music. But I watched enough to see what was actually happening on that stage.

Bieber walked to the front and let fans touch him. Made eye contact with people who'd been screaming his name since grade school. Pulled up lyric videos onstage as a workaround, because he'd sold the rights to most of his catalog and needed something to read from to play the old stuff. During "One Less Lonely Girl," his wife Hailey pushed Billie Eilish out on stage and let Billie have her crush moment with him. The crowd got the old songs they'd come for, and then Bieber played the new era music he actually wanted to share.

He gave them all of himself. In the parts he wanted to give.

The real fans loved it. The drag came from critics and people who weren't really there for the music, and they're always louder than the fans, so the story online became "phoned in" by Sunday weekend 1.

---

That's what JJ and Bieber had in common Saturday night, though I didn't put it together until the drive home.

Both of them showed up as themselves, gave what was real, and didn't perform anything they weren't feeling. JJ slept when he was tired. Bieber played the crowd's old songs without pretending to love them and played his new songs without pretending the crowd wanted them.

Neither got read as present by the people watching from outside. The crowd wanted a performance from one and a peak festival moment from the other. Neither gave them that.

---

Back to the music.

After Madeon, Knock2 played "dashstar*" in his b2b with DJ Snake. "dashstar*" is my hype song. My friends know it as my song. We played it at JJ's gender reveal and at the drop, we pop cannons that revealed what I already knew in my heart, our 1st born was going to be a boy.

Two songs on the same night carried big parts of my life. I got to finally share them with my son. Unexpectedly. At Coachella.

I went into the weekend expecting to survive a festival I didn't love so Jen and Laarni could have a good time. I came out with something I didn't know Coachella had to give me.

Coachella was what it was. I was looking at it wrong.

Next to the female filming her own face to ensure her makeup was holding up, and some guy mouthing "We need more shots". Past the guy in a fit that screams I paid a lot for this taking a photo was Jen, quietly dancing to a song from our wedding with our son.

From the outside, it all looks like phones in the air. You can't tell presence from performance from the outside.

---

We left early Sunday afternoon. JJ was out cold in his car seat, ready for the drive home from a fun weekend. Ryan, Jen, and I took one last selfie at Jen's Airbnb property, where we stayed for the weekend, to say goodbye before she sells it and commemorate Ryan's first Coachella, too, and somehow the whole weekend had worked out for everyone who came with us.

Jen looked at me in the car and said, "Well, that worked better than expected." I laughed. We drove home with the music down.
