## The Post (with Hook Option 1)

Yesterday my two-year-old had the biggest meltdown of his life. It was withdrawal.

I'd been telling myself a story for months. The story was: I'm hustling for this family. Clients, money, food on the table. If JJ gets the iPad while I take a call, that's the cost of building something for him.

Yesterday his whole body forgot how to be a body for forty minutes. The kind of crying that comes from a small nervous system that learned to expect a hit on a schedule and didn't get it.

I stood in the hallway outside his room and watched my excuse die in real time.

Cheap dopamine for a two-year-old isn't free childcare. It's a withdrawal account. He'd been running a deficit and I'd been calling it efficiency.

Here's the deal I've made with myself, and I'm writing it down so I can't unsee it: when he naps, I grind. I bust my ass. I move money. While he's awake, I'm with him. Eyes on him. Phone face-down. If a screen ever comes out again, I better goddamn be doing the thing I claimed I was sacrificing for, not scrolling because I'm tired.

The lie wasn't "I need to work." The lie was using "I need to work" to cover for "I want to check out."

I don't know a single parent or founder who hasn't run that script in some form. The cover story changes — for some it's email at the dinner table, for others it's the third pour of wine, for others it's something else entirely. The shape underneath is identical: a noble-sounding excuse stapled to a small daily betrayal of the person you said you were doing it for.

That's the work for today. Tomorrow JJ wakes up at 6:43, and I want to be there when he does.
