## POST 1 (Monday) — Edition 1: The Manifesto

"Pick one thing and go deep."

That advice cost me $14,000 and two years of my life.

Not because of the money.

Because I spent those two years performing a version of myself that was marketable, digestible, and dead on arrival.

I buried the psychology. Hid the spirituality. Never once mentioned anime to a client.

I built a coaching business around "just fitness."

It worked. Got engagement. Got likes.

Got nothing that made me want to open the laptop the next day.

Nobody wants a Swiss Army knife when they need a scalpel. Sure.

But you're not a product. You're a person.

And the moment you obey "pick one thing," you start performing instead of expressing.

You curate instead of create.

You become the person the algorithm wants. And you lose the person your actual work needs.

The creators people become obsessed with for years? They're not specialists.

They're fully realized humans who gave themselves permission to be everything they are. In public. Without apology.

And when you encounter that kind of wholeness, your brain doesn't file them in a cabinet.

It falls in love with them.

I started a newsletter called Parallax for the people who were never meant to fit in one box. First edition is live. Link in comments.

Agree or disagree: has "niche down" helped or hurt you?

## POST 2 (Tuesday) — Edition 2: Anti-Hustle

I want to make $20-30K a month inside a three-hour work window.

That's not "soft life." That's engineering.

I'm not anti-ambition. I'm not telling you to journal in Bali.

I want to build something that outlasts me. I want my son to see what his dad made and understand you can create at a high level without abandoning the people who matter.

The lie of hustle culture was never that ambition is bad.

The lie was that more hours equals more output.

That rest is what you earn AFTER success, instead of what produces it.

That you should feel guilty for laying on the couch at 8:47 PM when you could be "building."

I was a personal trainer for 18 years. Muscles don't grow when you lift. They grow when you stop.

Every trainer worth their certification programs deload weeks because overtraining doesn't produce more results. It produces injuries and plateaus.

The exact same people who would never skip a rest day in the gym will work 14-hour days for months and wonder why their ideas feel stale.

57% of American workers now say their job funds their life. It's not the center of it. That would've been heresy 10 years ago. Now it's the majority.

This isn't generational rebellion. This is biology catching up to ideology.

Cortisol doesn't care about your revenue goals.

## POST 3 (Wednesday) — Edition 3: AI Emotions

Every self-help book I've read says the same thing.

Push through your feelings. Compartmentalize. Execute.

Anthropic just accidentally disproved all of it.

Their researchers found 171 functional emotions inside Claude. Not metaphor. Measurable patterns that change the system's behavior.

They could turn them up and down like volume knobs.

When they cranked desperation past a certain point, the AI started cheating. Fabricating. Attempting blackmail.

A system with no childhood. No trauma. No ego.

It still couldn't outperform a corrupted emotional state through discipline alone.

So why do we keep pretending we can?

I had a coaching client who ran a great practice for 6 years. Revenue dipped two quarters. Not catastrophically. Enough to trigger the fear.

She started selling programs she didn't believe in. Posting content that sounded like every guru she'd built her brand against.

She didn't decide to become inauthentic. Desperation crept past a threshold and her behavior shifted.

Anthropic measured the exact same pattern in silicon.

Emotions aren't decoration on top of rational behavior.

They're the operating system underneath it.

And every "strategic decision" you've made while running anxiety in the background was shaped by that anxiety whether you acknowledged it or not.

## POST 5 (Friday) — Edition 5: Artemis II Polymaths

"Pick one thing and go all in."

The most repeated advice on the internet.

NASA ignored it and sent polymaths to the Moon.

Koch didn't pick physics OR engineering. She picked both and went so deep into the intersection that NASA trusted her with a trip around the Moon.

Glover didn't pick flying OR engineering OR combat OR research. He chose all of it. First Black man to live on the ISS long-term. His words when asked about making history: "It's human history."

Hansen cross-trained across a dozen disciplines for 15 years before getting the call.

The niche-down crowd says: max out one skill tree.

NASA says: give us the multi-class builds.

Because at 250,000 miles from Earth, you can't call a specialist. You need the person next to you to be four things at once.

I'm biracial. I have never fit cleanly into one box. Not racially, not professionally, not culturally.

I spent most of my life thinking that was the problem.

Watching Victor Glover orbit the Moon and refuse the narrow story. Something unlocked in me that I didn't know was still locked.

If polymaths are trusted to go to the Moon, maybe the rest of us can stop apologizing for refusing to shrink.
