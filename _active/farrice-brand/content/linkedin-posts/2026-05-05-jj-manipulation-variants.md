### THE POST (copy-ready)

I just manipulated my two-year-old son, and I don't know how to feel about it.

He was overtired. We moved him from the crib to a real bed last week, and the bed has too many associations with play. He knows he needs to sleep. His body knows. He just can't get there on his own yet.

So I read him. I lowered my voice to the exact register that turns his shoulders down. I named the thing he was scared of before he could name it. Then I gave him real stakes... in the only economy he understands. In our house, everything goes to sleep when he does... the lights, the dog, his toys. So I told him: if he wants his toy to play with later, he has to sleep now. Because if he doesn't, the toy isn't going to wake up. To anyone outside this house, that sounds insane. To him, it's the only frame that lands. He chose the toy. He was asleep in four minutes.

Some people would call that manipulation.

I think those people have never had to actually get someone where they need to go.

Manipulation without positive intent is a knife. Manipulation with love in your heart and the other person's benefit on the line is just being good at your job. It's the difference between a con artist and a doctor who finally gets the diabetic to eat. Same tools. Opposite soul.

Most marketers I know are afraid of this. They want to "be authentic" and "let the work speak." That's code for not wanting to be responsible for the outcome.

I get it. I've been the writer who softened the line that would have actually helped the person reading. I told myself I was being kind. I was being scared, and I was billing them for it.

If you actually give a fuck about the person on the other side, you'll learn to speak their exact language. Even when it sounds insane to anyone else. Even when it isolates the people who aren't them. That isolation is the price. Broad palatability is what you choose when you'd rather not land than risk landing wrong.

I sat with the manipulation question for a while today. I'm still not fully comfortable with it. I think that's the point. The day it stops bothering me is the day I should worry, whether it's with JJ or with the audience reading this.
