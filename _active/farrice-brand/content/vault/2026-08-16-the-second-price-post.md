# The second price

A woman in a Costco aisle is doing arithmetic on her phone.

On the pallet, a two-pack of prenatal, $48.99, sixty days. She is already subscribed to the same product from the same brand, at $39 a month.

She checks the site because she does not believe it. Ritual's own page tells her it is the exact same formula, same sourcing, no changes, and calls the Costco price its lowest cost per day available anywhere it is sold.

Her loyalty costs $1.30 a day. The shelf costs 82 cents.

Nothing went wrong here. Club retail runs on bigger packs at lower unit cost, and any brand that wants that volume pays that math. Ritual is also one of very few brands in this category with its own published clinical work, which is the harder thing to buy and the easier thing to lose track of.

What is missing is one sentence.

Landing a retail account does not add a channel. It publishes a second price for the same product, and the person most likely to find that price is the customer who believed you first, at full freight, before you had a shelf.

Someone in the building has to decide what the subscription is for now. Cadence, guidance, the thing you can do for a person who is thirty days in that a warehouse pallet structurally cannot.

If nobody decides it, the price tag decides it, and the price tag's answer is: you were paying too much.

Nobody raises this in the retail meeting. That meeting is about pallets and PO dates and everyone is happy.

Open your subscription page next to your retail price and write the sentence that survives both.

If you cannot write it, that is the finding.
