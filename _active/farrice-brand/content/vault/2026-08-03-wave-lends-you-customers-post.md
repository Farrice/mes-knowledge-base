Eighteen Januaries on a gym floor teach you one trick: by week two, you can tell who will still be there in April.

It was never effort. Week-two effort looks identical. The tell was the reason they walked in. Some people arrived carrying a problem they had felt for years. Others arrived carrying a resolution the calendar handed them. Same program, same coaches, same soreness. By April, one group was still showing up.

Supplements are having a January every quarter now.

Cortisol content pulled roughly 800 million TikTok views. Fiber jumped 20 percent as a category behind the fibermaxxing wave. Sleep routines turned into an equipment list. Every wave hands funded brands the cheapest acquisition they have seen in years.

The cohort math underneath is less friendly. In one 2,000-adult survey, 53 percent of Gen Z said they buy wellness products partly to keep up. And when UK researchers asked shoppers about the fiber wave, under 8 percent credited social media for their own changes. The wave delivers buyers who cannot tell you the wave sent them. Your attribution reads clean. Your reorder curve tells the truth later.

A buyer who came for the trend leaves with the trend. A buyer who came for a felt problem stays as long as the product keeps its promise.

Your creative decides which one your money acquires. That is the real job of an angle: choosing the belief a customer walks in carrying.

The wave will lend you customers either way. Whether you keep any of them was decided in the ad, before the first bottle shipped.
