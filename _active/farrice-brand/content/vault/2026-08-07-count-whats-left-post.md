# Count what's left (flagship post)

A founder posted that his Meta ads had stopped working.

Same budget, same product, nothing touched in the account. His words: "nothing changed on my side."

He was right. That was the problem.

Three things happened this week that had nothing to do with him and everything to do with his numbers.

IM8, nineteen months old, described how it pays for marketing: General Catalyst funds 70% of whatever it spends each month, in advance, inside a billion-dollar facility. Function Health took $450 million from the same fund the same week.

OpenAI started running product carousels inside ChatGPT. It pulls products from retailer feeds and decides which ones sit side by side. You do not buy that placement.

And Meta's targeting keeps swallowing decisions that used to belong to a person with a spreadsheet.

Count what's left.

Spend, partly underwritten on someone else's balance sheet. Placement, chosen by a model. Targeting, absorbed by an algorithm. Three levers a founder used to pull, each one now somewhere he cannot reach.

One lever did not move.

Which belief the money is buying. What the product should claim, who has to believe it, what proof goes in front.

That decision still sits entirely inside his company, and in most companies it is not assigned to anybody. Creative production receives it. Compliance reviews it afterward. Retention inherits it. Media spends against it. Nobody makes it.

So it gets made by default, by whoever's brief was loudest that month.

I spent years behind a vitamin shop counter. The people who did well were never the ones with the biggest display. They were the ones who could say the true thing in one sentence when somebody finally asked.

That is still the only part of this you own.
