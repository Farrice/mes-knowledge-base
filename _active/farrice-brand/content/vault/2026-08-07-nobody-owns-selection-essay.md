# Nobody owns selection

Four complaints run through supplement brands constantly.

One says the cohort didn't retain. Another says legal keeps killing our best line. A third wants more creative. The fourth just says CAC is up.

Every one of those is locally accurate. They belong to different departments, so they never get said in the same meeting, and nobody notices they are four vocabularies for one problem.

Together they describe a function that does not exist on any supplement brand's org chart.

Look at what a growing brand actually staffs. Creative production. Media buying. Compliance review. Retention operations. Brand and PR.

Every one is a production function. Each takes a decision as an input and turns it into output, into assets and placements and approvals and flows. Not one is chartered to make the decision itself: what this product should claim, who has to believe it, and what proof goes in front.

For most of this category's history that was fine, because the founder made that call and he was physically in every room. It stops working somewhere around five million dollars, when he isn't. Nothing announces the failure. The function simply goes unstaffed while every department keeps producing at full speed.

Watch how the four complaints resolve once you see it.

The cohort didn't retain because the ad acquired people who believed something the product does not deliver. That was decided in the ad, not in the retention flow.

Legal killed the best line because the claim boundary arrived after the creative existed instead of before it. That was decided in the brief, or rather it wasn't.

We need more creative is thirty variations of one belief being mistaken for thirty angles. That was decided by whoever wrote the first one.

And CAC is up partly because a competitor has seventy percent of its monthly ad spend wired in advance by an outside fund, which is not a decision anyone at your company made at all.

Four departments. One missing function.

Each diagnosis relocates the failure somewhere it can be argued about safely, as a budget line. That is exactly why the real thing survives every quarterly review untouched.

It stays unstaffed because claiming it costs something.

A founder's authority rests on understanding the product better than anyone alive. A growth lead's rests on running a rigorous testing program. Neither can pick up the selection function without conceding it was never held, and that concession is expensive in a way no line item is.

So the decision defaults. And a defaulted decision is still a decision. It gets made by whoever's brief happened to be loudest that month, and then thirty people build against it for a quarter.

I don't think this is a discipline problem or a talent problem. Every person in that chain is doing the job they were hired for. The job nobody was hired for is the one that sets the value of all the others.

If you want to find it in your own company, don't audit the creative. Ask who decided what the product has to prove, and when. If four people give you four answers, or if the honest answer is that it came from a deck somebody made in March, that's the finding.

It was always the founder's job. It just stopped being in the room.
