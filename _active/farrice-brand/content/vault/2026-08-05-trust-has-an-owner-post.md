# Trust has an owner (flagship post)

Monday afternoon, CNBC. P&G announces it is buying Thorne, the supplement brand practitioners recommend most, for $3.8 billion.

By dinner, the top post on r/Supplements was a funeral. "RIP Thorne, it was nice knowing you." 855 upvotes.

Nothing on the product side moved. Not the formulas, not the certifications, not the manufacturing, not the price, not a single word on the label. The label tells the truth today at exactly the rate it did Monday morning.

The best customers left anyway. In public. In an afternoon.

Read the thread and you find buyers keeping records nobody assumes they keep. One commenter dated the brand's decline to 2023, when private equity took over, three years before this week's headline. Another recited the category's ownership map from memory: which brands Nestlé holds, which one P&G just took. The most-upvoted replacement pick earned its 329 upvotes on one qualification: "an owner who refuses to bend over or sell out."

Zero top comments mention ingredients.

A premium supplement label makes one promise underneath all the others: you can stop re-vetting us. This week showed who actually underwrites that promise: the owner, the scientists who stayed, whatever part of the process a buyer can still watch. When the underwriter changes, the promise goes unbacked, while every printed word stays true.

P&G's own announcement names the asset it bought: "a trusted, science-backed brand." The buyers read that sentence too.

I spent years behind a vitamin-shop counter. Customers never asked me which label was right. They asked who was behind it. That question just became the whole category, out loud.

If your deck says "trusted brand," this was the week to find out what's underwriting yours.
