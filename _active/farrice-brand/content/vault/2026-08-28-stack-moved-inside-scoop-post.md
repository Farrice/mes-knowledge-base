# A product can simplify the routine while its campaign complicates the decision

A literal stack of bottles sits on the counter: energy, focus, stress, sleep,
plus the outcome that did not fit neatly on the front label.

The buyer wanted a simpler routine. The campaign gave them another decision to
make.

That tension is showing up in the market. A current Biohackers post drew 370
comments. In a separate checked thread, one buyer put the feeling plainly:
“This is why people eventually simplify.”

But there are two kinds of simplicity.

Routine simplicity means fewer products, fewer steps, fewer bottles. DIZZY
makes that compression tangible: 12 ingredients in one £38 formula versus five
products totaling £93.

Decision simplicity is different.

It means the buyer immediately understands which problem the formula is for,
when it belongs in their life, and why they should remember it.

A product can simplify the routine while its campaign complicates the decision.

That is why the next creative round can feel busy even when the product is
simple to use. Benefits become angles, outcomes compete for the opening, and
ads try to carry the whole formula.

The result is a campaign with plenty of ideas and no hierarchy.

Your formula may do several jobs, but your campaign still has to choose which
one the buyer should remember.

That choice should not come from whichever benefit sounds most exciting in a
brainstorm. It should come from the strongest intersection of buyer occasion
and defensible evidence.

Try this audit today:

Count every outcome named before the first proof point in your current creative.

Then group those outcomes by buyer occasion: the moment, situation, or recurring
need that makes someone look for help.

Finally, choose the one occasion-outcome pairing your strongest evidence can
defend.

That becomes the lead angle. The other benefits can support it instead of
competing with it.

If your formula is simple but the campaign still feels crowded, DM ANGLE. The
Angle Map is built to help make that decision.
