# Your cancel rate is a review of your angle (flagship post)

A guy came back to my counter in week three and told me he wasn't feeling anything.

He wasn't angry. He was a little embarrassed, like he had failed the product.

Eighteen years I stood on that side of the counter, and that conversation happened every week. Nobody had ever told him what week three was supposed to feel like. The label promised a benefit. The ad promised he would feel it. Neither one said when.

So he made up a deadline. Everybody does. Four weeks, six weeks, whenever the bottle runs out.

I read a founder thread this week where a guy had spent three thousand dollars on ads, brought in about thirty-five customers, and one of them subscribed. His whole model needs roughly a third of them to subscribe. The room told him to fix the funnel, fix the offer, kill the campaign, try Google.

Nobody asked what his ad had promised. Nobody asked by when.

In another thread, a woman explained why she cancelled. Her words: "I would have subscribed if I had gotten the results that they promised. It was supposed to take a few weeks to begin seeing results."

That is a customer telling a brand the sale went fine and the promise was wrong.

Your subscription rate is the market grading the promise your ad made, on a deadline your ad implied. Retention software cannot argue with that grade. It can only pay monthly to delay it.

Somewhere in your company, somebody decided when your customer would feel something. There is a decent chance nobody knows who.
