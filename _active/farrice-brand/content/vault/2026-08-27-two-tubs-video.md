# Two tubs (45-60s)

**[0:00–0:06, cold open, no intro]**
Two tubs. One in each hand. He is not reading the ingredients, he is reading two numbers.

**[0:06–0:14]**
One says five grams. One says ten. He looks at me for permission. I sold him the ten. I had nothing better to point at.

**[0:14–0:24]**
That was my job for five years, and it was never a consumer problem. It was a marketing decision that failed so far upstream it reached all the way down to a counter in a vitamin shop.

**[0:24–0:36]**
Today's launch sheet has two new creatine products at ten grams. One says out loud that most products are five or less. The number is the whole strategy. And your competitor beats a number by Tuesday with a label revision.

**[0:36–0:48]**
Worse than that. Go and read what people write about high dose creatine. They are not comparing brands. They took a second scoop because a creator told them to. You are charging a premium for a decision they already made with a spoon.

**[0:48–0:58]**
Same launch sheet, one brand did not raise a number. It named three million people who work nights. Nobody copies that by Tuesday.

---

**Retention beats:** the open is an image with no setup; the first loop closes at 0:14 on "I had nothing better to point at" and immediately opens a second; the beat at 0:36 is the one most viewers cannot predict, and it is deliberately the free-scoop cost rather than the lawsuit, because the lawsuit is the expected beat; the close hands over an idea rather than a call to action.
**Better-off test (Outer ring):** the viewer leaves with a transferable idea about competing on a metric the customer can self-serve, whether or not they sell supplements.
