# The drawer where supplements go to die (flagship post)

There's a drawer in my kitchen where supplements go to die.

Third one down. A tub missing two scoops. A $70 bottle I bought at 1 a.m. because the ad found me at the exact right moment of a bad week.

Every one of them promised I'd feel something. Focus. Clean energy. A sharper morning.

I coached people for 18 years and spent five of those behind a Vitamin Shoppe counter, so I know how this story usually gets told: the products are junk, the industry is a scam, case closed.

That's not what I see.

Most of those products have a real mechanism. What they don't have is a calendar that matches their promise. The label sells today. The mechanism runs on weeks. And in the space between, the customer decides you lied.

The buyers already know this, by the way. There's a guy on Reddit who spent a month tracking his own response to a nootropic stack and built graphs from his own data. He trusts his spreadsheet more than any label in the category. He is not an outlier. He is the market now.

Meanwhile the category's hottest brands are raising millions on the promise of a feeling.

The brands that survive the next cycle will make a different kind of promise. Not "you'll feel it." Something a customer can actually check: what should be true at day 7, what shouldn't be true yet, and what to watch for at day 30.

A supplement doesn't fail in the body. It fails in the calendar.
