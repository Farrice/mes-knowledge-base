**On-screen title: "Thirty-six women"**

The entire creatine-for-menopause wave is standing on thirty-six women.

[beat]

That trial behind the headlines about brain benefits in midlife: thirty-six participants, split into four groups, for eight weeks. Nine women per arm.

And one more detail nobody's marketing team kept: the trial used creatine hydrochloride at small doses. The tub in your cart is almost certainly monohydrate at five grams. Different form. Different dose. Same headline.

[beat, hands up]

This is not me calling creatine snake oil. Monohydrate has one of the deepest research bases of any supplement on earth. That's exactly the point. The strong evidence and the new headline are about different things, and the buyer can't tell where one ends and the other begins.

If you run a brand in this lane, that gap is your angle decision. Sell what the deep evidence carries. Say what the new evidence is, plainly: early, interesting, and small.

The woman doing her own homework will trust you more for it, because everyone else just yelled the headline at her.
