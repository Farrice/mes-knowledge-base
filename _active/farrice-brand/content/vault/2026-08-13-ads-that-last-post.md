# The ad that started last July

On the second monitor, somebody pulls up a competitor's ad. The date stamp underneath says July 2025.

Same ad. Still running. Thirteen months.

Your account has burned through a few hundred creatives in that window and killed most of them by week three.

That gap is now measurable. An agency pulled the 500 strongest supplement ads on Meta this year and measured how long each one survived. Median: 394 days. The same study last year put the average top performer at 215. The winners in this category are not being refreshed. They are being left alone.

The next number is the one I keep turning over. Across nine opener types, the one most associated with running long is the problem callout, where the ad names what is wrong before it mentions the product. It appears in 4.2% of the ads. The largest single category, at 33.2%, is ads that open with no verbal hook at all.

The longest-lasting opener is the rarest one, and a third of the field has stopped talking.

There is a reason and it is not laziness. In this category, naming the problem out loud is the line that goes to legal and comes back missing. So the ad opens on a jar and a woman looking relieved, and three weeks later it needs replacing.

Behind the counter I could tell which products would move by whether I could say the sentence out loud without adding a paragraph after it.

An ad that fatigues in three weeks is usually an ad nobody decided anything about, aging on schedule.

Open the ad library. Find three competitors. Read the start dates.

If the oldest ad in your category belongs to somebody else, the missing thing is not a batch.
