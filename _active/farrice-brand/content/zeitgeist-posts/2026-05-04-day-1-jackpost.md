## DRAFT POST

```
"Why are we still doing this manually?"

Six different founders posted some version of this on my feed this week.
The question went viral. It feels insightful. It IS insightful.

But after the seventh post, I started noticing something else.

If you're the ONLY one in your company asking that question —
you don't have an automation problem.

You have a delegation problem.

Elijah Farrell put it perfectly on this feed last week:
automation without delegation is just faster micromanagement.

The manual task is downstream.
The real problem is upstream — nobody else on your team has the
permission, the context, or the courage to flag what should be
automated.

Which means the highest-leverage question for a founder right now
isn't "what should we automate?"

It's "why am I the only one who notices?"

Build a culture where that question gets asked from every seat,
and the automations build themselves.

Build it from your seat alone, and you'll be the only person who
can ever turn them on, fix them, or scale them.

What's a manual task in your business right now that someone ELSE
on your team noticed before you did?

Tell me in the comments — I'm collecting examples for a piece on
"automation without delegation" next week.
```
