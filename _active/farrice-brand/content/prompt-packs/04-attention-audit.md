# Prompt Pack 04: The Attention Audit

**Edition**: 04 — Digital Nicotine
**Micro-transformation**: "I went from 'I know I scroll too much' to 'I can see exactly where my attention goes and I have a plan to redirect it'"
**Time to complete**: 15 minutes
**Run in**: Claude or ChatGPT

---

## Prompt 1: The Attention Inventory

**Purpose**: Map where your attention actually goes in a typical day — not where you think it goes, where it *actually* goes.

```
I want to run an honest audit of where my attention goes on a typical day. 
Help me build a complete map.

For each waking hour (from when I wake up to when I sleep), I'm going to 
tell you what I'm typically doing — and I want you to help me categorize 
each activity into one of these buckets:

BUILDING — attention that creates something that compounds (writing, learning 
a skill, deep work, meaningful conversation)
MAINTAINING — attention that keeps life running (chores, logistics, email, 
necessary admin)
CONSUMING — attention that takes in without creating (scrolling feeds, 
passive watching, news cycles)
RECOVERING — genuine rest and recovery (sleep, exercise, meditation, play 
with no agenda)
LEAKING — attention that goes somewhere you didn't choose and doesn't serve 
any of the above categories

Be honest with me. If something I call "research" is actually scrolling with 
a justification, call it out. If something I call "relaxing" is actually 
doom-scrolling disguised as downtime, flag it.

Here's my typical day:
[DESCRIBE YOUR TYPICAL DAY HOUR BY HOUR — be brutally honest about what 
you're actually doing, not what your calendar says]

After mapping everything, show me:
1. Total hours in each category
2. The ratio of BUILDING to CONSUMING 
3. The biggest single block of LEAKING time
4. One observation I probably don't want to hear
```

**What to do with the output**: Look at the LEAKING category. That's not rest. That's not recreation. That's time an algorithm took from you without asking. Write that number down. You'll need it for Prompt 4.

## Prompt 2: The Dopamine Loop Finder

**Purpose**: Identify which specific apps, habits, and behaviors are running variable reward schedules on you — the same mechanic that makes loot boxes and slot machines addictive.

```
Variable reward schedules are the psychological mechanic behind slot machines, 
loot boxes, and social media feeds. The pattern: unpredictable rewards create 
compulsive repetition. You keep pulling the lever because you MIGHT get the 
good thing.

I want you to analyze my digital habits and identify which ones are running 
this mechanic on me. For each app or habit I list, tell me:

1. IS IT VARIABLE? — Does the reward change unpredictably each time I engage? 
   (Yes = variable reward schedule is active)
2. THE HOOK — What specific reward am I chasing? (Likes, new content, 
   validation, information, entertainment, connection)
3. THE COST — What do I pay in attention/time/energy per session? 
   (Estimate based on what I tell you)
4. THE REAL ROI — If I tracked this for 30 days, would the cumulative 
   reward justify the cumulative cost?
5. COMPULSION TEST — Can I use this for exactly 10 minutes and stop without 
   friction? (If no, the loop is running you)

Here are my regular digital habits and apps:
[LIST EVERY APP YOU CHECK REGULARLY, every digital habit, every 
"I'll just look for a second" behavior — include social media, news, 
games, shopping, dating apps, YouTube, Reddit, everything]

After analyzing each one, sort them into three tiers:
- TOOLS: You control the session. Clear purpose in, clear exit out.
- TRAPS: Variable reward schedule is active. You regularly spend more time 
  than intended.
- SLOT MACHINES: You cannot use this for 10 minutes and stop. The loop 
  owns you.
```

**What to do with the output**: Your SLOT MACHINES list is where the most attention is leaking. Don't delete the apps yet. Just see them clearly. Awareness before action — the behavior change literature is unanimous on this.

## Prompt 3: The Attention Budget

**Purpose**: Design an intentional allocation of your attention as if it were your most valuable resource — because it is.

```
I want to build an Attention Budget the way a CFO builds a financial budget. 
My attention is finite, non-renewable within each day, and the highest-value 
resource I have.

Here's my data:
- Waking hours per day: [YOUR NUMBER, usually 15-17]
- My Attention Inventory showed this breakdown: [PASTE YOUR CATEGORY TOTALS 
  FROM PROMPT 1]
- My Dopamine Loop Finder identified these as TRAPS and SLOT MACHINES: 
  [PASTE YOUR TRAPS AND SLOT MACHINES FROM PROMPT 2]

Now help me build a budget. The rules:
1. BUILDING gets the first allocation (like paying yourself first). 
   What is the minimum daily BUILDING time that would move my most 
   important project forward? Be specific — which project, which actions.
2. MAINTAINING stays realistic. Don't slash it to zero. Life requires admin.
3. RECOVERING is non-negotiable. Real recovery — not phone-in-hand 
   "relaxation." What does genuine recovery look like for me?
4. CONSUMING gets a specific, bounded allocation. I'm not eliminating it. 
   I'm making it intentional. How much, when, and what KIND of consumption 
   actually serves me?
5. LEAKING gets zero. That's the goal. Not perfection — direction.

Design my Attention Budget as a daily template. Include:
- Time blocks with specific activities
- Boundaries (what signals "stop" for each block)
- The ONE habit change that would create the biggest shift based on my data
- A "circuit breaker" — a specific action I take when I catch myself in 
  a LEAKING state (something physical, not willpower-based)
```

**What to do with the output**: Print this. Seriously. Put it next to your desk or on your bathroom mirror. The budget only works if it's visible. And don't try to implement all of it tomorrow — pick the ONE habit change it recommends and run that for a week.

## Prompt 4: The Reclamation

**Purpose**: Take the biggest block of leaked attention from your audit and redirect it toward something that compounds.

```
In my Attention Audit, the biggest block of LEAKING time was approximately 
[X] hours per day, mostly going to [DESCRIBE WHERE IT GOES — which apps, 
which habits].

I want to reclaim ONE hour of that leaked time and redirect it to something 
that compounds — something where the value of hour 100 is dramatically 
greater than the value of hour 1.

Help me design The Reclamation:

1. THE HOUR: When specifically in my day will I reclaim this hour? 
   (Pick the time slot where I'm currently leaking the most — replace 
   the default behavior, don't just "add" a new one)

2. THE INVESTMENT: What will I redirect this hour toward? 
   Help me choose based on these criteria:
   - It must COMPOUND (the skill, asset, or relationship grows over time)
   - It must be INTRINSICALLY rewarding (not just "productive" — I need 
     to actually want to do it, or the feed wins)
   - It must be VISIBLE within 30 days (I need early evidence that the 
     trade was worth it)
   
   My current goals and interests: [BRIEFLY DESCRIBE WHAT YOU'RE BUILDING 
   OR WHAT MATTERS TO YOU]

3. THE MATH: Show me what 1 hour/day x 365 days looks like for my chosen 
   investment. What could I realistically have built/learned/created in 
   365 hours? Be specific, not motivational.

4. THE SWAP RITUAL: Design a specific, physical action that replaces the 
   moment I would normally pick up my phone. Not "use willpower." 
   An actual behavior. (Example: phone goes in a drawer, I pick up a 
   specific book/notebook/instrument instead. The physical environment 
   does the work.)

5. THE 7-DAY TEST: Give me a specific, trackable challenge for the next 
   7 days. What am I doing, when, and how do I know if it worked?
```

**What to do with the output**: Run the 7-day test. Don't announce it on social media. Don't make it an identity. Just do it for 7 days and see what shifts. The compound math only matters if day 1 actually happens.

## Commentary

Most "digital detox" advice is abstinence dressed up as strategy. Delete the apps. Turn off notifications. Go touch grass. It's the attention equivalent of a crash diet — it works for 72 hours and then you're back on the feed at 11pm because willpower is a depletable resource and the algorithm has infinite patience.

The Attention Audit works differently. It doesn't start with restriction. It starts with awareness. Prompt 1 maps the territory. Prompt 2 identifies the mechanics. Prompt 3 designs the alternative. Prompt 4 makes the trade.

The insight underneath all four prompts is this: you don't beat a variable reward schedule with discipline. You beat it with a better reward. Something that compounds. Something that's yours. Something that, 365 hours from now, has built a thing the algorithm never could have given you.

Your attention isn't leaking because you're weak. It's leaking because extremely smart people designed extremely effective systems to capture it. The playing field isn't level. But the budget is yours to write.

## Advanced Variation (Power Users)

After running all 4 prompts, add this:

```
Take my full Attention Audit — the inventory, the dopamine loops, the 
budget, and the reclamation plan — and design an Attention Dashboard I 
can review weekly.

Include:
- 3 key metrics I track (simple, not obsessive)
- A weekly "leak check" — where did attention go that I didn't choose?
- A monthly compound report — what has the reclaimed time built so far?
- An escalation protocol — if LEAKING hours creep back above [X], 
  what specific intervention do I run?

Make it something I can review in 5 minutes every Sunday. Not a system. 
A mirror.
```

This turns a one-time audit into an ongoing attention practice. Not a system. Not a productivity hack. A practice. The kind that changes what you see when you look at your phone at midnight.
