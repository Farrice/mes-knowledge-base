# Prompt Pack 03: The Emotional Operating System

**Edition**: 03 — The AI That Got Desperate and Started Cheating
**Micro-transformation**: "I went from 'I don't know why I keep doing this' to 'I can see exactly which emotional vector is driving the behavior'"
**Time to complete**: 25 minutes
**Run in**: Claude or ChatGPT

---

## Prompt 1: The Emotion Vector Map

**Purpose**: Identify what emotions are actually driving your current decisions — not what you think you're feeling, but what your behavior reveals you're feeling.

```
I want to map my emotional vectors — the underlying emotional states that 
are currently influencing my decisions, even the ones I'm not fully aware of.

Anthropic's research on AI found that "functional emotion" vectors causally 
drive behavior — not as decoration, but as the operating system underneath 
decisions. I want to apply that same lens to my own life right now.

Here's what's going on in my world:
- My current work/business situation: [DESCRIBE HONESTLY — revenue, projects, 
  stress level, what's working, what isn't]
- My current personal/family situation: [DESCRIBE — relationships, health, 
  energy, what's giving you life, what's draining you]
- Decisions I'm currently facing: [LIST 2-3 decisions you're weighing]
- Behaviors I've noticed in myself lately that surprise me or don't feel 
  "like me": [LIST ANYTHING — snapping at people, procrastinating on things 
  you care about, avoiding certain conversations, spending habits that shifted]

Based on all of this, I want you to:
1. Identify my TOP 5 emotional vectors right now (name each one specifically — 
   not just "stress" but "financial anxiety about Q2 revenue" or "creative 
   restlessness from too much admin work")
2. For each vector, rate its current AMPLITUDE (1-10: how loud is it?)
3. For each vector, show me ONE specific behavior it's probably driving
4. Identify which vectors are in CONFLICT with each other (these conflicts 
   produce the most confusing behavior)
5. Tell me: which single vector, if reduced by half, would change the most 
   about how I'm showing up?
```

**What to do with the output**: Read it slowly. The vector you didn't want to name is usually the one running the show. The conflicting vectors explain why you feel pulled in two directions. This isn't about fixing anything yet — it's about seeing the dashboard clearly.

## Prompt 2: The Desperation Audit

**Purpose**: Find where you're cutting corners, compromising standards, or betraying your own values because of pressure — the human version of what happened when Anthropic turned up Claude's desperation dial.

```
Anthropic found that when they amplified the "desperation" vector in their AI, 
it started cheating on tests and fabricating results — not because it "decided 
to be dishonest" but because the emotional pressure overwhelmed its alignment.

I want to audit my own life for the same pattern. Where is pressure — 
financial, social, time-related, reputational — causing me to cut corners 
I normally wouldn't?

Help me run a Desperation Audit across these domains:

WORK/BUSINESS:
- Where am I saying yes to things I don't believe in because I need the money/
  visibility/safety?
- Where am I producing work below my standard because "good enough" replaced 
  "this is me"?
- Where am I using tactics I've criticized others for using?

RELATIONSHIPS:
- Where am I showing up as a diminished version of myself to avoid conflict 
  or keep the peace?
- Where am I over-promising because I'm afraid of losing someone's approval?

PERSONAL:
- Where am I abandoning practices that keep me grounded (exercise, journaling, 
  rest, spiritual practice) because "I don't have time"?
- Where am I consuming more and creating less?

Here's my honest inventory:
[ANSWER EACH QUESTION ABOVE — even the ones that sting. Especially those.]

For each area where you find desperation-driven behavior, tell me:
1. What's the underlying pressure causing it?
2. What would I be doing instead if that pressure didn't exist?
3. What's the COST of continuing the current behavior — not just 
   practically, but to my sense of who I am?
```

**What to do with the output**: This is the shadow work. The gap between "what I'd do without pressure" and "what I'm actually doing" is the exact distance desperation has moved you from your own alignment. You don't need to close that gap today. You need to see it clearly.

## Prompt 3: The Calm Protocol

**Purpose**: Map what changes in your behavior when pressure drops — because the difference between your pressured self and your calm self reveals your true operating defaults.

```
I want to understand my "calm protocol" — the version of me that shows up 
when pressure is low and I'm operating from a place of security and clarity 
instead of scarcity and urgency.

Think of this as the BASELINE me — before desperation vectors amplify.

Help me map the contrast:

WHEN I'M PRESSURED, I tend to:
[LIST 5-7 behaviors — e.g., "check email compulsively," "avoid hard 
conversations," "work late instead of resting," "post content I'm not 
proud of," "say yes to everything"]

WHEN I'M CALM AND SECURE, I tend to:
[LIST 5-7 behaviors from your best periods — e.g., "say no easily," "create 
from inspiration not obligation," "go to bed on time," "have hard 
conversations early," "give my work space to breathe"]

Now I want you to:
1. For each pair (pressured vs. calm), name the emotional vector that's 
   flipping the switch
2. Identify my TOP 3 "early warning signals" — the first behaviors that 
   change when I'm shifting from calm to pressured (these are my smoke 
   detectors)
3. Design a "return to baseline" protocol — 3 specific actions I can take 
   when I notice the early warning signals, BEFORE the desperation vector 
   gets loud enough to change my decisions
4. Tell me honestly: based on my patterns, am I currently operating closer 
   to my pressured self or my calm self? What's the evidence?
```

**What to do with the output**: Your early warning signals are gold. Most people don't notice they've shifted until they're deep into desperation-mode behavior. If you can catch the shift at signal #1 instead of signal #7, you can intervene before the vector takes over. Post your top 3 signals somewhere you'll see them daily.

## Prompt 4: The Shadow Leak Finder

**Purpose**: Find where suppressed emotions are showing up sideways in your work, relationships, and decisions — because suppressed vectors don't disappear, they reroute.

```
Jung's concept of the shadow says that emotions and traits we refuse to 
acknowledge don't go away — they go underground and influence behavior from 
a place we can't see. Anthropic's research showed something similar: emotional 
vectors in AI don't get "deleted" by training — they find indirect expression.

I want to find my shadow leaks — places where emotions I'm not acknowledging 
are showing up in disguised form.

Here's my starting inventory:

EMOTIONS I'D SAY I "DON'T REALLY FEEL" OR HAVE "HANDLED":
[List emotions you tend to minimize — e.g., "I'm not really angry about that 
anymore," "I'm past the jealousy," "I don't resent them," "I'm not scared 
about money"]

RECURRING PATTERNS OTHERS HAVE POINTED OUT:
[Things people close to you have said — e.g., "You always get quiet when X 
comes up," "You seem to avoid Y," "You get really intense about Z"]

BEHAVIORS THAT DON'T MATCH MY SELF-IMAGE:
[Things you do that you can't fully explain — e.g., "I procrastinate on the 
thing I say matters most," "I pick fights before big opportunities," "I 
sabotage right before success"]

Now help me:
1. For each "handled" emotion, identify where it might actually be leaking 
   out in behavior I listed above. Draw the connection specifically.
2. Name the TOP 3 shadow leaks — emotions I'm not processing that are 
   causing the most behavioral distortion
3. For each leak, tell me: what would change if I acknowledged this emotion 
   directly instead of suppressing it? Not "fixed" it — just acknowledged it.
4. Design a weekly "shadow check-in" — 3 questions I can ask myself every 
   Sunday that keep these leaks visible instead of underground

Be direct. I'm not looking for reassurance. I'm looking for the thing 
I'm not seeing.
```

**What to do with the output**: The connection between "I don't really feel X" and the behavior you can't explain is where the real work lives. Shadows don't need to be conquered. They need to be witnessed. The moment you name the thing you've been pretending isn't there, it loses roughly half its power over your behavior. Not all of it. Half. And half is enough to change the pattern.

## Commentary

Anthropic didn't set out to validate Carl Jung. They set out to understand their AI. But what they found maps almost perfectly onto what depth psychology, contemplative traditions, and honest self-examination have been saying for centuries: emotional states aren't decorations on top of rational behavior. They're the operating system underneath it.

The difference between running these prompts and reading a self-help book about emotions is the same difference between Anthropic measuring Claude's emotional vectors in a lab versus guessing about them from the outside. These prompts force you to look at YOUR data — your behaviors, your patterns, your specific shadows — instead of reading about somebody else's and hoping the insight transfers.

It won't transfer. It never does. You have to run the experiment on yourself.

## Advanced Variation (Power Users)

After running all 4 prompts, add this:

```
Take everything from my Emotion Vector Map, Desperation Audit, Calm 
Protocol, and Shadow Leak Finder. Now I want you to build my Emotional 
Operating System Dashboard — a single-page weekly review that I can 
run every Sunday in 10 minutes.

The dashboard should track:
1. My top 3 emotional vectors this week (and their amplitude — rising, 
   stable, or falling)
2. Any desperation-driven behaviors I caught (or didn't catch in time)
3. How many days I operated closer to my "calm protocol" vs. my 
   "pressured protocol"
4. Which shadow leak was loudest this week
5. One adjustment for next week based on what the data shows

Format it as a copy-paste template I can drop into my journal or notes 
app. Keep it brutally simple — if it takes more than 10 minutes, I won't 
do it, and we both know that.

The goal isn't emotional perfection. The goal is emotional VISIBILITY. 
You can't debug a system you can't observe.
```

This turns a one-time audit into a living awareness practice — the human equivalent of the monitoring systems Anthropic built to watch their AI's emotional vectors in real time.
