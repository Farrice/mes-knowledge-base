# 3 LinkedIn Concepts, Founder-Mirror Method (2026-07-19)

Method: mine the personal-yes founder's private register first (adpsy Pattern 1), veto anything a real founder wouldn't think (lived-experience test), then build I-narrative arcs. Voice: BLEND. These are concept cards; final drafts go through writers-room with a fresh prose check.

## What the founder says privately and posts nowhere

"I wrote our positioning at 1am and nobody has ever pressure-tested it." · "If I say 'clinically dosed' in one more investor meeting I'm going to hear my competitors' deck coming out of my own mouth." · "I know the product works. I felt it work. I just can't prove it in words." · "One FTC letter and we're done, and I honestly don't know if our claims are safe." · "I already paid for the ads before I finished the sentence they were supposed to prove."

Every concept below is one of those lines, said back to them in Farrice's voice.

---

## Concept 1 · The Unfinished Sentence

Hook: **"I watched a brand spend forty grand on creative for a message they hadn't finished writing."**

The scene is an ad account: gorgeous creative, and a positioning line that changed every quarter because nobody ever settled it. From 18 years of coaching, the same law applies to launches as to lifting: you don't add load to a movement you haven't learned. The reader leaves with the reframe that the sentence is the cheapest line item in the launch and the only one that compounds. Close on the image of a founder reading their own homepage out loud.

RECEIPT CHECK before ship: anchor the forty-grand story to the actual client instance Farrice has in mind, or soften to the honestly observed pattern. Killed in veto: every you-framed dictation version ("your launch will fail without..."), and the listicle-opener version of this idea, both banned registers. Expect saves over comments; this is a private-shame category.

## Concept 2 · The Two Languages

Hook: **"'Supports cognitive performance' means nothing to me. I've watched what actually changes when someone sleeps."**

This one runs on Farrice's unfakeable ground: eighteen years coaching bodies through the exact things supplement decks claim, now reading those decks and seeing two languages that never touch. Lab-coat words on one side; what a body does on week three on the other. The guide move is generous, not superior: the founder already owns the second language, they lived it building the product, and the gap is translation, not truth. Bookend close: "I stopped guessing what happens in a body around 2012. Most decks I read are still guessing."

Passes the Practitioner Test cold. Killed in veto: the copywriter-mockery angle (punching down, fails the wince test) and anything reading as a clinical claim about a named product. The replies to farm: founders naming the one thing their product does that no copy has captured. Each of those is a warm lead and a claim-safe rewrite candidate.

## Concept 3 · Nine Homepages

Hook: **"I lined up nine supplement homepages last week. I couldn't tell you which brand was which."**

PRACTITIONER GATE, non-negotiable: run the actual sweep before posting (ten minutes) so the line is literally true and the screenshots exist for DM replies. The post is the sweep as a scene: nine tabs, the same three phrases rotating, a buyer's eyes stopping on none. It points at the founder's most private version of this pain: rehearsing the raise pitch and hearing borrowed language in their own mouth. Differentiation is usually real at the product level and borrowed at the language level; the raise story goes flat in that gap. Image close on the nine identical tabs.

When commenters ask which brands: decline publicly, every named critique stays private (binding teardown rule). Founders DM-ing their own homepage is the designed outcome; the $250-500 Stress-Test Walkthrough is the natural next line in DMs, never in the post. Also killed in veto: "science-backed is dead" (absolute-claim clickbait; the truer line is that it turned into wallpaper).
