## Post 1 — Tuesday (Archetype: Origin Wound)

**Pillar:** The 90% Pattern

---

I was the fastest kid on the track. 

They threw me in the lockers for it.

That lesson wasn't intellectual. My body learned it: being visibly exceptional is dangerous. So I built a system. Get to 90%, stop before the moment anyone sees you at full size. Near-completion is safe. That last 10%—the part where you're *actually* seen—is where the alarm goes off.

I ran that system for decades. Fitness, writing, strategy, AI. Always stopping one breath before the finish.

Then I had a son. And half-presence started feeling like betrayal.

The work right now is shipping the last 10%. On the page. In the open. Still scared.

## Post 2 — Thursday (Archetype: Confession Pivot)

**Pillar:** Presence vs. Screens

---

I have a two-year-old. 

Every piece of work I build happens in his naptime. The moment he wakes up, the MacBook closes. Not metaphorically—it *closes*. Screens off. Dog comes inside. Toys wake up. We're present, or we're not.

I made that rule because I spent the first eighteen months of his life being someone I didn't recognize. Working during wake-time. Thinking about content while he needed me. Teaching him that half-presence from someone who claims to love him is normal.

It's not.

So I cut the compromise. Work window: ninety minutes, once a day, when he sleeps. That's the boundary. Everything else—all of it—happens in the presence it deserves.

People ask how I'm this productive in naptimes. The answer: I'm not doing anything else.

## Post 3 — Friday (Archetype: Image That Names It)

**Pillar:** Cross-Domain Synthesis

---

Every person told me to pick one lane. 

Psychology or fitness. AI or writing. Strategy or fatherhood. You get to be good at one thing, the world said. More than one is unfocused. More than one is a problem.

But the best ideas I've ever had? They lived at the seam.

Where fitness psychology meets AI systems. Where fatherhood reframes presence as the highest leverage. Where Neville Goddard's imagination-as-cause connects to how habit formation actually works.

The seam is where you can see things nobody else in a single lane can reach.

Most people narrow because the world told them to. I stopped apologizing for the range because I realized: that's not fragmentation. That's the whole point.

## Post 4 — Monday (Archetype: Asset Drop)

**Pillar:** Transformation Psychology

---

Everyone says they want to change.

Almost nobody does.

This gap—between *saying* and *doing*—is where I've spent eighteen years watching real humans. Not theories. Not what they claim on the internet. Actual behavior, actual barriers, actual why.

And the thing is: it's not motivation. 

It's not inspiration. It's not the right program. It's not even *knowing* what to do. I've watched thousands of people who knew *exactly* what to do and still didn't do it.

The gap lives somewhere else. In identity. In the stories they tell about who's allowed to change. In what it costs them—socially, psychologically—to stop fitting the pattern.

That's the work. Not the knowing. The becoming.

Most advice skips right over this. Tells you the system, the steps, the hack. Ignores the part where you have to stop being who you've been.

That's the hard part. Everything else is a detail.

## Post 5 — Wednesday (Archetype: Philosophy)

**Pillar:** Reader-as-Protagonist

---

Most writing is about the writer.

Here's what I mean: the author puts themselves *in front* of the story. They're the hero, the expert, the one with answers. The reader is the audience. You're invited to learn from them. Consume their insight. Become more like them.

That backwards.

The reader is the protagonist. They're the one living the story. They're the one with the real problem. The thing I do is enter as the answer to a question they can *already* feel—not as someone imposing a new question they didn't ask.

When I write, I'm not teaching. I'm recognizing.

"Wait, you see that too?" "So you've felt this exact thing but couldn't name it." That recognition is the moment something shifts.

Most people won't do this because it requires reading your audience. *Actually* reading them. Understanding what they live in daily. Speaking their private language, even when it sounds weird to anyone outside.

It's harder than writing about yourself.

But it lands different.
