# Angle Map: claim-safe creative direction read

## The offer verdict

Productize this capability as the **creative-direction edition of the existing Angle Map**, not a new audit or a new economic spine.

The paid job is narrow and consequential: a funded supplement or performance brand has real proof, active makers, and no campaign argument strong enough to coordinate them. Farrice reads the evidence, buyer tension, and category creative, then chooses what the team should make first.

## Buyer

Best fit:

- founder, head of brand, or creative lead at a funded supplement/performance company;
- real product, documented substantiation, and a named legal/regulatory claims owner;
- an active launch, control, or dated commercial occasion;
- internal or external makers already available;
- frustration with safe sameness, disconnected briefs, or claims that lose their force in creative.

Poor fit:

- pre-product founders seeking a category idea;
- brands asking Farrice to invent efficacy claims;
- teams wanting ad-account management, a full rebrand, or unlimited production;
- anyone treating an editorial screen as legal approval.

## Promise

**In 48 hours, your team gets three campaign directions grounded in the proof you can use, the buyer tension you need to settle, and the category codes worth breaking. You leave with one recommended direction and the first asset to build.**

## What the buyer receives

1. **Evidence and claim boundary**: the supplied proof, approved claim set, forbidden territory, and open substantiation questions.
2. **Live 60-minute read**: Farrice reads the product, current creative, category, and skeptical-buyer break with the decision makers present.
3. **Three named campaign directions**: each contains an argument, world sentence, proof object, visual grammar, first three assets, and clear risk.
4. **One decision**: which direction to build first and why the other two wait.
5. **Executor handoff**: a concise brief the existing designer, editor, or agency can start without another strategy meeting.
6. **Thirty-minute handoff call**: questions with the executor, within seven days of delivery.

## Proof gates

### Gate 1: Evidence supplied

Every factual product claim maps to client-supplied substantiation or an approved claim source. Missing evidence is marked `UNCONFIRMED`; the direction does not depend on it.

### Gate 2: Claim boundary understood

The client names the legal/regulatory owner. Farrice's screen is described as editorial judgment. No sentence implies approval by counsel, the FTC, the FDA, Meta, or another platform.

### Gate 3: Directions differ materially

Each route changes the campaign argument, proof object, visual world, and first asset. Palette swaps fail.

### Gate 4: One decision survives

The final document names one recommended route. Equal-weight option decks fail.

### Gate 5: Executor can begin

The named maker can state the first asset, source material, no-go territory, review owner, and stop condition without another strategy session.

### Gate 6: Proof states remain honest

- delivery of the read and handoff: implementation proof;
- team clarity and reduced rework: client-reported evidence until measured;
- creative performance, conversion, and revenue: `UNTESTED` before launch;
- three paid buyers, one credit into Sprint, and collected revenue: initial market proof.

## Capacity and risk controls

- Cap at two reads per week until real production time exists.
- One decision maker consolidates feedback.
- The 48-hour clock begins after the full input pack arrives.
- One clarification round covers factual errors or missed supplied evidence, not a new strategy.
- Personal-brand examples stay separate from confidential client material.
- No public teardown, logo use, testimonial, or case study without written permission.
