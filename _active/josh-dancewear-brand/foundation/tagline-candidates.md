# Subline — Tagline Candidates

**Date**: 2026-05-01 · **Phase**: Foundation 3 of 3
**For tonight's poster**: one selected line below.

---

## Risk to surface upfront

**Josh's working tagline — "Don't let them see you sweat" — is a near-direct echo of Dry Idea's iconic 1980s campaign "Never let them see you sweat."** That line ran for years across antiperspirant advertising and is still cited as one of the most-remembered slogans of its era. Using it (or this close variant) for an apparel brand carries two risks:

- **TM/legal**: Verify status before any external use. The phrase has been licensed and re-used across categories. Even where TM is technically clear, the cultural echo is loud enough that press and dance media will read it as derivative.
- **Brand cost**: A tagline that sounds borrowed undercuts the entire "engineered by dancers, taken seriously" positioning. Subline can't afford to launch on a line that another brand made famous in deodorant.

Recommendation: hold the line as an internal north-star reference; ship one of the alternates below as the public tagline.

---

## The 7 Candidates

### 1. *Don't let them see you sweat.* (Josh's original)
- **Register**: Direct, declarative, audience-facing.
- **Why it works**: Names the central anxiety in the dancer's voice. Punchy. Memorable.
- **Why it doesn't**: Dry Idea echo, full stop. Reads as borrowed.
- **Best application**: Internal brief language only. Not for public use.
- **TM-instinct flag**: ⚠️ Strong cultural-association risk regardless of legal status.

### 2. *The line beneath the line.*
- **Register**: Control / engineering claim.
- **Why it works**: Native vocabulary (*line* is the dancer's word). Names the product's exact role. Could carry the entire brand by itself — wordmark + tagline lock together.
- **Best application**: Master tagline, packaging, hangtag, video close-card.
- **TM-instinct flag**: Clean.

### 3. *Composure is engineered.*
- **Register**: Control / mastery.
- **Why it works**: Reframes the resistance. The dancer thinks composure is a personal trait she should already have; the brand says it's infrastructure. Tracksmith-register without imitation.
- **Best application**: Manifesto poster. Brand film. Out-of-home.
- **TM-instinct flag**: Clean. Generic phrase, but combination is brand-able.

### 4. *Sweat in the studio. Stage-clean on the stage.*
- **Register**: Reframe the work.
- **Why it works**: Splits the "real dancers sweat" belief without fighting it. Honors the grind in the first half, gives permission for a different rule in the second. Pulled directly from the bridge-message hypothesis in the ICP profile.
- **Best application**: Social ads, TikTok hook frames, dancer-evangelism posts. Top-of-funnel.
- **TM-instinct flag**: Clean.

### 5. *The work is yours. The proof isn't ours to leave behind.*
- **Register**: Reframe the work.
- **Why it works**: Inverts the "work showing" pride. The dancer's work belongs to her — her training, her discipline, her preparation. The sweat-evidence is the brand's job to handle, so the work itself stays hers. Slower, more declarative.
- **Best application**: Long-form / editorial. About page. Founder note.
- **TM-instinct flag**: Clean.

### 6. *Engineered for the eight-show week.*
- **Register**: Engineering claim.
- **Why it works**: Inside-baseball specificity (Broadway language) reads as authority. A Tracksmith-tier line. Proves the brand knows the use case rather than telling.
- **Best application**: Product page, packaging, men's/Broadway-segment ads. Best on category lockups.
- **TM-instinct flag**: Clean.

### 7. *The audience sees the art. We work in the line beneath it.*
- **Register**: Stand line (manifesto closer).
- **Why it works**: Closes the manifesto and stands alone as a tagline. Names the brand's role in the dancer's relationship with her audience. Two beats — the audience's experience, then the brand's work — and the second beat lands.
- **Best application**: Brand film closer, hero-page H2, founder letter sign-off.
- **TM-instinct flag**: Clean.

## Ranked recommendation

### 🥇 *The line beneath the line.* — master tagline
This is the one. It locks with the brand name (SUBLINE = sub + line = the line beneath the line). The wordmark and the tagline are the same thought, said twice with different cadence. Native dancer vocabulary. Industrial-mono typesettable. It works on a hangtag, a poster, a closing frame, a tagline-only billboard.

### 🥈 *Sweat in the studio. Stage-clean on the stage.* — campaign / social workhorse
The line that does the educational work. Reframes the resistance, splits the belief, opens the door for the buyer who was previously pre-contemplation. This is the line that runs on the first 30 dancer-evangelist posts, the audition-week ads, the pre-tour-fitting targeting.

### 🥉 *Engineered for the eight-show week.* — proof tagline for product / category lockups
The credentialing line. Sits under the master tagline on PDPs, hangtags, and Broadway-segment marketing. Tells the working dancer the brand knows the actual job.
