## 1. BRAND ESSENCE

### Brand thesis (≤20 words)
**SUBLINE makes performance undergarments for working dancers, so the audience sees the art instead of the labor.**

### Three brand truths

1. **A dancer is an athlete trained to look like she isn't one.** The product exists to protect that illusion. Every design decision should make the labor harder to see, not easier.
2. **The line is the dancer's vocabulary.** Not "shape," not "silhouette," not "form" — *line.* The brand's name, tagline, and product architecture are all built on this single word, because it's the word she already uses about her own body.
3. **The dancer is the authority — not the founder, not the lab.** Proof comes through working dancers using the product on tour, in the show, on the playback. Founder myth supports; never leads. The visual system reflects this: bodies are the heroes, faces are secondary, the founder never appears alone in hero photography.

### Visual register
**Performance-Tech Editorial.** Industrial mono typography on chalk and bone, hard-light photography of working bodies in backstage architecture, a single acid accent (Spotlight Amber) used the way a runway light is aimed — once, on purpose. The register sits at the intersection of Tracksmith (editorial restraint, dignity-of-craft), Apolla (dance-credentialed technical authority), Under Armour HeatGear-era (product-line architecture as positioning), and Aimé Leon Dore (typography-light, image-confident). It is a brand that looks like it was engineered backstage, not designed in a wellness studio.

### What this brand does NOT look like (anti-register, named explicitly)
SUBLINE is not Lululemon. SUBLINE is not Alo Yoga. SUBLINE is not Beyond Yoga, Vuori, Free People Movement, Athleta, Gymshark, FP Movement, Outdoor Voices, Sweaty Betty, or any brand whose homepage uses the words *boldly, ritual, intention, balance, self-care, empower, unapologetic, glow, journey, mindful,* or *feel good in your body.* It is not a wellness brand. It is not athleisure. It is not lifestyle. It is gear for a working professional whose paycheck depends on the costume not betraying her, and the visual system has to pass that test on every surface.

## 2. COLOR SYSTEM

### Source / inspiration
Pulled from **stage architecture**, not from a palette generator. True black is the proscenium when the house lights drop. Bone is the inside of a brand-new dance shoe before it's worn in. Graphite is the painted floor of every studio in LA and NYC. Spotlight Amber is the gel color of a single follow-spot on a soloist when the stage goes black around her — the moment the audience stops seeing the dancer and starts seeing the line. The palette refuses pastels because dancers wince at them; it refuses warm earth tones because they read as wellness; it refuses navy because every athletic challenger brand from 2014 already used it.

### Primary palette

#### Stage Black — `#0A0A0B`
- **RGB**: `10, 10, 11`
- **CMYK**: `0, 0, 0, 96`
- **Role**: Primary background for editorial surfaces, packaging exteriors, video close-cards, the proscenium of the brand. Never `#000000` pure — that reads digital. `#0A0A0B` reads inked.
- **Mood**: Curtain down. Lights cued. The breath before the first count.
- **When to use**: Hero backgrounds, hangtag base, packaging boxes, video closers, social grid type-only tiles, web hover states for primary CTAs.

#### Bone — `#F2EFE8`
- **RGB**: `242, 239, 232`
- **CMYK**: `0, 1, 4, 5`
- **Role**: Primary light surface. Replaces white everywhere. Reads as raw cotton, dance-shoe leather, marley floor before it's swept.
- **Mood**: Studio at 7am before class. Quiet. Pre-warm-up.
- **When to use**: Web body backgrounds, packaging interiors (the reveal when the box opens), hangtag reverse, editorial caption blocks, garment label fabric color reference.

#### Chalk — `#E5E2D9`
- **RGB**: `229, 226, 217`
- **CMYK**: `0, 1, 5, 10`
- **Role**: Secondary surface, one tone deeper than Bone. Used to differentiate cards, sections, and product blocks without breaking the monochrome.
- **Mood**: The dust on a rosin box. The matte on an old marley.
- **When to use**: Section dividers, card backgrounds on Bone pages, secondary buttons, product-grid alternating rows, packaging tissue paper.

#### Graphite — `#2A2A2C`
- **RGB**: `42, 42, 44`
- **CMYK**: `5, 5, 0, 83`
- **Role**: Body text on Bone, secondary surface on Stage Black, the working-color of every non-hero piece of type.
- **Mood**: Studio floor under stage light. Not black, not gray — graphite, the way the word means it.
- **When to use**: All body copy on light surfaces, subhead type on Stage Black, hangtag spec text, product PDP body, technical labeling.

#### Steel — `#6E6E72`
- **RGB**: `110, 110, 114`
- **CMYK**: `4, 3, 0, 55`
- **Role**: Tertiary text. Metadata, timestamps, micro-copy, fabric composition lines, batch numbers.
- **Mood**: Backstage hallway concrete.
- **When to use**: Captions, metadata, footer copy, technical sub-labels, the "dancer-tested-by" stamp.

### Accent — Spotlight Amber — `#F2A33C`

**The pick. Decision rationale below.**

- **RGB**: `242, 163, 60`
- **CMYK**: `0, 33, 75, 5`
- **Role**: Single acid accent. Appears once per surface, never twice. The color of the gel on a follow-spot — the moment the audience's eye is told where to look.
- **Mood**: Cue. Mark. The light hits.

**Why amber over the alternatives:**
- **Vs. safety orange (Apolla-adjacent)**: Safety orange (`#FF6900`) is owned by Apolla in the dancer's eye. Borrowing it concedes the precedent rather than extending it. SUBLINE is not Apolla for the body if it dresses like Apolla. Amber sits one drawer over — adjacent enough that the dance world reads it as credentialed-technical, distinct enough that it doesn't compete on Apolla's literal palette.
- **Vs. surgical blue**: Reads cold-medical. SUBLINE is biomechanical, not clinical. Blue makes the brand feel like it was made in a lab; amber makes it feel like it was made in a theater.
- **For amber specifically**: Stage lighting is the dancer's working environment. Spotlight Amber is the color of every theatrical follow-spot, every backstage tungsten work-light, every dressing-room mirror bulb. It is the color the dancer's body is photographed in for half her career. The brand is using a color she already lives inside.

**Usage discipline**: Amber appears on no more than **10% of any surface area**. Never as a background. Never on body copy. Never paired with any second accent. It is the cue color: hover state, single hangtag stripe, one rule beneath the wordmark on a hero poster, the underscore on the campaign workhorse tagline, the subscribe button on a single email. If it appears twice on the same screen, one of those two appearances is wrong.

### Forbidden colors

- All pastels (rose, sage, lavender, blush, mint, dusty blue, nude pink). Wellness signal.
- Earth tones (terracotta, mustard, ochre, olive). Lifestyle signal.
- Navy. Athletic-challenger cliché — every brand from 2014.
- Pure white (`#FFFFFF`). Reads as default web template; use Bone.
- Pure black (`#000000`). Reads as digital placeholder; use Stage Black.
- Any color named after a feeling (`feel-good blue`, `confidence coral`). Wince guaranteed.
- Gradient anything. The brand is single-source-light. Gradients are stage washes; SUBLINE is the follow-spot.

### Photo color treatment

- **Black-and-white default** for editorial, founder, and process imagery. High-contrast film stock reference: Kodak Tri-X 400 pushed one stop, or digital equivalent — `+30 contrast, +10 clarity, 0 saturation, deep blacks crushed to 5/255, highlights held at 240/255`.
- **Color treatment**, when used for product or campaign hero: graded toward the brand palette only. Skin tones held neutral; backgrounds drift toward Graphite or Stage Black; any incidental color in frame (a costume, a wall) desaturated by 40-60% in post.
- **Never warm-and-soft.** No Instagram-warm preset. No golden-hour glow. No magenta lift in the shadows.
- **Never washed.** No hazy / dreamy / film-grain-bloomed lifestyle treatment. The brand looks at the body the way a costume head looks at the body — clinically, with respect.
- **Hard light only.** Single-source key, no fill or 1:8 fill ratio maximum. Shadows are part of the composition, not a flaw to soften.

## 3. TYPOGRAPHY

### Display typeface — **GT Pressura Mono** (Grilli Type)

**Pick rationale**: GT Pressura Mono is the industrial-mono with the most theatrical bone structure in the licensable tier. Designed by Marc Kappeler and Dominik Huber at Grilli Type, it pulls from the visual language of price-tag printers and shipping-label stencils — exactly the register of a backstage prop room, a costume tag, a wardrobe rack ID. The letterforms are confident at large display sizes (96px+) without feeling decorative; they hold technical authority at micro sizes (10px) without feeling generic. The default uppercase setting reads as carved, not typeset.

- **Licensing tier**: Trial license $19; full desktop + web $390 for the family. Affordable for Year 1; scales to 5+ seats without category-jump in cost.
- **Fallback stack** (when GT Pressura Mono not loaded): `"GT Pressura Mono", "JetBrains Mono", "Berkeley Mono", "IBM Plex Mono", ui-monospace, "SF Mono", Menlo, monospace`
- **Alternate consideration**: JetBrains Mono Variable is the free fallback if budget blocks GT Pressura. Loses ~15% of the editorial-weight quality but holds the register.

### Body typeface — **Söhne** (Klim Type Foundry)

**Pick rationale**: Söhne is the technical neo-grotesque of this decade — Klim Type Foundry's reinterpretation of Akzidenz-Grotesk for the screen. It has the engineered precision of Helvetica without Helvetica's banality, and the warmth of Inter without Inter's defaultness. Söhne sets perfectly at 14-18px on web, holds at 60+ for editorial, and carries the technical-document register the brand needs for spec copy, packaging, and PDP body. It is the typeface of NYT's product surfaces, MoMA's web type, and most premium-restraint brands shipped after 2020.

- **Licensing tier**: Mid (~$300/style desktop, ~$50/month commercial web). Subline ships on Söhne Buch (regular), Söhne Kräftig (medium), and Söhne Halbfett (semibold). Three weights, no italic, no light, no black.
- **Fallback stack**: `"Söhne", "Inter", -apple-system, "Helvetica Neue", "Aktiv Grotesk", system-ui, sans-serif`
- **Alternate consideration**: Inter Variable is the free fallback. Loses ~10% of the editorial calibration but is acceptable for Year 1 if Söhne licensing is deferred.

### Wordmark — SUBLINE

The wordmark is **GT Pressura Mono Regular, all caps, +120 letter-spacing units (0.12em), set on a baseline with a single 1px Graphite rule beneath**. The rule is the visualization of the tagline — *the line beneath the line.* The rule extends 0.5em to the right of the final E and 0.5em to the left of the S, anchoring the word in its own underline.

```
S U B L I N E
─────────────
```

- **Letter-spacing**: 0.12em. Wider than default mono; the word reads as inscribed, not typed.
- **Weight**: Regular only. No bold, no light, no italic variants of the wordmark. Ever.
- **Case**: All caps. Lowercase variant does not exist.
- **Rule width**: Exactly 1px at all sizes ≤32px wordmark height. Scales to 2px at 33-72px, 3px at 73px+. The rule never thickens beyond 3px.
- **Rule color**: Graphite (`#2A2A2C`) on Bone surfaces. Bone (`#F2EFE8`) on Stage Black surfaces. Never amber. Never broken or dashed.

### Anti-type rules

1. **No italics for emphasis, ever.** The brand emphasizes through type *change* (mono ↔ neo-grotesque) or weight (Buch → Halbfett). Italics read as soft.
2. **No double-weight headlines.** A headline is one weight. If a phrase needs to be split visually, use color (Graphite ↔ Steel) or rule division — not weight contrast.
3. **No all-caps body copy.** Mono body in Söhne sentence case only. All-caps is reserved for display, micro labels, and the wordmark.
4. **No display script, no serif of any kind, no rounded sans, no humanist sans (Avenir, Gotham, Proxima Nova family).** Two typefaces only: GT Pressura Mono and Söhne. The system enforces itself by exclusion.
5. **No underline for emphasis on body copy.** The 1px rule is the wordmark's mark. Underlining elsewhere dilutes it. Use color or a 1px Graphite divider above/below the line instead.
6. **No drop caps. No pull quotes set in italic. No type-on-photo without a Graphite scrim at 60% opacity minimum behind the type.**

### Tagline lockup

The master tagline (*The line beneath the line.*) sets to the right of the SUBLINE wordmark on horizontal applications, and below it on stacked applications.

**Horizontal lockup** (hangtag fronts, hero posters, packaging side panels):
```
S U B L I N E   |   THE LINE BENEATH THE LINE.
─────────────
```
- Vertical pipe divider in Graphite, 1px width, full height of wordmark cap-height.
- Tagline set in GT Pressura Mono Regular, 14px / 0.08em letter-spacing, all caps, color matched to wordmark (Graphite on Bone, Bone on Stage Black).

**Stacked lockup** (vertical packaging, social tiles, video closers):
```
S U B L I N E
─────────────
THE LINE BENEATH THE LINE.
```
- Tagline below the rule, 0.5em gap. Same type spec as horizontal.

**Negative-space lockup** (the largest applications — billboards, brand films, hero posters where the wordmark commands the entire frame):
- Wordmark only. No tagline. The empty space below the rule is the tagline.

## 4. LOGO / WORDMARK SYSTEM

### Primary lockup
The wordmark + 1px rule combination above. This is the canonical mark and appears on every primary brand surface. Min size: 80px wide horizontal, 32px tall.

### Stacked variant
Used on vertical surfaces (banner ads, app icons larger than monogram size, packaging side panels, hangtag verso). Tagline stacked below the rule per the lockup spec above.

### Monogram — `SL`
Used for favicon, app icon, social profile picture, hangtag corner stamp, garment care-label brand mark.

- **Specification**: Two characters, `S` and `L`, GT Pressura Mono Regular, all caps, 0 letter-spacing (tight). The two letters share a single 1px rule beneath, extending 0.25em to either side of the SL pair. The rule beneath SL is the contracted version of the rule beneath SUBLINE — same idea, smaller scale.
- **Min size**: 16px square (favicon).
- **Color treatment same as full wordmark**: Graphite on Bone, Bone on Stage Black.

### Subline / Subscript element — the line itself
The 1px rule is the brand's load-bearing graphic asset, separate from the wordmark. Specs:

- A horizontal Graphite rule, 1px–3px depending on context, used as a section divider, a hangtag separator, a campaign poster mid-line, an Instagram grid divider, a video transition mark.
- The rule is the only allowable graphic element besides type and photography. There is no second graphic family. No icon set beyond what is necessary for cart, search, account, and menu functions; no decorative pattern; no background texture.
- The rule visualizes the brand thesis. Every use is doing work.

### Minimum sizes

| Application | Min wordmark width |
|-------------|---------------------|
| Web header | 100px |
| Print (hangtag, packaging) | 24mm |
| Embroidery on garment | 22mm |
| Favicon (monogram only) | 16px |
| App icon (monogram only) | 60px @1x |

### Clear space

Clear space around the wordmark is equal to the cap-height of the wordmark itself, on all four sides. Nothing — type, photo, edge of surface — encroaches.

### Forbidden uses

1. Do not stretch the wordmark vertically or horizontally. Mono spacing must remain mono.
2. Do not outline the wordmark. Solid fill only.
3. Do not place the wordmark on a busy photograph without a 60%-opacity Graphite scrim or full Stage Black panel behind it.
4. Do not drop-shadow the wordmark.
5. Do not animate the wordmark with bouncy easing. Linear easing only, ≤300ms duration.
6. Do not pair the wordmark with any logo lockup other than approved partner co-brands (which do not exist yet).
7. Do not color the wordmark anything other than Graphite, Bone, Stage Black, or — once, in the entire brand history — Spotlight Amber, reserved for a single launch poster or a single founding-edition product. Treat that ration as inviolable.
8. Do not break the rule beneath the wordmark. The rule is solid, continuous, single-stroke, every time.

## 5. PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

### Lighting

- **Single-source key**, hard quality. Either a 1k tungsten Fresnel ($150 rental, replicates backstage tungsten work-light) or a strobe with a 7" reflector and 20° grid (no soft-box, no umbrella, no scrim).
- **Fill ratio**: 1:8 maximum. Most frames run 1:16 or naked single-source. Shadows are part of the composition.
- **Direction**: Side or 45°-overhead. Never flat-front. Never under-lit. Stage lighting reference: a follow-spot from the lighting booth, not a soft-box from a beauty campaign.
- **Color temperature**: Tungsten warm (3200K) for backstage / dressing-room narrative; daylight (5600K) for studio / rehearsal narrative. Never mixed in a single frame.
- **Background**: Stage Black void, Graphite-painted seamless, or in-situ backstage architecture (concrete hallway, prop room, dressing-room mirror with the bulbs visible). Never a beach. Never a window with curtain light. Never a rooftop.

### Composition

- **Bodies as biomechanical study.** The body is the subject. The face is a secondary or absent element. Composition treats the dancer the way a sports-medicine textbook treats an athlete — full extension, mid-correction, stress-pose, the moment between two counts.
- **Wide framing.** Negative space is generous. The body occupies 30-50% of the frame; the architecture or void carries the rest. The composition makes room for the line to be read.
- **Asymmetry over center.** Subjects placed on rule-of-thirds intersections, never dead center. Stage Black brand frames are the exception — there, the body anchors center and the surrounding void becomes the proscenium.
- **Mid-rep moments.** The catch, the recovery, the held position, the breath between phrases — not the climax. SUBLINE photographs the labor the audience never sees.

### Casting and body language

- **Real working dancers, sourced through MSA, Bloc, McDonald Selznick, and direct outreach to commercial / Broadway / drag / contemporary segments per the ICP profile.** No models who don't dance. The casting director must be a dance-industry insider; the legal release pack must include union compliance for SAG-AFTRA, AGMA (ballet/opera), and Equity (Broadway) backgrounds.
- **Body language**: power poses, contemplative poses, mid-correction poses. Ready position, fourth position, off-balance recovery, lift catch. Never "pretty" poses. Never group-joy shots. Never running-toward-camera-laughing.
- **Face**: secondary. Most hero frames hide it (back of head, profile, body-only, hair across face). When the face is present, it is neutral or focused — never smiling at the camera, never gazing soulfully. The dancer is at work, not modeling.
- **Diverse representation**: across commercial / company / drag / hip-hop / Broadway / ballet sub-segments, across body types within professional dance norms, across racial and gender presentations. Maya (the Buyer-First Avatar from the ICP profile) is one face among twelve in the launch campaign, not the only face.

### Subject matter — what to shoot

- The body in motion or in held tension.
- The negative space the costume creates around the body.
- Backstage hallways, dressing-room mirrors with bulbs visible, the studio mirror in mid-class, the rosin tray, the wardrobe rack, the costume tag.
- The garment itself in close-detail studio: stitch line, fabric weave, seam tape, technical label.
- The dancer mid-correction: a hand adjusting the costume, a hair tie being snapped, the moment before the curtain.
- The empty stage from the wing.

### Subject matter — what NOT to shoot

- The stage during performance with audience visible. (That is where the ad-banner brands live.)
- The Sunday-morning lifestyle moment (coffee, journal, sunrise, white linen).
- Group-joy / squad shots / friend-circle laughter.
- Yoga poses. Any pose.
- Beach, pool, rooftop, café, brunch, "in the wild" lifestyle.
- Founder portraits in hero photography. The founder appears in editorial founder-letter slots only, not on hero or campaign surfaces.

### Color treatment

- **Default**: black-and-white at the contrast spec in §2 (Photo color treatment).
- **Color when used**: graded toward the palette. Skin tones held neutral, backgrounds drift Graphite, incidental color desaturated 40-60%.
- **Banned**: warm Instagram presets, magenta-shadow lifts, golden-hour oversaturation, "moody blue" tints, film-grain bloom, soft-focus haze.

### Reference photo briefs (the campaign team can shoot from these)

**1. The Wing.** Wide horizontal frame. Tungsten work-light from above-left, hard, casting a cleaner-than-feature-film shadow on the painted black floor. We see her costume, her shoulder, her profile, half her face. ## 6. VOICE / VERBAL IDENTITY

### Core voice rules (from `positioning-manifesto.md`, refined)

1. **The reader is the working dancer. Always.** Second person. Her vocabulary, her stakes, her bag. Never "the modern woman," "the athlete," "she" in third person. Specific or it's nothing.
2. **No wellness vocabulary.** Banned, in full: *self-care, intention, ritual, balance, empower, empowering, boldly, unapologetic, feel good in your body, journey, glow, mindful, holistic, aligned, in tune, your truth, badass, fierce.* When in doubt, check the wince list in `icp-profile.md` §Language Map.
3. **No athletic-marketing slop.** Banned: *athleisure, activewear, performance enhancement, move freely, unleash, unstoppable, conquer, push limits.*
4. **Sentences earn their length.** Short to mid is the spine. Long sentences only when the sentence is doing technical or rhythmic work. No two-clause aphorisms hung at paragraph endings. No "It's not X. It's Y." reveals. No triple-beat anaphora ("for the dancer who shows up. For the dancer who delivers. For the dancer who never breaks.").
5. **Show the work, don't claim the work.** Not "engineered for elite performance." Show: "Tested at minute 280 of an eight-show week." Specifics carry credibility. Every claim has a measurable referent or it doesn't ship.
6. **Talk about competitors obliquely, never by name.** "The catalog tier." "Athletic incumbents." "The 1985 fabric." Never Capezio, Bloch, Lululemon, Nike by name. Naming is acknowledgement; SUBLINE positions itself outside the conversation rather than within it.
7. **The dancer is the authority.** Not the founder, not the lab. Proof comes through working dancers using the product on tour, in the show, on the playback. Founder myth supports, never leads.

### Surface-specific micro-rules

8. **Hangtag copy never exceeds 12 words on the front, 60 on the back.** The garment's silence is part of its quality. Anything more reads as overclaiming.
9. **Product names always pair with line architecture.** Every SKU is a *line*: **STAGE LINE** (performance hero, the eight-show garment), **RUN LINE** (rehearsal/training, durability-graded), **EIGHT LINE** (men's / dance-belt category, Broadway-segment), **TOUR LINE** (travel-case kit), **STUDIO LINE** (entry-tier, the audition-week garment). Product names are typeset in GT Pressura Mono, all caps, never in Söhne.
10. **Numbers carry the weight of claims.** "Eight shows a week." "Minute 280." "Four-week tour test." "Forty-percent compression hold at the seam." Specific numerals beat adjectives every time. No "ultra-." No "pro-." No "max."
11. **No exclamation marks. Anywhere. Ever.** Including marketing emails, including launch announcements. The brand does not get excited.
12. **Em-dash budget: maximum 1 per piece of copy under 200 words; maximum 2 per piece under 500.** This is the AI-tells defense from MEMORY.md.

## 7. MOTION / INTERACTION

### Cadence rules

1. **Deliberate, mechanical, frame-perfect.** Reference: industrial machinery, theatrical stage cues, a metronome. Anti-reference: bouncy social-media motion, spring physics, anthropomorphic UI.
2. **Linear easing default**, not ease-in-out. Use cubic-bezier `(0.4, 0.0, 0.2, 1)` (Material's "standard easing") only for content reveals; everything else is linear or cubic-bezier `(0.65, 0.0, 0.35, 1)` (precise, mechanical).
3. **Duration cap: 300ms.** Hover states 150ms, page transitions 250ms, modal entry 200ms. Nothing animates longer than 300ms. The brand does not wait for motion to finish.
4. **Never bouncy. Never springy. No friendly-anthropomorphic motion ever.** No bounce-in, no rubber-band, no tilt-on-hover, no scale-pop on click. Buttons fade or shift in opacity; they do not jump.
5. **Motion appears once per scroll viewport maximum.** A page where eight elements animate as you scroll reads as a wellness brand. SUBLINE animates one element per fold, or none.

### Web / UI feel

- **Negative-space ratio**: 60% empty, 40% content on hero and editorial sections. PDP and product-grid pages relax to 50/50.
- **Scroll cadence**: long, deliberate, single-column primary content with sectioning by 1px Graphite rules. No parallax. No sticky scroll-jacking.
- **Hover states**: opacity shift from 100% to 70% on type links; underline appears as a 1px Graphite rule beneath, mirroring the wordmark's mark. Spotlight Amber hover only on the primary CTA per page.
- **Buttons**: industrial. Rectangular, 6px corner radius (matches Linear's spec for restraint), 1px Graphite border on Bone, 1px Bone border on Stage Black, no fill on default state. Hover: fill becomes 100% Graphite on Bone (or Bone on Stage Black) with text inverted. Primary CTA: Stage Black fill, Bone text on Bone background; or Spotlight Amber fill, Stage Black text — once per page only.
- **No pill-shaped buttons. No floating action buttons. No "ripple" on tap.**

### Video feel

- **Edit cadence**: long takes, hard cuts. No transitions. No dissolves. No motion-blur transitions. The brand film cuts on the count or on the breath.
- **Sound design**: silence + ambient room tone is the default. When music is used, it is a single instrument (a piano, a single string, a metronome) — never a track, never a needle-drop song. The dancer's breath, the floor, the costume against the body, the stage cue. *Anti-reference*: any "inspirational" music bed.
- **Closer**: every video ends on the master tagline (*The line beneath the line.*), set in GT Pressura Mono on Stage Black, held for 1.5 seconds, no music under. The wordmark and the rule appear, hold, fade — in 250ms.
- **No talking-head founder footage as primary content.** Founder appears in voice-over only, or in a 3-5 second cutaway in the brand film.

## 9. WHAT THIS BRAND IS NOT

This section exists to protect future contributors from drift. When a designer, copywriter, photographer, or product manager joins SUBLINE, they read this section first.

**SUBLINE is not Lululemon.** It is not a wellness brand. It does not sell "feeling good in your body." It sells the layer that disappears under the costume so the body can do its job.

**SUBLINE is not Alo Yoga, Beyond Yoga, FP Movement, Vuori, Gymshark, Outdoor Voices, Sweaty Betty, or Athleta.** These brands sell lifestyle as the product. SUBLINE sells gear for a working professional. The aesthetic distance is on purpose.

**SUBLINE is not athleisure.** The garments do not exist to be worn to brunch. They are not loungewear. They are equipment.

**SUBLINE is not a "performance enhancement" brand.** It does not promise to make the dancer faster, leaner, stronger, or more flexible. Those are the dancer's responsibility. The brand promises one thing: that the audience does not see the labor.

**SUBLINE is not Capezio, Bloch, Yumiko, Énergétiks, or any catalog-tier dance brand.** Those brands serve the dance industry on 1985 fabric and 1985 marketing. SUBLINE is what they failed to build.

**SUBLINE is not Apolla.** Apolla owns feet. SUBLINE owns torso. The two brands sit adjacent, not in conflict, and the visual systems differentiate at the accent (Apolla safety orange / SUBLINE Spotlight Amber) and at the photography register (Apolla studio-product / SUBLINE editorial-body).

**SUBLINE is not "inspired by dance."** That phrase is the wince trigger. The brand was built by a dancer, for working dancers, and it shows up dressed like a dancer's working life — backstage tungsten, hard light, mono labels, no glitter.

**SUBLINE is not soft.** Not in tone, not in typography, not in photography, not in motion, not in voice. The brand is engineered. The dancer is honored. The audience is respected. Soft is what fails on the playback.

**SUBLINE is not the catalog tier.** It is not the "luxe fabrics, classic lines, endless comfort" register. It is not a "studio collection." It is the gear layer the working dancer owns because she is a working professional and the brand respects that.

**SUBLINE is not the founder.** Josh is the proof of the thesis, not the face of the marketing. Founder content lives in a single editorial slot per quarter. Hero campaigns feature working dancers — never Josh in a Subline garment, never a "founder portrait" hero.

**SUBLINE is not friendly.** It is not personable. It is not chatty. The voice is the voice of a costume head reviewing a tape — focused, technical, respectful, brief. The brand does not want to be the dancer's friend. It wants to be the gear she trusts.

If a future contributor produces a surface, a piece of copy, a photograph, or a product that violates any of the above — the surface is wrong, regardless of how polished it looks. This document is the standard.
