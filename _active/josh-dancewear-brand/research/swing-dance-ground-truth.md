## TL;DR — the single truth

**Modern competitive Lindy Hop is a visually distinct sub-culture that has consciously moved AWAY from costume cosplay toward modern athletic-influenced clothing with vintage NODS.** The aesthetic register is closer to a Tracksmith editorial — "amateur-spirit competitive athletes who care about craft and history" — than to a 1940s USO recreation. SUBLINE for swing should look like *Tracksmith for dancers who happen to also love jazz*, not like an Andrews Sisters tribute act. The serious dancers (Skye Humphries, Naomi Uyama, Frida Segerdahl, Gaby Cook, Caleb Teicher, Bianca Locatelli, Felix Berghäll) signal status by *modernity* and movement-engineering — white Adidas, rolled trousers, billowing-sleeve blouses for arm visibility — not by literal period accuracy. The white-spaced opportunity for SUBLINE is real: there is no Tracksmith-equivalent in swing yet. ([Lindy Shopper ILHC report — VERIFIED](https://lindyshopper.com/2020/12/06/ilhc-2020-virtual-fashion-report/), [Swingin' Denver / Gaby Cook on Sw!ng Out — VERIFIED](https://www.swingindenver.com/blog/lindy-hop-as-thoroughly-vernacular))

## 1. What serious modern Lindy Hoppers actually wear

### A. PRACTICE / TRAINING context (studio drilling, technique work)

**[VERIFIED]** Practice-wear is conspicuously *normal modern athletic-casual* — there is no "swing practice uniform." Comfortable T-shirts, tank tops, leggings, joggers, athletic shorts, sneakers. The community-published guidance is uniform across continents:

- **Pittsburgh Swing Dance Club** ([source](https://www.pittsburghswingdance.com/faqs)): "Clothes you're comfortable moving and sweating in. Casual attire or dressing up is fine."
- **Heptown (Sweden)** ([source](https://www.heptown.com/getting-ready-for-your-lindy-hop-weekend/)): "Attire is usually a mixture of training and everyday clothes... Comfortable and loose-fitting or stretchy is good, and most people avoid tight jeans."
- **Swing Island Malta** ([source](https://swingislandmalta.com/tips)): "Anything that lets you move freely and won't make you too hot. Jeans or casual trousers are fine as long as you can move easily in them, e.g. not too tight."

**[LIKELY]** No specific "Lindy Hop training brand" has captured the segment the way Lululemon owns yoga or Tracksmith owns running. Dancers default to whatever they already own — Nike, Adidas, Uniqlo, generic athletic. This is the white space. (Inferred from the absence of any swing-native training brand in 6+ research sources; reinforced by SocalSwingDance guidance saying "you don't need any special dance attire" for class.)

**Image-brief implication**: Practice / training visuals should look like a Tracksmith × Apolla reference — fitted modern athletic separates, no period costuming, in a real studio environment. The SUBLINE base layer can be the visible *or* over-layered garment in this register because the over-clothing is athletic, not costume.

### B. SOCIAL DANCE context (Saturday-night social, regular venue, dancing for fun)

This is the bulk of the wearable-culture surface area. **[VERIFIED]** The social-dance dress register is "casually fancy" — the words used by Swingstep ([source](https://swingstep.com/this-is-swing/dress-code/)): *"Most people choose to wear something that they might also wear if they'd meet friends for a drink in the evening — what we like to call 'Casually Fancy.'"*

Three distinct sub-registers exist within social dance:

1. **The everyday social** (Frim Fram Jam Thursdays, regular Tuesday classes, smaller scenes) — jeans, button-up or T-shirt, comfortable shoes. SoCal Swing ([source](https://socalswingdance.com/lindyclothes.htm)): *"Dress tends to be pretty casual with jeans as most people's choice."*
2. **The "live music night" social** (Saturday Night Swings at YSBD with George Gee, NYLX with Eyal Vilner) — the bar lifts. Men wear button-ups, vests, suspenders sometimes; women wear A-line dresses, midi skirts, vintage-cut blouses. Dressing up is encouraged, not required.
3. **The "exchange" or "weekender" gala night** — black-tie-ish for the NYE Lindy Focus party; high-end vintage-influenced for ILHC nights; "semi-formal encouraged but not required" per [Lindy Focus FAQ — VERIFIED](https://lindyfocus.squarespace.com/faqs).

**[VERIFIED] Two separate dress communities co-exist within "swing"**:
- **Lindy Hop dancers** (the serious athletic register — see Section 4)
- **Vintage / rockabilly cosplayers** (often overlap with the swing scene but are a distinct community)

The Dogpossum essay on "vintage fashion and lindy hop" ([source — VERIFIED](https://dogpossum.org/2018/02/vintage-fashion-and-lindy-hop-lets-add-race/)) names this directly: *"Lindy hop has gotten a lot more athletic generally than it was in the 2000s. Parachute pants are great for smoothy dancing, but a shorter skirt or more fitted trousers are better for lots of solo jazz, leaping about, etc... All this athletic training has also changed professional or competition dancers' physiology: more muscles, a greater range of movement = more care with fashion choices. A lovely 1940s dress is too precious to risk on a hardcore showcase routine with aerials."*

**Image-brief implication**: Social-dance visuals should show *over-clothing* — a dress, a button-up shirt, a vest, a midi skirt — with the SUBLINE base layer peeking at the cuff, collar, or shoulder strap. Never bare-base-layer-on-the-floor.

### C. PERFORMANCE / COMPETITION context (ILHC, ULHS, Lindy Focus Stompoff, Camp Hollywood Showdown)

**[VERIFIED]** ILHC's official rule is *"colorful, decorative, dramatic attire enhances the entertainment value of the performances. Tasteful dance clothing is accepted and strongly encouraged."* ([source](https://ilhc.com/competition-rules/)) Camp Hollywood: *"Creative clothing is encouraged and appreciated."* ([source](https://camphollywood.net/contest-info/))

The 2020 ILHC fashion report ([Lindy Shopper — VERIFIED](https://lindyshopper.com/2020/12/06/ilhc-2020-virtual-fashion-report/)) names specific, observable trends from competition floor:

- **Sleeves were "the star"** — long, flowing, billowing sleeves used to highlight arm movement (period-appropriate AND functional).
- **White as visual punctuation** — white Adidas sneakers (credited to **Skye Humphries' influence** specifically), white suits, white trousers. White draws the eye to footwork.
- **Specific named competitors**: Sakarias Larsson (with Frida Segerdahl) wore white Converse All-Stars + light rolled trousers + loose yellow shirt + black knit cap — described as *"skater punk Lindy"* — and was a fan-favorite. Italy's Milano Swing Team did matching white double-breasted suits with fierce tailoring. Aurelien Darbellay (Barcelona) wore the same skater-punk-with-yellow-shirt aesthetic.
- **Pops of yellow / chartreuse** as a single-color statement to draw judge attention.

**[VERIFIED] The named competitive elite (referenced repeatedly in primary sources)**:
- Skye Humphries, Naomi Uyama, Frida Segerdahl, Nina Gilkenson — multi-decade competitors and instructors. Skye specifically credited as fashion influencer (white Adidas) per Lindy Shopper.
- Gaby Cook, AJ Howard, Felix Berghäll, Moe Sakan, Bianca Locatelli, Nils Andrén — current generation winning Camp Hollywood / ULHS / ILHC ([Camp Hollywood 2023, 2024, 2025 finals — VERIFIED](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IjpTqqwmXJU)).
- Caleb Teicher — choreographer of *Sw!ng Out*, the Joyce Theater touring production that has reframed swing as a contemporary American art form.

**[LIKELY]** Performance wear leans toward modern-athletic with vintage details: high-waisted trousers, button-up shirts, loafers or modern dance sneakers, A-line skirts at knee/midi length. Period costuming (zoot suits, victory rolls) is largely *not* what the winners wear — that's the cosplay subculture.

**Image-brief implication**: If a performance moment is shown, the dancer is in a vest + shirt + high-waisted trousers OR a midi A-line skirt + tucked blouse. The SUBLINE base layer peeks at the buttoned-shirt cuff (when sleeves are rolled), at the V or scoop neck of the dress, at the shoulder strap above a tank — but never IS the costume.

## 2. Where they actually dance — real environments

### Lindy Focus — Crowne Plaza Resort, Asheville, NC
**[VERIFIED]** Lindy Focus is held at the **Crowne Plaza Resort Asheville** (1 Resort Dr) every Dec 26 – Jan 1. ([source](https://lindyfocus.squarespace.com/faqs)) The dancing happens in the **Crowne Ballroom** (~7,000 sq ft, divisible into Crowne 1 & 2), the **Crowne Exposition Center** (~16,600 sq ft, used for the New Year's Eve big-band party), and breakout rooms (Dogwood, etc.). ([source](https://www.ihg.com/crowneplaza/hotels/us/en/asheville/avlss/hoteldetail/events-facilities))

**Visual register**: This is a working hotel resort ballroom. Drop-tile or coffered ceilings, hotel-standard chandelier or pot-light overheads, beige/cream walls, and a hardwood/laminate ballroom floor (the kind of room that hosts weddings the rest of the year). It is **NOT a romantic 1940s ballroom**. The *culture* is romantic; the *room* is functional. There's an Expo bar that opens nightly at 8pm. The dance schedule runs 8pm – 2am most nights, with an **NYE Breakfast Buffet at 1:00am – 2:15am** (eggs, bacon, biscuits, sausage gravy in the Dogwood room — a real and beloved camp ritual). ([source](https://lindyfocus.squarespace.com/dance-schedule))

### Camp Hollywood — LAX Marriott, Los Angeles
**[VERIFIED]** Camp Hollywood runs in the LAX Marriott's 12,500 sq-ft ballroom (5855 W Century Blvd) since 2002. ([source](https://camphollywood.net/location/)) Same physical register as Lindy Focus: hotel-resort ballroom, not vintage hall.

### You Should Be Dancing (YSBD) — NYC's home venue
**[VERIFIED]** Located at **37 W 26th St, 2nd floor, NYC** ([source](https://www.youshouldbedancing.nyc)). Hosts **Frim Fram Jam Thursday nights** (NYC's longest-running weekly Lindy Hop, since 2001), Saturday Night Swings monthly with live big bands, and Sunday-night NYLX socials. The venue itself describes: *"Spacious, sprung wood floor in a room that's designed with dancing in mind. Sound system and AC are state-of-the-art... Beautiful venue! Gorgeous sprung dance floor! Intense A/C! Huge fans! Epic ventilation!"* ([source](http://frimframjam.com))

**Visual register**: A 2nd-floor NYC dance studio. Sprung hardwood floor, mirror walls (likely on at least one wall — standard for any rentable dance studio), exposed ductwork or industrial ceiling, big floor fans (mentioned by name on their site), a small bar or refreshments table. **This is the modal "real social Lindy venue."** Not a converted theater — a working dance studio with the lights up.

### NYLX (NY Lindy Exchange) — touring NYC venues
**[VERIFIED]** NYLX rotates across multiple NYC venues per year ([source](https://www.nylindy.com/dances-2025)) — historic Estonia House (243 East 34th), Prince George Ballroom (15 East 27th), and YSBD itself. So for one weekend a swing dancer might dance in three radically different rooms: a stately old-world ballroom, a converted historic event hall, and a 2nd-floor sprung-floor studio. **The community travels across architectural registers and cares more about who's there than what the room looks like.**

### Herräng Dance Camp — Sweden
**[VERIFIED]** Held in the small Swedish village of Herräng each July; described as "the Burning Man of Lindy Hop" ([Rikomatic 2016](https://rikomatic.com/2016/08/herrang-2016-solo-jazz-the-bus-stop-swedish-pancakes.html)). Dance happens on three primary surfaces: the **dansbana** (an outdoor wooden dance platform — *dansbana* means "dance floor" in Swedish), the **Folkets Hus** (community hall — the ballroom, a true community space, not luxury), and a **library** dance floor (smaller, intimate). Dancing runs *"after the daily Camp Meeting at 9pm... typically doesn't stop until breakfast starts in the morning at 8am, and sometimes goes even later."* ([source](https://rikomatic.com/2016/08/herrang-2016-solo-jazz-the-bus-stop-swedish-pancakes.html))

**Visual register**: Outdoor wooden platform under summer Nordic light. Wooden community-center interiors. Small village streets. Tap jams in ice cream parlors. *"Just walking around the small village and seeing all of the dancers doing their thing... a couple might be practicing an airstep on the grass, an old timer might be holding court... in the cafe."* (Rikomatic, verbatim.) **This is the most photographable register in all of swing** — sunlight, wood, water, casualness, joy. The Edition 02-grade error of staging dancers in fog-machine 1940s ballroom recreation is exactly the wrong direction.

### Image-brief implication for venues
Real serious-modern Lindy venues are: (1) **hotel resort ballrooms** (Crowne Plaza Asheville, LAX Marriott — pragmatic, beige, ceiling-tile); (2) **NYC sprung-floor 2nd-floor studios** with mirror walls and giant floor fans (YSBD); (3) **converted historic halls** rented for one night; (4) **outdoor wooden dance platforms** in summer light (Herräng). NEVER fog-machine-romantic-vintage-ballroom recreations. The honest modern Lindy aesthetic is *"hotel ballroom or sprung-floor studio with 200 sweating dancers and a live 12-piece band."*

## 3. The foundation-layer visual grammar — how it actually shows up

This is the most critical research for the brief. **The base layer is supporting cast, not a hero.** Where does it become visible in real photography?

**[VERIFIED]** Standard athletic / dance photography conventions for foundation-layer peek:

1. **Cuff peek** — the dancer's button-up shirt sleeves are rolled to forearm; the SUBLINE long-sleeve base-layer cuff shows ~2-3cm at the wrist. This is the cleanest, most universally legible peek and the most useable in lifestyle photography. Common in cycling, running, and prep-school athletics imagery.
2. **Collar peek** — the base-layer crewneck shows ~1cm above the dancer's vest, button-up shirt collar, or unbuttoned-button-down V. Equivalent to a Patagonia capilene under a flannel — recognizable, legible, premium.
3. **Strap shoulder line** — bra strap or compression strap visible above a tank top or under a strapless dress. *Caveat*: this is the most "athleisure" of the peek modes; use sparingly to avoid a Lululemon register.
4. **Neckline edge** — base-layer scoop or V visible at the deeper neckline of a follower's dress or a leader's open shirt.
5. **Back panel** — visible through the cut-out of an open-back blouse or low-back dress (relevant for follower performance wear).
6. **Mid-change moment** — the documentary photograph: dancer in a backstage/dressing-room hallway between sets, peeling off the over-shirt, base layer fully visible. **This is the only context in which the base layer can be the visible hero in a SUBLINE image** — the dancer is not on the dance floor; they are in transition between dances.

**[LIKELY]** Reference this peek-grammar from adjacencies the brief team can study directly:
- **Tracksmith** "Session" editorial photography — runners shown in over-layers (windbreakers, singlets, cool-weather quarters) with the base layer as a 1cm collar or cuff line. The base layer is *implied* as quality infrastructure, not the visible product.
- **Patagonia** capilene-under-flannel imagery from outdoor lifestyle magazines.
- **Apolla** dance compression-sock photography — they show the product on its own in product shots, but in editorial they show it as a *peek* under leg-warmers or under a tendu line.
- **Broadway / musical theater backstage photography** — dressers' room shots show actors in base layers between scenes; this is the canonical "between-songs" SUBLINE register.

**Image-brief implication — the four legitimate base-layer-visible registers for SUBLINE swing**:
1. **The cuff peek mid-dance** (a dancer in motion, hand reaching, base-layer cuff visible at button-up wrist)
2. **The collar peek mid-conversation** (between-songs portrait, base layer at the open collar)
3. **The dressing-room transition** (backstage hallway, dancer half-changed, base layer visible — NOT on the dance floor)
4. **The product-only or wardrobe-flat-lay shot** (no human, just the garment)

NEVER show: a dancer mid-Charleston in just the base layer.

## 4. Serious modern Lindy aesthetic vs. vintage cosplay aesthetic

This is the strategic core. **[VERIFIED]**

### Serious modern Lindy aesthetic
Defined most clearly by **Gaby Cook** describing the *Sw!ng Out* show ([source — VERIFIED](https://www.swingindenver.com/blog/lindy-hop-as-thoroughly-vernacular)):

> *"Wants to be a display of swing dance in modern bodies/modern clothes and doing things that feel true to the dancers' bodies. As dancers we are asked to bring ourselves into the dance including our entire lived-in experience... avoiding asking them to re-enact the teachers' exact movement because Lindy Hop is real now and not just the past."*

The visual register:
- High-waisted trousers (wide-leg or tapered) — vintage NOD, modern cut
- Button-up shirts in linen or breathable cotton, often with rolled sleeves
- Athletic dance shoes (Aris Allen suede-soled, Keds, Converse, white Adidas) — NEVER period leather brogues for hardcore competition use
- A-line midi skirts or dresses in modern stretch fabrics (cotton blends, rayon)
- Modern stretch/wicking *under-fabrics* increasingly used (Dogpossum essay confirms this trend)
- Subtle period accents (a fedora at evening dance, suspenders, a pocket square) but rarely the full costume
- Cleaner color palettes — neutrals, single-color statements (yellow, chartreuse), white-as-punctuation

### Vintage cosplay aesthetic
Defined by **literal 1938-1945 historical recreation**:
- Zoot suits (extreme high-waisted baggy trousers, oversized jackets, wide lapels, shoulder pads)
- Victory rolls hairstyles, period-correct makeup
- Pinup-style dresses with petticoats
- Full fedoras, period-correct shoes (oxfords, brogues, T-strap heels)
- Suspenders + bow tie + pocket square together
- Often overlaps with rockabilly, pinup, and 1940s WWII reenactment communities
- Strong brand presence: Dancestore.com, Swingdanceshop.com, Chloe Hong (Seoul), Dallas Vintage Shop ([source](https://www.dallasvintageshop.com/1940s-uso-swing-dance-gal-swing-dance-vintage-dresses/))

**[VERIFIED] Quote from Lindy Shopper on Sakarias Larsson's winning ULHS look**: *"His choice of attire isn't haphazard just-rolled-out-of-bed skater punk, it's Lindy Hop because of his choices — white Converse All-Stars to draw your eye; loose light colored trousers rolled up for efficiency of movement; a loose, but not ill-fitting yellow shirt (again, drawing attention), and a black knit cap that coordinates with his socks and keeps his hair out of his face."*

This is the canonical modern-competitive aesthetic in two sentences. Skater-punk references, athletic shoes, deliberate movement-engineering choices, modern fabrics. **Not** a costume. **Image-brief implication**: SUBLINE swing photography must avoid every visible signal of vintage cosplay (no zoot suits, no victory rolls, no full fedora, no period petticoats, no Andrews-Sisters group choreography). Period nods are fine and expected (high-waisted trousers, suspenders, button-up shirts, A-line skirts) — but they must read 2026, not 1944.

## 5. Sweat reality and recovery rituals

**This is the brand's home turf.** The community talks about sweat constantly and openly — and the rituals are concrete, visible, and brief-able.

### Verbatim quotes from the community

**Bees' Knees Dance — community-voted #1 pet peeve** ([source — VERIFIED](https://beeskneesdance.com/lindy-hop-pet-peeves/)):
> *"Excessive sweatiness. Sweating is understandable but if you know that you sweat enough, please be courteous and bring as many number of change of tops as required. **It's not unheard of to bring 6 extra t-shirts to a dance and to change throughout the night.** A towel is also a good idea. No one wants a handful of sweat, or to be sprayed by your sweat, to have your sweat get on their nice outfits, or to slip on your sweat."*

This is the central insight. **Bringing six T-shirts to a dance is the cultural norm for heavy sweaters, called out by name in pet-peeve guides.**

**Same source, with the killer caveat for SUBLINE engineering**:
> *"Wicking shirts. Nice for you but not so nice for your partner since these materials will often transfer your sweat away from you and on to your partner!"*

**[VERIFIED] Major engineering implication**: Standard moisture-wicking poly is *socially unacceptable* in Lindy because it transfers sweat to the partner. SUBLINE's pitch can't be just "wicking" — it has to be *absorption + lock-in* (sweat held within the layer, away from the over-shirt, and not transferred). This is a real, named, lived problem the community discusses. **The SUBLINE base layer needs to absorb without transferring — and that's a category-defining difference from existing athletic compression brands.**

**Long Island Swing Syndicate** ([source — VERIFIED](https://www.liswingsyndicate.com/general-info)):
> *"Bring extra shirts if you think you'll sweat through one. Seriously, there's no shame in this. It happens all the time in swing! If you do sweat a lot, try experimenting with different fabrics. **Plain cotton shirts will last about ten minutes in a crowded social before your dance partners start getting squeamish.**"*

**Black Pepper Swing** ([source — VERIFIED](https://blackpepperswing.com/lindyhop/)):
> *"There is a reason, why many lindy hoppers bring a couple of extra shirts to their classes and parties. Lindy hop can be physically very demanding and it is 100% normal to sweat during this dance."*

**Pittsburgh Swing Dance Club** packing list ([source — VERIFIED](https://www.pittsburghswingdance.com/faqs)):
> *"Handkerchief, extra shirts, a fan, deodorant... Lindy Hop is a very active dance and a bunch of dancers in a room can get sweaty quickly. Plan accordingly!"*

**Jitterbuzz "You might be a Lindy Hopper if..."** ([source — VERIFIED](http://www.jitterbuzz.com/lincon.html)):
> *"You routinely bring a change of clothes, water, and a towel or two for a night out... You sweat through your dance partner's shirt."*

### The tools: sweat towels and the BJ Dance Towels brand
**[VERIFIED]** A Lindy-specific microfiber towel exists — **BJ Dance Towels** (covered in [Lindy Shopper's "Savoy towel" review](https://lindyshopper.com/tag/lindy-hop/)). Designed to hang on the back of a chair without touching the floor; doubles as a scarf for warmth. Made from recycled plastics. Marketed with the German Swing Kids opera-scarf reference. **Sweat-rags as both function and style is a real and current Lindy thing.**

**[LIKELY]** From Dance Forums ([source](https://www.dance-forums.com/threads/sweat-towels-hanging-out-of-the-back-pocket.52425/)): *"I've seen this in Salsa and West Coast Swing events. There are dancers (generally leads) who have a white towel in their back pocket. It's almost like a fashion trend. I guess it can be a way of saying 'I keep myself dry and I have a clean towel to do that.'"* — back-pocket towel as a fashion-functional signal exists in West Coast Swing and salsa, less commonly visible in Lindy social photography but consistent with the towel-on-chair convention.

### Image-brief implications for sweat / recovery rituals
- **Six-shirt stack on a chair** at the side of a dance is a real and photographable moment. SUBLINE lifestyle imagery can show a dancer's chair at a dance camp — the over-shirts neatly folded, a SUBLINE base layer visible underneath one, a water bottle, a towel.
- **The between-songs change** is the most native "lifestyle" hero moment for SUBLINE — a dancer in the hallway between dances, peeling the soaked over-shirt off, the SUBLINE base layer visible, calmly putting on the next over-shirt.
- **The ice-water-bottle as victory** ("you think an iced bottle of water is the ultimate prize for a dance contest" — Jitterbuzz) is a real cultural beat.
- **The towel on the chair-back, doubled as a scarf for warmth between dances** — extremely SUBLINE-on-brand. Could even be a future product.
- **NEVER stage the sweat as glamorous** (gleaming-collarbone Calvin Klein register). Stage it as *managed*. The product solves the problem; the photograph shows the solution working.

## 6. The "luxury swing" question — does it exist yet?

**[VERIFIED] No.** There is no Tracksmith-equivalent for Lindy Hop. The premium-aspirational position is unclaimed. The current brand landscape:

- **Dancestore.com / Swingdanceshop.com / LindyShopper.com** — community e-commerce, cultural hubs, vintage-republished aesthetic, NOT premium-design-led.
- **Aris Allen** — the dominant swing-dance shoe brand. Functional, pricey ($110-180), but not aspirational-cool. Solves a real problem (suede sole + ankle support).
- **Chloe Hong (Seoul)** — high-craft dancewear, mostly performance-wear and team uniforms. Cult following but not mass-aspirational, focused on the troupe market.
- **BJ Dance Towels** — accessory niche, not apparel.
- **Capezio / Bloch / Wolford** — adjacent dancewear brands but their swing relevance is limited; they own ballet/contemporary, not Lindy.

**Tracksmith-comparable brands in adjacent niches** (for reference of what SUBLINE-for-swing could BE):
- **Tracksmith** (running) — "Amateur Spirit," collegiate-vintage aesthetic, refined never edgy, runs cost $90-200, founded 2014, Boston-based, "Trackhouses" as community hubs, sponsors non-pro elite runners ([source](https://www.tracksmith.com/pages/about)).
- **Bandit Running** (NYC) — community-built, NYC running-club-native, limited drops, tighter-edged ([source](https://www.fieldmag.com/articles/best-running-brands)).
- **Satisfy** (Paris) — "MothTech," counter-cultural, premium-edgy.
- **Soar Running** — function-dictates-form, elite-athlete-grade, high-tech.

**The strategic read**: A "Tracksmith for serious swing dancers" position is genuinely unclaimed. The swing community is small but committed and global, with documented willingness to pay for premium dance shoes ($150+) and travel internationally to camps. **The community trades in cultural status the way running clubs do** — a SUBLINE long-sleeve worn at Frim Fram with a peek of cuff would function the way a Tracksmith Van Cortlandt tee does at the Brooklyn Track Club Saturday run.

**Image-brief implication**: SUBLINE swing visuals should reference the Tracksmith editorial register — high-craft photography, real athletes (not models), authentic locations, refined never edgy, vintage NODS but contemporary execution. Color palette already locked in the brand spec (true black, bone, graphite, spotlight amber) translates cleanly to the swing context.

# Ground-Truth Checklist for Image Generation

The next subagent should generate prompts directly against this checklist. Every SUBLINE swing image must satisfy these constraints.

### ✅ DO show
1. **Real serious-modern-Lindy register**: high-waisted trousers, button-up shirts, A-line midi skirts, suede-soled dance sneakers (Keds, Converse, Aris Allen, white Adidas).
2. **The peek, not the hero**: SUBLINE base layer visible as a 1-3cm cuff at a rolled-shirt wrist, a 1cm collar above an unbuttoned shirt, a strap above a tank, a back-panel through an open-back blouse.
3. **Real venue contexts**: hotel-resort ballrooms (Lindy Focus / Camp Hollywood register), 2nd-floor sprung-floor NYC dance studios with mirror walls and giant floor fans (YSBD register), outdoor wooden dance platforms in summer light (Herräng register), or backstage hotel hallways at exchanges.
4. **Sweat as managed reality**: a stack of folded over-shirts on a chair, a towel hung on a chair-back, a water bottle, a base-layer-clad dancer mid-change in a hallway between sets.
5. **Modern athletic construction with vintage NODS**: high-waisted but contemporary cut; rolled sleeves; suspenders or vest as a single accent; never the full period costume.
6. **Real working dancer body types**: athletic, varied, multi-ethnic, ages 22-45 (the actual competitive Lindy demographic). Cite Skye Humphries, Naomi Uyama, Frida Segerdahl, Gaby Cook, Felix Berghäll, Bianca Locatelli, Caleb Teicher, LaTasha Barnes as physical reference points.
7. **Movement engineering visible in the over-clothing**: billowing sleeves on followers, rolled trousers on leaders (per ILHC 2020 trend report — these are functional choices that read on camera).
8. **Spotlight Amber accent (`#F2A33C`) used sparingly**: stage-light reference, not costume color. Echoes the modern white-and-yellow ILHC trend while staying brand-locked.

### ❌ DO NOT show
1. **The base layer as the visible hero on the dance floor.** Ever. The over-clothing is always present in dance moments.
2. **Vintage cosplay register**: zoot suits, full fedoras, victory rolls, pinup petticoats, full Andrews-Sisters group choreography, period-correct mid-1940s wartime aesthetic.
3. **Fog-machine / soft-pastel "1940s ballroom recreation" lighting**. The real venues are hotel ballrooms and sprung-floor studios — light them honestly.
4. **Gleaming-collarbone Calvin Klein "sweat as glamour" register**. Swing dancers solve sweat with dignity (six folded shirts on a chair); they don't pose with it.
5. **Generic moisture-wicking athleticwear visual register** (compression tops, gym shorts, neon performance branding). SUBLINE is *between* athleticwear and dancewear — refined-technical, not gym-tech.
6. **Lululemon / Alo Yoga wellness-pastel register**. Brand spec already excludes this; reinforced here.
7. **Solo poses on a bare cyclorama / studio backdrop with no community context**. Lindy is a partner-and-community dance — every image should imply or show partnership/community.
8. **Dance shoes that aren't suede-soled or smooth-soled**. Black-soled outdoor sneakers are explicitly banned at most Lindy venues for floor-protection reasons — show suede, leather, or worn-down Keds/Converse only.
9. **Aerial / acrobatic moves as the dominant visual**. ILHC competitions use aerials, but the bulk of swing is grounded social dancing. Aerial-heavy imagery reads as "ballroom-show" not "real Lindy."

### 🎯 Most-native visual moments (prompt these first)
1. **The cuff peek, mid-swingout** — leader and follower in a swingout connection, leader's rolled button-up cuff revealing a 2cm SUBLINE long-sleeve cuff at the wrist. Hotel ballroom or sprung-floor studio, evening lighting.
2. **The between-songs change** (THE hero scene) — backstage hallway at Lindy Focus or backstage at Camp Hollywood. Dancer mid-change, soaked over-shirt in hand, fresh over-shirt on the wall hook, SUBLINE base layer visible. This is the brand thesis in a single frame.
3. **The chair at the side of the dance** — folded stack of clean over-shirts, a microfiber towel hung over the chair-back, a water bottle, a pair of suede-soled dance shoes, a dancer's bag. Suggests the whole sweat-management ritual without showing a single body.
4. **The studio practice frame** — daytime, a sprung-floor studio with mirrors and floor fans, a dancer drilling solo footwork in a fitted SUBLINE long-sleeve and high-waisted trousers, sneakers, the SUBLINE wordmark just visible at a hem. Modern athletic register with vintage cut on the trousers.
5. **The Herräng outdoor wooden platform at golden hour** — couple in modern-athletic attire dancing on the dansbana, summer-evening light, SUBLINE base layer at the leader's collar visible above an open shirt. Most photographable register in the entire culture.

8. **The class / camp warm-up** — mid-morning at Lindy Focus, dancers in a sprung-floor studio in fitted athletic-casual gear (modern tees, joggers, leggings, sneakers) drilling 8-counts. Most-relatable register for the broader Lindy-curious buyer.
