## Manifesto

### I. The Truth

Every working dancer trains as an athlete and performs as an erasure.

The lifts, the reps, the cross-training, the cardio, the years of conditioning — that body is built like a sprinter's. Then she walks out under stage lights and her job is to make that body look like it was never built at all. The audience can't see the labor or the work breaks. The illusion is the art.

Sweat is what happens when the illusion slips. The pit stain on the playback. The wet stripe down the back of the costume. The moment in row 3 when someone notices. It is not a hygiene problem. It is the audience watching the curtain on her training fall and seeing the athlete she trained ten thousand hours to hide.

### II. The Engineering

Subline is the layer between the dancer and the costume. It is built to block visible sweat where it shows on stage, to hold the line where the costume sits, to disappear where the costume can't.

It is engineered for the eight-show week. Tested on tour. Cut for the body the costume sits on — for développé, for second, for partnered work, for the lift in dress rehearsal that ruined the last layer she tried. It is the wardrobe of the working dancer, sold direct, made to outlast the contract.

The fabric is the proof. The fit is the difference. Sweat in the studio. Stage-clean on the stage.

### III. The Stand

A dancer is a professional athlete trained to make the audience forget she's an athlete. That is the highest discipline in performance. It deserves infrastructure built to honor it.

The catalog tier dressed dancers in 1985 fabrics and called it a category. The athletic incumbents made gear for runners and lifters and called it close enough. Neither one watched a dancer in dress rehearsal at minute 280 of an eight-show week and asked what the costume was hiding.

We did. We are dancers, and we are building this for dancers, and we are not asking anyone for permission to take the work seriously.

The audience sees the art. We work in the line beneath it.

## Voice Rules — bind on all Subline copy from here

1. **The reader is the working dancer. Always.** Second person. Her vocabulary, her stakes, her bag. Never "the modern woman" or "the athlete." Specific or it's nothing.
2. **No wellness vocabulary.** "Self-care," "intention," "ritual," "balance," "empower," "boldly," "unapologetic," "feel good in your body" — banned. Pull from the wince list in the ICP profile when in doubt.
3. **No "athleisure," "activewear," "performance enhancement."** Lululemon-adjacent register kills the brand on contact. The brand is gear, not lifestyle.
4. **Sentences earn their length.** Short to mid is the spine. Long sentences only when the sentence itself is doing technical or rhythmic work. No filigree. No two-clause aphorisms hung at paragraph endings.
5. **Show the work, don't claim the work.** Not "engineered for elite performance." Show: "Tested at minute 280 of an eight-show week." Specifics carry the credibility every time.
6. **Talk about competitors obliquely, never by name.** "The catalog tier" and "athletic incumbents" do the work. The implication is the criticism. Naming Capezio or Nike in copy weakens the brand by acknowledging them as peers.
7. **The dancer is the authority. Not the founder, not the lab.** Proof comes through working dancers using it on tour, in the show, on the playback. Founder myth supports; never leads.
