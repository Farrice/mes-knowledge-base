# Naming Candidates — Performance Dancewear Brand

**Date**: 2026-05-01 · **Phase**: Foundation 1 of 3 · **For**: Josh × Farrice, garage review tonight
**Locked register**: Performance-Tech (industrial, material-science, biomechanical) — closer to Rolex than Lululemon.
**Test all candidates pass**: pronounceable on first hearing · wince test clear · industrial-mono typesettable · doesn't cannibalize "Apolla for the body" (no foot territory) · consonant teeth.

---

## Territory A — Movement / anatomy / dance-vocabulary roots

### 1. SOTTO
- **Roots**: Italian musical term, *sotto voce* — "under the voice." Stage direction for what's played beneath the dominant line.
- **Why it works**: Names the layer's role — the line under the line. Dance-literate without being twee. Reads as a brand a wardrobe head would order from.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / ✓
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Italian musical heritage, neutral across English / Spanish / French / Japanese markets. No religious or appropriation triggers. Clean.
- **TM-instinct**: Possible collision with food / wine / hospitality (Italian word, common). Apparel category likely thinner. Verify.
- **Visual instinct**: Tight serif or industrial-mono. Black on bone. The two T's give it a typographic anchor.

### 2. APLOMB
- **Roots**: French ballet term — perfect vertical balance, the dancer's plumb line. Also the English word for composure under pressure.
- **Why it works**: Two meanings collapse into one product promise — the engineered vertical line of the body, and the composure that comes from not worrying about your costume.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / red flag — silent B is a hurdle in audio-first contexts (TikTok, podcast).
- **Cultural-connotation check**: French ballet root is on-domain, not appropriative. Neutral globally.
- **TM-instinct**: Likely contested in beauty / fragrance. Apparel may be open. Verify.
- **Visual instinct**: All-caps mono. The silent B becomes a typographic feature — APLOMB sets like a watch dial.

### 3. RELEVÉ (or RELEVE — accent-flexible)
- **Roots**: French — "raised, lifted." The action of rising onto the ball of the foot.
- **Why it works**: Lift is exactly what compression does to the line. Dance-native, instantly recognized inside the world.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / red flag for non-dancers (RUH-luh-VAY).
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Clean. Direct domain term.
- **TM-instinct**: Studios and small labels likely use it. Crowded.
- **Visual instinct**: Editorial. But too on-the-nose — sounds like the brand was named by a dance teacher, not engineered by a founder.

### 4. LATENS
- **Roots**: Latin — "lying hidden, concealed." Same root as *latent*. The athletic capacity beneath the artistic surface.
- **Why it works**: Names the entire thesis — what's underneath, what stays hidden, what's there in reserve. Reads technical-medical (like a fabric trademark).
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / ✓ (LAY-tenz).
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Latin root, no live cultural ownership. Clean.
- **TM-instinct**: Strong — sounds defensible, feels coined. Verify pharma / biotech adjacency.
- **Visual instinct**: Industrial-mono lockup. All caps. Could carry a tech-spec sub-mark like LATENS-01.

## Territory B — Abstract performance-identity (single-syllable / ownable)

### 5. UNDER
- **Roots**: English — directional, foundational, infrastructural.
- **Why it works**: Owns the category by claiming the position. *Under* the costume. *Under* the lights. Foundational. Reads as confident as Apple naming a product *Air*.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / ✓.
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Clean across markets. Translates obviously.
- **TM-instinct**: Almost certainly contested as a standalone — too generic. Workable as UNDER. or UNDER/ with a wordmark stylization, or paired with a category modifier (UNDERLINE, UNDERFORM).
- **Visual instinct**: Period or slash as fifth character. UNDER. Reads as a statement.

### 6. SUBLINE
- **Roots**: Coined — *sub* (beneath) + *line* (the dancer's word for the body's silhouette). Built from native vocabulary.
- **Why it works**: "Line" is the most loaded word in a dancer's mouth. "She has beautiful line." Subline = the line beneath the line. The product's job in three letters of native language.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / ✓.
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Clean.
- **TM-instinct**: Likely open in apparel. SaaS / publishing may have collisions on the word. Verify.
- **Visual instinct**: One word, mono-spaced, lowercase or all-caps. Carries a typographic system — every product becomes a *line*: STAGE LINE, RUN LINE, EIGHT LINE.

### 7. FORM
- **Roots**: English — the body's shape, the technical execution, the foundational structure.
- **Why it works**: One syllable, ownable, dance-native (every correction in class is "fix your form"). Anchors the brand in technique rather than aesthetic.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / ✓.
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Clean.
- **TM-instinct**: Heavily contested as a standalone. Workable only as a paired mark (FORM/CO, FORMWEAR, FORMLAB) or with a category lockup.
- **Visual instinct**: Reads like a fabric trademark. Strong but the TM ceiling is real.

### 8. CARRIAGE
- **Roots**: English — how a dancer holds the body. Royalty-adjacent. Posture as identity.
- **Why it works**: Names what the product produces — the carriage of the working dancer. Tracksmith-register vocabulary that hasn't been claimed in athletics.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / ✓.
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Clean — British aristocratic register but not appropriative.
- **TM-instinct**: Hospitality / luggage may collide. Apparel likely open.
- **Visual instinct**: Long word, editorial. Sets beautifully but loses the consonant-edge brief calls for. Soft middle.

## Territory C — Coined / made-up

### 9. SUDOR
- **Roots**: Latin — sweat. Same root as *sudoriferous*. The thing the brand defeats, named directly.
- **Why it works**: Naming the enemy is a power move — like calling a sleep brand *Insomnia*. Owns the category by owning the word the category exists to solve. Hard consonants front and back.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / ✓ (SOO-door).
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Spanish reads "sweat" plainly — could feel literal/clinical to Spanish speakers. Latin/English markets read it as coined. Mixed but not appropriative.
- **TM-instinct**: Probably open in apparel. Verify Spanish-speaking trademark databases.
- **Visual instinct**: Industrial-mono, all caps. Looks like a fabric line at a Tokyo workwear store.

### 10. VELUM
- **Roots**: Latin — veil, sail, membrane. The thin layer between things.
- **Why it works**: Names the membrane between athlete and art. Single-syllable feel despite two. Clean phonetics. Sounds engineered.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / ✓.
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Anatomical term (soft palate) but not loaded. Clean.
- **TM-instinct**: Likely contested — popular Latin-coined name. Verify.
- **Visual instinct**: Editorial sans. Sets like a luxury fragrance, which fights the performance-tech brief. Risk.

### 11. OCCULTA
- **Roots**: Latin — hidden, concealed (feminine of *occultus*). Same root used in medical Latin (*spina bifida occulta* — hidden).
- **Why it works**: Technical Latinate reads like a pharma-grade material brand. Names the function in the founder's voice (Josh-the-engineer).
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / red flag — *occult* connotation in English carries baggage. Wince risk.
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Occult-association in English markets is a problem. Kill.
- **TM-instinct**: Open but moot.
- **Visual instinct**: Sets well. Doesn't matter — wince fail.

### 12. SUBROSA
- **Roots**: Latin — *sub rosa*, "under the rose." The phrase for what happens in confidence, beneath notice.
- **Why it works**: Names exactly the product's job — what happens under the costume, in confidence between the dancer and the work. Two-syllable, consonant-anchored.
- **Pronounceable / spell on first hearing**: ✓ / ✓ (sub-ROH-zuh).
- **Cultural-connotation check**: Clean. Wine / hospitality may carry the phrase but apparel is open.
- **TM-instinct**: A handful of small brands and bars use it. Not a category-blocker but verify in apparel.
- **Visual instinct**: Two-tone wordmark — SUB / ROSA stacked, or SUB·ROSA mid-dot. Strong system potential.

## TOP 3 RANKED

### 🥇 1. SUBLINE — **Recommended primary**
Wins on every gate. *Line* is the most native word in the dancer's vocabulary — "she has beautiful line." Subline names the product's job in three syllables of language they already speak. The system extends naturally into product naming (STAGE LINE, RUN LINE, EIGHT LINE — Eight for the eight-show week). Industrial-mono typesettable. Consonant teeth (B, L, N). Doesn't cannibalize Apolla's foot territory. The wince test passes — Maya reads it once and gets it.

The trade-off: it's coined-from-known-words, so the TM defensibility is real but not bulletproof. Acceptable for Year 1; can be deepened with stylization and product-line architecture.

### 🥈 2. LATENS
Strongest pure brand-feel. Reads like a Patagonia or Goretex-tier fabric trademark. Latin root gives etymological depth without appropriation. Names the thesis — what's hidden, what's held in reserve. Clean phonetics. The risk: less native to dancers than SUBLINE. They'll trust it once they see it on a peer; they won't intuitively *know* what it means at first glance.

### 🥉 3. SOTTO
The dignified pick. Carries Tracksmith-tier register without being precious. Italian musical-vocabulary root is on-brand for an art form built on counts and dynamics. Risk: the food-and-hospitality TM crowd around the word.

## Working primary for Phase 3 (visual / brand-system)

**SUBLINE** — locked.

The line under the line. Industrial-mono wordmark. Black, bone, chalk. Photography in the hard light of a backstage hallway, not the soft light of a wellness brand. The product-line architecture writes itself — every SKU is a *line* (Stage Line, Run Line, Eight Line, Tour Line). The system has somewhere to go.

Alternates (LATENS, SOTTO) stay live for Josh's preference. If TM clears for SUBLINE in apparel, lock it. If contested, LATENS is the fallback that loses zero strategic ground.
