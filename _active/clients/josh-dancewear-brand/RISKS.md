## 🔴 ACTIVE — needs resolution before public launch

### R1. Tagline derivation — "Don't let them see you sweat" / Dry Idea echo
- **Surfaced**: 2026-05-01 (Phase 1 of brand identity build)
- **Issue**: Josh's working tagline *"Don't let them see you sweat"* is one phoneme from Dry Idea deodorant's iconic 1980s campaign *"Never let them see you sweat"* (originally Procter & Gamble, deodorant brand, decades-long campaign, persists in cultural memory). Even where TM is technically clear for apparel, the cultural association is loud enough that press and dance media will read SUBLINE as derivative.
- **Mitigation tonight**: Generated 6 alternate tagline candidates; `*The line beneath the line.*` recommended as master tagline (locks with the brand name SUBLINE — wordmark and tagline are the same thought).
- **Action required**: Confirm with Josh that he's open to retiring the original as public-facing copy. Hold "Don't let them see you sweat" as internal north-star reference only.
- **Owner**: Josh (decision) + Farrice (legal verification).
- **Status**: 🔴 Active. Mitigated tonight via alternate tagline; final disposition pending Josh's call.

### R2. Trademark availability — SUBLINE / LATENS / SOTTO in apparel class
- **Surfaced**: 2026-05-01
- **Issue**: All three top naming candidates have been instinct-flagged but NOT trademark-verified. SUBLINE is the strongest pure name but is built from common words (SUB + LINE), so defensibility may be moderate even if open. SOTTO is heavily used in food/wine/hospitality; apparel may be open but verification needed. LATENS is the strongest pure brand-feel and likely the most defensible.
- **Action required**: USPTO TESS search across IC 25 (apparel) for all three names + close variants. Consider a TM attorney consult before any external use. Domain capture for the chosen name (subline.co, getsubline.com, runsubline.com — assume subline.com is taken).
- **Owner**: Farrice (search) + Josh (final name lock) + TM attorney (formal opinion).
- **Status**: 🔴 Active. Hard blocker for any external launch. Not urgent for tonight's review.

### R3. Marketing claims about sweat-blocking — substantiation required
- **Surfaced**: 2026-05-01 (manifesto Phase 2)
- **Issue**: The brand thesis ("blocks visible sweat") is a performance claim about a textile. Apparel claims of this kind require substantiation under FTC standards in the US (and equivalent in EU/UK if shipping there). Saying "stage-clean on the stage" is fine as voice; saying "blocks 100% of visible sweat for 280 minutes" requires lab data. Tracksmith / Apolla / UA all walk this line carefully — claims are scoped to what their R&D can defend.
- **Action required**: Before any marketing copy ships, define the substantiated claim envelope. What does Josh's actual fabric do? Lab tests? Real-world tests on dancers? Until then, voice can be aspirational ("tested on tour") but specifics must wait.
- **Owner**: Josh (R&D) + copywriter (claim discipline).
- **Status**: 🔴 Active. Will tighten as R&D matures.

- **Status**: 🟡 Watch.

### R5. Dance-retail gatekeeper risk
- **Surfaced**: 2026-05-01 (competitive intel finding)
- **Issue**: The dance-retail incumbents (Capezio, Bloch, Discount Dance Supply, dancewear specialty stores) fund their P&L on legacy dance brands. A premium-tech challenger like SUBLINE could be locked out of those distribution channels — the gatekeepers protect the incumbents. Apolla Performance Wear faced this and built DTC + studio direct as their primary channel, reaching wholesale only after proof.
- **Action required**: Phase 1 channel = DTC + Instagram-targeted dancers + tour wardrobe departments. Phase 2 specialty wholesale only after 12-month DTC traction. Don't pitch Capezio's wholesale buyers in Year 1.
- **Owner**: Josh (channel strategy) + Farrice (advisory).
- **Status**: 🟡 Watch. Already incorporated into the strategic recommendation.

### R6. TAM math — pro dancer market is small
- **Surfaced**: 2026-05-01 (competitive intel)
- **Issue**: The "professional working dancer" addressable market is small enough (~tens of thousands globally) that a brand priced to support full-margin DTC needs serious-amateur volume to fund the operation. Tracksmith's playbook: brand the pro identity, sell to the aspirational amateur who wants that identity. SUBLINE has the same dynamic — pro dancers are the credibility, serious adult-amateur and competition-circuit dancers are the volume.
- **Action required**: Pricing and marketing must be built for both audiences. The pro is the "who we make this for"; the amateur is "who buys most of it."
- **Owner**: Josh (pricing model) + Farrice (positioning).
- **Status**: 🟡 Watch.

### R7. Founder-as-face trap
- **Surfaced**: 2026-05-01 (DESIGN.md voice rules)
- **Issue**: Many DTC apparel brands lead with founder personality marketing too early, before product proof. The DESIGN.md voice rules explicitly call this out. SUBLINE's authority must come through working dancers using the product on tour, in the show, on the playback — not through Josh as the personality.
- **Action required**: Founder-myth content (Josh's story, the engineer-founder origin) supports but never leads. Ratio rule: every 10 brand assets, no more than 2 reference Josh directly.
- **Owner**: Farrice (content discipline) + Josh (humility).
- **Status**: 🟡 Watch.
