## Candidate Notes

### Commit On Eight

**Why it passes:** It is clean, wearable, and broadly understandable across dance and developer worlds.

**Risk:** It may be too subtle for people who want louder humor.

**Fix:** Make it the lead image and let the campaign page explain the overlap lightly.

### No Drops In Prod

**Why it passes:** It has the strongest culture-specific laugh and a useful safety undertone.

**Risk:** "Prod" may not land for non-software dancers.

**Fix:** Keep it visually cool enough that the unknown term feels intentional instead of confusing.

### Push / Pull Request

**Why it passes:** It is the strongest nerd-culture bridge.

**Risk:** It may read too technical if the design becomes a diagram.

**Fix:** Use strong typography, minimal lines, and no software logos or interface mimicry.

## Red Team Findings

### 1. Risk: The Shirts Become Clever But Not Wearable

**Problem:** Nerd jokes can turn into novelty shirts fast.

**Control:** Use minimal typography, muted shirt colors, and small design footprints. If it looks like a conference swag shirt, revise.

### 2. Risk: Josh Overbuilds The First Drop

**Problem:** Too many products, colors, or ideas will blur the signal.

**Control:** 3 designs max, tee only, 10-day batch, clear winner criteria.

### 3. Risk: The Audience Is Too Narrow

**Problem:** The overlap of swing dancers and software nerds may be smaller than it feels in the garage.

**Control:** Treat V1 as signal capture. Success can be comments, DMs, saves, or preorders, not only huge sales.

### 4. Risk: Copyright Or Trademark Issues

**Problem:** Meme culture often borrows protected characters, logos, song lyrics, team names, and event references.

**Control:** Keep all three V1 candidates original. Do not use GitHub logos, movie references, lyric fragments, event names, or existing shirt designs.

### 5. Risk: Bonfire Setup Changes Profit Reality

**Problem:** A design can look good in this doc but be unprofitable or awkward in Bonfire's live product picker.

**Control:** Final price and product choices must be decided using Bonfire's live base cost and profit preview.

## Anti-Slop Audit

**Color slop:** Pass. Recommended palettes are apparel-native and restrained, not default AI gradients.

**Typography slop:** Pass with review. The designer must choose strong condensed or utilitarian type, not default system fonts.

**Layout slop:** Pass. All finalists can work as simple centered or small-label shirt graphics.

**Detail slop:** Pass. Each shirt has one small signature detail: count marks, back-neck line, or protocol bracket.

**Soul slop:** Pass with review. "No Drops In Prod" has the strongest authored point of view; "Commit On Eight" is the most wearable; "Push / Pull Request" is the most niche.

## Final Verdict

Ship the V1 package after Josh picks the final art treatment.

Recommended lead design: **Commit On Eight**.

Recommended funniest design: **No Drops In Prod**.

Recommended nerd-pure design: **Push / Pull Request**.

If Josh wants the safest launch, publish all three but lead with Commit On Eight. If he wants a sharper statement, lead with No Drops In Prod.
