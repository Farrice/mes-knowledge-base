## 1. Executive Verdict

Eightward should move beyond "funny shirts for dancers" and become a serious social-dance identity brand:

**Eightward makes apparel for dancers who carry the joy of Lindy Hop forward.**

The brand's strongest lane is not vintage swing merch. It is **social-floor utility with hidden lineage**: clothes that work at workshops, late-night socials, practice rooms, diner hangs, and everyday life, with coded details that serious dancers recognize.

The core business should be identity apparel and scene-native utility goods. The nerd shirts should remain as a satellite line called something like **Debug Club**, where jokes reward insiders after the garment already looks good.

Immediate go/no-go:

| Decision | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Lead name | Keep **Eightward** | Continue private testing, but do not public-launch until counsel clears mark risk. |
| Backup name | Keep **The Eighth House** as a world/capsule phrase only | Do not use as company name unless counsel clears it. |
| Risky name | Retire **Airstep/AIR STEP** for now | Too close to footwear/Class 25 risk unless counsel rescues it. |
| Cultural posture | Lineage-first, low-ego, reviewed | Hire paid cultural review before root-forward public launch. |
| Product path | One hero tee, one satellite joke tee, one scene utility object | Avoid a big catalog until demand is proven. |

### The Brand Lesson From The Research

Josh's phrase "acolyte and ambassador for the joy of dancing Lindy Hop" points to a real desire: people want a wearable sign of belonging.

The public language should be gentler:

- Best phrase: **carry the joy forward**
- Secondary phrases: **wear the count**, **honor the floor**, **made for dancers who know the room**
- Use with care: steward, roots, lineage, ambassador
- Avoid as public copy: acolyte, disciple, gospel, convert, worship, official, pure

Reason: Frankie Manning is widely treated as an ambassador of Lindy Hop. Eightward should not sound like it is taking that title. It should sound like a respectful participant brand.

## 6. Fashion And Silhouette Direction

Design thesis:

**Danceable, social, post-event clothing.**

Eightward should feel like the shirt you change into after class and then keep wearing to late night. It should sit between dance utility, workwear, quiet streetwear, and smart social-club apparel.

### Garment Rules

| Rule | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Works while moving | Room through shoulders, back, hips, rise, and thigh. |
| Partner-safe | No scratchy prints, rough hardware, heavy scent, giant plastisol where partners make contact. |
| Looks good after sweat | Washed colors, midweight fabric, neck stability, darker base colors. |
| Not costume | No forced suspenders/fedora/poodle-skirt language. |
| Not gymwear | Danceable, but designed enough for after-social life. |
| Scene utility first | Shoe bags, towels, spare-shirt pouches, socks, and caps can be more native than another slogan tee. |

### Core Silhouettes

| Product | Fit | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| After Seven Tee | Boxy, washed, strong collar | Entry product; spare-shirt friendly; main logo and back print. |
| Floorcraft Camp Shirt | Lightweight, open collar, breathable | Hero garment; dinner-after and social-floor friendly. |
| Breakaway Utility Trouser | Relaxed pleat or fatigue cut, mobile rise | Real apparel signal; connects dance movement to workwear/skatewear logic. |
| Late Set Chore Jacket | Light canvas/twill, unlined | Makes Eightward feel like an apparel brand, not only merch. |
| Practice Shell | Lightweight coach jacket or packable nylon | Warmup, travel, event hallway. |
| Social Kit | Towel, socks, shoe bag, spare-shirt pouch | Scene-native utility goods. |
| Cap/Bandana | Low-profile identity marker | Small signal for people who do not want loud graphics. |

## 9. Cultural Safety, Legal, And IP Risk Map

### Green/Yellow/Red Risk Zones

| Zone | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Green | Original artwork, original phrases, 8-count geometry, floorcraft diagrams, social-floor behaviors, private testing, quiet Eightward marks. |
| Green | Public-domain/CC0 source material only when the item page clearly allows commercial reuse. Keep records. |
| Yellow | Eightward as public brand until counsel runs full federal, state, common-law, phonetic, domain, marketplace, music, and apparel clearance. |
| Yellow | `The Eighth House` as a capsule phrase. It is crowded and should not be treated as clean without counsel. |
| Yellow | Historic terms, venues, people, events, schools, festivals, or archives. Review each use. |
| Yellow | Educational copy about Lindy Hop. Claims need source links and modest language. |
| Red | Airstep/AIR STEP as a brand or product line unless counsel clears it. |
| Red | Song lyrics, copied song phrases, album art, event logos, dance-school logos, famous images, routine names used as selling devices. |
| Red | Historic dancers' names, faces, signatures, voices, or likenesses on merch without estate/publicity review. |
| Red | "Official," "authentic," "revived," "owned," or "certified" language around Lindy Hop culture. |

### Key Legal Principles For Counsel Prep

Trademark:

- USPTO says likelihood of confusion can come from sound, appearance, meaning, or commercial impression, not only identical marks.
- USPTO can refuse apparel specimens where the wording is merely decorative on the front of a shirt instead of source-identifying.
- Josh should ask about Class 25 apparel and whether Class 35 online retail belongs in the first filing plan.

Copyright:

- Original graphics can be protected.
- Clothing shape itself has limited copyright protection because clothing is a useful article.
- A graphic separable from the garment may be copyrightable.
- Do not copy historic photos, event art, posters, or logos without item-level rights review.

Publicity and affiliation:

- Names, likenesses, signatures, and personas can carry publicity risk.
- State law varies, especially for deceased public figures.
- Disclaimers do not cure every implied-affiliation issue.
