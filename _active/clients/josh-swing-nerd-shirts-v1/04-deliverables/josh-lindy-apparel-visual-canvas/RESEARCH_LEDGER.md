# Josh Lindy Hop Apparel Visual Canvas - Research Ledger

## Purpose

This ledger backs the visual canvas with source-informed taste rules. It is not a final brand strategy, legal review, or cultural authority claim. It is a working creative brief for Josh to react to before naming, marks, and finished graphics.

## Accepted Design Principles

1. **Root without costume.** Use Harlem/Savoy/Lindy roots as ethical and musical context, not as literal 1930s dress-up.
2. **Motion first.** Every piece should answer a dancer's body: shoulder rotation, high knees, sweat, floor grip, pocket depth, heat, and travel.
3. **Blank zones before graphics.** Josh's designs need honest garment surfaces: chest, back, sleeve, towel, bag, inside label. The apparel must still work with no print.
4. **Tailoring plus utility.** The best lane is not only tees. It is camp collars, wide trousers, cropped jackets, ripstop practice pants, shoe bags, towels, derbies, and sneakers.
5. **Community signal, not hype signal.** The wearer should read as someone who belongs on the floor and respects the room, not as a billboard.
6. **Modern diversity without tokenism.** Show Black roots and global participation in the same world while avoiding claims that flatten origin, revival, or stewardship.

## Three Creative Lanes

### Tailored Social Floor

**Use when:** Josh wants the brand to feel elegant, adult, dance-night ready, and culturally serious.

**Core pieces:** camp collar, cropped chore jacket, wide pleated trouser, derby, heavyweight tee with back print option.

**Graphic behavior:** small front placement, one larger back story print, soft ink, limited palette.

### Practice Utility

**Use when:** Josh wants the brand to solve real dancer needs and show daily community use.

**Core pieces:** washed black tee, cropped practice tee, ripstop pant, cap, towel, shoe bag, low canvas sneaker.

**Graphic behavior:** towel corner, bag front, neck label, small sleeve or hem hit.

### Music-Rooted Craft

**Use when:** Josh wants warmth, history, hand-feel, and a deeper product story.

**Core pieces:** tobacco overshirt, chore jacket, textured knit, faded blue trouser, canvas shoe.

**Graphic behavior:** woven labels, subtle embroidery, discharge print, garment-dye softness.

## Questions For Josh

1. Which lane feels most like what you love about Lindy Hop right now?
2. Which outfit would you wear to a social without explaining it?
3. Which blank zone should your existing graphics occupy first: chest, back, sleeve, towel, bag, or neck label?
4. Should the first offer be a graphic tee capsule, a dance-night uniform capsule, or a utility object capsule?

## Guardrails

- No final name, logo, monogram, or mascot in this preview.
- No religious-signifier framing.
- No old-timey swing costume shorthand.
- No claim that a new apparel brand owns Harlem, Savoy, or Black dance history.
- No sterile SVG identity board.
- No graphic-heavy tee line before silhouette taste is chosen.
