## Name: "Mark, 33" — the guy with eleven tabs open at 11pm

Composite, not a real person. Deliberately gender-flexible in application (the SAHD wedge sells to this *seller* identity, not a narrowed *buyer* demographic — research doc, counter-read #3) but I'm anchoring the interior monologue on a specific person because vague avatars produce vague copy.

## Snapshot

Mark is 31–40, married or partnered, one or two kids under 8, household income $55K–$95K, W2 or hourly, renting or early-mortgage. He is not broke — he pays the bills — but he watches money leave the account faster than it should and there's no slack in the system. He works a job that is fine, not soul-crushing, just permanently un-thrilling: logistics coordinator, retail assistant manager, dental hygienist, IT help desk, teacher. He scrolls Threads and TikTok on the couch after the kid is down, phone brightness low so he doesn't wake anyone. He has bought at least one thing before — a course, an ebook, a "start your Etsy shop" bundle — and either never opened it or opened it once and closed the tab. He does not think of himself as lazy. He thinks of himself as someone who keeps starting and never finishing, which is a worse story to tell yourself than lazy, because lazy is a trait and this feels like a verdict.

## The $0 → $1K psychological moment (the actual barrier, per research doc)

**Current belief:** "I'm not the kind of person this works for. Other people have something — a personality, a hustle gene, a network, a lucky break — that I don't have. I've tried before and it didn't take, which proves it."

This is not a strategy problem and he half-knows it. He can Google "how to make money online" and get 40,000 answers by lunch. What he can't Google is why none of them stuck. The barrier is:

- **Overwhelm** — not "what do I do" but "there are nine things to do and I don't know which one is real, so I do none of them." Decision paralysis dressed up as research.
- **"I'm not expert enough"** — he doesn't feel qualified to sell anything, teach anything, or post anything, because he's not a marketer, not a "creator," doesn't have a personal brand, doesn't know what an avatar or a funnel is, and every course assumes he already does.
- **Fear of judgment** — the single biggest silent one. He is more afraid of his coworkers, his brother-in-law, or his high school friends seeing him post "start your online business" content and thinking *oh no, he fell for one of those* than he is afraid of failing privately. Public failure > private stagnation, always, in his risk math.

**Desires (in order of what actually moves him, not what he'd say in a survey):**
1. Proof, fast, that this isn't fake — one real deposit, not a promise.
2. A next step so small and specific he can't talk himself out of starting it tonight.
3. Permission to not be "an entrepreneur" — to just be a guy who does one dumb small thing that works.
4. To stop being the guy who buys things and doesn't finish them. The identity repair matters more than the money.

**Fears (ranked):**
1. Getting scammed again — he's already been burned, or knows someone who has, or has just watched enough YouTube teardown videos to be pre-inoculated.
2. Doing it publicly and having someone he knows screenshot it and laugh.
3. Spending money he can't easily explain to his partner if it doesn't work.
4. Discovering, again, that the thing he bought requires forty hours of setup before it produces anything — confirming the "I always quit" story.

## Private language — exact phrases in his own head

_These are the words he'd never say out loud to a stranger but would nod hard at if he heard them from someone else. Use verbatim in hooks/copy where it fits the voice rules._

- "I know I should just pick one thing and stick with it."
- "Is this one actually real, or is it another one of those."
- "I don't even know what I'd sell / what I'm supposed to be good at."
- "I've already spent money on stuff like this before." (shame-adjacent, said flatly, not dramatically)
- "I'm not trying to quit my job, I just want some breathing room."
- "If this doesn't work either, I'm done trying this stuff." (the quiet ultimatum he sets for himself, never announced)
- "I'll look into it this weekend" (the lie he tells himself that closes the tab)
- "I don't want to be that guy posting about his side hustle." (identity-protective, not performance-protective)
- "Just tell me what to actually do, step one, today." (craves specificity over inspiration)
- "Course graveyard" energy even if he doesn't have the phrase — the feeling of a folder full of PDFs he never opened.

## Course-graveyard skepticism — shared spine with Farrice, not a coincidence

He has been burned, or is one scroll away from being burned, by the exact "$10K/mo passive income, copy my system" category the FTC is now tightening (ground-truth doc). He has seen the YouTube exposés. He assumes — correctly, often — that whoever is selling him this is lying about the ceiling and hiding the floor. This is why:

- Any income number in the copy triggers instant distrust, not desire. It is the tell that outs the seller as one of the 40,000.
- A visible person naming the controversy before he can ("yeah, this space is full of scams, here's why I'm not one of them") reads as credible precisely because it's the thing a scammer wouldn't say.
- A coach who tells him who it's NOT for (integrity screen, angle G) is more persuasive than one who says it's for everyone — because "for everyone" is what every scam says.

## What he must believe to buy

1. This person isn't hiding a number from me, because they're not showing me a number at all — they're showing me a process.
2. This is not the 41st version of the same "AI passive income" lie — it does something specific and boring: it removes friction, not effort.
3. The first goal isn't "quit my job," it's one grand — which is small enough that failing at it wouldn't be humiliating, and succeeding at it would prove something real.
4. The reason I've failed before wasn't that I'm the kind of person this doesn't work for — it's that nobody ever told me follow-through is a skill, not a personality trait, and skills can be trained.
5. If I buy this and DM the receipt, a real person who has spent 18 years getting real humans to actually do things is going to hand me something extra — not because I'm special, but because that's the deal.
6. I can do this in the pockets of time I already have (a commute, a lunch break, after the kid's asleep) — not a second job.

## Core objections (the ones he's actually running, not the polite version)

- "This is just another affiliate scheme where you get paid to convince me to buy so you can convince someone else." (True, and must be handled by disclosure + honesty, never denial.)
- "Everyone selling this is selling the exact same thing — why would I buy through you instead of the 40,000 other people with this link?" (Answered directly by the toolkit bonus — never dodge this, name it.)
- "I've done the '30 minutes a day' thing before and it turned into 3 hours and then zero hours." (Follow-through skepticism — this is where the behavior-change credential does real work, not decoration.)
- "AI is just the new 'passive income' lie dressed up in a hoodie." (Must be pre-empted with the anti-hype AI-as-spotter frame, angle D.)
- "If it's really $497, that's real money I have to explain." (Never minimize this. Respect it. Don't oversell past it.)

## The bridge — one line that moves him from where he is to where he needs to be

**"You don't need to believe you can build a business. You just need one small proof that your brain can't argue with — and the system that gets you there in the time you already have between naps, shifts, and everything else."**

This works because it never asks him to identity-shift into "entrepreneur" before he's ready — it asks for one proximal rep (the $1,000 belief-unlock, research doc) inside the exact life he already has (naptime economy, not a second job), and it routes the "why him" objection through credential (behavior-change coach) rather than income proof (which he's inoculated against).

## Do not say — trigger words that cause disgust/distrust in this specific reader

- Any dollar figure attached to potential earnings ("$5K/mo," "$10K," "financial freedom") — hard compliance rule AND a trust-killer for this exact reader, who has seen this number lied to him before.
- "Passive income" used uncritically/positively — he's numb to it as a synonym for "scam," unless it's being deconstructed (angle H does this correctly — naming it as the lie).
- "Escape the 9-to-5" / "fire your boss" / "financial freedom" — hustle-bro register that reads as the exact grift he's guarding against.
- "Guru," "system," (unqualified) "proven method," "step-by-step blueprint" — category-poisoned words; he's seen them used to sell nothing a hundred times.
- "Anyone can do this" / "no experience needed" — the inverse-honesty tell; a real coach disqualifies (angle G), a scammer includes everyone.
- Countdown/urgency language ("only X spots," "offer ends tonight") — instantly reads as manufactured, also a hard compliance violation.
- Income screenshots or implied proof-by-number in any form — compliance-banned and psychologically the single fastest way to lose this specific reader, who has learned to distrust exactly that image.
- "Hustle," "grind," "scale" — also banned in Farrice's own brand voice; doubly wrong here since this reader is tired, not hungry for more hustle-speak, he wants relief from the hustle-speak everyone else is selling him.
