## 1. The Hook (line 1)

> Eighteen years coaching people through changing their bodies, and I never once told a client to find two extra hours in the day. You don't have two hours. I've got a baby monitor clipped to my shirt and thirty minutes before my son wakes up.

**Why this hook, not the others in the bank:** The pure naptime-scene hooks ("The tantrum started at 1:47…") are stronger cold-scroll-stoppers, but they make the SAHD detail the whole premise — that's the narrative arc the chosen frame explicitly forbids. This hook leads with the credential (the thing that actually differentiates him from 40,000 other affiliates), states it as fact rather than status-claim, and folds the naptime constraint in as the second beat — texture proving the credential applies to his own life, not the headline. It's a statement hook (no question), and it opens inside a real moment (a coaching floor, a clipped-on monitor) rather than an abstract claim.

## 4. Why the Behavioral Insight + Mechanic Pairing Works

The insight (nobody trains follow-through, only material) never sits alone — Post 2 immediately converts it into the concrete mechanic (the AI toolkit, and specifically what it does: removes blank page / tech overwhelm / no-next-step). This satisfies the ground-truth counter-read #2 (behavior-coach frame reading as therapy) by never letting the introspective beat run without an action object attached to it in the same breath. Post 3 closes the loop by naming that the mechanic sits on top of a real product (DWA) without pretending it's his own course, and hands the reader forward to where the transaction actually happens.

## 5. Face-Forward Visual Direction (shoot in the naptime window)

**Shot:** Single photo, vertical, taken by Farrice himself (arm's length or propped phone) — not a produced video, not a ring light.

- **Where:** The hallway outside JJ's room, or the kitchen counter, mid-afternoon. Whatever light is actually in the house at nap time — no studio lighting, no gym backdrop, no desk-with-monitors "founder" setup.
- **What's in frame:** Farrice from the chest up, plain shirt, the baby monitor visible clipped to his collar or waistband — the prop that makes the hook's second sentence true on camera, not just in the caption. A half-drunk coffee or a laundry basket in the background is fine; it should look like a house mid-afternoon, not a set.
- **What's NOT in frame:** JJ's face (privacy), any screen showing income numbers or dashboards, anything that reads as "home office" or "content studio."
- **Expression/energy:** Direct to camera, steady, not smiling performatively — the register is "coach across the table," quiet and matter-of-fact, not hype. No exclamation-point energy in the face either.
- **Caption placement:** Text overlay optional; if used, only the hook line ("You don't have two hours. I've got a baby monitor clipped to my shirt and thirty minutes before my son wakes up.") oversized at the bottom third, Muji-plain type, no stickers/emoji.
