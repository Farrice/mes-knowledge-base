## Concept 1: buyer acquisition

### The $100,000 headline

**Who recognizes it:** A prospective first-time buyer who saw an assistance number and silently decided they either qualify or do not.

**Source:** [LACDA Home Ownership Program](https://www.lacda.org/home-ownership/home-ownership-program), checked September 1, 2026. The page says HOP80 and HOP150 can provide up to $100,000 or 20% of the purchase price, whichever is less. It also identifies a second mortgage, 0% interest, deferred repayment, shared equity, income and property rules, education, and a participating-lender requirement.

**Visual headline:** `$100,000 is the headline. these are the rules.`

**Spoken Reel script**

> if you saw “up to one hundred thousand dollars” and immediately decided you qualify... or you don’t... slow down.
>
> lacda’s current page says this help is a second mortgage with zero percent interest, deferred repayment, and shared equity. your income matters. so do the property location, purchase price, homebuyer class, and participating lender.
>
> the big number got your attention. the rules tell you whether it belongs in your plan.
>
> comment “check” and i’ll send you the five questions i’d answer before looking at homes.

**Phone-first visual plan**

- Jen begins seated at a kitchen table with `$100,000` on screen.
- She turns the phone toward the official LACDA page as each condition appears in plain text.
- Return to Jen for the final line. No stock-home montage and no approval claim.

**Human response path**

Jen sends the five-question public checklist, then asks: “are you checking what may be possible, or do you already have a city and price range in mind?” Individual eligibility goes to a participating lender. No sensitive financial information is collected in Instagram DMs.

**What this can create:** A buyer-planning conversation with someone already thinking about affordability. The post does not claim the person is qualified or that a conversation becomes a client.

**Jen approval question:** Would she naturally say “the big number got your attention; the rules tell you whether it belongs in your plan”?

## Concept 2: seller authority

### We changed the price. The offer came the next day.

**Who recognizes it:** A homeowner whose listing is getting activity without producing an acceptable offer.

**Source:** Jen’s own published Canoga Park closing story in the July 25 profile scrape. Her caption says the sellers tested an initial price, adjusted after market response, received an offer the next day, and ultimately closed above list. Jen must confirm the transaction wording and permission before reuse.

**Visual headline:** `we changed the price. the offer came the next day.`

**Spoken Reel script**

> we changed the price. the offer came the next day.
>
> one of our canoga park sellers wanted to test a number they felt comfortable with, so we did. then we watched what buyers actually did.
>
> when the response pointed somewhere else, we adjusted. the home eventually closed above list.
>
> the win came from seeing the signal early enough to act.
>
> if your listing is getting traffic and no offers, send me “pivot.” i’ll show you the four signals i’d read before changing anything.

**Phone-first visual plan**

- Open on Jen reviewing a printed activity report, not standing in front of a house.
- Cut to four unlabeled visual cues: showing count, repeated feedback, competing inventory, and offer activity.
- Use permissioned closing imagery only after Jen confirms the client story can be reused.

**Human response path**

Jen asks for four non-sensitive facts: days active, showing activity, repeated feedback, and any offer history. If the owner wants property-specific advice, the next step is a seller strategy conversation. No automatic valuation or price promise is made.

**What this can create:** A high-intent seller conversation. It demonstrates how Jen thinks under pressure instead of saying she is “strategic.”

**Jen approval question:** Is this the decision she is proud of, and is “closed above list” accurate relative to the final list price?

## Concept 3: local personality and referral memory

### Borrow one Saturday in Porter Ranch

**Who recognizes it:** Someone considering Porter Ranch who has only experienced it through listing pages and quick drive-throughs.

**Source:** [Valley Cultural Foundation’s Boeckmann Park listing](https://valleycultural.org/venue/jane-and-bert-boeckmann-park-porter-ranch/), checked September 1, 2026. It lists a free Nautical Thrust yacht-rock performance on September 19 from 4 to 7 p.m. at 11930 Mason Avenue. Refresh before use.

**Visual headline:** `before you search porter ranch, borrow one saturday.`

**Spoken Reel script**

> before you search porter ranch, borrow one saturday.
>
> there’s a free yacht-rock show at boeckmann park on september nineteenth, from four to seven. go early. notice the drive, where you stop before the music, how arrival feels, and what you do after.
>
> a listing can show you the kitchen. it can’t show you whether your normal weekend works here.
>
> if porter ranch is on your maybe list, comment “saturday.” i’ll send you the little neighborhood field test i use with buyers.

**Phone-first visual plan**

- Jen speaks the hook while walking toward the park entrance.
- Local B-roll: the Mason Avenue sign, park entrance, parking information, and one nearby stop Jen genuinely uses.
- Use the event page as a quick green-screen insert. Do not imply that attending predicts neighborhood fit.

**Human response path**

Jen sends a five-prompt routine check and asks: “which part of your normal weekend are you trying to make easier?” If the person mentions a move, she asks whether they are exploring or planning. Content-only followers receive the resource without a property pitch.

**What this can create:** Local familiarity and referral memory. It is less commercially direct than the buyer and seller concepts, so it belongs in the content mix rather than leading the entire strategy.

**Jen approval question:** Does the music angle feel like her genuine local-life voice, or does it feel engineered?

## Judgment surface

These examples make the system testable:

- **Concept 1 succeeds** if Jen feels credible and useful to a serious buyer without sounding like a loan officer.
- **Concept 2 succeeds** if a homeowner can see Jen’s decision-making ability without a generic “results” claim.
- **Concept 3 succeeds** if Jen’s personality and local knowledge feel inseparable, while the real-estate relevance remains clear.

The system is useful only if Jen would record at least one of these substantially as written. If all three feel like agency copy wearing her voice, the voice-and-conviction layer has failed regardless of the sourcing score.
