# Jen Santulan: Money, Route, Routine

## Four-week local conversation campaign

**Draft date:** September 1, 2026  
**Status:** `DRAFT / NOT READY TO PUBLISH`  
**Campaign window:** September 3–27, 2026  
**Production ceiling:** One flagship Reel and one optional low-lift Story per week  
**Campaign job:** Turn useful local evidence into qualified human conversations without confusing attention with pipeline.

## Campaign idea

Most buyer content begins after someone opens a property app. Jen’s local point of view begins one decision earlier:

> before you choose a listing, get clearer on what you can carry, how you move, and what your actual week looks like.

That becomes a simple four-week arc:

| Week | Flagship Reel | Signal | Conversation keyword | Real-estate memory |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The assistance headline is not your answer | LACDA program page | `PLAN` | Buying power requires current, individual due diligence |
| 2 | What changes September 11 on the G Line path | Metro G Line path notice | `ROUTE` | Test a route against real origin, destination, and schedule |
| 3 | Borrow one Saturday in Porter Ranch | Local live-music event | `SATURDAY` | Observe your routine; do not infer who belongs or what values will do |
| 4 | Money, route, routine | Synthesis of all three | `MAP` | Clarify the life decision before collecting listings |

The cadence is a drafting constraint, not a publishing recommendation. Jen has not approved the opinions, scripts, recording schedule, or cadence.

## Week 1 Local Content Card

### The assistance headline is not your answer

**Intended local person and tension**  
An SFV or Los Angeles renter who sees a large assistance number and wants a clear yes/no answer before understanding the program conditions.

**Source and evidence status**  
[LACDA Home Ownership Program](https://www.lacda.org/home-ownership/home-ownership-program), checked September 1. `VERIFIED WITH CONFLICT`: the page posts income limits effective August 24 and core terms, but its introduction says HOP120 while the landing and detailed copy say HOP150. Exact naming and client fit stay with LACDA or a participating lender.

**Original Jen POV**  
Trust is more useful than a dramatic qualification promise. Jen can show a buyer which questions matter while refusing to turn a public page into personal financial advice.

**Visual headline**  
`the assistance headline is not your answer`

**Camera-ready script**

> if a down-payment-assistance headline made you think you either qualify or you don’t, pause for a second.
>
> lacda’s current page lists income limits, eligible locations, purchase-price limits, a homebuyer course, a participating lender, and repayment terms. and the page currently uses two different program names, so i’m not going to tell you in thirty seconds that you qualify.
>
> i made a five-question plan check so you can see what needs a real answer before you shop. comment “plan” and i’ll send it. then a participating lender can confirm your individual numbers.

**Beat map**

1. Name the tempting shortcut: assistance number equals eligibility.
2. Show the official page and the conditions a short Reel usually omits.
3. Make the source inconsistency visible.
4. Offer a checklist, not a verdict.

**Visual plan**

- 0–3 sec: phone-first talking head; headline fills the upper third.
- 3–12 sec: green-screen crop of the official LACDA page with the update date visible.
- 12–23 sec: fast, readable text cards: `location`, `price`, `course`, `lender`, `repayment`.
- 23–34 sec: back to Jen; on-screen `a checklist, not an approval`.
- Do not show private client numbers or imply an endorsement by LACDA.

**Low-pressure CTA and human bridge**  
Keyword: `PLAN`. Jen manually sends Response Asset A, asks which public criterion is least clear, and routes personal eligibility questions to a participating lender. No income, Social Security number, documents, or other sensitive financial information is requested in Instagram DMs.

**Measures**

- Attention: qualified local reach, 3-second hold, completion, saves, shares, profile visits, `PLAN` requests.
- Pipeline: identifiable replies, checklist conversations, lender-ready introductions with consent, buyer-plan appointments, represented clients, collected revenue.
- Baseline: `NO EVENT` across both layers.

**Receipt**  
Originality: Jen-specific source-literacy POV; no copied wording. Sourcing: official current page, conflict disclosed. Compliance: no approval, payment, eligibility, or appreciation claim. Human checkpoint: Jen voice/POV plus lender review required.

**Optional Story**  
Poll: `which part of assistance is least clear?` Options: `who qualifies` / `what you repay`. Follow with the official link and “general info only; a participating lender confirms your situation.”

## Week 2 Local Content Card

### What changes September 11 on the G Line path

**Intended local person and tension**  
A Valley renter or buyer who walks, bikes, or connects to the G Line and needs to know what changes now, without a real-estate value prediction attached.

**Source and evidence status**  
[Metro’s August 18 newsletter](https://cloud.sfmc.metro.net/TheMetroNewsletter), checked September 1. `VERIFIED`: improvements are scheduled to start September 11, with resurfacing, lighting, striping, wayfinding, bike parking, temporary closures, and a detour between Winnetka and Canoga. Refresh the notice before recording.

**Original Jen POV**  
A local update becomes useful when it changes someone’s actual route. The present detour and future improvements belong in separate sentences.

**Visual headline**  
`your g line path changes september 11`

**Camera-ready script**

> if you use the g line path between winnetka and canoga, your route changes september 11.
>
> metro says path work will include resurfacing, lighting, striping, wayfinding, and bike parking. during the work, parts of the path will close, and cyclists in that stretch are being directed to the la river path.
>
> that tells us what’s changing. it does not tell us your commute, your home value, or whether the route works for you.
>
> comment “route” and i’ll send the official notice plus the three things i’d test at your actual travel time.

**Beat map**

1. Lead with the location and effective date.
2. Distinguish planned improvements from the active detour.
3. Refuse commute and value inference.
4. Invite a personal route test.

**Visual plan**

- 0–4 sec: Jen foreground with a recognizable G Line/path shot; large date card.
- 4–16 sec: safe stationary B-roll of posted path signage and the official notice on screen.
- 16–26 sec: simple split card: `changing now` / `promised improvement`.
- 26–35 sec: Jen talking head with CTA.
- Film from a legal, non-obstructive location; do not film while driving or cycling.

**Low-pressure CTA and human bridge**  
Keyword: `ROUTE`. Jen manually sends Response Asset B and asks for the public origin area, destination area, and approximate travel window the person wants to test. If the conversation becomes property-specific, Jen compares observable route options without rating neighborhood safety, quality, or protected-class composition.

**Measures**

- Attention: qualified corridor reach, completion, saves, shares to a local contact, official-link taps, `ROUTE` requests.
- Pipeline: identifiable route conversations, property-search conversations, buyer-plan appointments, represented clients, collected revenue.
- Baseline: `NO EVENT` across both layers.

**Receipt**  
Originality: current local interpretation in Jen’s voice. Sourcing: official Metro notice. Compliance: no commute guarantee, value claim, or neighborhood ranking. Human checkpoint: Jen voice/POV and recording-day source refresh required.

**Optional Story**  
Question sticker: `what trip would you actually test on this path?` Reply with the official notice, not a blanket commute estimate.

## Manual response kit

These are the promised assets. They make each CTA operational without activating an automation.

### Response Asset A: PLAN

**Jen’s manual reply**

> here’s the five-question plan check i promised:
>
> 1. is the property in an eligible location?
> 2. is the purchase price within the current limit?
> 3. which income and household rules apply?
> 4. what course and participating-lender steps are required?
> 5. how do repayment and any shared-equity terms work?
>
> the official page currently uses two program names, so please confirm the current program and your individual fit with lacda or a participating lender. which of these five is least clear right now?

**Handoff rule:** Give the official link. Do not collect sensitive financial details in DMs. With permission, introduce a participating lender for individual eligibility.

### Response Asset B: ROUTE

**Jen’s manual reply**

> here’s metro’s current notice: https://cloud.sfmc.metro.net/TheMetroNewsletter
>
> for a real route check, test three things at the time you’d normally travel: the current detour, the full door-to-door trip, and a backup option. what origin area, destination area, and time window are you trying to understand?

**Handoff rule:** Share observable route options and current official notices. Do not guarantee commute time or make a neighborhood-quality inference.

### Response Asset C: SATURDAY

**Jen’s manual reply**

> here’s my no-sales-pitch routine check:
>
> - how did the trip there feel at the time you’d normally go?
> - what did parking or arrival actually require?
> - what would you do before or after?
> - what felt easy, and what felt annoying?
> - would this fit a normal month, not just a special event?
>
> which part of your usual weekend are you trying to make easier?

**Handoff rule:** Discuss the person’s stated routine and public facts. Do not rank who belongs, safety, schools, or demographic fit.

### Response Asset D: MAP

**Jen’s manual reply**

> here’s the one-page version:
>
> **money:** what is verified, what still needs a lender, and what monthly or upfront limit is yours?
>
> **route:** what trip matters, when does it happen, and what current route or backup needs a test?
>
> **routine:** what do you repeat every week, and what friction are you unwilling to add?
>
> circle the fuzziest column. which one should we find a factual answer for first?

**Handoff rule:** Answer with a primary source where possible. Bring in a lender, inspector, attorney, or other professional when the question exceeds Jen’s role.
