# Reference Harvest

## What the references have in common

These carousels work because they behave like small editorial products, not decorated information cards.

1. **The photograph carries atmosphere.** Type does not fight the image; the image creates the world and the typography names it.
2. **Two type voices create tension.** A dominant editorial voice and a quieter utility or handwritten voice make the work feel art-directed.
3. **Hierarchy is obvious in half a second.** Every slide has one unmistakable entry point.
4. **Repetition creates identity.** Mastheads, location labels, footer rules, doodles, or edition language recur without making every composition identical.
5. **The decks pace themselves.** Large title slides alternate with detail, reflection, or collage slides. They do not repeat one locked template.
6. **The content is specific enough to feel lived in.** Named places, routines, small moments, and sensory observations outperform generic “tips.”
7. **Restraint creates taste.** Each system uses a tiny visual vocabulary and commits to it.

## 1. After Hours Guide

**Why it works:** Oversized fashion-editorial serif turns an ordinary place photo into a point of view. The title can occupy nearly half the frame because the supporting information remains tiny. Warm, dark photography makes every slide feel collected from one city magazine even when locations change.

**Harvested mechanics:** full-bleed image; 35–50% headline footprint; one compact detail block; small location and series labels; deep shadow veil; no cards; warm interior or golden-hour grade.

**Jen translation:** curated Valley guides, local businesses, premium listing atmosphere, and “what the block feels like” stories. It should make Jen feel discerning and locally fluent.

**Do not copy literally:** café names, supplied copy, exact photo selection, or any template brand name. The transferable asset is the hierarchy and editorial temperature.

## 2. Sunlit Local Notes

**Why it works:** Yellow against dark green creates a recognizable signal across wildly different photographs. Loose white doodles make the work feel noticed by a person, not exported by a corporate template. The composition stays asymmetrical and lightly imperfect.

**Harvested mechanics:** sunny serif title; green/textured street photography; one or two white marks; small handwritten label; location pin; conversational body copy; relaxed asymmetry.

**Jen translation:** local gems, neighborhood routines, parks, coffee, farmers markets, and “one thing I noticed” posts. This is the strongest affinity engine.

**Do not copy literally:** decorative scribbles without meaning. Every mark should point, circle, underline, or move the swipe.

## 3. Quiet Home Editorial

**Why it works:** The design trusts one human image and one thought. White serif type feels timeless while tiny corner labels keep the system disciplined. The emotional power comes from candid scale and negative space, not decoration.

**Harvested mechanics:** intimate portrait or domestic detail; small masthead; large white serif; narrow supporting paragraph; lower shadow fade; calm asymmetric positioning.

**Jen translation:** her point of view, home-life reflections, permissioned client stories, and calm reframes of stressful decisions. This is the strongest trust engine.

**Do not copy literally:** generic inspirational sayings. The sentence must be something Jen has earned through experience.

## 4. Valley Moments

**Why it works:** Bold yellow sans provides speed and clarity; the script phrase provides emotion. Thin rules and a persistent footer create a modern magazine shell. Separated body notes let the image breathe while still producing a narrative beat.

**Harvested mechanics:** bold sans + one script phrase; yellow/white over dark cinematic image; spark marks; top and bottom rules; handle and slide number; two-zone supporting copy.

**Jen translation:** commutes, arrival rituals, weekend rhythm, neighborhood moments, and short “what this changes” stories. This is the strongest narrative-energy engine.

**Do not copy literally:** script as decoration or complete sentences in script. It is an emotional accent, never the information layer.

## 5. Hidden Address Journal

**Why it works:** A vintage serif and edition language turn the deck into a collectible series. Dark photographs provide continuity. Inset frames behave like evidence—small views that make a place feel explored rather than advertised. A yellow arrow or smile prevents the system from becoming precious.

**Harvested mechanics:** edition masthead; butter/ivory serif; dark architectural photo; narrow centered body; two or three rectangular insets; one hand-drawn arrow or smile; no rounded cards.

**Jen translation:** property-plus-neighborhood storytelling, hidden features, address journals, and premium local editions. This is the strongest listing-to-lifestyle bridge.

**Do not copy literally:** invented “hidden gem” claims. A place, feature, or routine must actually justify the label.

## Decision matrix

| Content job | First choice | Second choice |
|---|---|---|
| Local affinity and saves | Sunlit Local Notes | After Hours Guide |
| Jen trust and personal POV | Quiet Home Editorial | Valley Moments |
| Listing atmosphere | Hidden Address Journal | After Hours Guide |
| Neighborhood routine | Valley Moments | Sunlit Local Notes |
| Premium local guide | After Hours Guide | Hidden Address Journal |
| Dense buyer/seller education | Existing Valley Native system | None of these unless the copy is radically compressed |

## Grounding boundary

The source images establish composition, color behavior, hierarchy, motifs, and photographic treatment. Exact original fonts are **UNCONFIRMED** for the four raster packs. The installed Bodoni, Inter, and SignPainter families are deliberately chosen functional analogues, not claims about the Canva source files.
