# Quiet Home Editorial

## Vibe

Intimate, reflective, mature, and human. It should feel like an editorial portrait essay about the life a home supports—not a motivational quote card.

## Use it for

Jen's point of view, permissioned client stories, honest reflections on home and life, and calm reframes of stressful decisions.

## Visual recipe

- One intimate portrait, domestic detail, or candid human moment dominates each slide.
- White old-style serif supplies the emotional voice.
- Tiny corner labels create quiet discipline.
- One narrow paragraph may sit away from the headline.
- Dark lower fade protects copy and gives the image depth.
- Branding stays almost invisible until the viewer looks for it.

Palette roles: ink `#171713`; olive `#4F5440`; stone `#DDD4D7`; sand `#B8A99D`; shadow `#2C2B25`; type `#F8F7F2`.

Typography: Bodoni 72 Book or comparable old-style serif for headlines and short body thoughts; Inter for mastheads and source labels.

## Slide rhythm

1. **Cover reflection:** one sentence Jen has earned.
2. **Human observation:** a specific moment that supports the sentence.
3. **Detail:** the ordinary home behavior most people overlook.
4. **Practical reframe:** what the observation changes in a decision.
5. **Close:** a gentle question or honest next step.

## Allowed variation

Portraits may be full-body, cropped, or environmental. Headlines may span the subject when contrast remains strong. One slide can be object-only—light, keys, a doorway—if it still feels human.

## Failure signs

- Generic inspiration unrelated to Jen's experience.
- Stock-model emotion.
- Loud branding or multiple accent colors.
- Several competing text boxes.
- A “story” without permission or provenance.
