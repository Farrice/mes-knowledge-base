## 2. the five content pillars

### pillar 1 — real programs, real math
**the job it serves:** dissolves *"the programs aren't for someone like me / those lotteries never hit."* names what's open AND what's closed, then does the arithmetic so the help is believable instead of hype.
**example episode titles (her voice):**
- "MyHome explained: the down payment program that's actually open in 2026 (and the one that isn't)"
- "is Dream For All coming back? what the closed lottery means for valley buyers right now"
- "i ran a real $600K valley condo with down payment help: here's every number"

### pillar 2 — is it possible for me? (belief-dissolution)
**the job it serves:** dissolves *"i need 20% down / perfect credit"* and *"i waited too long, i'm 40 and renting."* reframes the wall, then shows a real person who walked through.
**example episode titles:**
- "you don't need 20% down (almost nobody does anymore): here's what you actually need"
- "i'm 40 and still renting. did i miss it? what the numbers actually say"
- "the credit score myth that's keeping valley renters renting"

### pillar 3 — valley neighborhood guides
**the job it serves:** the "where can i actually afford / where's the entry point" job. turns the abstract dream into a real street, a real price band, a real commute. story-led, never a listing tour.
**example episode titles:**
- "where a first-time buyer can actually afford to live in the valley (2026 entry map)"
- "reseda vs canoga park: what your first-home budget really gets you on each side"
- "the foothill question: sylmar, sunland & the insurance line every valley buyer should check first"

### pillar 4 — the buyer journey, demystified
**the job it serves:** kills the fear of the unknown machinery: co-buying, inspection, closing costs, the *new* buyer-agent fee, insurance. the "i don't even know what i don't know" job.
**example episode titles:**
- "the new buyer-agent fee, explained: the cash-to-close line nobody warned you about"
- "buying with your sister, your best friend, your mom: how co-buying actually works in 2026"
- "closing costs in plain english: every dollar between 'offer accepted' and 'keys in hand'"

### pillar 5 — story / behind-the-scenes (the lifestyle equity)
**the job it serves:** the attractive-character engine. her wins, her life, her people. this is the pillar that already outperforms everything 10x. it earns the trust the other four pillars spend.
**example episode titles:**
- "she cried in the kitchen: the day a 3-year wait turned into keys 🥹"
- "a day in the life: showings, a toddler, and EDC weekend (realtor edition)"
- "the clients who became friends: why 'more than just a house' isn't a slogan"

## 5. the atomization map

one long-form video becomes seven assets. the rule for each is *which moment to lift*, not "summarize the video."

| asset | which moment to cut | extraction rule |
|---|---|---|
| **reel 1 — the hook reel** | segment 1 cold-open (0:00–0:20) extended to ~25–35s with the belief-flip punchline from segment 5 stitched on | lift the *exact 1am thought* plus the single reframe line. the property/program never opens it. ends on the emotional beat with one emoji. |
| **reel 2 — the math reel** | segment 4 (the on-screen arithmetic) | lift the single cleanest number comparison (e.g. $3,000 vs $3,758 → $750). one number, one frame, her VO. caption restates it as plain text. this is the "screenshot-and-send-to-my-partner" reel. |
| **reel 3 — the proof reel** | segment 5 belief-flip plus (if a story episode) the client moment | lift the demographic/social-proof stat that makes them feel normal ("40 is the new normal," "37% are co-buying"). pairs the stat with one real human beat. |
| **carousel (IG)** | the teach (segment 3) broken into steps | slide 1 = the hook thought. slides 2–6 = one teaching step each, one number per slide. final slide = soft "let's run your real numbers" + worksheet mention. every adjective on every slide pays rent in a number. |
| **stories (3–5)** | scattered B-roll, one math frame, one caveat line, the CTA | story 1: tease the 1am thought ("posting the answer to this today"). story 2: one math frame. story 3: the honest caveat ("the part i won't skip"). story 4: link sticker to the worksheet. (story 5 optional: a poll, "what's the number in YOUR head? 20% / 10% / no idea"). |
| **facebook post** (/realestatewithjing) | the belief-flip (segment 5) written long | FB skews older and warmer, so lead with the full reframe paragraph in her best-friend register, embed the YouTube link, close with "let's." slightly longer than IG, same voice. |
| **email** ("the real number" list) | the honest caveat (segment 6) plus the CTA | subject = the 1am thought as a question ("is 40 too late to buy in the valley?"). body = the reframe, the one stat, the honest caveat (this is the trust move that drives opens long-term). single CTA: the worksheet. one P.S. = the client win. |

**master atomization rule:** the hook is *always* a person, feeling, or the midnight thought. never the listing, the program name, or the price. the number is the *proof inside*, never the opener. one piece, one number, one emotional beat. if an asset can't trace back to "the down payment isn't the wall, income and access are," it gets cut, not posted.

## 6. the thumbnail formula

**the equation:** `jen's face (emotion) + ≤4-word headline + ONE number`

| element | the rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| **face** | jen, real, mid-emotion (relief, warmth, a knowing look). never stock-smiling, never arms-crossed-agent-pose. occupies left or right third. | her, looking at the viewer like she's about to tell them the truth |
| **headline** | ≤4 words, lowercase or high-contrast caps, the *belief* not the topic | "you don't need 20%" · "is 40 too late?" · "$750 more. worth it?" |
| **the one number** | a single large number that creates the open loop, the curiosity gap | `20%` (slashed through) · `40` · `+$750` · `3.5%` |
| **contrast rules** | one dominant color block behind text (her brand palette), face well-lit against a darker/simpler background, text readable at phone-thumbnail size (~120px wide). | dark valley-dusk background, warm cream text block, one accent for the number |

**hard don'ts (thumbnail):** no red arrows, no shocked open-mouth face, no 🔥/🚨/💯, no five-word-plus headlines, no listing photo as the thumbnail. the curiosity comes from the *number contradicting the belief* (the slashed "20%"), not from manufactured shock. permission, not pressure, even in the thumbnail.

**thumbnail-to-title pairing rule:** the thumbnail carries the *belief* (the emotional hook); the title carries the *search term* (the literal query). together they hit both the "is it possible for me?" feeling and the "first home valley real math" search. never make them say the same thing.
