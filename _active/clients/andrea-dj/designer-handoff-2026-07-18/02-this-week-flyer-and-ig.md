### Post 1 — The Announcement *(hero — post first)*

> "How do you even meet someone in this city anymore."
>
> I've heard it four times this month. Once from a friend who said she'd stopped trying and didn't mean it.
>
> The honest answer is that the rooms are wrong. The apps want the swipe, not the meeting. The bar at 11pm wants you three drinks in before you can walk across it. None of them are built for the thing you actually want.
>
> So I built a different room. It opens at 2pm on Saturday, July 18, at Movement on Montrose. There's no bar. The music is mine, beat to beat. Everyone on the floor applied and said yes to the same thing before they got the address.
>
> You meet someone the way you meet a song. Your body knows before your mind does.
>
> Fifty seats. The application takes about four minutes. If you're a hell-yes, it's in my bio. If you're not sure yet, the next post might be the one for you.
>
> Resonance. Daytime. Sober. Curated. Chicago.

**Image:** Andrea at the decks in real 2pm window light, side profile, hand on the fader — or a wide daytime wood-floor room shot. No overlay text, or a small "Resonance" wordmark top-left. **First comment:** `#chicago #chicagoevents #daytimeparty`

### Post 2 — The Moment *(story / contemplation)*

> A guy came to one of my sets last winter wearing a jacket you only own if you've been collecting them for ten years.
>
> He stood at the back for half an hour. Didn't dance. Listened. When the song turned over — second track, Roy Ayers — I watched his shoulders drop about an inch. Three songs later he was on the floor with his eyes closed, doing nothing for anyone. Just there. Later he was talking to a woman who'd been doing the same thing across the room.
>
> I don't know what came of it. I'm not in the business of promising what happens after.
>
> I'm in the business of building the room where that half-hour at the back is allowed. Daytime. Sober. The music is mine. The floor is curated. You don't have to be the loudest person in it. You can stand at the edge and listen for a song or two, and the room will hold you while you do.
>
> July 18, 2pm, Chicago. Application's in my bio.
>
> What's the song that drops your shoulders an inch?

**Image:** a wide shot of a wood floor in afternoon light, a few pairs of shoes near the edge, no faces — the quiet of a room that just held something. **First comment:** `#chicago #chicagohouse #daytimedanceparty`
