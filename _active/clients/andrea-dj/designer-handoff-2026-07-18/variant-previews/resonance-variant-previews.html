Three ways the same brand can look.

Same room. Same words. Same daytime, sober, curated promise. Only the palette and type change. You already liked the orange one — that's Variant A. B and C are here so you've seen the alternatives and can say "yes, A" with confidence (or surprise yourself).

How to choose: scroll through all three. For each, look at the little Instagram post and flyer. The right one should make you think "that's the room." The other two should make you think "that's a room I respect, but it's not mine." Then reply with one letter: A, B, or C. Your designer builds everything in that palette.

Why A might be the one

It's the most universal and the easiest to execute well — your designer can be confident in it on day one. It's already proven: it's the website you responded to. The risk: it's a register a lot of good brands could land. It wins on warmth and clarity, not on being unmistakably yours.

Why B might be the one

It's the only direction where your specific story does work the system can't fake. Moss-green instead of midnight is the whole move — the color of Latin-American interior shadow at 2pm. It reaches Latin-American Chicagoans and the design-literate directly. The risk: it demands the most discipline to keep from drifting into kitsch.

C

Quiet Luxury Daylight

A Gentlewoman spread on a marble table at 3pm. Cream, sage, warm gold, ink. Terracotta demoted to a single accent.

For the audience that doesn't need to be shouted at. The brand that trusts the room to speak for itself. Hardest to execute well.

Why C might be the one

It's the most grown-up. Ink-on-cream instead of a loud brand color — a brand confident enough to be recognized by its restraint. Most likely to be quoted in press untouched. The risk: it needs the most discipline from a designer, and a weak photo makes the restraint read as "empty" instead of "assured."
