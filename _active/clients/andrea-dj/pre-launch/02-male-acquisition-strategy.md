## The Asymmetric Problem

Most curated singles, wellness, and sober events default to 60-65% female attendance. Daybreaker, The Wing's events catalog, Pure Dating's IRL nights — the public reporting on each skews women-heavy by design and by default. The reason is structural: women in the 30-40 band are more proactively in the contemplation-and-action audience state on dating, are more responsive to non-app singles formats, and have higher tolerance for daytime / sober / curated framings. To hit twenty men in a fifty-person Resonance room, we engineer it intentionally.

The BOS spine carries four structural defenses that are unusually male-friendly: DJ-led music is a gender-neutral draw with built-in competence cues — Andrea is the curator, the room reads as a music room first; anti-wellness positioning closes the taste gap most men recoil from on curated singles events; daytime plus sober strips the bar's permission slip in a way that lands harder for men who are tired of nightlife performance than for women who never trusted it; and the hell-yes filter is equally male-readable, signaling seriousness in a register men trust. The spine does the heavy lifting. The four channels below operationalize the rest.

## The 4 Channels (in priority order)

### Channel 1 — Warm referrals (Andrea + Farrice + JR personally vouch for)

**Dominant avatar reached:** Marcus.
**Target:** 8-12 men.

Each principal (Andrea, Farrice, JR) generates a list of 8-15 men in their network meeting Profile #3 criteria. Standards: the hell-yes test, applied with the additional male-specific filter that this is a man any of you would unhesitatingly want next to your sister, best friend, or colleague in a sober daytime dance room.

Outreach is a warm voice memo or text, not a promotional DM — leading with *"I'm hosting/helping host a thing and you came to mind"* and immediately giving the format in one sentence so the recipient doesn't have to negotiate confusion before deciding. One round per principal, cadence: initial → 5-day nudge → no third nudge (the third nudge converts no one and costs relationship). The list is the asset; cull aggressively before sending.

Why Marcus is dominant in this channel: the founders' personal networks intersect Marcus's lived geography — Chicago Black creative-class circles, NYO and Latin/conservatory alumni, JR's DJ adjacents. Daniel sits outside these networks structurally; he gets reached via Channel 4.

### Channel 2 — Friend-pair mechanics (confirmed women nominate one man each)

**Dominant avatars reached:** both Marcus AND Daniel.
**Target:** 6-8 men.

After Phase 1 women are confirmed, each confirmed female attendee gets one explicit ask, from Andrea, in writing: *"Is there one man in your life — friend, brother, cousin, colleague — who you would vouch for being in this room? Not a date. Someone you would happily see meet someone here."*

The frame is critical: not "bring a guy" (which signals desperation and rates the men as accessories) but "is there one man you would vouch for." Women in our target audience have better social capital with the men we want than direct male marketing has. They know which of their male friends is a hell-yes; they will not nominate someone they would be embarrassed to see in the room.

On the application gate: the +1 nomination question is post-confirmation only, never on the public application form (public-form nominations invite adversarial tourists). Andrea and Farrice approve every nomination; nominated men get a personal outreach from Andrea — not Farrice — with the framing *"[Female nominator] thought you would actually like this. Here's what it is. Here's the date. Let me know."*

This channel is the only one that reaches both Marcus and Daniel at the same yield rate, because the women who become hell-yeses for Profile #1 and #2 each tend to have one of each in their lives.

### Channel 3 — Andrea's music network (NYO Chicago alumni + JR's DJ network + Latin/conservatory adjacents)

**Dominant avatar reached:** Marcus.
**Target:** 4-6 men.

Three sub-channels: (a) Andrea audits her national youth orchestra alumni network for any men 30-40 currently in Chicago, sends a personal note framed around the music and curation lineage — the NYO arc is recognition material, not flattery; (b) JR generates a 10-15 name list of male DJs, producers, and music-scene operators in his network who'd fit the room as attendees, not as guest performers — the DJ Homies frame from the BOS applies, this is community-warming not commission; (c) Andrea audits the Chicago Latin music scene and DePaul/Roosevelt/CCPA male alumni she knows by name or one degree out.

JR's network specifically unlocks Phase 2 expansion — a DJ Homie in the room becomes a co-host, opener, or sponsor-network bridge for Event #2-3.

Why Marcus is dominant in this channel: this is Andrea's music-network territory, and the Chicago music network is heavily Latin and Black creative-class. Daniel appears in this pool occasionally but Marcus is in it structurally.

### Channel 4 — Male-targeted IG content (Daniel-tuned)

**Dominant avatar reached:** Daniel.
**Target:** 2-4 men for Event #1, mostly contemplation-stage warm-up for Event #2-3.

Distinct from female-facing content. Different hooks, different visual proof choices, different language. The male wince-list as guardrails: no manifest, container, sacred, energy-as-marketing-noun, conscious, soul, journey, embodied, feminine/masculine, hold-space, healing, girl-coded language, or "crafted experience"-coded design appropriation.

Visual proof choices: photos that include men in the room visibly relaxed, visibly listening, visibly themselves — not men dancing performatively, not men in vulnerability poses, not men with arms-raised crowd shots. Soundtrack choices on Reels: source music from Andrea's actual sets — no ambient lo-fi, no spiritual house, no instrumental wellness piano.

Cadence: 1x/week of explicitly male-targeted content, separate from the broader IG calendar, served via Stories with the option to swipe to the public application gate.

Conversion expectation: low single digits direct from IG for Event #1; the bigger value is warming the Channel 1 and Channel 2 invitees who scope the IG before saying yes. Why Daniel is dominant in this channel: Marcus will not RSVP from public copy. He will RSVP because his sister or a trusted woman friend texts him. Daniel is the avatar IG can actually convert.

## What we DO NOT do

- Discount male tickets / "girls free" framing — signals cheapness, attracts wrong men, breaks the hell-yes-is-the-floor logic.
- Explicit "we need more guys" messaging — looks desperate, repels Profile #3 specifically.
- Affinity-targeted Facebook/IG ads on gendered interests — too crude, breaks the curation logic by inviting cold strangers into a hand-curated room.
- Bro-coded language to compensate ("doors at 2pm, beers at home") — fails the voice document, fails the brand.
- Bring-a-female-friend incentives that make male attendees feel like accessories.
- Paid promoter networks (anchor Line 7 — no commissioned promoters).
- Any framing that positions men as "the missing element" — every man in the room is a person, not a demographic deficit being solved.
- Name the 60/40 floor ratio in male-targeted copy. The math is operator-internal (Command Center §1, §3, §8); the male-facing copy speaks to curation and presence, never quota.

## What success looks like

- 18-20 confirmed men at door time. Acceptable floor: 16 confirmed men (32% at 50 attendees, or 40% if tightened to 40). Below 16: delay or tighten. We do not pad to hold the headcount.
- 0 attendees who showed up for "the experience" rather than to meet someone. Tourist filter held (ICP master §Section 7).
- 0 men describing the room as "the male presence" rather than as a room they were in. Signal: post-event interviews, men describe the room the same way women do — body, music, specific moments. Not "the vibe," not "the energy," not "the ratio."
- Post-Event #1: 6+ men say they want to come back; 2+ men nominate another man for Event #2 (Channel 2 fed forward).

## Hook Bank — split half-half by avatar

### For Marcus (Channels 1, 2, 3 — warm-introduction copy; rarely public IG)

Marcus does not respond to public copy. These hooks live inside Andrea's personal DM to a Marcus-shaped invitee, a friend's text-forward when she sends Resonance's IG to a man in her life, and the Channel 2 nominee-outreach script. Some are Andrea voice (first-person); some are room voice (third-person or imperative). Frame is noted per line.

2. *"You don't have to be the loudest person in the room to be the one she remembers."* — room voice; Marcus's canonical magnet, preserved.
3. *"My grandparents met at a dance. I'm building a daytime version of that for adults who actually want to meet someone."* — **Andrea voice only**; for Andrea's personal DM to a Marcus-shaped invitee. Not for third-party share.
4. *"This isn't a singles event. It's a room where the music does the introducing and the curation does the rest."* — room voice; any warm-DM context.
5. *"The room is small on purpose. Hell-yes only. The address goes out the morning of."* — room voice; works in any warm-DM context, doubles as a filter line.
6. *"Daytime. Sober. Chicago. The DJ I picked knows what she's doing — and you won't be the only Black man in the room because we already thought about that."* — **Use case: trusted-inviter DMs only.** *(Note: "DJ I picked" preserves Andrea's curatorial authority. Andrea is MCing + hosting Event #1, not performing — see `08-andrea-event-role-doctrine.md`.)* Andrea + Farrice each sending personal invites to Black men in their networks. **Never public IG.** Requires Andrea sign-off before deployment. The implicit-only version (lean on hook #2) is the safe default; this is the sharper instrument when channel context allows.
7. *"A room for people who are tired of formats but not tired of meeting people."* — room voice; works in any warm-DM context, doubles as a Bridge-Message-adjacent.
