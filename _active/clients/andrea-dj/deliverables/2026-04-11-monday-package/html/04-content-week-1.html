The Story Capture System (Build It Before Event #1)

The "we met at Pulse" flywheel only works if you're actually capturing stories. Here's the system — practice it before Event #1 so it's muscle memory on the day.

The Three Capture Points

1. Pre-event: The Intention Card

When guests check in, they get a small card with the timeline + consent agreements. Add one more element: a single-sentence prompt.

"In one sentence: what drew you here today?"

There's a box for them to write in. They hand it back at check-in. You keep them. Later, these become the raw material for "Pulse Couples" posts and testimonials — real words, in real handwriting, from real people who showed up.

2. During: Andrea's Mental Notes

You're running the room. You see things. The two people who caught each other's eye during the witness circle. The group of three who found each other in Act 2 and haven't stopped dancing since. The person who stepped back and cried during the slow song (this will happen — plan for it).

Keep a mental list. During the closing ritual, while everyone's in the circle, you already know the 3-5 people you want to pull aside before they leave.

3. Post-event: The Exit Interview

This is the most important one. 15 minutes before the end of the event (around 4:45pm), you announce:

"If anything moved you today, I'd love to talk to you for sixty seconds on camera as you leave. No pressure, totally optional. Just real talk about what you felt."

Set up a phone on a tripod in the exit corridor. One friend (or you) holds it. Three questions, same for everyone:

Exit Interview Script

  1. "What was the moment that surprised you today?"
  2. "Did you meet anyone you want to see again? You don't have to name them."
  3. "If a friend asked you tomorrow what Pulse is, what would you tell them?"

Rules:
- 60 seconds each. Strict. No monologues.
- Phone stays horizontal (16:9 or 9:16 — pick one and be consistent).
- Record audio separately if possible (AirPods into phone).
- No follow-up questions. Let silences be silences.
- Do not post the interviews immediately. Review them Sunday, pick the three strongest, edit them down to 15-20 seconds each, post one per week over the next three weeks.

The gold is the couples. If two people meet and exchange numbers at Event #1, you need their story. Ask them at the end. Exchange contact info. Text them a week later: "Hey — how's it going? Would y'all be willing to share how you met at Pulse if it turns into something?" You don't need them to have a full relationship yet. You just need them to confirm "we met at Pulse."

That's the first Pulse Couple. One is all you need to start the flywheel.

Story (the IG feature) Strategy

Post to stories every day you have something real to share. Target: 3-5 stories/day during Weeks 1-4, building to daily during Weeks 5-9.

Story content buckets:

  1. Behind the scenes — prepping, venue scouting, playlist building, Andrea's morning routines
  2. Andrea's curation — short clips of songs she's considering for the set, her explaining why a song made the cut, her reacting to music she loves. The "music as permission slip" angle.
  3. Polls and questions — "What song wrecks you emotionally?", "When's the last time you danced sober?", "Would you come to a daytime dance party?"
  4. Countdowns — "X days until the first room"
  5. Community — reposts of attendees who share after committing ("Can't wait for Pulse")

Story rule: If it's real, share it. If it's posed, don't. The whole point is that stories are the unpolished layer that makes the feed feel alive.
