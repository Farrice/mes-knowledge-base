# Aesthetic References v2 — Party-Coded Extension

> Extends `aesthetic-references.md` (v1). v1 is museum-coded (Blue Note, NYRB, Tyler Mitchell, Gentlewoman) — it gives the **visual register**: palette, type, geometry, restraint.
> v2 is **party-coded** — it gives the **energy register**: body language, connection signals, how-bodies-actually-look-when-they-want-to-be-there.
> **Use both.** A photograph that follows v1 without v2 is a museum catalog. A photograph that follows v2 without v1 is a club flyer. Resonance is the intersection.
>
> *Last updated 2026-05-25. Confidence labels: VERIFIED = primary source confirmed via URL. LIKELY = single source / reasonable inference. UNCONFIRMED = could not verify; flagged for Andrea to confirm before use.*

---

## What Failed in v1 (and Why v2 Exists)

The hero-shot prompts pulled from v1 references generated "group fitness class for elderly people" output — rated 0/10. Root cause: v1 references are dominated by *single-subject* portraiture (Leibovitz, Tyler Mitchell), *institutional design* (Blue Note, NYRB), and *fashion-photographer composition*. None of them teach the AI how **multiple bodies look when they are connected to each other** — proximity, gesture toward another person, mid-conversation laugh, hand on shoulder.

v2 fills that gap with footage and stills of people *actually* in motion together, in daylight, sober-coded, in real architectural rooms.

## Group 10 — Film Energy (How to Frame Connection)

**F1. *Climax* (Gaspar Noé, 2018) — opening dance sequence.** [VERIFIED]
- Cinematographer: Benoît Debie. Single-take five-minute opening.
- Reference: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o47gi3-l-3U`
- *What it teaches:* The camera rises, circles, dips — finding bodies. The lens stays *with the dancers*, not behind the DJ. **The single most important framing lesson for Resonance crowd photography.** Caveat: *Climax* turns nightmarish — Resonance only borrows the opening's joyful body-density.

**F2. *Beats* (Brian Welsh, 2019) — climactic rave sequence.** [VERIFIED]
- Glasgow warehouse, 1,500 background actors, monochrome filter that bleeds color at the emotional release.
- Reference: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beats_(2019_British_film)`
- *What it teaches:* Two friends hugging in the middle of a room full of strangers. The *emotional* energy of dance — not the production energy. Pull the embrace + the wide-room frame.

**F3. *Y Tu Mamá También* (Alfonso Cuarón, 2001) — daytime party + pool scenes.** [VERIFIED]
- Cinematographer: Emmanuel Lubezki. Handheld, documentary-realist, natural light.
- *What it teaches:* What a Mexican daytime gathering looks like *in real light* — relaxed bodies, conversation, dance-as-secondary-to-being-there. This is the Latin-daytime cinematic register Resonance wants.

**F4. *In the Mood for Love* (Wong Kar-wai, 2000).** [VERIFIED]
- Cinematographers: Christopher Doyle + Mark Lee Ping-bing. Corridor framing, two-body proximity, shallow depth of field.
- *What it teaches:* How to frame **two people noticing each other** in a room. Resonance's hero shots are not group shots — they are *two-body recognition moments inside a group room*. Wong + Doyle invented the grammar for this.

**F5. *Pose* (FX, 2018–2021).** [VERIFIED]
- *What it teaches:* Community-formation through dance. Ballroom culture. How to frame a room where every body is *known by name* by every other body — opposite of stadium-anonymity. Caveat: ballroom is not Resonance's mechanic; the *recognition register* is what transfers.

**F6. *Babylon* (Damien Chazelle, 2022) — opening party only.** [VERIFIED]
- *What it teaches:* The energy of a room of people who are *actually having a real time*. Caveat: it's maximalist and 1920s-coded; **only pull the body-density and motion-blur from the wide shots**, not the production design.

## Connection Cues (Specific Body Language to Capture)

- One body leaning in to whisper to another, both mid-laugh
- Two bodies facing each other on the dance floor, the DJ blurred in the background (not facing the DJ — facing each other)
- Hand on shoulder, gaze on a third person (active social attention)
- Hand on the small of a back, consensual partner-dance frame
- Eye contact between two people across a small room, mid-recognition
- Mid-laugh between strangers — teeth visible, eyes crinkled, head tilted toward each other
- Multiple bodies blurred in motion, intermingled (not lined up)
- A body in the foreground laughing, another body in the middle distance also reacting — proves the laugh is real
- One person in conversation with two others — three-body social geometry
- A still body in the frame's edge, watching, *also looking happy to be there* — wallflower as participant, not as exile

## Disconnection Cues (Kill on Sight)

- Backs of heads in a line (yoga-class lineup)
- All bodies facing the DJ in a stadium-pose (anonymous fan, not connected human)
- Solo body isolated in the center of the frame, no other bodies in soft focus
- Hands-in-air arena pose (festival grammar)
- "Smile at the camera" group portraits
- Posed in-line wedding-style shots where everyone is performing for the lens
- Bodies in identical wellness uniforms (white linen lineup = retreat, not party)
- AI-perfect lighting on every face (real rooms have shadows; perfect even light = AI tell)
- Hands holding phones (kills the mechanic — Resonance is a phones-down room)
- Glass-of-wine signaling (Resonance is sober; any cocktail visual is a kill)

### Scaffold A — "Editorial Broadsheet"
*v1 spine: Tyler Mitchell + Gentlewoman. v2 spine: Boiler Room Tenaglia Loft (B1) + Wong Kar-wai (F4).*

> Documentary-realist photograph of two adults in their early-30s on a Chicago loft dance floor in mid-afternoon, soft natural light coming through a tall industrial window left of frame. They are facing each other, one mid-laugh with head tilted slightly back, the other's hand on the first's shoulder. Around them, other bodies are softly out of focus, dancing. Wood floor, cream walls, no club fixtures. Wardrobe: linen + earth tones. Shot on 35mm at f/2, slight motion blur on the soft-focus crowd. Color palette: cream, terracotta, midnight blue accents. **Reference: Christopher Doyle's two-body framing in *In the Mood for Love*, body density of Boiler Room Tenaglia Loft session 03/09/2013, palette of v1 Casa Wabi / Tyler Mitchell.** Avoid: hands in air, faces toward camera, phones, wellness-retreat aesthetic.

### Scaffold B — "Latin-American Daylight"
*v1 spine: Casa Wabi + Círculo Mexicano. v2 spine: Cercle Adana Twins at Palais Longchamp (C1) + Y Tu Mamá También (F3).*

> Photograph of a daytime dance floor in a sun-drenched stone-and-wood Chicago loft. Mixed-age adults, 30–45, in earth-tone linen + cream. Two figures in foreground in conversation, one's hand at the small of the other's back in a social-dance frame. Late afternoon light, terracotta wall in the background. The DJ booth is barely visible in deep background, slightly out of focus. Handheld documentary-realist framing à la Emmanuel Lubezki in *Y Tu Mamá También*. **Reference: Cercle Adana Twins crowd framing at Palais Longchamp (Oct 2019), Lubezki natural-light handling, v1 Casa Wabi architectural palette.** Avoid: jungle, lasers, polychrome lighting, Coachella-coded festival wardrobe.

### Scaffold C — "Quiet Luxury Daylight"
*v1 spine: Gentlewoman + Aimé Leon Dore. v2 spine: Cercle Lee Burridge Omnia Bali (C2) + Boiler Room Four Tet/Floating Points (B5).*

### Scaffold D — "Two-Body Recognition"
*v1 spine: Annie Leibovitz quiet portraits + Nan Goldin daytime frames. v2 spine: *In the Mood for Love* (F4) + *Beats* (F2) embrace.*

Bodies of other dancers blurred around them. Natural window light, terracotta wall, wood floor. Shallow depth of field, 50mm lens, f/1.8. **Reference: Doyle's corridor-glance framing in *In the Mood for Love*, the embrace beat in *Beats* (2019), the no-permission-asked closeness of Nan Goldin's daytime kitchen frames.** Avoid: kissing (too sexual for the mechanic), staring (too intense), looking-at-camera (kills the moment).

*v1 spine: Sandro Miller Chicago environmental portraiture + Blue Note layout. v2 spine: Public Records IG documentation + Cercle architectural framing.*

Light pours in from upper-left, casting long warm shadows. Crowd is mixed-age (30–45), mixed-race, in earth-tone wardrobe. Two clusters visible — one of three people in conversation foreground-right, another of four dancing in middle distance. **Reference: Public Records NYC daytime outdoor programming as documented on @publicrecordsnyc, Cercle's wide architectural framing (Palais Longchamp / Omnia Bali), Sandro Miller's Chicago environmental portraiture.** Avoid: club lighting, lens flare, anything that signals "venue" over "room."

## How to Use This Document

**For a photographer:** Bring 2 frames from v1 (Group 1–2) + 2 frames from v2 Group 6 (Boiler Room Tenaglia Loft + one Cercle architectural) + 1 still from Group 10 (*In the Mood for Love* corridor or *Beats* embrace). Five total references. That's the pre-shoot scout deck.

**For an AI-image generator:** Use one of the five scaffolds above verbatim. Generate 3 variants. Frankenstein. Re-prompt with explicit references to specific Group 6/9/10 entries when the output drifts toward wellness/yoga/festival aesthetics.

**For Andrea or Farrice in review:** Hold the proof against the **Connection Cues / Disconnection Cues** checklist before anything else. If three Connection Cues are present and zero Disconnection Cues are present, the photograph is approvable. If any Disconnection Cue is present, kill the proof immediately regardless of how "pretty" the lighting is.

**For the next round of hero-shot generation specifically:** Use Scaffolds A and D first. They are the most precise translation of the brand mechanic (two-body recognition inside a room) into a prompt. Save C for "atmosphere" shots, B for the Latin-heritage flag, E for wide hero/promo.
