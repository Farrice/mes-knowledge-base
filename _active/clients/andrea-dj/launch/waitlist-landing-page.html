The Room

Saturday. 2pm. Chicago.

01

You walk into a sunlit studio on a Saturday afternoon. No bar. No name tags. No icebreaker games. Just music, dancing, and the people who said yes to being here.

02

You notice someone across the floor. The way they move. The way they close their eyes when the song shifts. You don't need a pickup line. Your body already started the conversation.

03

Fifty people. That's it. I keep it small because the room only works when everyone in it chose to be there. Not stumbled in. Chose it.

04

I pick every song. Every transition. The music isn't background — it's what moves you toward the person you're about to meet.

"You've left a thousand rooms with a phone full of contacts and no one to call. This is not that room."

What to expect
when you walk in

A studio with hardwood floors and big windows. Afternoon light. Fifty people dressed the way they'd dress for a Saturday they were looking forward to. Music I picked, song by song, because every transition matters when no one in the room is drunk.

No icebreakers. No rotations. No one telling you when to switch partners. You dance. You notice someone. Maybe you move a little closer. Maybe they smile first. The music is doing half the work — you just have to show up and let it.

Three hours. 2pm to 5pm. You'll leave in daylight. You'll probably exchange a number with someone whose name you learned after you already liked them. That's the whole idea.

This is not speed dating with a DJ. This is not a singles mixer with a dance floor. If you've been to one of those and left feeling worse than when you arrived, you already know why I built something different.

Why It's Curated

The room only works
if the right people are in it.

Everyone who walks through the door filled out an application. I read every one of them myself. Not a form that auto-approves — a real read. I'm looking for people who are serious about meeting someone, not people looking for a fun Saturday activity.

The room is fifty people because fifty is the number where you can still notice the person standing by the window. Go bigger and you lose that. I'd rather run ten events of fifty than one event of five hundred.

If you're a hell-yes to this, I want you in the room.
If you're a maybe, the next one might be yours.
