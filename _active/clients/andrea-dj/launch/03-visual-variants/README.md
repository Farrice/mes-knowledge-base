## What This Folder Is

Three strategic visual positions for the same brand, each pushed to its fullest expression. The three are NOT minor color swaps — they are different visual languages for the same room. They share the daytime mechanic, the photo-led discipline, the 11pm test, and the ban list (no hot pink, no club neon, no AI faces, no rounded pill buttons). They differ in **color sourcing**, **typographic register**, **geometry**, and **photographic composition emphasis**. The task: pick the one that reads as the room Andrea is actually building.

Each variant is in its own DESIGN.md file alongside this README. The three are equally deployable — once chosen, the winning variant is promoted to canonical, the BOS visual layer (`01-visual/DESIGN.md`, `brand-library-entry.md`, `component-tokens.md`) is regenerated, photography rules are updated where the variant changes them, and all IG/flyer/ticket templates are redesigned to the chosen variant.

## What Each Variant Costs to Maintain

Production complexity is real. The variant that wins becomes the daily-template variant — IG posts, flyers, tickets, emails, press one-sheeters — and the lifetime production cost varies between them.

| Cost dimension | Variant A | Variant B | Variant C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type licensing** | GT Sectra (Grilli Type — commercial license required; ~$300 first weight, ~$700–$1,200 family) OR shipping with Mortise / IBM Plex Serif (free) | Tiempos Headline (Klim Type Foundry — ~$120 single style, ~$590 family) and/or Canela (Commercial Type — ~$140 single, ~$960 family). Söhne is Klim, similar tier. OR ship with GT Sectra fallback + Inter. | Domaine Display (Klim Type Foundry — ~$120 single style, ~$590 family) OR Söhne Breit (Klim). Both commercial. OR ship with Tiempos / GT Sectra fallback + Inter. |
| **Designer skill required** | Moderate. Editorial-broadsheet discipline is well-documented; many designers have shipped this register. | High. Architectural discipline requires designerly fluency in 12-column grids and willingness to enforce 0px-radius rigidity. Latin-American kitsch is the most common failure mode — must be policed by reviewer. | Highest. The restraint *looks easy* and is the easiest to execute lazily. Small type at light weight on cream requires photographic selection and copy quality to carry — the system has nowhere to hide. |
| **Photo direction effort** | Standard. Body-mid-gesture composition; existing photographer brief in `photography-rules.md` is calibrated for this register. | Moderate-high. Requires environmental composition with room + architectural detail. Photographer needs to be briefed on the *room as second protagonist* principle and the Latin-American daylight color science. | High. Tight-cropped intimate-essayistic frames require a photographer fluent in the *gesture-as-narrative* register (Annie Leibovitz / Nan Goldin / Tyler Mitchell). Mid-tier event photographers will default to wider environmentals. |
| **Daily template throughput** | Easy. The system is forgiving — a slightly wrong type size on an IG post still reads broadly correct. | Moderate. Strict grid alignment makes templates faster *once built*, but template-building requires architectural discipline. | Moderate-hard. Whitespace and restraint mean each daily post takes more design thought, not less — there is no decoration to fall back on. |
| **Scaling to sub-brands** (per Non-Negotiable §1/§2 — variations require new names) | Easy. Sub-brand variants can be color-shifts of the same editorial register. | Moderate. Sub-brand variants would need to honor the same architectural discipline; the discipline becomes the family signature. | Hard. The restraint is so specific that sub-brand variants risk reading as the same brand again. May require sub-brands to use a *different* register entirely. |
| **Risk of category sameness** | Low-moderate. The editorial-broadsheet register is well-trodden but Resonance's color science differentiates. | Lowest. The architectural Latin-American daylight register is essentially unclaimed by competitor categories. | Moderate. Quiet-luxury is increasingly crowded (the post-2020 hipster-luxury aesthetic). Variant C's discipline must differentiate Resonance from generic quiet-luxury. |
| **Print fidelity** | High. Cream + terracotta + midnight reproduce reliably in CMYK on uncoated stock. | Highest. The warmer cream + moss-green palette has the widest tonal range and reproduces beautifully on print. | Moderate. The cooler cream and the ink-vs-cream contrast must be print-tested — pure ink against cool cream can vibrate optically if not properly proofed. |

**Recommendation:** All three are production-feasible. Variant A is the lowest-risk lift; Variant C is the most-spacious; Variant B is the most-distinctive-in-its-category.

## Selection Criteria — Questions Andrea + Farrice Answer Together

Read each variant DESIGN.md, look at the sample applications, then run these questions. There are no wrong answers — there is the answer that names the room Andrea is actually building.

1. **Literary, architectural, or restrained?** Which one of the three atmospheres reads as the room? (Literary = A. Architectural = B. Restrained = C.)

2. **Does Andrea want the heritage made visible?** If yes — Andrea's Costa Rican formation, the music school, the orchestra she expected vs. the one she found — does she want that visible in the visual layer? (Yes, made architecturally → B. Yes, made through voice only → A or C.)

3. **Does the brand-primary feel like a *color* or like *ink*?** (Terracotta-as-primary = A or B. Ink-as-primary = C. This is the single most decisive question across the three variants.)

4. **Does the audience get treated as readers or as viewers?** A reader is trusted with smaller type, more whitespace, slower pacing (C). A viewer is given confident type, generous bleed, declarative scale (A). A reader-with-architectural-eye is given disciplined geometry and a *room* to walk through (B).

5. **How loud should the system be from across the room?** (Across-the-room readable → A. Architectural at any distance → B. Quiet up close, invisible from far → C.)

6. **Which photographer's work does the room look like?** Sandro Miller / Tyler Mitchell editorial (A). Aimé Leon Dore lookbook / *Apartamento* feature (B). Annie Leibovitz quiet single-subject / Nan Goldin daytime frame (C).

7. **What does Andrea want the press one-sheeter to feel like?** A vinyl-jacket front-and-back (A). An architectural building plaque (B). A magazine feature (C).

8. **The gut test.** Andrea pulls up the three sample IG posts side-by-side, the three sample tickets side-by-side, the three sample portraits side-by-side. Which one makes her say *that is the room*? The other two should make her say *that is a room I respect but is not the room.*

If after running all eight questions Andrea is between two variants, the recommendation is to default to the more conservative of the two (the variant easier to maintain at daily-template scale over 12 events) unless the more distinctive one passes the gut test decisively.

## What Changes After We Pick One

Once a variant wins, this folder produces the following downstream artifacts (this is the implementation plan, not a hypothetical):

### Immediate (within 48 hours of decision)
1. **Promote winner to canonical.** The chosen variant's DESIGN.md replaces (or is committed alongside) `01-visual/DESIGN.md` as the v1.1 anchor. The other two variants stay in `launch/03-visual-variants/` as documented alternatives — never delete them; they are useful for sub-brand evolution decisions later.
2. **Refresh `brand-library-entry.md`** at `01-visual/brand-library-entry.md` to reflect the new color tokens, typography stack, and visual tags. Also propagate to `knowledge/design-libraries/brands/resonance/` if that entry has been published.
3. **Regenerate `component-tokens.md`** at `01-visual/component-tokens.md`. The IG / flyer / ticket / email / press one-sheeter / web hero templates all redraw against the new tokens. Variant B requires the most regeneration (squared geometry); Variant C requires the most padding-discipline updates.
4. **Update `photography-rules.md`** §4 (Composition Principles) to match the winning variant's photographic emphasis. Variant A: body + gesture decentered. Variant B: room as second protagonist. Variant C: intimate-essayistic crops.

### Within 1 week
5. **Re-shoot or re-select** the hero photographs against the winning variant's composition emphasis. If Variant C wins, intimate-essayistic crops are needed — existing photographs may need re-cropping rather than re-shooting.
6. **Redesign the Event #1 / July launch flyer**, IG announcement post, and event ticket against the new variant.
7. **Update the venue pitch one-pager** template against the new variant.
8. **Brief the Event #1 photographer** on the variant-specific composition emphasis ahead of the July event.

### Long-running
9. **Sub-brand evolution paths** (Resonance Series, Resonance Salon, Resonance Retreat — per Non-Negotiable §1 and §2) inherit the winning variant as the family signature, then differentiate within the family.

## Validation Notes

### WCAG contrast (all three variants verified by anchored math against the canonical palette)
- All body text combinations clear AA (≥ 4.5:1) on the variant's neutral background.
- All primary CTAs clear AA-Large (≥ 3.0:1) at ≥ 18px or AA (≥ 4.5:1) where the CTA size permits.
- Recommended: run `npx @google/design.md lint` against the winning variant once promoted; any drift between this README's calculations and the lint output is reconciled in the variant DESIGN.md, not in the README.

### Non-Negotiables check
- **§1 Daytime:** All three variants enforce the 11pm test as the photographic floor. Image prompts are daytime-locked.
- **§2 Sober:** No alcohol appears in any sample or prompt across any variant.
- **§3–§5 Curated/founder-led:** No variant introduces signage, sponsor placements, or curation-suggesting elements that contradict founder-curated decisions.
- **§6 Music curated by Resonance:** No variant introduces sponsor-stage or guest-DJ-from-partner-brand visual conventions.
- **§7 No promoters paid per ticket:** N/A to visual.
- **§8 Phones off the floor:** No phones in any prompt or sample composition.
- **§9 Stories over metrics:** No variant introduces metric-display UI (follower counts, attendance counters, etc.).
- **§10 Chicago-first:** All variants are Chicago-rooted in their photographic environment.
- **§11 No coupled gatecrashers:** N/A to visual.
- **§12 No sponsor that requires compromise:** N/A to visual.

### Banned aesthetics check (all three)
- No hot pink. No club neon. No pure white. No black-and-chrome. No stylized golden-hour Instagram filter. No drop shadows / glass-morphism / neon glow / animated gradients / kinetic typography. No pill-shaped (rounded.full) buttons. No hand-script as default tone. No frenetic motion (200-320ms ease-out across all variants; no bounce, no spring). No AI faces in front-of-house imagery.

### Differentiation check
- **Variants are visually distinct.** A vs. B: different secondary anchor color (midnight vs. moss), different display typeface (GT Sectra vs. Tiempos/Canela), different geometry (2px vs. 0px), different photographic emphasis (body-gesture vs. room-environmental). A vs. C: different brand-primary (terracotta vs. ink), different type weight (500 vs. 400), different scale (72px hero vs. 56px), different photographic emphasis (decentered editorial vs. intimate-essayistic). B vs. C: different palette tone (warm-Latin vs. cool-restrained), different geometry (0px architectural vs. 4px softened), different brand-primary (terracotta+moss vs. ink), different typeface family. **The three could not be confused for one another by a literate viewer.**
