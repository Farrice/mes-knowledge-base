# Section A — The Playbook Overview

## The 2-3 Week Sprint

Today is 2026-05-25. The venue lock deadline is 2026-06-02. Event day is Saturday July 18 (default) or July 25 (backup). The sprint runs in three phases:

| Phase | Window | What lands |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 — Surface and seed** | Days 1-5 (May 25-29) | Network inventory done. Tier 1 friend asks out (4-6). Tier 2 cold pitches out (8-10). Tier 3 Plan B candidates named privately. |
| **Phase 2 — Convert and triage** | Days 6-10 (May 30-June 3) | Replies coming in. Site visits booked. Friend-track and paid-track each have 1-2 live candidates. Decision gate at Day 10. |
| **Phase 3 — Lock or activate Plan B** | Days 11-14 (June 4-7) | One venue signed OR Plan B (house party) activated. Confirmation in writing. Calendar locked. |

The math: a 14-day funnel needs 12-15 contacts to produce 3-5 serious conversations to produce 1 firm yes. The Friend track is higher-conversion (warm leads convert at 30-50%), the Paid track is lower-conversion (cold leads convert at 5-15%), so we work both simultaneously to hedge.

## The Two-Track Model

Two paths to the same outcome — a locked room by June 2. They run in parallel and they require different muscles.

**Friend-with-Space Track (PRIORITY — Section B)**
- Warm leads. People Andrea already knows.
- Lower friction, faster yes, lower cost (often free or barter).
- Higher relational stakes — getting the ask wrong damages the friendship.
- Conversational register. DM, text, voice memo, coffee.
- Andrea owns this track. Farrice supports.

**Paid Venue Track (PARALLEL — Section C)**
- Cold leads. Venues Andrea has no relationship with.
- Higher friction, slower yes, real cost ($500-$1,500 typical).
- Lower relational stakes — a "no" from a stranger is information, not damage.
- Polished register. Email, contract, COI, deposit.
- Farrice owns the volume execution. Andrea owns brand-fit judgment + tour walkthroughs.

## Decision Tree — Which Track to Push When

When you're standing at a crossroad on any given day of the sprint, this is the logic:

```
Has Andrea sent her Tier 1 friend asks yet?
├── No → Send them today. Friend-track gets first move because the relational warm-up takes longer than a cold pitch.
└── Yes → Continue:
    │
    Is a friend-with-space lead actively in conversation (replied within 48 hours, asking specific questions)?
    ├── Yes → Push the friend-track lead to a site visit / specific commitment this week.
    │          Paid-track stays warm in the background. No new paid pitches until friend-track resolves.
    └── No → Push the paid-track. Volume of cold pitches becomes the move.
              Friend-track stays warm but the burden of conversion has shifted.

Has any track produced a firm verbal yes by Day 10?
├── Yes → Move to contract / written confirmation. Stop new outreach. Convert the yes.
└── No → Escalate. Activate Plan B candidate conversations (Section E). Friend-track gets the
         awkward-conversation playbook (Section B.6). Paid-track gets the negotiation talking points
         (Section C.5).
```

## The Single Most Important Principle

> **The room is the curation. The space serves the room, not the other way around.**

Every decision in this playbook runs through that sentence. A space that requires us to soften the curation — to invite people we wouldn't otherwise invite, to let a friend bring their own people, to add alcohol because the venue's licensed for it, to take a Thursday because the venue can't do Saturday — that space is the wrong space, regardless of how beautiful it is or how cheap it is.

Andrea is curating fifty hell-yes people for a daytime sober dance party. The room serves that. If a venue option threatens that, the option is wrong. This is not stubbornness; it's the moat. The flagship name carries the mechanic. Sub-brands carry their own.

When in doubt — when the math is tempting, when the friendship is generous, when the deadline is close — re-read the 12 Non-Negotiables. They were written for these moments.

# Section B — Friend-with-Space Track (PRIORITY)

## B.1 — The Friend-with-Space Filter

Before approaching any friend about their space, Andrea answers these 8 questions privately. If three or more answers are "no" or "I don't know," the friend is not a Tier 1 ask — they're a Tier 2 ask (introduction to someone they know, not their own space).

1. **Does this friend's space have real daylight (windows in the main room, not just skylights or transom panes)?**
   The whole format depends on people seeing each other in real light at 2pm. A basement loft with a single light well is a no. A studio with a wall of west-facing windows is a yes. If you can't answer, you haven't been in the room enough times to ask.

2. **Can the space hold 40-50 people comfortably with floor space to dance?**
   The math: about 800 sq ft of open floor for 50 people dancing, plus another 200-300 sq ft of perimeter for the photographer, the phone basket, the drinks station, and people standing/talking between songs. A space that holds 50 standing-room only is too small.

3. **Is the floor surface dance-ready?**
   Wood, polished concrete, dance-studio sprung floor, tile that won't slip — yes. Plush carpet, area rugs over hardwood, parquet that creaks under weight, painted concrete with a sealant that gets sticky when warm — no. If you'd hesitate to do a quick spin in heels on it, attendees will hesitate too, and the body-first mechanic dies.

4. **Is this friend comfortable with their address being known to ~50 vetted strangers?**
   The address goes out 24 hours before to confirmed attendees only. But once it's out, it's out. A friend whose space is also where they live, who has a partner who doesn't know about this yet, whose neighbors are sensitive about strangers in the building — that friend's space is not a yes, even if the friend says yes.

5. **Does this friend's neighborhood support a 2pm-5pm Saturday gathering?**
   Three sub-questions: (a) Is there parking or a bus/L line within 5 minutes? (b) Will neighbors tolerate 3 hours of music at indoor-event volume, on a Saturday afternoon? (c) Is the area safe enough that women arriving alone won't text Andrea asking "is this the right block?" If any of those three is a no, the space is wrong.

6. **Will the friend hold the curated frame or will they want to "invite their own people"?**
   This is the single biggest friend-track risk. A friend who says yes to the space but adds "and I'll bring a few of my friends too" has just dissolved the curation. Resonance is fifty people Andrea said yes to one at a time. If the friend can't honor that, the answer is no — even though the answer is hard to give.

7. **Can the friend be present (host, vouch) or absent (just hand over the space)?**
   Both work, but they're different events. Present friend = warmer room, friend introduces Andrea, gets a host honorific in the recap. Absent friend = cleaner curation, Andrea controls every variable, friend gets rental rate + thank-you. Either is fine; ambiguous is not. Decide before the ask.

8. **Has this friend been to one of Andrea's events or sets before?**
   If yes, they have a felt reference for the kind of room Andrea builds and the ask is easier. If no, the ask needs to include "I'll send you the manifesto / a recording / a video of my last set" so the friend can calibrate what they're saying yes to. Don't ask a friend to host a room they've never been in.

If a friend passes 6 or more of these, they're a Tier 1 ask. 4-5, they're a Tier 1 conversation but not a confident ask — talk it through over coffee first. 3 or below, they're either a Tier 2 introduction (do they know someone with the right space?) or a Tier 3 Plan B candidate for a smaller house event.

### B.3.c — In-Person / Coffee Version

For the meaningful conversations — the ones where the friendship matters more than the space, where Andrea wants to be in the room when she asks. Not a script — talking points. Andrea reads them once before the coffee, then puts the phone away.

**Talking points (memorize these, then sit with the person):**

1. **Open with where you are.** "I'm doing the first Resonance event. Mid-to-late July." Don't over-build. They've heard you talk about this.

2. **Name the room you're trying to build.** One sentence. *"It's the daytime, sober, curated thing — about 50 people, Saturday afternoon, no bar, music is mine, all vetted before the address goes out."* This is voice pattern 3 (crystallized phrases) compressed into speech.

3. **Name why you're asking them specifically.** Two sentences max. *"I'm thinking about your space because [the daylight / the floor / how it felt at your last thing / what you've built there]. And I trust you to hold the curation with me."*

4. **Make the ask.** *"Could your space be the room for this? July 18 or 25, 2-5pm Saturday. I'd handle everything — sound, vetting, insurance, setup, cleanup. You'd be loaning me the room."*

5. **Open the door for the no.** *"If it's a no, that's information, not a problem. I'd rather you say no early than say yes and feel weird about it."* This protects the friendship and signals you're an adult about it.

6. **Name what they get in return.** Don't make them ask. Pre-empt with one or two reciprocity offers from Section B.5 that fit this specific friend.

7. **Wait.** Don't fill the silence. Let them think out loud. The 8-question filter (B.1) is running in their head whether they know it or not — give them time to surface their own concerns.

8. **If they say yes:** *"Amazing. Let me send you the manifesto and the format spec tonight, and let's plan a walkthrough this week or next."*

9. **If they say maybe:** *"Take a few days. I won't ask again — but if you want to talk it through, I'm here. If you want to see the room I'm trying to build, I can send you the one-pager."*

10. **If they say no:** *"Thank you for being honest. Two questions, just so I can keep looking — would the space be a yes if the format were different (date, time, fewer people)? And do you know anyone with the kind of space I should be looking at?"* This salvages the conversation as a Tier 2 ask (B.6.d).

## B.5 — The Reciprocity Architecture

Andrea's principle: never ask a friend for a favor without naming what they get back. Without this, the ask collapses into "do me a favor" — which is fine once, but corrosive at scale. With it, the ask becomes a trade — which is repeatable and dignified.

Five reciprocity formats to mix and match. Each one is real (Andrea can actually deliver) and each one is something friends actually value.

### 1. DJ services exchange
**What it is:** Andrea DJs one of the friend's events (their next opening, their birthday, their pop-up, their partner's launch) for free.
**Value:** $400-800 equivalent at Chicago DJ rates.
**Best for:** Friends who run events / spaces / brands and have a real use for a set. Mutual industry favor.
**Caveat:** Be specific about scope — one set, 2-3 hours, mutually agreed playlist direction, no surprise "can you also MC?" expansion.

### 2. Friend-rate paid rental
**What it is:** A flat-fee rental at below-market rate. $300-700 typical, with a documented thank-you on top (small gift card, dinner, etc.).
**Value:** Direct cash, no ambiguity, respects that this is partly a business transaction.
**Best for:** Friends who run venues semi-professionally and would feel weird taking pure favor. Money is the cleanest exchange when the relationship is "warm-business" not "deep-personal."
**Caveat:** Pay quickly, pay in writing (Venmo / check / wire with a memo), pay before the event not after.

### 3. Cross-promotion bundle
**What it is:** IG tags during the build-up (story shoutouts, post mentions when the venue is announced), a post-event recap article on the Resonance Substack / IG that names + credits the space and the host, photographer shots shared with usage rights, and reciprocal cross-promotion of the friend's work to Resonance's audience.
**Value:** Indirect but real — exposure to Andrea's network + a permanent piece of branded content with the space's name in it.
**Best for:** Friends who are growing their own audience and value visibility as much as money.
**Caveat:** Specify volume and timing — "two IG story mentions in the two weeks before, one recap article post-event, photographer shots delivered within 14 days." Don't leave it as vague "I'll promote you."

### 4. Hosting honorific in event copy
**What it is:** The space and the host get named publicly. "Resonance Event #1, hosted at [Space Name] in partnership with [Friend Name]." It appears in: the IG post announcing the venue, the email confirmation to attendees, the photographer's captions, the post-event recap.
**Value:** Reputational — the friend gets a public association with a curated brand on its launch.
**Best for:** Friends who care about being part of something at the ground floor, not getting paid for it.
**Caveat:** Get a clear yes on the public visibility ask. Some friends want this; some want full anonymity (especially if the space doubles as their home). Ask: "Visible or invisible?"

### 5. The future seat
**What it is:** A reserved spot for the friend + a plus-one at Event #2 (or any future event), on Andrea, no application needed.
**Value:** Implicit "I'm bringing you in," especially if the friend is single themselves and curious about the format.
**Best for:** Friends who have been generous out of love for what Andrea is building, not because they need anything specific in return. The future seat is the gift, not the trade.
**Caveat:** This is the only currency Andrea controls absolutely. Don't promise more seats than she can deliver — and don't make it a "membership" — make it a one-event, one-friend, hand-extended invitation.

**The combination rule:** A real reciprocity package usually pairs 2 or 3 formats. The most common pairing: friend-rate rental ($400) + cross-promotion bundle + hosting honorific. That hits cash, visibility, and reputation simultaneously and reads as a real partnership, not a favor in either direction.

**The thing you don't say:** "We'll figure it out." That phrase is reciprocity-debt-disguised-as-generosity. Always name the currency, even if you let the friend choose which combination they want. Generosity that names itself is honest. Generosity that stays vague becomes resentment.

## B.6 — The Awkward-Conversation Decision Tree

What happens when the ask lands but the response is complicated. Four common branches. Each one has a specific script and a clear escalation path.

### B.6.a — Friend says yes but with caveats that violate the 12 Non-Negotiables

Example caveat: *"Yes, but we'd want to keep the bar open — it's part of how the space works."*

This is a no, even though the friend said yes. The 12 Non-Negotiables aren't preferences; they're the floor. A bent line at Event #1 becomes a precedent for Event #2.

**Script:**

> Hey [name] — thank you for the yes, and thank you for being honest about the bar. The sober part isn't a preference for me, it's structural to what the room does. So if the bar has to stay open, this specific event isn't the right fit for your space — but I'd love to bring a future Resonance Series event to you (we're building a sub-brand that's structured differently, evenings, alcohol, more relaxed format). I'll keep you in the loop on that.
>
> For July, I'll keep looking. Really appreciate you considering it.

**Other caveat variants to watch for:**

| Caveat | 12NN Line touched | Response posture |
|---|---|---|
| "Can we extend it to 7pm so it ends as the bar opens?" | Line 1 (Daytime) | Daytime is the format. Decline and redirect to Resonance Series. |
| "What if we made it 18+ instead of strict admission?" | Line 3 (Curated admission) | Curation is the protocol. Decline gently. |
| "I'd want my brand on the marketing too" | Line 12 (No sponsor compromise) + B.6.c | Decline as venue, redirect as sponsor conversation later. |
| "Can we do it on a Thursday?" | Line 1 (Daytime — Saturday daytime is the format) | Decline. Saturday daytime carries the format. |
| "Can my friend who's also a DJ play a set?" | Line 6 (Music curated by Resonance) | Decline. Andrea sets the sound. |

The pattern across all of these: **acknowledge the friend, name the line, redirect to a sub-brand or a future event, end warm.** Never compromise. The friend stays a friend; the line stays a line.

### B.6.b — Friend says yes but wants to invite their own people

Example: *"Yes! And I'll add a few of my own friends to the guest list so it's not all strangers."*

This is the single biggest friend-track risk. A yes that dissolves the curation is worse than a no.

**Script:**

> Hey [name] — I love that energy and I want to be honest about why I have to say no to that part. The curation is the format. The whole thing works because every person in the room said yes to the same agreement before they got the address. If I let even 3-4 people in who didn't go through that filter, the room shifts and the people who DID go through it can feel it — even if they can't name it.
>
> Here's what I can offer instead: you get the seat next to me for Event #1, and a +1 you pick. Beyond that, if there are friends of yours who'd be a hell-yes to this room, send them my way and I'll run them through the same application as everyone else. If they're in, they're in on the same terms as everyone else. That's the only way the room stays the room.
>
> Does that work?

**Why this script:** It names the principle ("curation is the format"), names what they CAN have ("you + 1, plus referrals through the normal door"), and asks for a confirmation. If the friend says "no, I want my people in or it's off," the friend is asking Andrea to break the format for their space — and the answer is no.

**Escalation:** If the friend won't budge, escalate to script B.6.d (the introduction salvage). Their space is no longer Tier 1; their network might still be Tier 2.

### B.6.c — Friend wants to make it a "co-event" with their brand

Example: *"This would be amazing — could we co-brand it as a Resonance × [Their Brand] collab?"*

This is the sponsor-doctrine question disguised as a venue ask. Line 12 of the 12NN: "If sponsorship requires drift, the sponsorship is wrong."

**Script:**

> Hey [name] — I love that you'd want to put your name on it, and I have to think about this carefully because of how Resonance is set up. The flagship is single-curator: Andrea curates the room, the music, the people, the protocol. Adding a co-brand at Event #1 creates a precedent I can't actually honor at Event #2 and beyond (every co-brand becomes a new line item, and the curation starts being filtered through multiple voices).
>
> Here's the version I can do at Event #1: your space gets named and credited publicly as the host venue, you're in the recap article, you're tagged across the IG build-up, and you get the seat next to me + 1. That's a hosting partnership, not a co-brand — different category, real visibility.
>
> If a deeper partnership is interesting down the line, let's talk after Event #1 about whether there's a Resonance Series collaboration that fits. But for the flagship, I have to keep the curation single-source.
>
> Does the hosting partnership land for you?

**Why this script:** It distinguishes "hosting partnership" (allowed, even encouraged — Section B.5 currencies #3 and #4 cover it) from "co-brand" (not allowed at flagship). Most friends asking for "co-brand" are actually asking for visibility, which the hosting partnership delivers without crossing Line 12. If they really want a co-brand, the answer is a polite no with a "later, on a different event."

### B.6.d — Friend says no but offers to introduce Andrea to someone else

Example: *"My space won't work for this, but my friend [Owner] has a studio in [neighborhood] that might. Want me to make the intro?"*

This is the best possible "no." Convert it immediately into a Tier 2 ask.

**Script (in the moment):**

> [Name] — that would be huge, yes. Could you send a one-line text or DM to [Owner] today (or whenever's easy) just saying I'll be reaching out about a Saturday daytime rental, and that you vouch for me? Doesn't need to be long. I'll handle the actual ask from there.
>
> Thank you. Really.

**Follow-up to the friend 24 hours later (only if they haven't done the intro):**

> hey [name] — quick bump on the intro. no rush, just wanted to make sure it didn't get lost. happy to draft the text for you to send if that's easier?

**The drafted text Andrea sends the friend to forward (if asked):**

> hey [owner] — my friend andrea (DJ, runs resonance — a daytime sober dance event in chicago) is gonna reach out about a saturday daytime rental in july. she's the real deal and i think your spot might be exactly what she's looking for. you should expect her DM / email this week.

**Why this script:** The introduction salvages the "no" into a warm lead. The drafted forwarding text removes the friction — if the friend hasn't sent the intro after 24 hours, it's often because they're stuck on what to write, not because they changed their mind. Give them the text.

**The follow-up to the introducer post-Event-#1 (closure):**

> hey [name] — just wanted to close the loop. [outcome — landed at owner's space / didn't but found another / activated plan b]. either way, thank you for the intro. owe you one.

This closes the loop dignifiedly whether the intro converted or not. The friend gets thanked; the relationship stays warm for next time.

## C.2 — The Venue Long-List Builder

Five methods, run in parallel on Day 1. Target output: 12-15 candidate venues with contact info.

### Method 1 — Peerspace / Splacer / Giggster searches

**Peerspace queries:** "Chicago daytime event space 50 people" · "Chicago dance studio rental weekend" · "Chicago photo studio rental Saturday" · "[Neighborhood] event space" (Logan Square, Pilsen, Wicker Park).

**Splacer (now Peerspace, but check separately):** same queries; some listings differ.

**Giggster queries:** "Chicago event venue daytime" · "Chicago loft rental for event" · "Chicago studio with daylight."

Filter: capacity 40-80, daytime availability, hourly rate under $200 (3-hr booking under $600 maps to Lean Path). Per listing capture: name, direct booking contact, capacity, sq ft, daylight photos, hourly rate × 3hr estimate. Note: Peerspace adds ~12% markup — direct outreach to the venue often saves money.

### Method 2 — Instagram method

**Search:** IG search "[Neighborhood] studio rental" / "Chicago dance studio rental" / "Pilsen creative space." Hashtag browse: `#chicagostudiorental`, `#chicagocreativespace`, `#chicagolofteventspace`.

**Bio signals = rentable:** "rental" / "book us" / "DM to book" in bio, "spaces@" or "book@" email, recent event coverage in grid, Linktree with booking link, posts using rental language ("available for events").

**Bio signals = skip:** pure performance venue with packed self-programming, personal account with no business indicators, bar-driven feed (all evening, all alcohol).

Capture: IG handle, contact info, one post showing daytime light.

### Method 3 — Direct Google patterns

Queries that pull non-platform-listed venues: "Chicago daytime event space rental" · "[Neighborhood] event space rental afternoon" · "Chicago private event venue daytime" · "Chicago non-alcoholic event space" · "Chicago dance studio rental private event" · "Chicago gallery rental event" · "Chicago photo studio rental Saturday afternoon."

Top 3-5 results per query with a real website (skip aggregator listings).

### Method 4 — Word-of-mouth chain (Farrice's outreach)

Friend categories who know spaces. Send this DM/text to each:

> hey [name] — quick one. helping a friend lock a venue for a 50-person daytime saturday event in chicago (mid-july, no bar, ~3 hours). you know any spaces — dance studios, photo studios, lofts, galleries — that rent for that scale? open to logan / pilsen / wicker park / west loop / humboldt / bucktown / avondale.

Send to: 3-5 photographer friends, 2-3 dance instructor friends, 2-3 gallery owner friends, 2-3 indie wedding planner friends, 2-3 music venue manager friends. Replies come back fast — the ask is concrete and the favor is small.

### Method 5 — Specific Chicago venue archetypes to research

Not endorsements — research targets. Verify each before pitching.

**Daytime-friendly archetypes that tend to rent in target neighborhoods:**

1. **Logan Square dance studios** — sprung floors, often small house PAs, $80-150/hr.
2. **Pilsen arts collective spaces** — mature artist-run ecosystem, mission-aligned, weekend rentals common.
3. **West Loop photography studios** — 1,000-2,500 sq ft open floors with dramatic west daylight, $150-300/hr; weekends often discounted off shoot premium.
4. **Wicker Park / Bucktown lofts** — converted industrial event spaces, $500-2,000 for 3 hours; daytime is dead time, leverage it.
5. **Humboldt Park arts collective studios** — Pilsen-equivalent at lower rate range, week-class/weekend-event multi-use.
6. **Avondale industrial-conversion spaces** — less polished, less expensive, big factory windows. Check floor surface.
7. **Indie performance venues with daytime calendars** — 50-150 cap music venues with house PAs that rent empty afternoons.
8. **Art galleries with event rental programs** — Pilsen / West Town / Logan; white walls + concrete + daylight; 40-60 cap.
9. **Yoga / movement studios off-class** — sprung floors, $80-150/hr; wellness-coded aesthetic risk but floor works.
10. **Co-working spaces with event rooms** — sterile risk; last resort.
11. **Boutique cafes with adjoining event rooms** — good aesthetic; usually 30-40 cap, too small unless we reduce.
12. **Recording studio + event space hybrids** — excellent acoustics, daylight varies.
13. **Boutique fitness studios off-class** — barre / Pilates / boxing floors, aesthetic mixed.
14. **Pop-up retail / showroom spaces** — emerging in West Loop and Wicker; Saturday dead time, high aesthetic potential.
15. **The room above a restaurant** — bar adjacency risk (Line 2). Use only if bar is closed during event.

**Anti-targets (skip):** River North hotel ballroom annexes (sterile, expensive, wrong neighborhood). Lincoln Park family-coded restaurants. Fulton Market converted warehouses (overpriced, corporate-saturated). South Loop high-rise event suites (no daylight). Any "club" or "lounge" name.

## C.3 — The Cold Pitch Email (Improved)

The version at `pre-launch/01b-venue-cold-pitch-email.md` is the current canonical cold pitch. This section provides three sharpening upgrades on top of it.

### Upgrade 1 — Three new subject line variants for A/B testing

Already in `01b-` we have 5 subject lines. Add these three to expand the test bank:

**Subject A (operational specificity):**
`Daytime, 50 people, no bar — Sat July 18 or 25`

Reads like a calendar entry. Some venue managers triage their inbox by keyword scan; "no bar" pops out and pre-qualifies fast.

**Subject B (founder-first):**
`Andrea from Resonance — Saturday daytime room inquiry`

Reads like a person, not a brand. Works better for venue contacts who screen for human-vs-corporate.

**Subject C (frame-then-sharpen compressed):**
`Curated 50-person event, daytime, no alcohol service`

The format itself is the subject. Filters out venues that need bar revenue to host.

### Upgrade 2 — The body in polished register, sharpened

Use the version in `01b-` as the canonical send. Two sharpening tweaks if you want to A/B against it:

**Sharpening tweak 1 — front-load the COI mention earlier.** Some venues read the first paragraph and skim the rest. Moving the COI to paragraph 2 (instead of the "what we bring" bullets) signals professionalism faster.

**Sharpening tweak 2 — replace the PS with a one-line credentials note in the body.** The current `01b-` PS does the founder-origin work. For tighter venues (Profile B from `venue-pitch.md` — restaurant/hotel managers who care about commercial cleanness), drop the PS and add one line after the format paragraph: *"I've been DJing in Chicago for [X] years; references on request."* The autobiographical material lands worse with commercial venue managers than with arts-space owners.

Decision rule: use the `01b-` PS version for Profile A venues (arts spaces). Use the credentials-line version for Profile B venues (commercial/restaurant).

### Upgrade 3 — Honor-the-venue's-time anti-template checklist

Before sending any cold pitch, sweep for these tells. Any one of them and the email goes to archive instead of sent:

- "I hope this email finds you well." → Skip. Stock pleasantry.
- "We'd love to partner with your incredible space." → Skip. "Incredible space" is flattery; flattery from strangers reads as manipulation.
- "Quick question..." → Skip. The question is never quick; venues know this.
- "I'll keep this brief..." → Skip. Either be brief or don't comment on it.
- "Would love to hop on a quick call to discuss further." → Skip. Replace with: a specific time window or specific dates for the tour.
- "Beautiful space" used as the only descriptor of the venue. → Skip. Either name something specific (the light, the floor, the proportions) or don't comment on the space at all.
- Title-case subject line. → Skip. Reads spam. Use sentence case.
- "Reach out if interested!" with an exclamation. → Skip. Replace with: "If [Venue] is the right room, a 15-min tour this week works. If not, a one-line no is fine."

## C.4 — The Venue Triage Scorecard

When 2-3 venues respond positively, how to choose. Score each on the 8 dimensions below, 1-5 scale. Weighted total = decision input (not the only input — Andrea adjudicates final).

| Dimension | Weight | Scoring criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand fit** | 25% | 5 = aesthetically aligned (daylight, real materials, no nightclub coding); 3 = neutral / generic but acceptable; 1 = aesthetically clashes (bar-coded, sterile, AI-deck style) |
| **Daylight** | 15% | 5 = abundant windows, full daylight 2-5pm; 3 = some daylight, supplemental lighting needed; 1 = basement / no real windows (auto-fail, skip) |
| **Floor surface** | 15% | 5 = sprung dance floor or polished wood; 3 = concrete or sealed wood (acceptable); 1 = carpet, painted, or sticky-when-warm (auto-fail) |
| **Capacity match** | 10% | 5 = 60-80 cap (fits 50 + buffer comfortably); 3 = 50-60 cap (snug but works); 1 = under 45 or over 100 (too small or too cavernous) |
| **Price** | 15% | 5 = under $500 (Lean Path); 4 = $500-800 (sweet spot); 3 = $800-1,200 (Standard Path); 2 = $1,200-1,500 (upper ceiling); 1 = $1,500+ (consult math) |
| **Neighborhood** | 5% | 5 = Logan / Pilsen / West Town / Humboldt / Avondale; 3 = Wicker / Bucktown / West Loop; 1 = River North / Lincoln Park / Fulton Market / South Loop |
| **Friction-of-yes** | 10% | 5 = "yes, when do you want to tour"; 3 = "send the one-pager and we'll discuss"; 1 = "we need a 30-page proposal, a board meeting, and 6 references" |
| **Venue contact quality** | 5% | 5 = responsive, friendly, treats you like a peer; 3 = polite but transactional; 1 = condescending, slow, ambiguous |

**Total score = sum of (dimension score × weight). Out of 5.0.**

| Total | Decision |
|---|---|
| 4.0+ | Strong yes. Move to contract. |
| 3.5-3.9 | Yes with conditions. Tour first to verify; negotiate price if needed. |
| 3.0-3.4 | Marginal. Only choose if no higher-scored option is available. |
| Below 3.0 | Skip. Look elsewhere. |

**Tiebreaker rule:** When two venues score within 0.3 of each other, the higher Brand Fit score wins. Brand fit at Event #1 compounds for Event #2-N.

## C.5 — The Negotiation Talking Points

When a venue replies positively and the conversation moves to terms. Specific moves, written as sentences Andrea or Farrice would actually say.

### Rate negotiation

**If the rate is above ceiling:**

> The format works for us at [target rate] for the 3-hour window. If [Venue] can flex to that rate for a first partnership, we're ready to lock the date this week. If not, totally understand — and we'd want to come back for Event #2 or #3 when the model is proven.

**If they propose revenue share instead of flat rate:**

> We work on a flat rental model — revenue share creates pressure on door curation we don't carry, and the curation is what makes this work. If [Venue] can do a flat rate within [range], let's keep talking. If the rev share is non-negotiable, totally understand.

**If they want a deposit higher than 50%:**

> We can do a 25% deposit at signing with the balance 14 days before event date. That's how we structure all our venue contracts. If your standard is higher, walk us through the reason.

### COI (Certificate of Insurance) handling

**Confirming what they need:**

> We carry $1M event liability with the option to add [Venue] as additional insured. We can have the COI documented and emailed to you 14 days before event date. What's your preferred recipient address for the COI binder?

**If they ask for higher coverage ($2M):**

> $2M is doable — we can bind that for this event. Adds approximately $50-75 to the policy, which we'll absorb. Let me confirm with our broker and get back to you within 48 hours.

### Sound and music

**Confirming the format:**

> We bring our own PA and DJ rig. We'll use your power and stay within whatever dB ceiling your building / neighborhood requires — we self-monitor with a decibel meter at the booth. No live amplified vocals, no subwoofer above [their ceiling]. The set is recorded and curated music only.

**If they require their house sound engineer:**

> Our DJ runs her own setup — turntables, mixer, monitors. We don't typically use a venue engineer, but if your insurance or contract requires it, we can have [their engineer] on standby for the setup hour. Run me through what that looks like cost-wise.

### Day-of access

**Setting expectations:**

> We need building access from noon for setup. Event is 2-5pm. We're out and the space is restored by 6pm. Photographer documents 1:30-5:15. If your standard turnover window is different, walk me through it.

**If they want a shorter window:**

> If 1pm is the earliest access, we can compress setup to 60 minutes. Tighter than that and we'd need to do a pre-event sound check the day before — which we'd pay for if your standard allows it.

### Address / privacy protocol

**Setting expectations:**

> The address goes out only to vetted attendees 24 hours before the event. We don't publish [Venue]'s address on the landing page, the IG, or any pre-event copy. The first time the address appears publicly is in the post-event recap, with your sign-off.

**If they want public marketing:**

> We can absolutely tag [Venue] in the post-event recap and on photographer shots shared with permission. The pre-event privacy is structural — attendees need to feel the room is small and protected before they walk in. After the event, you're credited.

### When to walk away

If the venue:
- Won't budge on requiring alcohol service → walk. Line 2.
- Won't budge on a daytime slot → walk. Line 1.
- Wants creative input on the music or attendees → walk. Lines 5, 6.
- Imposes a revenue-share model with no flat-rate option → walk. Drift risk.
- Is condescending or treats this as a "cute first event" → walk. The energy carries to event day.

The walk-away script:

> Thanks for the conversation. Sounds like [Venue] isn't the right fit for this specific format — really appreciate the time. We're building a Resonance Series variant later that's structured differently; would love to come back when that's ready.

End warm. The Chicago venue scene is small. Today's walked-away venue is next year's yes for Event #5.

| Day 5 | May 29 | Reply rate | 3+ replies received (any direction) | Reconsider subject lines + opener strategy; send Day 6 batch wider |

The discipline is reading the gate criteria honestly. "Almost a yes" is not a yes. "Verbal nod from a friend who hasn't named a date" is not a yes. Only "Verbal yes with named date" counts. The cost of pretending a soft yes is firm is a Day 13 surprise — and Day 13 has no recovery time.

# Section E — Plan B (House Party, Tier 3)

If Day 14 hits with no friend-space and no paid venue, Plan B activates. The house party version of Event #1 still works — the format isn't about polish, it's about the curation, the music, and the protocol. But the operational differences are real and need to be planned BEFORE Day 14, not on Day 14.

## E.1 — The Plan B Activation Criteria

Plan B activates when ALL of the following are true at end of Day 10 (June 3):

- No Paid venue at "Contract signed" or "Verbal yes" status.
- No Friend-with-Space at "Verbal yes" or stronger.
- Fewer than 2 live conversations in any track.
- Andrea hasn't named a postponement (i.e., Event #1 is still firm for July 18 / 25).

If 3 of 4 are true, Plan B activates as parallel insurance — Andrea reaches out to the named Plan B candidates with a soft warm-up ("might be a bigger ask coming in 4 days, just wanted to put it on your radar") so they're warm by the time the formal ask lands.

## E.2 — The Plan B Candidate Shortlist (Andrea names privately by Day 7)

Andrea identifies 1-2 specific friends privately by Day 7 (June 1) using the criteria below. The names don't enter any shared document — they're private to Andrea's own notes.

**Plan B candidate criteria (more permissive than Tier 1):**

1. Friend whose home has 35-45 person dance capacity (smaller than ideal, larger than impossible).
2. Real daylight in the main room (still non-negotiable).
3. Floor that survives 35-45 people moving for 3 hours (wood, sealed concrete, OR a rug-free living/dining combo).
4. Neighborhood that won't trigger noise complaints on a Saturday afternoon.
5. Friend who has been to one of Andrea's previous sets OR has had Resonance explained to them in depth (not a cold ask).
6. Friend whose partner / housemate is also a soft yes (or they live alone).
7. Friend Andrea would owe a real favor to — not a casual debt.

**How Andrea identifies these candidates without making it weird:**

She runs the Section G network inventory questions in her head. The 1-2 names that come up for her are the candidates. She doesn't tell those friends until Day 7+ — until then it's just a private mental shortlist.

## E.3 — The Plan B Trade-Offs

**What's lost:**

- Capacity ceiling drops from 50 to 35-40. Smaller room means fewer attendees; ticket pricing math changes (see Command Center Section 4 Tier 3 row — $40 × 35 attendees = $1,400 revenue vs. $2,000 at 50).
- Aesthetic polish drops. A house is a house. The visual library that comes out of Event #1 looks like "intimate gathering" not "curated venue moment."
- The address-disclosed-late protocol is more sensitive — a residential address has more privacy stakes than a commercial venue.
- The photographer brief changes — no exterior shots of the building, no shots that show neighbors or address signage.

**What's gained:**

- Zero or near-zero rental cost. Plan B venue is $0-200 in thank-you currency. Lean Path profit climbs to $1,000+.
- Full curation control. No venue contract terms, no day-of staff coordination with venue managers, no negotiation on sound or access.
- Faster lock. A friend says yes in 24-48 hours; a venue contract takes 7-14 days.
- Higher intimacy. 35-40 people in a friend's home reads differently than 50 in a commercial space — and "intimate" is consistent with the brand even if "polished" is downgraded.

**The honest framing:** Plan B is not a worse event. It's a different event at a smaller scale. The brand carries because the format carries. A first event in a friend's living room, done right, can be a stronger origin story than a first event in a rented venue.

## E.4 — The Plan B Execution Differences

If Plan B activates, these specific operational pivots happen:

### Room setup pivots
- Furniture removal: the friend agrees in advance which furniture moves out (or Farrice + a setup helper handle removal at noon, return at 6pm).
- Phone basket: stays. The protocol doesn't soften because it's a house.
- Drinks station: moves to the kitchen (a counter, not a separate "bar setup"). Same drinks: water, iced tea, kombucha, sparkling.
- Sound: Andrea's PA still comes — house Bluetooth speakers aren't loud enough or quality enough.
- Lighting: rely entirely on daylight. No supplemental lighting at a house.

### Photographer brief pivots
- No exterior shots showing the building, the address signage, or the street.
- Interior shots OK with attendee consent (same release form as venue version).
- Capture the "warm room" frame — fewer wide shots, more close-ups of the dancing, more detail shots (the basket, the drinks counter, the daylight on the floor).
- Hosts (the friend) get tagged in any post-event content unless they opt for invisibility.

### Run-of-show pivots
- Setup window compresses to 90 minutes (instead of 2 hours). Less venue gear to coordinate.
- Sound check happens at 1:00pm (30 min before doors).
- Doors open 2:00pm. Address goes out 24h before to vetted attendees only.
- Hard end at 5:00pm. Cleanup begins at 5:15. Space restored by 6:00pm.
- Andrea introduces the friend (the host) at the welcome moment if they're present.

### Ticket / capacity pivots
- Capacity capped at 35-40 (not 50). Communicate this in any pre-event copy as "Event #1: intimate first edition."
- Ticket price stays $40 (the math still works at 35 × $40 = $1,400 minus ~$830 Tier 3 costs = $570 profit).
- Waitlist management: if more than 40 hell-yes applications come in, defer the overflow to Event #2 with a personal note from Andrea.

### Communication pivots
- The pre-event email to attendees names "intimate first edition" framing without apologizing for the venue choice.
- The post-event recap can name "hosted in a friend's [neighborhood] home" without naming the friend (unless they opt in).
- The IG announcement of the venue (or non-announcement, as the case may be) is replaced with a "the room is locked" beat that doesn't disclose location.

# Section F — Outreach to JR (Anchor DJ) — Follow-Up Playbook

Farrice has reached out to JR (Andrea's anchor DJ candidate, Farrice's cousin). Awaiting response as of 2026-05-25. JR may not be available for Event #1 — Plan B for the lineup needs to exist.

## F.1 — The Follow-Up Script (if JR hasn't replied by Day 7 / June 1)

**Andrea sends this, not Farrice.** The reason: a DJ-to-DJ follow-up reads differently than a family-member follow-up. Andrea framing the ask as a peer-to-peer booking treats JR as a working artist, not a relative being asked for a favor.

**Voice register: conversational, peer-to-peer DJ. Andrea-to-JR voice.**

> hey [JR / nickname] — andrea here. farrice mentioned he reached out about resonance last week. wanted to circle back myself since it's my event and figured it was weird to leave you waiting on a cousin-of-the-organizer follow-up.
>
> here's the actual ask: first resonance event saturday july 18 (backup july 25), 2-5pm, chicago daytime sober dance party for ~50 people. i'd love you to anchor the b2b with me — i'm thinking we trade hour-and-a-half each, or whatever set length works for you, and i'd want your taste on the back half when the room is at peak.
>
> it's a real working booking, not a favor. happy to talk rate, talk format, talk what would make it a hell-yes on your end. and if july doesn't work, that's fine too — i'd want you on a later one rather than force this date. let me know either way?

**Voice notes (do not include in send):**
- 188 words. Within DM range.
- One em-dash. Pass.
- Voice patterns: out-loud-asking (the "here's the actual ask" line, used as a peer-acknowledging move, not as an AI tell — the phrase functions here because it follows an explicit context-setting first paragraph), mechanic-as-sentence (date/time/format details).
- Conversational register markers: lowercase, "wanted to circle back," "it's a real working booking, not a favor," "let me know either way."
- Banned phrases swept: no "honored," no "would mean the world," no "would love to have you" alone (it's paired with specific terms).
- The "cousin-of-the-organizer" line acknowledges the awkward dynamic without dwelling on it.

## F.2 — The "Yes But" Script (JR says yes but with conditions)

Common "yes but" patterns and Andrea's response. Each one is a real working DJ negotiation — handle them like a peer, not a relative.

### F.2.a — JR says yes but wants a paid rate above what's budgeted

Common scenario: JR says "yes, my rate is $X." If $X is above the Event #1 budget for talent (Andrea hasn't formally budgeted talent fees — the Lean Path math assumes Andrea + JR free or near-free).

**Script:**

> [JR] — appreciate the directness on rate. event #1 math is tight (lean path, $40 ticket × 50 cap, ~$1,000 profit margin). i can offer [$X — try a number that's 50-60% of his ask, framed as the launch rate]. if that's too low for the date, totally understand — and if you want a different cut structure (door percentage, post-event payout from net), let me know and i'll run the math.
>
> alternatively, if you'd rather hold for event #2 or #3 when the math has more headroom, that's a real option too. zero pressure either way.

**Why this script:** Acknowledges his professional rate, names the budget reality, offers a counter, offers an alternative structure, opens the door for him to defer to a later event without losing face. Treats him as a peer making a business decision.

### F.2.b — JR says yes but wants a longer set / different role

Common scenario: JR says "yes, I'd want to play 2.5 of the 3 hours" or "I'd want to open instead of close."

**Script:**

> [JR] — we can flex on set length and slot. talk me through what you're thinking. the only structural constraint is that i need to be on the decks during the welcome moment (first 15 min) and the goodbye moment (last 15 min) since i'm MCing those — but everything between is open. open to back-to-back, alternating, or front-half-back-half splits. what works for you?

**Why:** Names the one structural constraint (Andrea on the decks at open and close because she's also MCing), then opens the rest. Treats his preferences as legitimate input, not as obstacles.

### F.2.c — JR says yes but wants branded mention / co-promotion

Common scenario: JR says "yes, and could we co-promote it — Andrea x JR?"

**Script:**

> [JR] — yes on the lineup credit, with one caveat — the flagship event has to read as "Resonance" not "Resonance x [JR's brand]." that's a structural thing with the brand at launch (single-curator format). but you get full lineup billing, IG tag during the build-up, a feature post leading up to the event, and the recap article names the b2b. effectively all the visibility benefits of a co-promo without the co-brand structure. work for you?

**Why:** Same logic as B.6.c with friends — distinguishes "lineup credit" (allowed, encouraged) from "co-brand" (not allowed at flagship). JR gets real visibility; the brand stays clean.

## F.3 — The "No" Script + Plan B for Lineup

If JR says no — for July, for the rate, for the format, for any reason — Andrea responds warm and pivots fast.

**The "no" response script:**

> [JR] — totally get it. thank you for being direct, that's better than ambiguous. let's stay in touch about a later event — i'd love you on event #2 or #3, and the rate / format conversation will be easier once the math has more room.
>
> in the meantime, would you have anyone in chicago in mind you'd vouch for as a b2b partner for this one? same daytime / curated frame, similar taste lane to yours. no pressure if no one comes to mind.

**Why:** Closes the door warm on July. Opens the door on a future event. Asks for a referral as a Tier 2 lineup move. JR's network is high-signal — anyone he vouches for is probably worth talking to.

### Lineup Plan B — 3 archetypes Andrea considers

If JR can't make July (or as parallel insurance from Day 5 onward), Andrea fills in three names from her own DJ network. The archetypes below describe the kind of DJ to look for; Andrea names actual names from her contacts.

# Section H — Voice Audit + Final Check

Every script in this playbook was written against the Voice Document Section 6 AI Handoff Block. Before any script gets sent, run the 7-point check below.

## The Pre-Send Checklist (run on every script before it goes)

1. **Could Andrea say this to a friend over coffee?** Friend scripts (B series, F series): say-to-friend test. Cold venue scripts (C series): say-in-business-call test. Different surface, same voice.
2. **Lead with the room, not the brand.** Format facts (daytime, sober, 50 people, no bar) appear before any abstraction. Body-first language ("on the floor by 2:15", "the music does the warming") carries the explanation.
3. **Name the enemy where naming clarifies.** Friend scripts don't need explicit enemy-naming. B.6.a names alcohol-as-Line-2 structurally. C series cold pitches reference enemies implicitly through "no bar" / "daytime" / "vetted."
4. **Show > tell at sentence level.** Every script opens with a concrete: a room, a moment, a specific date, a named space. No abstractions in opening lines.
5. **Em-dashes ≤ 2. Zero AI-tell openers.** Every script swept. Em-dash count per script: 0-2. Zero "Here's what / why / how / the thing" openers.
6. **At least one voice pattern doing real work.** Mechanic-as-sentence is the dominant pattern in this playbook (operational surface). Frame-then-sharpen carries C.3 cold pitch openers + B.6.c. Crystallized phrases carry B.3.c step 2 + B.4. Out-loud-asking carries B.3.a + F.1. Hell-yes filter carries B.4 + C.3 + F series ("if it doesn't work, zero pressure" is the DJ-version).
7. **Hell-yes filter present in any "who this is for" copy.** B.4 names the protocol explicitly so friends self-select. C.3 uses "if [Venue] is not the right room, a one-line no is plenty." F series uses the DJ-version above.
