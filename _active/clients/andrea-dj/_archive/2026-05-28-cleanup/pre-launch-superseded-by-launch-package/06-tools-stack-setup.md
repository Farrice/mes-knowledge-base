*Part of the Pre-Launch Command Center (see `00-command-center.md`).*

*Total Week 1 cost: ~$19. Total setup time: ~2-2.5 hours across both people.*

---

## The Operating Theory of the Stack

A two-person launch can't afford a tool that has to be re-learned every week. Every tool below earned its slot by clearing one bar: does it produce value within an hour of setup, and does it survive at the scale of 10 weeks of pre-launch + recurring Resonance events afterward? The stack is narrow on purpose. Each tool has one job, the jobs don't overlap, and the total monthly cost stays under $50 even after Event #1 ships. The discipline is to keep it that way: every new tool added is a tool to maintain, a credential to share, and a learning curve to absorb. The default answer to "should we add X?" is no.

The stack assumes Andrea and Farrice are operators, not designers or developers. Tools that require a third skill to operate (Figma, Adobe, custom-coded landing pages) are out of scope until Event #4-5 when the brand has earned the right to specialist tooling.

## Section 1 — Canva Pro (90 min — Thursday 5/21)

**Status:** Andrea has Canva Pro. ✓

### Why this tool

Canva is the daily design workhorse, and it earned the slot for four reasons. First, the Brand Kit feature lets us pre-load every type and color token from the BOS once, so every future graphic inherits the system without manual reapplication — the cost of staying on-brand drops from 15 minutes per piece to zero. Second, Magic Studio + Brand Voice produces on-voice copy variations 10x faster than starting from scratch, and the AI can be trained on Andrea's voice document so the first draft is already half-right. Third, Content Planner schedules direct-to-IG, saving Andrea ~50 minutes per week on manual upload. Fourth, the Pro tier is the cheapest professional design tool that survives at-scale use: Figma is for product teams, Adobe is for designers, and Canva is for founders who need to ship one finished asset every day for ten weeks straight. For a two-person team running a 10-week pre-launch, Canva Pro is correctly priced.

**Primary playbook:** **`06a-canva-pro-action-steps.md`** — the cold-to-ship-in-90-minutes action playbook. Open that file and execute its 5 stages.

**Deep system reference:** `brand-operating-system/04-ai-handoff/04-canva-component-spec.md` — exact font weights, color hex codes, template specs. Referenced from inside 06a where needed.

**What 06a covers:**

1. **Stage 1 — Brand Kit deploy** (30 min) — fonts, colors, logos
2. **Stage 2 — Brand Voice training** (10 min) — paste voice doc patterns into Canva's AI so Magic Write matches voice
3. **Stage 3 — Folder structure** (10 min) — by 7 Content Pillars
4. **Stage 4 — Star 12 templates** (15 min) — daily workhorses
5. **Stage 5 — Day 1 graphic + Magic Resize** (20 min) — ship the Mon 5/26 post

Plus a quick-reference card with: Magic Write caption prompts, iPhone photo color-grading workflow, the Sunday-night batch ritual, and Content Planner setup for scheduling from Canva.

**Why this matters:** every Week 1 IG post + every Story uses this system. Andrea's Sunday-night ritual produces the entire week's content in 90 min instead of 90 min per post.

## Section 1.5 — Claude Pro (60 min — Thursday 5/21)

**Status:** Andrea has Claude Pro ($20/mo). ✓

### Why this tool

Claude Pro is the brand's writing partner. Without it, every IG caption, every DM reply, every email response requires Andrea to start from a blank page. With it, the Brand HQ Project carries the BOS + Voice Document + Hook Library as persistent context, which means Andrea opens Claude, types a thought, and gets an on-voice caption in 30 seconds. The advantage is not in the AI; it's in not having to re-explain the brand every session. Claude Pro is the difference between writing 30 pieces of content over 10 weeks (sustainable) and writing 30 pieces of content over 10 weeks while also retraining your AI 30 times (not). For a brand whose voice is the moat, the writing partner is non-negotiable.

The reason this tool sits next to Canva (not above it) is operational: Canva handles the visual, Claude handles the words, and the daily content stack is the two of them together. Either alone is half a system.

**Primary playbook:** **`06b-claude-pro-action-steps.md`** — the Resonance Brand HQ Project setup. Open that file and execute its 5 steps.

**Deep system reference:** `brand-operating-system/04-ai-handoff/00-ai-brain-master.md` (the source AI Brain Master) and `brand-operating-system/04-ai-handoff/01-claude-pro-project-setup.md` (Project setup spec).

The free tier covers Weeks 1-3 without compromise; Pro becomes worth $24/year only if custom branding starts mattering for press coverage, which is an Event #2 conversation. ## Section 4 — Beehiiv (30 min — Wednesday-Thursday 5/20-21)

**Tier:** Free. Beehiiv's free tier includes newsletter, email automation, and a basic landing page (perfect for "what is Resonance + signup").

### Why this tool

Beehiiv is the email home and the interim landing page in one — two infrastructure jobs done by one free-tier tool, which is the kind of consolidation a two-person team requires. The newsletter handles the long arc: attendees from Event #1 stay subscribed for Event #2 and #3, and the cost of keeping them informed approaches zero. The landing page handles the short arc: from Week 2 onward, every IG bio link, every venue email signature, every press one-sheeter points to a Beehiiv page that captures email and hands the visitor the manifesto in one scroll. Mailchimp lost because its free tier is worse and its design defaults look 2010s. ConvertKit lost because it's priced for creators with audiences, not for brands building one. Beehiiv's modern email design and built-in landing page is what makes "Andrea sends a newsletter that doesn't look like spam" possible without hiring a designer.

The free tier is enough for Event #1 and likely Event #2-3 as well. The upgrade trigger is when the subscriber list crosses 2,500 names, which is an Event #4+ problem.

**Setup:**

1. Go to beehiiv.com, sign up with Andrea's email (or shared Andrea+Farrice account if that's simpler)
2. Publication name: **Resonance**
3. Description: *"Daytime, sober dance room in Chicago for adults seeking a partner. First event July 18, 2026."*
4. Domain: leave as `resonance.beehiiv.com` for Week 1 (skip custom domain decision until Week 2)
5. **Welcome email setup** (30 min) — see template below
6. **Landing page setup** (10 min) — Beehiiv auto-generates one; Andrea customizes copy in Week 2 once landing page copy doc ships
7. **Newsletter signup form** — embed code for landing page + IG bio link option

**Why Beehiiv over Mailchimp / ConvertKit:** free tier is meaningfully better, landing page included, modern email design that doesn't feel 2010s. Switch later if needs change.

## Section 7 — Carrd (optional, Week 2 decision)

**Tier:** Pro Lite $19/yr (only paid item in stack).

### Why this tool (maybe)

Carrd is the only paid tool in the stack and the only one with a deferred yes-or-no decision. It exists in the maybe column because Beehiiv's landing page is good enough for most first events, and Carrd's value only earns out if (a) the brand wants a custom domain like `resonance.chicago` or `resonancechi.com` and (b) Beehiiv's design constraints become a problem. The cost is trivial ($19/year); the real cost is the maintenance overhead of running two landing pages on two different platforms, where every brand update has to happen twice. For Event #1, the right answer is almost always "skip Carrd, run Beehiiv only." For Event #4-5 when the brand is positioning for press and partnerships, custom-domain ownership becomes worth the second platform. The decision is pushed to Week 2 on purpose so it's made with evidence (does Beehiiv's design hold?) instead of speculation.

**When to use:** if Beehiiv's landing page doesn't have enough visual control AND we want a custom domain like `resonance.chicago` or `resonancechi.com`.

**When to skip:** if Beehiiv's landing page is good enough (it usually is for Event #1).

**Decision deadline:** end of Week 2 (May 30). Defer if uncertain.

**Recommendation:** Beehiiv only for Event #1. Carrd if we have proof Beehiiv visual control isn't enough.

## Section 8 — VSCO (5 min — anytime)

**Tier:** Free (basic filters + editing). VSCO Membership is $30/yr — skip for Week 1.

### Why this tool

VSCO exists in the stack for one specific job: turning Andrea's iPhone candids — coffee shots, hand-on-record shots, lakefront walks — into images that look like they belong on the Resonance grid in 30 seconds. The cream-and-terracotta brand palette has a specific warmth that IG's in-app filters can't match. VSCO's preset filters (A6, C1, HB1) are pre-calibrated to that warmth, which means consistency across 30+ Stories per week without manual color-correcting each one. The free tier is enough; Membership is a Week-4+ decision if Andrea wants the advanced presets. The alternative — in-app IG editing — is fine for one-offs but produces grid drift across a week of posts, and grid coherence is one of the three things a curious profile-visitor judges at the 8-second mark.

**Setup:**

1. Download VSCO app on Andrea's iPhone
2. No account needed for basic editing
3. Filter recommendations for Resonance candids:
   - **A6** — soft warm, good for daytime indoor photos
   - **C1** — clean, slight cream tone
   - **HB1** — warm earth tone, on-palette
   - Avoid: G3 (too cool/blue), AV4 (too desaturated), anything with neon-coded color shift

**Use case:** Andrea's iPhone candids (coffee shots, hand-on-record shots, lakefront walks) get a 30-second VSCO pass before posting. Adds the cream-and-terracotta cast that makes them feel on-brand.

**Alternative:** in-app IG editing is fine. VSCO just makes consistency easier.

## Section 12 — What's NOT in This Stack (and Why)

A curated stack is also defined by what it deliberately excludes. Every tool below is a tool that solves a real problem at scale — they're just not problems Resonance has yet. Naming the exclusions in writing protects the team from the slow tool-stack creep that turns a $50/month operation into a $400/month one without anyone noticing.

Tools NOT recommended for Event #1:

- **Eventbrite** for ticketing. Skip until Event #2-3 when scale demands it. Event #1 ticket sales can run through a simple Beehiiv landing page with a Stripe payment link OR through a Tally form with payment integration. Eventbrite's per-ticket fee structure makes sense at 200+ attendees, not 50.
- **Mailchimp** — older interface, weaker free tier than Beehiiv, design defaults that age the brand.
- **Hootsuite / Later / Buffer** — IG scheduling tools. Skip for Week 1; manual posting is fine for 1 post/day. Revisit Week 3+ only if scheduling becomes friction.
- **Squarespace / Wix** — too heavy for a single-event landing page. Beehiiv or Carrd is sufficient and avoids the maintenance overhead.
- **Figma** — Andrea isn't designing components. Canva covers everything she ships in 10 weeks.
- **Discord / Slack** — no group chat to host yet. Event #1 is event-attendee-list-driven, not gathering-platform-driven. The persistent group chat comes after the room produces evidence worth gathering around (Event #3+).
- **Patreon / Memberful** — no subscription product yet. Year-2 conversation per BOS doctrine.

Resist tool-stack creep. Every tool added is a tool to maintain, a credential to share, and a learning curve to absorb. The default answer to "should we add X?" is no until the existing stack visibly fails the job.
