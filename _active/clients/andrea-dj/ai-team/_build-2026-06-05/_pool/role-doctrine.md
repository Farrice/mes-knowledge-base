## The Doctrine

At Event #1, Andrea is the MC, the host, and the curator. A hired DJ plays. Andrea has a microphone and a room to read. The role is calibrated this way because Resonance is a curation brand, and the doctrine has to make that legible from the first event onward. The room is the show. Andrea is the architect of the room. The DJ is the architect's specialist, executing the playlist Andrea built. Conflating the two on Event #1 would split her attention across two demanding jobs and degrade both, which is exactly the failure Event #1 cannot afford.

Andrea hosts. Andrea does not perform. At Event #1, she is the MC, the host, the curator, and the room-worker. She has a microphone, not a DJ controller. She welcomes guests at the door, opens the room briefly from the mic, works the floor between songs, and reads the room as the curator. The music is played by a separate DJ-of-record — a hire, locked by Week 4, named publicly Week 7. Andrea will DJ at a future Resonance event when the room is proven and a co-host has emerged who can carry the floor while she plays. Until then, the doctrine holds.

## What Andrea Does on the Day

The role decomposes into eight time blocks across roughly four hours. Each block has one job, no overlap. The discipline is to stay inside the block — every drift into another time block's responsibility (Andrea handling load-in at T-90 because the DJ is late, or Andrea answering venue-staff questions during T+30 floor-work) is an attention split that costs the room what it came for.

| Time block | Andrea's role |
|---|---|
| T-90 min → T-30 min | Pre-arrival walkthrough — confirms music, lighting, water/snack flow, staff briefing, door protocol with DJ + photographer + door host |
| T-30 min → T-0 (doors) | Final check, mic test, Andrea takes position at door OR adjacent (depending on whether door host is solo or assisted) |
| T-0 → T+15 (arrival window) | Welcomes every guest by name. Eye contact. Hand to shoulder if invited. The first impression of the room IS Andrea. |
| T+15 → T+30 (room settles) | Mic open, brief welcome (60-90 sec, NOT a speech — see opening script doctrine in Run-of-Show file when written). Sets the room. Steps back. |
| T+30 → T+90 (active room) | Works the floor. Checks in. Reads who's connecting, who needs an introduction, who looks lost. The curator on the move. |
| T+90 → T+120 (peak) | Mic-light (one short check-in midway). Continues to work the room. Mostly invisible — letting the music + curation do the work. |
| T+120 → T+140 (winding down) | Mic for the close (60-90 sec). Names the room. Thanks people. Sets the tone for what's next (newsletter, Event #2, exit interview). |
| T+140 → end | Goodbyes at the door. Same warmth as arrival. Books exit interviews on the spot for the 3-5 most-energized attendees. |

## What Andrea Does NOT Do on the Day

The negative space is as load-bearing as the positive space. Every item below is a job Andrea is structurally protected from owning so the room's central job — being read, worked, and made visible — gets her full attention. The discipline is not "Andrea is too important to do these"; the discipline is "these jobs have other owners and Andrea taking them on degrades the role they exist to support."

- Plays music. The DJ-of-record handles that. Andrea may sit next to the DJ booth for one or two songs (presence shot for photographer) but does not touch the controller.
- Manages logistics-in-the-moment. Door, ticket scanning, phone basket, ID checks are handled by paid or volunteer staff briefed pre-event.
- Captures social media live. Photographer + designated capture person handles all photo and video.
- Handles incidents. Floor host or designated staff escalates anything physical, verbal, or medical. Andrea is informed; staff resolves.

Andrea has ONE job during active room: read the room, work the floor, make the curation visible. Every other job has an owner.

## Why Andrea Is Not Performing

Three reasons. Each load-bearing. None of them are about Andrea's skill as a DJ — that's never been the question.

1. Attention split would degrade both jobs. Running a 50-person curated singles room is a demanding social job. Performing as a DJ is a demanding craft job. Splitting Andrea across both means neither lands at full quality. Event #1 is the proof event; the proof can't survive a split. The math is simple: a 50% MC and a 50% DJ produces a 50% room. Resonance can't earn Event #2 on a 50% room.
2. The proof is curation, not performance. Resonance's spine is curation + room mechanics + couples formed. Andrea's value proposition is that she's the one picking who's in the room and what plays. Performing puts her inside the music; the room needs her above the music, watching what the music is doing. The room is the show. Andrea is the architect of the room. The curator on the floor is what makes "I picked every person here" legible to the people who got picked.
3. Andrea's DJ arc is not yet ready for a 50-person curated singles room. This is her assessment, not anyone else's. The first DJ-of-record moment at a Resonance event should land for Andrea too — a future event where she's playing for a room she trusts, with no other job to do, where the performance is the thing she's giving the room and not the thing she's juggling against four other responsibilities. We are not rushing it. Forcing Event #1 to be Andrea's DJ-debut would deprive her of the future event that's actually built to honor that arc.

## Andrea's DJ Arc (Future)

DJing at a Resonance event is a future arc, not a never. The doctrine protects the future moment by refusing to force it into Event #1. Specifics:

- Event #2 or #3+ — Andrea decides. Likely after Event #1 proves the model and a co-MC or assistant-host candidate has surfaced (someone who can run the room while Andrea plays). The co-host emerging is the operational unlock; without that, Andrea-as-DJ recreates the attention split the doctrine exists to prevent.
- Public framing when it happens: "Andrea plays this one" — short, declarative, no fanfare. The room will already know her as a DJ by identity; the performance is the surface, not the headline.
- Signal to watch for: when Andrea says she's ready. Not before. The team does not pressure-test this. It is a creative and identity decision, not an operational one, and forcing the calendar produces a worse first-DJ-moment than waiting.

## The DJ-of-Record for Event #1

A separate person plays at Event #1. The selection is not a generic DJ hire; it is a casting decision about who can hold Andrea's curated set in a daytime curated singles room without making the music about themselves. Specifics:

- Decision lock: Week 4 (per Command Center Section 8 — staffing).
- Public announcement: Week 7 (T-minus 5-6 weeks from event). Until then, internal-only.
- Selection criteria: woman preferred (peer parity for Andrea + room reads as music-room, not nightclub); curatorial sensibility (soul, 90s house, Latin, classic Resonance sound — not EDM, not current pop); known to Andrea's network (warm trust; one degree out from JR or NYO alumni is ideal); daytime-comfort (not someone whose set lives at 1am).
- Backup: a second DJ confirmed by Week 6. No-show contingency.
- Pay structure: market rate for Chicago daytime curated set — $300-500 plus expenses. Locked in writing.

The DJ is not a guest performer. They're a hired collaborator playing Andrea's curated set. Andrea sends the playlist and the spine. The DJ plays. Andrea hosts. The relationship is closer to a session musician than a featured artist, and the public framing reflects that.

## Identity vs. Performance — The Line

The single most important distinction in this doctrine: Andrea is a DJ by identity. That stays canonical in her bio, her founder story, her brand voice, every photoshoot. Andrea is not performing as DJ at Event #1. That distinction holds across every public surface — captions, venue pitches, founder content, photography direction. The line below is what every piece of public-facing work checks itself against before it ships.

| Andrea IS | Andrea is NOT |
|---|---|
| A DJ by identity, craft, lineage | Performing as DJ at Event #1 |
| The curator of Resonance's music | The performer of Resonance's music (yet) |
| A music school + national youth orchestra-shaped person | A nightclub-DJ-shaped person |
| The reason the room sounds the way it sounds | The reason the room sounds the way it sounds in the moment |
| Future DJ at a Resonance event | Current DJ at Event #1 |

**Photography note:** hands-on-records, headphones-around-neck, at-the-decks-from-behind shots are identity — they show who Andrea is. Andrea-with-mic, welcoming, room-working shots are role — they show what Andrea does on the day of Event #1. The photoshoot brief covers both — see Shot Categories in 05-photoshoot-brief.md. The distinction matters because identity shots earn their place in the founder story and the long-arc brand voice; role shots earn their place in Event #1's specific captions and the day-of recap.

**Caption note:** "Andrea DJs" or "I'll be DJing" is incorrect for Event #1 copy. Correct frames: "Andrea hosts," "Andrea is your MC," "Andrea curates the music — [DJ NAME] plays it" (post-Week 7), "I'm running the room — [DJ NAME] is on the music" (Andrea voice, post-Week 7). Any caption that conflates the two collapses the doctrine and forces a correction the brand cannot afford in its first 10 weeks.
