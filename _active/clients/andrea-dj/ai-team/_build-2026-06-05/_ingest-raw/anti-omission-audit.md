Anti-Omission Audit — First-Time Event Producer Blind Spots
Part of the Pre-Launch Command Center (see 00-command-center.md).
The "what an experienced event producer would catch" checklist. 42 items grouped by category, each with stakes, owner, and escalation path.
Read this before every Andrea call. Update as items move from open → resolved.
How to Use This File
Every event that goes wrong goes wrong in the same way: someone assumed a thing was handled because it didn't appear on a list. The list is the defense. Every item below carries four things: what the item is, who owns it, what specifically breaks if it gets missed, and what to do when it breaks. The third and fourth columns are the ones first-time producers usually skip. They're also the columns that turn a setback into a recoverable Monday rather than a brand-ending Sunday night.
The reading order is: scan the table for what's open, read the stakes line so you remember why it's open, then assign or escalate. Sunday review means moving items from open to resolved and capturing new ones the week surfaced. The audit becomes more useful the more it's used; a static audit is a dead document.
Each item carries:
	•	Item — the thing to check / decide / build
	•	Stakes — what specifically breaks if this gets missed (the one sentence that makes the item un-skippable)
	•	Owner — Andrea / Farrice / both / hire
	•	Escalation — what to do when it goes wrong (not if; when)
	•	Priority — Must-have (M) before Event #1 / Nice-to-have (N) / Defer-to-Event-2 (D)
	•	Status — Open / In progress / Resolved
Goal: every Must-have lands by event day. Nice-to-haves land if time allows. Defer-to-Event-2 stays parked.
Weekly cadence: scan this file every Sunday review. Move items from Open → Resolved. Surface new items as they appear.
Category 1 — Legal / Liability (8 items)
Legal is the category where the most expensive mistakes are the cheapest to prevent. Every item below costs less than $500 to handle correctly and more than $50,000 to handle wrong. Treat the column as the highest-priority pre-event work after the venue lock.
1.1 — Event liability insurance
What: One-day policy from Eventbrite / Markel / Thimble. Coverage minimum $1M general liability. Required by most venues. Bind Week 1-2. Cost ~$150-$250.
Stakes: One unbound event with an injured attendee can end the brand, Andrea's financial life, and her housing situation in 90 days. The insurance is the difference between a setback and a catastrophe. Most venues won't sign a contract without proof of coverage in hand.
Owner: Farrice.
Escalation: If a binder isn't issued by T-30, halt all ticket sales until it lands. No exceptions, no soft launches. T-21 means the venue contract is renegotiated or the event is rescheduled.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
1.2 — Photo release form
What: Every attendee signs digitally (Tally form or check-in QR) consenting to photography use for marketing. Released for: brand-owned IG, newsletter, press, sponsor pitches.
Stakes: Without this, none of the photoshoot becomes usable content. Post-event recap dies. Sponsor pitches lose their proof-image library. One attendee who shows up in a recap and didn't consent can demand takedown and force a brand-trust crisis at exactly the moment Event #2 needs momentum.
Owner: Farrice (form build), Andrea (review of release language).
Escalation: If release isn't in the application flow by T-21, every attendee gets a paper release at door check-in. Any attendee who declines is photo-redacted in post-event content.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
1.3 — Code of Conduct
What: 1-pager covering respectful conduct, no harassment, no unwanted contact, hosts can ask anyone to leave. Sent with confirmation email + posted at door.
Stakes: Resonance is a singles room. The risk of one attendee crossing a line is non-zero, and the cost of being unprepared is brand-defining. A documented Code of Conduct lets Andrea ask anyone to leave without negotiation, gives staff a script when they need one, and protects every other attendee from a moment that could turn the brand's reputation into a cautionary tale.
Owner: Both (Andrea owns the tone, Farrice owns the legal-light language).
Escalation: If a CoC violation occurs at Event #1, the response is pre-rehearsed: floor host removes the person, Andrea is informed within 60 seconds, refund issued the next business day if the violator paid for the ticket, written incident note filed within 24 hours.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
1.4 — Refund policy
What: Written and on the landing page before ticket sales open. Recommend full refund 7+ days out, 50% 3-7 days, no refund within 72 hours. Hardship exceptions at Andrea's discretion.
Stakes: Without a written policy, every refund request becomes a one-off negotiation that consumes Andrea's email hours and erodes the consistency the brand needs. With one, declines are easy and approvals feel fair.
Owner: Farrice (writes), Andrea (signs off).
Escalation: Hardship cases (medical, family emergency, financial) get an Andrea-personal reply with a refund OR a guaranteed-spot transfer to Event #2. Template lives in Farrice's drafts folder.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
1.5 — ADA accessibility
What: Venue must have wheelchair access OR explicit communication on landing page if not. Step-by-step accessibility info (parking, entry, restroom) in confirmation email.
Stakes: One attendee who arrives in a wheelchair and can't enter is a brand crisis Resonance cannot afford in Event #1. The right answer is to confirm access pre-pitch OR name the limit publicly. The wrong answer is to hope nobody asks.
Owner: Andrea (venue conversation), Farrice (landing page copy).
Escalation: If venue lacks step-free access, the landing page names it: "This venue has [X] step at entry. If that's a barrier for you, reach out and we'll figure it out together." Compromise venues are out of consideration past T-30.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
1.6 — Sound permit / decibel limits
What: Chicago noise ordinance allows daytime music up to ~70-80dB residential / 80-90dB commercial. Venue confirms compliance. Decibel meter app on photographer or staff phone to monitor live.
Stakes: A noise complaint mid-event can end the room in 15 minutes if a neighbor calls 311 and the venue lease has decibel terms. Pre-confirming the ceiling protects the venue relationship and the room. The downside risk is large; the prep is one conversation and a free phone app.
Owner: Farrice (venue confirmation), hired sound or volunteer (live monitoring).
Escalation: If decibels approach the ceiling, DJ-of-record reduces gain in 2-3dB steps. If a neighbor complains, Andrea steps to the door with the venue's contact info ready and the music drops 5dB until resolved.
Priority: N. Status: Open.
1.7 — Alcohol-related liability
What: Verify venue policy. Even though Resonance is sober, if venue allows BYO in their lease, sign waiver excluding BYO from Resonance event.
Stakes: Resonance's sober Line protects the brand only if it's enforced operationally. If the venue's lease permits BYO and an attendee shows up with a flask, the room's sober claim collapses on Event #1 — and the photo evidence circulates faster than any apology.
Owner: Farrice.
Escalation: If an attendee arrives visibly intoxicated, floor host quietly removes them at door, refund issued the next day with a private note from Andrea. No negotiation in the moment.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
1.8 — Tax / business entity
What: Is Andrea operating as sole proprietor or LLC? Income from Event #1 hits her taxes. Don't delay LLC formation past Event #2 if Resonance continues.
Stakes: Sole prop is fine for Event #1 revenue. LLC matters once liability surface expands across multiple events. The risk of skipping LLC formation past Event #2 is personal-asset exposure that compounds with every event.
Owner: Andrea (with accountant).
Escalation: If Resonance Event #1 nets >$1,000 profit AND Event #2 is confirmed, LLC formation begins within 30 days of Event #1 post-mortem.
Priority: D. Status: Defer to Event 2.
Category 2 — Day-of Logistics (12 items)
Day-of is where preparation becomes operations. Every item below is a Saturday-afternoon decision someone has to make in the moment, and the goal of the column is to make as many of those decisions on a Tuesday three weeks earlier as possible. The room runs on the work that was done before the room existed.
2.1 — Check-in flow at door
What: 40-50 attendees arriving in ~20 min window. Need pre-printed name list, check-off system, QR scanner or paper list, 1-2 greeters minimum. Plan for arrival rush.
Stakes: A 20-minute arrival rush with one bottleneck greeter produces a line outside the door. The first impression of Resonance becomes "I waited 12 minutes in the heat to get in" instead of "Andrea welcomed me by name." The room's whole spine — curation, care, attention — collapses at the threshold.
Owner: Farrice plus a hired or volunteer door host.
Escalation: If arrival rush exceeds 6 people queued, second greeter splits the line by last name (A-L / M-Z). Andrea remains at the welcome position; logistics is handled by the door host so Andrea can keep her attention on the people, not the clipboard.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
2.2 — Phone basket security
What: Labeled baskets, one phone per slot, attendees claim by name. Someone monitors. Phones not stolen, not stepped on, not visible from outside.
Stakes: Phones-in-basket is one of Resonance's three load-bearing mechanics. A single missing phone on Event #1 is a brand-defining incident that turns the basket from a feature into a liability for every future event. Insurance doesn't cover this; trust does, and trust is built operationally.
Owner: Both — Farrice owns the physical setup, the door host owns live monitoring.
Escalation: If a phone is misplaced at end-of-event, dedicated 15-minute search by all staff before any attendee leaves. If still missing, immediate replacement offer (up to $1,000) plus written incident note. Brand commits to making attendee whole faster than they expected.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
2.3 — Bathroom queue management
What: At 50 people in a 3-hour event, expect 1.5-2 restroom visits per attendee, i.e. 75-100 visits across 3 hours. Venue's restroom capacity (count the toilets) determines if this is a bottleneck.
Stakes: Bathroom queues are the silent destroyer of room momentum. If a 6-person line forms at the women's restroom in the middle of the peak, half the floor leaves the music and the room's emotional temperature drops. Bathroom math is unsexy and load-bearing.
Owner: Farrice (venue confirmation).
Escalation: If venue has fewer than 2 restrooms for 50 people, escalate to Andrea: either reduce attendance to 35-40 OR add an attendant who keeps the line moving. Don't event-day a queue you can predict.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
2.4 — Water + snack table
What: Non-alcoholic drinks (sparkling, iced tea, kombucha), light snacks (dried fruit, nuts, dark chocolate). Stocked before event, refilled mid-event. Place where it doesn't compete with the dance floor.
Stakes: The drink table is the negotiation space between the floor and the room's edge. It's where strangers introduce themselves between songs and where Andrea reads who's connecting. An understocked table forces the room outdoors or to the bar (where the brand's sober Line lives) and breaks the room's contained intimacy.
Owner: Both (Andrea owns aesthetic, Farrice owns logistics and refill).
Escalation: If drinks run low at T+90, designated runner restocks within 15 minutes. Pre-position 30% excess inventory in venue closet or kitchen so refill is a 5-minute task, not a CVS run.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
2.5 — Coat check / bag storage
What: Even at 2pm in July, attendees arrive with bags. Designated corner or closet, monitored. NOT on the dance floor.
Stakes: A purse on the floor is a tripping hazard at the exact moment two attendees are starting to dance together. Bag storage is a small operational thing that protects the room from one accidental injury.
Owner: Both.
Escalation: If venue lacks a dedicated coat area, door host designates a corner with chair rows and a visible-but-discreet monitor.
Priority: N. Status: Open.
2.6 — Decibel monitoring
What: Phone app (NIOSH SLM, free) running during music. Andrea or volunteer keeps eye on it. Sustained >85dB triggers a step-down. Protects venue relationship + neighbors.
Stakes: Sustained high-decibel exposure is a long-tail liability (hearing damage claims) and a short-tail venue risk (lease violation). Both end Event #2 plans before they start.
Owner: Hired sound person OR designated volunteer with phone app.
Escalation: Sustained >85dB for 60 seconds means immediate 3dB step-down. Sustained >90dB means DJ stops mid-song, room takes a 30-second breath, music resumes at a calibrated level. Andrea names the moment from the mic if it happens.
Priority: N. Status: Open.
2.7 — Lighting as daylight shifts
What: At 5pm Saturday in July, daylight is still strong. By 5:15pm it starts shifting. Confirm venue has warm lamps to layer in as natural light fades. No overhead fluorescents — they kill the mood.
Stakes: Light is one of the three sensory variables the brand controls (with music and curation). Bad light at the close turns the room from "the song that bent us toward each other" into "we should probably leave." The wrong lighting at the wrong moment undoes 90 minutes of room-building.
Owner: Andrea.
Escalation: If venue lighting is overhead-fluorescent only, Andrea brings 4-6 portable warm-tone lamps and positions them at room corners and the drink table. Budget line item: $100-200, reusable across all future events.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
2.8 — Emergency exits walkthrough
What: Andrea + 2-3 staff know all exits before event. Communicated to attendees verbally at welcome or just signposted. Required by code.
Stakes: Fire-code violation is a venue-closing event. Beyond compliance, a staff team that can't name the second exit in a moment of need is a staff team that fails the room when it matters most. The walkthrough is 5 minutes; the cost of skipping it is unbounded.
Owner: Farrice (walkthrough lead).
Escalation: Pre-event briefing includes exit identification at T-60. Door host carries laminated venue map with exits marked. Andrea names the primary exit in the opening mic moment as a quiet code-comply gesture.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
2.9 — First-aid kit
What: Basic kit (bandaids, pain reliever, electrolyte packets, tampons/pads). Stored visible-but-not-prominent. Andrea knows where.
Stakes: A blister on the dance floor, a headache mid-event, a period emergency — every one of these is a small moment that gets resolved invisibly if the kit exists and visibly if it doesn't. The kit is care infrastructure.
Owner: Both.
Escalation: Pre-event check at T-90 confirms kit is stocked. If an attendee asks for something not in the kit, door host fast-walks to nearest CVS within 10 minutes.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
2.10 — Incident protocol
What: What if someone (attendee or staff) gets hurt, arrives drunk from pre-gaming, overwhelmed, or harassed? Pre-decided script for who handles, where to take them, when to call 911, when to ask someone to leave.
Stakes: A first-time producer who hasn't pre-rehearsed an incident response will improvise badly under stress. The improvisation becomes the brand story. Pre-deciding the script means the response is calibrated, repeatable, and survives the moment with the brand intact.
Owner: Andrea (script ownership), all staff (rehearsal).
Escalation: Floor host owns first-line response and escalates to Andrea only if intervention beyond a quiet conversation is required. 911 threshold is named in writing in the briefing doc. No one improvises medical or legal decisions.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
2.11 — Trash + cleanup plan
What: Who picks up cups and napkins during the event, who breaks down at end. Venue may require restoration to original state. Set 30-min cleanup window post-event.
Stakes: A trashed venue is a venue that doesn't host Event #2. The cleanup is the negotiation that determines whether the venue relationship compounds or dies on Event #1.
Owner: Farrice + hired or volunteer cleanup helper.
Escalation: Pre-event walkthrough captures "before" state. T+135 cleanup begins (during the close-out music). T+170 final pass with venue contact for sign-off. If venue flags damage, immediate written acknowledgment + repair plan within 48 hours.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
2.12 — DJ setup load-in/load-out timing
What: DJ-of-record gear arrives ~12pm (2 hours before 2pm doors). Out by 6pm (1 hour after 5pm end). Loading dock or street access? Permit needed?
Stakes: Load-in is when most first-event production failures happen — a gear cable missing, a venue power outlet that doesn't carry the DJ's draw, a loading dock that requires a permit nobody pulled. Pre-confirming the venue's load-in conditions means the room is ready when doors open, not 20 minutes after.
Owner: Andrea coordinates with DJ-of-record; Farrice handles venue/permit logistics.
Escalation: If load-in slips past T-60, opening time slides 15 minutes (Andrea names it from the door). If gear failure occurs at setup, DJ-of-record has backup plan documented in their contract; otherwise venue-house sound is the fallback.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
Category 3 — Staff + Volunteer Briefing (5 items)
The staff is the room's nervous system. They see what Andrea doesn't have time to see, they catch what would otherwise become an incident, they make Andrea's host role possible by absorbing every job that isn't reading-the-room. A briefed staff produces a calm room; an unbriefed one produces a room that asks Andrea to be in three places at once.
3.1 — Staff arrival time
What: Staff arrive 1.5-2 hours before doors (12pm for 2pm event). Briefing happens at 12:30pm. No staff arriving "right at door time" — they need orientation.
Stakes: Staff who arrive at door time have never seen the room, don't know where the exits are, and meet each other for the first time while attendees are walking through the door. The result is a staff that improvises instead of executes, which is the opposite of what Resonance promises.
Owner: Farrice.
Escalation: If a staff member is going to be late, they're reassigned to a non-floor role on arrival (cleanup, runner). Door host and floor host roles require pre-briefing; no exceptions.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
3.2 — Briefing script
What: 15-minute walkthrough covering code of conduct, incident protocol, who handles what, walkie-talkie or group text channel for the duration, exit interview process.
Stakes: Without a briefing script, every event has a different staff experience and the same mistakes repeat across events. With one, Event #1 produces a transferable runbook that makes Event #2's staff briefing 50% faster.
Owner: Both — Andrea owns the room-philosophy section, Farrice owns the operational section.
Escalation: Briefing script lives in pre-launch/15-staff-briefing-script.md (to be written Week 6). Post-event AAR identifies one section to add, remove, or sharpen for next event.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
3.3 — Communication channel during event
What: Group text or Slack channel for staff. Loud music means no calls, only text. Pre-event tested.
Stakes: A staff that can't reach each other across the room is a staff that can't coordinate when a moment requires coordination. Texts only because the music is too loud for calls; pre-tested because untested tools fail in production.
Owner: Farrice.
Escalation: If the channel goes down during event, designated runner (Farrice or pre-named volunteer) carries verbal updates between Andrea, door host, and floor host. Channel-down is a 10-minute fix, not an event-killer.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
3.4 — Volunteer thank-you protocol
What: Who delivers what to whom post-event. Don't leave it informal. Write down: "Maria gets the bottle of wine, Jen gets the Resonance tote, Lou gets dinner on me."
Stakes: Volunteers who feel taken-for-granted don't come back, and their absence shows up in Event #2 staffing math. A specific named thank-you turns one-time volunteers into recurring crew, which is how the staff cost stays low across multiple events.
Owner: Andrea.
Escalation: Thank-yous delivered within 7 days of event. If a volunteer didn't get one, Andrea sends a hand-written note within 14 days — the recovery costs nothing and prevents a quiet brand loss.
Priority: N. Status: Open.
3.5 — Backup staff
What: Backup names for the photographer, the ticket worker, the music backup. One name each, ready to call if primary cancels in the last 48 hours.
Stakes: Cancellations happen in the last 48 hours roughly 15% of the time. A first event without backups is an event where one phone call from a photographer kills the entire visual record. Backups are insurance, not paranoia.
Owner: Farrice.
Escalation: If primary cancels at T-48, backup is called immediately. If backup is unreachable at T-24, role is absorbed: Farrice covers photo with iPhone, Andrea absorbs ticket-worker role with door host, DJ-of-record's backup is the named secondary DJ from Section 5.2.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
Category 4 — Attendee Experience (8 items)
Attendee experience is the column where the brand earns its second event. Every touchpoint between RSVP and post-event follow-up is a chance to confirm what the manifesto promised or to undermine it. The standard isn't "no complaints" — it's "an attendee who didn't connect still leaves thinking the room was built with care."
4.1 — Confirmation email cadence
What: At RSVP, T-14 days (preview + what to expect), T-7 (logistics + arrival), T-1 day (final reminder + parking + transit). Sequence built in Beehiiv.
Stakes: Attendees who paid for a ticket and then hear nothing for 21 days will doubt their decision by the time the event arrives. The cadence is what keeps the "yes" alive. Each email also re-states the room's mechanics (sober, daytime, phones off) so no one is surprised at the door.
Owner: Farrice.
Escalation: If any email in the sequence fails to send, manual send within 2 hours of detection. T-1 reminder is non-negotiable; if it fails, Andrea sends a personal IG DM to every attendee instead.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
4.2 — Day-of reminder text
What: Separate from email, at T-3 hours (morning of event). Phone list collected during application. Sent via group text or paid SMS tool.
Stakes: Email gets buried. Text gets seen. A T-3 text reduces no-shows by roughly 5-8 percentage points in event-industry data — at 50 attendees, that's 2-4 people who would have ghosted but show up because their phone buzzed.
Owner: Andrea (her phone, her voice).
Escalation: If T-3 text doesn't fire, Andrea sends individually-addressed texts to anyone she has a number for. Goal: every attendee gets one message Saturday morning.
Priority: N. Status: Open.
4.3 — Parking + transit info
What: Venue's parking situation, nearest CTA / Metra, rideshare drop-off point. Sent T-7 + T-1.
Stakes: An attendee circling for parking at 2:05 misses the welcome window and arrives flustered. Logistics info sent in advance is the difference between attendees arriving curious and attendees arriving annoyed.
Owner: Farrice.
Escalation: If parking is a known issue at the venue, the T-7 email names it: "Street parking is tight near the venue. Plan to arrive by 1:45 or take the [CTA line] to [stop]."
Priority: M. Status: Open.
4.4 — Arrival window flexibility
What: Door opens 2pm, music starts 2:15. Latest arrival window before the floor is closed (recommend 2:45 hard cutoff to protect the room's beginning). Communicated in confirmation.
Stakes: Late arrivals break the room's opening. An attendee walking in at 3:15 during a peak moment is a disruption to the 47 people who showed up on time. The cutoff exists to protect the early arrivers.
Owner: Both.
Escalation: Door closes at 2:45. Anyone arriving after gets a private text from the door host: "Doors closed at 2:45 to protect the room. We'll save your spot for Event #2 with full credit." Refund policy applies as written; this isn't a refund discussion at the door.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
4.5 — Exit interview capture
What: Per 05-ops/05-exit-interview-protocol.md. Set up at exit: one person with phone + question card asking 3-5 questions. Recordings (audio or written) saved within 24 hours.
Stakes: Exit interviews are the source material for Event #2's positioning, the founder story's next chapter, and the proof points that make press coverage possible. Without them, post-event content is generic and Event #2 is positioned from memory instead of evidence.
Owner: Andrea (script, decides who to interview), hired or volunteer interviewer (capture).
Escalation: If exit interviewer cancels, Andrea conducts 3-5 interviews herself in the final 20 minutes before close. Better fewer interviews captured well than 10 captured poorly.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
4.6 — Photo of every attendee on entry
What: Optional. With consent (via release form), capture a Polaroid-style portrait of each guest. Becomes content + memento. Test if Andrea wants this.
Stakes: Entry portraits create a per-attendee artifact and produce an entire content library in one afternoon. The risk is operational: if the photographer is also doing portraits, the room's candids suffer. Decide one or the other; don't split.
Owner: Both.
Escalation: If portrait setup slows door flow, suspend portraits and pivot to candid coverage only. Don't sacrifice the welcome for the souvenir.
Priority: N. Status: Open.
4.7 — Post-event thank-you within 24 hours
What: Automated Beehiiv email going to every confirmed attendee. Says: "Thank you for being in the room. If you met someone, we want to know. If you didn't, we want to know what almost worked."
Stakes: The 24-hour window is when the room is still alive in attendees' memory. After 72 hours, the response rate drops by half. The email is also where the couples-formed metric starts; without it, Resonance has no way to count the thing it claims to count.
Owner: Farrice.
Escalation: If automation fails, manual send within 36 hours. Andrea adds a personal P.S. to any attendee she had a specific conversation with on the day.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
4.8 — Couples follow-up at T+30 days
What: If any couples form, light follow-up to ask "did you see them again?" Captures the actual brand metric (couples formed).
Stakes: Couples formed is the brand's success metric per BOS Line 5. Without the T+30 capture, the brand can't tell the story of what Resonance actually does. Every couple confirmed becomes a load-bearing proof point for Event #2's positioning and every venue/press pitch from there forward.
Owner: Andrea.
Escalation: If a couple stops responding, Andrea sends one final check-in at T+90. After that, the data point stays as "uncertain" in the post-event AAR.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
Category 5 — Contingencies (6 items)
Contingencies are the column that separates first-time producers from experienced ones. An experienced producer assumes something will go wrong and has the response pre-built; a first-time producer assumes everything will go right and improvises when it doesn't. Every item below is a pre-built response to a thing that has, in some version of this event, gone wrong.
5.1 — Venue back-out backup
What: If locked venue backs out 7-14 days before, what's Plan B? Identified Tier 3 friend's house OR a sister venue that's said yes informally.
Stakes: Venue back-out is rare but brand-defining. Without a Plan B, Event #1 becomes a public refund event. With one, the venue change is a single email to attendees and a story Andrea tells later about the room that almost wasn't.
Owner: Both.
Escalation: Plan B is named in writing by Week 3. If primary venue cancels at T-14, Plan B is activated within 48 hours. Attendees receive update email + revised parking/transit info. Refund offered to anyone the new venue doesn't work for, no questions asked.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
5.2 — DJ backup
What: If DJ-of-record (or JR if used) is sick or has an emergency, who steps in? Pre-arranged "you might get called."
Stakes: A music failure at a music-led event is a brand failure. Without a backup, the room defaults to a playlist on a laptop — which is exactly the experience Resonance is built to be the opposite of.
Owner: Andrea (relationship), Farrice (logistics).
Escalation: Backup DJ is identified by Week 6, confirmed in writing with a $100 retainer for being on-call. If primary cancels at T-48 or later, backup is called and paid full rate. Andrea calls personally — the relationship matters more than the email.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
5.3 — Photographer backup
What: If photographer cancels 24-48 hours out, who takes their place? At minimum, a friend with a good iPhone + a list of must-capture moments.
Stakes: Photo evidence is the post-event content engine and the next venue's pitch deck. Without it, Event #2 is positioned from memory; with it, every later event has visual proof of what the room looks like.
Owner: Farrice.
Escalation: If primary cancels, named backup (one friend with photo skill + iPhone) is called immediately. Must-capture shot list lives in 05-photoshoot-brief.md. Farrice carries backup iPhone for redundancy.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
5.4 — Weather contingency
What: If event has any outdoor element (rooftop, patio, courtyard) and Chicago weather turns, what's the inside-only plan? Communicated to attendees morning-of if needed.
Stakes: A surprise thunderstorm at 1:45 with a rooftop-dependent venue means improvising a 50-person rearrangement at the moment doors are opening. Pre-deciding the inside-only floor plan means the rearrangement is one room change, not a moving puzzle.
Owner: Both.
Escalation: Weather check at T-24 and T-3. If rain expected, attendees get a morning-of email confirming the inside-only plan + same start time. Outdoor element gets reframed as "we'll come back to the outdoor floor next event."
Priority: N. Status: Open.
5.5 — No-show rate
What: Industry average for paid singles events is 10-20%. Plan for 8-10 no-shows out of 50 RSVPs. Overbook by 5-8? OR maintain waitlist that converts within 24 hours of cancellation. Andrea decides — overbooking risks crowding; waitlist risks empty seats.
Stakes: No-show math defines the room's actual headcount. Underplanning produces a half-empty floor (brand-damaging at Event #1). Overplanning produces a crowded room (mechanic-damaging — Resonance's curation breaks if the room exceeds capacity).
Owner: Andrea.
Escalation: Andrea picks one approach by Week 5 and locks it. If overbooking, capacity is set at 55 confirmed for 50-target. If waitlist, list is built to 15-20 names with auto-convert language: "A spot opened up. Hell-yes to Saturday?"
Priority: M. Status: Open.
5.6 — Last-minute application surge handling
What: If applications spike Week 8-9, who reviews and approves or declines? Process bottleneck is Andrea. Pre-write the decline script so she's not writing each one.
Stakes: Application surge in the final two weeks is a good problem made worse by improvisation. Each unprocessed application is a person waiting, and the waiting period is when goodwill leaks. The decline script + pre-set criteria let Andrea process 30 applications in 60 minutes instead of 30 applications across a week of decision fatigue.
Owner: Both — Farrice owns the system, Andrea owns the judgment.
Escalation: If application volume exceeds 30/week in Week 8-9, application review moves to scheduled daily 30-minute blocks. Farrice pre-sorts by hell-yes signal so Andrea's blocks are decision-making, not screening.
Priority: N. Status: Open.
Category 6 — Post-Event (3 items)
Post-event is the column most first-time producers under-invest in because they're exhausted by the time it starts. That exhaustion is exactly why the work has to be pre-scheduled, not improvised. The 48 hours after the room closes are when the content library, the story, and the next-event narrative get built — or lost.
6.1 — Photo selection within 48 hours
What: Photographer delivers 100+ raw frames. Andrea + Farrice select 15-25 within 48 hours. Without this turnaround, post-event content momentum dies.
Stakes: Post-event content has a 72-hour attention window. After that, the room is yesterday's room and the photo library becomes archive material instead of launch material. A 48-hour selection sprint is how the post-event content engine fires on time.
Owner: Both.
Escalation: If photographer delivers late, photo subset is selected from Farrice's iPhone candids and Andrea's Stories captures. Content goes live T+72 regardless. Better imperfect content on time than perfect content too late.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
6.2 — Recap content cadence
What: First recap content goes live T+24 hours (one Story or one Reel). Long-form recap (carousel + caption) T+5-7 days. Story Pillar content (real moments) T+10-14 days.
Stakes: Cadence is what turns one event into three weeks of content. Skipping any of the three drops kills the compound. T+24 captures the room while it's still alive in attendees' phones. T+5-7 captures the narrative. T+10-14 captures the proof.
Owner: Andrea (voice), Farrice (production support).
Escalation: If T+24 slips, T+48 is the hard fallback. If T+5-7 slips, the long-form carousel ships as a Reel instead. The cadence flexes by 24-48 hours; it does not skip.
Priority: M. Status: Open.
6.3 — Refund-request window
What: Open for 7 days post-event for genuinely unhappy attendees. Pre-decided: Andrea responds personally, not by template. Volume should be tiny.
Stakes: Refund requests at Event #1 are rare but high-signal — every one is a brand-improvement note from someone who paid to find out. Treating them as customer-service tickets instead of brand-research is the cheap move; treating them as research is what makes Event #2 better.
Owner: Andrea.
Escalation: Each refund request gets a personal Andrea reply within 48 hours. Refund issued in full if the request is genuine. Detailed feedback captured into AAR for Event #2.
Priority: M. Status: Open.

What's NOT in this audit (intentionally)
A first-event audit is also defined by what it deliberately leaves out. Every line below is a category that earns its way into a future audit; none of them belong in Event #1's scope. Naming the exclusions in writing protects the team from the slow scope creep that turns a 10-week sprint into a 16-week one.
	•	Sponsor relationships — Resonance doesn't take sponsors for Event #1 (BOS doctrine).
	•	Press strategy — defer to Event #2 minimum. Press for Event #1 happens organically via the manifesto post + recap content, not via active pitching.
	•	Influencer partnerships — actively avoid. Curation can't survive influencer-tier guests who didn't apply.
	•	Affiliate / referral programs — too early. Phase 2 conversation.
	•	Subscription / membership model — Resonance Subscription is the Year-2 vision (Anchor §5). Don't build it pre-Event #1.
Resist the urge to add scope. Event #1 is one job: make 50 people meet someone in a daytime sober dance room. Every additional system is a future-event problem. If a category above starts feeling urgent before Event #1 ships, that's the audit's job to catch.
