# Property Dossier — Image Art Direction

## Selection order

1. Choose the photograph that communicates the property's spatial promise in under one second.
2. Prefer depth, connected rooms, natural light, and a clear visual path over decorative detail.
3. Use a feature image only when the feature is the subject of the board.
4. Reject images with competing focal points, severe lens distortion, clipped highlights, visible clutter, or no clean text-safe region.

## Crop rules

- Property occupies at least 56% of a listing board.
- Preserve architectural verticals; never crop a doorway or cabinet line at an awkward sliver.
- Keep the room's destination visible: balcony door, kitchen run, seating area, or building entrance.
- Text never sits over a visually active zone. If the image lacks negative space, use a separate paper field.
- One image earns dominance. Secondary images are evidence, not decoration.
- Avoid filters that change wall, flooring, cabinet, or fixture color. Mild contrast and saturation restraint only.

## Mobile checks

- Headline readable at 25% zoom.
- Address, date, price, and CTA readable without pinching.
- The property remains recognizable as a thumbnail.
- No essential face, doorway, window, appliance, or balcony edge falls under Instagram UI zones.
- If the image and headline compete, remove copy before shrinking it.

## Rejection examples

- Four equal photos with no visual lead.
- Text layered over cabinetry, furniture, or window detail.
- Tight crops that make a room feel smaller than the source photograph.
- Decorative arches, stamps, rules, or metadata added only to fill space.
- Generic neighborhood imagery when actual listing photography exists.
- Feature labels that repeat what the photograph already proves.
