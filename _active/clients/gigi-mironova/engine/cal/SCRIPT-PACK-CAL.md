### Script (word for word)

[Packet on the table, both hands on it.]

Two hundred pages, a few days, and one of them decides whether you get a bill after you move in.

I spent sixteen years in litigation support before I sold a home. Reading a file against a clock was already my job.

Start with the reserve study. Percent funded separates dues that drift up from a bill you never budgeted for.

Then twelve months of minutes, because a repair gets argued about for a year before anyone is billed. There is one word to hunt for in there.

Then the insurance deductible, the delinquency figure, and litigation.

The word is discussed. Ask the board what is discussed and not yet billed.

Write HOA and I will send the five-page checklist.

## Video 11 · You do not have to empty it first · TH · keyword NET

Reader: the small landlord who thinks the tenant is the problem and is afraid of losing months of income.

### Hooks

1. [pattern-interrupt] "Your tenant is not what is costing you. The empty months are."
2. [stakes] "Empty it and you lose months of rent. Sell it occupied and you lose half the offers. There is a third answer."
3. [specificity] "You do not have to empty it first. Your lease decides who is even allowed to want it."  **RECOMMENDED**

### Script (word for word)

You do not have to empty it first. Your lease decides who is allowed to want it.

Owners think the tenant is the problem. It is the lease, and I read it first.

An investor is buying rent that already arrives. An owner-occupier is buying keys on closing day, and your lease can remove them.

There is also a notice rule most owners never read.

You give your tenant written notice that the property is for sale. For one hundred twenty days after that, showings run on twenty-four hours' notice, which can be by phone. Civil Code 1954.

Pull your lease this week and find the end date.

Write NET and I will run it both ways, occupied and vacant.

### Bullet version

1. Hook: "You do not have to empty it first. Your lease decides who is even allowed to want it."
2. Reframe: the tenant is not the problem. The lease is the variable.
3. Two pools: investor buys the rent, owner-occupier buys the keys.
4. Open the loop: there is a notice rule most owners have never read.
5. Close it: written notice of sale, then 120 days of showings on 24 hours' notice, which may be oral. Civil Code 1954.
6. Move: pull the lease and find the end date.
7. CTA: "Write NET and I will run your number both ways, occupied and vacant."
