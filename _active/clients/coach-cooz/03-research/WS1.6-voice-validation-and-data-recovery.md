### 1.4 The #1 viral Sheedy post (and its honest exception)

Sheedy's **19,878-like / 535K-play** post from March 31, 2026 actually opens with: *"Pay attention to what you feed… it either builds you or breaks you ✝️"*

This is a "you" opener — AND it's his top performer. So the rule isn't "never open with you." The rule is:
- **Short-form "you" openings work** when they are one imperative line followed by minimal body. Sheedy's post is 12 words of caption over a video.
- **Long-form "you" openings fail** when they become sustained scene narration telling the reader what he is doing, feeling, and thinking for paragraphs.

"Pay attention to what you feed" is an **instruction**, not a **narration**. It trusts the reader to place themselves in the frame. Batch 01's "You pull into the driveway, the engine goes off, for about 30 seconds you just sit there" is **projected inner monologue** — the writer is inside the reader's head narrating back to them. That's where "icky" lives.

They open in first person, earn the right to the reader's attention through specificity of their own experience, and pivot to "you" only briefly, at the moment of universal recognition, and usually only for 1-3 sentences.

### 1.6 When "you" IS appropriate (three contexts, all narrow)

1. **The POV one-line hook** (Lara Acosta template): `"POV: you stopped spending 10 hours writing a post"` — one line, punchy, then the body becomes I-led or instructional.
2. **The imperative one-liner** (Sheedy template): `"Pay attention to what you feed"` — instruction, not description. Under 12 words. Followed by minimal body or just a visual.
3. **The universal pivot** (Sheedy Easter post, Justin Welsh): After 3+ paragraphs of I-led story, ONE line that names the reader's condition. Not more than 2 sentences. Never a scene description of what the reader is doing right now.

**Everything else that starts with "you" is projected narration — the writer telling the reader what the reader is thinking, feeling, and doing. That is the Batch 01 failure mode. The reader can feel it, even if they can't name it. It reads as: *you don't know me, and you're pretending you do.***

### 1.7 How the I becomes universal (the mechanism)

This is the question Farrice was really asking: *how do the personal stories become universally resonant without the "you" crutch?*

From the data, three techniques:

**A. Specificity collapses into universality.** Sheedy doesn't write "I used to feel empty despite achievement." He writes "For years I've made myself king. My goals, my achievements, my identity. All of it built on the belief that if I just achieved enough, I'd feel like enough one day. But I never did." The specific word "king." The specific cadence of the three repetitions. The specific word "one day." Specificity gives the reader a concrete object to project themselves onto. Vague universals give nothing to land on. **Counter-intuitively: the MORE specific to the writer's own life, the MORE the reader projects.**

**B. The writer names the internal state, not the external scene.** Compare:
- Batch 01 Post 1: *"You pull into the driveway. The engine goes off. And for about 30 seconds, you just sit there."* ← EXTERNAL scene narration in you.
- Sheedy: *"I finally admitted that everything I was running toward was leaving me empty."* ← INTERNAL state named in I.
The reader can accept (A) = "I also do that thing externally" — but it feels invasive because the writer is watching them. The reader readily accepts (B) = "oh, he felt that, and I also feel that" — because the writer is pointing at his own chest, not theirs.

**C. The witness, not the narrator.** From the StartUpIndia post in WS1.5: *"I know a founder. Smart guy. Built something real."* This is a third-person peer observer — the gossip-you-weren't-supposed-to-hear voice. It's shareable, safer, and the reader inserts themselves into "smart guy" voluntarily instead of being told they are him. This is the Witness voice from voice-calibration-real.md. It's underused in Batch 01 (Post 14 is the only approximation and it's I-led).

### 1.8 The exceptions (where "you" narration does work)

To be honest about the data: there ARE contexts where sustained you-narration works. They are narrow:

1. **Carousel posts with instructional framing** (Dr. Carolyn Frost's "10 One-Minute Stress Resets"). The "you" is implied ("[if you want to reset stress] do X, do Y"). This is instructional copy, not storytelling.
2. **Ultra-short punchy sentences** — Luke Iha style, Harmon Brothers style. If the whole post is 40 words, "you" works because there's no room for it to become invasive.
3. **Satirical/comedic frame** — "You know that moment when..." as a comedic set-up. Requires the writer to break the fourth wall and admit they're performing.

Coach Cooz content is none of these. It's vulnerable story-based content for a guarded buyer who is specifically allergic to being told who he is. **Sustained you-narration is the worst possible voice for this audience.**

### 2.2 YouTube — wrapper bug fixed, 8 results recovered

**Wrapper bug in `execution/apify_client.py` line 318-326**: the `apidojo/youtube-scraper` actor rejected `searchQueries` and `maxResults`. The actual input schema (confirmed via web search of the Apify input-schema docs) expects:
- `keywords` (array) — for keyword search (NOT `searchQueries`)
- `maxItems` (int) — for result cap (NOT `maxResults`)
- Also accepts `startUrls`, `youtubeHandles`, `getTrending` as alternatives

**Fix applied** (commit would read "fix(apify): youtube wrapper uses keywords/maxItems not searchQueries/maxResults"):

```python
def cmd_youtube(args):
    run_input = {
        "keywords": [args.query],
        "maxItems": args.limit,
    }
    if args.transcript:
        run_input["downloadSubtitles"] = True
        run_input["subtitlesLanguage"] = "en"
    print(json.dumps(run_actor("youtube", run_input, args.limit), indent=2))
```

## 3. Recommended Voice Ruleset for Cooz Content

### 3.1 The governing rule (write this on the wall)

> **I-led story, you-pivot at the universal moment. Never sustained you-narration.**

The 2026 winning content from Justin Welsh, Lara Acosta, Dakota Robertson, Michael Sheedy, Dickie Bush, Nicolas Cole, Cole Schafer, and Kallaway is consistent: the personal story lives in first-person. The writer earns the right to the reader's attention through specific self-disclosure. Only after that trust is earned does "you" appear — and when it does, it appears as one pivot sentence (max two) that names what the reader now recognizes in themselves. Then the post ends.

### 3.2 The I-Perspective Formula (the new template)

Use this for every story-led post. This is distilled directly from Sheedy's Easter post (788 likes), Sheedy's Kelsey post (1,314 likes), Dakota Robertson's template, and Nicolas Cole's "Personal & Vulnerable" style.

```
1. OPENING LINE (first-person, declarative, specific):
   "I [verb] [specific object]." or "For [time period] I [behavior]." or
   "[Time marker] ago, I was [state]."

   Good: "For years I dragged my body around like an afterthought."
   Good: "Three years ago, I stopped recognizing the man in my mirror."
   Good: "I was 34 when I realized my body was the only thing I wasn't negotiating with."

   Bad: "You pull into the driveway."
   Bad: "You just walked through the front door."
   Bad: "Your wife asks what you want for dinner."

2. SPECIFIC EXPANSION (2-4 short paragraphs, all I):
   Name 2-3 concrete, sensory, non-generic details from your own life.
   The more specific, the more universal. Use proper nouns if possible.
   (Sheedy: "My goals, my achievements, my identity" — three-beat rhythm.)

3. INTERNAL TURNING POINT (in I):
   "I finally admitted that..." / "The moment happened when..." /
   "It took [event] for me to see..."
   Name the INTERNAL state. Not the external scene.

4. (Optional) PUBLIC DECLARATION LINE (in I):
   "This is what I do now." / "I decided..." / "I'm not doing that anymore."

5. THE YOU-PIVOT (maximum 2 sentences, first time "you" appears):
   ONE universal-condition sentence that the reader recognizes from the
   story they just read. Not a scene. Not a narration of their day.
   A statement of their condition.

   Good: "You can have the body, the business, the money and still be
          the most lost person in the room." (Sheedy)
   Good: "Pressure without recovery isn't sustainable. It's a countdown."
          (Existing Batch 01 Post 7 line — THIS line works, it's everything
          before it that doesn't.)

   Bad: "You pull into the driveway. The engine goes off. And for about 30
        seconds, you just sit there."
   Bad: "Your kids will get the leftover version. Your wife will get the
        efficient version."

6. CLOSE (short — a prayer, a brand line, a period, or silence):
   Sheedy ends with "Jesus is King 🙏🏼". Justin Welsh ends with a
   question. Kallaway ends with nothing. Cooz's options: a question,
   a resurrection tag, or silence. Never a CTA stacked on the emotional beat.
```

### 3.3 When "you" is allowed (three narrow contexts)

1. **The POV hook one-liner** (Lara Acosta style):
   `"POV: you stopped starting over."` — one line, then I-led body.
2. **The imperative one-liner** (Sheedy's viral style, 19k likes):
   `"Pay attention to what you feed."` — instructional, under 15 words, video or single image carries the rest.
3. **The universal-pivot sentence** (Sheedy's Easter post):
   After 3+ paragraphs of I-led story, one sentence max two that names the reader's condition. Never a scene.

**If "you" appears more than 5 times in a 300-word post, the post is failing the voice rule.** Apply Red Pen: circle every "you" / "your" and count.

### 3.4 When "you" is FORBIDDEN

- **Sustained you-scene narration**: "You walk through the door. You drop your keys. You snap at your wife." This is the Batch 01 failure mode. Zero of the 15 Batch 01 posts should do this.
- **You-projected inner monologue**: "You're already calculating how to optimize around it. You hear it but you're already thinking…" This presumes the reader's thoughts. Invasive.
- **You-narrated emotion**: "You feel the weight of it. You just don't have it in you." Same problem. Writer is inside the reader's head.
- **You-narrated history**: "You used to be the guy who…" — also invasive. Rewrite as "I used to be the guy who… and I know a lot of you recognize that version."

### 3.5 The 4 Content Voices (replacing the current 5-voice rotation)

The WS1.5 voice calibration doc has 5 voices (Scene 35%, Confession 20%, Reframe 20%, Witness 15%, Proof 10%). Given the voice validation data, **the Scene voice as currently defined is the problem voice.** It's the voice that produces all the "You walk through the door" openings. It needs to be either killed or rewritten.

**Proposed new 4-voice rotation**:

| Voice | % of posts | Perspective | Example opener |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Confession** (replaces Scene) | 40% | Pure I | "For years I dragged my body around like an afterthought." |
| **2. Reframe** | 25% | I with maxim pivot | "Most men think discipline is what's missing. It's not. I learned this the hard way." |
| **3. Witness** | 20% | Third-person peer | "I know a guy. Built something real. Raised. Now he's checking Slack at 2 AM." |
| **4. Proof** | 15% | I about a client | "Brian came to me 30 lbs heavier than he wanted to be. Here's what changed in 90 days." |

**What dies**: The Scene voice as written — "It's 6:47 PM on a Tuesday. You pull into the driveway…" — is retired. It's replaced by **Confession** which handles all the same emotional terrain but from the writer's own life, and only pivots to "you" in the final 1-2 sentences.

**What lives from Scene**: The specificity. The sensory detail. The emotional precision. All of it transfers to Confession, just in I. "It was 6:47 PM on a Tuesday. I pulled into my driveway and for 30 seconds I just sat there…" works. It feels entirely different. The reader is now allowed in voluntarily.

### 3.6 The CTA rotation stays (it was fine)

The 6-option CTA rotation in voice-calibration-real.md (Question / Confession Ending / Challenge / Soft Invite / Direct / No CTA) doesn't need changing. The problem was never the CTAs — it was the body.

### 4.1 The current version (fails the voice rule — pure you-scene narration)

> It's 6:47 PM on a Tuesday.
>

>

**What's wrong**: "You" appears 24 times in 220 words. The writer is narrating the reader's Tuesday evening in microscopic detail — what the reader is doing, feeling, thinking, and saying. The reader has no agency, no space to opt in. Forced intimacy = icky.

### 4.2 The rewrite (I-led, one universal pivot, Sheedy-template)

> It was 6:47 PM on a Tuesday. I was sitting in my driveway with the engine off, and I couldn't make myself walk through the door.
>
> Not because of a call. Not because of an email. Because I had given twelve hours to people who paid me, and I had nothing left for the people who didn't.
>
> I'd been patient all day. I'd been strategic. I'd held three rooms together. And I knew — before I even touched the handle — that my wife was going to ask me a simple question about dinner, and I was going to answer like a man with nothing in the tank.
>
> Because I was.
>
> My kids were going to get the leftover version that night. My wife was going to get the efficient version. The "I just need 20 minutes" version that always turns into the whole evening.
>
> The crazy part? The company had never been healthier. The KPIs looked great. Nobody at work saw anything wrong.
>
> They weren't the ones sitting in the driveway.
>
> **If your family is getting the leftover version of you while your LinkedIn profile is getting the best of you, you're not a bad husband. You're a man whose body ran out of fuel six months ago and nobody's told you yet.**
>
> When was the last time they got your best? Not your leftovers. The actual man.

**Word count**: 240 words (up 20). **Use of "you/your"**: 7 times total, 6 of them in the final two paragraphs. The first 180 words are pure I. The pivot is one sentence. Then a close question that's direct but not invasive because the story has earned it.

**Why this works**:
- The reader recognizes himself in my story voluntarily, instead of being told he is the protagonist.
- The specificity (6:47 PM, twelve hours, three rooms, 20 minutes → whole evening) transfers cleanly from you-mode to I-mode.
- The emotional truth is identical, but the ownership is reversed: it's the WRITER confessing, not the reader being narrated at.
- The pivot sentence ("If your family is getting the leftover version…") arrives after earned attention. It's permission-giving, not accusing.
- The closing question is now a genuine question, not a shaming interrogation.
