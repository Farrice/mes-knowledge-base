## 1. THIS WEEK IN HIS ICP'S WORLD

### Trend #1 — "AI Brain Fry" hit the mainstream (published 3 days ago)
**The moment:** On April 4, 2026, Axios published "AI agents are scrambling power users' brains" — reporting that founders using Claude Code, OpenClaw and similar agents "operate like slot machines," creating addictive loops that researchers are calling "AI brain fry." This follows a February Harvard Business Review piece and a UC Berkeley 8-month study finding that employees who embraced AI worked LONGER days, not shorter, and that 62% of associates report AI-related burnout. One researcher quoted: *"We're probably the first people to be collateral of these systems."*

**Why it matters to his ICP:** Cooz's founders bought AI thinking it would buy them back their evenings. Instead it handed them a slot machine. They're now working harder than they were in 2024, with the added shame that "the tool that was supposed to save me is the reason I'm up at 2 AM." This is the exact texture of "running on fumes" — except now it has a name and a Harvard study behind it.

**The specific angle Cooz can hit:** *"Your AI stack is writing code faster than your body can recover from the hours it's making you work. The hardware (you) is the bottleneck. Not the software. Not the prompt. Not the agent. You."* This is the Resurrection Coach thesis with zero edits — it landed on the news cycle this week for free.

### Trend #2 — Jack Dorsey fired 4,000 people and told every other founder "you're next"
**The moment:** In late February/early March 2026, Block CEO Jack Dorsey laid off 40% of his company (4,000 people) and publicly told other CEOs: *"I think most companies are late. Within the next year, I believe the majority of companies will reach the same conclusion and make similar structural changes."* The backlash is ugly — Bloomberg accused him of "AI-washing" old-fashioned cost-cutting, Block employees leaked internal messages, and Fortune ran a piece literally titled "3 questions every CEO needs to ask about the AI jobs doom loop." Every founder with 1–20 employees read that and heard: *figure it out or die.*

**Why it matters to his ICP:** Cooz's ICP doesn't have a 4,000-person headcount to cut. They have 1–20 employees, many of whom are friends or family. The Dorsey moment isn't inspiring to them — it's terrifying. It compounds the "checking Slack at 2 AM because I'm terrified" pain into a bigger fear: *"If Jack Dorsey has to fire half his company to survive, what chance do I have?"* They're responding by working MORE hours, not fewer. And their bodies are paying the bill.

**The specific angle Cooz can hit:** *"Jack Dorsey can fire 4,000 people. You can't. You have 6 employees who are basically family. So the only variable left for you to cut is sleep, food, and your own body. And that's exactly what you've been doing — and it's exactly why you haven't made a real strategic decision in 4 months."* The body/hardware move here: you can't out-think a threat you can't sleep through. Fix the hardware FIRST, then face the fire.

### Question A — "The 2 AM Slack check"

**Question:** *"Tell me about the last time you stood in the kitchen at 2 AM, phone in hand, checking Slack or Stripe or an email — and what you noticed about your body in that moment. Not your mind. Your body. What hurt? What felt numb? What did you NOT want to feel? And then tell me what you did next — did you close the laptop, or did you open it again?"*

**Why this question now:** The AI Brain Fry / Axios piece (3 days old) put the phrase "founders as collateral" in the news cycle. Your ICP spent the last 48 hours quietly recognizing themselves in a Harvard study. This question meets them exactly where they are this week — the 2 AM moment they're currently trying to pretend didn't happen last night.

**What I'd love you to talk about:**
- The physical details — the cold kitchen floor, the stiff neck, the headache you were ignoring, the stomach churn you attributed to "stress" but was actually 14 hours without real food
- The voice in your head that said "just five more minutes" and what it was really protecting you from
- What you now know — as the Resurrection Coach — that the 2 AM you didn't know. What would you have said to him? What would you have put in his hands BEFORE his mind?

### Question B — "Jack Dorsey can fire 4,000 people. You can't."

**Question:** *"Jack Dorsey just fired 40% of Block — 4,000 people — and told every other CEO they're next. Your clients don't have 4,000 people to cut. They have 6. Maybe 12. Some of them are family. Talk to me about the moment a founder realizes the only variable left for him to cut is his own body — and what that realization sounds like, looks like, feels like. Then tell me about the first time you cut YOUR own body and what it cost you."*

**Why this question now:** The Dorsey layoffs are THE founder-culture story of the last 30 days. Fortune, Bloomberg, CNN are all running "what do small founders do" takes. Your ICP is reading those articles at midnight. This question turns the biggest business news of the quarter into a body question — which is your entire angle.

**What I'd love you to talk about:**
- What "cutting your own body" actually looks like week-to-week — the skipped dinners, the 2-hour sleep debt, the "I'll eat later" that becomes once a day
- The moment YOU personally made a cut you can't take back — a missed family dinner, a health scare, a number on a bloodwork panel — and what it taught you
- The brave-choice pivot: the difference between a founder who cuts his body vs. a founder who cuts ONE thing in his business to protect his body. What does that one cut usually look like?

### Question C — "Bryan Johnson spends $2M not to die. You spend $0 and are slowly killing yourself on purpose."

**Question:** *"Bryan Johnson just opened a $1 million 'don't die' program and 1,500 founders applied in 30 hours. Most of your clients will never pay $1M. But they've all paid something — usually their family, their sleep, their presence — to stay in business. Talk to me about the gap between Bryan Johnson's protocol and the actual move your ICP needs to make this week. What's the $0 version of 'don't die'? And why is it harder than the $1M version — even though it's free?"*

**Why this question now:** The Bryan Johnson moment gave your ICP cultural permission to take their body seriously. But 99% of them are paralyzed by the gap between "Whoop strap" and "brain biological age scan." They need a bridge. Your answer IS the bridge, and this question makes you build it out loud.

**What I'd love you to talk about:**
- The one or two "free resurrection moves" you actually recommend in week 1 with a new client — the ones that cost $0 and 20 minutes a day, but that 90% of founders won't do
- Why doing the $0 version is emotionally harder than wiring $1M — what it exposes, what it admits, what it threatens about his identity as "a builder"
- Your own version: the first "free" thing you did to resurrect your body, and the exact resistance you had to overcome to do it
