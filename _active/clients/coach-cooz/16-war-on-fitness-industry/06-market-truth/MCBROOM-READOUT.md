# Cody McBroom / Tailored Coaching Method — Study-the-Mentor Readout

Context: McBroom appears as a positive endorser on coachcooz.com — "Coach Cooz goes all in. He's experienced, credible, and gets results. But mostly, he actually cares." (OBSERVED, coachcooz.com, "Trusted By The Pros" section). This is a study doc, not a takedown. Purpose: give Cooz the mechanism-level read of the operator he's reverse-engineering, so he copies the right 20% and skips the parts that only work at McBroom's scale.

## 1. Positioning — what the slogan actually filters

The line Cooz is chasing exists across McBroom-controlled channels in close variants: "We Help Average Individuals Achieve Above Average Physiques and Live Above Average Lives" (OBSERVED, Skool bio, skool.com/@cody-mcbroom-2832) and the paraphrase on the TCM About page, "helping the average person become above average… living an above average life," tied to the "Choose Hard™" brand story (OBSERVED, tailoredcoachingmethod.com/about). Important nuance: the literal current homepage H1 is NOT this line. Today's hero copy reads "Evidence-Based Online Fitness and Nutrition Coaching — for Sustainable Fat Loss, Muscle Growth, and Total Body Transformation" and "SUSTAINABLE WEIGHT LOSS IS WHAT WE DO" (OBSERVED, tailoredcoachingmethod.com). So "above average" is durable brand language used in bios/podcast descriptions/founder story, not the live front-door promise — the front door has moved toward outcome-language (fat loss, muscle, sustainability). Flag this for Cooz: the slogan he's reverse-engineering is McBroom's origin myth, not his current highest-intent sales page.

What it filters: IN — people who self-identify as "average" (not athletes, not already lean, not bodybuilding-adjacent) who want an *identity* upgrade ("above average life"), not just a number on a scale. OUT — people chasing extreme/elite transformation. The About page states the founding insight directly: the industry's push toward "more — more intensity, more restriction, and more cookie-cutter templates… wasn't delivering results" (OBSERVED, tailoredcoachingmethod.com/about). This is the same burnout-with-extreme-culture read in Farrice's frame — McBroom already partially claimed it at the industry level, years ago. He did not, however, go feeling/identity-first at the surface: the current homepage's mindset language is instrumental only — "mindset coaching to shift the way you think about dieting" (OBSERVED) — diet psychology, not worth/confidence psychology. That gap is still open.

## 2. The Machine

**Funnel:** Public CTAs are "Apply for Coaching" and "Book a Strategy Call" (OBSERVED, homepage); exact application/assessment questions are gated behind the form and not visible on-page (UNCONFIRMED). Entry offer beneath 1:1 is the **Tailored Trainer App** at $27/mo (OBSERVED via search-indexed page, tailoredcoachingmethod.com/personal-trainer-app).

**Content system:** The flagship moat is volume + duration. The podcast (now "Choose Hard with Cody McBroom," formerly "The Tailored Life Podcast") has **1,069 episodes**, released roughly 2–3x/week, averaging ~50 minutes (OBSERVED, podcast-platform listings — Apple/Spotify/Podbean aggregate data, high-confidence but not McBroom's own analytics dashboard). That's multiple years of compounding SEO, guest-network reciprocity, and audience trust — a structural asset a new solo operator cannot replicate on any timeline under a year. Supplementary blog at tailoredcoachingmethod.com/blog and an Instagram account reported at ~54K followers (single-source web estimate — UNCONFIRMED, not verified against Instagram directly).

**Offer stack + real pricing:** "All-Inclusive Coaching" (training + nutrition) at **$497/month**; nutrition-only at **$347/month** (OBSERVED, tailoredcoachingmethod.com/online-fitness-coaching-cost — this is McBroom's own self-published pricing-transparency article, a deliberate content play in itself: he ranks for "online fitness coaching cost" and uses the article to justify his own price point against a stated industry range of $250–$500/mo). No paid-in-full option exists by design (see retention below).

**Team/credentialing:** "Grown from a single coach into a world-class collective" — 7 named team members, including a Chief Science Officer with a PhD/MBA/CSCS*D/TSAC-F/CISSN (OBSERVED, tailoredcoachingmethod.com/about). This buys institutional authority independent of McBroom's personal charisma.

**Coach mentorship (B2B2C line):** A separate mentorship product for working coaches earning $5–15K/month, targeting $30K+, structured as weekly 90-minute calls with three specialists across Coaching/Business/Scale, 4-month minimum, with an explicit "ROI Guarantee: increase revenue by your investment within 60 days or receive free coaching until you do" (OBSERVED, tailored-coaching.com/mentorship-coaching). This monetizes category authority as a second revenue stream, separate from client acquisition — McBroom sells the how-to-build-what-I-built layer too.

**Retention model:** 16-week (4-month) minimum commitment, monthly billing only, and a stated progress guarantee — "if you fulfill your baseline coaching compliance and do not see documented progress, we will waive your fees or continue coaching you at no cost until you do" (OBSERVED, tailoredcoachingmethod.com/faq). The FAQ states the monthly-only rationale explicitly: "people are more accountable when making smaller investments regularly compared to one large amount" — retention engineered as a behavioral mechanism, not an accounting default.

## 3. Why It Works — Load-Bearing Mechanisms (evidenced, not vibes)

1. **Content-volume moat.** 1,000+ episodes over years compounds discoverability and trust in a way no ad budget replicates short-term (OBSERVED).
2. **Team-based authority transfer.** A PhD Chief Science Officer and multiple credentialed coaches let the brand claim rigor beyond one founder's Instagram presence (OBSERVED).
3. **Monthly-only billing as a retention lever, stated as policy, not incidental** (OBSERVED, FAQ).
4. **Progress guarantee as risk-reversal**, tied explicitly to "trackable data" — physical proof remains the non-negotiable trust currency even in the "relationship-first" pitch (OBSERVED).
5. **Price anchored at the top of self-reported market range, justified via self-published comparison content** rather than discounting — a confidence signal (OBSERVED).
6. **Second revenue engine (coach mentorship)** funds and reinforces category-authority positioning independent of the core client funnel (OBSERVED).

## 4. Honest Comparison — What Cooz Can, Can't, Shouldn't Copy

**Can emulate at solo/in-person scale:** the qualifying step before selling (a simple intake form, even without ScoreApp-grade tooling); the monthly-billing accountability logic; a stated, explicit progress-guarantee policy (even a simplified version); consistent — not high-volume — content cadence that compounds over 12+ months; leading with a real founder credential/story instead of borrowed authority.

**Cannot replicate structurally:** the 1,000-episode archive (years, not a sprint); the 7-person credentialed team (headcount Cooz doesn't have); a second monetized coach-mentorship arm (requires McBroom's already-proven track record); price-anchoring backed by years of public proof.

**Shouldn't copy:** topical breadth. TCM's blog/FAQ covers training, nutrition, business-building, and coach-hiring because it has specialists to staff each lane. A solo LA operator spreading into that breadth dilutes the one advantage a small operator actually has — depth on a narrow, felt problem. Also shouldn't copy the "sustainable fat loss / total body transformation" headline verbatim — that's McBroom's exact outcome-first turf, defended by his scale; competing on identical language means competing on his proof volume, which Cooz doesn't yet have.

## 5. Verdict — Is the Feeling-First Wedge Compatible or Contradictory?

**Compatible, with one hard constraint.** McBroom's own founding insight already names the extreme-transformation-culture burnout Farrice's frame identifies — but his execution stayed outcome-first at the headline (fat loss, muscle, sustainability) with mindset treated as an instrumental add-on ("shift the way you think about dieting"), not an identity-level promise. That leaves the surface-want-to-terminal-want ladder (abs → attraction/status/authority → feeling worthy) genuinely unclaimed at the top of TCM's funnel. Cooz's feeling-first wedge can occupy that unclaimed layer without competing head-on with McBroom's content archive or team.

The hard constraint: McBroom's guarantee is explicitly anchored to "measurable physical progress validated by trackable data" — even the most relationship-forward player in this space treats visible physical proof as the non-negotiable trust currency. A feeling-first pitch that skips physical proof would contradict how this market actually converts. The wedge must read as *feeling, delivered through and evidenced by physical progress* — not feeling instead of physique. That is fully consistent with the desire-ladder logic already in Farrice's frame; it just needs to stay explicit in Cooz's execution so the anti-fitness-industry angle doesn't drift into anti-physical-proof, which the market — including its most sophisticated operator — does not reward.
