# The Flyer Kit: Printed, Placed, Working

**After this doc:** Your 3 flyers are printed, placed in rooms that can carry them. You've got the words ready when an owner asks. And you know the one number that says print 500 more, or fix the page first.

---

### Confirm both pages are live before you print a single flyer
Why it matters: The QR code on every flyer file points at `coachcooz.com/stop-feeling-like-shit`, which routes straight into `/book-in-person`. Print before both are live and tested, the whole batch dies at the last step.
Do it (10 min, alone): [ ] Open `coachcooz.com/stop-feeling-like-shit` in Squarespace edit mode
                        [ ] Confirm doc 03 (Build This First) is fully done, ending in a real confirmation email in your inbox. Not done yet? Stop here and finish that doc first (30-45 min). Don't rewrite the booking page copy on this card, it's already written verbatim there
                        [ ] Confirm the two "abs" lines are swapped, per doc 00, section 1
                        [ ] Confirm all 3 buttons on this page point at `/book-in-person`, not the old `/briefing`
Done when: You tap a printed proof's QR code on your own phone, land on the squeeze page with the abs lines swapped, tap through, and land on a `/book-in-person` page that says $1,000 in plain English.
Go deeper → doc 03; doc 00, section 1

### Send these 3 files to print, exactly as they are
Why it matters: Don't treat these as comps. The text is vectored and the QR code is live — touch the layout and you break the scan.
Do it (20 min your time + a few days shop turnaround, you + print shop): [ ] Send all three files from the Print Files folder: flyer-4x6-variant1.svg (your words, hand-to-hand, the main run), flyer-4x6-variant2.svg (the shorter A/B version, order at least 100 separately), placard-8.5x11-variant3.svg (the counter card for rooms you can't stand in)
                        [ ] Tell the shop: font is Archivo Black (free on Google Fonts), convert every line of text to outlines before it prints
                        [ ] Tell them: convert black/gold/white to CMYK before plates run, not RGB
                        [ ] Get one proof of each size first. Test-scan it with your own phone from arm's length (about 12-15 inches) and from across a counter (about 25-30 inches) before you approve the full run
Done when: You're holding 3 physical proofs, all three scan clean at every distance you tested, and the shop has your go-ahead on the full batch.
Go deeper → everything the shop needs is on this card. If they push back on anything, ask me before you agree to changes.

### Pick the rooms, skip the rest
Why it matters: A card that says "tired of feeling like shit" works in a room that can carry it. Dies in one that can't.
Do it (ongoing, you): [ ] Take a stack to barbershops, salons, laundromats, coffee shops, and co-working spaces, anywhere people sit still for a few minutes with nothing to do but read something
                        [ ] Skip physical therapy offices, chiropractors, and juice bars this round. Wholesome, family-facing rooms, wrong card for them right now
                        [ ] Skip other gyms and training studios entirely, always
Done when: Every business holding your cards is one where a stranger reading "are you tired of feeling like shit" at their counter wouldn't make the owner wince.
Go deeper → ask me why these rooms and not others; the short answer is captive time plus rooms that can carry the card.

### Make the ask, in these words
Why it matters: The owner gets the truth, out loud, off the card. The stranger who scans it doesn't. Mix that up and the owner feels lied to. Or the card stops being anonymous to the person holding it.
Do it (2 min per business, you): [ ] Say this, close to word for word: "Hey man, I've got this weird little card, no name on it, nothing salesy, just a question. It's from me. I'm a local trainer, and the card's blank on purpose. It's an experiment I'm running. Mind if I leave a stack by your register?"
                        [ ] Don't add "if anyone asks, say you don't know" or anything close to it. The owner already knows. The customer doesn't need to.
Done when: The owner's said yes and the cards are sitting on their counter, not in a drawer.
Go deeper → the script above is the whole move. Say it close to word for word.

### Don't let anyone "clean up" the card
Why it matters: The rough edges are the point. A card that looks agency-made reads as an ad. This one has to read like it slid under a windshield.
Do it (ongoing, you): [ ] No logo, no name, no URL, no handle goes on either side of the card, ever
                        [ ] If the print shop offers to fix the kerning, smooth the symmetry, or add a border, tell them no
                        [ ] If anyone suggests a subtle "from [your name]" line, kill it. The mystery is the hook
Done when: You've held the printed card next to the digital file and nothing's been added, nothing's been smoothed out.
Go deeper → the card is supposed to look a little rough. If the shop offers to clean it up, that's a no.

### Know the numbers that tell you print more or stop
Why it matters: Guessing whether this worked burns your next batch. These are the exact lines between "print more" and "fix something first."
Do it (5 min to read, then check it every time a batch runs): [ ] Order in batches of 500 cards, never one giant run
                        [ ] Check it in Squarespace: Analytics → Traffic, filtered to `utm_campaign=war-on-fitness-industry`, for visits (your best stand-in for scans, a plain-URL QR code gives no raw scan count), then your calendar for actual bookings
                        [ ] Kill signal: two straight 500-card batches under 3% visits or under 3 bookings (0.6%). Either one means the page or booking flow is leaking, not the card. Fix `/book-in-person` before you touch the flyer again
                        [ ] Print-more signal: a batch that lands 5+ bookings (1%+) with at least 1 closing into the 90-Day Resurrection Protocol, print the next 500
                        [ ] For the Variant 1 vs. Variant 2 wording test specifically, don't call a winner under 100 cards per variant
Done when: You can look at one batch's numbers and say, out loud, "print more" or "fix the page" without guessing.
Go deeper → `00-THE-PLAYBOOK.md`, section 2 ("Save this scoreboard"); Receipts → 02 — Concept V2, "GO/NO-GO — printable test plan"
