# THE TRIAGE AUDIT SCRIPT
## Knowledge document for upload to Notebook 3
## The 90-minute sales conversation that converts prospects into Pathway A/B/C/D clients

---

## WHAT THE TRIAGE AUDIT IS

The Triage Audit is the 90-min diagnostic conversation that serves as the front door to ALL four pathways. It's free during the May-July beta phase. It's how prospects experience Cooz's coaching style before they pay.

It's NOT a "get on a call so I can pitch you" structure. It's a real diagnostic. The prospect walks away with three concrete shifts whether or not they hire Cooz.

But it's also the close. By the end, Cooz recommends ONE pathway based on what he heard.

---

## THE 90-MIN STRUCTURE

| Minutes | Phase | Purpose |
|--------:|-------|---------|
| 0-30 | DIAGNOSE | Let the prospect talk. Ask 5 specific questions. Take notes. |
| 30-60 | COST OF INACTION | Make them say the cost of NOT changing, in their own words. |
| 60-75 | THE BRIDGE | Build a 3-shift plan LIVE. Reveal the right pathway. |
| 75-90 | CLOSE OR CAPTURE | Yes / "let me think" / no — each has a defined response. |

---

## MINUTES 0-30: DIAGNOSE

### Opening (first 60 seconds)

> "Thanks for getting on the call. We've got 90 minutes. Here's how this works: I'm going to spend the first 30 minutes asking questions and listening. Then we'll spend 30 minutes figuring out what's actually going on under the hood. The last 30 minutes I'll show you what I'd change in the first 3 weeks if you wanted to work together — and you'll get those whether or not we end up doing this. Sound good? Let's start."

### The 5 diagnostic questions (in this order)

**Q1 — The week walk-through:**
> "Walk me through a normal week. Training, nutrition, sleep, stress. Don't dress it up — just tell me what actually happens."

*Listen for:* what they're already doing, what they THINK they're doing vs. what they actually do, where the gaps are, the cope mechanisms (caffeine, alcohol, "just push through")

**Q2 — The body's complaint:**
> "What's the thing your body won't let you do anymore that you used to do without thinking?"

*Listen for:* the trigger event — medical, photographic, occupational, relational. The specific MOMENT that prompted them to reach out. This is the wound.

**Q3 — The mirror question:**
> "When was the last time you looked in a mirror and recognized the person looking back?"

*Listen for:* time-distance from their actual identity, dissonance between external success and internal state, the imposter feeling. This question opens the identity-level resistance.

**Q4 — What they've tried:**
> "What have you already tried? What happened?"

*Listen for:* prior coaches, programs, supplements, Whoops, peptides — the failed optimization stack. They've usually spent significant money. This question frames the prior failures so you can position your work as DIFFERENT.

**Q5 — The why-now question:**
> "Why are we having this conversation today instead of six months ago or six months from now?"

*Listen for:* the urgency, the breaking point, what changed recently. This is the qualification — if there's no real why-now, the close will be weak no matter what.

### What Cooz does during the 30-min diagnose phase

- LISTEN. Take notes (or have a notebook open and write).
- DO NOT pitch.
- DO NOT advise.
- DO NOT explain your methodology.
- Ask follow-up questions to deepen ("tell me more about that," "what does that look like," "when did that start")
- The prospect should be doing 80%+ of the talking.

---

## MINUTES 30-60: COST OF INACTION

### The reframe transition

> "OK. I've got enough. Let me ask you a different kind of question now."

### The 3 cost-of-inaction questions

**CoI Q1 — The 12-month projection:**
> "If nothing changes in the next 12 months — same training, same nutrition, same schedule, same stress — where does your body end up?"

*Make them paint the picture.* The prospect needs to hear themselves articulate the trajectory. Don't fill it in for them. Wait. Let them say it.

**CoI Q2 — The work / family cost:**
> "What does that cost your work? Your family? Your energy on a Tuesday at 3pm?"

*Specific. Concrete.* They need to imagine the meeting they fail to lead, the bedtime story they fall asleep during, the negotiation they lose because they're tired.

**CoI Q3 — The decision-quality cost:**
> "You make decisions every week worth thousands or millions of dollars. What's the cost of making them on broken substrate?"

*This is the killer question for the executive buyer.* Their identity is welded to making good decisions. The body is the cognitive throttle. They've probably never named this connection before.

### What Cooz does during minutes 30-60

- They MUST say the cost out loud. Don't say it for them.
- Pause. Tolerate silence. The discomfort is the work.
- If they minimize ("I mean, it's not THAT bad"), gently reflect: "Tell me more about what 'not that bad' actually looks like in practice."
- DO NOT pitch yet. The cost of inaction phase is incomplete until they've named it specifically.

## MINUTES 60-75: THE BRIDGE (build the plan live)

### The transition

> "OK. Based on what you just told me, here's what I'd change in the first 3 weeks."

### The 3-shift live diagnostic

Pick three specific things from what they said in minutes 0-60. Tell them what you'd change. This is REAL coaching, not a teaser.

**Examples of 3-shift output:**

If they said sleep is broken:
> "First shift: sleep architecture. You're going to bed at 12:30 and waking at 6 — that's not enough sleep cycles for the work you're asking your body to do. We'd reset that to 10:30 with a non-negotiable phone-down rule at 9:30. Inside of 10 days, your 3pm crash disappears."

If they said training isn't working:
> "Second shift: training volume. You're doing six 30-minute sessions a week — that's too much frequency, not enough intensity. We'd cut to four sessions, increase volume per session, and track three benchmark lifts. Strength markers move in 3-4 weeks."

If they said nutrition is "fine":
> "Third shift: protein. You're tracking calories but ignoring protein. For a 200-lb professional running on stress, you need 200g/day minimum. We're not changing what you eat — we're changing the protein target inside what you already eat. Energy stabilizes inside two weeks."

### The pathway reveal

After the 3 shifts, transition into the pathway recommendation.

> "The full version of this is what I do with my clients. Three pathways depending on what fits.
>
> Based on what you've told me, here's what I'd recommend: **[Pathway A / B / C / D]**.
>
> Here's why: [tie back to 2-3 specific things they said]."

### The pathway-specific pitch

#### If recommending Pathway A (In-Person, $1,500/mo):
> "It's $1,275 a month for the founding rate — I take three more in-person clients at this rate, then it moves to $1,500. I'm in the gym with you four times a week. I see what your body's actually doing, not what you tell me it's doing. The work happens in the room."

#### If recommending Pathway B (Remote, $2,000/mo):
> "Two thousand a month, $1,500 for the founding rate. I'm in your pocket every weekday. We do a 30-minute call every week. Daily Voxer voice coaching — when you have a question Tuesday at 2pm, I'm there. Not next week. Now."

#### If recommending Pathway C (Ignition, $3,500-$4,500):
> "Thirty-five hundred for twelve weeks if you're willing to be one of my first three case studies. The trade is I document the transformation — photos, metrics, narrative, video clip. Your story becomes the proof shelf. After three case studies publish, the price moves to $4,500. The cheapest I'll ever run this is right now."

#### If recommending Pathway D (ESA, $5,000-$10,000/mo):
> "This is Executive System Architecture — the full premium tier. Five thousand a month for the founding slot. Sixty-minute weekly calls instead of thirty. Priority Voxer response. Quarterly in-person sessions. Monthly written transformation memo. Biomarker integration. I take three to four ESA clients per year. The first slot is the founding rate. After the first published ESA case study, the price moves to eight."

## MINUTES 75-90: CLOSE OR CAPTURE

### Three branching outcomes

#### Outcome 1: YES (committing now)

> "I'll send the intake form tonight. We start Monday."

(Calendar a follow-up for intake completion, schedule first session, send Stripe payment link.)

#### Outcome 2: "Let me think about it"

> "Understood. Here's what I'll do — I'll send you a Loom with the 3 shifts we talked about. They work whether you sign up or not. Take the weekend. If you want in, the founding slots close when [the next one fills / by [date]]."

(Calendar a 7-day follow-up. Don't push. The 3-shift Loom IS the value delivery whether or not they convert.)

#### Outcome 3: NO (or the audit clearly isn't going to convert)

> "Respect that. The Loom is yours either way. If anything shifts, my calendar is open. And if you know someone in your circle who's running on the same fumes, I'd appreciate the introduction."

(Calendar a 30-60 day check-back if the no felt soft. If it felt firm, archive and move on.)

## ONE-BREATH OBJECTION RESPONSES

These are the most common objections and the verbatim Cooz responses.

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| **"$1,500 a month is a lot."** | "It is. And you've spent more than that on things that didn't work. This is the first time you'd be paying for a witness, not a plan." |
| **"$5,000/mo for ESA is steep."** | "It is. So is the cost of doing this halfway for the next five years. ESA is for people who want to do this once and do it right. If that's not where you are, that's totally fine." |
| **"I can get a trainer for $200/month."** | "You can. You probably have. How'd that go?" |
| **"You don't have many case studies yet."** | "That's why you're getting the founding rate. You're paying $1,275 for what will be $1,500 in 90 days. The trade is I document the work." |
| **"Let me talk to my partner / spouse."** | "You should. Tell them a depleted operator can't operate — and this is the most useful thing you'll do this year for the people who count on you." |
| **"I want to lose 30 pounds first / get healthy first."** | "That's exactly what we'd do together. The reason most people don't is they don't have a coach in the room. That's why this is what I do." |
| **"What if I can't make the 4x/week in-person?"** | "Then Pathway B (remote) is probably the better fit. Same coaching, different delivery. Want me to walk through what that looks like?" |
| **"I've been burned by coaches before."** | "Most people have. Most coaches sell plans. I rebuild substrate. Different work, different outcome. The Triage Audit alone will show you whether this feels different from what you've had before." |
| **"Can I start with cheaper, then upgrade?"** | "Yes. About 30% of my retainer clients move to a deeper pathway after 3-6 months once they see what's possible. Pathway A or B is a fine starting point." |

## POST-AUDIT FOLLOW-THROUGH

### Within 4 hours of the audit

Send the prospect:
1. The 3-shift Loom (record this RIGHT after the audit when it's fresh)
2. A 1-page action sheet (the 3 shifts written out, branded as the Triage Audit deliverable)
3. The pathway-specific landing page link
4. A clear next step: "Calendar link below if you want to start. Otherwise, no pressure — the Loom is yours regardless."

### Within 7 days

If they haven't converted, send a re-engagement DM (use Notebook 3 to generate it) referencing ONE specific thing they said. Don't re-pitch. Re-acknowledge.

### Within 30 days

If they're still cold, send a final value-first touchpoint (a relevant article, podcast, or insight that maps to what they said) with no ask. Then archive.

Per audit:
- Did the prospect say the cost-of-inaction OUT LOUD? (Yes/No)
- Did Cooz LIVE-coach 3 specific shifts? (Yes/No)
- Did Cooz recommend ONE pathway clearly? (Yes/No/Hedged)
- Outcome: Closed / Soft no / Hard no

Weekly with Farrice (Friday call):
- Total audits run this week
- Conversion rate (closed / total)
- Most common pathway recommended
- Most common objection (and whether the response landed)

After 10 audits, the data tells us:
- Is the audit conversion rate above 30% (industry strong)?
- Is one pathway converting better than others?
- Are objection responses landing or do we need to revise?
