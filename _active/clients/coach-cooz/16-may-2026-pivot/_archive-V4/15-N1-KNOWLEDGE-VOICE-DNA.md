## THE RHYTHM (the 5-beat pattern Cooz writes in)

Every post follows this rhythm structure:

```
BEAT 1: SHORT      (3-5 words)
BEAT 2: SHORT      (4-6 words)
BEAT 3: SHORT      (3-5 words)
BEAT 4: MEDIUM     (14-20 words — builds tension or context)
BEAT 5: SHORT      (5-7 words — the punchline or the turn)
```

### Verbatim example (Cooz-written, do not paraphrase)

> "You didn't lose your edge. It went dormant. And you've been performing without it ever since. Most men skip straight to purpose because they want clarity, vision, meaning — but purpose without infrastructure collapses. You can't calibrate a dormant compass."

Three short punches. One medium wave. One short close. **This is the rhythm.**

### Intensity variants

| Mode | Sentence ratio | When to use |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| **Diagnostic** (calm, clinical) | 60% medium / 40% short | Naming a problem, teaching a concept |
| **Confessional** (vulnerable, personal) | 50% medium / 50% short | Telling Cooz's own story, admission posts |
| **Provocative** (challenging) | 30% medium / 70% short | Reframes, pattern interrupts, contrarian takes |

### Rhythm rule (non-negotiable)

If three consecutive sentences exceed 15 words, the draft FAILS rhythm check. Compress.

## THE OPENER (the first 1-3 sentences)

Cooz opens with a VIVID SCENE, not an abstract claim. The reader should be able to see the moment in their head before they read the analysis.

### Opener patterns that work

**Pattern 1: Specific time + location + state**
- "It's 3:17 AM. The house is perfectly quiet, but you're wide awake fighting a ghost."
- "It's Tuesday afternoon. You just navigated a high-stakes meeting. Your arguments were sharp, and you secured the outcome."
- "It's 11:00 PM. You're sitting in the dark of your home office, and the house is completely silent."

**Pattern 2: I-led admission**
- "I traded the edge for safety. Got a career. Got into a relationship."
- "For 5 years I told everyone I was building. I was actually hiding."
- "I watched a founder I coach close out a board deck at 11 PM, then sit in his car for 22 minutes before driving home."

**Pattern 3: Mechanism reveal**
- "You don't need a third cup of coffee at 2:45 PM. You have a fuel leak."
- "Burnout is a corporate diagnosis. Dormant is a body diagnosis."
- "Most of what you call discipline is just adrenaline with a deadline."

**Pattern 4: Question that names the wound**
- "If we drained the business accounts and stripped the title tomorrow, who is the man left standing?"
- "When was the last time you looked in a mirror and recognized the man looking back?"

### Opener patterns that FAIL (never use)

- "Here's what I've learned..."
- "Here's how to..."
- "You know that feeling when..."
- "Let me tell you about..."
- "I want to share something..."
- "Today I want to talk about..."
- Any opener that explains what's coming instead of just doing it.

## THE I → YOU PIVOT RULE

Most of every post is in I (Cooz's voice, his diagnosis, his admission). The shift to "you" happens ONCE, late in the post, and lasts no more than 2 sentences.

### Correct structure

```
[Open in I or vivid scene]
[Continue in I — 2-3 paragraphs]
[ONE you-pivot, max 2 sentences — the universal landing]
[Close — usually 1 short sentence, often back in I or in a brand line]
```

### Example (correct — V4 update)

> "I traded the edge for safety once. Got the career, got the relationships, looked great on paper. Woke up at 3:17 AM not recognizing the man holding it all up.
>
> For three months I tried to out-discipline it. Read every protocol. Bought every supplement. Slept 8 hours and woke up tired anyway.
>
> What I learned: you can't out-discipline a body that's been logging the bill. You rebuild the body first.
>
> They look like themselves again. That's the work."

I-led for the first 3 paragraphs. ONE 2-sentence you-pivot. Closer uses verified buyer verbs ("look like themselves again") + verified Cooz close ("That's the work"). Replaced V3's "rebuild the substrate" + "they're rebuilt" — both V4-banned.

### Example (broken — don't do this)

> "You ever feel like you're running on fumes? You've been pushing for years, and you can't quite figure out why nothing's working. **You** know the symptoms — afternoon crashes, broken sleep, wine at 9pm just to come down. **You** need to rebuild your substrate. **You** need to fix the hardware first."

Five "you"s in five sentences. No I. No specific scene. Reads like a coach lecturing a passive audience.

## CLOSING PATTERNS (the line that lingers)

The last sentence is what readers remember. Cooz's closes are:
- **Specific** — not "stay strong" or "go get it"
- **Concrete** — they reference an image, a moment, a measurable thing
- **Brand-aligned** — they fit Cooz's universe

### Closing patterns that work (V4 verified)

- **"That's the work."** (V4 verified Cooz close — use as default)
- **"They look like themselves again."** (verified buyer-verb close, V4-01 Q3.3)
- **"They came back to a body they recognize."** (V4-02 §4 verified pattern)
- "The body that built this is breaking. I rebuild it." (verified V4 bridge → mechanism)
- "I rebuild the body that built the life." (V4 cold-pitch close-adjacent)
- "The work is mostly done in private."
- "Let's go." (used sparingly — reserved for the highest-stakes posts)

### Closing patterns KILLED in V4

- ❌ "They're rebuilt." (V3 closer — "rebuilt" as identity word fails female corpus + tensions with Resurrection brand. Killed.)
- ❌ "You optimized everything except the operator." (V3 bridge — "operator" unattested in V4-02 §3A buyer corpus. Killed.)
- ❌ "Most people optimize around it. I rebuild it." (V3 — "optimize" is female-corpus-absent per V4-01 §1 verb table.)
- ❌ "The body has filed a formal complaint against the life." (V3 — clever but unattested as Cooz-grade voice; reads strategist-inferred.)

### Closing patterns that fail

## BRAND LAYERS (V4 — the 4-layer reconciled framework)

Cooz's brand operates at four layers. V3 created a contradiction (Rebuilt vs. Resurrection); V4 reconciled it. Each layer uses different language; don't conflate them.

| Layer | Word | Purpose | Where it shows up |
|-------|------|---------|-------------------|
| **Brand essence** | The Resurrection Coach | The signature/legacy — the "who" | Brand identity, podcast, podcast cover, banner. Never body copy. |
| **Buyer identity** | Resurrected (brand surface) / "look like myself again" (tactical) | What the client BECOMES — the "what" | Brand surfaces use "Resurrected." DMs, posts, conversations use the buyer's own verbs: "look like myself again," "came back," "regain." |
| **Mechanism verb** | Rebuild the body / rebuild the foundation | How it works — the "how" | Lane 1 protocol content, sales explanations. Use as VERB only — "we rebuild your body's foundation." Never as IDENTITY noun ("rebuilt") — that died in V4. |
| **Premium framing** | Body-first foundation / 1:1 transformation coaching | Cooz's positioning vs. competitors | LinkedIn About, headline, Pathway D copy. **Replaces "Body-first OS"** (V4-05 Fix 2 — OS metaphor saturated). |

When generating content:
- For Lane 1 / Lane 2 (most posts) — buyer-verb register: "look like myself again," "came back," "performing at 5 out of 10"
- For ESA-tier content (rare) — premium framing language is appropriate, but lead with verified Cooz mechanism vocab (foundation, hardware, fuel leak), not saturated OS-language
- "Resurrection" — brand only, never body copy
- "Resurrected" — brand surfaces only; in tactical copy, use buyer's own verbs
