# PART 1 — SLOP DIAGNOSIS ON V3 CRITICAL COPY

## The V3 Cold Pitch (the load-bearing failure)

> "I work with operators — entrepreneurs, founders, professionals — who optimized everything except themselves. My clients show up tired of being tired, trying to out-discipline a body that's been quietly logging warnings for years. Twelve weeks later they sleep through the night, they're 10-20 lbs leaner, their three benchmark lifts moved up, they look like themselves again. They run on substrate, not adrenaline. They're rebuilt."

### The 8 AI-tells

1. **TRIPLE-LIST OPENER** — "entrepreneurs, founders, professionals." Real humans never list three audience categories in a row. AI does. Pattern-fail.

2. **THE "X-ED OF X-ING" RHYTHM** — "tired of being tired." Linguistically clever, AI-coded. Cooz in the wild writes "running on fumes" or "fighting a ghost" — sensory imagery, not abstract recursion.

3. **AI ANTITHESIS PATTERN** — "They run on substrate, not adrenaline." The "not X, but Y" reveal is the most common AI structural tell. (This is on the Always-On feedback list from MEMORY.md as a banned move.)

4. **NUMBERED-RESULT LIST** — "Twelve weeks later they sleep through the night, they're 10-20 lbs leaner, their three benchmark lifts moved up, they look like themselves again." Four outcomes in serial. AI loves cumulative outcome lists. Real coaches name ONE cinematic outcome.

5. **NO SCENE, NO MOMENT** — Pure attribute description. Cooz's actual posts open at 3:17 AM. The cold pitch opens at "I work with..." That's bio copy, not a pitch.

6. **NO ADMISSION** — The cold pitch is pure third-person diagnosis. Cooz's diagnostic-confessional register REQUIRES the I-admission. Look at his real opener: *"I traded the edge for safety. Got a career. Got into a relationship."* — that's admission. Our V3 cold pitch has no "I" admitting anything. We got the diagnosis without the confession.

7. **ABSTRACT BUYER** — "they" / "operators" / "high-functioning professionals." No SPECIFIC person we can see. The just-signed female client isn't in this pitch. No real human is.

8. **"Logging warnings for years"** — "logging" is software engineering jargon. Cooz's metaphor is hardware. Mixing software-logging with hardware-substrate is metaphor-incoherence. AI does this when it pattern-matches without reading its own metaphor stack.

### Verdict: Composite slop score

**HIGH AI-tell density.** 8 of 8 AI patterns present. This isn't 70/30 human/AI. This is 30/70 human/AI.

The cold pitch must be rewritten from zero in V4 — not edited. Editing AI-coded copy produces edited AI-coded copy.

# PART 2 — BRAND-IDENTITY RECONCILIATION (Rebuilt → Resurrected)

## The contradiction we created in V3

V3 introduced **REBUILT** as the one-word identity transformation. But Cooz's brand has 9+ years of equity in the Resurrection vocabulary:
- "The Resurrection Coach" (brand name)
- "The Resurrection Protocol" (program)
- "The Resurrection Series" (content series)
- "The Phoenix Protocol" (content asset)
- "Reclaiming Edge, Energy, and Command" (existing tagline)

**Rebuilt** is engineered, hardware, mechanical.
**Resurrection** is mythic, biblical, dramatic.

These are two different conceptual worlds asking the brand to do conflicting jobs.

## The decision

**Commit fully to the Resurrection vocabulary across all four brand layers.**

| Layer | V3 word | V4 word | Why |
|-------|---------|---------|-----|
| **Brand essence** | The Resurrection Coach | The Resurrection Coach | Unchanged ✓ |
| **Buyer identity transformation** | Rebuilt | **Resurrected** | Aligns with 9+ years of brand equity. Mythic register matches Cooz's diagnostic-confessional voice. |
| **Mechanism verb** | "We rebuild your hardware" | "We rebuild your hardware" | Verb is fine. Rebuild remains the working noun for what HAPPENS. |
| **Substrate work** | Substrate rebuild | Substrate rebuild | Internal-mechanism description, unchanged |

## Why this resolves the V3 tension

### Cognitive coherence
One vocabulary. Resurrection / Resurrected / Rebuild as verb. Internally consistent. No more Cooz wondering "wait, am I selling Resurrection or Rebuild?"

### Emotional register match
Resurrection carries DEATH → REBIRTH narrative weight. The buyer is hiring Cooz because something in them feels DEAD. "Rebuilt" doesn't carry that emotional load. "Resurrected" does.

### Voice DNA alignment
Cooz's actual voice register (3:17 AM, fighting a ghost, internal liability, undeclared ledger) is mythic-confessional, not engineering-mechanical. Resurrection matches. Rebuilt doesn't.

### Brand-buyer translation
The brand says "Resurrection." The buyer translates that into their own words: "feel like myself again," "look like myself again," "got my edge back," "aging in reverse" (Robin's verbatim phrase).

The bridge from brand → buyer language is:

| Brand layer | Buyer-voice translation |
|-------------|------------------------|
| **Resurrection** (brand) | "I want to feel like myself again." |
| **Resurrected** (identity) | "I look in the mirror and recognize her." |
| **Rebuild** (mechanism) | "We're rebuilding the foundation." |

Cooz uses Resurrection in his BRAND surface. He uses the buyer's verbs in his COPY. Both can coexist.

## The 3 risks of this decision (and the mitigations)

### Risk 1: "Resurrection" reads overwrought to professionals who haven't hit rock bottom

The new ICP (high-functioning professionals, just-signed female client) hasn't hit "I'm ruined" — they've hit "I'm tired and behind." Resurrection might feel mismatched.

**Mitigation**: Reserve "Resurrection" / "Resurrected" for BRAND surfaces (logo, hero copy, podcast title). For tactical copy (DMs, posts, Triage Audit script), use the BUYER'S verbs ("feel like myself," "got my edge back"). The brand frame stays mythic. The conversation stays grounded.

### Risk 2: Religious connotation alienates non-religious buyers

Resurrection has biblical baggage. Some buyers will read it as Christian-coded.

**Mitigation**: Cooz's existing 9 years of brand has handled this fine — buyers who've signed haven't been put off. The word has secular use (a "career resurrection," a "team's resurrection") that's well-established. Don't overcorrect.

### Risk 3: Female buyers may not connect to "Resurrection" the way male buyers might

Resurrection is gendered male in Christian iconography (Christ, Lazarus, the Phoenix is sometimes feminine but is the rare exception).

**Mitigation**: Gender-test the V4 copy specifically — does the just-signed female client (and the avatar of women like Anne Marie Chaker) recognize themselves in "resurrected"? If voice-mine returns suggest she'd say "I came back" or "I found myself again" instead of "I was resurrected" — let that be the BUYER-voice translation while the BRAND-voice keeps Resurrection.

## The V4 cold pitch closer (post-reconciliation)

**V3 closer**: "They're rebuilt."

**V4 closer candidate** (pending voice-mine validation):

Option A: "They're resurrected." — direct word swap. Aligns brand. Mythic register.

Option B: "They came back." — buyer-voice translation. Lower mythic load, higher recognition.

Option C: "They look like themselves again." — pure customer language (verified from multiple sources).

My recommendation pending voice-mine: **Option C as the closer in tactical copy, Option A in brand copy.**

The cold pitch (used in DMs, Triage Audits, podcast intros) closes with C — the buyer's own words.
The brand surfaces (banner, podcast cover, About hero) close with A — the brand's own words.

This solves the brand-buyer translation problem without forcing one word to do two jobs.
