## PHASE 0 — VISITOR INTENT TRIAGE

Five people land on your profile. Each comes with a different question. The profile has to answer all five inside the first 5 seconds.

| Visitor type | How they got here | What they're asking | What the profile must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Post Reader** | Read your post, clicked your name | "Who is this person and is everything else this good?" | Tagline confirms the post's premise. Featured section delivers proof. About deepens it. |
| **The Search Discoverer** | Typed "Burbank executive coach," "high-performing professional fitness," "long-arc body coach" | "Does this person actually do what I'm searching for?" | Headline + first line of About match the search intent verbatim. Keywords embedded throughout. |
| **The Comment Stalker** | Saw you comment intelligently on someone else's post | "Are you legit or just loud?" | Featured + Experience + recent posts triangulate. No CV theater — only proof work. |
| **The Referral Visitor** | Mike, Cody McBroom, or Mariano sent them. They were told you're "the real one." | "Does the profile match the recommendation?" | Story-led About in Cooz's actual voice. The named long-arc clients show up. The Brave Choice methodology is named. |
| **The Cold-Outreach Target** | You DM'd them for the Burbank engine | "Why is this guy DMing me and is he for real?" | Profile has to do the trust work the DM couldn't. Banner + photo + tagline + first 3 lines of About all aligned to one message. |

**Implication for the rebuild**: The profile is one document with five readers. It has to be specific enough to convert the referral visitor (who's already half-sold) and clear enough to convert the search discoverer (who's coming in cold). The way it does both: name the buyer specifically in line one, name the proof in line two, name the methodology in line three.

## PHASE 1 — TAGLINE (HEADLINE) ENGINEERING

LinkedIn truncates the tagline at ~60 characters in feed previews. The whole tagline runs ~220 characters maximum.

Your tagline today is a job description. The new tagline opens the conversation already happening in the reader's head — Ron Lynch's standard. Voice-accuracy first; brand essence second.

### The structural formula (Lynch reader-pointed + Lara Acosta Pattern 20 + Donald Miller GP1 hero inversion)

> **READER STATE** (their existing thought) · **THE WORK** (your differentiator) · **PROOF** (the anchor)

Three variations below. Recommended pick at the end. Don't blend them — pick one and ride it for 90 days.

### Variation A — Recognition + speed-and-retention (recommended default)

> **If your body is starting to break under the life it built — that's the work I do. Real change in 90 days. Clients who stay 4, 5, 8, 9 years because the work compounds.**

**Why it works**: The first 9 words are the reader's interior monologue made visible (Lynch voice-accuracy). The "that's the work I do" pivot earns the proof. Then BOTH proofs land in 17 words: speed ("real change in 90 days") and durability ("4-9 years because the work compounds"). The duration is reframed from wait-time to renewal-evidence — clients chose to stay because results came AND held.

**Truncation behavior**: Feed shows "*If your body is starting to break under the life it built...*" — pattern interrupt + reader-in-protagonist seat in 12 words.

### Variation B — Speed-then-retention contrarian (recommended if cold-search converting but warm-referral isn't)

> **Most coaches deliver fast results that don't hold, or slow work that does. I deliver both. Real change in 90 days. Clients who stay 4, 5, 8, 9 years.**

**Why it works**: Names BOTH category enemies in one beat (the 12-week-sprint coaches AND the slow-and-steady coaches who take 18 months to show results). Stealing-thunder (Wiebe) — the reader's "yeah, I've tried both" objection is pre-empted. Then the value prop lands as "I do both" — the rare position. The retention claim becomes the proof of the speed claim, not a wait-time threat.

**Truncation behavior**: Feed shows "*Most coaches deliver fast results that don't hold, or slow work that does...*" — category-enemy-naming in two beats, hooks both burned-out buyer types.

### Variation C — Receipts-led (recommended for ESA Tier D / premium-tier acquisition)

> **Mari: 16 lbs of muscle in 3 months. Mike: still with me at year 9. Carron: #2-ranked national powerlifter at year 3. I rebuild the body that built the life — fast results, durable work. Resurrection Coach.**

**Why it works**: Three named anchors stacked — speed (Mari), retention (Mike), elite specificity (Carron). The "fast results, durable work" line names the asymmetry directly. Premium-tier readers hire on receipts; this leads with three of them in 25 words. Brand essence appears last, after the receipts have already earned it.

**Truncation behavior**: Feed shows "*Mari: 16 lbs of muscle in 3 months. Mike: still with me at year 9...*" — verifiable specifics in 14 words.

### THE PICK

**Default: Variation A.** It opens with the reader's existing thought — the highest-conversion mechanic for cold profile visitors who don't yet know who Cooz is. Lynch voice-accuracy + Mallet finally-someone-said-it.

**Switch to Variation B** if cold-search traffic is converting but warm-referral visitors aren't (the contrarian framing helps category-aware burned-out buyers see why this is different).

**Switch to Variation C** when ESA Tier D becomes the primary acquisition target. Receipts-led works once the buyer is already premium-shopping.

## PHASE 2 — ABOUT SECTION (THE LANDING PAGE BODY)

LinkedIn truncates the About section after ~290 characters in the "see more" preview. The first 290 characters do 80% of the conversion work. Lines 1-3 are the only real estate that gets read by 90% of profile visitors.

### Structural architecture — Ron Lynch reader-as-protagonist + StoryBrand GP1 (Hero Inversion)

The reader stays in their own head for 60% of the doc. Cooz enters as the answer to their problem, not as the subject of his own resume. Receipts are compressed into one rhythm-controlled paragraph — not paraded. Brave Choice methodology emerges as the reader's own questions, not as Robin's verbatim feature.

The single most common LinkedIn About failure: pivoting to "I've been doing this for 9 years and here's my client roster" by paragraph 3. Reader was still in their own head. The CV breaks the spell. They click away.

The fix: stay in the reader's interior monologue longer than feels comfortable. Make them feel seen 6 times before introducing the guide.

| Section | What it does | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** (lines 1-3) | The reader's existing thought made visible. Stops the scroll. | 3 lines |
| **Recognition stack** (¶ 2-5) | Deeper into the reader's interior monologue. The "you mean THAT thought I have at 2am? that one?" moment, repeatedly. | 6-9 lines |
| **The cost** (¶ 6) | What 5 more years of this looks like. Loss aversion vivid. | 3-4 lines |
| **The doorway** (¶ 7) | Cooz enters — briefly. Empathy then authority. | 1-2 lines |
| **The speed proof** (¶ 8) | Mari · 3 months · 16 lbs of muscle — defuses "this guy must be slow" misread. | 2-3 lines |
| **The retention proof** (¶ 9) | Mike + Carron + Corey + Robin compressed — durability claim. | 4-6 lines |
| **The compounding reframe** (¶ 10) | "They aren't locked in. They renew because the work compounds." Retention = renewal-evidence, not wait-time. | 1-2 lines |
| **The methodology** (¶ 11-12) | Brave Choice surfaces as the reader's own 3 questions, not as Robin's feature. | 5-7 lines |
| **The CTA** (¶ 13-14) | Conditional resolution. V5-verified close. | 2-3 lines |

### Why this About section converts (the writers-room read)

**The hook (lines 1-3) — Lynch voice-accuracy**

> *"The business is thriving. The man behind it is running on fumes. You engineered your company for scale. You let your own body slip to the background..."*

These lines aren't Cooz speaking to the reader. They're the reader's existing thought, transcribed back to them. The conversation already going on in their head — made visible. Anyone who's been quietly running this internal monologue for 6 months feels involuntarily seen. Anyone who hasn't, scrolls — correct triage.

**The recognition stack (¶ 2-5) — Mack vivid-specificity + Mallet finally-someone-said-it**

The 4 PM fog. The lifts that don't come easy at 47. The 5 AM workout window that's been months since it felt like it worked. The "I'll fix this when the next quarter slows down" promise that's now in year 3. The mirror reading different.

This is interior monologue archaeology — the specific thoughts the reader has been having that they haven't heard anyone else articulate. Mack's "naming an unnamed experience" — the moment the reader thinks *this person knows me*. Each beat works because it's vivid and specific (Mack); because it surfaces something they've felt but never heard (Mallet); because it's their voice not Cooz's (Lynch).

**The cost (¶ 6) — Wiebe loss aversion + stakes polarity**

> *"Five more years of this and the rebuild takes 18 months instead of 9, runs 3x the price, and the people closest to you have already adjusted to the diminished version."*

This paragraph turns the reader's "I'll get to it eventually" into a vivid cliff. The line *"You don't get back the version they remember. You get the version they settled for"* is a Wiebe meme-test pass — screenshot-worthy, t-shirt portable, finally-someone-said-it.

**The doorway (¶ 7) — Lynch identity-pivot + Donald Miller GP1**

> *"I work with people who refuse to settle for that."*

Cooz enters in 9 words. Not as a personal trainer. As the person who serves the reader's identity-decision (refuse to settle). The reader pre-qualifies themselves into the relationship. Most LinkedIn Abouts get this paragraph wrong by introducing the writer — *"I've spent 9 years..."* This version introduces the reader's choice — *"I work with people who refuse to settle."*

**The receipts (¶ 8-10) — dual-proof: speed + retention**

Mari leads (3 months · 16 lbs muscle · fastest transformation Cooz has had) — anchors the SPEED claim and defuses the "this guy must be slow because he talks about 9 years" misread. Then Mike + Carron + Corey + Robin compressed into one paragraph (60 words) anchor the RETENTION claim. Then ¶ 10 reframes retention from wait-time to renewal-evidence: *"They aren't locked in. They renew because the work compounds."* The duration is proof of compounding, not proof of patience.

**The methodology (¶ 9-10) — Brave Choice surfaced as the reader's own questions**

Robin's verbatim ("Brave Choice") is named once in ¶ 8 and then immediately recedes. The 3 questions appear in ¶ 9 — but they're framed as *"Three questions you ask yourself..."* The questions belong to the reader the moment they read them. This is Lynch's customer-as-media principle applied to methodology: the reader becomes the unpaid distributor of the IP because they recognize themselves as already running it.

**The close (¶ 11-12) — Donald Miller GP8 cognitive resolution**

The conditional CTA ("If your body is starting to break...") doesn't ask the reader to take action. It asks them to confirm the diagnosis they've already self-administered through the previous 6 paragraphs. By the time they hit "book the Triage Audit," the math is done.

## ALGORITHMIC OPTIMIZATION LAYER (Diandra Escobar)

LinkedIn's 360 Brew algorithm scores profiles on 5 fields: Headline, About, Experience, Featured, Activity. Profiles that don't optimize for AI retrieval get suppressed even when post quality is high.

### The 5-field checklist

| Field | What to check | V5 fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Headline (tagline)** | Contains 2-3 search-intent keywords + named buyer + named outcome | Variation A above embeds "Resurrection Coach," "high-performing professionals," "long-arc clients" — three keywords + buyer + outcome |
| **About first 3 lines** | Truncation-survivor — first 290 characters carry the entire pitch | The "business is thriving / man running on fumes" opener does this verbatim |
| **About body** | Real names + real numbers + real arcs (AI retrieval favors specificity) | Mike 9 yrs, Carron #2, Corey "The Night Agent," Sam 200+ lbs, Robin 8 yrs — every paragraph has 2+ verifiable proof points |
| **Experience descriptions** | Each entry tells a story (not a job description) | Restructured above — every entry earns its place |
| **Activity (recent posts)** | Posting cadence ≥3x/week, 50%+ posts in declared semantic lane | V5-LINKEDIN-CONTENT-SYSTEM.md handles this. Posts ship Mon/Wed/Fri, lane-aligned. |

### Semantic lane signaling

Diandra's 90-day semantic lane rule: pick 2-3 topic lanes max, post 80%+ of content in those lanes for 90 days. The algorithm needs that long to map you to a topic cluster.
