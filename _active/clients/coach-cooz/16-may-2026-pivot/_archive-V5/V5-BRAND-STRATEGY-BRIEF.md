## SECTION 1 — THE BRAND IN ONE LINE

**The Resurrection Coach** for high-performing professionals — **real change in 90 days, clients who stay 4, 5, 8, 9 years because the work compounds.**

**Why both halves matter together**: The market is split — coaches deliver fast results that don't hold (12-week programs) OR slow work that does hold (slow-and-steady coaches whose results take 18 months to show up). Cooz delivers BOTH. Mari (3 mo · 16 lbs of pure muscle gained · "by far the fastest transformation I've ever had") is the speed proof. Mike (9 yrs · still showing up 4x/week) is the durability proof. The duration claim is RENEWAL evidence (clients keep choosing to stay because the work pays off), not a wait-time claim. The brand spine line MUST surface BOTH proofs simultaneously — leading with speed alone reads transactional; leading with retention alone reads slow.

### 2B — CONSUMER POSTURE (Dai Media — Occupation / Activity / Thought Process)

Replaces demographics. Demographics tell you where to place ads. Posture tells you who you're talking to.

#### OCCUPATION (their role in Cooz's brand world — not their job title)

They are **the resurrection candidate**. Not a victim. Not a beginner. A high-achiever in identity-pre-crisis.

What this means: They've ALREADY built the thing they were supposed to build (the career, the company, the family, the platform). They aren't "trying to figure out their life." They're trying to figure out why the body that built it is starting to break — and why the will and the income and the time-management skills they used to build everything else aren't translating to fixing this one thing.

The role they play in the brand: They've climbed the wrong mountain in the right way. They optimized the externals and let the internal infrastructure decay. Cooz isn't here to teach them new mountains — he's here to repair the load-bearing structure they neglected while they were summiting.

#### ACTIVITY (the rituals and behaviors that reinforce identity)

What they do at 5am: Wake up before the alarm. Lie in bed for 6 minutes deciding whether to lift today. Decide no. Or decide yes and resent the decision either way.

What they do at 7am: Drink coffee before water. Three cups before the email triage. Look in the bathroom mirror for 4 seconds and look away.

What they do at 11am: Crash. Eat something they didn't plan. Tell themselves they'll fix the diet on Monday.

What they do at 4pm: The 4 PM fog. Read the same email four times. Check Slack at the moment when the meeting they're in actually needs them.

What they do at 9pm: Put kids to bed. Pour the second drink. Tell their spouse they're "just decompressing." Stay up to 12:15 watching short-form video they don't remember in the morning.

What they do at 2am: Wake up to a thought that won't leave. Check Slack. The check is the symptom — the thought is the cause.

What they do on Sunday: Plan to start the program on Monday. They've done this 47 times. They know they're doing it. They keep doing it.

The collection of rituals reads like a man / woman performing their old life while the body underneath has stopped producing the energy required to make the performance feel real.

#### THOUGHT PROCESS (the internal logic — what feels aligned vs inauthentic)

**Their internal logic**: *"I built this. I should be able to handle this myself. If I can't, that says something about me I don't want to know."* Self-reliance-as-virtue is the master frame. Hiring a coach feels like ceding ground that they should hold themselves.

**What feels aligned**: A diagnostic. A specialist. A peer. A coach who treats them as someone with executive capacity who needs an expert partner on a specific problem — not someone with motivational deficits who needs cheerleading.

**What feels inauthentic**: Being talked down to. "Crush it." "Level up." "Alpha." "Founder mode." Anyone who calls them "King." Wellness language. Therapy speak. The transformation industry's promise that 12 weeks fixes 7 years of breakdown.

**What they tell themselves at 2am that they wouldn't say at the dinner table**:
- *"I look 50, not 45."*
- *"I'm one bad sleep away from a real problem."*
- *"Everyone keeps telling me I look great. I know I don't."*
- *"I want my back to stop catching when I lift my kid."*
- *"I'd be embarrassed to take my shirt off at the pool this summer."*
- *"I'm tired of hearing myself say 'I'll get to it.'"*
- *"I used to be able to."*
- *"I want one day where I'm not faking energy."*
- *"I'm afraid the diminished version is permanent."*

This list IS the V5 brand's interior monologue archive. Every line is screenshot-worthy. Each one Cooz can deploy verbatim in DMs, sales calls, content hooks, podcast intros — and it will land because it's THE BUYER'S OWN LANGUAGE, surfaced before they could surface it themselves.

### 2E — BELIEF-CHANGE ARCHITECTURE (David McRaney)

McRaney's deep canvassing principle: **People don't change beliefs because of new information. They change beliefs because they encounter someone who has already made the change and didn't lose what they were afraid of losing.**

The 5 beliefs that block the V5 buyer from action — and the shifts that move them:

#### BLOCKING BELIEF 1 — *"I should be able to handle this myself."*
**Why it blocks**: Hiring a coach signals (to themselves) that they've failed at self-reliance. Self-reliance is their identity master frame.
**The shift**: From self-reliance-as-virtue → self-reliance-as-blind-spot. The high-performers who DID get this right hired help YEARS before the body forced them to. Mike hired Cooz at 9 years ago. Carron at 3. Corey at 4. They aren't outliers — they're the survivors. The ones still trying to handle it themselves are the ones whose bodies are forcing the decision now.
**Where it gets shifted**: Triage Audit Phase 2 (diagnose), "Mike was tempted to handle this himself" backstory, blog post on self-reliance as a foundation that's now collapsing.

#### BLOCKING BELIEF 2 — *"I'll start Monday / next quarter / when this thing passes."*
**Why it blocks**: Postponement feels like agency. As long as the start date is on the calendar, they aren't failing — they're "preparing."
**The shift**: The next quarter has not slowed down in 3 years. Postponement isn't agency — it's the symptom. The body doesn't wait for the calendar. The 47th Monday they didn't start is the data point.
**Where it gets shifted**: LinkedIn About paragraph 4 ("the next quarter hasn't slowed down in three years"), Lane 2 confessional posts on postponement, Triage Audit listening phase (count how many times they say "I'll get to it").

#### BLOCKING BELIEF 3 — *"12-week programs work — I just need more discipline."*
**Why it blocks**: It's a way to frame the failure as their fault instead of the program's fault. Self-blame protects the system that failed them.
**The shift**: The reason it didn't hold isn't your discipline. It's that 12 weeks doesn't rebuild a foundation that's been compromised for 3-7 years. The program was solving the wrong layer. You don't have a discipline problem — you have a category-mismatch problem.
**Where it gets shifted**: Brave Choice methodology lead-magnet, blog post on category mismatch, V5-PERCEPTION-ENGINEERING-BRIEF reframe 6 (Work Frame).

#### BLOCKING BELIEF 4 — *"I'm too old for this to work."*
**Why it blocks**: It absolves them from trying. If the window has closed, the failure isn't theirs.
**The shift**: Mike at year 9 is at his strongest in our work together. The body has remodel-capacity at 50 that the culture doesn't acknowledge. The window is real but you're inside it, not outside it. The 50-year-old who builds the foundation now is the 65-year-old who's still in their kids' graduation photos, fully present.
**Where it gets shifted**: Lane 3 case-study posts on Mike (60+) and Buzz (65+), YouTube long-form on remodel-capacity, ESA Tier D pitch.

#### BLOCKING BELIEF 5 — *"$1,500/mo for a coach is excessive."*
**Why it blocks**: The number feels disproportionate to "a personal trainer." Their internal pricing comparison is to commodity training, not to specialist long-arc work.
**The shift**: $1,500/mo over 12 months = $18K. The post-collapse rebuild costs $50K+ and 18 months. The math isn't $18K vs $0. It's $18K now vs $50K+ later. And the slow-collapse cost in lost presence at home, lost capacity at work, and lost years of the body holding for your kids — that's the real number, and it's bigger than either.
**Where it gets shifted**: Triage Audit Phase 3 pricing reveal, V5-PERCEPTION-ENGINEERING-BRIEF reframe 3 (Cost Calculation), email nurture day 5.

### 2F — AUDIENCE STATE MAP (Chris Cimorelli)

Cimorelli's audience-state framework: prospects move through 5 states from *unaware* to *most-aware*. The right content meets them at the state they're in — content that assumes a more-aware state pushes them away.

The V5 audience state distribution (estimated from buyer behavior + V5-COOZ-TRUE-VOICE patterns):

| State | % of TAM | What they're aware of | What content moves them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | 30% | Don't yet know their body is breaking. Performing energy, ignoring the data. | Recognition content (Lane 2 confessional posts that name the unnamed experience). Goal: move them to Problem-Aware. |
| **Problem-Aware** | 40% | Know the body is breaking. Don't have a name for the category of solution. Trying biohacking / 12-week programs / Equinox. | Mechanism content (Lane 1 Body-First Foundation posts that name what's actually happening at the diagnostic level). Goal: move them to Solution-Aware. |
| **Solution-Aware** | 20% | Know they need long-arc coaching. Don't yet know Cooz exists or why he's the right one. | Proof content (Lane 3 case studies — Mike 9 yrs, Carron #2 national, Corey Hollywood) + methodology content (Brave Choice). Goal: move them to Coach-Aware. |
| **Coach-Aware** | 7% | Know Cooz exists. Considering whether he's the right fit. | Triage Audit-direct content (objection-handling posts, the 4-pathway diagnostic explainer, the founding-rate locking mechanic). Goal: move them to Most-Aware. |
| **Most-Aware** | 3% | Ready to book. Just need a CTA. | Triage Audit booking page · email day 14 final call · DM warm-up. Goal: convert. |

**The single biggest mistake the V5 brand can make**: producing only Most-Aware content (booking CTAs, "DM me to talk") when 90% of the TAM is in earlier states. The Lane 1 + Lane 2 mix in V5-LINKEDIN-CONTENT-SYSTEM is calibrated for the actual state distribution: 55% Lane 1 (Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware), 30% Lane 2 (Unaware → Problem-Aware), 15% Lane 3 (Solution-Aware → Coach-Aware).

### 2G — THE IDENTITY COOZ IS ACTUALLY SELLING (Michael Bernoff Identity Engineering)

Bernoff's identity engineering principle: **People don't buy products. They buy the identity the product confirms or creates.**

The V5 brand sells **"The Resurrected"** — the identity, NOT the body change.

What "The Resurrected" means:
- The person who refused to settle for the diminished version
- The one who looked at the cliff at 47 and chose the rebuild over the slow accumulation of lost presence
- The one whose body holds for the next 30 years because they chose to make it hold
- The one their family stops adjusting to — because they came back
- The one who, at 60, looks at their kids' graduation photos and is in them, fully

Body change is the byproduct. The identity is the product.

This is why the brand never sells aesthetic transformation. Aesthetic transformation is the marketing motion of competitors. The V5 brand sells the identity-restoration that everyone else accidentally promises and almost no one actually delivers.

When Cooz writes a piece of content, runs a Triage Audit, or makes a brand decision — the test is: *"Does this confirm or create the Resurrected identity for the buyer?"* If yes, ship. If no, regenerate.

## SECTION 3 — STORYBRAND 7-ELEMENT FRAMEWORK

The Donald Miller SB7 framework treats every business message as a story where the customer is the hero and the brand is the guide. Cooz's complete BrandScript:

### ELEMENT 1 — THE CHARACTER (THE HERO)

**The customer (the hero)**: A high-performing professional running on fumes.

**Specifically**:
- 35-55 years old (sweet spot 38-50)
- Has built something that's now demanding more than the body can give: a company, a career, a household, a public-facing platform, a creative output
- Is performing at 5 out of 10 and knows the difference
- Has the income ($200K+ household) and the will but not the time or the diagnostic clarity
- Is checking Slack at 2 AM, faking energy in rooms where people are watching, telling everyone they're "building" while quietly hiding
- Has tried 12-week programs, biohacker stacks, Equinox memberships, Whoop straps, fasting protocols. None of them held.
- Is starting to wonder if "this is just what 45 looks like" — and refusing to accept that quietly

**What the hero wants**: To look like themselves again. To stop performing exhaustion. To rebuild the body that built the life — without losing the life in the process.

**Note on gender**: V5 brand is gender-neutral by virtue of who Cooz actually serves (Mike, Robin, Carron, Corey, Sammy, Buzz are men · Sam, Mari, Paige, Jessica, Karima, Patricia are women). The hero is "the high-performing professional" — gender shows up in proof, not in positioning.

### ELEMENT 2 — THE PROBLEM (THREE LEVELS)

The StoryBrand 3-level problem stack — most coaches address only the external problem. V5 addresses all three.

**External Problem** (the visible, tangible):
- Body is breaking down — chronic fatigue, recovery time doubled, sleep architecture wrecked, energy crashes at 4 PM, weight gained that won't come off, lifts plateaued, joints starting to ache in patterns that weren't there at 35
- Performance metrics in work / family / public roles are slipping while effort stays high

**Internal Problem** (the emotional experience):
- Performing at 5 out of 10 and knowing it
- Faking energy in rooms where people are watching
- Watching it happen before knowing what to call it
- The 3:17 AM not-recognizing-the-man-holding-it-all-up moment (or the woman version of it)
- Quiet shame that the body that built this is breaking and no one knows
- Resentment toward the optimization industrial complex that promised the fix and delivered burnout

**Philosophical Problem** (why it shouldn't be this way):
- High-performing professionals shouldn't have to choose between the career they built and the body they need
- The market makes you choose between fast results that don't hold (12-week sprints) OR slow work that does (18-month rebuilds). You shouldn't have to choose.
- Being "the coach who actually stays" should be the default, not the differentiator
- A body that built a life this big deserves a coach who delivers fast change AND sustained partnership — not one or the other

**The internal problem is the buying motivation. The external problem is the rationalization.** Mike doesn't pay $1,500/mo to deadlift 300 lbs. He pays to look at his sons and feel like the executive, the father, and the husband he wants to be — given the stresses of his life and job.

### ELEMENT 6 — FAILURE STAKES (THE NEGATIVE)

The cliff the hero falls off if they don't act:

> *"You spend the next 5 years checking Slack at 2 AM, faking energy in rooms where people are watching, watching the body that built this break — until you wake up one morning at 3:17 AM not recognizing the man holding it all up. The stakes are not "you won't lose weight." The stakes are "you will lose the version of yourself the people you built this for actually need." That's the loss aversion that buys premium-tier coaching.

Your kids see the version of you the photo on the mantle promised. Your spouse stops adjusting. Your team feels the difference in the room before you say anything. The summit isn't aesthetic transformation. It's *durability* — the rebuild that holds for the next three decades. That's the unique-angle difference between V5 brand and 12-week-sprint coaches.

## SECTION 4 — APRIL DUNFORD 5-COMPONENT POSITIONING CANVAS

April Dunford's positioning framework: 5 components that, when aligned, lock a brand into a defensible category position.

### COMPONENT 1 — COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVES

**What does the buyer compare us to?** (Not just direct competitors — anything that solves the same job.)

| Alternative | What they offer | Why they fail the V5 buyer |
|---|---|---|
| **12-week transformation programs** (BJ Gaddour, MetPro, ATHLEAN-X, Beachbody) | Aesthetic transformation, calorie deficit, 6-pack abs in 12 weeks | Doesn't hold past month 4. Treats the symptom (weight) not the system (foundation). Buyer has tried 3-5 of these. |
| **Biohacker stacks** (Andrew Huberman, Bryan Johnson, Tim Ferriss, Whoop, Eight Sleep, Function Health) | Optimization science, supplements, wearables, blood panels | Information abundance, application scarcity. Buyer has the data, doesn't have the practice partner. |
| **Equinox / Burbank Athletic Club / luxury gym memberships** | Premium facilities, group classes, occasional trainer | The gym is the venue, not the relationship. Buyer needs a coach, not a facility. |
| **Online fitness apps** (Future, Caliber, Trainerize, MyFitnessPal premium) | App-based remote coaching, monthly subscription | Asynchronous depth without presence. Buyer needs a person, not a notification. |
| **Local independent personal trainers** (Mariano-tier in Burbank) | In-person training, hourly rate or monthly retainer | No long-arc methodology, no proprietary frame, no premium-tier ladder. Buyer eventually outgrows the trainer. |
| **Executive coaches / business coaches** (Vistage, EOS Implementer, Tony Robbins) | Mindset, business strategy, leadership coaching | Solves the wrong layer. Buyer's problem is the body running the executive, not the executive running the body. |
| **Doing nothing / "I'll fix this when I have time"** | Free | The default state of 60% of the TAM. The most common alternative. The hardest one to compete against. |

**The Cooz positioning move**: Don't compete on "better workouts." Compete on **the both-and**. Fast coaches don't hold; slow coaches don't move fast enough. Cooz delivers real change in 90 days AND clients who stay 4-9 years because the work compounds. The market forces a choice between speed and durability; the V5 brand refuses the choice.

## SECTION 7 — WHAT TO PROTECT (BRAND ASYMMETRIES)

Three asymmetries that compound the brand. Protect them at all costs.

### Asymmetry 1 — Speed-AND-retention as the primary differentiator

The both-and is the wedge. Most brands force a choice between fast results (12-week sprints) and durable work (18-month slow-and-steady). The V5 brand refuses the choice — Mari proves speed (3 mo · 16 lbs muscle); Mike proves retention (9 yrs · still showing up). Every brand surface should surface BOTH proofs together. Speed alone reads transactional. Retention alone reads slow. The asymmetry is the simultaneity.

### Asymmetry 2 — Brave Choice methodology as proprietary IP

Robin named it. We've kept the naming. Productize it (lead-magnet PDF, podcast pillar, blog series, methodology page on coachcooz.com). The naming is defensible because the testimonial trace is verifiable. Don't dilute by adding new "frameworks" until Brave Choice has saturated.

### Asymmetry 3 — Multi-vertical credibility (Hollywood + executive + elite athletic + older-pro + military + health-pro)

Most coaches specialize in one vertical. Cooz's roster spans 7. This is rare and hard to fake. Lean into it in proof — every Lane 3 case study should rotate through different verticals to keep the multi-vertical credibility visible.

## SECTION 8 — WHAT TO IGNORE (OFF-BRAND TEMPTATIONS)

Five tempting moves that would dilute the brand. Resist all five.

1. **Daily-content cadence** (the Daily Body Coach mechanism). Wrong identity for V5. Daily texts feel transactional; the V5 brand is relational long-arc. Stay at 3 LinkedIn posts/week + 2 IG/week + 1 YouTube/week + 1 blog/week. Quality over volume.
2. **Generic wellness content** (mindset, journey, healing, holistic). Banned vocabulary. Wrong category.
3. **Founder / executive / creator triple-list audience naming.** Banned move. The buyer is "the high-performing professional running on fumes" — singular, specific, gender-neutral.
4. **Aesthetic transformation focus** (before/after photos as the lead, six-pack-in-12-weeks angle). Wrong positioning. Cooz is durability-focused, not aesthetic-focused. Aesthetic outcomes happen as a byproduct, never as the lead.
5. **Adding more pricing tiers / more service variations.** Four pathways. Locked. Adding tiers breaks the diagnostic close. If a buyer doesn't fit the 4 pathways, they're not the buyer.
