# Sunday Dinners Growth Strategy
## From One Table in Chicago to a National Movement

**Prepared for**: Javier Payano, Human Values Collective
**Date**: March 2026
**Status**: Strategic roadmap — ready for execution

---

> "In a world where screens are raising our kids, a group of parents and teachers started having Sunday dinner together."

---

## 1. The Vision

Picture this: It is 2028. In 150 cities across America, thousands of parents and teachers sit down together on Sunday evenings. Not for a PTA meeting. Not for a school board hearing. Not for a lecture from an expert. For dinner.

They pass dishes. They talk about what happened that week — the moment a kid stood up for someone, the conversation that went sideways, the question a student asked that nobody knew how to answer. They wrestle with what it means to raise a good human being when the culture is pulling in every direction at once.

There is no curriculum binder on the table. There is no sign-in sheet. There is food, and there are people who give a damn, and there is a conversation framework rooted in five values that have been tested across cultures for decades: Truth, Right Conduct, Peace, Love, and Non-Violence.

Every dinner generates a story. Every story becomes content. Every piece of content reaches a parent or teacher somewhere who thinks: *I want that in my neighborhood.* They go to the website. They find a dinner near them — or they request a host kit and start one themselves.

The flywheel:

**Dinners produce stories. Stories produce demand. Demand produces new dinners. New dinners produce new stories.**

Sunday Dinners becomes the OneTable of character education — a decentralized, community-owned movement that scales through culture, not curriculum. Javier is the founder and face, but the movement belongs to the hosts. HVC becomes the training ground, quality standard, and storytelling engine behind a national network.

The end state: **Sunday Dinners is the first name people think of when they hear "parents and teachers working together on character."** Not a school program. Not a nonprofit initiative. A cultural practice — like book clubs, but for raising humans.

## 2. Current State Assessment

### What Exists
- Regular Sunday Dinner gatherings happening in Chicago
- Javier Payano as host, convener, and philosophical anchor
- A tested philosophical framework (Sathya Sai Education in Human Values — five core values)
- Real relationships between parents and teachers built through these dinners
- Javier's credibility: Lane Tech music teacher, CPS Music Content Specialist, CEO of HVC, 3Arts Make a Wave 2021 recipient, trained at the Institute of Sathya Sai Education in Thailand
- A nonprofit entity (Human Values Collective) to house the work

### What Is Missing
- **Zero digital footprint.** No website, no social media presence, no podcast, no newsletter. The dinners exist in physical space only. This is both the biggest vulnerability and the biggest opportunity — when the content machine turns on, it will feel like discovery, not marketing.
- **No documentation system.** No photos being taken intentionally. No testimonials being collected. No attendance being tracked. No stories being captured.
- **No host playbook.** The format lives in Javier's head. If he gets sick on a Sunday, the dinner does not happen. This is a single point of failure.
- **No measurement framework.** We cannot prove impact because we are not tracking anything.
- **No fundraising infrastructure.** No donor pipeline, no grant applications in progress, no case statement.

### The Core Insight
Sunday Dinners already works. The product is proven. What is missing is the capture system (documentation + content) and the replication system (host kit + training). Everything in this strategy is about building those two systems while protecting what makes the dinners work in the first place.

## 3. Phase 1: Document & Prove (Months 1-3)

The single goal of Phase 1: **Build an irrefutable evidence base that Sunday Dinners changes lives.** Nothing scales without proof. Nothing gets funded without stories.

### 3.1 Documentation System

**Every dinner gets documented. Every single one.** Assign a "dinner documenter" role — this is NOT Javier. Javier hosts. Someone else captures.

**Photo protocol:**
- 3-5 candid shots per dinner (people laughing, passing food, leaning in during conversation)
- 1 group shot at the end (optional, only if natural)
- Food close-ups (the meal matters — it is the vehicle)
- Phone on a tripod works fine. No professional photographer needed yet.
- Store in a shared Google Photos album, tagged by date

**Audio capture:**
- Record 10-15 minutes of open conversation per dinner (with consent)
- Use Voice Memos or Otter.ai for auto-transcription
- Pull 2-3 quotable moments per recording
- These become podcast seeds and social content

**Testimonial collection:**
- After every dinner, text 2-3 attendees: "What is one thing from tonight's dinner that stuck with you?"
- Screenshot the responses (with permission)
- Quarterly: sit down with 3 regulars for 15-minute video testimonials (phone camera, natural light, kitchen table setting)

**Attendance tracking:**
- Simple Google Sheet: date, number of attendees, new vs. returning, parent vs. teacher ratio
- Note any breakthroughs, emotional moments, or recurring themes

### 3.2 Metrics to Track from Day One

| Metric | How to Track | Why It Matters |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| Attendance per dinner | Google Sheet | Growth baseline |
| New vs. returning ratio | Google Sheet | Retention signal |
| Parent-to-teacher ratio | Google Sheet | Balance indicator |
| Unsolicited referrals | Ask "how did you hear about us" | Organic pull signal |
| Testimonial quality score (1-5) | Subjective rating | Story readiness |
| Post-dinner text response rate | Track responses | Engagement depth |
| Conversation themes | Documenter notes | Content pipeline fuel |

### 3.3 Building the Evidence Base

**Immediate actions:**
1. Compile a one-page fact sheet: number of dinners held to date, estimated total attendance, neighborhoods represented, schools represented
2. Collect 5-10 retroactive testimonials from long-time attendees ("Looking back, what has Sunday Dinners meant to you?")
3. Document 3 "transformation stories" — specific people whose parenting or teaching shifted because of the dinners. Names can be changed, but details matter.
4. Create a simple impact narrative: "X dinners, Y people, Z neighborhoods, and here is what happened..."

**Research alignment (for grant applications):**
- Map every dinner practice to peer-reviewed research: shared meals (Dunbar), family dinner outcomes (Harvard), character education demand (86% of parents), loneliness epidemic (Surgeon General)
- Build a 2-page "Science Behind Sunday Dinners" document
- This becomes the backbone of every grant application and press pitch

### 3.4 Content from Each Dinner (Phase 1 — Light Touch)

Even before the full content engine is running, each dinner should produce:
1. **1 photo post** for future social media (bank them now)
2. **1 quote card** from an attendee or from the conversation
3. **1 short reflection** written by Javier (voice memo → transcript → edit, 200 words max)
4. **1 data point** added to the tracking sheet

This creates a content bank of 12-15 posts before any social account even launches.

## 4. Phase 2: Content Engine (Months 2-6)

The content engine has one job: **turn every Sunday Dinner into a reason for someone new to care.** Content is not marketing. Content is proof that this thing is alive.

### 4.1 Podcast: "Sunday Dinner Conversations"

**Format:** 25-35 minute episodes. Two types:
- **Table Talk** (weekly): A lightly edited recording from an actual dinner conversation. Raw, real, imperfect. Listeners feel like they pulled up a chair. Javier provides 2-3 minutes of context at the top and a 1-minute reflection at the end.
- **The Deep Plate** (monthly): Javier sits down with one parent or teacher for a longer conversation about their journey — what they are struggling with, what shifted, what they wish someone had told them.

**Why this format:** Authenticity is the product. Overproduction kills it. The podcast should sound like dinner, not like a TED talk.

**Technical setup:**
- Record at the dinner table using a Zoom H1n or similar ($100)
- Edit in Descript or GarageBand (free/cheap)
- Host on Spotify for Podcasters (free) — distributes to Apple, Spotify, Google
- Cover art: warm, inviting, photo-based (not illustrated)

**Frequency:** Weekly Table Talk + monthly Deep Plate = ~5 episodes/month

**Distribution strategy:**
- Spotify and Apple Podcasts (primary)
- YouTube (audio with static image or simple video)
- Clips on social media (see below)

**Episode 1 suggestion:** Javier tells the origin story. Why he started having people over for dinner. What happened. Where it is going. This becomes the foundational piece everything else links back to.

### 4.2 Social Media Strategy

**Primary platform: Instagram.** Here is why:
- Visual-first (food + faces + community = native to the platform)
- Reels have massive organic reach for new accounts
- Parent demographic (30-50) is heavily active
- The aesthetic of Sunday Dinners — warm lighting, home-cooked food, real conversations — is inherently shareable

**Secondary platform: Facebook Groups.** Not a Facebook page (organic reach is dead). A private Facebook Group called "Sunday Dinner Parents & Teachers" for attendees and interested people nationwide. This becomes the community layer.

**Tertiary: LinkedIn.** For Javier's personal thought leadership, grant/partnership signaling, and reaching education administrators. 1-2 posts per week, long-form.

**TikTok: Not yet.** Revisit at Month 6 if the Instagram Reels strategy is working.

**Content cadence (Instagram):**

| Day | Content Type | Example |
|-----|-------------|---------|
| Monday | Quote card from Sunday's dinner | "My son asked me why I never apologize. I didn't have an answer. That's why I keep coming back." |
| Wednesday | Photo carousel or short Reel | 30-second clip of laughter at the table, or 4 photos from dinner |
| Friday | Javier reflection or value spotlight | 150-word caption on one of the five values, tied to a real dinner moment |
| Sunday | "Tonight's table" — real-time story | Instagram Stories showing prep, the table, arrivals |

**Content pillars:**
1. **The Table** — what happens at dinner (moments, quotes, food)
2. **The Values** — the five human values made practical and modern
3. **The People** — profiles of parents and teachers, their stories
4. **The Why** — the crisis in character education, loneliness, screen culture
5. **The Movement** — behind-the-scenes of building something

### 4.3 Newsletter: "The Sunday Dinner Letter"

**Format:** Weekly email, sent Monday mornings. Short — 500 words max.

**Structure:**
- **What We Talked About** — 2-3 sentence summary of Sunday's conversation theme
- **The Moment** — one specific story or exchange from the dinner
- **Your Turn** — a question or conversation prompt the reader can use at their own dinner table that week
- **One Link** — podcast episode, article, or resource related to the theme

**Platform:** Substack (free to start, built-in discovery, easy to monetize later)

**Why this works:** The newsletter is a low-commitment entry point. Someone who will never fly to Chicago for dinner can still participate in the conversation. And every "Your Turn" prompt turns the reader into a practitioner — they start having these conversations at home, which makes them want the full experience, which drives demand for local dinners.

**Growth target:** 500 subscribers by Month 6 (achievable with podcast cross-promotion + Instagram bio link + Javier's existing network)

### 4.4 Video Content Strategy

**Do not over-invest here early.** Video is expensive and time-consuming. Start with:

1. **Phone-shot Reels** (30-60 seconds): Javier talking directly to camera after a dinner, sharing one thing that happened. Raw, unscripted, in the kitchen with dishes in the background. This is the aesthetic.
2. **Testimonial clips** (60-90 seconds): Parents and teachers sharing what the dinners mean to them. Phone camera, natural setting.
3. **One "mini-doc"** at Month 6: 3-5 minute produced piece showing a full dinner from prep to goodbye. This becomes the fundraising centerpiece and the "share this with everyone you know" piece.

**Do not hire a videographer yet.** Authenticity > production value at this stage.

### 4.5 How One Dinner Becomes 10+ Pieces of Content

Here is the content extraction map for a single Sunday Dinner:

```
ONE SUNDAY DINNER
       │
       ├── 1. Podcast episode (Table Talk) — 25-35 min
       ├── 2. Newsletter issue — Monday morning
       ├── 3. Instagram quote card — Monday
       ├── 4. Instagram photo carousel — Wednesday
       ├── 5. Instagram Reel (30-sec dinner moment) — Wednesday
       ├── 6. Javier reflection post (Instagram + LinkedIn) — Friday
       ├── 7. Instagram Stories (real-time) — Sunday
       ├── 8. Podcast audiogram clip — for social sharing
       ├── 9. Facebook Group discussion prompt — from dinner theme
       └── 10. "Your Turn" conversation card — shareable graphic
```

One dinner. Ten pieces of content. Every week. The flywheel feeds itself.

## 5. Phase 3: Train & Replicate (Months 4-9)

Phase 3 answers the question that content will inevitably generate: **"How do I start one of these in my neighborhood?"**

The answer cannot be "call Javier." It has to be a system.

### 5.1 The Sunday Dinner Host Kit

A complete package for someone to launch and sustain their own Sunday Dinner. Physical + digital.

**What is in the kit:**

1. **The Host Guide** (20-30 pages, designed)
   - The origin story and philosophy
   - The five values explained in plain, secular language
   - How to set up your first dinner (space, food, invitations)
   - Conversation facilitation guide: how to open, how to go deep, how to close
   - "What if" scenarios (conflict, someone dominates, nobody talks, tears)
   - Food suggestions and potluck coordination tips

2. **Conversation Card Deck** (52 cards, physical)
   - Each card has a question or prompt tied to one of the five values
   - Designed for a parent or teacher to pull one and start a conversation
   - Example: "When was the last time you told your child the truth about something hard? What happened?"
   - Example: "What is one thing you wish a teacher had said to you growing up?"

3. **The First 4 Dinners** (structured curriculum for new hosts)
   - Dinner 1: "Why We Are Here" — introductions, the concept, light conversation
   - Dinner 2: "Truth at the Table" — focused on honesty in parenting and teaching
   - Dinner 3: "Right Conduct" — what we do when no one is watching
   - Dinner 4: "Open Table" — community chooses the theme
   - After Dinner 4, the host is free-range with the conversation cards

4. **Digital Resources**
   - Printable invitations and social media templates
   - Spotify playlist ("Sunday Dinner Vibes")
   - Host onboarding video (Javier, 10 minutes)
   - Access to the private Facebook Group for hosts

5. **Reporting template** — simple Google Form after each dinner (attendance, theme, one story)

**Cost to produce:** ~$35-50 per kit (conversation cards are the main expense). Subsidized for first 50 hosts through grant funding.

### 5.2 Host Training Curriculum

Not everyone who wants to host should host. The dinner is not just a meal — it is a facilitated values conversation. Bad facilitation kills the magic.

**Training structure:**
- **Module 1: Attend** — Every prospective host attends at least 2 Sunday Dinners (in Chicago or via a satellite host). No exceptions.
- **Module 2: Learn** — 3-hour virtual training with Javier covering the five values, facilitation techniques, and common pitfalls. Cohort-based (8-12 people).
- **Module 3: Practice** — Host a "test dinner" with 4-6 friends/family. Submit the reporting form. Debrief with Javier or a senior host.
- **Module 4: Launch** — Receive the Host Kit. Host first official dinner. Check-in call with Javier after Dinner 1 and Dinner 4.

**Ongoing support:**
- Monthly "Host Circle" Zoom call (hosts share what is working, troubleshoot)
- Slack or WhatsApp group for real-time questions
- Quarterly virtual gathering of all hosts

### 5.3 Quality Standards: What Makes a Sunday Dinner a Sunday Dinner

Not every dinner with a values conversation is a Sunday Dinner. The brand means something. Standards:

1. **Shared meal.** Not snacks. Not coffee. A real meal, eaten together. Potluck is fine.
2. **Parents AND teachers present.** This is not a parents-only group or a teacher PD session. The intersection is the point.
3. **Values-grounded conversation.** At least one conversation prompt tied to the five human values framework.
4. **Inclusive and secular.** The values are universal. No religious framing, no political agenda, no sales pitches.
5. **Regular cadence.** At least monthly. The power is in repetition and relationship-building over time.
6. **Free to attend.** Sunday Dinners are never ticketed. Potluck contributions welcome, but no one is turned away.
7. **Reporting.** Hosts submit the brief post-dinner form. This feeds the national story and the data for funders.

Dinners that meet these standards are listed on the platform and can use the Sunday Dinner name/brand.

### 5.4 First Expansion Strategy

**Do not pick cities. Let cities pick you.**

The "pull" model:
1. Content creates awareness nationally
2. People reach out: "I want this in my city"
3. Those people enter the host training pipeline
4. First 10 satellite dinners are hand-selected from inbound interest

**Priority signals for early expansion:**
- Existing relationship with Javier or HVC
- Education community (teacher, principal, school counselor)
- Track record of community organizing
- Geographic diversity (not 10 dinners in Chicago suburbs)

**Likely first expansion markets** (based on education reform energy + community dinner culture):
- Atlanta (strong education nonprofit ecosystem)
- Los Angeles (massive parent community, diverse)
- Washington DC (education policy hub, high parent anxiety)
- Detroit (community resilience culture, OneTable has presence)
- A rural community (to prove it is not just an urban concept)

**Target: 10 satellite dinners active by Month 9.**

## 6. Phase 4: Platform & Scale (Months 6-12)

### 6.1 Discovery Platform: "Find a Sunday Dinner"

A simple website (not an app) where someone can:
1. Find a Sunday Dinner near them (map view + list view)
2. Learn what Sunday Dinners are (the story, the values, the science)
3. Apply to become a host
4. Sign up for the newsletter
5. Donate

**Technical requirements:** This is a Squarespace or Webflow site with an embedded Airtable or Google Maps integration. It does not need to be custom-built. Spend $0 on custom development in Year 1.

**Domain:** sundaydinners.org (check availability) or sundaydinnersmovement.org

### 6.2 Partnership Strategy

**Tier 1 — Schools:**
- Start with CPS (Javier's home turf). Pitch to 3 principals: "We will host a Sunday Dinner for your parents and teachers. Free. You just spread the word."
- Document the results. Use them to pitch the next 10 schools.
- Long-term: Sunday Dinners as a sanctioned parent engagement program in school districts

**Tier 2 — Faith communities and community centers:**
- Churches, mosques, temples, and community centers already have kitchens, tables, and communities. They are the natural host sites.
- Approach: "We bring the framework and the training. You bring the space and the people."
- Explicitly secular framing makes this work across faith traditions

**Tier 3 — PTA/PTO chapters:**
- 22,000+ PTA chapters nationwide
- Sunday Dinners as a PTA program is a natural fit
- National PTA partnership conversation begins at Month 9 (need proof points first)

**Tier 4 — Corporate:**
- Companies with family-values positioning: Chick-fil-A Foundation, Newman's Own Foundation, grocery chains (Whole Foods community programs)
- Employee resource groups (ERGs) for parents at major employers
- Approach: sponsorship of host kits, not logo placement at dinners

### 6.3 Affiliate / Chapter Model

By Month 12, formalize the structure:
- **Sunday Dinner Hosts** — individual facilitators (trained, kitted)
- **Sunday Dinner Chapters** — city-level organizations with 3+ hosts, a chapter lead, and local partnerships
- **HVC National** — training, quality standards, content, fundraising, storytelling

Chapters are not franchises. No fees. Chapters commit to standards, reporting, and participation in the national community. HVC provides training, materials, and storytelling support.

## 8. Content Strategy Deep Dive

### 8.1 The Story Arc: From Local Dinner to National Movement

The content strategy follows a three-act narrative structure over 12 months:

**Act 1 (Months 1-4): "Come to the Table"**
- Origin story: Why Javier started this. The problem he saw. The first dinner.
- The people: Profiles of parents and teachers who keep showing up.
- The moments: Real conversations that happened at the table.
- Tone: Intimate, warm, invitational. "This is happening and it is beautiful."

**Act 2 (Months 4-8): "Something Is Happening"**
- The evidence: Research backing, testimonials, impact data
- The spread: First satellite dinners launching. New cities. New faces.
- The conversations: Deeper, more challenging content about values in modern parenting
- Tone: Growing momentum. "This is bigger than one table."

**Act 3 (Months 8-12): "Your Table Is Waiting"**
- The movement: Map of dinners nationwide. Host stories from new cities.
- The invitation: "Start one. We will help."
- The vision: Where this goes next.
- Tone: Empowering, urgent. "This is yours to build."

### 8.2 Ten Content Pieces from One Sunday Dinner

**The dinner:** A conversation about Truth — specifically, when is it right to be honest with your child even when it is hard?

1. **Podcast episode** — "The Hardest Truth I Ever Told My Kid" (28 min, lightly edited table conversation)
2. **Newsletter** — "This Sunday, a teacher said something that made every parent go quiet." (450 words, Monday morning)
3. **Instagram quote card** — A parent's words: "I realized I had been protecting my daughter from the truth, but I was really protecting myself."
4. **Instagram Reel** — 30-second clip: Javier asking the opening question, the table reacting (faces, laughter, someone leaning back and going "wow")
5. **Instagram carousel** — "5 Ages, 5 Truths: What Kids Can Handle (And What They Already Know)" — practical guide pulled from the dinner discussion
6. **LinkedIn post** — Javier's reflection on what he learned from parents that night, framed for education professionals
7. **Facebook Group prompt** — "We talked about truth at the table this week. When was the last time you told your kid something hard? What happened?"
8. **Audiogram clip** — 60-second podcast highlight with waveform animation, shared on Instagram Stories and Twitter
9. **"Your Turn" conversation card graphic** — Shareable image: "Dinner question: What is one truth you wish someone had told you sooner?"
10. **Behind-the-scenes Story** — Instagram Stories: Javier prepping food, setting the table, greeting people at the door, the full plates, the goodbyes

### 8.3 Platform-by-Platform Strategy

**Instagram (primary — 4-5 posts/week):**
- Aesthetic: warm tones, natural lighting, real photos (never stock), handwritten-style text overlays
- Hashtag strategy: #SundayDinners #RaisingGoodHumans #ParentsAndTeachers #CharacterEducation #ValuesBasedParenting
- Engage with: parenting accounts, teacher accounts, education reform accounts, community dinner accounts
- Goal: 5,000 followers by Month 12

**Podcast (consistent — weekly + monthly):**
- Cross-promote on every other platform
- Submit to "New & Noteworthy" on Apple Podcasts (first 8 weeks of consistent publishing are critical)
- Guest strategy (Month 4+): invite education researchers, parenting authors, community organizers
- Goal: 5,000 monthly downloads by Month 12

**Newsletter / Substack (weekly):**
- Enable Substack's recommendation engine (other newsletter authors recommend you)
- Offer a free "Conversation Starter Pack" (5 dinner questions) as subscriber incentive
- Goal: 2,500 subscribers by Month 12

**LinkedIn (Javier's personal — 1-2 posts/week):**
- Position Javier as a thought leader in character education and community-based learning
- Content: reflections, observations from the table, education system critiques (values-grounded, not political)
- Engage with education leaders, foundation program officers, school administrators
- Goal: establish Javier as a go-to voice in this space

**Facebook Group (community — daily engagement):**
- Private group for dinner attendees, hosts, and interested community members
- Weekly: share podcast episode, dinner prompt, and host spotlight
- This is the community layer — where people build relationships between dinners
- Goal: 500 active members by Month 12

### 8.4 "Stories Build the Movement" Principle

Every content decision passes through one filter: **Does this make someone who has never been to a Sunday Dinner feel like they are missing something?**

Not FOMO. Not exclusivity. The feeling of recognizing something you have been craving but could not name. The content should make people feel:
- "I wish I had this when my kids were younger."
- "This is what PTA meetings should be."
- "I want to be at that table."
- "I could do this in my neighborhood."

The content is not about promoting Sunday Dinners. The content IS Sunday Dinners — extended to people who are not in the room yet.
