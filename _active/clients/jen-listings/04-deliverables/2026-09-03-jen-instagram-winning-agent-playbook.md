# Jen Santulan Instagram Growth and Lead Playbook

**Decision:** Jen should not copy one successful agent or turn herself into a content creator. She should own a recognizable point of view: **the calm, protective guide for the move behind the move.** She helps SFV and Greater LA buyers and sellers see the real decision, sequence it safely, and know what to verify next.

**Built from:** Jen's private Instagram baseline; 96 Jen posts; 96 Coffee & Contracts posts; 132 recent public posts from 11 local agents; 120 popular comments across 10 benchmark posts; ten LA/SFV consumer discussions; a 12-agent local benchmark; eight national proof models; and two independent niche-bending passes.

**Evidence boundary:** Public likes, comments, followers, production claims, and polished profiles do not prove social-media-generated clients. Only Jen's own attributable DMs, consultations, signed agreements, closings, and GCI can prove that. Every proposed Jen franchise in this playbook is **UNTESTED** until her pilot creates those signals.

**Safety boundary:** Research and planning only. The Instagram account remained unchanged. The work did not include outreach or publishing.

## The verdict in one page

Jen already has attention. The prior audit verified 197,425 views, 38,275 viewers, and 7,637 interactions in 90 days. Personal content is her discovery advantage; Stories are her relationship surface. The leak is the bridge from attention to a relevant buyer or seller conversation.

The benchmark says the commercially useful agents do six things repeatedly:

1. Own a named series instead of posting unrelated ideas.
2. Make one place, property, decision, or tradeoff specific.
3. Explain the mechanism behind a result instead of posting "Just Sold."
4. Attach property to culture, history, design, or a real buyer dilemma.
5. Offer a useful next object, such as a guide, route, review, list, or valuation, instead of a vague "contact me."
6. Move the response into a trackable conversation.

Jen should launch three complementary series, but only two lead magnets:

| Series | Job | What makes it Jen's | Lead action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Move Behind the Move** | Emotional relevance and self-recognition | Warm personal storytelling plus real transaction sequencing | DM `NEXT` for the 7-Question Move Map |
| **Jen's Second Look** | Protective expertise and buyer/seller trust | She starts with what is attractive, surfaces one decision-changing question, and routes the right verifier | Comment or DM `LOOK` for the matching Second-Look Sheet |
| **One Move, Three Routes** | Practical conversion and consultation intent | She turns a complicated SFV/LA move into three plausible sequences without forcing one answer | DM `ROUTE` with the move being sequenced |

Do not launch three separate production machines. **The Move Behind the Move** is the emotional spine. **Jen's Second Look** is the proof spine. **One Move, Three Routes** is a recurring decision format that can live inside both.

### The strategic stack

```text
1. Real life or local discovery
2. Self-recognition: “this is my situation”
3. Jen's protective question or route
4. One useful object: NEXT / LOOK / ROUTE
5. Manual DM handoff and one qualifying question
6. Consultation, agreement, transaction, permissioned proof story
7. New content grounded in a real decision
```

## 1. The client Jen is actually speaking to

The niche is not merely "buyers and sellers in the San Fernando Valley." That describes geography, not a reason to choose Jen.

The sharper ICP is:

> **A decision-stuck SFV or Greater LA mover whose housing problem is entangled with money, timing, property condition, location logistics, and a life change. This person wants calm judgment without pressure.**

### Their visible questions and the problem underneath

| What they say | What may be underneath | Content response | Appropriate next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Should we wait for rates to come down?” | Fear of making an irreversible financial mistake | Compare the cost and risk of three timing routes; do not forecast certainty | Move Map or lender scenario review |
| “We have to sell before we buy.” | Sequence anxiety and fear of being displaced | Show three overlap routes and the dependency in each | `ROUTE` DM |
| “There is nothing good in our price range.” | Inventory fatigue or criteria conflict | Separate must-have, routine, and cosmetic constraints | Buyer criteria reset |
| “The house is remodeled, so it should be fine.” | Appearance substituting for verification | A Second Look on permits, systems, disclosures, insurance, or specialist review | Matching `LOOK` sheet |
| “Which neighborhood is best?” | Daily life has not yet been translated into property and logistics criteria | Compare commute mechanics, lot type, maintenance, price, and routine. Do not compare people or protected-class proxies. | Lifestyle-to-criteria conversation |
| “Should we fix everything before we list?” | Fear of wasting cash or leaving money behind | Show keep, repair, credit, or price-around routes with a real case mechanism | Seller prep triage |
| “How do I know which agent to trust?” | Fear of incentives, pressure, and information asymmetry | Show the questions Jen asks, the experts she credits, and a decision she advised against | Consultation |
| “We are not ready yet.” | Often an order problem rather than a motivation problem | The smallest next decision, not an urgency pitch | `NEXT` Move Map |

### What not to do with the niche

- Do not rank neighborhoods as "best for families," "safe," "good," or "up-and-coming."
- Do not infer preferences from race, religion, family status, disability, nationality, age, or other protected characteristics.
- Do not make school, crime, demographic, or community-composition judgments.
- Do not turn a client's sensitive transition into content without clear permission.
- Do not promise predictions, guaranteed prices, or inspection/legal expertise Jen does not hold.

The content may compare properties and logistics. It should not sort people into places.

These are conservative content rules, not legal advice. They are informed by the California Department of Real Estate's [advertising guidelines](https://www.dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/re27.pdf) and its current [AI-in-real-estate advisory](https://www.dre.ca.gov/Licensees/Advisories/Advisory_2026_03_17_AI_in_California_Real_Estate.html), which stress accurate advertising, licensee responsibility, and fair-housing compliance. Jen and her broker should review the final public system.

### The language worth turning into content

The most useful comments did not say "great post." Instead, the writers revealed criteria and objections:

- parking and neighborhood-change consequences;
- freeway noise and floor-plan disappointment;
- preservation versus teardown;
- wanting the location of a distinctive home;
- whether it is a good time to buy;
- what happens when two nearby properties behave differently;
- whether a cosmetic transformation really changes buyer perception.

This gives Jen a clean editorial rule:

> **A comment becomes content when it contains a decision, dependency, objection, correction, or lived local detail. Praise and emojis do not enter the idea bank.**

## 4. The Comment Signal Bank

Use one simple sheet or database. Do not automate collection until the manual loop proves it creates usable ideas and conversations.

| Field | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Exact comment or question | Preserve the person's words privately; paraphrase publicly unless permission is clear |
| Source and date | Jen post, benchmark post, Reddit discussion, Story reply, DM, consultation |
| Signal type | Decision, dependency, objection, correction, local detail, life change |
| Stage | Curious, planning, active buyer, active seller, buy-and-sell, post-transaction |
| Tension | Money, timing, condition, location/logistics, trust, life transition |
| Series route | Move Behind the Move, Second Look, Three Routes, proof post, Story answer |
| Evidence needed | Jen experience, public source, MLS fact, lender, inspector, contractor, title, insurance, attorney |
| Safety check | Confidentiality, permission, fair housing, scope-of-practice, unsupported claim |
| CTA object | NEXT, LOOK, ROUTE, or no CTA |
| Outcome | Reply, qualified DM, consult, agreement, closing, no event |

### Weekly comment-farming loop

1. **Collect:** review Jen's comments, Story replies, and DMs first; then a bounded sample from five benchmark accounts and relevant local discussions.
2. **Filter:** keep only questions, objections, dependencies, corrections, and self-identification. Discard praise, emoji chains, agent networking, and keyword-only comments.
3. **Cluster:** place each signal under money, timing, condition, local logistics, trust, or life transition.
4. **Route:** convert the strongest signal into one of Jen's three series.
5. **Verify:** source factual claims and assign the right specialist. Jen asks and guides; she does not diagnose outside her role.
6. **Publish manually:** one response asset, one next object, one measurement goal.
7. **Close the loop:** log whether the post created a real conversation. A comment without a substantive DM is not a lead-path success.

Recommended weekly research ceiling after the pilot: **five accounts, ten posts, and no more than 100 comments**. Depth comes from better filtering, not endless scraping.

### Franchise 1: The Move Behind the Move

**Promise:** Real-estate content about the life transition that created the housing decision, with Jen as the calm guide who makes the sequence feel possible.

**Canon:**

1. The life change happens before the search.
2. "More space" often names a routine problem, not a room-count problem.
3. Readiness is often an order problem, not a motivation problem.
4. Every move has a visible goal and a quieter fear.
5. The first protective question can be more valuable than the tenth listing.
6. A good plan removes unnecessary decisions before adding options.
7. Plan the equity, money, timeline, and housing choice together.
8. A home can support a new season; it cannot manufacture one.

**Recurring beats:**

- **Life Changed First:** the recognizable human moment before real-estate language appears.
- **The Question I'd Ask:** one protective question instead of a list of ten tips.
- **Your Next Kind Step:** the smallest action that reduces uncertainty.

**What makes it hard to copy:** Jen's real photographs, her lived perspective, de-identified or permissioned transaction decisions, and the exact way she reduces pressure without avoiding hard truths.

**Lead object:** **The 7-Question Move Map**, with a buyer, seller, and buy-and-sell version.

**CTA:** “DM `NEXT` and tell me whether you plan to buy, sell, or do both. I’ll send the matching Move Map.”

**First qualifying reply:** “Which part feels hardest right now: money, timing, the house itself, or the order?”

**Forbidden:** borrowed client stories; therapy performance; urgency theater; stock-family imagery; making Jen the hero; generic listicles; pitching on every personal moment.

### Franchise 2: Jen's Second Look

**Promise:** A calm, photo-led second pass on the detail that can change a buyer's or seller's decision after the beautiful first impression.

**Canon:**

1. Attraction earns a first look; questions earn a safer decision.
2. Every beautiful feature has an operating, maintenance, or verification question.
3. A floor plan is best judged by the path of an ordinary day.
4. Cosmetic change can improve appearance without changing systems. This is the Lipstick Test.
5. Privacy includes distance, sightline, sound, and access. Fence height alone cannot establish it.
6. A disclosed issue can be evaluated; an unasked question stays unpriced.
7. Neighborhood fit is schedule and logistics, not a ranking of people.
8. Jen's job is to surface the next question and route the right verifier, not diagnose every system.

**Recurring beats:**

- **What the Photo Gets Right:** begin generously and name the legitimate appeal.
- **The Second-Look Question:** annotate one detail that changes the decision.
- **Who Verifies It:** credit the inspector, contractor, lender, title professional, insurance agent, HOA documents, or other proper source.

**What makes it hard to copy:** the question Jen notices, the point at which she refuses to assume, the right specialist she brings in, and the outcome of the verified decision.

**Lead object:** modular **Second-Look Sheets** for pools, hillside property, HOAs, ADUs/conversions, "updated" homes, privacy, and buy-and-sell scenarios.

**CTA:** “Comment `LOOK` with the feature you're considering, and I'll DM the matching sheet.”

**First qualifying reply:** “Is this a home you're actively considering, or are you building your checklist?”

**Forbidden:** defect declarations from a photo; identifiable listing ridicule; "red flag" panic bait; crime-scene language; hidden-camera content; unattributed inspector language; hook typography that overwhelms the photograph; more than one verification question per short asset.

### Format 3: One Move, Three Routes

**Promise:** Show three legitimate routes through one complicated move. Name the dependency and tradeoff in each. Let the person's situation determine the fit.

**Fixed card:**

1. Starting situation.
2. The first fork.
3. Route A, B, and C.
4. What each route protects.
5. What each route risks.
6. The question that decides which path fits.

**Visual language:** one real photograph; warm ivory space; restrained navy or charcoal route lines; serif headline; small sans-serif labels; no orange, faux transit iconography, or loud arrows.

**CTA:** “DM `ROUTE` and tell me what you're trying to sequence. I'll show you the first fork.”

**Fair-housing rule:** Routes may compare price, timing, lot, property type, commute mechanics supplied by the client, maintenance, insurance, and transaction dependencies. Routes may not rank communities or encode people or demographics as property fit.

### Story companion template

**Frame 1, recognition:** “Which part would make you pause?” with two neutral options.

**Frame 3, hand-raise:** “Reply with the part you're trying to solve. I'll tell you the first question I'd ask.”

Stories are the conversation layer, not a repost graveyard. Each feed asset should open a smaller, easier response in Stories.

## 8. The 80–90% production system

### Weekly input packet

Jen supplies only what cannot be templated:

1. three to five real photos or short clips;
2. a local observation;
3. the question a buyer or seller asked;
4. a safely shareable decision from a real transaction;
5. the personal moment she wants to connect to the work;
6. required permission, sources, or specialist notes.

The system supplies:

- series selection;
- hook options;
- carousel/voiceover structure;
- caption draft in Jen's calm voice;
- Story companion;
- CTA and DM response;
- design specification;
- source and compliance checklist;
- measurement row.

### Content card

Every asset should fit on one card:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Person and moment | One plausible local buyer/seller situation |
| Tension | One of money, timing, condition, local logistics, trust, or life transition |
| Jen observation | A specific thing she saw, asked, changed, or refused to assume |
| Proof | Receipt, public source, credited specialist, or clearly labeled scenario |
| Series | Move, Second Look, or Three Routes |
| Primary job | Discovery, trust, hand-raise, or proof |
| Next object | NEXT, LOOK, ROUTE, or none |
| Story question | One easy response that reveals stage or tension |
| Safety | Permission, fair housing, confidentiality, scope, unsupported claim |
| Success event | The one downstream behavior this asset is testing |

### Visual grammar

- Real Jen/property/local photographs first.
- Warm ivory, natural neutrals, navy, and charcoal. No orange or terracotta.
- Editorial serif for the hook; clean sans serif for labels and evidence.
- One thought per frame, generous negative space, readable on a phone.
- Keep annotation surgical: one route, circle, underline, or margin note. Avoid an infographic explosion.
- Keep the photograph visible. The design should help Jen notice, not make the template the star.
- No generic stock families, fake luxury, crime-scene boards, or trend costumes.

### Caption grammar

1. **Specific opening:** the human moment or detail.
2. **Tension:** what the first impression misses.
3. **Jen's judgment:** the question or route she would introduce.
4. **Boundary:** what must be verified and by whom.
5. **Next step:** one useful object or one genuine question.

Avoid “Here are five tips,” “the market is shifting,” “dream home,” “now is the time,” and invented certainty. Jen's advantage is not louder expertise. It is felt protection.

## 9. The lead path

### Two objects, three doors

| Door | Object | Delivery | Qualifying question | Consultation trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `NEXT` | 7-Question Move Map | Send the buyer, seller, or both version | “Which part feels hardest: money, timing, the house, or the order?” | A specific move within 12 months or a material dependency |
| `LOOK` | Matching Second-Look Sheet | Ask which feature/property; send one module | “Is this a home you're considering now or a checklist you're building?” | Active property, inspection/disclosure concern, or seller-prep decision |
| `ROUTE` | Move Map plus a simple three-route reply | Ask starting situation and deadline | “What has to happen first, and by when?” | Buy-and-sell, contingency, relocation, or timeline complexity |

### Manual DM rules

- Deliver what was promised before asking for a call.
- Ask one qualifying question, not an intake form.
- Reflect the person's situation before giving advice.
- Do not turn every reply into a pitch.
- When the situation becomes individualized, high-stakes, or dependent on documents, invite a conversation and the appropriate professional.
- Log the source post, keyword, tension, stage, and next outcome.

### The only metrics that matter during the pilot

| Layer | Metric | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Non-follower reach, views, profile visits | Did the post earn discovery? |
| Quality | Consumer-intent comments, saves, shares, Story replies | Did the right situation recognize itself? |
| Hand-raise | Keyword or free-text DMs per 1,000 views | Did attention become permission to talk? |
| Conversation | Substantive back-and-forth DMs | Did the doorway lead anywhere? |
| Commercial | Consultation requests and completed consults | Did a real need surface? |
| Revenue | Signed clients, closings, GCI | Did social contribute to business? |

Do not combine these into one vanity score. A local-history post can succeed at discovery without producing a consultation. A route post can have modest reach and still be a commercial winner.

### Pilot advancement rule

A series advances after four posts only if it clears both:

1. **Audience-quality signal:** qualified DMs per 1,000 views or substantive consumer replies beat the appropriate recent baseline; exclude agents/vendors and keyword-only comments from organic-conversation totals.
2. **Business signal:** the four-post cohort creates at least three substantive DM exchanges and one attributable consultation request, or a precommitted equivalent calibrated to Jen's current volume.

If neither series clears, change the tension or next object before adding formats. If one clears, put 80% of production into it and use 20% to test one adjacent structure.

## 12. Do this / don't do / we're wrong if

### Do this

Run the 12-post manual pilot. Build the Move Map and the first seven Second-Look Sheets. Treat comments and Story replies as research inputs. Log every hand-raise through consultation and signed business. Use local/history content to earn discovery, then decision content to create a hand-raise.

### Don't do this

Do not redesign the whole profile, automate DMs, buy distribution, or produce thirty posts before the first four-post cohort shows conversation quality. Do not turn Jen into Andrew, Jose, Tyler, Lauren, O'Connor, or Coffee & Contracts. Borrow the mechanism; keep Jen's face, photographs, judgment, and language.

### We're wrong if

- Jen cannot consistently supply real observations, photographs, or permissioned/de-identified decisions to fill the franchises.
- The pilot attracts mostly agents/vendors despite consumer-specific tensions.
- People request the resources but do not continue the conversation.
- The calm tone suppresses specificity and becomes vague reassurance.
- The second-look concept makes sellers feel mocked or makes Jen sound like an inspector.
- Qualified conversations rise but consultations and signed business do not over a reasonable sales cycle.

If that happens, the first repair is not more content. It is a sharper tension, a better next object, or a clearer consultation handoff.

## 13. Ready-to-build lead-object copy

These are working drafts for the two pilot objects. They need Jen's licensing, brokerage, and local-market review before public use. Keep them to one page each. Their purpose is to start a useful conversation, not replace professional advice.

### The 7-Question Move Map

**Cover:** *Your move probably has more than one route. These seven questions help us find the first fork.*

**Buyer version**

1. What changed that made a move worth considering now?
2. Timing: is there a date you want to move by, or a date you must move by?
3. Money: which monthly-payment range has a lender confirmed, and what still needs verification?
4. Daily life: which part of an ordinary weekday should the new home make easier?
5. Condition: what would you gladly improve, and what would stop the purchase?
6. Before an offer, what must be true for you to feel ready?
7. Choose the smallest useful next step: finance clarity, criteria reset, neighborhood logistics, or a first tour.

**Seller version**

1. What changed that made selling worth considering now?
2. Is there a target date, a hard deadline, or only a desire to understand options?
3. Where would you go after the sale, and how certain is that plan?
4. Which repairs or updates are already on your mind?
5. How much time, disruption, and cash would you accept before going to market?
6. Which matters more in this move: price, speed, certainty, or flexibility?
7. What would make the next conversation useful: value range, preparation routes, sale timing, or a buy-and-sell plan?

**Buy-and-sell version**

1. Which event creates the move: selling the current home or securing the next one?
2. What deadline affects either side?
3. How much of the next purchase depends on current-home equity?
4. Is temporary housing acceptable if it protects price or certainty?
5. Name the heavier risk: two-home ownership, a housing gap, or a weaker offer.
6. List what needs lender, title, tax, insurance, or legal confirmation before choosing a route.
7. Choose the first fork that needs an answer: buy first, sell first, or design an overlap.

**Footer:** *A map does not choose the route for you. It helps us see what the choice depends on. Reply with the question that stopped you.*

### Second-Look Sheet template

Every module uses the same six boxes:

1. **Appeal:** what the photo gets right.
2. **Second-look question:** the issue that could change the decision.
3. **Evidence:** disclosure, permit, quote, document, measurement, or firsthand observation.
4. **Verifier:** the properly qualified person or source.
5. **Decision impact:** the answer may change the price, timeline, insurance, upkeep, available use, finance path, or go/no-go choice.
6. **Notes:** the buyer or seller records the answer and next action.

**Pool module**

- Question: What are the current operating condition, insurance implications, and likely upkeep?
- Review: disclosures, equipment age where documented, inspection, insurance quote, and service history if available.
- Verifier: qualified pool inspector or contractor and the buyer's insurer.

**Hillside module**

- Question: Which site, drainage, access, insurance, or geologic facts need confirmation?
- Review: disclosures, available reports, permits, drainage observations, and insurance availability.
- Verifier: the appropriate inspector, engineer, geologist, contractor, insurer, or public record. Jen routes the question and does not diagnose the site.

**HOA module**

- Question: Which rule, reserve, assessment, insurance, or use restriction could affect the plan?
- Review: governing documents, budget, reserve information, minutes, insurance, assessments now due or under consideration, and the buyer's intended use.
- Verifier: HOA documents and the appropriate legal counsel, insurer, lender, or inspector.

**ADU or conversion module**

- Question: Is the advertised use supported by the available permits and records?
- Review: public records, permits, plans, disclosures, appraisal and lending treatment, utility setup, and current use.
- Verifier: relevant city/county source and the appropriate contractor, architect, lender, appraiser, or attorney.

**Updated or remodeled module**

- Question: Which changes are cosmetic, and which systems or approvals changed with them?
- Review: permits where applicable, receipts, warranties, disclosures, system ages, and inspection findings.
- Verifier: public record plus the appropriate inspector or licensed trade. This is where the **Lipstick Test** lives.

**Privacy module**

- Question: What do distance, sightline, sound, access, and future use reveal beyond the fence or photograph?
- Review: visit at relevant times, inspect sightlines and access, review boundaries and easements through proper documents, and confirm any planned use.
- Verifier: firsthand observation, title/survey information where appropriate, and the relevant professional.

**Buy-and-sell module**

- Question: Which dependency could break the preferred timeline?
- Review: lender scenarios, equity assumptions, possession needs, contingency options, likely preparation window, and backup route.
- Verifier: Jen coordinates the transaction questions with the lender, title/escrow, tax or legal adviser, and other relevant professionals.

### Three manual DM responses

**NEXT:** “Absolutely. Is this about a purchase, a sale, or both? I’ll send the matching Move Map. Once you look at it, tell me which question feels hardest. That’s usually where the real plan starts.”

**LOOK:** “I have a sheet for that. Is this tied to a home you're actively considering, or are you building your checklist? I’ll send the right version, and if you want, I can tell you which question I would verify first.”

**ROUTE:** “Tell me the starting point and the date that matters most. I’ll map the first three routes I would consider and name what each one depends on. We can keep it general here, then move to a call if documents or personal numbers need to enter the conversation.”
