## 1. Top 5 hook formulas, ranked by how often they appear (across the 48 reels on the four live reels tabs)

1. **Place + era or category on screen** ("1954 Topanga Hideaway", "Mission Hills Mid-Century Modern", "$2.4M in silver lake", "Corona, Ca Luxury New Construction"): 10 of 12 on @takesunset, 3 of 12 on @samarnesonhome, the @RobertBearCedeno pinned reel. The default listing grammar; reach is steady, comments are thin.
2. **A why-question about a place you drive past** ("Why have you never seen a lake in Toluca Lake?", "Why is there no Beverly Hills Country Club?", "Can Rick Caruso's Village save Pacific Palisades?"): 5 of 12 on @tyler.therealtor. His comment counts are the highest in the set.
3. **A first-person taste opinion, the house as evidence** ("10/10 would take a midcentury over a modern farmhouse", "they just don't make houses like they used to", "i have this thing with vintage colorful tiled bathrooms"): 5 of 12 on @samarnesonhome, including her two biggest pinned reels.
4. **A contrarian claim or a dated threat about a place** ("Hancock Park is the original Beverly Hills", "Capitol Records is architecture's craziest coincidence", "'Godzilla' El Niño expected this winter"): 4 of 12 on @tyler.therealtor.
5. **The identity callout or the life the house makes possible** ("If you love the Practical Magic house…", "you, me, and a wooden canyon house?", "imagine coming home to this vintage house", "POV your house is the hang out spot"): 3 of 12 on @samarnesonhome, 1 on @takesunset. The smallest count and the closest to Jen's approved specimen.

## 2. Top 3 CTA patterns

1. **Share-to-a-named-friend plus an opinion question** ("send this to your witchy / midcentury-obsessed / mermaid-inclined friends", "would you live here?"). Every recent @samarnesonhome reel. Fair-housing-safe as long as the friend type is a taste, never a protected class.
2. **Multi-door**: comment a keyword for a guide, plus a phone number on screen, plus "buying or selling in [place] or anywhere in LA." Every @tyler.therealtor reel. The Sept 3 comment sample already showed keyword posts draw the keyword and nothing else; the conversation happens in the DM or not at all.
3. **A real next step, or no ask at all**: an open-house time (@takesunset), or nothing (the 237.8K atrium, the SOLD). Listing reels close on a listed-by credit; the credit is the community proof.

## 3. The proof-post pattern (closings and process without bragging)

- The only explicit closing in the set (@takesunset SOLD, 13.2K, about half her recent median): address, one era adjective, specs, "congrats to our sellers and buyers," credits to the stager and photographer. No price, no volume, no "top producer."
- The proof that performs is indirect: "listed by @friend" credits on other people's houses (community proof), and Tyler's first caption line "as someone who's lived through some of LA's biggest storms" (lived experience before the lesson).
- For Jen, that means proof rides inside a concrete exchange, the way the approved specimen does ("a screenshot with ??? underneath"): what the client sent, what she did, credit to everyone, no number on the frame. A closing is packaged as what the client got through, never as a sale.

## 4. What NOT to copy for a warm Valley brand

- **Luxury porn.** @takesunset's $2.4M–$2.6M architecture reels reach a design audience, not a buyer at $800K–$2M; on Jen's own grid property-first hooks are the bottom quartile.
- **Tip dumps.** "Five platforms I use to convert leads" (@RobertBearCedeno, 2.3K plays).
- **Media comedy and hustle.** "How to support your realtor friends" (147 plays), "respect the hustle" car clips.
- **Stacked doors.** Text a code + link in bio + book a call in one caption.
- **Talking-head explainers.** Tyler's delivery is her banned format; only his question grammar transfers.
- **Keyword-comment guides.** Jen finds them cheesy; the comment sample shows they produce the keyword and no conversation.
- **Another agent's address on a frame.** @takesunset does it as a courtesy; Jen's rule is neighborhood, price, specs, and the address in her DM.
- **Stacked myths** did not appear in this set at all; the accounts that sell do not run them.

## 5. Five structure cards

**Card 1 · The taste callout** (from @samarnesonhome `DbvjhP0urV-`)
Format: photo-motion reel, 20–45s, her photos. Hook formula: "if you love [a thing her buyer already loves]…" on screen; the house, price, and place on card 2. Beat map: identity line → the house as evidence, three or four shots → one feeling line in first person → the ask. CTA: send this to your [taste]-friend, plus "would you live here." Jen ICP angle: "if you keep saving the ones with the original tile…" then what $9xxK buys in tarzana this week, so the reader's taste opens the post and the price band sorts the room.

**Card 2 · The opinion, house as evidence** (from @samarnesonhome `DcObmvMuI60`)
Format: reel with ONE card for the whole run, or a single photo card. Hook formula: "10/10 would take [A] over [B] >" in lowercase first person. Beat map: the opinion → 20 seconds of the house, nothing else on screen → caption carries price, place, and the share line. CTA: opinion question in the caption. Jen ICP angle: her taste memo becomes the line ("10/10 would take a valley view over a beach view >" is the vault's "not my thing" post, ready to render).

**Card 3 · The question you've driven past** (from @tyler.therealtor `Dc9RDDovDdm`, `DcZPOWnv66q`)
Format: photo-motion reel over her own place photos, never a talking head. Hook formula: a why-question about a Valley place people pass daily, or a contrarian claim about it. Beat map: the question → two or three dated answers in serif over photos → "would you live here" → the door. CTA: soft DM ("send me the street"), never a keyword. Jen ICP angle: a Tarzana or Reseda question answered with a fact already VERIFIED in RESEARCH-PACK, so local discovery carries a buyer decision in beat 3.

**Card 4 · News to your house** (from @tyler.therealtor `DcwYx6mv8Q0`)
Format: three-slide photo card or a short reel. Hook formula: a dated event with a stake on screen. Beat map: the event → what history says, one fact → what it means at your address. CTA: send me the street; the answer is the file she already keeps. Jen ICP angle: the FAIR Plan Oct 15 post is this card; the hillside-Valley winter (drainage, slope, insurance) is the next one, dated and sourced.

**Card 5 · POV, the life the house makes possible** (from @takesunset `DcUVpSFhjnD`, and Jen's own budget-vs-wishlist outlier)
Format: reel or photo-motion over a backyard or kitchen. Hook formula: "POV: [a scene from the life you want]"; the listing is beat 2. Beat map: the POV line → three shots of that life → address and price → the showing. CTA: a real next step (a showing time, "DM me for saturday"). Jen ICP angle: her Convert slot for 5421 Bothwell and future listings, so the address stops being the hook and the Saturday afternoon becomes it.
