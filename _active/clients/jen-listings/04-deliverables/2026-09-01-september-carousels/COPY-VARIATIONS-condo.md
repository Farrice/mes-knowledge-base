# condo carousel · copy variations (jargon rewrite)

farrice — same 7 slides, same locked facts, three different ways in. every lender word is either gone or wearing a six-word gloss. numbers do the persuading; adjectives do nothing.

---

## V1 · "plain words"
*optimizes:* the safest fix — original structure, every term swapped for the thing it actually means.
*risk:* it teaches well but it doesn't feel like anything. reads a little like a handout.

1. **your credit can be perfect. the condo still falls through.** · the part nobody tells first-time buyers.
2. **the building gets checked too now.** · since august 3, 2026, lenders run a full review of the building on almost every conventional condo loan... its money, not just yours.
3. **01 · the building's savings account** · it pays for roofs and pipes. the floor is 10% of the building's budget today, and 15% for loans dated january 4, 2027 or later.
4. **02 · the last 12 months of meeting notes** · leaks, insurance trouble, and a surprise bill split between owners all get discussed in here months before anyone gets billed.
5. **03 · the building's own insurance** · it has to cover a full rebuild, and your unit's share of a claim has to stay under $50,000. otherwise conventional financing stops.
6. **04 · who's behind on dues** · 15% or more of units 60+ days late and no bank will lend on any unit in it. yours included. · *small print: fannie mae lender letter LL-2026-03 · freddie mac bulletin 2026-C*
7. **send me the address before you write.** · i'll read the package with you. it takes an afternoon and it costs you nothing.

## V2 · "the scene"
*optimizes:* the 5200 armida body style — the fact lands inside a moment, jen narrating in first person. most like her, best save rate.
*risk:* the numbers sit inside a story instead of on top of it; a skimmer could swipe past slide 4 and miss the 15%.

1. **the loan was fine. the building wasn't.** · one email, on a tuesday.
2. **you were pre-approved. that was never the question.** · since august 3, 2026, lenders run a full review of the building on almost every conventional condo loan. the building, not you.
3. **i open the budget before i look at the balcony.** · i'm looking for one thing: the savings account. 10% of the building's budget today, 15% for loans dated january 4, 2027 or later.
4. **then twelve months of meeting notes.** · page four, last november, "roof discussed." that's a surprise bill split between owners, sitting there months before it reaches your mailbox.
5. **then the building's own insurance.** · i want a full rebuild covered, and your unit's share of any claim under $50,000. over that, conventional financing stops.
6. **then the list of who's actually paying.** · 15% or more of units 60+ days late and no bank will lend on any unit in it. yours included. · *small print: fannie mae LL-2026-03 · freddie mac bulletin 2026-C*
7. **send me the address before you write.** · i'll read the package with you. i'd rather find it on a tuesday than in escrow 🏡

## V3 · "permission"
*optimizes:* the midnight thought, named and answered. turns a scary rule into four things a renter can read for herself.
*risk:* leans on feeling for two slides before it earns it; if the flip lands soft, slide 1 just confirmed the fear.

1. **what if the building is the problem, not me?** · the question under the 1am scroll.
2. **usually it is. and it's readable.** · since august 3, 2026, lenders run a full review of the building on almost every conventional condo loan. four documents decide it.
3. **you can read the savings account.** · one page. the floor is 10% of the building's budget today, and 15% for loans dated january 4, 2027 or later.
4. **you can read twelve months of meeting notes.** · dull, and worth it. a surprise bill split between owners gets discussed in here long before it gets mailed.
5. **you can read the building's own insurance.** · two things: a full rebuild covered, and your unit's share of a claim under $50,000. under it, you're financeable.
6. **you can read who's behind on dues.** · 15% or more of units 60+ days late and no bank will lend on any unit in it. that's not your credit. · *small print: fannie mae LL-2026-03 · freddie mac bulletin 2026-C*
7. **you can read this. i'll read it with you.** · send me the address before you write. i'll read the package with you ✨

## glossary card *(8th slide, or a caption block)*

- **reserve study** → the building's savings plan for roofs, pipes and balconies
- **special assessment** → a surprise bill, split between all the owners
- **master insurance** → the building's own policy, separate from yours
- **replacement cost** → enough coverage to rebuild the whole thing
- **per-unit deductible** → your unit's share of a claim before insurance pays
- **non-warrantable** → no bank will lend on any unit in it

## which one and why

**V2 "the scene."** it's the body style jen already won with, and the four documents become four moments instead of four rules.
V1 is the safe backup — ship it if she wants the slides to work as a saved reference more than a story.
V3 has the strongest slide 1 of the three; if the hook is what we're testing, run V3's opener on V2's body.
V1 and V2 carry the same seven facts, so an a/b between them tests register only — one variable, clean read.
