# writers' room · condo carousel + four reels

Six experts, isolated contexts, same draft: Kallaway (attention), Brock Johnson (share/save), Nicolas Cole (sentence craft), Harry Dry (three rules), Enrico Incarnati (IG realtor mechanics), Jenny Hoyos (shorts structure). No fact or date moved. Every AFTER line below is in Jen's register; her verbatim lines are placed, never rewritten.

The room converged hard on one diagnosis nobody was briefed toward: **the carousel's middle is labelled, not loaded.** Slides 4, 5 and 6 all open with "then the..." while the payload sits in the body. Three of six flagged it independently, from three different mechanics: retention (predictable openers kill anticipation), forwardability (a screenshot arrives with no slides around it), and rhythm (a sequence label is a position, not a sentence). That is the single highest-value fix in the package.

## 1 · the ten changes, ranked

**1. slide 6 — the hardest number in the deck, promoted out of the body.**
> **15% or more of units 60+ days behind on dues... and no bank lends on the building. yours included.**

Backed by Cole (dangling "in it" drains the terminal echo; "behind on dues" is already her own gloss), Brock (highest-share fact in the package, buried under a sequence label; "you can pay perfectly and still be unlendable" is what makes someone forward instead of save), Kallaway (anaphora). Keep the small print exactly where it is.

**2. slide 4 — headline becomes the found detail.**
> headline: **page four, last november. "roof discussed."**
> body: **that's a surprise bill, split between every owner. it was in the notes months before it hits your mailbox.**

Kallaway and Brock both promote the detail to the headline; Harry independently named this line the strongest in the whole package and the bar every other line should be measured against. Cole's split of the body is what stops the headline and body saying the same thing twice.

**3. slide 5 — the cliff edge in the headline, the rebuild clause stays on the slide.**
> headline: **if your share of one claim goes over $50,000... the loan stops.**
> body: **and the policy has to cover a full rebuild.**

Brock's case: the dollar figure is the only part that survives a screenshot. Brock wanted the rebuild clause moved off the slide entirely to the glossary; I kept it as the body line so the locked fact stays on the artifact rather than depending on a caption a forwarded screenshot doesn't carry.

**4. reel 3 hook — the current one announces her process before the viewer has a stake.**
> **you can be fully approved... and the insurance quote still moves your payment.**
> and keep as the closing beat: **i want the quote on a tuesday, not in escrow. i do this to protect you and your best interest ✨**

Harry and Jenny both killed the existing hook, for different reasons and with different replacements (see fork 2). This is the compound: Jenny's viewer-first opener, Harry's picture ("a tuesday, not in escrow") preserved as the landing instead of buried in paragraph five.

**5. reel 2 — the second beat is five ideas in one breath.**
> **eleven stations. van nuys to pacoima. it opens december 2031.**
> then: **metro signed the $2.43 billion contract in august, so it's real... it's just five years out. and that changes a question nobody asks me.**

Jenny (she speaks one word per beat; a spoken data dump costs four seconds she cannot get back) plus Kallaway (the hook already spent 2031, so the middle needs a new live question, not a recital). 6.7 miles and the 7 to 10 minute headways survive on the on-screen cards and in the caption.

**6. reel 3 caption — cut the second-policy line.**
Remove: *"it covers fire only... you add a second policy for theft, water and liability, together well above a standard one."*

Cole and Enrico landed on this line independently. It opens a second cost between the payoff and the CTA that nobody can act on today, and it ends the caption's strongest passage on the word "one."

**7. slide 2 — leave the loop open.**
> **you were pre-approved. the building wasn't.**

Kallaway alone, but structural: slide 1 asks "is it me or the building," and the current slide 2 answers it completely, so slides 3 to 6 run with nothing pulling the swipe. The rewrite keeps the flip and leaves the gap live (qualify how, on what) across the whole document sequence.

**8. all four reels — her close, then the loop line. never the CTA last.**
> reel 1: **send me the address first. i'm here for you... that's my job. your credit was never the problem 🏡**

Jenny alone. A CTA as the literal final words is a flat dead end. Placing her verbatim close, then one line that answers the hook, restarts the replay clean. Apply the same order to all four.

**9. reel 3 on-screen rows — translate them for the muted view.**
> **the insurance quote comes first now · oct 15: the state's backup fire policy goes up 29.1% · hillsides move more than the valley floor · the day your policy starts locks your rate · a tuesday, not in escrow**

Enrico alone, but it enforces the operator's standing verdict: the script already translates "FAIR plan," and the screen hands a sound-off scroller the raw acronym plus an arrow and a "≠". Sound-off is the default view.

**10. the DM bridge — keep address-first, drop the floor.**
> **my DMs are open... send me the address. or just the street, if that's as far as you've gotten. i'll tell you which paper i'd read first.**
> her first reply back: **got it... give me a day with the numbers and i'll come back with what i'd read first, what i'd ask the listing agent, and what we do next. we'll go from there.**

Enrico alone. It keeps the deliberate address-first CTA (self-qualifying, hands her the one artifact she needs), lets a buyer who has not picked a building yet still reply, and names the trade so the DM reads as help rather than a lead form. Her reply carries her recap habit.

**also carried, unranked:** Kallaway cuts the glossary as an 8th slide and keeps it in the caption only (eleven terms at the tail of a seven-slide carousel is the dead zone). Cole rewrites the one line a buyer must act on out of lender-passive: **start before the 15th and you generally keep today's rate for the whole term.** Jenny cuts "just breathe" from reel 3 and keeps the valley-floor reassurance, so the signature line is not spent twice in one drop. ## 2 · the disagreements

**fork 1 · reel 1's hook.**

Jenny says keep it: it is the line her new ending loops back to, and reel 1's whole promise is that the building has to qualify too.
**My call: keep it.** The 15% fact is the carousel's climax (change 1). Spending it in reel 1's first two seconds means the carousel's best slide arrives as a repeat. Harry's critique is right about the line's genericness; Jenny's ending fix is what pays it off.

**fork 2 · reel 3's replacement hook.**
Harry says lead with the picture: "i want the fire quote on a tuesday, not in escrow... on october 15 the FAIR plan goes up 29.1%." Jenny says lead with the stake: "you can be fully approved... and the insurance quote still moves your payment."
**My call: Jenny's opener, Harry's picture kept as the landing.** Harry's version puts "FAIR plan" untranslated in the first frame, which is exactly the failure the operator already rejected once.

**fork 3 · the cover slide.**
Harry says "what if the building is the problem... not me?" fails all three rules at once and should become "the bank checked your credit. since august 3, it checks the building's savings account too." Kallaway builds his slide-2 fix on the cover staying as the open question.
**My call: keep the cover.** Harry's replacement is a date and a mechanism stated flat, and the calibration log has already failed spec-recital hooks twice. His visualization complaint is answered instead by slide 2 giving the fear a target ("the building wasn't").

**fork 4 · slide 4's body.**
Cole splits the sentence; Harry says leave the line untouched, it is the best in the package.
**My call: split it.** Harry was defending the content, and the content survives. Once the found detail moves up to the headline, the body cannot repeat it, so the split is forced anyway.

**fork 5 · how much of reel 2's data survives out loud.**
Kallaway keeps 6.7 miles and the headways in the spoken beat and adds a bridge question. Jenny cuts the spoken beat to three facts and pushes the rest to cards.
**My call: Jenny's cut plus Kallaway's bridge.** She delivers roughly one word per beat, so a five-fact run is four seconds of screen she cannot buy back, and Enrico's caption fix already puts the reveal above the fold.

## 4 · raw notes, appended in full

### Kallaway — attention, loops, retention

**(a) three highest-leverage changes**

**1. Slide 2 — the loop closes before the middle starts.**
BEFORE: "you were pre-approved. that was never the question."
AFTER: "you were pre-approved. the building wasn't."
Slide 1 asks "is it me or the building" and slide 2 answers it fully, so slides 3-6 run with no open question and nothing to pull the swipe. The rewrite keeps the flip but leaves the gap live (qualify how? on what?) through the whole document sequence.

**2. Slide 4 — the "then" anaphora flattens 3, 4, 5, 6 into a list.**
BEFORE: "then twelve months of meeting notes."
AFTER: "page four. last november. two words... 'roof discussed.'"
By slide 4 the reader can predict the shape (another document, another number), and predictability is where anticipation dies. Leading with the detail before its meaning re-opens a loop mid-carousel; the body line then pays it off. Vary the openers on 5 and 6 for the same reason.

**3. Reel 2 — the hook spends the reveal, then the middle recites it back.**
BEFORE: "that's the east valley light rail. six point seven miles, eleven stations, the G line down to san fernando road, through van nuys, panorama city, arleta and pacoima. trains every seven to ten minutes at rush hour. metro signed the $2.43 billion contract in august. it opens december 2031."
AFTER: "six point seven miles, eleven stations. van nuys, panorama city, arleta, pacoima. trains every seven to ten minutes at rush hour. metro signed the $2.43 billion contract in august, so it's real... it's just five years out. and that changes a question nobody asks me."
The hook already gave 2031, so the middle is five numbers with no live question. The bridge hands the middle a new one and carries it into "wrong question."

**(b) cut:** the glossary card as an 8th slide. Eleven terms at the tail of a seven-slide carousel is the dead zone. Keep it in the caption only.

**(c) leave alone:** reel 4's three-question spine. Naming the count up front is the one list that helps retention, because it promises an end, and it is almost entirely her verbatim. Do not restructure it.

### Brock Johnson — save and share triggers

**(a) three highest-leverage changes**

BEFORE: "then twelve months of meeting notes."
AFTER: "page four, last november. 'roof discussed.' nobody's been billed yet."
Why: a forwarded screenshot arrives with no slides 1-3 around it. "Then twelve months of meeting notes" is a position in a sequence; the found line is a complete scene, and it hands the sender the thing to say ("this is what we're supposed to be looking for").

### Nicolas Cole — sentence craft

**(a) three highest-leverage changes**

**1. Slide 6 — the hardest number in the deck, ending on a dangling pronoun.**
BEFORE: "15% or more of units 60+ days late on dues and no bank will lend on any unit in it. yours included."
AFTER: "15% or more of units 60+ days behind on dues... and no bank lends on the building. yours included."
Why: "in it" has no antecedent in the sentence and drains the terminal echo; landing on "building" makes "yours included" hit as the reversal it is. ("behind on dues" is already her own translation in the glossary.)

**2. Reel 3 — the one instruction a buyer must act on, written in lender-passive.**
BEFORE: "one that starts before the 15th is generally priced at today's rate for its term."
AFTER: "start before the 15th and you generally keep today's rate for the whole term."
Why: "starts... starts" repeats across two consecutive sentences, and "is generally priced at" buries the action in passive voice; the imperative gives her a walkable line and keeps the hedge intact.

**3. Slide 4 — one sentence carrying two jobs, so neither lands.**
BEFORE: "that's a surprise bill split between every owner, sitting there months before it reaches your mailbox."
AFTER: "that's a surprise bill, split between every owner. it was in the notes months before it hits your mailbox."
Why: the definition and the dread are fighting inside one 16-word run; splitting them gives the slide its short-after-long and lets "mailbox" land alone.

**(b) cut:** Reel 3 caption, this line: "it covers fire only... you add a second policy for theft, water and liability, together well above a standard one." It opens a second insurance concept after the caption has already paid off, and ends on "one." The caption's job is october 15. Let the DM carry the rest.

**(c) leave alone:** Reel 2: "a train five years out doesn't tell you what to pay today. it tells you how long to plan on staying." Long, then the turn. Both sentences end on substance, it reframes without a single number, and it gets the pivot done without an "it's not X, it's Y." Touching the rhythm here would cost more than it buys.
