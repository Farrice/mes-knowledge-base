# Jen Instagram Lead-Path Audit

**Decision:** Jen does not need more generic content. She needs a visible bridge from the attention she already earns to a trackable buyer or seller conversation.

**Evidence window:** Private Instagram Insights observed September 3, 2026; Coffee & Contracts public posts sampled May 28–September 3, 2026; existing Jen research and system documents.

**Safety boundary:** Read-only inspection. No posts, profile fields, messages, follows, likes, settings, or content were changed or deleted.

## Executive verdict

Jen already has real reach and affinity:

- 197,425 views and 38,275 viewers in the last 90 days.
- 42.4% of views came from non-followers.
- Personal Reels can break far outside the audience; one family Reel reached 32,573 views with 95.5% non-followers.
- Property Reels also reach non-followers, but the inspected examples produced almost no profile activity or follows.
- Stories are the account's strongest relationship surface, generating 64.0% of views and 53.1% of interactions over 90 days.

The leak is after attention:

- 1,589 profile visits from 197,425 views: 0.80%.
- 12 external-link taps from 1,589 profile visits: 0.76%.
- Just 0.006% of all views became an external-link tap.
- The inspected top personal Stories created replies, shares, and sticker taps, but almost no profile or link activity.
- Qualified DMs, consultations, signed clients, and closed transactions are not attributable from the current dashboard.

This means reach is VERIFIED. Lead conversion is UNCONFIRMED. The current system cannot honestly claim that social content is generating clients until the hand-raise and downstream events are recorded.

### What the numbers say

1. **Personal life is Jen's discovery advantage.** It creates the strongest reach and emotional response.
2. **Relatability alone is not a lead system.** Most inspected posts did not give a buyer or seller a relevant next step.
3. **Agent humor attracts agent attention.** The Realtor-humor Reel reached outsiders but produced zero profile activity.
4. **Property footage is not the problem; property-first framing is.** Listing Reels reached non-followers but did not create enough curiosity or utility to move them.
5. **Stories are the best place to create a hand-raise.** The audience already watches and replies there.

### Why the posts work

#### 1. They convert information into self-recognition

They rarely stop at “Here is the market update.” They rewrite it as something a person can recognize:

- Generic: a chart of local prices.
- Specific: “St. Pete home prices are up 10%.”
- Generic: “Here is my new listing.”
- Specific: “A new build in this neighborhood shocked me because it is under $400K.”

The audience sees a place, number, opinion, or tension that feels personally relevant.

#### 2. They use contrast to make the lesson instantaneous

The “Market updates are dead” carousel repeatedly pairs a weak post with a stronger one:

- hard to read versus locally meaningful;
- interchangeable versus opinionated;
- informative versus conversation-starting;
- generic versus recognizable;
- boring versus shareable.

The reader does not have to infer the lesson. The visual sequence demonstrates it.

#### 3. They publish identity people want to borrow

“I'm a Realtor, not an influencer” works because an agent can repost it as a statement about herself:

- “I show up online so I can show homes offline.”
- “The goal isn't more followers. It's more eyeballs on your listings.”
- “Relationships over reach.”

The post succeeds as identity merchandise. Its 231 reposts matter more than the cosmetic treatment.

#### 4. The aesthetic reduces cognitive load

The repeatable grammar is:

- full-bleed neutral photo or simple object;
- dark or muted treatment;
- one large claim per frame;
- clean sans serif paired with a high-contrast serif or italic;
- generous negative space;
- one visual idea at a time;
- a final proof/action frame.

The design is consistent enough to feel branded, but the hook and example do the persuasive work.

#### 5. Proof appears inside the content, not only in testimonials

They use screenshots, specific numbers, agent messages, closing claims, and case studies. “Six buyer clients from one post” is stronger than “content can get you leads” because the result and the mechanism are adjacent.

That result is still a creator-published claim, not independently audited transaction evidence.

#### 6. Every successful post has a job

The public system appears to rotate among:

- attract: identity, humor, contrarian claims, local recognition;
- nurture: teaching, examples, templates, Stories;
- convert: case studies, audits, trials, comment keywords.

The post is connected to a product or next step. It is not left to “post and die.”

## 3. What Jen should copy—and what she should not

### Transfer

- One sharp thought per slide or Reel.
- Feeling or decision first; real-estate detail second.
- Local specificity: actual Valley place, price, street, commute, or tradeoff.
- Before/after contrast instead of generic tips.
- Evidence in the post: what happened, what changed, what the client chose.
- Stories as the companion conversation layer.
- A repeated visual grammar using Jen's real photography.
- One natural action matched to the topic.
- Case studies that demonstrate judgment, not just a happy-client quote.

### Do not transfer

- Agent-facing topics that produce admiration from Realtors but no consumer demand.
- Keyword bait on every post.
- Loud or snarky copy that fights Jen's calm, warm voice.
- Generic templates that could belong to any city or agent.
- A high-volume production target before the lead path is measurable.
- Treating likes, views, or comments as client proof.
- Talking-head dependence.
- Protected-class targeting, “safe neighborhood” claims, or school steering.

The goal is not to make Jen look like Coffee & Contracts. It is to transfer the mechanics into a San Fernando Valley buyer-and-seller language that feels unmistakably hers.

## 4. The buyer and seller problem map

These are content hypotheses to validate through replies and consultations, not claims about every Valley resident.

### Buyer tensions

| Tension | What the buyer may be thinking | Useful content |
|---|---|---|
| Affordability ambiguity | “What does my number actually buy here?” | Same-budget comparisons across Valley micro-markets |
| Payment shock | “The listing price is not the monthly reality.” | Calm payment and ownership-cost walkthroughs |
| Location tradeoffs | “How much house am I trading for commute, street, or condition?” | Honest side-by-side tradeoff stories |
| Hidden friction | “What am I not seeing in the photos?” | Inspection, insurance, HOA, repair, and resale questions |
| Readiness confusion | “Do I need perfect credit or 20% down?” | Myth correction with lender-backed boundaries |
| Representation doubt | “Can I just use the listing agent or browse online?” | Decision stories showing where buyer advocacy changed an outcome |
| Fear of regret | “What if I buy at the wrong time or choose the wrong area?” | Scenario planning instead of forecasts |

### Seller tensions

| Tension | What the seller may be thinking | Useful content |
|---|---|---|
| Timing uncertainty | “Should I sell now, wait, or not move at all?” | Decision trees tied to life constraints |
| Prep uncertainty | “Which upgrades matter, and which are wasted money?” | Before/after prep choices with cost and rationale |
| Pricing distrust | “Will an agent tell me the truth or chase the listing?” | Evidence-backed pricing opinions |
| Buy-before-sell complexity | “Where do I live and how do I sequence this?” | Timeline scenarios and contingency explanations |
| Marketing skepticism | “Will my home get more than a listing portal upload?” | Behind-the-scenes proof of positioning and distribution |
| Privacy and disruption | “How invasive will this become?” | Calm process previews and expectation setting |
| Agent sameness | “Why this agent instead of the other five?” | Local judgment, client stories, and unmistakable point of view |

| Field | Why it exists |
|---|---|

| District | Attract, Position, Connect, or Convert |
| Format | Reel, carousel, Story, or static |
| Topic and hook | Reveals repeatable demand themes |

| Views and non-follower share | Measures discovery |
| Shares and saves | Measures utility or identity |
| Profile visits and follows | Measures curiosity |
| CTA phrase | Shows which invitation was used |
| Inbound DMs | Measures response |
| Qualified DMs | Records a street, number, timeline, address, or explicit help request |
| Consultation/showing/CMA | Measures sales movement |

Primary leading metric: **qualified DMs per 1,000 views.**

Revenue metric: **signed clients and closed transactions attributed to content.**

## 7. Jen's non-cheesy hand-raise architecture

The invitation should match the decision tension:

| Content job | Natural invitation |
|---|---|
| Buyer comparison | “Send me the two areas you're comparing.” |
| Affordability | “Send me the number you're trying to make work.” |
| Listing curiosity | “Send me the street and I'll tell you what I'd look at first.” |
| Seller timing | “Send me your rough timeline—even if it is six months out.” |
| Home-specific seller post | “Send me the address if you want the honest version.” |
| Story poll | Ask one real choice, then personally reply to people who opt in |
| Personal/Connect post | No conversion CTA required; ask a human question |

Not every post should convert. Every conversion post should make the next step unmistakable.

## 8. Thirty-day proof sprint

Use Jen's existing four-district system rather than creating a third content engine:

| District | Posts | Job |
|---|---:|---|
| Attract | 4 | Local recognition, lifestyle, and shareability |
| Position | 4 | Demonstrate buyer/seller judgment |
| Connect | 2 | Preserve the personal affinity advantage |
| Convert | 2 | Invite an address, number, street, or timeline |

For each feed post:

1. Publish one Story companion within 24 hours.
2. Use one poll, question, slider, or natural reply invitation.
3. Record the content ID when a DM begins.
4. Tag the DM as qualified only when it contains a real situation.
5. Record the next sales event.
6. Review weekly by qualified conversations, not likes.

### Test cells

| Cell | Hook family | Hand-raise |
|---|---|---|
| A | “What this budget buys in two Valley pockets” | Send the two areas |
| B | “The seller upgrade I would skip” | Send the address |
| C | “A client choice that looked wrong until…” | Send the decision you're stuck on |
| D | “A Valley street/home detail most buyers overlook” | Send the street |

### Thirty-day decision gates

- **Keep:** a format generates at least two qualified conversations and can be repeated without becoming generic.
- **Revise:** it gets reach or engagement but no buyer/seller hand-raise.
- **Retire:** it attracts mostly agents or entertainment attention for three attempts with no useful downstream movement.
- **Do not automate yet:** wait until inbound volume repeatedly exceeds what Jen can tag manually, roughly five qualified conversations per week.

Do not predeclare a conversion-rate target without a real baseline. The first month establishes the denominator and exposes where the leak actually occurs.

## 9. The flywheel

~~~mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[One real client question] --> B[Reel or carousel]
    B --> C[Story poll or reply]
    C --> D[Qualified DM]
    D --> E[Consultation]
    E --> F[Anonymized decision story]
    F --> A
~~~

One buyer or seller question should create:

- one discovery post;
- one Story interaction;
- one saved reply;
- one future case study;
- one update to the problem bank.

This is how the content compounds. The post is not the asset by itself; the captured decision pattern is.

## 10. Do this / Don't do / We're wrong if

### Do this

- Treat Stories as Jen's conversation layer and Reels as discovery.
- Keep personal content; connect selected posts to real-estate judgment.
- Translate Coffee & Contracts' contrast, specificity, identity, and proof mechanics into Jen's voice.
- Track every hand-raise to an actual content ID.
- Judge the sprint by qualified DMs, consultations, signed clients, and closings.

### Don't do

- Do not redesign the brand around Coffee & Contracts.
- Do not copy agent-facing subject matter into a consumer account.
- Do not call comments or link taps “leads.”
- Do not read or export private messages for this audit.
- Do not add automation before the manual path works.
- Do not make fair-housing-sensitive lifestyle claims.

### We're wrong if

- Buyer/seller Stories already generate substantial qualified conversations that are simply untracked.
- Most business comes from silent followers who never use the proposed hand-raises.
- The next 30 days show that profile positioning—not content-to-conversation—is the dominant leak.
- Jen's actual consultations reveal a different problem hierarchy from the hypotheses above.
