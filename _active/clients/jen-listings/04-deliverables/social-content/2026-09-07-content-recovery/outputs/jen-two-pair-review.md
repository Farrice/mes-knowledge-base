## 1. When family weighs in

### On-screen text

“My dad doesn’t like the house.”

Tell me what worried him.
Then tell me what you thought before you called.

### Caption

sometimes you call to tell someone you found a house you love, and twenty minutes later you’re defending the backyard.

tell me what he noticed. if there’s something worth looking into, i want to know. it helps to have someone looking out for you while you’re picturing your life there.

but if he’s imagining a lawn to mow and you’re imagining a table outside, that’s a different conversation. you might both be seeing the yard clearly.

i want to hear what you liked before you started defending it—and what you’re wondering about now.

you were hoping he’d say, “when can i come see it?” having to explain yourself instead can take some of the fun out of finding a place. i’d like to hear the excited version, too.

## 2. Try leaving the house

### On-screen text

Before you fall in love with the house,
try getting out of the driveway.

### Caption

preferably in the direction you actually need to go.

it’s easy to leave a sunday showing, turn right because that’s where lunch is, and never try the left you’d need every morning.

then you go home and look through the photos again. the driveway probably doesn’t get much of your attention. you already know your car fits.

if you’re seriously considering the house, make the trip you’d actually be making, around the time you’d normally make it. see how it feels to get out onto the street and head where you need to go.

maybe going around the block makes it easy. maybe you discover you’d spend every morning waiting for someone to let you out, getting increasingly annoyed while your coffee gets cold.

that’s useful to know while you still get to choose. i’d happily spend a little more of the house hunt sitting in traffic with you if it saves you finding out on your first monday there.
