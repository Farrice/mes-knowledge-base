## 1. See the street again

### On-screen text

Before you make an offer:
See the street before work.
Go back after dinner.

### B-roll

A short passenger-seat clip arriving on a residential street, or existing footage of Jen walking toward a showing. Leave the full text visible long enough to read comfortably; background music only.

### Caption

you’ve checked the bedrooms. you’ve opened the pantry. give the street a little of that attention, too.

come back around the time you’d normally get home. park where you’d actually park. spend a few minutes outside instead of heading straight for the front door.

you might notice that the easy parking from sunday’s showing looks different once everyone’s home. or hear traffic you barely noticed while we were inside talking about the kitchen.

and if you work from home, make one of those visits during your working day. you’re the one who knows whether a little outside noise disappears into the background or becomes the only thing you can hear on a call.

i want you to have those details while you’re still deciding. if something bothers you, we can look into it. if you come back and feel even better about the place, i want to hear that, too.

have a home saved somewhere in the Valley? DM me the listing and tell me what a normal weekday looks like for you. let’s talk about what you’d want to check when you go see it.

## 2. You got the keys

### On-screen text

You finally got the keys.
Before you unpack,
get these five things sorted.

### B-roll

An existing clip of Jen opening a front door and walking into an empty room. Show the complete text together over the clip; background music only.

### Caption

please take the photo with the keys. you waited a long time for that one.

then, while the house is still empty, a few things are easier to handle:

1. arrange to have the exterior locks rekeyed or changed. getting the seller’s keys doesn’t tell you how many other copies are out there.

2. find the main water shutoff and the breaker panel. have someone who knows the house show you where they are before there’s a reason to go looking in a hurry.

3. take photos of the empty rooms. get the floors and walls before furniture covers them, so you have a record of how everything looked when you moved in.

4. clean inside the cabinets and drawers before you fill them. once the mugs go in, nobody’s volunteering to take them all out again.

5. gather the appliance manuals, warranties and leftover paint labels in one place. future you, trying to match a wall color, will appreciate this very much.

you can leave the guest room boxes for another weekend. getting familiar with the house is a pretty good use of the first one.

buying in the Valley and wondering what happens between an accepted offer and moving day? send me a DM with where you are in the process.
