# Jen: just starting to look

Review history only. Farrice found this below the approved standard and questioned its relevance and delivery. Excluded from writing references. The original drafting rationale below is retained as history; its production recommendation is superseded by [the source-fit correction](jen-source-fit-correction.md).

## On-screen text

for everyone in the Valley who wants to see the house
but doesn’t want to explain their entire life
at the front door.

## B-roll

Use an ordinary clip of Jen opening the front door at an open house, followed by a slow look into the living room. Keep the complete overlay visible across the clips, with time to read it comfortably. Jen chooses the footage and cover. No spoken component.

## Caption

“we’re just starting to look” is plenty 😂

you might have a neighborhood picked out and no idea what happens after that. or you’ve spent months looking online and you’re finally seeing one in person.

come look at it. the room you loved in the photos might be the one you walk straight through. you might spend longer in the laundry room than the kitchen because, honestly, having your own washer would be a big deal.

that’s the stuff i want to hear. you don’t have to arrive with a finished wish list. sometimes you find out what belongs on it by walking through a house you don’t even want to buy.

and if i ask about your timeline, “we’re trying to figure that out” tells me something, too. i can meet you there.

send me the open house you keep looking at. tell me what made you stop scrolling.

## Reference-to-adaptation connection

[Marianne Mehrer's August 22 Reel](https://www.instagram.com/p/DcV7iZSxkxY/) is the external execution anchor. It addresses a local person who wants to learn Mahjong but feels intimidated by experienced players. Its full caption acknowledges that feeling, describes the beginner experience and social setting, then gives a booking invitation. B-roll shows the venue and people at the table while the audience-specific overlay remains visible in the inspected views.

Jen's version transfers the interested-but-hesitant audience opening, footage showing the place they would enter, concrete experience in the caption, and an easy next contact. It changes the subject to an early buyer's first open-house visit and the narrator to the welcoming agent. The laundry-room detail is a conditional illustration, not an invented client story. Jen's archived open-house post supports the hosting context. No event attendance or source business result is attributed to her.

Compared with [the approved Jen floor](jen-asking-again.md), the caption answers the actual awkward sentence and lets ordinary details carry the warmth. The approved original makes a repeated question welcome; this candidate makes an unfinished search useful to discuss. It does not add a lesson or keyword funnel. Both still use reassurance; this tests transfer within that purpose, not a claim of broader creative range.

Editorial verdict: ready for a direct taste comparison. The opening and caption form one exchange, and the concrete experience replaces a generic promise to help. The remaining judgment is whether the quieter humor and laundry-room turn earn their place beside the approved original. This is not declared an equal, an approved exemplar, or proof that the system is repeatable.
