## 3. What the data says (INFERENCE, each backed by at least three reels)

1. **The first second is a spoken sentence with the claim in it, and the same claim sits on screen.** Five of six watched reels speak at 0:00. Nobody opens with "hi, i'm…" or a mood shot. The on-screen line is two lines max and holds three to eight seconds.
2. **A number in the hook beats an adjective.** Price, down payment, square footage, car count, "under a million." The down-payment number is the single most repeated winning device across accounts of every size.
3. **One surprising feature outperforms a feature list**, and the feature is something the viewer has never seen in a house, not something nice (laundry in the closet, lazy river, bowling alley, bunk room).
4. **The tour holds people by promising the best room and delaying it.** Said out loud: "we're going to make you wait for the backyard." Then the reveal is physical (slide the doors, walk the bridge).
5. **Price early or price last, never buried in the middle.** Either the first sentence (nava, dulin, kriz) or the literal last word as the payoff (rickyt). Jen's current scripts put it two lines from the end, inside the sign-off, which is the dead zone.
6. **The ask is a comment keyword, not a DM keyword.** Comments feed distribution. The DM happens after, in the reply.
7. **The agent is in the frame and doing something.** Walking toward camera from the driveway, pointing, touching, opening. Static talking heads did not appear in the outlier set.
8. **Small accounts win big with this format.** @tassirealtor 3K followers 117K views · @soldbynat 3K 39K · @_therealestateman 826 followers 58K · @lizbrown 40K 130K. Follower count is not the gate. The hook is.

## 4. What transfers to Jen, what does not

Transfers as-is: speak at 0:00 · claim on screen at 0:00 · a number in the hook · one surprising feature · the delayed-reveal tease · price in the first line or as the last word · comment keyword · her body in the frame doing the thing · one shoot cut into a series.

Does not transfer (register): "GUYS," countdown urgency, "EVERYTHING" in caps, "insane," the wide-arms "now this is what I call luxury." Her version of the same moves is quieter: the number stated flat, the feature named deadpan, the tease said as "i'm saving the backyard… stay with me."

Fair-housing note: none of the winning devices need protected-class language. The down-payment number is math (20% of list), labeled as such in FACTS.md, never a lender quote.

## 5. Reiterate off this (the checklist for every future listing reel)

- [ ] First spoken word at 0:00, no music intro, no greeting
- [ ] On-screen line at 0:00, two lines max, one number in it, held ≥3s
- [ ] One surprising feature or one misconception in the first sentence
- [ ] Price in sentence one, or as the literal last word
- [ ] Mid-tour tease of the best room, then a physical reveal
- [ ] Agent in frame, moving, touching, opening
- [ ] Close: comment keyword, then the answer-word last
- [ ] Series: one 60s tour + 15s cuts, each a different opener, "part 2" tease
- [ ] Caption opens with the on-screen line, comment keyword repeated
