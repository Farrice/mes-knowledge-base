## Post 3: "Buy / Keep Renting / Get Ready" | Day 7–9

**Core narrative:** Your rent is $3K/month. A house payment would be $4.2K. Should you buy? Here are the three honest paths.

**Hook (text):**
> "My rent is $3K. A house payment would be $4.2K. Should I buy now?"  
> 
> That question deserves three real answers, not one. 💭

**Beat one (three honest verdicts):**
> **OPTION 1: Buy now.**  
> When: You have $60K+ down payment, 6 months reserves, and a lender pre-approval that feels solid.  
> Why: LA real estate appreciation outpaces inflation. You stop paying a landlord and start building equity.  
> The cost: You trade flexibility for ownership. If you need to move in 18 months, you lose money to closing costs.  
> Honest truth: This makes sense if you're staying Valley for at least 4 years. ✅

> **OPTION 2: Keep renting.**  
> When: Your down payment is under $40K OR your reserves would drop below 3 months of total expenses.  
> Why: Renting keeps your capital flexible. If your job shifts or a better opportunity lands in a different city, you're not trapped.  
> The cost: You pay landlord profit instead of building equity. In 5 years, you've paid $180K in rent and have zero ownership.  
> Honest truth: This is rational if you're under-funded or your next 2 years feel uncertain. ✅

> **OPTION 3: Get ready.**  
> When: You want to buy but not right now. You're building down payment, paying down student loans, or waiting for a promotion.  
> Why: 6–12 months of preparation turns "I might qualify" into "I'm a strong buyer."  
> The cost: You keep renting a bit longer. But when you buy, you buy strong.  
> Honest truth: This is the path that actually wins. 💪

**Beat two (the deciding question):**
> The real question is not "Can I afford this payment?"  
> It's: **"After the payment, can I still afford my life?"**  
>
> If the answer is "not comfortably," you're Option 2 or 3.  
> If the answer is "yes, and I have reserves left," you might be Option 1.

**Beat three (lead path):**
> If you're trying to figure out which path is actually yours, send me your rent, down payment, and rough monthly expenses.  
> I'll send back the honest verdict in writing. No pitch. Just: buy, keep renting, or get ready. 📋

**DM invitation (natural language):**
> Send me "RENT VS BUY" and I'll map which of these three paths fits where you are right now.

## Post 4: "What I Handled So They Didn't Have To Chase It" | Day 10–12

**Core narrative:** Buyer found a house. Loved it. But the seller was asking about an assumable mortgage structure that didn't match his situation. I translated.

**Hook (text):**
> Last week a buyer called: "The seller mentioned an assumable mortgage. Am I supposed to assume it?"  
> 
> Translation needed. 🤝

**Beat one (the confusion):**
> His seller's loan was from 2017 at 3.8% interest.  
> Today's rates are 6.8%.  
> The seller *wanted* him to "assume" that old loan.  
>
> His first thought: "If I assume their old loan, do I get their old rate?"  
> (Spoiler: not always.)

**Beat two (what got translated):**
> I pulled the loan docs and confirmed:  
> - The original lender allows assumption on this property ✅  
> - "Assumption" means he takes over the existing loan balance and terms  
> - His lender said he *can* do it, but would need to qualify separately, and the rate doesn't transfer  
> - Net result: He wasn't getting a 3.8% rate; he was getting his own current rate on an odd loan structure  
>
> Clearer path: Accept the property, get his own financing at market rates, and skip the complexity.  
> Seller still happy. Deal still moves. One translation saved hours of confusion. 

**Beat three (what he didn't have to do):**
> He didn't have to:  
> - Call three lenders asking if he was "allowed" to assume a loan  
> - Google "assumable mortgage" and read Reddit threads  
> - Wonder if he was leaving money on the table  
>
> I handled the translation. He handled the decision. ✅

**Beat four (the lead path):**
> If you're looking at a property and the words "assumable mortgage" or "seller financing" or "three-party coordination" come up, ask me.  
> Not because it's scary. Because one 10-minute conversation prevents one week of confusion. 📞

**DM invitation (natural language):**
> Send me "CONFUSED" and I'll translate whatever the seller or lender just told you into plain English.

**Beat two (when to wait for $60K):**
> **KEEP SAVING** if:  
> - You're putting 45K down on a $500K purchase (9% down). Lenders prefer to see 10–15%  
> - Your post-down-payment reserves would drop below $20K  
> - Your job might shift in the next 18 months  
> - You have consumer debt you haven't paid off yet  
>
> Stretching too thin now doesn't feel like winning in year two. 📉

**Beat three (the get-ready path):**
> **GET READY** if:  
> - You want to be a *strong* buyer, not a qualified one  
> - You're paying down debt or improving your credit score in the next 6 months

>
> Six more months of preparation = one fewer sleepless night during escrow. 💭

**Beat four (the actual answer):**
> The real question: If you bought tomorrow with $45K down and used your remaining reserves for the move and surprises, would you feel okay?
