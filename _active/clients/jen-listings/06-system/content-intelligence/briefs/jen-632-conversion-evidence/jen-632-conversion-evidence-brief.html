the diagnostic passes conditionally

TWELVE BUYER JOURNEYS

Clean examples passed: two W-2 buyers aligning payment and commutes; a solo buyer needing decision support; and a mixed W-2/1099 buyer organizing a lender-ready question.

Overlap examples exposed the flaw: siblings co-buying with variable income and a newly self-employed buyer purchasing with a W-2 partner both belong to #6 and #2. A single-choice identity branch loses material context.

Non-fit examples needed the revised safeguards: a low-intent 18+ month browser, a represented out-of-area buyer seeking an underwriting answer, and a buyer asking for subjective neighborhood-safety guidance need nurture, route-out, or an objective-criteria redirect—not a consultation push.

the revised diagnostic contract

Preserve 6-3-2 as the strategy while making the buyer experience accurate, useful, and low-burden.

01

Route by blocker, not identity

Use four opening choices: coordinating with another buyer; buying on my own; organizing nonstandard income; or not sure.

02

Allow one optional secondary tag

A #6-primary, #2-secondary buyer receives the coordination result while Jen sees the income-documentation context.

03

Use five structured taps and one optional sentence

Primary blocker, optional secondary blocker, SFV/commute anchor, timeline, and stage; then 'What feels hardest right now?' Do not repeat these questions in DM.

04

Deliver value before qualification

Each route names one next decision. After delivery, ask whether the buyer wants Jen's help.

05

Check representation and consent after the result

Before offering services, ask whether the buyer is already working under a buyer-representation agreement and whether follow-up is welcome.

06

Use three service states

Active conversation, nurture fit, and route out. These are service-routing states, not demographic or financial scores.

07

Keep financial and legal detail out of Instagram

No exact income, credit score, tax returns, bank records, Social Security numbers, another buyer's private details, eligibility predictions, or legal conclusions.
