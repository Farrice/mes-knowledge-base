# Jen's SFV Condo-vs-House Decision Map

**Share this with buyers who are weighing a condo or townhome against a single-family house. This is the comparison framework Jen mentions in her Valley Tradeoff posts.**

---

## When to use this map

A buyer says: "I'm looking at a condo in one neighborhood and a house in another. Same price. I don't know which one is actually better."

Or: "If I buy a condo, am I trapped? Or if I buy a house, am I stuck with maintenance?"

Or: "How much does HOA actually cost me? I'm confused by all the numbers."

**Send this map.** It's not a sales tool. It's a clarity tool.

---

## The Side-by-Side Frame

This is NOT "condos are bad" or "houses are better." It's "here's what each choice actually costs you and what it gives you back."

### Monthly Cost Reality

|What you pay| Condo | House |
|---|---|---|
| Mortgage payment | $4,800 | $4,850 |
| HOA dues | $450 | $0 |
| Property taxes | $350 | $380 |
| Insurance | $140 | $160 |
| Maintenance reserve | $50 (in HOA) | $200–$400 (you choose) |
| **Total monthly obligation** | **$5,790** | **$5,590–$5,790** |

**The frame shift:** It's not "condo is cheaper." It's "which version of ongoing cost can you sustain for five years?"

## The Actual Difference: What Each Protects and What Each Risks

### CONDO or Townhome

**What the HOA does (protects you):**
- Roof repair: HOA pays, not you. Budgeted annually.
- Exterior walls and paint: HOA handles.
- Common-area maintenance: parking lot, landscaping, pool.
- Building insurance: HOA insures the structure.
- Reserves: HOA sets aside money for known future work.

**What the HOA does NOT do (creates risk for you):**
- Special assessments: If the roof fails earlier than expected, or the foundation needs urgent work, HOA votes to assess all units. You might owe $5K–$15K immediately.
- Approval needed: Want to repaint your interior? Certain HOAs approve. Want to install a water heater? Some require bidding or approval.
- Rules enforced: No noise after 9 PM, guest parking limits, limits on rental duration.
- Dues are not fixed: If the HOA misbudgets or makes poor financial decisions, dues can climb 5–10% annually.
- Mortgage complications: Some lenders hesitate on condos with low occupancy, high delinquency rates, or high special-assessment history.

**Who you call when something breaks:**
- Roof leak on your ceiling? HOA contractor. (Fast, covered, HOA liable if done wrong.)
- Your refrigerator breaks? You. (Your problem, your cost.)
- Foundation crack? HOA insurance / HOA judgment call. (Depends on if it's deemed structural.)

**The honest truth:**
- You own the box inside. You are not responsible for the box outside.
- You are responsible for HOA financial decisions affecting your wallet.

---

### SINGLE-FAMILY HOUSE

**What you control (and must pay for):**
- Roof: Your call when to replace. Budget: $15K–$20K every 20–25 years.
- Exterior paint: Your call. Budget: $3K–$8K every 7–10 years.
- Driveway/concrete: Your call. Budget: $2K–$5K every 10–15 years.
- Landscaping: Your choice of how much or how little.
- Building insurance: You select and purchase.
- Repairs: You hire the contractor, negotiate the price, absorb the cost.

**What you don't pay for:**
- HOA dues. Zero. Every dollar stays in your pocket.
- Special assessments. Zero. You never get surprised with a $7K bill.
- HOA approval. Paint your garage purple tomorrow if you want to.
- Rental restrictions. Rent it, lease it, AirBnB it, live in it—your decision.

**Who you call when something breaks:**
- Roof leak: You call your roofer, you pay, you wait for the appointment. (Slower, your problem if done wrong.)
- Foundation crack: You call the inspector, get bids, decide whether to fix. No committee involved.
- Everything is your responsibility and your timeline.

**The honest truth:**
- You own the whole thing. You control everything. You pay for everything.
- You are responsible for repair surprises. A $12K foundation repair is your problem.
- Your maintenance timeline is your call—which can be smart (fix early) or risky (defer maintenance and compound the problem).

## The Five Questions That Actually Matter

Use these to narrow down which choice fits your situation.

### Question 1: "What size emergency fund do I have after closing?"

**If you have $15K+ left:** A house is manageable. You can handle a surprise repair without panic.

**If you have $5K–$15K left:** A condo is safer. You're protected from surprise roof or foundation bills. HOA handles those. But you need to watch the HOA financials.

**If you have less than $5K left:** Both are risky. Keep renting and build your reserves.

---

### Question 2: "How long am I staying?"

**If 5+ years:** Houses often win on total cost. Your appreciation exceeds transaction costs. Maintenance timing becomes your advantage (do it on your schedule, not the market's).

**If 3–5 years:** Roughly equal. Transaction costs (5–6% of sale price) on both eat into short-term appreciation gains.

**If under 3 years:** Condo edges out. Selling is simpler. No "has the house been neglected?" surprise for the next buyer. Lower carrying costs overall.

---

### Question 3: "How predictable do I need my monthly payment?"

**If "I need to know my payment won't change":** Condo—but check the HOA financial history first. Rising HOA dues are a risk, but at least they're gradual and announced.

**If "I can handle maintenance surprises":** House. Your payment is fixed (property tax usually only grows slowly). But maintenance might cost $0 one year and $8K the next.

---

### Question 4: "How much time/energy do I want to spend on property decisions?"

**If "I want someone else managing this":** Condo. HOA handles contractor selection, vendor quality, timing. You write a check.

**If "I want full control":** House. You select your roofer, negotiate the price, decide "do I fix this now or wait?"

---

### Question 5: "What's my noise tolerance?"

**Condo reality:** Shared walls. Upstairs neighbor's footsteps. Adjacent neighbor's television. Parking-lot noise. You can't control it.

**House reality:** Your own roof. Neighbors at distance. Noise is external (street, freeway) not internal (neighbor's toilet).

If noise is a dealbreaker, rule out any condo within three blocks of a major street or near a parking garage.

## Reading the HOA Documents (For Condos)

If you're serious about a condo, read these three things:

### 1. The HOA financials (last 3 years)

**What to look for:**
- Dues: Are they climbing 5%+ annually? (Rising faster than inflation = troubled HOA.)
- Reserve fund: Is the HOA saving 20%+ of dues toward future work? (Under 20% = underfunded, special assessment risk is high.)
- Delinquent accounts: Are 2%+ of unit owners behind on HOA dues? (High delinquency = weak HOA, lender approval issues.)

**Red flags:**
- Dues rose 15%+ in a single year. (Why? Emergency repair? Poor budgeting?)
- Reserve fund is under 10% of annual budget. (Underfunded. Assessment coming.)
- 5%+ delinquency rate. (Weak enforcement, or building is struggling.)

### 2. Meeting minutes from the last two years

**What to look for:**
- Announced assessments. (Special assessments show up here first.)
- Major repairs planned. (Roof, foundation, parking-lot work coming?)
- Disputes or conflicts. (Owner complaints? Contractor failures? Hints of mismanagement.)
- Loan activity. (Did the HOA take a loan for repairs? Why?)

**Red flags:**
- Assessment vote scheduled. (This building is about to assess you.)
- Structural repair bids requested. (Roof, foundation, or parking-structure work = major cost coming.)
- Attorney discussion or litigation. (Conflict between HOA and owners = trouble.)

### 3. The CC&Rs (Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions)

**What to look for:**
- What can you modify inside your unit? (Paint? Flooring? Plumbing?)
- What can you modify outside? (Balcony? Door color? Patio furniture?)
- Rental restrictions. (Can you rent it out? For how long? Minimum lease term?)
- Pet restrictions. (Breeds? Weight limits? Number limits?)
- Parking rules. (Can you install a charger? Reserved or shared?)

**Why it matters:**
- If you plan to rent out the condo someday, strict rental rules reduce your future buyer pool.
- If you have a dog, some HOAs ban certain breeds. You find out *after* you own.
- If you want to upgrade your unit, some HOAs require bids or approval, delaying your timeline.

## The Mortgage Impact (Lender Perspective)

### Condo lending is stricter

Lenders worry about condos more than houses. Why? Because the HOA can vote to assess you, leaving you unable to pay the mortgage.

**What lenders check:**
- HOA financial strength (is the reserve fund adequate?)
- Building occupancy (do most units have owners vs. renters? Lenders prefer owner-occupied.)
- Delinquency rate (are people paying their HOA dues?)
- Special-assessment history (how often does this HOA assess?)

**Result:**
- Some condos take 2–3 weeks longer to get lender approval.
- Some condos are harder to finance (higher rates, stricter down-payment requirements).
- A condo with a weak HOA may not be financeable at all, even if the property itself is sound.

### House lending is more straightforward

Lenders care about: the house, your credit, your income, your down payment.

They don't care about:
- Whether other homeowners in the neighborhood maintain their properties.
- Whether your street voted to pave the road.
- What your neighbor does with their house.

You're approved or not based on the house and your financials.

## The Resale Consideration

**If you sell a condo:**
- Buyer will review the same HOA documents you did.
- If HOA is weak or assessment is imminent, the next buyer will factor in that risk, pushing your sale price down.
- "Why are you selling?" is partly answered by "Did you flee the HOA?"

**If you sell a house:**
- Buyer will get an inspection.
- If the roof is 20 years old or plumbing is sketchy, the inspection will flag it, and the buyer will demand a price reduction or repair credit.
- You can't hide deferred maintenance. The inspection exposes it.

**The leverage point:**
- Condo: HOA decisions affect the whole building's desirability.
- House: Your personal maintenance decisions affect the building's desirability.

## The Framework for Your Decision

**Pick CONDO if:**
- You want predictable monthly obligations and protection from surprise major repairs.
- You have less than 5 years planned in the property.
- You don't have a large maintenance reserve.
- You prefer HOA to manage common-area upkeep.
- You value simplicity: no roof decisions, no contractor bidding.

**Pick HOUSE if:**
- You have 5+ years planned in the location.
- You have $15K+ emergency reserves.
- You want full control over maintenance timing and contractors.
- You can handle (emotionally and financially) repair surprises.
- You want the lowest long-term cost and predictable monthly payments.
- Noise, neighbors, parking, and rental flexibility matter to you.

## What Jen Does With This Map

When a buyer sends a DM saying "I'm torn between these two properties," Jen walks them through this:

1. **Ask the five questions.** The answers usually point toward one choice or the other.
2. **Pull the HOA documents** (if it's a condo) and review the financials together.
3. **Compare actual monthly costs** using the side-by-side table.
4. **Walk the property at different times.** Morning rush (hear the noise). Evening (parking reality). Weekend (see the neighbors).
5. **Give the honest recommendation.** Not "this one is better" but "here's what this choice means for the next five years."

**The close:**
"You have two good options. Let's make sure you choose the right one for your life, not just the right price."
