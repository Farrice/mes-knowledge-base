## 1. The one-line job

Sell and deliver a $200 productized content kit to individual real-estate agents,
starting with three on Jen Santulan's team, and prove the system works well enough
that Jen adopts it herself.

**Not** a content agency. Not retainers, not posting-for-clients, not managing
accounts. One SKU, delivered repeatably. Every idea gets tested against that line.

## 3. The offer (locked — do not redesign)

**The Listing Launch Kit — $200 founding rate, $450 standard.** One active listing
becomes, within 48 hours:

1. **Shoot Sheet** — every shot for the whole kit in one 40-minute phone walkthrough
2. **Teleprompter Pack** — 3 reel scripts in the agent's own voice, word for word
3. **Caption + Hook Set** — captions, hooks, a designed carousel, pick-one options
4. **Stories Sequence + DM Keyword** — 5 frames ending in a keyword CTA
5. **Compliance Pass** — fair-housing screen on every line + a don't-say list

Fallback SKU, same price, when there's no active listing: **Agent Engine Starter**
(profile fix + 10 demand-researched scripts + 2-week calendar).

Founding terms: first 3 agents, paid up front, in exchange for screenshot rights
and one sentence of feedback. The discount is a trade, so $200 never anchors as
the real price.

## 5. The creative standard

**Design.** Two brand systems are already approved and must be inherited, not
reinvented:

- *Farrice-facing assets* (sample kit, landing page): "Premium Minimal" — canvas
  `#F3F3F0`, paper `#FAFAF8`, ink `#101010`, graphite `#555553`, hairline `#D8D8D3`,
  stone `#8C8C82`. Helvetica Neue only, weights 400/500/700, display tracking
  −0.025em, uppercase labels +0.16em. Sentence-case headlines. Structure via rules
  and whitespace, never containers. Square corners. One dominant idea per surface.
- *Agent-facing carousels*: built in the agent's OWN brand, never Farrice's. Jen's
  two lanes and the approved v2 system are specified in `agents/jen-brand-card.md`
  and the Claude Design handoff.

**Banned everywhere:** stock photos, emoji as design elements, gradients, drop
shadows, clip art, text touching edges, rounded accent cards, more than one idea
per graphic, and anything that reads as default AI output. If it looks generic,
redo it — the caliber gap IS the product.

**Copy.** Plain, specific, buyer-first. Numbers and proper nouns carry trust, not
adjectives. Client-facing documents contain zero operator language (no workflow
names, no internal jargon). No manufactured urgency, ever.

## 6. Hard guardrails

1. **Fair housing is non-negotiable.** Never describe the people ("perfect for
   families / couples / professionals"), never "safe" / "low-crime" / "good
   neighborhood," no schools on camera, no religious or demographic landmarks, no
   invented urgency, no monthly-payment figures without a lender's quote. Every
   deliverable gets screened before it ships.
2. **Facts are labeled.** Market stats carry a source line and a sample-figures
   hedge; program terms that change ("has covered up to 20%") stay past tense.
   Agents verify numbers before posting.
3. **Sends stay human.** Draft DMs and texts; Farrice sends them. Never auto-send.
4. **Jen's material needs her permission** before it's shown to her teammates.
5. **Scope discipline.** "Can you post it for me?" is a different (later) tier —
   the $200 kit builds, the agent publishes.
6. **Never knock the VA.** Position a caliber jump; any takeover happens on the
   client's initiative.
