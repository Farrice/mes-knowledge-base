### Story and emotional promise

Micah Reed is a 17-year-old half Black, half Filipino-American athlete, born in Chicago and now living in Los Angeles. An ACL injury ended the basketball future that had organized his identity. At Harborline Lanes, a worn community bowling alley, he discovers a different kind of athletic intelligence: repetition, oil, geometry, patience, and a team that values reliability over spectacle. His unconventional two-finger release earns him a place at the fictional Pacific Crown Youth Exchange, where he meets Ren Akiyama, a precise but warm left-handed bowler from Kobe. The trailer should sell recovery, belonging, exactness, and competitive obsession—not a simple underdog victory.

Title: **MIDNIGHT OIL**  
Tagline: **THE LANE REMEMBERS EVERYTHING.**

### Character locks

**Micah Reed:** lean 17-year-old half Black/half Filipino-American boy; warm-brown skin; compact black curls; midnight-navy jersey with restrained copper piping; black pants and bowling shoes; black sleeve on the left knee with one copper repair stitch; cobalt tape on the right middle and ring fingers. He bowls **right-handed, one-handed, no-thumb, two-finger only**. He slides on the **left leg**. His ball is matte black with one thin gold crescent and **exactly two holes total**, with no thumb hole. When a strike line is shown, it enters the right-hander’s 1–3 pocket.

**Nia Brooks:** Black teenage girl; deep umber skin; compact athletic build; microbraids; cream and sea-green jersey. Right-handed spare specialist with a controlled stroker release. Her ball is white with a coral stripe. She is dryly funny and dependable, never reduced to exposition.

**Gabe Alvarez:** 42-year-old Mexican-American lane technician and coach; salt-and-pepper beard; square mechanic glasses; navy work shirt with lane-17 patch; oil pencil; grease-worn hands. Calm, exact, never mystical.

**Ren Akiyama:** Japanese teenage boy from Kobe; straight black hair with a silver underlayer; ivory and indigo jersey; warm, funny, exact. He bowls **left-handed with a conventional thumb-in grip** and slides on the **right leg**. His pearl-white ball has a vermilion core and exactly three conventional holes. When a strike line is shown, it enters the left-hander’s 1–2 pocket.

### World locks

**Harborline Lanes:** a worn 24-lane community alley at sunset; sea-green ball returns; amber masking units; faded geometric carpet; west-facing windows; lane 17 is the emotional center. It should feel repaired, inhabited, and loved—not retro kitsch.

**Pacific Crown Youth Exchange:** a fictional U.S.–Japan youth invitational in a clean, large tournament arena with indigo/cobalt light, regimented score displays, and controlled spectacle. It must never inherit Harborline’s sea-green returns, amber masking units, carpet, or sunset windows.

### Approved dialogue—use no more than four lines

- Micah VO: “When basketball ended, I thought the athlete in me went with it.”
- Gabe: “Unusual is not the same as unaccountable.”
- Ren: “Your release isn’t the problem. Your timing is.”

Let images and sound carry everything else. Do not add generic announcer copy.

### Cinematography and animation language

- 80% grounded bowling mechanics and human observation; 20% subjective psychological spectacle.
- Favor low approach-level tracking, rear three-quarter mechanics views, profile release views, lane-level compression, macro inserts of tape/oil/shoes/fingers, and restrained 35–85mm character coverage.
- Preserve screen direction and the camera axis. Never mirror a lane to solve continuity.
- Animate on expressive 2s with selective 1s at release and pressure peaks; use deliberate held drawings before acceleration.
- Keep faces on-model. Do not beautify Micah into a generic anime lead or age him up.
- Effects are cobalt oil contours, indigo pressure lines, faint copper sparks, and momentary geometry. They reveal perception; they do not become magic powers.
- All subjective overlays vanish before pocket impact. Return to real-world action for ball path and pin reaction.

### Sound and music

Build the sound bed from HVAC, ball-return machinery, bowling shoes, tape pull, fingers leaving holes, rolling ball, pin scatter, fries wrapper, distant arcade bleed, crowd inhale, and controlled silence. Use an original score only: bowed bass, felt piano, dry hand percussion, restrained electronic pulse, and tightening strings. No copyrighted melody or soundalike theme. The final approach should shed music element by element until only breath, slide, release, and roll remain.

### Bowling and anatomy vetoes

- No hand switching.
- No wrong slide leg.
- No extra, disappearing, duplicated, or migrating fingers or holes.
- No thumb entering Micah’s ball.
- No no-thumb grip on Ren’s ball.
- No floating ball, fused hand, backward approach, reversed follow-through, or foot beyond the foul line before release.
- No eleven-pin, fused-pin, random-pin, or mirrored racks. A full rack is ten pins in a 1-2-3-4 triangle.
- No wrong pocket: Micah/right-handed uses 1–3; Ren/left-handed uses 1–2.
- No fake motion inferred solely from the mechanics stills. Use real mechanics clips for timing, finger exit, wrist motion, skid-hook-roll, pocket entry, and pin collision.
- No Harborline/Pacific Crown location blending.
- No near-readable invented tournament text; either make required text exact or keep it abstract and non-legible.

### Planning acceptance gate

Before asking to render, confirm every shot has four assigned channels: **identity reference, gear/mechanics reference, location reference, and motion reference**. A missing channel is an automatic revision. Flag any shot that cannot satisfy the canon rather than improvising. After approval, render only the quoted proof clip first. Expand to the full trailer only after that proof passes identity, handedness, slide-leg, ball drilling, lane geography, and bowling-physics review.
