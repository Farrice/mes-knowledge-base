# Deep Research Prompt — Offer Validation, Round 2

Paste the block below, unchanged, into Gemini Deep Research, then Perplexity Deep Research, then Claude Deep Research. Same prompt all three times. Bring all three outputs back.

Running it three times is not redundancy. Where the three agree, confidence goes up. Where they disagree, that is the highest-signal thing in the exercise.

Gemini: best breadth, longest runs, let it plan then approve the plan.
Perplexity: fastest, tightest citations, strongest on pricing pages and job listings.
Claude: best reasoning, will actually argue with the premise, read its dissent closely.

---

You are a market research analyst. Your client is about to commit the next 90 days and his remaining runway to a specific service offer. Your job is to find out whether it holds up, and to tell him plainly if it does not. He does not want encouragement. He wants to know what is true.

THE OPERATOR

Solo. Roughly two focused hours a day. Delivers entirely online, no in-person work. Strong at creative strategy, copywriting, messaging strategy, and pattern recognition. Runs an unusually capable AI production system, so his output volume is far higher than a typical solo operator's. He has zero case studies in DTC ecommerce and zero in B2B SaaS. His only client testimonials are from personal training, an unrelated field. He needs collected revenue within weeks, not quarters.

THE OFFER BEING TESTED

Free front end: a named-brand teardown. An angle and saturation map of one DTC brand's live Meta ad creative against 8 to 10 category competitors, pulled from the public Meta Ad Library, sent directly to that brand's growth lead and also published publicly.

Paid product: 950 to 1,200 dollars, delivered in about five days. A gap brief plus 5 to 8 finished static ads, written and designed into the angle gap the teardown identified. Finished, runnable assets, not concepts.

Buyer: the person who owns paid creative at a US DTC brand in supplements, skincare, functional beverage, or performance nutrition, currently running 15 or more active Meta ads with several live 30 days or longer.

Deliberately excluded: any monthly retainer, until one-time delivery has proven renewal appetite.

ALREADY ESTABLISHED, DO NOT SPEND BUDGET RE-CONFIRMING

These are verified from live pages. Build on them. Correct them only if you find they are wrong or out of date.

DTC.SUPPLY sells a fixed-scope sprint at 950 dollars, two weeks, 15 static ads (dtc.supply).
CREVARI sells a 1,000 dollar paid teardown called Creative Autopsy, five days, credited toward a 4,000 dollar per month pilot, and requires 50,000 dollars per month in Meta spend (crevaricreative.com).
Darkroom publishes 5,000 to 15,000 dollar per month tiers priced on asset volume, with creative strategy listed but never itemized (darkroomagency.com).
A Dribbble DTC retainer sells 20 concepts per month at 3,200 dollars with creative strategy included (dribbble.com).
YunoJuno, using real booking data, puts Creative Strategist at 69 dollars per hour average (yunojuno.com).
1,000 dollars is the same-day single-manager purchase approval ceiling for companies under 10 million dollars revenue; 1,001 to 5,000 dollars escalates to a senior manager and adds one to two business days (Tallyfy approval matrix).
Brand voice guidelines is roughly a 750 dollar commodity deliverable (WebFX live page).

WHAT I NEED YOU TO ANSWER

Ordered by how much each would change the decision. Spend your budget accordingly.

TIER 1, these decide whether the offer is viable at all.

1. Does a solo operator with no case studies in a vertical actually land paying clients in it? Find documented, specific first-client accounts from creative strategists, ad creative freelancers, and marketing consultants between 2024 and 2026. What substituted for a portfolio: spec work, free audits, results on their own account, a referral, something else? How long did it take? Find the failures too. How often is no portfolio genuinely disqualifying, and does it matter more in DTC or in B2B?

2. How does teardown-led or audit-led outreach actually perform in DTC specifically? The only operator I found with a fully documented teardown-to-paid motion is FletchPMM, and they are B2B SaaS, selling to founders, working through inbound accretion at three to four posts a week, and their cold outreach failed. I need to know whether that mechanic transfers to DTC growth leads. Find real reply rates, close rates, and documented failures for unsolicited teardowns, audits, and I-made-you-something outreach aimed at ecommerce marketing buyers. Is critiquing someone's live paid creative without being asked received as generous or as insulting? Find actual buyer reactions, not marketer opinions.

3. Does a one-time paid creative sprint convert into repeat or recurring work, and at what rate? This determines whether a 950 dollar product is a business or a one-off stunt. Find sellers of one-time productized creative sprints and any data on repeat purchase, upsell into retainer, or churn. Include the negative case: sellers who found their one-time buyers never came back.

4. Is DTC creative strategy as a bought service growing or shrinking in 2026? Are brands in-housing it because AI made production cheap? Is spend shifting away from strategy toward volume, toward tooling, or toward in-house hires? Find hiring data, agency revenue trends, agency closures, and any evidence about where creative budget is actually moving. If this category is contracting, say so directly.

TIER 2, pricing and packaging.

5. Map the entire sub-2,500 dollar entry-product landscape. Find at least 20 productized offers with public prices aimed at DTC brands in ad creative, creative strategy, creative audits, or angle research. For each one give the seller, exact price, exact scope, turnaround, and whether strategy is sold standalone or bundled into asset volume. I want to know whether 950 to 1,200 dollars sits in a real band or an empty one.

6. On the B2B SaaS side, does any low-ticket entry product exist in positioning, messaging, or AI search visibility (GEO/AEO)? A paid audit, a paid teardown, a one-page diagnostic, a workshop? Or is that category entirely 5,000 dollar and up engagements? Give real sellers and real prices.

7. What do these buyers actually reject and complain about? Find real buyer voice from forum threads, Reddit, LinkedIn comments, and agency review complaints. Where have DTC brands been burned by creative agencies and freelancers? What makes them say no? What language do they use for the problem in their own words, not in marketers' words?

TIER 3, the positioning thesis.

8. Re-test this claim hard, because my first pass may have looked in the wrong place. The claim is that "our marketing sounds like everyone else's now, AI-generated content has hollowed out our brand voice" is a real feeling with no budget attached to it. My first pass found brand voice guidelines at 750 dollars, AI humanizing SaaS at 14 to 20 dollars per month, and roughly 14 brand voice job listings with zero framed around AI content quality. Before I accept that, check adjacent categories where the same pain might be funded under a different name: differentiation consulting, category design, brand strategy retainers, creative fatigue and ad saturation services, positioning sprints. Does money change hands for this problem under any label at all? Give a hard verdict.

9. GEO and AEO. What is actually being purchased, by whom, at what price, and is there any evidence it works? Separate real retainers with named sellers from pricing-guide speculation. Find any published case study showing measurable citation lift, and find any credible skeptic arguing the category is premature.

TIER 4, channel.

10. Where do DTC growth leads and creative owners actually congregate, and does LinkedIn reach them? Is LinkedIn where ecommerce marketing buyers are, or is that a B2B SaaS assumption I am importing? Find evidence about which channels reach this specific buyer: LinkedIn, X, Slack and Discord communities, newsletters, industry events, cold email.

EVIDENCE STANDARD, NOT OPTIONAL

Every factual claim carries a URL that actually resolves. If you cannot produce one, label the claim UNCONFIRMED and move on. Do not manufacture a plausible citation.

Distinguish two things sharply. A live page where a named seller charges a specific price is evidence. A pricing guide or blog post asserting what is typical is marketing collateral, and must be labeled as such.

Payroll and salary data is not vendor pricing data. A company paying 4,000 dollars a month for an employee tells you nothing about what it pays an outside vendor. Flag anywhere I might be tempted to confuse the two.

Prefer 2025 and 2026 sources. Flag anything older with its date.

Where sources conflict, show the conflict. Do not average it away.

OUTPUT FORMAT

First, a headline verdict in three to five sentences. Does the offer hold up, and if not, what breaks first?

Second, an answer per question, Tier 1 first, each carrying a confidence label of VERIFIED, LIKELY, or UNCONFIRMED.

Third, a claim table with claim, verdict, source URL, and date.

Fourth, what you would change about the offer, in priority order, with the evidence behind each change.

Fifth, the strongest case against this offer. Argue it properly, as if you were being paid to talk me out of it. Do not soften this section.

Sixth, what you could not find out, stated plainly, and what it would take to find out.

ONE LAST INSTRUCTION

If the honest answer is that this offer is weak, or that the buyer does not exist as described, or that the price is wrong, say so in the first paragraph. Telling me what I want to hear costs me money I do not have. I would rather be wrong now than in six weeks.
