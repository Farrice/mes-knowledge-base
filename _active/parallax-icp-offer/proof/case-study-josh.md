## S — Story: what Josh was actually up against

Josh Banday is a working TV actor. Series regular on *Upload* (Amazon) as Ivan. Series regular on *Not Dead Yet* (ABC) as Dennis. Second City and Groundlings trained. Black, Filipino, Mexican, Jewish, and queer — an identity stack no other writer in any writers' room is walking in with.

Two years ago, he handed Greg Daniels — yes, that Greg Daniels, *The Office*, *Upload* — a script. Greg read it. Said Josh was a good writer. Gave specific notes.

Josh froze. He didn't know how to follow up. He kept acting. The notes sat in a folder.

By the time we got in a room together, Josh had:

- Two completed pilots
- One animated feature in progress
- No agent moving his writing
- No positioning statement anyone could repeat
- A manager who wasn't pushing the writing side
- A fellowship cycle opening in six weeks with no materials ready
- And a warm door at one of the most powerful showrunners in television that he'd been scared to knock on for 24 months

## L — Lesson: the repositioning Josh couldn't see from inside

Every other fellowship applicant is an "emerging writer." Unknown. Risky. The fellowship director's fear is hiring someone who freezes in a room, can't take notes, or doesn't understand production.

Josh is the exact opposite of risky. He's done 100+ table reads. He knows what a rewrite feels like at 2 AM. His dialogue is already actor-proof because an actor wrote it. His Second City improv training is literally the same cognitive operating system that a writers' room runs on.

He wasn't an emerging *writer*. He was an emerging *showrunner* — and the fellowship was a certification he was applying for as if it were a learning permit.

**The lesson: when the work is already strong, the job isn't to improve the work. The job is to reframe the applicant.** You don't teach Josh to write better. You teach the fellowship director to see what Josh already is.

## A — Actionable: what I actually built

**The Identity Architecture (The "Actor-Auteur")** — Five layers: The Individual (the intersectional identity as creative engine, not bio stat), The Occupation (set-tested pro, not student), The Activity (the Improv Pivot — "Yes And" as a writing mechanism), The Thought Process ("I've played the Best Friend; now I write the Main Character"), and The Posture (he's not asking for a break, he's asking for the chair).

**The Fellowship Targeting Matrix** — four programs, four angles:

| Program | The positioning hook | What the judge sees |
|---------|---------------------|--------------------|
| Disney Entertainment | "Home Team" — already in the family via *Not Dead Yet* | "Vetted. HR knows him. Easy win." |
| NBC Launch | "Improv DNA" — the Tina Fey / Mike Schur pipeline | "Speaks our language. Can Yes-And in a room." |
| WB Discovery Access | "HBO/Streamer Pivot" — layered POV + edge | "Premium voice we don't have." |
| Paramount Writers | "Professional Polish" — work ethic as selling point | "He's an adult. Won't flake." |

Each application positions differently — same writer, different frame per judge.

**The Warm Reactivation Protocol** — the three-step system for unfreezing the Greg Daniels door: find the original notes, implement them (even two years late), send a specific reactivation message that doesn't ask for anything. The exact DM was written for Josh, not left as a template:

> *"Greg — Two years ago you gave me notes on [SCRIPT NAME]. I finally implemented them. Your feedback on [SPECIFIC THING] completely changed how I approached the story. I wanted to say thank you. If you're ever curious how it turned out, I'd be happy to share. Either way, grateful for the time you gave me back then."*

**Three Pitch-Ready Show Concepts** — not show ideas. Full packages, every one of which passes the "Only Josh" test (no other writer in Hollywood could write these):

- ***Potluck*** — a queer mixed-race music teacher becomes the accidental unlicensed therapist of his South Central middle school community. *Abbott Elementary* meets *Somebody Somewhere* meets *Nurse Jackie*, with a season-long ticking-bomb secret underneath the episodic comedy.
- ***Dad's Plus-One*** — an A-list movie star gets divorced and discovers he has no idea how to parent without his ex-wife running the infrastructure. His 9-year-old daughter starts bringing *him* as *her* plus-one. *Entourage* meets *Black-ish* meets *Catastrophe*.
- ***Guest Star*** — an actor who's been a guest on every show for 15 years decides to sabotage his career to force Hollywood's hand, and his sabotage keeps making him more famous. *Atlanta* meets *The Comeback*. The most Josh-specific concept in the package.

Each concept includes the logline, comp title, full pitch narrative, six-episode series engine with per-episode breakdown, and decision-maker bullets a studio exec can use to pitch upward in their own meeting.

The mechanism isn't "I wrote some things for Josh." The mechanism is **reframe the applicant → give him four different angles to walk into four different rooms → weaponize the dormant warm contact he'd frozen on → and ship him creative IP with his fingerprints on every page.**
