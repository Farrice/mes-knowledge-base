# Testimonial Ask — Javier Payano

**Context:** Javier is Farrice's friend, not a customer. He didn't pay for the HVC work. He does, however, have the most emotionally real response to that work of anyone in Farrice's network. The ask has to honor that relationship, offer something back, and make it trivially easy for Javier to say yes — or say no without friction.

**Medium:** DM (iMessage preferred over email — this is a friend). Timing: mid-week, evening, when Javier isn't grading.

---

## THE MESSAGE (Primary Version — iMessage)

> Hey brother —
>
> I'm putting together the public version of what I do — productizing the ICP + content work into something I can sell on a repeat basis. You already know this because I've been looking for how to help you productize what YOU do, so I got a taste of my own medicine.
>
> The HVC work is the strongest case study I have. It's the one I'm most proud of and the one where the before/after is clearest. I'd love to feature it, with you front and center, if you're willing.
>
> Two things I'd ask:
>
> **1.** Could you write me 2-3 sentences on the specific part of the work that helped you most? Not a generic "Farrice is great" — I want the real thing. Something like: "When Farrice showed me [X], I realized [Y], and now I can [Z]." Whatever the actual moment was for you. You already used the phrase "truly magical" when I sent it over — if that still feels true, even just expanding what made it magical would be gold. If it doesn't feel true anymore, tell me and I'll adjust.
>
> **2.** Would you be willing to let me attribute it to you by name + HVC? Not anonymized. Your name and the organization. You'd get a backlink every time someone reads the case study.
>
> In exchange, here's what I'll do for you:
>
> - Feature HVC in the case study in a way that drives traffic to **your** newsletter signup, not just mine. I'll link Sunday Dinners, the Value of the Week, whatever you want elevated.
> - If it works and this case study brings me clients, I'll send 10% of the first $X back to HVC as a tax-deductible donation. (That's not a gimmick — it's how I want this to work: your story helps me eat, and I kick back to the mission.)
> - Full veto on framing. If anything in my draft feels off, tell me and I'll kill it. This case study doesn't exist without you comfortable, full stop.
>
> Draft is in [Google Doc / attached]. Read it, mark it up, tell me what to change.
>
> Or if the timing isn't right — if you're heads-down on the classroom or HVC and don't want one more thing on your plate — say the word and I'll put this on the shelf until summer.
>
> Love you man.
>
> — Farrice

---

## WHY THIS WORKS (the architecture)

**It preserves the friendship first.** The opening isn't "I need a favor." It's "here's what I'm building and you're in the middle of it" — which is the truth. It also explicitly offers an exit ramp at the bottom ("if the timing isn't right, say the word") so Javier never feels cornered.

**The ask is specific.** "Would you write me a testimonial?" is the weakest ask on earth — it outsources the hard part (knowing what to write) to the person who owes you the least. The prompt structure "When Farrice showed me [X], I realized [Y], and now I can [Z]" takes the cognitive load off Javier. He just fills in three blanks.

**It anchors to a real thing Javier already said.** "Truly magical" is Javier's own phrase from the session — so the ask isn't fishing for flattery, it's *recovering* something he already gave. That shifts the psychology from "write something nice about Farrice" to "confirm what you already said."

**It offers reciprocity, not quid pro quo.** Three specific forms of value flow back: traffic, a revenue kickback to HVC, and veto power. Veto power is the secret weapon — it's the thing that tells Javier he's still in charge of how his story gets used.

**It names the asymmetry.** The part that says "your story helps me eat, and I kick back to the mission" is the emotional grounding. Farrice isn't pretending this is equal. He's acknowledging Javier is doing him a favor and making the payback structural.

**The veto is load-bearing.** "Full veto on framing" is the line that makes the ask non-transactional. A friend who has veto power can't be used. A friend who's being asked to rubber-stamp something can.

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do not** ask Javier to "share it" or "post about it" in the initial ask. That conflates two asks (testimonial + amplification) and makes the friction cost double. If he says yes to the testimonial and it goes well, you can ask about sharing later.
- **Do not** send this if Javier is in the middle of a rough week (CPS stuff, classroom crisis, family). You know his rhythms. Time it.
- **Do not** follow up aggressively if he doesn't respond in 48 hours. Send once. Wait 10 days. Send one gentle check-in: *"No pressure on this — just flagging in case my last message got buried."* After that, let it rest.
- **Do not** use the testimonial without explicit written confirmation. A vague "yeah cool" is not permission. You want a thumbs-up emoji on the specific draft, at minimum.
- **Do not** over-edit Javier's words. If his testimonial is awkward, tighten punctuation. Don't rewrite it into marketer-prose. His voice is the point.

## KICKBACK MATH (for Farrice's decision before sending)

The "10% of first $X" language is a placeholder. Before sending, Farrice should commit to a specific number:

- **Conservative:** 10% of first $10K earned from case-study-attributable leads = up to $1,000 back to HVC
- **Aggressive:** 10% of first year's case-study-attributable revenue, no cap

Pick one and put the actual number in the message. "10% of first $10K back to HVC as a tax-deductible donation" is stronger than a hand-wave.
