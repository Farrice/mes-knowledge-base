# Comment Strategy — 30-Day Distributed Growth Playbook

> Jasmin Alic's Distributed Commenting doctrine: 10 targeted comments per day on outlier posts in the buyer zone. Each comment is a mini-post that validates the Parallax thesis in public, on someone else's real estate, in front of the exact audience that should find Farrice's profile.
> Thirty days. Three hundred comments. Approximately 12,000 profile impressions with zero ad spend. That's the plan.

---

## The Doctrine (why this works)

LinkedIn's algorithm treats high-signal comments as mini-content in their own right. A great comment on a viral post gets:
- Served to a slice of the original post's audience
- Displayed on the commenter's profile activity feed (so the commenter's own followers see it)
- Pushed to anyone who "follows the conversation" on that post

A 400-character thoughtful comment on a post with 500 reactions can generate 1,000-3,000 impressions for the commenter. Multiplied across 10 comments per day for 30 days, the distribution math becomes serious.

**The key constraint**: the comment must be a mini-post, not "great insight!" Surface-level comments don't get served. They don't pull. They don't convert profile visits.

Jasmin Alic's frame: *"Every comment is either content or noise. If it's noise, don't post it."*

## The Comment Structure (Mini-Post Template)

A Parallax-quality comment has three layers:

```
[Layer 1 — Recognition of what the creator actually said, in your own words. Proves you read the post, not the headline. 1-2 sentences.]

[Layer 2 — The angle shift. Where you add dimension, gently disagree, extend the idea into a new frame, or name what the post didn't say. This is the signature Parallax move. 1-3 sentences.]

[Layer 3 — A specific, concrete example OR a line-level residue. A sentence the casual scroller can lift. 1 sentence.]
```

**Word count**: 60-150 words per comment. Not too short (no pull), not too long (looks like you're hijacking).

**Voice**: Farrice-to-a-friend-over-coffee. NOT "Great post, Justin! I completely agree..." NOT "I would add..."

## Three Executed Comment Examples

### On a Justin Welsh post about Substack growth

**His hypothetical post**: *"I spent 8 years building an email list. If I could do it over, I'd start on Substack. Here's why..."*

**Your comment**:
```
The interesting thing about the Substack migration for existing writers isn't the tooling — it's that Notes retrains your reflex from "schedule and wait" to "publish and converse." That change shows up in the paid conversion numbers six months later because the reader now *knows your rhythm*, not just your content.

The people who'll benefit most from the move are the ones whose open-rate is already high and whose paid-conversion is stuck. Substack turns the conversion gap into a visibility problem, which is the one kind of problem the platform actually helps solve.
```

**Why this works**:
- Recognizes the post (Substack migration) without repeating it.
- Adds a specific non-obvious frame (Notes retrains reflex) that's useful to the creator AND to the audience.
- Specific language (open-rate high, paid-conversion stuck) self-identifies Avatar 01 — they read this and go *"wait, who wrote this?"*
- No pitch, no "book me," no "DM me." Residue line: *"Substack turns the conversion gap into a visibility problem."*
- 89 words. Perfect range.

### On an April Dunford post about positioning

**Her hypothetical post**: *"Most companies don't have a positioning problem. They have a positioning COMMUNICATION problem. Their positioning is in a doc. It's not in their homepage."*

**Your comment**:
```
The communication gap is the harder problem because the positioning-in-the-doc is usually correct. People spent months on it. They workshopped it. They got consensus.

But the doc-voice and the homepage-voice require two different writers. The doc-writer reasons toward truth. The homepage-writer reasons toward a stranger's five-second scan. Almost nobody in-house can switch registers cleanly. So the doc stays brilliant and the homepage stays vague, and the leadership team keeps wondering why the "positioning" isn't working — when really, the positioning is fine and the register is broken.

Fluent-in-the-meeting-room isn't fluent-to-a-stranger. That gap is its own category.
```

**Why this works**:
- Extends April's frame (doc vs communication) with a new axis (doc-voice vs homepage-voice).
- Specific language ("reasons toward truth" vs "reasons toward a stranger's five-second scan") is lift-able.
- The Parallax Cut buried at the end: *"Fluent-in-the-meeting-room isn't fluent-to-a-stranger."* — this is the kind of line that gets screenshot-quoted.
- Avatar 02 reads this, feels seen, clicks the profile.
- 127 words.

### On a Seth Godin post (Avatar 03 zone)

**His hypothetical post**: *"Nonprofits aren't in the business of solving problems. They're in the business of telling the story of the problem to people who can help solve it."*

**Your comment**:
```
The painful corollary for most mission-driven orgs is that the telling-the-story-of-the-problem is done by the founder, in the founder's voice, from the founder's specific lived experience — and the minute that founder steps away, the articulation layer collapses.

Nine-year-old nonprofits with real outcomes and zero distribution are usually not bad at the work. They're founder-locked in the telling. The work is repeatable. The way it's currently said isn't.

The fix is rarely marketing. It's usually translation — getting the founder's voice into a shape that can be carried by other people, in other rooms, without diluting.
```

**Why this works**:
- Validates Seth's frame + extends it (founder-lock as the hidden constraint).
- Specific language ("founder-locked in the telling") is a category Farrice can own.
- Avatar 03 (mission founder) immediately recognizes themselves. The "founder-locked" phrase is sticky.
- References HVC-adjacent language without naming HVC (no spamming cases).
- 98 words.

## Quality Standards (Jasmin Alic-calibrated)

### Pass
- Mentions a specific thing the post actually said (not just a restatement)
- Adds a new dimension, frame, or specific example
- Has at least one line the reader could lift into their own thinking
- Uses Farrice's native voice (conversational, specific, zero jargon)
- 60-150 words

### Fail (delete before posting)
- "Great post, [name]!" openers
- Generic validation ("so true," "100%," "this")
- Repeats the creator's point with synonyms
- Contains any pitch, any URL, any "DM me"
- Has an em dash count > 2 or a "Here's why..." opener
- Reads like a LinkedIn-guru comment (you know the kind)

### The species test for comments

Ocean Vuong's filter: *"Has this comment been written before on this post, by someone else?"* If yes, don't post it. If you're not sure, read the top 10 existing comments. If yours is in the same register as 3+ of them, rewrite or skip.

## Daily Cadence (30 minutes/day)

**Morning (15 min)**:
- Open LinkedIn, check 10 target creators' latest posts.
- Identify 4-5 outliers.
- Write 4-5 comments.
- Post immediately (no scheduling — real-time = more algorithmic weight).

**Afternoon (10 min)**:
- Check comment replies — respond to any direct DMs or meta-replies.
- Check 5 more target creators for new posts.
- Write 3-4 more comments.

**Evening (5 min)**:
- Glance at comment engagement numbers (are comments getting reactions? Replies?).
- Flag the top 2 comments of the day in the tracking sheet (below).

**Total**: ~10 comments/day, ~30 min/day, ~300 comments/month.

## Tracking Mechanism

Simple Notion database or Google Sheet with columns:

| Date | Creator | Post Topic | Comment (full text) | Reactions on Comment | Replies on Comment | Profile Visits Driven (best guess) | DMs Generated |
|------|---------|------------|----------------------|----------------------|---------------------|--------------------------------------|----------------|

**Profile visit attribution**: LinkedIn's "Who viewed your profile" panel shows the source (e.g., "Viewed your profile after seeing your comment on [post]"). Track weekly.

**Weekly review**:
- Which 3 creators' audiences converted best to profile visits?
- Which 3 comment archetypes (recognition / angle-shift / specific-example-heavy) pulled best?
- What % of profile visitors from comments then engaged on Farrice's own posts?

**30-day compounding metrics**:
- Total comments posted
- Average reactions per comment
- Profile visits attributed to comments
- DMs initiated from comment exposure
- ICP Tell bookings from the comment channel

## Anti-Patterns Explicitly Banned

### Never do these
1. **Comment on every post from the same 5 creators.** Rotate. If you comment on 3 Justin Welsh posts in a week, you look thirsty.
2. **Comment on competitors' posts with pitches.** If April Dunford posts about positioning, don't say "this is exactly what I do — DM me." That's poaching. That kills the goodwill.
3. **Use the comment to recruit.** Don't say "if anyone here is struggling with this, I'm taking 4 clients this month." The comment's job is visibility and residue, not direct sales.
4. **Copy-paste comments.** LinkedIn will spam-flag it. Also, it's obvious.
5. **Comment on your own posts in disguised accounts.** Just don't.

## The 30-Day Arc

**Week 1 (Days 1-7)**: Calibration. Test comment archetypes. Track which style pulls most profile visits. Commit to 70 comments.

**Week 2 (Days 8-14)**: Lean into winning archetypes. Deepen relationships with 3-4 creators whose audiences converted best. Look for chances to comment on their outliers within the first 30 minutes for max distribution. 70 comments.

**Week 3 (Days 15-21)**: Start getting comment replies FROM the creators themselves. When a creator replies to your comment, their whole audience sees it — highest-leverage conversation. Keep cadence. 70 comments.

**Week 4 (Days 22-30)**: Compound. Best comments begin to show up in other people's feeds because their network reacted. Look for patterns: which specific Cut lines recur well in your comments? Those become future post hooks. 90 comments (push harder at the finish).

**Total month**: ~300 comments.

## Exit Signals (what success looks like by Day 30)

**Quantitative**:
- 500+ profile visits attributable to comment channel
- 10-20 DMs initiated from comment exposure
- 3-5 ICP Tell bookings from the comment channel
- 2-3 creators in the target list have engaged back publicly with Farrice (comment replies, quote-shares, follows)

**Qualitative**:
- A reader in Avatar 01 or 02 sends a DM saying some version of "I kept seeing your comments and finally clicked your profile."
- Another creator DMs Farrice asking to collaborate or interview him.
- The Parallax language ("named reader," "articulation layer," "rotating the lens") starts showing up, organically, in other people's posts and comments.

That last one is the real signal. That's when the language has escaped containment.
