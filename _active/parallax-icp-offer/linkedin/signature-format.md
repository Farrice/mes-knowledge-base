# Signature Format — The Parallax Cut

> Farrice's distinctive LinkedIn post structure. Designed so that one scroll through his feed makes a returning reader think *"that's a Parallax post."* The format does three things at once: pattern recognition (so it brands), pull-through (so the reader stays), and residue (so the reader carries a line into their day).

---

## Why "The Parallax Cut"

The word *parallax* describes what happens when you shift one eye and the object you're looking at moves relative to the background. One object, two angles, new depth.

The Cut works the same way. We take a scene the reader already sees — their own writing process, their own stalled pipeline, their own failed post — and we cut it at an angle they haven't tried. Same object. New depth. That's the signature move.

---

## The Skeleton (8 parts, rigid on structure, flexible on voice)

```
[Line 1 — 8 words or fewer. A scene, not a claim. Something the reader recognizes.]

[Line 2 — 8 words or fewer. A small pivot. The angle shifts.]

[Body — 40-180 words. SLAY compressed.
 STORY: one specific moment, named time/place/person.
 LESSON: the misread of the moment most people do.
 ACTIONABLE: the one move the Parallax lens makes different.
 YOU: the reader sees themselves in the pattern.]

[One-line rest. White space is a beat.]

[The Cut — one sentence. The angle. This is the line that gets highlighted and quote-shared.]

[One-line rest.]

[Residue — a question or a scene that lands after the reader closes the app. NOT a CTA dressed as a question. Something that makes them go quiet for a second.]

[Soft mention — Parallax, ICP Tell, or the work. Never in CTA voice. Phrased as an aside.]
```

**Rule on Line 1 + Line 2**: Must BOTH fit inside the mobile ~80-char rehook window. Lara Acosta's 8-word rehook architecture enforces the actual LinkedIn cutoff — if Line 2 hides behind "…see more," the post dies.

**Rule on The Cut**: This is the species-test line. Ocean Vuong's filter — has the species had this sentence yet? If yes, rewrite. The Cut is the reason the post exists.

**Rule on Residue**: Not "What do you think?" Not "Drop a 🙌 if you agree." A residue line is the thing the reader says out loud on their commute an hour later.

---

## When to Use (every post, with controlled variation)

Use the Parallax Cut on **every LinkedIn post**. The consistency is the brand.

**Vary**:
- The tone (observational vs personal vs contrarian)
- The subject of the scene (writer's desk vs consultant's pipeline vs founder's inbox)
- The Cut (different angle every time — never the same reframe reused)

**Never vary**:
- The 8+8 open
- The rest-line white space
- The residue-not-CTA close
- The soft mention (if present)

**Variation rule (Disney principle, per `feedback_writing-excellence-rules.md`)**: No two consecutive posts open with the same grammatical structure. If Post 1 opens with "You publish on Thursday morning." then Post 2 cannot open with "You refresh the dashboard twice." (both are second-person scene statements). Mix: scene statement → question → overheard line → object-in-room → action verb.

### Example C — Topic: A contrarian frame (polymath angle)

```
Everyone tells you to niche down.

Nobody tells you why it stopped working.

"Niche down" was invented for an internet where the scarce thing was information. In 2015, if you owned "productivity for designers," you owned a lane because there were twelve people writing about it.

In 2026, there are twelve thousand.
```

## Rules for Drafting Inside This Format

1. **Write Line 2 first.** The pivot is the hardest. If Line 2 doesn't land, the post doesn't land. Line 1 gets written afterwards to set up Line 2.
2. **The Cut is one sentence.** If you need two, you haven't found the angle yet.
3. **The Residue is not the Cut.** They do different jobs. The Cut is the quote-share line. The Residue is the line the reader says out loud an hour later.
4. **Soft mention is optional.** If the post is pure TAM / awareness, no mention. If there's an organic thread to the offer, aside-it. Never "book a call." Never "DM me."
5. **Species-test The Cut.** Has this sentence been said before? If yes, rewrite. If you can't tell, read it out loud — if it sounds like something from any consultant's feed, it's not a Cut, it's a summary.
6. **Voice check Line 2 + Residue.** These are the two lines most likely to fail the "would Farrice say this to a friend?" test. Extra scrutiny here.

## Signature Cues (so the format brands)

Small, consistent tics that, across 30 posts, become recognizable:

- **Two isolated lines at the top, both short, both arrest-scenes** (the 8+8 open)
- **White space as punctuation** — one-line paragraphs used deliberately
- **No em dashes in headers** (per `feedback_ai-writing-tells.md`)
- **Parenthetical soft mention at the end** when the offer is mentioned at all — the parenthesis signals "this is an aside, not a pitch"
- **No "Here's what / why / how" openers** (banned)
- **First word of the post is often a pronoun or a verb, rarely "The"** — biases toward scene energy over abstract energy

---

## Anti-Patterns Explicitly Banned Inside This Format

- "Three lessons from…" listicle close
- "Comment [keyword] below" engagement bait
- "Tag someone who needs to see this"
- "If this resonates, hit follow"
- Any first-person milestone announcement ("I hit $10K MRR in…")
- Any screenshot of a DM as proof
- "As a [consultant/writer/founder]…" self-labeling opener
- Hashtag stacks (max 2 hashtags, only if they add discoverability)

---

## How to Tell the Format Is Working

**Week 1 signal**: A reader comments "this format hits different." (They don't have words for it yet, but the shape lands.)
**Week 2 signal**: A reader quote-shares with a screenshot highlighting The Cut line.
**Week 4 signal**: Another creator copies the structure. Don't worry — the residue is the moat, not the skeleton.
