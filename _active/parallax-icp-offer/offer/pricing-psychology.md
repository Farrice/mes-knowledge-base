# Pricing Psychology — Parallax ICP Intelligence™

**Premise (Priestley):** Value exists in the buyer's situation, not the seller's credentials. Price to the stakes of the problem, not the inputs of the solution. A $1,497 ICP document is either ludicrously expensive or ludicrously cheap depending on what it costs the buyer to keep not having one.

**Second premise (Monk.Ai):** The price is a trust signal. Price too low and you become the $97 template they've already rejected. Price too high without stakes framing and you become the consultant they can't justify. The narrow band where the price MATCHES the felt pain is where conversion happens.

---

## ANCHORING STRATEGY

### The question that controls the anchor

Every sales conversation opens with the same question (from case-study-hvc.md):

> *"If a stranger landed on your homepage for five seconds, what would they say you do?"*

This is not a pitch. It's a diagnostic. It establishes that the SELLER's position is not "here's what I sell" but "here's what's wrong." The anchor isn't a number — it's the gap between who the prospect IS and who strangers THINK they are. Money becomes the smaller conversation.

### Which number do you say first?

**Never say $1,497 first.** Say $4,997 first.

**Rationale:** Anchoring research (Ariely, Kahneman) — the first number frames the range. If the prospect hears $1,497 first, they evaluate $2,997 and $4,997 as upsells. If they hear $4,997 first, they evaluate $2,997 and $1,497 as down-steps — which is psychologically MORE expensive per unit, not less, because the $1,497 tier reads as "the budget version" rather than "the starter version."

**The phrase to use on a call:**

> "Parallax runs three ways. The deepest is Launch — we co-write pillar pieces, draft the launch announcement, build the testimonial infrastructure. That's $4,997. The middle tier is Architecture — everything except the co-writing. $2,997. The entry tier is Crystal — just the ICP document and language map. $1,497. Which one are you?"

This phrasing does four things simultaneously:
1. Anchors high ($4,997 spoken first)
2. Positions the middle as the complete-but-not-done-with-you tier (the likely winner)
3. Positions $1,497 as the ENTRY, not the cheap option
4. Ends with a question that forces self-selection, not a pitch

## PAYMENT TERMS

| Tier | Payment options |
|---|---|
| **Crystal — $1,497** | Full only. No split. ("The engagement is 7 days — splitting doesn't make sense.") |
| **Architecture — $2,997** | Full OR 50/50 split (50% kickoff, 50% at Day 7 handoff of Crystal) |
| **Launch — $4,997** | Full OR 50/25/25 (50% kickoff, 25% at Day 14 Architecture handoff, 25% at Day 21 launch day) |

**Strategic note:** Offering splits on $2,997 and $4,997 but NOT on $1,497 is intentional. It signals that $1,497 is already inside the "full pay" band for the Crystal buyer. Splitting a $1,497 ticket implies the buyer might not be able to afford it — and Avatar 01 is specifically wounded by being treated as broke. They were about to pay $1,200 for a course. $1,497 is in-range by $297.

**Deposit-to-start:** All tiers require payment (or first installment) to trigger the intake form. No "we'll send the invoice after we start." Kickoff doesn't schedule until deposit clears.

## OBJECTION HANDLING — TOP 5 PER AVATAR

### Avatar 01 — Stalled Substack Operator

**Objection 1: "$1,497 is a lot for me right now."**

*Do not discount. Do not offer a payment plan. Do this:*

> "Let me ask it differently. You have [X] subscribers. If 30 of them converted to paid at $8/mo, that's $240/mo — and you'd have $2,880 in year one. How many paid subscribers do you have today? ... And what's your conversion rate? ... OK — if we move that from 0.8% to 1.8% over the next 90 days, the Crystal pays for itself by month four. The question isn't whether you can afford it. It's whether the last 90 days have proven you can afford NOT to."

This is Priestley's Tension Triangle live:
- Current reality: [their actual paid conversion]
- Desired reality: 2x conversion
- Obstacle: the reader is not named in the writing

The tension is mathematical. Let them sit in it.

**Objection 2: "How is this different from the Substack courses I've seen?"**

> "A course teaches you to do the thing. We do the thing. You don't end up with a workbook. You end up with a document that names three real humans you could DM this afternoon as your ideal subscriber, their language, and your next five posts."

**Objection 3: "I tried positioning work before and it didn't move the needle."**

> "What did they hand you?" [Listen.] "Right. That's the problem. Most positioning is inward — 'get clear on what YOU offer.' We do outward — 'get specific about who's watching.' Those are not the same document. One is a brand guideline. The other is an operating manual for your next 30 posts."

**Objection 4: "I'm not sure I have enough subscribers yet."**

> "The ICP Tell call will tell us. If the honest answer is 'come back at 1,500 subs,' you'll hear it free. Don't decide now. Book the call."

(This is the clean disqualification path. It builds trust by refusing the wrong sale.)

**Objection 5: "What if the document doesn't help?"**

> "If the Crystal doesn't name your reader in a way you can feel, we rework it. One rework is built in. If the second pass still doesn't land, full refund, keep the document. That's in writing."

### Avatar 02 — Signal Bleeder

**Objection 1: "I could hire a LinkedIn ghostwriter for $3K/mo."**

> "You could. The question is what you'd be hiring them to write TO. If the ICP is wrong, 4 posts a month with wrong positioning is worse than 1 post a month with right positioning. Architecture ($2,997 one-time) gives the ghostwriter — or you — the document they need to actually hit. If you want us to ghostwrite after, that's a separate conversation. We only take 3 retainers at a time."

**Objection 2: "I've already written my ICP. It says 'B2B SaaS founders $1M-$10M ARR.'"**

> "Right. Would one of them recognize themselves in that sentence? Or would they read it and say 'I guess that's me?' — which is the same as saying 'I'm not sure.' The Crystal names three specific humans. Not a revenue band."

**Objection 3: "I'm considering hiring a fractional CMO for $10K/mo instead."**

> "Different service. A fractional CMO executes. Parallax Architecture gives you (or the fractional CMO) the document both of you would be working from. It's upstream of that hire — not a replacement."

**Objection 4: "Why should I pay $2,997 for a document?"**

> "Because every week you post content written to a blurry buyer is $3,000 of ghostwriting or $10K of fractional CMO executing on a broken foundation. The document stops that leak. It's not a document. It's the thing that makes the next six months of content actually work."

**Objection 5: "What if my ICP evolves?"**

> "It will. The Crystal you get at Day 14 is Version 1. Most clients recalibrate at Month 6. We run the recalibration at a reduced rate for Architecture alums — $1,497 for the re-crystallization, not the full $2,997. Your investment compounds."

### Avatar 03 — Mission Founder With A Thin Room

**Objection 1: "We're a non-profit. $4,997 is hard to justify."**

> "Let's put it in the ratio your board thinks in. One rejected mid-size grant is $50K-$250K. The Launch package is 2-10% of one grant. We wrote a case study of the Human Values Collective engagement — Javier is a Chicago music teacher running a nonprofit Javier would tell you the ROI is not comparable. Would you like me to send you the 9-document system he got, as a sample?"

(Offering the HVC deliverable as a sample is the close. It collapses the sale.)

**Objection 2: "We don't have co-founder alignment on who this is for. Maybe later."**

> "That's the exact moment we help. Parallax Launch includes a working session where co-founders end up in the same document — not each in their own idea. Disagreement is not a reason to wait. It's the reason to start."

**Objection 3: "Won't this take time away from our programs?"**

> "21 days. Four working sessions totaling about 5 hours of your calendar. Less time than you've spent this year in grant meetings where the 'target audience' question took 35 minutes."

**Objection 4: "We already have a brand consultant."**

> "Brand consultants build your outward identity. We build the reader's identity. Those are complementary. Your brand consultant's work will land harder after the Crystal because you'll be telling them what reader the brand is FOR."

**Objection 5: "What if we can't replicate what you built for Javier?"**

> "That's what Tier 3 is designed against. We don't hand you a document — we sit in four sessions with you, co-write three pillar pieces, and run the launch together. The whole point of the Launch tier is that you don't leave the engagement with a manual you can't operate."

## WHEN TO OFFER A TRIPWIRE VS STAND FIRM

**Stand firm:**
- If the objection is a price objection from Avatar 01 who HASN'T yet done the math. Run the math with them. Let them see the payback window.
- If the objection is from Avatar 02 comparing to a $3K ghostwriter. Reframe — it's not a comparable.
- If the objection is from Avatar 03 on mission/budget grounds. Show them HVC. Let the artifact close.
- If the objection is a timing objection. Offer the ICP Tell anyway — free diagnosis is the softer commitment.

**Offer the ICP Tell (free) as the fallback:**
- Any prospect not ready to buy today. The free 20-min call is always available. It converts 30-40% to Tier 1 within 14 days.

**Never offer:**
- A $97 template. This is the thing Avatar 01 is fleeing — do not re-create it.
- A $497 "DIY course." Parallax is not a course. Offering one breaks the category.
- Discount on the headline tier. A $1,200 Crystal is a worse signal than a $1,497 Crystal. Discounting breaks the trust gradient.

**Once per quarter, offer a cohort:**
- 4 Crystal buyers in a group Slack, 30-day cohort, same 7-day delivery but with shared kickoff and shared handoff sessions. Price: $1,197 each (reduced from $1,497). Cap: 4. This is the ONE downsell lever — and it's a cohort structure, not a discount. The discount is paid by the reduced 1:1 Farrice time.

## STAKES FRAMING LANGUAGE (Priestley Tension Triangle, by avatar)

Each script follows: Current Reality → Desired Reality → Obstacle → the Crystal as the obstacle-remover.

### Avatar 01 stakes script

> "Right now your publication has [X] subscribers and a paid conversion rate of under 2%. You know the cap — it's probably $500-$800/month at best. That's runway for a side project. You want your work to have a shape — a paid base large enough that you can take an afternoon off without the dashboard haunting you. The obstacle is one document you haven't written. Not cadence. Not topic. Not SEO. The document that names three real humans you could DM tomorrow. That's what we build. That's what moves the conversion rate. And that's why this engagement is 7 days, not 6 weeks."

### Avatar 02 stakes script

> "Right now your practice books 1-2 calls a week, all warm intros. That's a hobby with tax implications. You want a pipeline that pulls — where the post you wrote Thursday brings a ready-to-buy call by Tuesday. The obstacle: every post is written to 'anyone with this pain,' and nobody sees themselves. The Crystal is the specific reader, named. Architecture adds the revenue ladder so the reader has somewhere to go once they recognize themselves. Six months of wrong-ICP content is $60K of your time. $2,997 is less than a week of that."

### Avatar 03 stakes script

> "Right now you're the ICP. Every person who finds the work finds it through you being in the room. You want to hand the pitch to someone else — to a funder, a co-founder, a host in a new city. The obstacle: the map of who this is for lives in your head and has never been externalized into a form another human can operate. Launch is that externalization. It's the grant application that doesn't get 'audience unclear' feedback. It's the Houston launch where 30 people show up instead of 3. It's the co-founder meeting that ends with alignment instead of an argument. $4,997 is 2% of one grant you'd otherwise lose."

## THE FINAL PRICING PRINCIPLE

If a prospect is evaluating whether to buy Parallax, they are NOT evaluating against $0. They are already spending:
- Avatar 01: $30-$100/month on Substack courses, Notion templates, YouTube fee-for-advice
- Avatar 02: $500-$5,000/month on tools, coaches, content hires, community memberships
- Avatar 03: $2,000-$30,000/year on brand consultants, grant writers, comms help

**The frame is never "Parallax vs. free."** The frame is "Parallax vs. the $[X] you're already bleeding into adjacent fixes that don't address the reader layer."

Every objection handling script should end with a number comparison to what the prospect is already paying for less. That's the close.
