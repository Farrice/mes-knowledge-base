## §2E — Belief-Change Architecture (David McRaney)

McRaney's deep canvassing principle: **People don't change beliefs because of new information. They change beliefs because they encounter someone who has already made the change and didn't lose what they were afraid of losing.**

The 5 beliefs that block the V6 buyer from action — and the shifts that move them:

### Blocking Belief 1 — *"I should be able to handle this myself."*

- **Why it blocks**: Hiring a coach signals (to themselves) that they've failed at self-reliance. Self-reliance is their identity master frame.
- **The shift**: From self-reliance-as-virtue → self-reliance-as-blind-spot. The high-performers who DID get this right hired help YEARS before the body forced them to. Mike hired Cooz 9 years ago. Carron at 3. Corey at 4. They aren't outliers — they're the survivors. The ones still trying to handle it themselves are the ones whose bodies are forcing the decision now.
- **Where it gets shifted**: Triage Audit Phase 2 (diagnose), "Mike was tempted to handle this himself" backstory, blog post on self-reliance as a foundation that's now collapsing.

### Blocking Belief 2 — *"I'll start Monday / next quarter / when this thing passes."*

- **Why it blocks**: Postponement feels like agency. As long as the start date is on the calendar, they aren't failing — they're "preparing."
- **The shift**: The next quarter has not slowed down in 3 years. Postponement isn't agency — it's the symptom. The body doesn't wait for the calendar. The 47th Monday they didn't start is the data point.
- **Where it gets shifted**: LinkedIn About paragraph 4 ("the next quarter hasn't slowed down in three years"), Lane 2 confessional posts on postponement, Triage Audit listening phase (count how many times they say "I'll get to it").

### Blocking Belief 3 — *"12-week programs work — I just need more discipline."*

- **Why it blocks**: It's a way to frame the failure as their fault instead of the program's fault. Self-blame protects the system that failed them.
- **The shift**: The reason it didn't hold isn't your discipline. It's that 12 weeks doesn't rebuild a foundation that's been compromised for 3–7 years. The program was solving the wrong layer. You don't have a discipline problem — you have a category-mismatch problem.
- **Where it gets shifted**: Brave Choice methodology lead-magnet, blog post on category mismatch, Perception Reframe 6 (Work Frame — see §14).

### Blocking Belief 4 — *"I'm too old for this to work."*

- **Why it blocks**: It absolves them from trying. If the window has closed, the failure isn't theirs.
- **The shift**: Mike at year 9 is at his strongest in our work together. The body has remodel-capacity at 50 that the culture doesn't acknowledge. The window is real but you're inside it, not outside it. The 50-year-old who builds the foundation now is the 65-year-old who's still in their kids' graduation photos, fully present.
- **Where it gets shifted**: Lane 3 case-study posts on Mike (60+) and Buzz (65+), YouTube long-form on remodel-capacity, ESA Tier D pitch.

### Blocking Belief 5 — *"$1,500/mo for a coach is excessive."*

- **Why it blocks**: The number feels disproportionate to "a personal trainer." Their internal pricing comparison is to commodity training, not to specialist long-arc work.
- **The shift**: $1,500/mo over 12 months = $18K. The post-collapse rebuild costs $50K+ and 18 months. The math isn't $18K vs. $0. It's $18K now vs. $50K+ later. And the slow-collapse cost in lost presence at home, lost capacity at work, and lost years of the body holding for your kids — that's the real number, and it's bigger than either.
- **Where it gets shifted**: Triage Audit Phase 3 pricing reveal, Perception Reframe 3 (Cost Calculation — see §14), email nurture day 5.

## §2F — Audience State Map (Chris Cimorelli)

Cimorelli's audience-state framework: prospects move through 5 states from *unaware* to *most-aware*. The right content meets them at the state they're in — content that assumes a more-aware state pushes them away.

| State | % of TAM | What they're aware of | What content moves them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | 30% | Don't yet know their body is breaking. Performing energy, ignoring the data. | Recognition content (Lane 2 confessional posts that name the unnamed experience). Goal: move them to Problem-Aware. |
| **Problem-Aware** | 40% | Know the body is breaking. Don't have a name for the category of solution. Trying biohacking / 12-week programs / Equinox. | Mechanism content (Lane 1 Body-First Foundation posts that name what's actually happening at the diagnostic level). Goal: move them to Solution-Aware. |
| **Solution-Aware** | 20% | Know they need long-arc coaching. Don't yet know Cooz exists or why he's the right one. | Proof content (Lane 3 case studies — Mike 9 yrs, Carron #2 national, Corey Hollywood) + methodology content (Brave Choice). Goal: move them to Coach-Aware. |
| **Coach-Aware** | 7% | Know Cooz exists. Considering whether he's the right fit. | Triage Audit-direct content (objection-handling posts, the 4-pathway diagnostic explainer, the founding-rate locking mechanic). Goal: move them to Most-Aware. |
| **Most-Aware** | 3% | Ready to book. Just need a CTA. | Triage Audit booking page · email day 14 final call · DM warm-up. Goal: convert. |

**The single biggest mistake the V6 brand can make**: producing only Most-Aware content (booking CTAs, "DM me to talk") when 90% of the TAM is in earlier states. The Lane 1 + Lane 2 mix in V6-LINKEDIN-CONTENT is calibrated for the actual state distribution: 55% Lane 1 (Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware), 30% Lane 2 (Unaware → Problem-Aware), 15% Lane 3 (Solution-Aware → Coach-Aware).

## §2G — The Identity Cooz Sells (Michael Bernoff)

Bernoff's identity engineering principle: **People don't buy products. They buy the identity the product confirms or creates.**

The V6 brand sells **"The Resurrected"** — the identity, NOT the body change.

What "The Resurrected" means:
- The person who refused to settle for the diminished version
- The one who looked at the cliff at 47 and chose the rebuild over the slow accumulation of lost presence
- The one whose body holds for the next 30 years because they chose to make it hold
- The one their family stops adjusting to — because they came back
- The one who, at 60, looks at their kids' graduation photos and is in them, fully

Body change is the byproduct. The identity is the product.

This is why the brand never sells aesthetic transformation. Aesthetic transformation is the marketing motion of competitors. The V6 brand sells the identity-restoration that everyone else accidentally promises and almost no one actually delivers.

When Cooz writes a piece of content, runs a Triage Audit, or makes a brand decision — the test is: *"Does this confirm or create the Resurrected identity for the buyer?"* If yes, ship. If no, regenerate.

# §7 — THE 8 BANNED AI STRUCTURAL MOVES

These are the 8 structural moves that signal AI-generated content even when individual sentences are clean. Catch all 8 before publishing.

1. *"It's not X. It's Y."* reveals (e.g., "It's not a fitness program. It's an infrastructure rebuild.") — banned as **closers / reveals**. 2. **Twin-sentence aphoristic endings** (e.g., "Most stay average. Few become exceptional.")
3. **Triple-beat anaphora** ("You don't need X. You don't need Y. You need Z.")
4. **Italicized mid-paragraph aphorisms**
5. *"Here is the part nobody tells you..."* framing
6. **Mic-drop + deflation endings** (huge claim → "Anyway, that's just my take.")
7. **Cross-piece rhythm repetition** (two posts in same week with the same closing cadence)
8. **Triple-list audience naming** ("entrepreneurs, founders, professionals")

If a draft hits any of these, regenerate. Don't patch.

# §9 — THE TRIAGE AUDIT (30/30/30 ARCHITECTURE)

The Triage Audit is the universal entry to the 4-pathway pricing system. 90 minutes total, broken into three 30-minute phases.

**The full call script lives in `V6-TRIAGE-AUDIT-SCRIPT.md`.** This section locks the architecture; the script doc operationalizes it.

## Phase 1 — LISTEN (Minutes 0–30)

**Goal**: Understand what's actually breaking. Use the prospect's verbatim language. No diagnosis yet.

**Opening line** (verbatim):

> *"Before I tell you anything about how I work, I want to hear what's actually happening in your body, your schedule, and your life right now. Take 5–10 minutes — what made you book this call?"*

Then shut up and listen for 25 minutes. Take notes on:
- Verbatim phrases (these become the close-back language)
- Specific moments referenced
- The gap between external and internal
- Which long-arc anchor profile their pattern most resembles
- Whether they've named the philosophical problem yet

**Listening rules**: DO NOT interrupt. DO NOT fill silence. DO NOT diagnose mid-listen.

**At minute 28–30, transition**: *"Okay. Let me read back what I'm hearing. Stop me if I get any of it wrong."*

## Phase 2 — DIAGNOSE (Minutes 30–60)

**Goal**: Read back the prospect's verbatim language. Diagnose the pattern. Run the 4-pathway decision tree internally. Don't reveal the recommendation yet.

The McRaney/Cimorelli reflective-listening read-back is non-negotiable. Read back in their words, not yours. Two outcomes:
1. They say *"Yeah. Exactly."* → trust opens. Close becomes inevitable.
2. They correct one detail → even better. Now they're collaborating on the diagnosis.

Then run 3–4 diagnostic patterns anchored in V6 testimonial library (e.g., "Mike — 9-year client of mine, district manager 33 years at his company — was running into the same wall in year 2 with me. We backed off training volume by 30% and he started actually growing.")

**The diagnose phase establishes specialist authority.** Empathy was Phase 1. Authority is Phase 2.

## Phase 3 — RECOMMEND ONE (Minutes 60–90)

**Goal**: Single pathway recommendation. Logistics. Founding-rate locking OR 7-day decision window.

**The recommendation move**:
> *"Three or four pathways exist depending on the situation. Yours is Pathway [A/B/C/D] — and here's why."*

Then explain ONLY that pathway. Anchor in a comparable long-arc client.

**The close-resolution** (Donald Miller GP8 — conditional):
> *"If what we just walked through matches what you actually need, the right move is to lock the founding rate now. If you need 7 days to think about it — that's fine, but the founding rate is gone if it's filled by the time you decide. I'd rather you commit at the right tier than the wrong one."*

## Anti-pattern: pitching the menu

NEVER show the prospect all four pathways during the call. The diagnostic IS the sales mechanism. Showing the menu signals "vendor, not specialist."

The only time to mention multiple pathways: during the 30-min listen phase, IF the prospect explicitly asks "what are the options" — then deflect:

> *"Three or four pathways depending on what fits — but we'll figure out which one in the diagnosis. Let me hear more about what's actually breaking first."*

```
IS THE PROSPECT IN LA / SFV?
├── YES → CAN THEY COMMIT TO 4X/WEEK IN-PERSON?
│         ├── YES → IS THEIR LIFE ARCHITECTURE COMPLEX (founder/VP/executive)?
│         │         ├── YES → CAN THEY INVEST $5K+/MO + 12-MONTH COMMITMENT?
│         │         │         ├── YES → PATHWAY D (ESA)
│         │         │         └── NO  → PATHWAY A (In-Person Burbank)
│         │         └── NO  → PATHWAY A (In-Person Burbank)
│         └── NO  → PATHWAY B (Remote Resurrection)
└── NO → IS THE BUDGET $1,500-2,000/MO ONGOING OR $3,500-4,500 PACKAGE?
          ├── ONGOING $1,500-2,000/MO → PATHWAY B (Remote Resurrection)
          ├── PACKAGE $3,500-4,500 (12-WK SPRINT) → PATHWAY C (The Ignition)
```
