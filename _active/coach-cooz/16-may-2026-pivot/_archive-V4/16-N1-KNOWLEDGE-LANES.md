### Subtopics

4. Specific lift / scale / measurement progressions

6. Pattern recognition: "Lessons from coaching X clients in 10 years"

### Format types for Lane 3

**The case study post (the gold standard)**
- 6-image carousel: before / after / mid-point / lift progression / measurement progression / pull-quote
- Caption: 1,000 words narrative breakdown of the 12-week arc
- CTA: link to full landing page case study (in first comment, not body)

**The case study fragment**
- Single-image post: ONE specific moment from a client's transformation
- 200-300 word narrative around that moment
- Lower-friction than full carousel — for posting between full case studies

**Industry case study commentary** (substitute when own cases aren't ready)
- Reference public PN/MyBodyTutor case
- Cooz's analysis of why the program worked for THAT client
- "Here's how I'd have approached the same case differently"

### The Temptation Test (run before posting any borderline content)

Three questions to ask yourself before publishing:

1. **Which of my 3 lanes does this serve?**
   - Lane 1, 2, or 3 → ship
   - "None" → don't publish, route to holding chamber
   - "Sort of fits Lane X" → reframe to clearly live in the lane, OR send to 7-day cool-down

2. **Does the first 50 words contain 3+ topic anchors from the lane?**
   - Yes → ship
   - No → rewrite the open

3. **Am I posting this because it serves the audience, or because I had a thought I wanted to share?**
   - Serves audience → ship
   - "Wanted to share" → route to holding chamber

## THE 7-DAY COOL-DOWN

For posts that "sort of fit a lane":

1. Save the draft to a `pending-7-day-review/` folder
2. Don't post for 7 days
3. After 7 days, re-read it
4. If it still feels lane-aligned → Farrice voice-checks → publish
5. If it now reads as forced or off-lane → routes to holding chamber

The 7-day delay alone kills 50%+ of borderline posts. The thought that felt urgent on Day 1 reveals itself as "off-lane impulse" by Day 7 most of the time.
