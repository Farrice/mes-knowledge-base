## SECTION 0 — PRE-CALL (24-HOUR-BEFORE CHECKLIST)

The call starts 24 hours before you click the Zoom link. Prep is the difference between a 7/10 and a 9/10 audit.

### What Cooz reviews — 24 hours before

1. **The intake form.** Highlight: the verbatim phrases describing what's breaking (gold for Phase 2 read-back) · geography (gates Pathway A) · delivery preference if stated · budget signals · specific event/date pressure (wedding, photo shoot, surgery, athletic comp).

2. **Their LinkedIn profile.** Job title + company (executive tier signal — director/VP/founder/senior creative) · years in role (long-arc capability signal — 18 years at one company commits more readily than 18-month jumpers) · public-facing or behind-the-scenes · anything they've publicly written (their actual voice register — match it).

3. **Any prior DM history.** Scan once. Note phrases worth re-surfacing.

4. **V6-CANON §10 proof spine — pick the long-arc anchor analogue.** Decide pre-call which one you'll deploy in Phase 2-3:
   - **Mike (M.G.) — 9 yr · executive · district-manager-tier** → executives, directors, VPs, senior leaders
   - **Corey (C.J.) — 4 yr · Hollywood writer · travels for shows** → traveling creatives, writers, directors
   - **Carron (C.W.) — 3 yr · #2 national powerlifter · CA State squat record** → competitive athletes, performance prospects
   - **Sam — 2.5+ yr · Hollywood production · go-go boots 225 deadlift** → women in production/creative; story-led prospects
   - **Mari — 3 mo · veteran · 132→148 lbs · fastest transformation** → defined event date or post-failed-program sprint prospects
   - **Robin — 8 yr · Brave Choice naming** → identity-pre-crisis prospects where methodology > workout
   - **Buzz — 1 yr · 65 · DP for "Sheldon" · post-Covid fall-off** → 60+ prospects with "am I too old" looming

5. **V6-CANON §14 reframes — pick 2-3 you'll likely need:**
   - Price mentioned → RF3 (Cost Calculation) + RF11 (Pricing Tier Psychology)
   - Remote/non-LA → RF9 (Delivery Frame)
   - Tried programs before → RF6 (Work Frame) + RF10 (Proof Frame)
   - 50+ → RF5 (Risk Frame — "the riskier choice is the do-nothing")
   - Status/exec comparisons → RF4 (Cody, Carron, Corey)

### Mental frame — the 5 minutes before the call

Empathy-then-authority dual lock from V6-CANON §15 Element 3. **Empathy first** — *"I've been there."* You were Cody McBroom's client. You know. **Authority second** — Mike at 9 years. Carron #2 national. Corey on "The Night Agent." Sam pulling 225 in go-go boots. Don't lead with the receipts; let them surface in Phase 2.

**The order is non-negotiable.** Empathy alone reads as therapist. Authority alone reads as biohacker. Both, in that order, every time.

Quiet centering line: *"This is one human, in identity-pre-crisis, who did the brave thing of booking. My job is to listen first."*

### Logistics — 30 minutes before

Zoom link tested · 90-min block protected (DND, calendar blocked) · notes doc open · water (no coffee in last 60 min — no caffeine cortisol mid-listen) · pen + paper backup (typing breaks listening trance).

## SECTION 1 — PHASE 1: LISTEN (MINUTES 0–30)

**Goal**: Understand what's actually breaking. Use the prospect's verbatim language. No diagnosis yet. Empathy half of the dual lock.

### The opener — verbatim (3-5 minutes to set frame)

The opener does three things: establishes the listening contract, signals you're not pitching, hands them the floor with a clear time signal.

#### Opener Variant 1 — DEFAULT

> *"Good to meet you. Before I tell you anything about how I work, I want to hear what's actually happening in your body, your schedule, and your life right now. I've blocked 90 minutes for us — and the way I run this call is, the first 30 minutes are entirely yours. I'm not going to pitch you anything in this first half. I'm going to listen, take notes, and then in the second half I'll read back what I'm hearing and we'll figure out what the right move looks like for you specifically. So — take 5 to 10 minutes. What made you book this call?"*

#### Opener Variant 2 — COLD-LINKEDIN-DM ORIGIN

> *"Good to meet you in person — or, well, on Zoom. I appreciate you taking the leap to book a call after a few DMs. Before I tell you anything about how I work, I want to hear what's actually happening in your body, your schedule, and your life right now. The first 30 minutes are yours — I'm not pitching anything in this first half. I'll listen, take notes, and in the second half we'll figure out what the right move looks like. Walk me through it. What made you finally book the call?"*

The "finally" is intentional — honors that they hesitated. Cold DMs come off a 3-week internal debate.

#### Opener Variant 3 — REFERRAL ORIGIN

> *"Mike said you might call — glad you did. He told me a little but I asked him not to give me too much, because I'd rather hear it from you. The way I run this call: 90 minutes total, first 30 are entirely yours. I'm not pitching anything in the first half — I'm listening. Then in the second half I read back what I'm hearing and we figure out what fits. So tell me. What's actually going on right now?"*

The referral framing confirms the relationship without weaponizing it.

#### Opener Variant 4 — LEAD-MAGNET-WARMED ORIGIN

> *"Good to meet you. I saw you came in through the Brave Choice methodology — which means you've already been thinking about the 3 questions for a couple weeks. Before I tell you anything about how I work, I want to hear where those questions landed for you, and what's actually happening in your body and your life right now. First 30 minutes are yours — I'm not pitching, I'm listening. So — what made you book the call this week, and not last month?"*

The "this week, not last month" question is a Cimorelli audience-state diagnostic — the answer tells you which belief shifted (V6-CANON §2E).

### Then shut up

After the opener, stop talking. Don't fill silence. Don't elaborate. Hand them the floor.

If they hesitate or say *"I don't really know where to start"* — give them ONE prompt, then go silent again:

> *"Start anywhere. What's the moment in the last week or two where you noticed something was off?"*

### The 10 listening prompts (deploy as needed)

You won't use all of these — probably 3-4. They exist for the moments when the prospect runs out of momentum.

1. *"What's the moment in the last week where you noticed something was off?"* (Default re-prompt when they freeze.)
2. *"Walk me through a typical Tuesday — wake up to bedtime."* (Surfaces the 5am/11am/4pm/9pm/2am rituals from V6-CANON §2B without them realizing.)
3. *"What does your body feel like at 4 PM right now?"*
4. *"What have you tried in the last 5 years?"* (Surfaces alternatives-they've-burned-through. Critical for Phase 2 read-back.)
5. *"What worked for a minute and then didn't?"* (Often surfaces the 12-week-program failure that primes Belief 3 shift.)
6. *"What does your spouse / partner say when you talk about this?"* (If they have one. Surfaces relational stakes — V6-CANON §2C.)
7. *"What does your kid see when they look at you?"* (Highest-voltage emotional question. Use sparingly. Don't deploy if they haven't mentioned family yet.)
8. *"What's the version of you that you remember being — and what's different now?"* (Surfaces body-memory.)
9. *"What's been going on at work in the last two years?"* (For executives — surfaces the load breaking the body.)
10. **THE McRANEY MOVE** — minute 18-22 if they've stayed surface: *"What are you not saying out loud yet?"*

### The McRaney "what are you not saying out loud yet" move — when and how to deploy

David McRaney's deep canvassing move. Surfaces the philosophical problem (V6-CANON §15 Element 2) when the prospect has only named external + internal.

**When to deploy**: They've talked 15-20 minutes but stayed surface (body, workout, sleep) · you can feel they're holding something back · they've mentioned family or identity but moved past it.

**When NOT to deploy**: They're already volunteering deep material on their own · it's the first 10 minutes (haven't earned the trust to land it) · they seem nervous or guarded.

**How to deploy** — soft, slow, no agenda:

> *"Let me ask you a different question. What are you not saying out loud yet?"*

Then SHUT UP. Don't elaborate. Don't soften. Don't add "if anything." The silence after this question is where the philosophical problem surfaces. You may sit in 15-30 seconds of silence. Let it.

What you'll often hear: *"I'm afraid the diminished version is permanent."* / *"My wife knows. We don't talk about it."* / *"I'm scared I left it too late."* / *"I look in the mirror and I don't recognize the guy looking back."*

Whatever they say next is GOLD. Write it verbatim. This is what you read back first in Phase 2.

### What to take notes on (the 5 categories)

Your notes doc has 5 columns. Fill in real-time:

1. **Verbatim phrases** — word-for-word, in quotes. Not paraphrased. *"I look 50, not 45."* / *"My back caught when I lifted my kid."* / *"I'm tired of saying I'll get to it."*
2. **Specific moments** — concrete events. *"At my daughter's recital I couldn't lift her without my back catching."* / *"I bought a Whoop in February and haven't opened the app since March."*
3. **External/internal gap** — what they say externally vs. what's hitting internally. Note the gap explicitly.
4. **Long-arc anchor analogue** — which §10 client profile. May shift mid-listen — start thinking "Mike," realize at minute 20 it's "Corey."
5. **Philosophical problem surfacing** — checkmark when they cross from external/internal into identity-level. If not by minute 20, deploy the McRaney move.

### Listening rules — the four non-negotiables

1. **DO NOT INTERRUPT.** Even if they say something inaccurate. Even if they tangent for 12 minutes. The interrupt destroys the listening trance.
2. **DO NOT FILL SILENCE.** When they pause, you pause. Count 5 Mississippi. Most prospects pick the thread back up at 4 — and what they say next is usually more honest. Silence is where truth lives.
3. **DO NOT DIAGNOSE MID-LISTEN.** Even when you know — and by minute 8 you'll often know — DO NOT name it yet. Naming in Phase 1 collapses the architecture. They feel handled, not heard.
4. **DO NOT TAKE THE PRICE BAIT.** They'll ask "what does this cost" or "what are the options" at minute 12. Verbatim deflect:

   > *"Good question — and the honest answer is, the price depends on what we figure out fits. There are three or four pathways depending on situation. We'll figure out which one in the diagnosis. Let me hear more about what's actually breaking first."*

   Return them to the listen: *"You were saying about the 4 PM fog — keep going there."*

### Redirecting off-track tangents — verbatim

Prospects sometimes spend 10 minutes on their job/company/kids' schedules — anywhere except the body (because the body is harder to talk about). Redirect gently:

- *"That's helpful context — bring me back to the body. What's happening there right now?"*
- *"Hold that thought — I'll come back to the work piece in Phase 2 when we figure out the rebuild. For now, I want to keep the focus on what your body is doing."*
- *"You mentioned [their specific phrase — e.g., 'sleeping like garbage'] — tell me more about that."*
- *"Let me ask it a different way: when's the last time you woke up and felt actually rested?"*

The redirect always honors the tangent (*"that's helpful"*) and returns them to the body.

### Reflective hand-back protocol — bookmarking verbatims

You'll read back 3-4 verbatim phrases in Phase 2. Bookmark in real-time without breaking the trance:

- Pen + paper for the verbatim list (separate from typed notes if you're typing)
- Star or underline the phrases that hit hardest as they say them
- Eye-contact stays on them — glance down only when they pause to gather thoughts
- At minute 25, do a 30-second scan of starred verbatims. Pick the 3-4 doing the most work.

The 3-4 should hit different layers: one **external** (body/symptom) · one **internal** (emotion/recognition) · one **stakes** (what they're afraid of losing) · one **philosophical** if they got there.

### Phase 1 → Phase 2 transition — verbatim

> *"Okay. Let me read back what I'm hearing. Stop me if I get any of it wrong."*

Pause 2 beats. Begin the read-back. No preamble. The compression IS the move.

## SECTION 2 — PHASE 2: DIAGNOSE (MINUTES 30–60)

**Goal**: Read back their verbatim language. Diagnose the pattern. Run the 4-pathway tree internally. Don't reveal the recommendation — that's Phase 3. Authority half of the dual lock.

### The read-back move — verbatim template

> *"Here's what I'm hearing. [Verbatim phrase #1 — external/body]. [Verbatim phrase #2 — internal/emotional]. [Verbatim phrase #3 — stakes or philosophical]. The body that built [their specific life — career / role / family / show / company] is starting to break, and you've watched it happen for [time period — '3 years,' 'since Covid,' 'since I had kids,' 'since the company sold']. You've tried [whatever they named — '12-week programs,' 'Equinox,' 'biohacking,' 'an app,' 'nothing']. None of it held."*

Then PAUSE. Don't add more.

#### Worked example — executive prospect

> *"Here's what I'm hearing. You said the 4 PM fog has been a thing for about a year and a half — you read the same email four times. You said your wife asked you last month if you're okay, and you didn't have a real answer. You said you're afraid that if you don't do something now, the diminished version is permanent. The body that built your career — 14 years at the same firm, the directorship, the team you run — is starting to break, and you've watched it happen since the pandemic. You've tried Tonal, you tried a Whoop, you tried doing what your trainer in college had you doing 20 years ago. None of it held."*

#### Worked example — Hollywood writer prospect

> *"Here's what I'm hearing. You said you can write for 14 hours but you can't deadlift 185 anymore. You said you stopped taking the trail behind the house because you're tired of being out of breath three minutes in. You said your fiancée asked you when you stopped sleeping — and you didn't realize until she asked. The body that built the writing career — the late nights, the show runs, the season-long sprints — is starting to break. You've tried apps, you tried a coach in Brooklyn for six months when you were on staff, you tried just lifting at the hotel gym on the road. None of it held."*

### Two outcomes after read-back — both are wins

**Outcome A — *"Yeah. Exactly."*** Trust opens. Don't celebrate. Don't accelerate. Nod, take a beat:

> *"Okay. Three or four things are usually happening when this pattern shows up. Let me walk you through what I see in your case."*

**Outcome B — They correct one detail.** *"That's mostly right, but it's the 11 AM crash, not the 4 PM."* This is BETTER — they're collaborating now. Trust is HIGHER. Don't argue. Don't try to incorporate too much. Acknowledge cleanly:

> *"Got it — so the 11 AM crash, not the 4 PM. That actually makes the picture clearer. Three or four things are usually happening when this pattern shows up. Let me walk you through what I see in your case."*

Re-litigating the correction makes them feel managed.

### The 4-pathway internal decision tree — running it live

While delivering diagnostic patterns, part of your brain runs the tree internally. By end of Phase 2, you've decided. Don't reveal until Phase 3.

```
FORK 1 — GEOGRAPHY: LA / SFV / Burbank / Glendale / Studio City?
├── YES → Pathway A on the table. Go to Fork 2.
└── NO  → Pathway A out. Go to Fork 4.

FORK 2 — IN-PERSON COMMITMENT: 4x/week in-room?
├── YES → Pathway A or D. Go to Fork 3.
└── NO  → Pathway B (remote daily Voxer). Go to Fork 4 to confirm budget.

FORK 3 — LIFE ARCHITECTURE COMPLEXITY: Founder / VP / public-facing creative
        with $5M+ company OR senior-leadership at scale?
├── YES → Pathway D (ESA) on the table. Go to Fork 5.
└── NO  → Pathway A.

FORK 4 — BUDGET TIER (non-LA OR LA-but-can't-do-4x):
├── ONGOING $1,500-2,000/MO → Pathway B
├── PACKAGE $3,500-4,500 (12-WK SPRINT) → Pathway C
└── PREMIUM $5K+/MO → Pathway D

FORK 5 — ESA QUALIFICATION: $5K+/mo + 12-month commit?
├── YES → Pathway D
└── NO  → Pathway A
```

You're not asking these questions explicitly during read-back — you're listening for answers in what they've already said. If a fork is unclear, ask diagnostically (not pricing-flavored): *"Logistically, what does your week look like? Are you in one place 4 days a week, or are you in three cities a month?"* / *"Tell me about the scope of what you're running right now — team size, your role, what your day-to-day actually looks like."*

NEVER ask budget directly. The intake form usually reveals it. If not, defer to Phase 3 — let the recommendation set the price, not the price set the recommendation.

### The 6 diagnostic patterns — verbatim, anchored in V6-CANON §10

You won't deliver all 6. You'll deliver 3-4 — the ones that match the read-back.

#### Pattern 1 — Sleep architecture broken (anchored in Mike year 2)

**When**: They mentioned hours but not sleep. Wakes at 2am. Coffee before water. Reading the same email four times. The 4 PM fog.

> *"Your sleep architecture is broken — you're getting hours, not sleep. There's a difference. Hours is the time you're horizontal. Sleep is the architecture underneath — the deep-sleep waves, the REM cycles, the temperature drop, the cortisol curve. You can be horizontal for 7 hours and have 3 hours of actual sleep. That's why the 4 PM fog is real and the 3 PM coffee isn't fixing it — caffeine doesn't replace sleep, it postpones the bill.*
>
> *Mike — 9-year client of mine, district manager 33 years at his company before they let him go this past January — was running into exactly this wall in year 2 with me. We backed off training volume by 30%, fixed the temperature in his bedroom, moved his last meal earlier, and he started actually growing. The protocol I run for sleep architecture in the first 90 days is specific — and it's the difference between performing energy and having actual energy."*

#### Pattern 2 — Under-recovered, not under-trained (Mike year 2 + Carron protocol)

**When**: They're trying to fix the body by adding more training. They're getting MORE tired, not less. Confused why discipline isn't working.

> *"You're under-recovered, not under-trained. More gym time would make this worse, not better. The body is signaling 'I can't recover from what I'm already doing' and you're answering with more volume. That's the wrong direction.*
>
> *Mike at year 2 had the exact same thing happening. We backed off training volume by 30% and he started actually growing — the strength gains came AFTER we cut, not when we added. Carron — #2-ranked national powerlifter — was already training enough when I came in. What changed his trajectory wasn't more lifting. It was getting his nutrition protocols aligned with his recovery capacity. The first thing I'd want to do with you is figure out what we cut, not what we add."*

#### Pattern 3 — Performing energy vs. real energy (caffeine-and-cortisol calibration)

**When**: 3 cups of coffee before noon. 4 PM crash. Faking energy. The internal logic is "I just need more discipline with my caffeine."

> *"You've been performing energy for so long you've stopped being able to tell the difference between real energy and caffeine-and-cortisol energy. That's not a discipline problem. That's a calibration problem — the gauges are reading wrong because they've been spiking and crashing for 3 years.*
>
> *The first thing I'd do is recalibrate the gauges. Cut caffeine after noon, real food before 11am, eight ounces of water before the first coffee. In about 12 days you'll start being able to feel the difference between the kind of energy that lasts and the kind that's borrowed against tomorrow. Until you can feel that difference, every protocol I'd give you would be guesswork. Mike's first 90 days with me weren't training — they were getting the calibration right so we could actually see what was happening."*

#### Pattern 4 — Discipline-vs-category-mismatch (V6-CANON §2E Belief 3)

**When**: They keep blaming themselves for failed programs. They use "discipline" multiple times. *"I just need to commit harder."* 3+ failed 12-week programs.

> *"You don't have a discipline problem. You have a category-mismatch problem. The reason the 12-week programs didn't hold isn't your willpower — it's that 12 weeks doesn't rebuild a foundation that's been compromised for 3 to 7 years. The program was solving the wrong layer.*
>
> *You have plenty of discipline. You ran a [their company / their team / their show / their schedule] for [N] years. That's not a person without discipline — that's a person whose discipline was correctly applied to a different category. The body needs a different category of work. Not more discipline. Different category.*
>
> *That's why my work is structured the way it is. Real change in 90 days, and then the relationship keeps producing for years because the foundation we build keeps holding. Mike's at year 9. Carron's at year 3. Robin — that's my dad, 8 years — named the methodology Brave Choice, because the work was changing how he made decisions in the gym AND in life. None of these people had a discipline upgrade. They had a category change."*

#### Pattern 5 — Self-reliance-as-blind-spot (V6-CANON §2E Belief 1)

**When**: *"I should be able to figure this out myself."* Self-reliance-as-virtue is the master frame. Deepest block.

> *"Can I name something I'm noticing? You said something like 'I should be able to figure this out myself.' I want to push back on that gently — because I hear it from almost everyone in your category, and it's the thing that costs the most years.*
>
> *The high-performers who got this right hired help YEARS before the body forced them to. Mike hired me 9 years ago. Carron at 3. Corey at 4. Robin — my dad, who named the Brave Choice methodology — has been with me 8 years. They didn't 'fail at self-reliance.' They recognized that self-reliance had built everything ELSE in their lives, and it had become a blind spot for the body. The body needs a specialist partner. The same way your accountant isn't 'you failing at finance' — they're the right partner for that category.*
>
> *The self-reliance frame served you for the career. It's the wrong frame for the body. The ones still trying to handle it themselves at 47 are usually the ones whose bodies are forcing the decision under worse terms a few years from now."*

Quasi-therapeutic without being therapy. Deploy carefully. Pause after delivery. Let them sit.

#### Pattern 6 — The body is signaling, not failing (Buzz post-Covid + V6-CANON §2E Belief 4)

**When**: Body symptoms described with shame — *"my body is failing me"* / *"I'm getting old."*

> *"I want to reframe one thing you said. You used the word 'failing' about your body. I'd push back on that — your body isn't failing. It's signaling. There's a difference.*
>
> *Failing means there's no path forward. Signaling means the body is sending you data — and the data is 'this is the load. We need to recalibrate.' That's a productive signal, not a death sentence.*
>
> *Buzz was 65 when he came to me — he's a director of photography, came out of Covid having fallen out of shape, was carrying heavy camera equipment and his back was telling him every day. He told me 'I'm 65, I used to be quite active, I needed someone to understand my age and health issues and build on that.' Inside a year he was stronger, more functional, sleeping better. He didn't have less age — he had a partner who treated the signal as data instead of as a verdict.*
>
> *The body has remodel-capacity at [their age — 47, 50, 55] that the culture doesn't acknowledge. The window is real, but you're inside it, not outside it. The 50-year-old who builds the foundation now is the 65-year-old who's still in their kids' graduation photos, fully present."*

### The Brave Choice 3-question diagnostic — deployed live

After 2-3 patterns delivered and the prospect is in recognition state — usually around minute 50-55. The transition:

> *"I want to do something with you for 4 minutes. There are 3 questions I ask my clients to ask themselves at the moments nobody is watching — when the gym is far and the comfortable choice is close. My dad named these the Brave Choice methodology. He's been with me 8 years. The 3 questions are part of how the work holds. I want you to answer them — out loud, just for me — and then we'll figure out the right pathway based on the answers.*
>
> *Question one. Will the version of you 5 years from now be glad you did this — or sad you didn't?"*

SHUT UP. Let them answer. Take notes.

> *"Question two. Is the comfortable choice the one that protects your future — or the one that protects your excuses?"*

Pause. Let them answer.

> *"Question three. Who are you being right now — the [man/woman] you built the life for, or the [man/woman] holding it all up?"*

Pause. Let them answer.

**What the answers tell you**: Quick + clear → identity-pre-action, recommendation will land, same-day close likely. Hesitation or emotion on Q3 → philosophical problem now surfaced; Pathway D may be more on-table than initially thought. Pushback or redirect → not yet at conviction; this is a 7-day-decision close, not same-day. **Data, not failure.**

After the third answer, transition straight into Phase 3:

> *"Okay. Based on what we've walked through and what you just said — here's what I think the right pathway looks like for you. Let me walk you through it."*

### Phase 2 → Phase 3 transition — verbatim

> *"Based on this, here's what I think the right pathway looks like for you. Let me walk you through it."*

No preamble. The compression is the move.

## SECTION 3 — PHASE 3: RECOMMEND ONE (MINUTES 60–90)

**Goal**: Single pathway recommendation. Logistics. Founding-rate locking OR 7-day decision window. The close was won in Phase 1-2. Phase 3 collects the commitment.

### The recommendation move — verbatim opener

> *"Three or four pathways exist depending on the situation. Yours is Pathway [A / B / C / D] — and here's why."*

Then explain ONLY that pathway. Anchor in a long-arc client analogue.

**Alternative phrasings** (if "three or four pathways" feels stilted in the moment):
- *"Based on what we walked through, the right move for you is Pathway [X]. Here's why I'm recommending that one specifically."*
- *"There's only one pathway that fits this — Pathway [X]. Let me tell you what it looks like."* (Slightly more authoritative — use when the prospect has been wavering and you want to compress the decision.)

### Per-pathway recommendation — verbatim scripts

#### Pathway A — In-Person Burbank ($1,275 founding / $1,500 standing) — anchor: Mike OR Carron

> *"Pathway A is in-person Burbank — 4x a week in the room with me, $1,275 a month at the founding rate. That locks for 12 months before it standardizes to $1,500. This is the pathway for you because [specific reason from diagnostic — geography fits + you've already shown you'll commit to a real schedule + the work needs the in-room daily presence to hold].*
>
> *Week 1: We do a baseline session — strength, mobility, sleep + nutrition intake. I write your first 4 weeks of programming based on what we see, not based on a template. We're not doing a fitness assessment in the cardio-stress-test sense. We're doing a diagnostic. By Friday of week 1 you have your first 4-week protocol and you understand the 'why' behind every piece of it.*
>
> *Month 3: The 4 PM fog is gone. You're sleeping through the night. Strength numbers are starting to climb but more importantly, you're tracking energy at 3 PM and 9 PM and the curve is flatter. You start being able to feel the difference between performing energy and real energy.*
>
> *Year 1: Mike — 9-year client now, district manager 33 years at his company — at year 1 wasn't where he thought he'd be on the scale. He was where he needed to be on the foundation. He kept showing up because the foundation kept holding. The scale shifted in year 2. The foundation is what kept him 8 more years. That's the trajectory you're on.*
>
> *Founding rate is $1,275 a month, locked for 12 months. Standing rate is $1,500. I have [N] founding spots open across all pathways and they're filling. Pathway A specifically is currently at [N of 4] capacity."*

#### Pathway B — Remote Resurrection ($1,500 founding / $2,000 standing) — anchor: Corey

> *"Pathway B is Remote Resurrection — daily Voxer presence, $1,500 a month at the founding rate. Locks for 12 months before it standardizes to $2,000. This is the pathway for you because [specific reason — not in LA OR travel too much for in-person OR schedule won't sustain 4x/week in-room].*
>

You send me how you slept, what you ate, what training you did, what's coming up tomorrow. I respond inside the day. Most coaches who go remote do weekly check-ins. I do daily presence. That's the whole reason this pathway exists at this price point.*
>

## SECTION 5 — COMMON MOVES TO AVOID (ANTI-PATTERN GUARD)

The 7 most common ways the audit gets degraded. Each sounds reasonable in the moment and kills the close.

1. **Pitching the menu of 4 pathways.** Showing the menu signals "vendor, not specialist." The diagnosis IS the sales mechanism.
2. **Diagnosing during Phase 1 listen.** Even when you know — and by minute 8 you'll often know — DO NOT name the diagnosis in Phase 1. Phase 1 is empathy. Collapsing phases collapses the architecture.
3. **Discounting Pathway A.** If they can't afford A, tier-down to B or C. Never discount A. Discounting breaks the tier system and signals the rate was inflated.
4. **Skipping the read-back transition.** *"Okay. Let me read back what I'm hearing. Stop me if I get any of it wrong."* This earns trust. Skipping it costs ~30% of closes.
5. **High-pressure follow-up between days 0 and 7.** No DMs. No "just thinking about you." The 7-day commit call IS the follow-up. Anything else reads desperate and breaks premium positioning.
6. **Filling silence.** Count 5 Mississippi. Silence is where truth lives. Filling at 2 Mississippi makes the audit shallow.
7. **Fixing the prospect's correction during read-back.** Acknowledge cleanly. Move on. Re-litigating makes them feel managed.

**Bonus — catches Cooz specifically**: Talking about your own backstory in Phase 1 to relate. You'll be tempted to bridge with *"Oh man, I had something similar at year 4 with my own coach."* DO NOT. Phase 1 is theirs, not yours. Your stories belong in Phase 2 (diagnostic patterns) and Phase 3 (anchor analogue).
