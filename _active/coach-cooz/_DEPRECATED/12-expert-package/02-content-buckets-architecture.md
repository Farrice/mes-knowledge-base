# BUCKET 1 — THE DEPLETION DIARIES
## Confession Voice | 30-35% Rotation

### A. The Emotional Job

**What this bucket does for the reader**: It gives him permission to name what has been happening to him for 18 months without admitting it's him. The reader is watching another man (Cooz) describe the exact moment he lived on Tuesday night and pretended didn't happen. The transformation offered mid-scroll is a single exhale — *"oh, someone named it, I'm not crazy, I'm not alone."* No sale. No fix. Just the first honest mirror he's looked into in a year.

**Specific interior pain touched**: The silent accumulation. The 2 AM Slack check driven by terror. The 30 seconds in the driveway. The performance of being fine. The co-founder silence. The wife who already gave up asking. The "successful but struggling" paradox named verbatim in Reddit pain language (WS1.5 quotes 6, 11, 12, 13, 17, 19).

**Why this bucket is the plurality (30-35%)**: WS1.5 and WS1.6 both confirm the buyer is *starving for permission to name their decline*. Sheedy's Easter confession (788 likes, 11,940 plays on a direct competitor) outperformed every fitness tip he posted. Confession is the dominant winning format in 2026 for this audience. This is the core.

### B. The Structural Formula

**Framework stack**: Kallaway's **Dopamine Ladder** (Stimulation → Captivation → Anticipation → Validation → Affection → Revelation) sequenced through Nicolas Cole's **atomic essay spine** (one idea, earned through story), with Luke Iha's **Vicious Hook** principles governing the first line and the **Sheedy 6-Step Formula** (WS1.6 §3.2) governing the whole body.

```
[LINE 1 — VICIOUS HOOK, I-led, Germanic, 15 words or fewer]
  → Kallaway Stimulation + Luke Iha Principle 1 (Relevant) + Principle 7 (Caveman)

[LINES 2-4 — SPECIFIC EXPANSION in I]
  → Nicolas Cole "specificity collapses into universality"
  → Name 2-3 concrete sensory details (time, place, object, person)
  → Kallaway Captivation — opens the loop without leaking the answer

[LINES 5-7 — INTERNAL TURNING POINT in I]
  → Sheedy Step 3: "I finally admitted that…" / "The moment happened when…"
  → Name the INTERNAL state, not the external scene
  → Kallaway Anticipation — ratchets tension toward the insight

[LINE 8 (optional) — PUBLIC DECLARATION in I]
  → "This is what I do now." / "I don't negotiate with this anymore."
  → Luke Iha Principle 4 (Stakes): Something has been decided, consequences named

[LINES 9-10 — THE YOU-PIVOT, maximum 2 sentences]
  → First time "you" appears in the post
  → Universal condition statement, NEVER scene narration
  → Kallaway Validation + Revelation

[LINE 11 — CLOSE]
  → A question, a brand tag, a period, or silence
  → Nicolas Cole Terminal Word Power: land on a noun or strong verb
  → Never a CTA stacked on the emotional beat
```

**Hard rules for this bucket**:
- "You"/"your" appears **7 times maximum** in the whole post, all in the final 2 sentences
- First 180 words are pure I
- No framework name-drops (Total Radius Ownership, 8 Tenets, etc. — saved for Bucket 5)
- No sales pitch
- No generalized "men like us" preaching

### C. Hook Template Library

Each hook is tagged to the expert framework it uses. All are I-led per the validated voice rule.

1. **(Luke Iha Vicious — Stakes + Specificity)** "For [time period] I had a habit nobody knew about. [Specific time of day], [specific location], [specific behavior]."
2. **(Kallaway Dopamine Ladder — Stimulation via confession)** "I [verb]'d my way through [specific time period] before I admitted [specific internal truth]."
3. **(Luke Iha Vicious — Consequence First)** "The most expensive thing I owned for [time period] wasn't [expected answer]. It was [unexpected answer that opens loop]."
4. **(Nicolas Cole Sentence Craft — Terminal Word Power + Atomic Opening)** "I was [age] when I realized my body was the only thing I wasn't negotiating with."
5. **(Luke Iha Insight Vector — Paradoxical Question as I-statement)** "I spent [time period] trying to [optimize/fix X] before I understood [X] was never the problem."
6. **(Kallaway Curiosity Loop — Specific Detail + Implied Question)** "For about [time], I stopped [specific small ritual]. Not [obvious reason]. [Unexpected reason]."
7. **(Luke Iha Vicious — Anti-Ad Disguise)** "There's a thirty-second window in my life nobody knew existed. I'm going to tell you about it."
8. **(Sheedy Template opening — I-declarative triple)** "For years I made [X] my king. [Second object]. [Third object]. All of it built on the belief that [false premise]."
9. **(Kallaway Stimulation — Bold Claim in I)** "I've never told anyone this. I'm going to tell you now because I think I'm not the only one."
10. **(Luke Iha Vicious — Stakes + Identity)** "I stopped recognizing the man in the mirror somewhere between [Event A] and [Event B]. Nobody noticed."
11. **(Nicolas Cole — Confession Opening + Specific Noun)** "I have a photo on my phone from [specific date/event]. I've never shown it to my wife."
12. **(Luke Iha Insight Vector — Reverse Causation)** "I thought [common belief] was what was killing me. It was actually [reversed]. Here's what I learned the wrong way."

### D. Topic Inventory (12 specific topics — record-ready)

Each topic is written so Cooz could open Voice Memos right now and riff on it for 15 minutes without a prep doc.

1. **"The thirty seconds in the driveway I couldn't tell anyone about"** — The 6:47 PM override, the hand on the door, the inability to walk in. Already validated by the flywheel demo.
2. **"Why I stopped tracking my HRV the day I fired my assistant"** — The moment the data became too accusatory to look at. The Whoop strap in the drawer.
3. **"The three things my wife said the week before I finally listened"** — Specific sentences. Not paraphrased. Exact words in exact order.
4. **"The meal I faked eating for 47 days in a row"** — The protein shake he didn't drink. The almond he counted as breakfast. The lie in the food log.
5. **"The 2 AM Stripe refresh that wasn't about revenue"** — What he was actually checking. What his body was telling him. What he didn't want to feel.
6. **"The conversation I kept rescheduling with my oldest friend"** — The text thread that went 14 months with a "let's catch up soon" loop. What he was avoiding. What broke it.
7. **"The year I wore the same three shirts because I couldn't open the closet"** — Decision fatigue as a physical symptom. The narrowed wardrobe as depletion ritual.
8. **"What I found in my own bloodwork that I pretended wasn't there"** — The panel he didn't show his doctor. The number he circled with his thumbnail. The six weeks he did nothing about it.
9. **"The first time my daughter asked me a question I was too tired to answer"** — Not a big event. A small one. Her face. His silence. The shame that arrived six hours later.
10. **"The gym I built while my own body was crumbling"** — The irony he couldn't name. The clients who got his best hours. The bathroom mirror at 9 PM.
11. **"Why I started taking the long way home"** — The 12 extra minutes he stole before the front door. What he listened to in those minutes. What he wasn't ready to face.
12. **"The morning I woke up and couldn't remember what I'd eaten the day before"** — The food amnesia of a depleted man. The Google-doc calendar that ran him. The body that had stopped filing invoices.

### E. Rotation Slot

**Days**: Monday + Wednesday + Friday in a normal week. Monday is the anchor (post-weekend resonance: the man most likely to have had a hard Sunday night is the man most likely to open his phone Monday morning). Friday is the relief beat (let him exhale into the weekend). Wednesday is the mid-week compound.

**Frequency**: 3 of the 7 LinkedIn posts per week (most weeks). In a heavy news week where Cultural Response takes 2 slots, drop to 2 Depletion posts.

**Why those days**: Sheedy's top engagement posts in the WS1.6 recovery cluster on mid-week and weekend-adjacent days. Depletion Diaries is the "open when the world is quiet" bucket.

### F. Example Opening (validated voice, ready to ship)

> I had a habit for two years that nobody in my life knew about.
>
> Every night, around 6:47 PM, I would pull into my driveway, turn the engine off, and sit there for exactly thirty seconds before I could make myself walk through the front door of my own house.
>
> Not because of a call. Not because of an email. Because I had given twelve hours to people who paid me, and I had nothing left for the people who didn't. I'd been patient all day. I'd been strategic. I'd held three rooms together. And I knew — before I even touched the handle — that my wife was going to ask me a simple question about dinner, and I was going to answer like a man with nothing in the tank. Because I was.
>
> I've never told anyone about those thirty seconds. I'm telling you now because I don't think I was the only one sitting out there.

# BUCKET 2 — THE RESURRECTION CHRONICLES
## Proof Voice | 15% Rotation

### A. The Emotional Job

**What this bucket does for the reader**: It gives him the thing Bucket 1 refuses to give him — evidence that dormancy is reversible. After enough Depletion Diaries posts have named his condition, the reader eventually needs to see a man who came back. Not a Before/After shredded-abs photo — a **narrative resurrection**. Someone who was where he is and isn't there anymore. The bucket answers the question *"can I actually come back, or is this who I am now?"*

**Specific interior pain touched**: Hopelessness disguised as realism. The quiet conviction that "the man I used to be" is gone forever. The fear that the business got the best version and there's nothing left to give. The shame of needing proof before trying. Marriage-survival-as-KPI (WS1.6 §2.3 comment gold).

**Why only 15% (not 30%)**: Proof posts are the highest-trust content but they cannot lead the rotation — the reader has to first accept the problem (Buckets 1, 3, 4) before a transformation story lands. Overused, Proof feels like marketing. Right-used, it feels like vindication. Sheedy's transformation posts were the *least engaged* of his top 15 (WS1.6 Sheedy table rows 9, 11, 15: 157, 107, 52 likes — all well below his Confession content). Proof is a closer, not an opener.

### B. The Structural Formula

**Framework stack**: Nicolas Cole's **Niche Positioning "Named Person"** rule (a real human being, never a persona) + Luke Iha's **Proof Ladder** (claim → proof → intensifier) + Kallaway's **Trust Density** (specific, dated, sensory proof markers) + the I-led voice rule.

```
[LINE 1 — NAMED PERSON + STATE CHANGE, I-led]
  → "Brian came to me [specific time ago], [specific starting state]."
  → Nicolas Cole: the client is a real person with a real name
  → Luke Iha Principle 5 (Specificity as Spice)

[LINES 2-4 — THE STARTING STATE, concrete]
  → What was true about Brian's life when he arrived
  → Named sensory details (the meeting where he cried / the bloodwork he was avoiding / the sentence his wife had stopped saying)
  → Kallaway Trust Density

[LINES 5-7 — THE SPECIFIC INTERVENTION in I]
  → What I actually had him do (named, simple, small)
  → Not the full protocol — one specific move
  → The move should be counterintuitive vs. optimization culture

[LINES 8-10 — THE STATE CHANGE, narrative not metric]
  → What changed, described as narrative not data
  → Sheedy template: "he is now in the best shape of his life"
  → Include ONE sensory proof marker (weight, waist, photo, specific sentence his wife said)

[LINE 11 — UNIVERSAL PRINCIPLE in I or you-pivot]
  → Extract the one transferable truth
  → "What Brian taught me was [X]." OR "If you're where Brian was, [one sentence]."

[LINE 12 — CLOSE]
  → A question OR a resurrection tag OR silence
```

**Hard rules for this bucket**:
- Use a **real name** — "Brian" or "Maverick" or "David" — not "one of my clients"
- Lead with the **narrative state**, not the metric
- **One sensory proof marker** per post, max (Sheedy's level, not a DEXA report)
- Permission to name the client is assumed — otherwise use a first-name pseudonym and note it once in bio
- Never a sales pitch; the proof IS the pitch

### C. Hook Template Library

1. **(Luke Iha Proof Ladder — Claim + Specificity)** "[Name] came to me [time period] ago. [Specific starting condition in one sentence]."
2. **(Nicolas Cole Named Person — Real Human Opening)** "I want to tell you about a guy named [Name]. Not a client avatar. A real man with a real name and a real wife."
3. **(Kallaway Curiosity Loop — Before/After Hook)** "[Name] is sitting across from me right now. Six months ago he couldn't [specific thing]. Today he [specific thing]. Here's what happened in between."
4. **(Luke Iha Vicious — Consequence First, Proof Variant)** "[Name] was [specific number months] away from [specific collapse]. He doesn't know this. I know this. Here's what we did."
5. **(Kallaway 3-Step Contrast Hook — Proof Version)** "Most fitness coaches would have told [Name] to [obvious answer]. I told him to [counterintuitive answer]. [Specific time later], [outcome]."
6. **(Nicolas Cole — Confession in Proof Voice)** "I almost turned [Name] away. I'm glad I didn't. Here's what he taught me about [specific insight]."
7. **(Sheedy Template — I-declarative about client)** "For years, [Name] made [X] his king. [Second thing]. [Third thing]. Then one Tuesday he called me."
8. **(Luke Iha Stakes + Identity)** "[Name]'s wife was three weeks from a conversation she didn't want to have. Neither of them knew it. I know it because he told me after."
9. **(Kallaway Dopamine Ladder — Revelation opening)** "I've been coaching [Name] for [specific time]. He just texted me the sentence I've been waiting to read."
10. **(Luke Iha Proof Mechanism — Proof as Hook)** "Here's a photo of a bloodwork panel. It belongs to a founder I work with. Eight weeks apart. I'll tell you the one thing we changed."
11. **(Nicolas Cole — Specific Date Opening)** "On [specific date], [Name] sent me a voice memo. I listened to it in my truck. I'm going to tell you what he said."

### D. Topic Inventory (10 specific topics)

1. **"The client who canceled our first session because he couldn't get out of his car"** — The parking lot moment. The phone call. What I told him.
2. **"Brian's 4:30 AM text that changed how I onboard every client"** — The message. Why it scared me. What I now do differently on day one.
3. **"The founder who hired me to fix his sleep and left his wife instead"** — The unintended consequence. The one honest conversation. The divorce that saved him.
4. **"The client I fired after two weeks (and why)"** — The man I couldn't help. The thing I wouldn't compromise on. What it taught me about readiness.
5. **"My own 16-week before-and-after (and the photo I still won't post)"** — My own resurrection. The frame I won't show. Why.
6. **"The client who resisted the protein-before-noon rule for six weeks"** — The specific battle. What broke his resistance. What happened in week seven.
7. **"The man who called me from a hotel bathroom at 11 PM"** — The rock bottom. The call that should've been to his wife but was to me. What I told him to do first.
8. **"Why I made a client cancel his Whoop subscription in our third session"** — The gadget as avoidance. The real data. The argument.
9. **"The guy who lost 22 pounds and still told me it wasn't working"** — The moment metrics stopped mattering. The sentence his daughter said that landed instead.
10. **"The father who brought his son to the gym for the first time in eight years"** — The grown son. The first workout. The conversation on the drive home.

### E. Rotation Slot

**Days**: Tuesday + occasionally Saturday. Tuesday because it's the "get to work" day — the reader already accepted the problem Monday morning (Depletion Diaries) and now he needs proof before he goes into his Tuesday sales calls. Saturday is optional — weekend posts earn higher save/share rates for proof content (Sheedy data).

**Frequency**: 1 of 7 LinkedIn posts per week on average. Some weeks zero if no client has given permission for a specific story.

**Why low frequency**: Proof content only lands after enough confession content has been paid in. A rotation with too much proof feels like marketing. Low-frequency, high-specificity.

### F. Example Opening (validated voice, ready to ship)

> I want to tell you about a guy named Brian.
>
> Not a client avatar. Not "one of the founders I work with." A real forty-three-year-old man with a construction business, a wife named Lauren, two kids, and a Whoop strap he hadn't charged in eleven weeks when he called me.
>
> Brian came to me last November with a specific complaint. He couldn't get through a normal Tuesday without hiding in his truck for twenty minutes between site visits. Not to take calls. To close his eyes. He'd been doing it since August and he'd stopped telling Lauren about it because she'd already given up asking.
>
> I didn't put him on a protocol. I didn't hand him a nutrition plan. In our first session I asked him one question: *when was the last time you ate something you actually tasted?* He couldn't answer. That was the whole diagnosis.

# BUCKET 3 — THE CONTRARIAN TRUTH
## Reframe Voice | 20% Rotation

### A. The Emotional Job

**What this bucket does for the reader**: It gives him the grown-up version of the anger he's been suppressing. He already suspects the cold-plunge optimization industry is bullshit. He already half-hates the "4 AM alarm" productivity gurus. He already knows mindset coaches are telling him to meditate while his body is bleeding out. But he hasn't had language for it, and he doesn't want to be a cynic, so he keeps performing the rituals. This bucket gives him permission to name the scam and walk away — without becoming the kind of guy who calls everything a scam.

**Specific interior pain touched**: The cognitive dissonance of doing all the "right" things and feeling worse. The $4,000 he spent on supplements and breathwork apps. The Whoop strap that made him feel worse about himself. The Huberman episode he listened to at 2x speed while skipping dinner. The productivity gurus who have never run a business with employees and children.

**Why 20%**: Reframe is the second-most-important voice because it validates the reader's existing half-formed suspicions. Once per two posts is the right frequency — enough to build Cooz's authority as the one honest voice, not so much that he becomes a hater.

### B. The Structural Formula

**Framework stack**: Luke Iha's **Insight Vector (Reverse Causation)** as the upstream idea generator + Kallaway's **3-Step Hook (Context Lean → Stun Gun → Contrarian Snapback)** as the structural arc + Nicolas Cole's **atomic essay "one clear idea"** rule + the I-led anchor to keep it from becoming a rant.

```
[LINE 1 — CONTEXT LEAN in I, acknowledging the common belief]
  → "I used to believe [common belief]." / "For years I ran on [common advice]."
  → Kallaway Step 1: establishes the +100 anchor the reader also holds
  → Note: starts in I so it doesn't feel like an attack

[LINES 2-3 — SPECIFIC EXPANSION, the cost you paid]
  → What it cost you to believe it
  → Time, money, marriage, sleep, specific event
  → Luke Iha Principle 4 (Stakes)

[LINE 4 — THE STUN GUN, I-led]
  → "But." / "Here's what I know now." / "And then I figured something out."
  → Kallaway Step 2: the hard contrast word that freezes the scroll

[LINES 5-8 — THE CONTRARIAN SNAPBACK, the reframe in I]
  → Name the reverse causation (Luke Iha Pattern 4: highest-leverage vector)
  → "It wasn't X that was killing me. It was Y. And X was actually making Y worse."
  → Nicolas Cole: one clear idea, no hedging, no "it's complicated"

[LINE 9 — THE UNIVERSAL PRINCIPLE, you-pivot or I-maxim]
  → "[Common belief] isn't discipline. It's [what it actually is]."
  → Or: "You're not [label they've accepted]. You're [reframed identity]."

[LINE 10 — CLOSE: a question, a period, or a one-sentence dare]
```

**Hard rules for this bucket**:
- Must **anchor in I** before attacking the belief — never open with "most people think…" (preachy)
- **Name one specific industry practice** (not "the fitness industry," but "the 4 AM alarm")
- The contrarian take must be **falsifiable** — no vague "the real truth is…"
- Never attack the person (Huberman, Goggins) — attack the practice being misapplied
- One reframe per post. No stacking.

### C. Hook Template Library

1. **(Luke Iha Insight Vector — Reverse Causation in I)** "I used to think [common belief] was the thing killing me. It wasn't. It was the thing protecting me from what was actually killing me."
2. **(Kallaway 3-Step Hook — Context Lean + Stun Gun)** "For [time period] I did [common optimization ritual]. Every day. Without fail. Then I realized [counterintuitive truth]."
3. **(Luke Iha Vicious — Belief Callout, I-anchored)** "The fitness industry has been gaslighting men like me for [time period]. I know because I was one of the men."
4. **(Nicolas Cole Niche Positioning — Category-Breaking)** "There's a word everyone in my industry uses that I've stopped using. [Word]. Here's what it's actually doing to the men who hear it."
5. **(Luke Iha Insight Vector — Paradox as Hook)** "The more [common practice] I did, the worse [outcome] got. I thought I was the problem. I wasn't. The practice was."
6. **(Kallaway Contrarian Snapback — Bold Claim in I)** "I'll say the quiet part out loud: [common advice] is the worst possible advice for a depleted man. I was the depleted man. I tried it. I almost didn't come back."
7. **(Luke Iha Vicious — Stakes + Anti-Industry)** "I spent [dollar amount] on [category of product/service] before I understood it was making me sicker. I have the receipts. I also have the bloodwork."
8. **(Nicolas Cole — Confession as Reframe)** "I used to be the guy who [common ritual]. I was also the guy who [contradictory fact]. Both things were true. Only one was working."
9. **(Kallaway Dopamine Ladder — Revelation Opening)** "There's a lie at the center of high-performance culture that I bought for [time period]. Here it is, in one sentence."
10. **(Luke Iha Insight Vector — False Boundary)** "They sold us [Category A] and [Category B] as opposites. They're not. They're the same scam. Here's how I learned."
11. **(Nicolas Cole Sentence Craft — Terminal Word Power)** "What I was calling [positive-sounding word] was actually [unflattering word]. I couldn't see it because I was the one doing it."
12. **(Kallaway Trust Density — I-led Reframe)** "I was wrong about [specific thing] for [specific time]. I paid for it with [specific cost]. Here's what I think now, and why."

### D. Topic Inventory (11 specific topics)

1. **"I used to call my exhaustion a 'tax on ambition.' It was actually a receipt for negligence."** — The story I told myself vs. the bill that was coming due.
2. **"The 4 AM alarm is a luxury for single men with no kids"** — Who the advice is actually for. Who it's destroying. The specific math of a 4 AM wake-up with a 10 PM wife.
3. **"I spent $4,200 on supplements before I figured out I hadn't eaten a real dinner in six months"** — The absurdity timeline. The specific pills. The specific missed meals.
4. **"Discipline isn't missing from your life. Fuel is."** — The reframe that changed how I coach. Why the internet's obsession with discipline is a body-shame script.
5. **"Mindset coaching sold to a depleted body is malpractice"** — The line I won't cross anymore. Why I refer out when a client's nervous system is in collapse.
6. **"The Whoop strap is a surveillance device. I stopped wearing mine in week three of my own resurrection."** — The gadget as guilt machine. What I use instead.
7. **"Cold plunges don't build discipline. They avoid it."** — The comfort of a hard, quick, visible ritual vs. the discomfort of eating dinner with your wife at a normal hour.
8. **"Huberman is for people who already sleep"** — Why optimization content fails the depleted. The prerequisites nobody mentions.
9. **"'Boundaries' is the word depleted men use to explain disappearing on their families"** — The softening of a hard truth. What actually happened.
10. **"The 3 PM crash isn't a crash. It's a receipt."** — What a crash actually is vs. what men have been told it is. Why coffee makes it worse at 3 PM and better at 6 AM.
11. **"Your morning routine is not the problem. Your 9 PM is."** — Why the optimization industry obsesses over the first hour. Why the last hour is what actually breaks you.

### E. Rotation Slot

**Days**: Thursday primary + Saturday backup. Thursday because by Thursday the reader has been grinding for 3+ days and is ready for a permission-giving anger. Saturday because weekend reading hours allow for slower, more argumentative content.

**Frequency**: 1-2 of 7 LinkedIn posts per week. One week in three, it's two posts (especially in weeks where the Cultural Response bucket has an anti-industry news hook that flows naturally into a Contrarian Truth follow-up).

### F. Example Opening (validated voice, ready to ship)

> I used to call my exhaustion a tax on ambition.
>

# BUCKET 4 — THE WITNESS
## Third-Person Peer Voice | 15% Rotation

### A. The Emotional Job

**What this bucket does for the reader**: It lets him recognize himself without being pointed at. The Confession voice (Bucket 1) asks the reader to identify with Cooz. The Witness voice asks the reader to identify with *a guy Cooz is watching* — which is emotionally safer. The reader gets to be the observer. He doesn't have to admit anything. But the specificity of the observation is so exact that he finishes the post knowing Cooz was describing him, and he gets to arrive at that conclusion voluntarily. This is the gossip-you-weren't-supposed-to-hear voice.

**Specific interior pain touched**: The co-founder silence (WS1.5 §3.3). The wife-as-only-honest-mirror problem. The "I'm not burnt out, I'm focused" lie. The Slack checking at 2 AM. The investor who just asked the uncomfortable question. The friend who went quiet. Every Reddit quote in WS1.5 Themes A-H fits here.

**Why 15%**: This voice is highest-leverage but limited-supply. You can't open every post with "I know a guy" — it becomes a gimmick. Used sparingly, it's the voice the reader shares in a DM to his co-founder with no commentary. It's the buy-side of "permission to name it."

### B. The Structural Formula

**Framework stack**: Luke Iha's **Insight Vector (Mental Model Map)** applied third-person + Kallaway's **Anti-Ad / Rubbernecking** posture + Nicolas Cole's **Specificity collapses into universality** + the I-as-observer frame (not pure third person — Cooz is still present as the narrator).

```
[LINE 1 — THE WITNESS OPENING in I-as-observer]
  → "I know a guy." / "There's a founder I've been watching quietly."
  → Luke Iha Principle 6 (Anti-Ad / reads like gossip)
  → The "I" is present but as watcher, not subject

[LINES 2-4 — THE SMART-GUY FRAME]
  → "Smart guy. Built something real. Raised [specific amount]."
  → Kallaway Trust Density: specific credibility markers
  → Why this frame: gives the reader permission to project onto a respected figure
```

# THE BUCKET-TO-TRIGGER MAP
## Automatic Routing Rules from Voice Memo to Bucket

When Cooz records a voice memo, Farrice (or the flywheel agent) routes it to a bucket using these triggers. No ambiguity. No "I don't know which bucket this belongs to."

## Bucket 1 — Depletion Diaries Triggers

1. **Cooz uses the word "I" in the first 10 seconds of the memo and describes a specific past moment** → Bucket 1
2. **Cooz mentions a specific time of day, a specific room, or a specific object he was holding** → Bucket 1
3. **Cooz uses the phrase "I never told anyone about this" or "I've never said this publicly"** → Bucket 1
4. **Cooz describes a physical sensation (heaviness, fog, the driveway sit, the 2 AM kitchen)** → Bucket 1
5. **Cooz mentions his wife, his kids, or a specific family-collapse moment in first person** → Bucket 1

## Bucket 2 — Resurrection Chronicles Triggers

1. **Cooz says a client's name (real or pseudonym)** → Bucket 2
2. **Cooz describes a session, an intake call, or a specific intervention move** → Bucket 2
3. **Cooz describes a state change over a specific time period** ("16 weeks later," "by month three") → Bucket 2
4. **Cooz mentions his own before/after transformation with a specific time marker** → Bucket 2
5. **Cooz references specific proof (bloodwork, photo, weight, sentence the client's wife said)** → Bucket 2

## Bucket 3 — Contrarian Truth Triggers

1. **Cooz says "I used to think…" followed by a common belief** → Bucket 3
2. **Cooz names a specific industry practice he disagrees with (4 AM alarms, cold plunges, Whoop, Huberman-style optimization)** → Bucket 3
3. **Cooz uses the phrase "the opposite is true" or "it's actually the reverse"** → Bucket 3
4. **Cooz expresses frustration with mindset coaches, productivity gurus, or the wellness industry** → Bucket 3
5. **Cooz tells a story about doing the "right" thing and it making him worse** → Bucket 3

## Bucket 4 — Witness Triggers

1. **Cooz says "I know a guy" or "I've been watching a founder" or any third-person opener** → Bucket 4
2. **Cooz describes a pattern he's seeing across multiple clients or peers without naming any** → Bucket 4
3. **Cooz references a recent conversation (coffee, DM, text thread) with someone who isn't a client** → Bucket 4
4. **Cooz uses observational language: "there's a sentence," "there's a pattern," "the people around him"** → Bucket 4
5. **Cooz describes a collapse behavior he's noticed in someone who looks externally fine** → Bucket 4

## Bucket 5 — Body-First Mechanics Triggers

1. **Cooz explains a body mechanism in plain language** (cortisol, protein timing, sleep debt, mitochondria) → Bucket 5
2. **Cooz uses an analogy for a body system** (checking account, battery, bleeding pipe, ledger) → Bucket 5
3. **Cooz gives a specific practical application with a "here's what I do" frame** → Bucket 5
4. **Cooz references a specific bloodwork marker, a specific number, or a specific nutritional rule** → Bucket 5
5. **Cooz corrects a common physiological misunderstanding** (the 3 PM crash myth, the sleep-catch-up myth) → Bucket 5

## Bucket 6 — Cultural Response Triggers

1. **Cooz references a news event from the last 7 days** → Bucket 6
2. **Cooz names a cultural figure** (Bryan Johnson, Dorsey, Dan Go, a recent study, a Netflix show) → Bucket 6
3. **Cooz reacts to something he read, watched, or heard in the last week** → Bucket 6
4. **Cooz uses a statistic from a specific recent source** → Bucket 6
5. **Cooz describes how founders around him are reacting to a cultural moment** → Bucket 6

## Routing Collisions (when a memo triggers 2+ buckets)

**If a memo triggers Bucket 1 AND Bucket 6 (e.g., Cooz reacts to Bryan Johnson with a personal confession)**: Route to Bucket 6 (the news hook is the relevance driver, and the confession becomes the body).

**If a memo triggers Bucket 2 AND Bucket 5 (e.g., Cooz tells a client story that explains a mechanism)**: Route to Bucket 2 (narrative leads; mechanism becomes an embedded teaching moment).

**If a memo triggers Bucket 3 AND Bucket 4 (e.g., a reframe told through a third-person story)**: Route to Bucket 4 (the Witness frame is rare and high-leverage; preserve it).

**If a memo triggers Bucket 1 AND Bucket 3 (confession + reframe)**: Route to Bucket 1 if the emotional beat is dominant, Bucket 3 if the cognitive reframe is dominant. Default: Bucket 1.

# THE BUCKET DISQUALIFIERS
## What Should NEVER Appear in Each Bucket

Use this as a pre-publish checklist. If any disqualifier is present, rewrite or reroute.

## Bucket 1 — Depletion Diaries Should NEVER Have:

1. **A sales pitch or CTA to book a call** — breaks the emotional pact the confession establishes
2. **A framework name drop** (Total Radius Ownership, 8 Tenets, Dormant Quarterback) — reroute to Bucket 5 if mechanism-driven
3. **Sustained "you" scene narration** ("You pull into the driveway…") — violates voice rule, rewrite to I
4. **A third-person "I know a guy" opening** — reroute to Bucket 4
5. **Statistics, studies, or citations** — breaks the confession voice, reroute to Bucket 5 or 6

## Bucket 2 — Resurrection Chronicles Should NEVER Have:

1. **Anonymous "one of my clients" framing** — use a real name or a first-name pseudonym, never "a client"
2. **Data-heavy proof** (DEXA scans, VO2 max charts) — narrative proof only, per Sheedy template
3. **A protocol dump** — one specific intervention move, never the full program
4. **A sales pitch** — the story IS the pitch
5. **Cooz positioning himself as the hero** — the client is the hero; Cooz is the guide who asked one good question

## Bucket 3 — Contrarian Truth Should NEVER Have:

1. **A personal attack on a named expert** (Huberman, Goggins, Dan Go) — attack the practice, not the man
2. **Vague "the real truth is" claims** — every reframe must be specific and falsifiable
3. **More than one reframe per post** — one clear contrarian idea, compressed
4. **Unanchored preaching** ("most men think…") — must open in I, never in generalization
5. **A rant without a constructive reframe** — the post must end with a clearer, truer frame, not just anger

## Bucket 4 — Witness Should NEVER Have:

1. **A named client** — reroute to Bucket 2
2. **Identifiable details from a real person's life** (specific company names, dollar amounts tied to a real deal) — composite only
3. **A diagnostic frame** ("he has adrenal fatigue") — stay in behavioral observation
4. **A CTA** — the post is a gift to the reader, never a sales vehicle
5. **Missing the "if you think I'm talking about you, I'm not" pivot** — this is the plausible-deniability rule; without it, the post feels invasive

## Bucket 5 — Body-First Mechanics Should NEVER Have:

1. **Jargon without a plain-language translation** (HPA axis, mitochondrial uncoupling) — rewrite or cut
2. **A full protocol** — one specific practical application, never a 10-step plan
3. **Academic citations** (study-name-dropping) — the mechanism stands on clarity alone
4. **Opening in textbook voice** — must open in I or with a confession-of-prior-ignorance frame
5. **Multiple mechanisms stacked** — one body system per post, always

## Bucket 6 — Cultural Response Should NEVER Have:

1. **News older than 7 days** — stale brand-jacking reads desperate
2. **More than one cultural reference per post** — no Dorsey + Bryan Johnson + Axios stacking
3. **A personal attack on the cultural figure** — attack the frame, never the man
4. **Missing the I-anchor within the first 3 sentences** — without it, the post reads as commentary, not confession
5. **A reframe that doesn't route through the body** — if it can't be reframed body-first, it's not a Cooz post

# APPENDIX B — QUALITY CHECKLIST (PRE-PUBLISH)

Before any post leaves the flywheel, run this checklist:

## Voice Rule Compliance (all buckets)
- [ ] First 180 words are pure I (except Bucket 4, which opens in observer-I)
- [ ] "You"/"your" appears **7 times maximum** across the whole post
- [ ] All "you" instances are in the final 2 sentences OR in a single POV one-liner
- [ ] No sustained second-person scene narration anywhere
- [ ] The pivot sentence is a statement of universal condition, not a scene description

## Luke Iha Vicious Hook Audit (Line 1 only)
- [ ] Relevant in the first 10-20 words
- [ ] No Latinate/French words where a Germanic equivalent exists
- [ ] Open loop is tight (someone reading cannot predict what comes next)
- [ ] Real stakes present (death, identity, marriage, health, money, reputation)
- [ ] Reads like confession/gossip/story, not an ad
- [ ] A 12-year-old could read every word

## Nicolas Cole Sentence Craft Audit (full post)
- [ ] Every sentence ends on a substantive noun or strong verb (Terminal Word Power)
- [ ] Second and third compression passes applied (word count reduced 20-30% from first draft)
- [ ] Audience vocabulary calibrated (no industry jargon)
- [ ] One clear idea per post, not stacked

## Bucket Disqualifier Check
- [ ] No disqualifiers from the assigned bucket's list are present
- [ ] If a disqualifier appears, post is rewritten or rerouted

## Rotation Check
- [ ] Post does not duplicate the same bucket two days in a row (except DD which can double)
- [ ] Weekly totals match the rotation percentages (±1 post per week is fine)

## Bucket-Specific Check (final pass)
- [ ] **Bucket 1 (DD)**: No CTA. No framework drop. No stats.
- [ ] **Bucket 2 (RC)**: Named client. One specific move. Narrative proof, not metrics.
- [ ] **Bucket 3 (CT)**: Anchored in I first. One falsifiable reframe. No personal attacks.
- [ ] **Bucket 4 (WI)**: "If you think I'm talking about you, I'm not" pivot present. Composite, not identifiable.
- [ ] **Bucket 5 (BM)**: One mechanism. Plain language. Analogy in Cooz-voice. One practical application.
