## Category 1: Your Story (Use These When You Want to Build Trust)

### Prompt 1.1 — The Quarterback Moment
Talk about who you were in your peak athletic years. Specifically: what did it feel like to operate as a unified system — body and mind working together — when you were playing? Then talk about the moment you realized that version of you had gone offline. Not the dramatic version. The quiet version. The morning you noticed something was different.

### Prompt 1.2 — The Worst Season
Talk about your darkest period. Don't sanitize it. Don't make it inspirational. Just describe what it actually felt like — the day-to-day texture of it. What did you tell yourself was the problem at the time? What did you NOT see clearly? When did the first crack of clarity come, and what triggered it?

### Prompt 1.3 — The Decision to Coach
Why coaching, and why this specific coaching? Not the marketing answer. The real one. What was happening in your life when you knew this was the work? What were you hoping it would do for you, not just the people you serve?

## Category 2: Your Methodology (Use These When You Want to Build Authority)

### Prompt 2.1 — A Real Client Session
Walk me through a recent session with a client. Start with what they came in saying, then what you noticed they didn't say. What did you actually do in that session? What was the moment something shifted? Use the actual client (with their permission) or a composite — but keep it specific.

### Prompt 2.2 — The Body-First Argument (Why Mindset Coaches Are Wrong)
Why do you start with the body when every other coach starts with mindset, purpose, or vision? Don't give the textbook answer. Give the conviction. What have you seen happen when someone tries to fix their mindset on a body that's running on cortisol and 5 hours of broken sleep?

### Prompt 2.3 — A Framework You Use, Explained Like You'd Explain It At a Bar
Pick ONE of your named frameworks (Daily Calibration, Total Radius Ownership, the 8 Tenets, etc.). Explain it like you're at a bar and a friend just asked "what is that, exactly?" No slides. No diagrams. Just words and a beer napkin if you need one.

## Category 3: The Problem (Use These to Resonate with the ICP)

### Prompt 3.1 — The Founder Who Came In Last Week
Tell the story of a recent client (or a composite) who exemplifies what you see over and over. The pattern. The way they walked in. The things they said in the first 5 minutes. The thing they tried to hide. The lie they were telling themselves that you had to gently call out.

### Prompt 3.2 — What Most Coaches Miss About Burnout
You've watched this for years. What does the coaching industry get wrong about burnout in successful men? What's the false story being sold? What's actually true that nobody wants to hear because it's not sellable as a tidy framework?

### Prompt 3.3 — The Cost Nobody Sees
What's the hidden cost of running on empty for 18 months that the founder himself can't see clearly? Talk about what you watch happen in their relationships, their decision-making, their physical body, their sense of self. Be specific. What's the moment a client realizes something irreversible has been done?

## Category 4: The Reframe (Use These to Challenge Conventional Wisdom)

### Prompt 4.1 — The Discipline Lie
Most "experts" tell exhausted founders they need more discipline. You disagree. Why? What are they actually missing? What does discipline look like when you DON'T have the foundation in place vs. when you DO?

### Prompt 4.2 — Why "Optimization" Is the Wrong Frame
Why does "optimize your performance" miss the point for the men you serve? What's the deeper word for what's actually happening, and why does language matter so much in this work?

### Prompt 4.3 — The Spiritual Side You Don't Always Talk About Publicly
You mentioned hearing the voice of God. You've had spiritual moments that shaped your work. Talk about how the body work and the spiritual work connect for you. This doesn't have to be evangelistic — but it's the part of you that's underrepresented in your content and it's part of why people will trust you over a generic fitness coach.
