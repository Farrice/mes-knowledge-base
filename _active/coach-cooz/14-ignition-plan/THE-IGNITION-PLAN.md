## Section 4 — The Case Study Mechanism

Every paid client is a proof asset. The proof is built into delivery from day one, not requested at the end. Cooz collects what he needs during onboarding, so there is no friction at exit.

### Intake (Week 1, mandatory)

- **Baseline photos.** Four angles — front, back, both sides. Standardized: same time of day, same lighting, same outfit (compression shorts, no shirt). Cooz provides the protocol PDF at intake.
- **Baseline metrics.** Weight. Waist, hips, chest, both arms. Tape measurements, not body fat estimates from impedance scales (impedance is noise).
- **Three benchmark lifts.** Squat, deadlift, overhead press. Current weight × reps. Recorded as a starting line, not a max test.
- **Intake questionnaire (1 page, 6 questions)**:
  1. Why are you here right now, in this week, not six months ago?
  2. What have you tried before, and what did each thing leave behind?
  3. What does your life look like in 90 days if this works exactly the way you want?

## Section 9 — The Self-Critique

This is the section where the plan attacks itself. Cooz needs to know which assumptions are loadbearing, which are fragile, and which probable failure modes the plan does not yet have a fix for. If the plan ships and doesn't work, the most likely reason will be one of the following.

**Failure mode 1 — Voice discipline collapses under cash pressure.** The Ignition Plan asks Cooz to hold a voice rule (I-led confession, you-pivot at universal moment) for 13 weeks while running on no MRR. The first time a Sheedy-template post gets 12 likes instead of 200, the temptation to "spice it up" or revert to the clinical voice is going to be enormous. The voice guide explicitly bans "Here's what" openers and emojis, but sad coaches under cash pressure cheat. If Cooz cheats on the voice rule even once in week 4 or 5, the algorithm rewards the wrong behavior and the lane discipline collapses for the rest of the cohort. **Mitigation**: weekly voice review against the Sheedy template checklist with Farrice. Hard rule: no post ships without a Farrice voice check during the first 4 weeks.

**Failure mode 2 — Warm network voice memos return zero closes.** Tactic 1 is the fastest channel and the plan leans on it for the first close. If Cooz sends 20 voice memos and gets 3 polite "I'll think about it"s and zero referrals, the cash pressure compounds and morale breaks before the slower channels (podcasts, audits) have time to compound. The most likely reason this happens: the voice memo script projects too much certainty in the founding-client framing and the warm network reads it as a desperate ask. **Mitigation**: review the first 5 voice memos with Farrice before sending the next 15. If the first 5 get zero responses, change the script BEFORE deploying the rest of the volume.

**Failure mode 3 — The Man in the Valley archetype doesn't exist at $1,997 in volume.** The empirical test is week 4. If 0 closes by end of week 4 across all 5 tactics, the archetype is wrong and Sections 2 through 5 need to be re-ported. The probable misspecification: the archetype as written is too psychologically nuanced. The actual buyer might be earlier in the awareness arc — someone who is NOT thinking in story-arc terms about himself yet, who responds to a simpler "lose 25 pounds" promise. If that's the case, Lane A content needs to drop the resurrection language entirely and run on protein math and lift numbers. This is a real possible outcome and the plan should not protect itself from naming it.

**Failure mode 4 — The hybrid trap closes around Cooz.** Cooz takes in-person locals during Ignition because they're easier and they pay faster. The business calcifies into a $5K–$8K/mo Burbank-area gig and the fully-online ambition recedes by 6 months because hybrid is more comfortable than online-only. The Section 8 trigger ($10K MRR online-only OR 3 online-only case studies) is the structural fix, but Cooz has to actually honor it. If he doesn't, the entire plan's destination collapses. **Mitigation**: Farrice tracks online-only revenue separately every week. The trigger is non-negotiable.

**Failure mode 5 — Content compounding is too slow to matter inside 90 days.** Instagram engagement on a sub-1K-follower account does not compound on a Sheedy curve in 13 weeks. Sheedy took longer to get to 19K-like posts than 13 weeks. If Cooz expects the content engine to do the heavy lifting on close volume, he will be disappointed. **The plan acknowledges this**: tactics 1, 2, 4, 5 are designed to operate INDEPENDENTLY of content reach. The content engine is the audience build, not the close engine. Cooz must internalize that distinction or he will judge the content engine on the wrong metric and abandon it.

**Failure mode 6 — Cooz refuses the founding-client price and reverts to $5K.** Cooz has been emotionally invested in $5K and $10K pricing for a long time. The $1,997 founding rate may feel beneath his brand to him personally, even though it's the structurally correct move. If he silently refuses to deploy it — quotes $1,997 in writing but pushes for $5K in conversation — the founding client cohort closes at zero and the price-drift cycle continues for another 90 days. **Mitigation**: Section 10 question 6 surfaces this directly. Farrice has to get Cooz's commitment in writing before week 1.

**Failure mode 7 — Cash runway runs out in week 5 or 6.** Cooz has no MRR. If he doesn't close a founding client by week 3, he's deep into week 5 with no revenue and the pressure starts breaking discipline (price drops, voice cheating, abandoning the structure). The plan assumes a week 2 close. If that slips to week 4, every downstream milestone slips with it. **Mitigation**: tactic 1 (warm network voice memos) MUST run in week 1, not after content has had time to compound. The plan is structured for this. Cooz has to actually do it.

**Failure mode 8 — Brian's testimonial is not actually publishable by week 4.** The plan assumes Brian's case study can be published in week 4 as the FIRST male proof asset. If Brian is not yet at the end of his transformation, or if he's reluctant to be on camera, or if the photo set isn't usable — the proof timeline pushes back two to four weeks and the entire content engine is running on fumes longer than expected. **Mitigation**: Section 10 question 7 confirms this directly. If Brian is not ready, the plan needs a backup male case study from Cooz's archive (he has earlier clients — at least one of them has to be willing to be packaged retroactively).

**Failure mode 9 — The single-Loom-as-standalone-tactic hypothesis is unproven, and Cooz might lean on it as a primary channel anyway.** When research went looking for a real story of a body coach landing first clients via a single Loom alone, NOTHING came back. The "Loom = first client" pattern is documented in SaaS sales, not in body coaching. The Loom is now positioned in the plan as a force-multiplier inside Tactic 1, not a standalone channel. **Risk**: If Cooz interprets the Loom as a standalone tactic and posts it on Instagram expecting it to convert cold viewers, he will be disappointed and may abandon Tactic 1 (the strongest channel) by association. **Mitigation**: the Section 6 reframe is explicit. The Loom is in service of the warm network, not a replacement for it.

**Failure mode 10 — The BJJ / men's group partnership cold-pitch fails because the precedent doesn't exist.** Research found no documented case of a body coach landing first clients by cold-pitching a BJJ academy or men's group. The pattern that works is *be a member first, then offer S&C*. Cooz should embed in 1 community in week 1 as a *participant*, not a *vendor*, and let the conversation come to him.

**The honest summary**: This plan is fragile in 4 places — voice discipline (failure 1), the warm network response rate (failure 2), the archetype precision (failure 3), and Cooz's emotional commitment to the $1,997 founding rate (failure 6). All four are inside Cooz, not inside the market. The market data supports the plan. The execution discipline is the variable. If Cooz is going to fail, it will be because he reverts to a version of himself the plan was designed to leave behind.

The plan is designed to be tested in 4 weeks. If the founding cohort has not closed at least 1 client by end of week 4, do not push harder on the same plan. Stop. Reassess. The most probable misspecification is the archetype, and the second is the channel mix (probably too much weight on warm network, not enough on podcast guesting).
