## 1. VERDICT (150 words)

**WORTH EXECUTING.** This is a high-signal angle with measurable kill conditions and earned credibility.

**The bet**: The fitness industry has stopped selling outcomes (feel better) and started selling theater (transformation photos, abs, bench numbers). Cooz holds a rare truth: the leanest he's been (14% BF) was his most miserable; heavier (22% BF) was his happiest. That inverted transformation story is not inspirational. It's *dangerous* because it's true and no one else in fitness is allowed to say it. The flyer weaponizes this asymmetry: an unbranded card that breaks the fitness industry's visual language (no trainer, no logo, no transformation photo) and redirects desire toward *feeling better* — the actual purchase every client makes.

**Core mechanism**: Psychological distribution + emotional hit + proof that *you* don't need to be miserable to be fit.

**Where it breaks**: If the squeeze page softens the villain (calls it "traditional fitness" instead of naming specific mechanisms). If testimonials are paraphrased instead of verbatim. If Cooz's body-fat numbers appear unqualified (label self-reported). If CTA appears once (psychology of repetition will fail). Otherwise, the funnel is sound.

## 2. FUNNEL ARCHITECTURE (250 words)

### Distribution Layer: The Unbranded Flyer

**What it is**: 4-line black/gold/white card (no name, no brand, no logo). Handed to local LA businesses (coffee shops, boutiques, corporate office managers, yoga studios, salons — anyplace with foot traffic).

**Psychological strategy**:
- Unbranded = mystery hook. Why would someone give away a card with no name? That question *is* the funnel entry.
- Profanity ("shit") = signal filter. Anyone disturbed will crumple it. Anyone curious will scan. You're self-selecting for permission.
- QR placement = frictionless conversion. They're already holding the card; scanning takes 3 seconds.

**Builder rule**: The flyer is not a "cute" design problem. Its weirdness IS the offer. Do not sand it. Do not add a subtle logo at the bottom. Do not make it look "professional" — it should look like something a person slipped under your windshield, not something a brand designed.

### Bridge Layer: Squeeze Page

**URL architecture**: New page on coachcooz.com (or standalone if Squarespace limits apply). No navigation. Single CTA repeated 2-3x (top, mid-scroll, bottom button). Title: "The Truth About Feeling Like Shit" or similar direct reframe.

**Sequential flow**:
1. **Hook** (immediate): "You scanned this because you're tired of feeling like shit. That's not weakness. That's signal."
2. **Villain naming** (specific, concrete): The fitness industry's actual mechanisms:
   - Transformation photos are faked (Photoshop, lighting, dehydration timing)
   - Before/afters erase the middle (you're seeing week 1 vs. week 16, not showing weeks 2-15 of looking worse)
   - Abs as status symbol (the meta-myth: "if I have abs, I will feel worthy")
   - "Motivation" instead of infrastructure ("just believe harder" fails when your cortisol is running your operating system)
3. **Cooz's inverted transformation** (lived proof):
   - Photo: Cooz at 14% BF (left/then) vs. 22% BF (right/now)
   - Caption: "This was me at my leanest. Most miserable I'd ever been. This was me nine months later. Happiest I've ever been. Same dude, 8% body fat difference." Label explicitly: *Self-reported, based on visual assessment.*
4. **Proof layer** (weaponized testimonials):
   - Pull 4-5 verbatim written testimonials from coachcooz.com or Yelp "Fitness Over Hollywood" page
   - Headline each with the client's first name + one-line proof — FORMAT EXAMPLE ONLY (there is no client "Sarah"; every real quote comes verbatim from RAW-PROOF-INVENTORY.md): [Name]: "[verbatim feeling-language excerpt]"
   - Do NOT use before/after photos (Cooz's strategy: written proof is unfakeable, photos are fabricated)
5. **The pitch** (framework, not plea):
   - "You don't need to be miserable to be strong. You don't need to hate yourself to change."
   - Introduce the 90-min Triage Audit ($1K credit) and 90-Day Protocol ($5K / $4K with podcast credit)
6. **CTA repeated**: "Book your Triage" button (top), inline link (mid), bottom CTA with urgency frame (e.g., "Only 3 spots this month")

**Tracking setup**: UTM parameters on QR code to measure:
- QR hit rate (total scans)
- Squeeze page conversion (scans → email/phone)
- Booking rate (email/phone → scheduled Triage Audit)
- Revenue per flyer (assign cost per card printed, track payback)

**Single-page discipline**: No navigation menu, no links to blog, no "other services." The page has one job: move the reader from "I'm tired of feeling like shit" to "I want to book."

### Booking Layer: In-Person Priority

**Goal**: Triage Audits booked in LA area (in-person). Online bookings are secondary.

**Flyer distribution** targets local businesses Cooz can visit in person to build relationships, not just paste and vanish. Each handoff is a micro-pitch: "This is what I'm testing. Tell me what you think."

**Proof metric**: Measure cost per local booking (flyer printing cost + Cooz's time) vs. revenue per 90-day client ($4K-$5K). Viable threshold: 3-5 bookings per 500 flyers (0.6-1% conversion).

## 3. MESSAGING SPINE (300 words)

### Position: "I specialize in helping you not feel like shit"

This is not a positioning statement for LinkedIn. This is the *lived frame* that governs every word. It sits between Cooz's two failed attempts:
- **Too deep**: "Resurrection deep-pain story" (works for podcast, not for cold unbranded flyer)
- **Too shallow**: "Physical transformation coach" (what he's zagging against — the industry standard)

**Feeling-first coaching** is the wedge. Every outcome (stronger, leaner, better posture) is a delivery mechanism for one purchase: *feeling better*.

### The Villain (Named, Not Faceless)

Generic enemy ("the fitness industry," "toxic culture") will not rally or frighten. The squeeze page must name specific mechanisms Cooz has permission to attack:

**Do name**:
- Transformation photos (faked via Photoshop, lighting, dehydration timing)
- Before/after theater (erases the 15-week middle where you look worse before better)
- Shame-based marketing ("If you don't look like this, you're failing")
- Abs as proxy (the lie: "if I get abs, I will feel worthy"; the truth: you won't)
- "Motivation" as substitute for infrastructure ("just believe harder" fails when your body is running your OS)
- INTERNAL REFERENCE ONLY — do not put in public copy: Cody McBroom's anti-fitness positioning ("average people, above-average physiques" framing) is the proof this wedge works commercially. Fact-verify gate (2026-07-08): slogan wording LIKELY but not verbatim-confirmed; seven-figure claim UNCONFIRMED; McBroom is also a positive endorser on Cooz's own home page. The squeeze page correctly CUT this reference. It stays cut unless verified verbatim AND Cooz signs off.

**Do NOT name**:
- Specific competitor coaches by name ("Coach X is fake") — this is gossip, not positioning
- Medical claims ("I fix your depression," "I heal your trauma") — outside Cooz's lane and legally risky
- Fake data ("Studies show 87% of transformation photos are fake") — if you don't have it, imply via story, never fake numbers

### Cooz's License: The Inverted Transformation

**Why he's allowed to say this**: His own story is the credibility engine.

- At 14% body fat: "The most miserable, unhappy and sad he had ever been in his life."
- At 22% body fat (nine months later): "On top of the world. Live in life and full of joy."
- Same dude. 8% body fat difference. Completely inverted feeling.

**How to use this proof**: 
- Photo (left = 14% miserable, right = 22% happy)
- Label as self-reported (he's calling his own body-fat assessment, not a DEXA scan)
- Narrate the lived moment: "I was leanest and most miserable. Then I decided to stop trying. Got heavier, got happy. Then rebuilt strength without the misery."

This is *not* a transformation photo in the traditional sense. It's an inversion. That weirdness is the differentiator.

### Proof Strategy: Written Testimonials Over Photos

**Why testimonials win**:
- Fabricated photos are epidemic in fitness (Photoshop, timing, lighting)
- Written stories are unfakeable (the specificity, the emotion, the language pattern — these are fraud-resistant)

**What to pull**:
- Verbatim testimonials from coachcooz.com "Resurrections" page
- Additional testimonials from "Fitness Over Hollywood" Yelp page (Cooz found this)
- Minimum 4-5 per squeeze page
- Label each: Client first name + one-line proof (FORMAT ONLY — no "Sarah" exists; real quotes are Karima, Jessica, Robin, Jess, Sam per RAW-PROOF-INVENTORY.md)

**What NOT to do**:
- Paraphrase testimonials ("Here's what Sarah said...") — kills authenticity
- Use before/after photos alongside testimonials — the photos undermine the whole frame
- Add testimonials that aren't verbatim on coachcooz.com or verifiable Yelp — verification flags this risk

### Sequencing: "Make them want it, then hold up the mirror"

**Hawley's principle**: First make the reader *feel* the wanting (you scanned this card because you're tired of feeling like shit). Then show them why (the fitness industry sold you a lie about what you actually need). Then give them proof (Cooz and his clients feel better). Then ask for the booking.

**Builder rule**: The squeeze page is NOT a sales page. It's a *breakdown* that leads to a booking. Every section should feel like Cooz is talking to a friend at a bar, saying what everyone's thinking but no one says out loud.

## 4. BUILDER GUARDRAILS (200 words)

**These are non-negotiable rules for copywriters, designers, and flyer-builders.**

### ✅ MUST DO

- **Keep profanity** ("shit," "fuck," "bullshit"). It's not edginess — it's the mechanism. It's how Cooz talks. Remove it, and you've neutered the psychological filter (you stop self-selecting for permission).
- **Name specific villain mechanisms.** Don't soften to "traditional fitness coaching" or "old approaches." Name: faked photos, shame-based marketing, abs-as-proxy, "motivation" as substitute for infrastructure.
- **Use Cooz's actual body-fat numbers and label them self-reported.** "14% BF (self-reported) — most miserable" and "22% BF (self-reported) — happiest." This transparency is credible. Hiding the self-report makes it look fabricated.
- **Repeat the CTA 2-3x on the squeeze page.** "Book your Triage" at top, inline link mid-scroll, CTA button at bottom. Single-CTA pages are amateur psychology. Repetition builds momentum.
- **Pull verbatim testimonials only.** If it's not word-for-word on coachcooz.com or the Yelp page, do not use it. Paraphrase = instant credibility death.
- **Write the squeeze page in Cooz's voice** (short-short-short, one medium wave, short close; I-centered, specific moment, peer-with-receipts tone — see voice profile).

### ❌ DO NOT

- **Make medical/therapy claims.** "This will fix your depression." "I heal your trauma." "This fixes your anxiety." These are outside his lane and legally risky. Stick to: "help you feel better," "strengthen your body," "rebuild your infrastructure."
- **Disparage competitors by name.** "Coach X is fake" = gossip. Naming mechanisms (fake photos, shame-based marketing) = positions Cooz. One is permitted, the other is not.
- **Invent stats or data.** Don't write "Studies show 87% of transformation photos are fabricated" unless you have the study. Imply via story instead: "Most of the transformation photos you see are timed and lit to hide what weeks 2-15 actually looked like."
- **Soften the unbranded flyer concept.** The weirdness — "Are you tired of feeling like shit?" with no name, no brand — IS the hook. Adding a subtle logo at the bottom, or framing it as "exclusive insider card," kills the psychological leverage.
- **Paraphrase the invitation into a pitch.** The flyer is not "Here's what I can do for you." It's "I see you. I know how you feel. Scan if you want to stop feeling like shit." Invitation beats pitch.
- **Use generic fitness language** ("transform your body," "unlock your potential," "crush your goals," "level up," "your best self"). All banned per voice profile. Use: feel better, stop hating, rebuild, come back, stronger, joyful.

### Verification Requirements (Factual Grounding)

Before the squeeze page ships:
- **Body-fat numbers**: Confirm Cooz's self-reported assessment (14% miserable, 22% happy, 8-9% drop in nine months). Label clearly as self-reported.
- **Cody McBroom slogan**: "We help average people achieve above average physiques" — verify verbatim or cut.
- **Ed Mylett quote**: "People don't actually want the things they say they want… they want what they think the things will make them feel" — verify or attribute loosely ("A mentor once said").
- **Testimonials**: Every quote must be verbatim from coachcooz.com "Resurrections" page or verifiable from Yelp "Fitness Over Hollywood." No paraphrase.
- **Prose gate**: Run final copy through `prose_classifier.py check` (ai-slop-ban-bank.md compliance — 64 banned phrases). One hit = rewrite.
