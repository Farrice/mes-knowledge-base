## 2. Curation

**The standard, in order**:
1. **Feeling-language first** — must name an internal shift, not just a rep count or a pound.
2. **Specific beats generic** — a named stake (thyroid cancer, 65 and post-COVID, "6 AM something to look forward to") beats "great trainer, highly recommend."
3. **Named beats anonymous** — first name plus a real detail, never "a client said."

## 4. Capture SOP Going Forward

**Applies now**: Celine, and the wedding-couple engagement. Written check-in is primary. Photos are secondary and optional — never the ask itself.

**Cadence**: once a week, same day, short.

**The ask, in Cooz's voice** (text or voice memo to the client):
> "Quick one. How'd you feel this week? Not the workout. The whole week. One or two sentences, your words. Give me the real one, not the polished one."

**Consent line** (say once, keep on file, don't re-ask every week):
> "Mind if I use what you send me? No last name, no location, just your words. Say no and it stays between us."

**What gets logged weekly**: the client's own sentence, verbatim, dated. Photos only if the client offers one unprompted — never requested as the primary artifact.

**Legacy quotes — health-stakes consent gap**: a testimonial already sitting on the site is not blanket consent to put someone's cancer diagnosis or blood pressure history into a paid ad rotation. Before Jessica's or Johnny's quote goes into an IG cold-read or a paid A/B variant, Cooz sends one text first: "Hey, your words on the site hit hard. Mind if I use them in some of my content?" Same rule applies to any future health-stakes quote.
