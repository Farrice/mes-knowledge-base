# Flyer Copy + Design Spec — "War on the Fitness Industry"
## Coach Cooz · Unbranded Handout/Placard · 2026-07-08

Nothing on it. No name, no brand, no logo, no URL. A question, a QR code, and an arrow.

---

## 1. COPY, MATCHED TO DISTRIBUTION MODE

Three variants. Each one is matched to how it lands on a stranger, not A/B'd blind against the other two. A card handed over by a person and a card sitting alone on a counter are facing different objections.

### Hand-to-hand (Cooz present) · CANONICAL: Variant 1

```
Are you tired of feeling like shit?

Do you not want to feel like shit anymore?

[QR CODE]

↑ Scan this QR code to not feel like shit anymore.
```

Cooz's exact words, untouched. Ships as-is unless a test proves otherwise. Two questions before the ask. The repetition earns the yes. Ship it as-is.

**Alternate for A/B within this mode only: Variant 2 (Two-Beat Compression, 11 words)**

```
Tired of feeling like shit?

[QR CODE]

↑ Scan it. Stop feeling like shit.
```

Tests whether one question plus a flat imperative scans as well as the double question. Minimum sample: 100 hand-to-hand cards per variant before calling it. If scan rate holds or improves, the second question was dead weight and Variant 2 replaces Variant 1 as canonical for this mode. If it drops, keep Variant 1.

### Unattended counter placards · CANONICAL: Variant 3, revised (no one there to vouch for it)

```
Still feeling like shit?

[QR CODE]

↑ Scan it.
```

A card sitting alone on a counter has to earn the scan with nobody there to make it feel safe. That's a job for plainness, not cleverness. The earlier line for this slot, "This isn't a gym ad. Scan it anyway," is cut. Naming what the card isn't is still explaining itself, and an unattended card that explains itself reads more like a pitch than an honest question. The blank card and the flat question are the whole answer.

## 2. DESIGN SPEC

**Palette** (approximate site tones, not a pixel match): background true black; headline and CTA type in gold; arrow and QR-square accents in white. No gradients, no drop shadows, no polish. It should look slipped under a windshield, not designed by an agency. Perfect kerning and dead-on symmetry undercut that read. A little roughness in the execution is fine, and it's actually the point.

**Layout order (top to bottom, both formats)**: Line 1 → Line 2 → QR code → upward arrow → CTA caption. Generous negative space above and below each line. The card should feel sparse, not packed.

| Element | 4"×6" Handout | 8.5"×11" Counter Placard |
|---|---|---|
| **Trim size** | 4" × 6" | 8.5" × 11" |
| **Bleed** | 0.125" all sides (4.25"×6.25" total) | 0.125" all sides (8.75"×11.25" total) |
| **Safe margin** | 0.25" from trim, all sides | 0.375" from trim, all sides |
| **Line 1 / Line 2 size** | 32–36pt bold sans, gold, centered | 60–72pt bold sans, gold, centered |
| **QR code size** | 1.25"–1.5" square, white background, black modules | 2.5"–3" square, white background, black modules |
| **QR quiet zone** | Min. 4 modules of white space on all sides; don't let type or bleed crowd it | Same rule, scaled proportionally |
| **Minimum scan distance** | ~12–15" (arm's length, matches hand-to-hand delivery) | ~25–30" (across a counter or register) |
| **Arrow / scan indicator** | Bold upward chevron, white, ~0.5" tall, directly under the QR pointing up at it | Same treatment, ~1"–1.5" tall |
| **CTA caption size** | 20–24pt, gold or white, centered, below the arrow | 36–44pt, gold or white, centered |
| **Brand elements** | NONE (no logo, no name, no URL, no handle) | NONE (same rule) |

**Print notes**: 300 DPI minimum. CMYK color space (convert site RGB swatches for gold/white/black before send-to-print). 0.125" bleed on all sides as above. Keep the QR code vector/native, not a rasterized screenshot, so it holds scan integrity at any output size. Test-scan the final proof from three distances before the print run goes out.

**Paper stock**: Handout: 14pt uncoated matte cardstock, postcard weight. Matte over gloss on purpose: gloss reads as "ad," matte reads as "found object." Placard: 100lb uncoated cover stock, or mount on foam-core if it sits upright on a counter for weeks instead of getting handed off.

## 3. DISTRIBUTION NOTE

**Trust is split by audience, and that's deliberate.** The scanner stays anonymous. Nothing on the card traces to Cooz. The owner does not stay anonymous to Cooz, and they shouldn't: an unbranded QR code that the owner is told to disclaim reads exactly like a scam sticker on their own counter, in front of their own customers. Fix: tell the owner the truth, out loud, never printed on anything. It's from him, he's a local trainer, and the blank card is on purpose. That costs nothing, because the scanner never hears that conversation, and the mystery for the scanner is untouched.

**Where it goes**: high-dwell, high-foot-traffic local businesses where people sit still for a few minutes and the room can carry a profanity-forward card: barbershops and salons (chair time), laundromats (wash-cycle wait), coffee shops and co-working spaces (counter and community board). Skip direct competitors (other training studios, traditional gyms). This card doesn't need a fitness-adjacent audience, it needs a captive one.

**Not this pass**: physical therapy offices, chiropractic offices, juice bars. These are wholesome, health-brand rooms, and "Are you tired of feeling like shit?" sitting at their register in front of families isn't a placement they'll take. It's not a fight worth having here. A profanity-free variant could open that channel later; none exists in this build.

**The ask** (Cooz, in person, peer-to-peer, not a pitch, an invitation):

> "Hey man, I've got this weird little card — no name on it, nothing salesy, just a question. It's from me. I'm a local trainer, and the card's blank on purpose. It's an experiment I'm running. Mind if I leave a stack by your register?"

Nothing about "if anyone asks, say you don't know." The owner already knows. The customer doesn't need to.

**Dependency flag for the squeeze-page build**: the card's last line promises relief on scan ("to not feel like shit anymore"). The squeeze page's first screen has to pay part of that off before it pitches anything: real insight into what's actually wrong, not an immediate booking ask. If the page opens with the offer instead of the breakdown, the card over-promised and the bounce happens at the fold.

## 4. IMAGE-GEN BRIEF (art direction for poster-generation pipeline)

**Composition**: Vertical card, dead-center symmetrical layout, heavy negative space top and bottom. Three distinct visual beats stacked with clear air between them: headline block, QR block, CTA block. Nothing competes with the QR code for attention; it sits in its own clean white square, isolated against the black field.

**Palette**: True black background. Gold text, a warm gold in the neighborhood of the site's, not an exact swatch pull, not brassy. White for the QR's background square and the arrow/indicator only. No secondary colors, no gradients, no texture overlays.

**Typography mood**: Bold, condensed or grotesk sans-serif. Confident and blunt, not decorative. Think a protest sign or a hand-lettered warning, not a wellness-brand headline font. No script, no serif, no soft rounded letterforms.

**What to avoid**: no photos of people, no gym imagery, no barbells/dumbbells/abs, no fitness iconography, no logo or wordmark anywhere in the frame, no gradient or glow effects, no glossy "ad" finish, no colors beyond black/gold/white, no tagline or subhead beyond the lines specified for whichever variant is being rendered, no border ornamentation. The result should look handmade and a little rough at the edges.
