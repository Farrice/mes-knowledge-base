## 1. The $10–20K/Month Math — Multiple Paths

The target: $10K–$20K/mo, solo, online-only. Below are six real combinations. Each is stress-tested against Cooz's actual time availability, ceiling, burnout risk, and premium-at-scale alignment.

### Path A — The McBroom Clone (commodity trap)

- **Price:** $497/mo
- **Clients to hit $10K:** 20 active
- **Clients to hit $20K:** 40 active
- **Delivery:** Weekly check-in video, program via TrueCoach, async messaging
- **Cooz hours/week:** Realistic 25–35 hrs at 20 clients (1–1.5 hrs/client/week of personal attention, plus programming, plus sales, plus content). At 40 clients, 50+ hrs/week. You become a full-time async responder.
- **Ceiling:** ~$25K/mo before delivery quality breaks without hires.
- **Burnout risk:** HIGH. This is the exact model McBroom built a team to escape. Solo version is grinder territory.
- **Ross McKay test:** FAILS. McKay's rule is "premium decoupled from price" — here premium IS the price. You are commodity 1:1. Against Cody McBroom's 13 years of brand equity at $497, Cooz has no edge.
- **Verdict:** Rejected. This is the commodity trap.

### Path B — The Hybrid Signature (current Cooz offer, stretched)

- **Price:** $5K per 90-day container (Cooz's current "Resurrection Protocol")
- **Cadence:** 90 days, so ~$1,667/mo per client over the container
- **Clients to hit $10K/mo equivalent:** 6 active 90-day containers running concurrently (2 new per month on rolling intake)
- **Clients to hit $20K/mo equivalent:** 12 active concurrent
- **Delivery:** 13 weekly calls (45–60 min), daily Voxer, TrueCoach, weekly metrics/photos, 8 Tenets curriculum
- **Cooz hours/week:** At 6 concurrent: ~6 hrs of calls + ~7 hrs of Voxer + ~3 hrs program review = 16 hrs/week delivery, leaving ~15 hrs/week for content, sales, podcast. Sustainable. At 12 concurrent: ~32 hrs/week delivery. Zero margin for sales, content, or rest.
- **Ceiling:** ~$12K/mo steady state before delivery cracks.
- **Burnout risk:** MEDIUM at 6 clients, HIGH at 12.
- **Ross McKay test:** Partial pass. The 90-day container is a "single SKU" (McKay's single-SKU focus principle). But at $5K/90 days, the price per hour of Cooz's time is weak.
- **Verdict:** Workable at the low end of the band ($10K/mo with 6 clients). Breaks above $12K without price increase.

### Path C — The Premium Container (price leverage)

- **Price:** $10K per 90-day container (return to original aspiration, but with delivery upgrades)
- **Cadence:** 90 days = ~$3,333/mo per client
- **Clients to hit $10K/mo equivalent:** 3 active concurrent containers
- **Clients to hit $20K/mo equivalent:** 6 active concurrent
- **Delivery:** Same weekly cadence as Path B, but ADD: monthly 90-min deep strategy sessions, labs/biomarker review quarterly, partner integration call (spouse if relevant), "Resurrection Summit" in-person option (optional upsell). The delivery doesn't need to double — the CONTEXT of delivery (who it's for, what it signals) has to double.
- **Cooz hours/week:** At 6 concurrent: ~20–25 hrs/week delivery. Real margin for authority work.
- **Ceiling:** ~$30K/mo at 9 concurrent clients. Delivery quality holds.
- **Burnout risk:** LOW-MEDIUM.
- **Ross McKay test:** Pass. This is "premium at scale" logic applied — same core SKU (the 90-day container), but positioned, priced, and delivered for the 9% of the market with expensive problems (Priestley Pattern 8). Fewer clients, more margin, more brand power.
- **Verdict:** STRONG candidate. This is the obvious direct path to $10–20K/mo.

### Path D — The Private Client Model (concierge)

- **Price:** $15K–$25K per 6-month engagement (or $2.5K–$4K/mo retainer)
- **Cadence:** 6 months with option to renew
- **Clients to hit $10K/mo:** 3–4 active
- **Clients to hit $20K/mo:** 5–8 active
- **Delivery:** Higher-touch. Biweekly 60-min calls, daily Voxer, quarterly on-site or travel-for-retreat option, labs, annual protocol, full concierge (restaurant menus, travel plans, supplement sourcing — Dan Go's play)
- **Cooz hours/week:** At 6 clients: ~22–28 hrs/week. High-touch but concentrated.
- **Ceiling:** ~$30–40K/mo with 10 clients, but delivery starts fraying above 8.
- **Burnout risk:** MEDIUM. Higher per-client intimacy = higher emotional load. Dan Go's positioning ("personal trainer + nutritionist + mindset coach + accountability partner + concierge all in one") is exactly this tier.
- **Ross McKay test:** Pass. This is the "Cadence beverage" principle — elevated delivery architecture against commodity price point competitors who are still selling weekly video check-ins at $497.
- **Verdict:** STRONG candidate. Highest revenue ceiling for a solo, but hardest to fill without existing authority.

### Path E — The Cohort + Intensive Hybrid

- **Price:** Group cohort at $1,500/month per seat (12-week cohort, $4,500 per cohort) + 2 private "Inner Circle" clients at $5K/90 days each
- **Math:** 6-seat cohort running = 6 × $1,500 = $9,000/mo + 2 private × $1,667/mo = $12,334/mo. Two cohorts running back-to-back (8 seats each, overlapping) = $24K/mo + private clients.
- **Clients to hit $10K/mo:** 6 cohort seats + 2 private
- **Clients to hit $20K/mo:** 12 cohort seats + 3 private
- **Delivery:** Cohort: 1 weekly group call (90 min), shared program, shared Voxer thread, community. Private: same as Path B.
- **Cooz hours/week:** Cohort amortizes delivery. 1 group call replaces 6 individual. Realistically 15–20 hrs/week for this combo.
- **Ceiling:** ~$30K/mo with 2 concurrent cohorts + 3–4 privates. Ceiling is higher than pure 1:1.
- **Burnout risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. BUT — cohorts require audience volume to fill. Priestley Pattern 4 (Signal Volume Engineering): cohort of 6 = 300 monthly signals needed. Cooz doesn't have that audience yet.
- **Ross McKay test:** Pass on leverage. Partial pass on premium — group coaching at $1,500/mo is a middle-tier price that can muddy the Resurrection Coach mythic brand.
- **Verdict:** Good at scale. Premature now — needs audience first.

### Path F — The Mythic 1:1 at Elite Price (the Priestley specificity play)

- **Price:** $25K per 90-day Resurrection (3–4 clients a quarter) OR $50K annual "Year of Resurrection"
- **Cadence:** 4 clients per quarter = 16/year at $25K = $400K/year ≈ $33K/mo average
- **Clients to hit $10K/mo:** 1.5 active
- **Clients to hit $20K/mo:** 3 active
- **Delivery:** The highest-touch imaginable. Weekly 90-min call, daily Voxer, quarterly 2-day in-person (Cooz travels to them or they come to him — still "online-only" primary delivery but the in-person "once per quarter" element is a premium signal, not the core delivery), labs, total concierge, family integration.
- **Cooz hours/week:** At 3 active: ~18 hrs/week delivery. Massive margin.
- **Ceiling:** Practical ceiling ~$50K/mo. Won't go higher without team. But sits comfortably above the target.
- **Burnout risk:** LOW on hours, MEDIUM on emotional load (deep coaching at this price means deep problems).
- **Ross McKay test:** HARD PASS. This is "luxury positioned above category noise" — exactly the Cadence play. The price itself becomes a filter that guarantees you only work with the 1% of Priestley's 1/9/90 market (Pattern 8) who will pay anything.
- **Verdict:** Highest-leverage path, but highest-authority prerequisite. Requires KPI status already built.

### Ross McKay's 4 Core Patterns — Translated for a Solo Body Coach

#### Pattern 1: "The 'Premium at Scale' Positioning" (McKay, genius.md)

**Direct quote:** *"They separate the concept of 'premium' from 'price point.' They build premium through design architecture, hyper-specific cultural associations (niche run clubs, elite athletes), and scientific data (500mg sodium), rather than high prices."*

**Translation for Cooz:**
- "Design architecture" = the visual language of the Resurrection Coach brand. Mythic, dark, masculine, clinical. The way an offer page LOOKS decides premium before price does.
- "Hyper-specific cultural associations" = Cooz should not market to "men who want to lose weight." He should market inside specific tribal contexts — founder communities, Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu academies at brown/black belt level, recovery community (ACA, men's work groups), high-performing trades (surgeons, fighter pilot alumni, tactical professionals). These are Cooz's "run clubs."
- "Scientific data" = Cooz's equivalent is **protocolized language**. Not "I'll help you get in shape." Instead: "200g protein/day, Phase 1 Stabilization, Voxer daily touchpoints, weekly AAR (After-Action Review), 8 Tenets curriculum." The specificity is the scientific over-index.

**What McKay would tell Cooz directly:** *"Your price isn't what makes you premium. Your shelf presence is. Right now your price is $5K — fine. But your shelf presence (website, voice, specificity) has to look $50K. When the two don't match, you look expensive. When they match, you look inevitable."*

#### Pattern 3: "Radical Single-SKU Focus" (McKay, genius.md)

**Direct quote:** *"They resist the urge to bloat the product line, understanding that manufacturing leverage comes from extreme volume on a single item... Put all capital and operational focus behind one SKU until it reaches a massive revenue threshold ($10M). Only launch tertiary products if they cement the core ICP."*

**Translation for Cooz:** This is the single most important McKay principle for Cooz's situation. Right now the Cooz catalog has:
- The Intensive ($4,997, 30 days)
- The Resurrection ($5K–$10K, 90 days)
- 8 Tenets framework
- Executive System Architecture
- Triage Audit (entry point)
- Body-led OS Reset

**McKay would slash this.** Single SKU: **The 90-Day Resurrection.** That's it. Nothing else sold until that hero SKU is humming at capacity. No 30-day sprint, no cohorts, no audits-as-products. The Triage Audit can exist as a *sales mechanism* (which it is), not a SKU.

**McKay's "SKU Scythe Protocol"** (Signature Move #3): *"Ross McKay will identify the single highest-potential SKU and propose cutting or severely deprioritizing all other products, regardless of minor existing traction, to funnel 100% of resources towards the hero SKU's path to $10M."*

**Applied to Cooz:** Kill The Intensive. Kill every side offer. One SKU. "The Resurrection." 90 days. One price. Ruthless focus until that SKU is at 6 concurrent clients minimum.

#### Pattern 2: "The 'Anchor Customer' Distribution Hack" (McKay, genius.md)

**Direct quote:** *"They treat distributors as mere box-movers... Do not pitch distributors. Pitch the end customer (Target, Walmart). Win the PO. Then take the PO to the distributor and dictate the terms."*

**Translation for Cooz:** In coaching, "distributors" = affiliate networks, agency marketers, lead-gen firms, dating apps for coaches (Arootah, Noom-adjacent platforms). "Anchor customers" = the actual buyer — the founder in crisis, the BJJ black belt post-injury, the recovering addict 2 years sober who doesn't know he's a dry drunk.

**McKay's play for Cooz:** Never route through platforms that commoditize the category (directories, BetterHelp-for-fitness, generic coach marketplaces). Go DIRECTLY to 5–10 "anchor customer" avatars, land 3–5 lighthouse clients, and use those lighthouse wins as the proof engine for everything else. Then if you later "distribute" (podcast guest spots, partnership with adjacent authority figures — recovery coaches, executive recruiters, men's work retreats), you go in with a case study that dictates terms.

#### Pattern 4: "Speed as the Ultimate Moat" (McKay, genius.md)

**Direct quote:** *"They operate with an unreasonable level of intensity that forces the market and team to keep up... If something takes a week, demand it in three days."*

**Translation for Cooz:** Cooz's biggest risk is NOT market size. It's drift. The authority flywheel, the book, the podcast, the new offer architecture — McKay would compress every single timeline by 50%. The book that's supposed to take 18 months, ship the first 3 chapters in 6 weeks. The 90-day offer restructure, ship in 14 days. Compression is the moat.

## 3. Daniel Priestley Oversubscribed — For a One-Coach Practice

**Load status:** Loaded fully. 27 genius patterns + 6 hidden knowledge items. Priestley's entire methodology is written for exactly this situation — a one-person expertise business that wants to become constantly oversubscribed without building an agency.

### How a Single Coach Becomes Constantly Oversubscribed — The 7 Priestley Patterns That Matter Most for Cooz

#### Pattern 1: The Demand Inversion (Priestley, genius.md line 13)

**Direct quote:** *"Focus on manufacturing WANTING, not improving quality. The sun doesn't revolve around your product; your product revolves around manufactured desire."*

**Applied to Cooz:** Cooz is spending cognitive energy on improving the product (more offer iterations, better framework docs, better website copy). Priestley would redirect that entirely. The question isn't "is the 90-day Resurrection a great product?" (It is.) The question is "how many founders woke up today wanting what Cooz specifically offers?" Right now the answer is "almost none, because nobody knows about him." Signal volume, not product refinement.

#### Pattern 4: Signal Volume Engineering (line 41)

**Direct quote:** *"You need 50 signals for every slot available. At 10 capacity, you need 500 people aware of your work monthly."*

**Applied to Cooz:** Cooz needs 6 concurrent clients in Path C = 2 new clients per month average = **100 signals (people aware of Cooz's work) per month minimum.** Reality check: with 837 LinkedIn connections, a paused LinkedIn strategy, one podcast with small audience, and a YouTube channel that is "most consistent" — Cooz is probably at 30–50 monthly signals today. He's underbuilt on signal volume by 2–3x.

**The math:** To hit Path C at $20K/mo (6 concurrent clients, ~12 clients per year intake), Cooz needs ~600 monthly aware humans. To hit Path D at $20K/mo (5–8 concurrent), he needs ~400 monthly aware humans. Neither is impossible — but neither is happening today. This is the real bottleneck.

#### Pattern 8: The 1/9/90 Market Architecture (line 76)

**Direct quote:** *"Every market has 1% with extreme problems (pay anything), 9% with expensive problems (pay premium), and 90% with cheap problems (price-sensitive). Your job is to find the 9%."*

**Applied to Cooz:** This is the single most important Priestley pattern for Cooz. The 90% = men who want to lose 20 lbs and care about the $/month. The 9% = founders whose bodies are collapsing while running companies worth $5M+, whose single bad decision costs them $500K, whose marriages are fraying. The 1% = C-suite executives with $10M+ liquid, board seats, public profile. 

**Cooz's target band IS the 9%** — Path C ($10K/90d) is explicitly 9% pricing. Path D ($15–25K/6mo) is upper 9%. Path F ($25K–50K) is 1%.

**Priestley's signature move (line 358):** *"The 'Stakes-Based' Pre-Qualification: Before discussing pricing with a prospect, Priestley will ask questions designed to uncover the financial, emotional, and strategic cost of not solving their problem."* Cooz's existing Triage Audit already does this. It just needs to be sharpened to surface stakes, not just symptoms.

#### Pattern 11: Official Capacity Declaration (line 103)

**Direct quote:** *"Scarcity is manufactured by declaring capacity publicly. 'I take 16 clients maximum' creates value that 'I'm available' destroys."*

**Applied to Cooz:** Cooz should publicly declare: **"I take 6 Resurrection clients at a time. When the 6 seats are full, there is a waitlist."** This should be on the website, in every podcast outro, on every YouTube video. The scarcity is real (it's his operational ceiling) AND it manufactures demand.

#### Pattern 12: The KPI 5-Part Framework (line 112)

**Direct quote:** *"Key Person of Influence status comes from: Pitch + Publish + Product + Profile + Partnership. Complete all five to dominate any domain."*

**Direct pull from the web research (see Research section above):** Priestley's "Key Person of Influence" book is literally this framework. Applied to Cooz:
- **Pitch:** Cooz needs a 30-second, 2-minute, and 10-minute pitch. NOT "I'm a body coach." Instead: "I help men in crisis reactivate the body so the mind can come back online. 90 days. 8 Tenets. Body-first." (Needs sharpening but direction is right.)
- **Publish:** The book ("The Path of the Parable"). This is already happening. It's the KPI publishing move.
- **Product:** The 90-Day Resurrection. One SKU.
- **Profile:** Website, podcast, YouTube, IG. Profile is the weakest leg today.
- **Partnership:** Podcast guesting, cross-promotions with adjacent authority figures (men's work leaders, recovery coaches, executive recruiters).

**All five must be in motion simultaneously.** If any leg is missing, KPI status doesn't compound.

#### Pattern 22: The "With or Without You" Energy (line 202)

**Direct quote:** *"Desperation repels. Abundance attracts. When you genuinely don't NEED any individual client, you become magnetic to all clients."*

**Applied to Cooz:** The April 2026 context file says Cooz dropped his price from $10K to $5K to $4K "until we get wins." **Priestley would flag this as the single biggest unforced error.** Dropping price signals desperation. The correct move at low pipeline is to RAISE price, reduce capacity, and tolerate 60 days of emptiness while you flood the top of the funnel with signals. Every single dollar discount is a $100 drop in perceived authority.

#### Pattern 25: The Specificity Principle (line 229)

**Direct quote:** *"Generic creates competition. Specific creates monopoly. 'Business coach' competes with millions. 'Revenue acceleration architect for B2B SaaS at $3-10M ARR' competes with almost no one."*

**Applied to Cooz:** "The Resurrection Coach" is better than "men's body coach." But it's still at a brand level, not a market level. The market-level specificity should be something like: **"I run 90-day physical operating system reactivations for founders in crisis who are winning on paper but losing in the mirror."** Or: **"I work with men who have built something real but are running on fumes — usually founders, sometimes recovery men, sometimes surgeons. 90 days. Body first."** That's the Priestley specificity tension — it should feel uncomfortably narrow.

## 9. The "Resurrection Coach" Brand Tension — Does Mythic Language Help or Hurt at $2K–$5K/Mo?

### The Honest Answer

**Mythic language helps at $10K+, hurts at $500–$2K, and is neutral at $5K.**

Here's why:

#### Why Mythic Language Helps at $10K+

At the $10K+/90-day tier, the buyer is NOT looking for a service. They're looking for a transformation. They've tried every $500/mo coach and are angry that nothing worked. They want someone who takes the work **seriously** — which at premium tiers means *mythically* seriously. The Resurrection Coach naming:
- Signals stakes ("this matters more than fat loss")
- Signals depth ("this isn't just physical")
- Signals filter ("not for dilettantes")
- Matches the buyer's internal monologue (Priestley Pattern 18 — The Private Conversation): *"I don't need another personal trainer. I need someone to bring me back to life."*

**The mythic brand at $10K is a match to the stakes.** A buyer paying $10K doesn't want "Dave's Fitness." They want "The Resurrection Coach." The language elevates, filters, and signals simultaneously.

#### Why Mythic Language Hurts at $500–$2K

At the commodity tier, the buyer is rational-brained, comparing specs. "Resurrection Coach" sounds like a cult next to "Cody McBroom — 13 years of clients, scientific approach, $497/mo." The mythic positioning at commodity price actively repels the rational buyer who's shopping on features.

#### Why Mythic Language Is Neutral at $5K

At $5K for 90 days, the buyer is in the middle — willing to pay, but still comparing. The mythic brand neither attracts nor repels decisively. This is the worst zone for mythic positioning.

**The implication for Cooz:** The Resurrection Coach brand works at $10K+ or gets compromised at $5K. Cooz's recent move to drop price to $5K/$4K is actively fighting his own brand. The brand wants a $10K+ price to feel correct.

### What Real Premium Coaches Do with Positioning

From the research:
- **Dan Go** — "High Performance Founder." Positioning is professional/functional, not mythic. Works at premium because of the *specific niche* (founders), not the language.
- **Michael Chernow** — "Kreatures of Habit." Playful/branded, slightly mythic. Works because of Chernow's celebrity chef backstory.
- **Ben Bergeron** — "CompTrain." Purely functional, no mythic language. Works because of CrossFit Games credibility.
- **Cody McBroom** — "Tailored Coaching Method." Purely functional. Works at $497/mo commodity tier.
- **Ultimate Performance** — Prestige brand positioning, not mythic. Works at luxury price through the "world-class private gym" element.

**Observation:** Most premium coaches use *functional* or *prestige* positioning, not mythic. Cooz is the outlier. This is NOT a reason to drop mythic — it's a reason to CHARGE for it. If you're going to position mythically, you have to price mythically. The two move together.

### The Movember Ethos Question

The Movember ethos (men in mental/emotional/physical crisis) adds weight to the mythic brand. This is NOT marketing dressing — it's the underlying gravity that makes "Resurrection" land. Without Movember ethos, Resurrection Coach sounds like a gimmick. With Movember ethos, it sounds like a vocation. **Priestley Pattern 15 — The Origin Story Power (line 139):** *"Your mess is their message received. Struggles create connection. Transformation stories create trust."* Cooz's Saturn return, recovery context, body-first philosophy — this is the Origin Story. It's the load-bearing beam under the mythic brand.

### Brand Pricing Resolution

- **$5K/90d with Resurrection Coach brand:** Incongruent. Price is fighting the brand.
- **$10K/90d with Resurrection Coach brand:** Congruent. Price matches the stakes language.
- **$15K–25K/6mo with Resurrection Coach brand:** Fully congruent. The brand gives permission for the price.
- **$25K–50K/year with Resurrection Coach brand:** Brand is the enabler. Without it, the price would be inexplicable.

**Honest conclusion:** Keep the mythic brand. Raise the price to match it. Never go below $10K for the 90-day container while using the Resurrection Coach name. The brand and the price must travel together or both weaken.

## 11. The Commodity Trap — Moves That Pull Cooz Back to Commodity Territory

These are the specific actions that would undo all the premium architecture above. Cooz needs to see them coming so he can refuse them in the moment.

### Commodity Move #1 — Discounting Below $10K

**Current April 2026 state:** Cooz dropped price to $5K, then effectively $4K with podcast listener credit.
**Why it's a commodity move:** Ross McKay's first principle is "premium decoupled from price" — but you CAN'T sell premium at a commodity price. The brand and price must travel together.
**The Priestley framing (Pattern 22):** *"Desperation repels. Abundance attracts."* Every discount is a desperation signal.
**The fix:** Hard-lock the Resurrection at $10,000 minimum. Never discount to the client. If the client can't afford $10K, they aren't the right client — refer them out (the proper Monk.Ai downsell, Pattern 5).

### Commodity Move #2 — Offering "Just a Program" or "Just Coaching Without the Full System"

**The temptation:** A prospect says "I just want the training program, not the full Resurrection." Cooz sells a $2K "just program" version.
**Why it's a commodity move:** Ross McKay's Pattern 3 (Single-SKU Focus). Line extensions dilute the hero.
**The fix:** One SKU. If someone wants "just the program," the answer is "I don't sell that. TrueCoach has apps. Or I can refer you to [X]. The Resurrection is the only thing I do."

### Commodity Move #3 — Adding More Calls or More Features to Justify the Price

**The temptation:** Prospect hesitates at $10K. Cooz adds a 4th call per month, more Voxer hours, more features.
**Why it's a commodity move:** This is "price competition" — adding more to justify the number. Real premium REMOVES, doesn't add. Ross McKay's "single SKU" and Priestley's Pattern 10 (Path of Least Resistance) both argue against feature-piling.
**The fix:** The Resurrection is what it is. If the price feels high, the answer is "the stakes are what's high. The price is calibrated to the stakes, not the hours."

### Commodity Move #4 — Selling Multiple Offers Simultaneously

**Current state:** Cooz has The Intensive ($5K) AND The Resurrection ($5K-10K) AND other framework products.
**Why it's a commodity move:** Ross McKay's "SKU Scythe Protocol" is explicit — one SKU until it hits capacity. Multiple offers fragment focus, confuse prospects, and dilute the brand.
**The fix:** Kill The Intensive. Kill every side product. One hero offer (The Resurrection). Period.

### Commodity Move #5 — Going on Generic "Fitness" or "Online Coaching" Platforms

**The temptation:** List on Arootah, Noom Coach, fitness coaching marketplaces, or any platform that aggregates coaches.
**Why it's a commodity move:** Ross McKay's "Anchor Customer" principle — don't pitch distributors. Platforms commoditize you by comparison. "Coach Cooz — 4.5 stars, $497/mo" next to 400 other coaches destroys all brand equity.
**The fix:** Direct relationship only. Owned audience. Podcast, newsletter, book, referrals.

### Commodity Move #6 — Accepting the Wrong Client "Just for Revenue"

**The temptation:** Cash is tight. A prospect who doesn't really fit offers to pay. Cooz says yes.
**Why it's a commodity move:** Wrong clients produce mediocre outcomes, which destroy the case study pipeline, which destroys signal volume, which destroys authority.
**The fix:** Priestley's "With or Without You" energy (Pattern 22). Have the discipline to walk away from a $10K check when the fit is wrong. This is the hardest discipline in premium solo work.

### Commodity Move #7 — Saying Yes to "Let Me Pick Your Brain for 15 Minutes"

**The temptation:** Someone asks for a free chat. Cooz says yes to be helpful.
**Why it's a commodity move:** Free brain-picking destroys the $497 Triage Audit positioning.
**The fix:** "Everything I do is structured. The entry point is the Triage Audit at $497. If we're a fit after that, the Resurrection is the next step. Otherwise, my podcast has 40 hours of free content on this."

### Commodity Move #8 — Explaining Instead of Diagnosing

**The temptation:** Prospects ask "how does your coaching work?" Cooz launches into the methodology.
**Why it's a commodity move:** Explaining features is commodity behavior. Premium coaches DIAGNOSE, they don't explain.
**The fix:** Priestley's Tension Triangle (Pattern 3). Every conversation starts with "tell me what's happening in your body right now" — the diagnosis surfaces stakes, and stakes justify price. Koe's C1 (Context) + C4 (Concerns) applied to human sales calls.

### Commodity Move #9 — Building a "Course" or "Digital Product"

**The temptation:** "I should productize my methodology into a course." This is the standard creator-economy advice.
**Why it's a commodity move:** At the premium tier, courses are downmarket. The Dan Go/Cody McBroom/Mark Carroll premium coaches DON'T primarily sell courses. Courses are the $200 tier, not the $10K tier. A course actively signals "I scale through digital, not through relationship" — which is fine if you're targeting the 90%, deadly if you're targeting the 9%.
**The fix:** The book IS Cooz's "digital product." The 8 Tenets framework is freely taught on the podcast. The premium offering is the relationship and the architecture around it, not a course.

### Commodity Move #10 — Hiring a Junior Coach to "Scale"

**The temptation:** Demand exceeds capacity. Obvious answer: hire a junior coach.
**Why it's a commodity move:** The moment Cooz delegates delivery, the buyer is no longer buying Cooz — they're buying the method, which is commodity. Dan Go built this out (he has a team). Cody McBroom built this out. Both had to build agencies.
**The fix:** Priestley's Capacity Declaration (Pattern 11). When oversubscribed, RAISE PRICE. Don't add coaches. At some point the price hits the Option 3 Mythic Elite tier and the capacity ceiling becomes luxurious, not limiting.

**The meta-rule:** Every commodity move above has the same structural shape: "scale by adding more of something." The premium answer is always "scale by concentrating on less, priced higher, felt deeper."
