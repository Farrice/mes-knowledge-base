# Proof Engine Plan — Claim-Safe AEO Ladder

Nobody in this niche publishes real numbers. Compliance consultants cite years-in-business. AEO agencies cite enterprise logos: Croud's 30% YoY organic lift for Thorne, Fermat's GNC reference, both at a scale Farrice can't claim and shouldn't try to. The lane nobody occupies is the honest, dated, SMB-scale result: three pilot audits, published on a schedule, before the numbers get big enough to be impressive. That's the raw material. This plan turns delivery into proof instead of letting it sit in a folder.

## 1. Pilot-to-Case-Study Pipeline

**Capture at intake (before the audit starts).** Add a consent line to the audit SOW, building on the founder-pilot terms already named in the offer doc:

- **Tier 1 (default, every pilot client):** anonymized aggregate metrics ("3 founder audits this quarter, average of X claims flagged, Y% of money-queries went uncited") may be published without further approval.
- **Tier 2 (opt-in, second sign-off after results land):** a named case study, quoted and attributed, may be published only after the client sees the draft and approves it in writing.

**Capture during delivery, per rung:**

| Rung | What gets measured | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Audit | Claim-risk count by bucket (before); citation snapshot, brand named on how many of 6 money-queries (before); one verbatim client quote from the intake or delivery call | Built into the `/claim-audit` and 3-engine spreadsheet SOP already |
| Install | Claim-risk count by bucket (after); citation snapshot (after); days to ship vs. the 10-day scope fence; one client quote on the delivered asset | Built into the sprint's before/after snapshot |
| Retainer | Weekly citation-check trend line rolled to monthly; cumulative claim-safe assets shipped; one outcome quote | Built into the weekly spreadsheet log |

Nothing here requires new tooling or new hours. It requires writing down what the delivery already produces and asking for the quote at the moment the client is happiest: right after delivery, not weeks later.

## 2. Publish-Real-Numbers Strategy

- **Teardown library (owned channel, no client names).** Screen-recorded AI-answer teardowns of public, named brands (Legion, LMNT, AG1) stay separate from client work. Per the refuter review, named-brand exposure content belongs on owned channels for education, never inside cold outreach. This is the top-of-funnel proof that the classifier works, published weekly regardless of pilot status.
- **LinkedIn.** Short-form, Tier 1 numbers first: "3 founder audits, here's what the claim-risk scan actually found." No client name until Tier 2 consent exists. This is where decision-makers see the methodology working before they're asked to pay for it.
- **Substack (Parallax-adjacent).** Longer process writeups: what a claim-safe audit actually finds in this category, why compliance-blind AEO agencies and content-blind compliance vendors both miss it. The methodology itself is the proof asset here; nobody else in the competitor sweep publishes their process at this depth.
- **Named case study, once Tier 2 exists.** One page, same shape as Croud/Thorne but honest about scale: shorter timeline, smaller numbers, real client, real quote. Never published before the client has approved the exact figures in writing.

Sequencing rule: teardowns and Tier 1 aggregate numbers can ship the day the audit closes. A named case study never ships ahead of the client's sign-off, even if the result is strong enough to be tempting.

## 3. Proof-Ladder Progression

**Teardowns → pilot aggregates → named results → category authority.**

Teardowns (public brands, owned channel) prove the classifier is real before a single client has paid. Pilot aggregates (Tier 1, all 3 slots) prove the delivery holds at small scale without naming anyone. A named case study (Tier 2, at least one of the 3) proves a real funded brand said yes and got a measurable before/after. Category authority is the compounding state that follows: once 2-3 named results exist, the pattern itself becomes the citable thing, quoted in outreach instead of asserted in it. Each rung is load-bearing for the next; skipping to named case studies before the teardown library exists means asking strangers to trust a claim with nothing public backing it.

## 4. 30/45-Day Scoreboard

Kill gate (binding, council-set): **45 days of genuine contact effort, zero paid audits and zero decision-maker calls, falsifies the thesis.** The weekly indicators below exist so trajectory is visible without reopening the strategy conversation every week.

| Week | Publish | Outreach | Pipeline target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 teardown + 1 LinkedIn post naming the offer | 10 named-brand exposure hooks sent to decision-makers | 0-1 replies |
| 2 | 1 teardown | 10 more hooks (20 cumulative) | 1+ decision-maker call booked |
| 3 | 1 teardown + Tier 1 aggregate draft started | 10 more hooks (30 cumulative) | 1 audit sold |
| **4 (Day-30 check)** | 1st Tier 1 aggregate post published | 30-40 cumulative hooks sent | **2-3 audits sold, at least 1 delivered.** If this line is thin, the fix is more outreach volume, not new positioning. |
| 5-6 | Ongoing teardowns; case-study draft if Tier 2 consent lands | Outreach continues at the same weekly rate | 3 audits sold, 1+ install started |
| **Day 45** | — | — | **Kill check: if zero paid audits and zero decision-maker calls by this point despite genuine weekly contact effort, the thesis is falsified per the council's binding criteria.** Anything short of zero-and-zero is a live thesis, even if slower than hoped. |

The Day-30 line is a trajectory read, not a kill gate: thin numbers there are a signal to push harder on outreach volume inside the existing plan, not a cue to re-litigate the offer. Day 45 is the only hard stop. It fires on contact effort plus outcome together.
