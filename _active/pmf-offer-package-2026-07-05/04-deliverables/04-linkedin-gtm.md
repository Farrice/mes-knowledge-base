## Post 1 — The creatine-gummy wake-up

**Hook:** A supplement brand's gummies tested at 0.005 grams of a 5-gram claim. The product still carried a 4.6-star rating.

**Body:**
Independent testing on six popular creatine gummy brands this year found four delivering close to none of the creatine on the label. One brand, Happyummmm, listed 5 grams per serving. The lab result came back at 0.005 grams. A thousandth of the claim.

The Amazon reviews never caught it. All six brands sat between 4.4 and 4.9 stars, including the ones that failed. Reviews measure taste, texture, whether the gummy dissolved right. They don't measure the ingredient.

That gap between what a product says and what it does used to be a lab problem. In 2026 it's a marketing problem too. The FTC is treating unsubstantiated dosing claims the same way it treats disease claims: enforceable, in what the agency itself is calling its most active health-claims year yet.

If your copy states a number, someone needs to be able to defend that number before it ships.

Most brands find out the hard way. The ones that check first don't end up in a post like this one.

## Post 3 — The 2026 FTC pattern

**Hook:** The FTC filed two health-claims cases in five days this June. Neither company saw it coming.

**Body:**
FTC v. Amare Global Holdings landed June 17. FTC v. TruHeight landed June 12. Golden Sunrise got hit back in January. Three enforcement actions in one year against health and wellness brands, in what the industry is calling the most aggressive health-claims enforcement stretch the agency has run.

Two changes matter most for a funded brand's copy right now. A 2023 rule closed the old "results not typical" escape hatch — a disclaimer under a testimonial no longer cures a claim the testimonial itself can't support. And the FTC-FDA memo now pulls influencer and social content into the same review standard as your website. The UGC brief your team approved last month carries the same exposure as your product page.

This is a marketing-side observation, not a legal opinion, and I'm not your attorney. It means: read last quarter's ad copy and testimonials, flag anything that outruns what you can prove, hand the flagged list to counsel.

Two filings in five days is this year's warning shot. Read last quarter's copy before the next one names you.

Two vendors, two invoices, and a gap between them nobody's covering.

## Post 5 — The Reddit-seeding story

**Hook:** In June, a Reddit community with 830,000 members got locked to new posts because sellers wouldn't stop gaming it.

**Body:**
404 Media and TechSpot reported that peptide and hormone-therapy companies flooded r/biohackers with coordinated posts, working to shape what Google's AI Overviews and Perplexity say when someone asks about their category. It worked well enough to matter — Reddit has been the single most-cited domain across AI Overview and Perplexity answers for the better part of two years. Then moderators noticed the pattern and shut new posts on the topic down entirely.

That's the current state of AI-search visibility in supplements and wellness: the fastest lever is also the one that gets you locked out of the room.

There's a slower version of the same goal that doesn't require astroturfing a subreddit. Publish claims you can actually defend. Structure them so an AI engine can lift them cleanly. Let citation build the way SEO used to before anyone learned to game it.

Nothing under a defensible claim gets found when someone finally checks it.
