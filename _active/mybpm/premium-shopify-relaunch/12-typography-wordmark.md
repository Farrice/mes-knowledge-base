# MyBPM Typography Gate — The Beat Gap

## Status

Photography V3 is **LOCKED**. The white-premium material system is **LOCKED**. This board defines the next single visual contract: how MyBPM speaks and signs its name.

## Decision this board serves

Does this wordmark behavior and typography hierarchy give MyBPM a serious, ownable voice across the storefront, garments, labels, packaging, and Night Society editorial—without copying Kith, relying on a novelty rave font, or sacrificing commerce readability?

## Verdict

Use one disciplined grotesk family for identity, editorial scale, and commerce. Add one monospaced utility layer for time, drop, garment, and sound-system notation. Make the brand recognizable through one wordmark behavior: **the beat gap**.

The system should feel like a gallery masthead interrupted by one measured beat.

## Wordmark concept

### Primary mark — `MY BPM`

- Always uppercase.
- Built from a custom-drawn, heavy grotesk base inspired by Archivo Black.
- `MY` and `BPM` remain one name but receive a calibrated internal gap of approximately `0.16em`.
- The gap is the identity device. It suggests one count of silence without drawing a waveform, equalizer, heart monitor, or music note.
- Primary production form is one-color black or white. It must not require the accent color to remain recognizable.

### Signal form — `MY■BPM`

- A small oxblood square may occupy the beat gap in campaign, packaging-interior, or editorial applications.
- The marker is approximately the optical area of the `B` counter, not a full-height divider.
- Use it rarely. The accent remains within the locked 5% material ratio.

### Compact notation — `MY/BPM`

- Utility-only signature for neck labels, hardware, timestamps, product codes, and narrow formats.
- Never replaces the primary storefront wordmark.
- Slash remains black/white except in rare campaign details.

### Production status

This board defines behavior, proportion, and hierarchy. The primary wordmark remains a **concept**, not a final trademark-cleared vector. Final production requires custom outline drawing, optical kerning, small-size testing, embroidery testing, and a basic trademark/confusion screen before publication.

## Typeface system

| Role | Typeface | Weight | Purpose |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Wordmark base | Archivo Black, custom outlined | 900 | Starting skeleton only; final mark must be redrawn |
| Display / editorial | Archivo | 800–900 | Short cultural statements, drop titles, campaign chapters |
| Commerce heading | Archivo | 600–700 | Collection names, product headings, navigation moments |
| Body / interface | Archivo | 400–500 | Product facts, descriptions, policies, buttons, forms |
| Utility / notation | IBM Plex Mono | 500–600 | Drop codes, timestamps, SKU, material, care, edition, captions |

Archivo and IBM Plex are available under the SIL Open Font License. Preserve their license files if font binaries are self-hosted in the eventual theme. The custom wordmark must use original outlines and must not be distributed under either typeface name as a modified font.

## Hierarchy

| Level | Desktop | Mobile | Line height | Tracking | Case |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Wordmark | optical, not typeset | optical, not typeset | n/a | custom | Uppercase |
| Campaign display | 112–180 px | 54–72 px | 0.80–0.84 | `-0.075em` | Uppercase, 3–8 words |
| Editorial H1 | 64–112 px | 46–64 px | 0.86–0.90 | `-0.055em` | Uppercase or sentence case |
| Section H2 | 36–56 px | 30–42 px | 0.94–1.02 | `-0.035em` | Sentence case preferred |

## Behavioral rules

- Use large all-caps for short cultural statements only. Never set long paragraphs, policy copy, or product descriptions in all-caps.
- Keep display lines short enough to preserve the hard, compressed silhouette.
- Left alignment is the default. Centering is reserved for the wordmark and rare garment placements.
- Product titles use controlled title or sentence case; pricing and conversion controls remain plain and immediate.
- Utility mono is information, not decoration. Every code, date, time, or specification must mean something real.
- The beat marker may appear once per composition. It does not become a bullet system, pattern, or confetti device.
- Motion, when eventually used, is a single entrance or count. No looping pulse, bouncing letters, equalizer animation, or strobe behavior.
- Body copy stays at least 16 px on commerce surfaces and maintains accessible black-on-white contrast.

## Surface applications

### Website

- Primary black wordmark in the white header; reversed white version only over approved Night Society imagery or black chapters.
- Large display language opens editorial moments, not every section.
- Product grids use quiet commerce type so garment names, price, fit, and purchase action stay legible.
- Mono labels organize drop number, environment, date, product material, and edition.

### Garments

- Primary wordmark: left chest `3.25–4 in`, center chest `8–11 in`, or upper-back `10–12 in` depending on design role.
- Compact notation: neck print, hem label, sleeve detail, or hardware engraving.
- Beat gap must remain visible at embroidery and woven-label scale; if it closes, use the compact notation instead.
- Typography can support both minimal pieces and maximal back graphics because the identity layer stays consistent.

### Packaging

- One-color wordmark on bright uncoated white stock.
- Mono production ledger for style, drop, material, size, and care.
- Oxblood beat marker only on an interior seal, label edge, or one reveal moment.
- Blind deboss uses the primary one-color form without the accent square.

## Elimination rules

- No Kith-style script or signature imitation.
- No generic techno, sci-fi, cyberpunk, racing, or festival-display font as the main identity.
- No waveform, heartbeat, equalizer, music-note, headphone, or play-button logo.
- No over-condensed body copy or illegible product information.
- No serif added merely to simulate fashion luxury.
- No dot-matrix overload, fake coordinates, meaningless serial numbers, or decorative technical language.
- No repeated giant all-caps slogan in every website section.
- No wordmark that requires oxblood, animation, texture, or a background effect to work.
- No final logo publication before custom vector drawing, small-size proofing, and confusion screening.

## Decision language

- `LOCK TYPOGRAPHY` — preserve the beat-gap wordmark behavior and build the product-image standard next.
- `REVISE TYPOGRAPHY: [specific hierarchy, font role, or wordmark behavior]`
- `ELIMINATE: [specific lockup, type treatment, or usage]`

The typefaces on this board are reference/build tools. The shown wordmark is a directional concept, not a finished trademark or production master.
