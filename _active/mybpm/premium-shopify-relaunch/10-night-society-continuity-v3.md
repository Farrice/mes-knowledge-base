# MyBPM Photography Gate V3 — One Identity, Three Environments

## Status

Photography V2 is **locked at the brand-idea level**:

- Night Society remains the permanent visual world.
- Afterimage Archive remains the emotional thesis.
- PLUR beyond the dance floor remains the purpose.
- The white premium commerce environment remains locked.

V3 refines execution before material and texture work begins.

## Decision this board serves

Can the same MyBPM person recognize themselves—and the same brand—in festival life, club/nightlife culture, and daily life?

## Verdict

The prior “afterimage” territory became too gritty and visually separate. It looked like another photographer, another customer, and potentially another brand.

The corrected system has three equal environments:

1. **Festival / Open Air** — collective scale, daylight expression, movement, care, and complete looks.
2. **Club / Nightlife** — intimate dance-floor proximity, deep practical light, controlled flash, visible faces, and garments that remain readable.
3. **Daily Life / City** — clean natural light, modern architecture, friendship, work, errands, listening, and calm confidence.

Afterimage is no longer a separate environment. It is connective tissue: coat check, doors, rides, food stops, rooftops, blue hour, and first daylight. These transition frames use the same polished visual standard as the three primary territories.

## Signature system

**One identity. Three environments.**

Each campaign follows the same recurring cast and hero looks through all three settings. The environment changes; the person, values, and garment identity do not.

## Continuity contract

- **Recurring cast:** four to six recognizable people across a season; avoid one-off anonymous models.
- **Recurring wardrobe:** three to five complete hero looks reappear across festival, club, and daily-life frames.
- **Lens family:** 35mm environmental frames and 50mm human proximity throughout; avoid switching to distorted club ultrawides.
- **Grade baseline:** natural skin, soft black, controlled white, graphite, muted oxblood, and midnight blue. One environmental color may appear without repainting the entire image.
- **Lighting translation:** open-air sun or overcast at festivals; practical light plus softened direct flash in clubs; window light or open shade in daily life.
- **Composition rhythm:** one full look, one medium human interaction, and one evidence detail per environment.
- **Behavior:** each environment contains visible peace, love, unity, or respect without staging the acronym.
- **Product clarity:** garments remain readable even at night. Atmosphere may never erase fit, silhouette, or fabric.

## Nightlife correction

Nightlife should feel immersive, intimate, and culturally credible—not grimy, chaotic, voyeuristic, or costume-driven.

- Faces remain identifiable.
- Black levels hold detail instead of crushing into mud.
- Motion blur is selective, not the entire visual language.
- Flash is softened or bounced enough to preserve skin and fabric.
- Club color is limited to one practical hue per frame.
- The camera stays inside the group rather than surveilling an anonymous crowd.

## Image balance

- **34% Festival / Open Air**
- **33% Club / Nightlife**
- **33% Daily Life / City**

Transition and afterimage frames are captured inside these three shoots rather than treated as a separate campaign world.

## Elimination rules

- No gritty underpass or wet-pavement imagery as the default afterlife.
- No muddy club darkness, blown-out flash, or unusable product visibility.
- No different cast, styling logic, or grade for each environment.
- No luxury lounge posing that removes actual dance culture.
- No anonymous crowd spectacle or stage-only photography.
- No daily-life imagery so clean that it loses personality and community.
