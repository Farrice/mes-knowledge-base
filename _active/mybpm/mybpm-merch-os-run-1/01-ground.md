# Phase 0 — GROUND

## The Sub-Identity Person

**Saturation Check**: EDM/rave culture is hypercompetitive (Insomniac, Ultra, Tomorrowland, festival merchandise, streetwear brands). Saturation = demand proof ✓

**Sub-Identity Gap**: Who does the market miss?

### The Raver as Identity Keeper (not Party Participant)

**Behavioral moments that define them:**
- They remember the exact moment PLUR clicked for them (specific set, specific person, specific conversation)
- They build real friendships with people they met once for 4 hours; those friendships stick year-round
- They wear PLUR in their regular life (work, home, online) — not just at festivals. It's a values anchor, not an aesthetic
- They trade kandi with strangers and still think about that person months later
- They go to 2–5 events a year and each one is a pilgrimage, not a party
- They know the difference between genres (House ≠ Hardstyle ≠ Dubstep ≠ Trance) because the VIBE is different and the community is different

**The identity statement they want to make:**
*"I'm the kind of person who chooses love over judgment. I believe in genuine human connection. My rave experience changed how I see people."*

**Why brands miss them:**
- Existing rave merch treats them as party-goers (hype-focused, visual-aesthetic-focused, generic festival references)
- They miss the *emotional ownership* — the shirt is not a party costume, it's a values statement
- Generic design tropes (neon + trippy visuals) don't signal "I know what this actually means" — they signal "I think raves look cool"

**Familiar bank (what they already know):**
- PLUR: Peace, Love, Unity, Respect (the four pillars)
- Kandi: beaded bracelets traded at events (represent values, friendships, promises)
- Flow arts: poi, hula hoops, LED fans (the physical practice of presence)
- Genre as identity: House = love/euphoria, Hardstyle = community/power, Dubstep = chaos/catharsis, Trance = transcendence, Techno = minimalism/precision
- The rave as a safe space where "normal" social rules don't apply
- The feeling of being truly seen by strangers (PLUR in action)
- The specific disorientation of walking out of an event at dawn and re-entering "normal" society

**Billboard statement:**
"A person who found family in a dark room with strangers and never stopped believing that's possible."

## Constraints (Inherited from Brand)

- **IP**: No licensed tracks, artist images, or event names (avoid trademark + licensing complexity). Original designs only.
- **Print method**: POD (Bonfire/Printful standard for t-shirts; hats/tanks/joggers on their preferred vendor). 
- **Territory**: Global — PLUR transcends borders; no geographic limits needed.
- **Banned elements**: Nothing that violates PLUR principles (exclusion, judgment, hate). Every design must *embody* the four pillars, not just reference them.
- **Brand voice**: Warm, inclusive, self-aware (humor about rave culture), never gatekeeping or cynical.

## Mirror-or-Poster Framing (Pre-Concept Check)

**NOT**: Generic rave aesthetics (neon, trippy, "festival mode" art). These are *posters* (decoration). Brands already do this.

**YES**: Specific behavioral moments, identity statements, the emotional truth of why someone goes to raves. These are *mirrors*.

Examples of mirror concepts (don't execute yet — just framing):
- "I traded kandi with you once and I think about you every time I shuffle"
- "I found people who chose me back"
- "My personality is Peace, Love, Unity, and Respect"
- "The dawn after the best set of my life"

Examples of poster concepts (will be killed at intake):
- Generic neon aesthetic
- "Rave mode activated" / "Rave life" (broad, no identity)
- Cool-looking designs with no identity resonance
