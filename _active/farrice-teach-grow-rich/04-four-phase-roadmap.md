## How to read every phase

Each phase below answers Omar's four questions in order:
1. **The teach** — what Farrice teaches at this phase (one thing per asset).
2. **The mechanism** — content / web class / fulfillment / next teach.
3. **The metric** — the signal that says *this phase is actually working* (not vanity).
4. **The unlock** — the milestone that earns the next phase. Do not advance before it fires.

The HOW-reconciliation rule governs disclosure across all four: **title in "how to," deliver WHAT/WHEN/WHY/WHERE (+ negations) in the free/transient phases (1, parts of 2), and reserve actual HOW for the paid, anyone-can-do-it container (3).** *"The context in which the content is consumed determines the value of the content."* Free is not gatekept — it is calibrated.

## PHASE 0 — Mine the IP (the rung nobody skips)

This sits before lead gen because you cannot teach to magnetize until you have something only you can teach. Omar: *"How do you make content no one can copy? You make content only you can make."*

**The teach (to yourself first):** the Client Rolodex. *"List out every single client you had... what situation they were in, what they did, and then what happened... find the thread in it to see — you have a framework in there."*

**Build sequence:**
- [ ] Open a doc (or your own AI). List every S&C client and every person you have helped articulate/ship knowledge — including yourself. For each: situation → what they did → what happened. [FARRICE: do this raw, 20+ entries. This is the single highest-leverage hour of the build.]
- [ ] Find the thread. The repeating move across the wins IS your framework. You almost certainly already run a *"subconscious formula"* (Omar's term) — you just haven't named it.
- [ ] Mine the alphabet (Myron's $110K gem — UNCONFIRMED, Omar's account): *"for every letter of the alphabet create frameworks and formulas for your expertise."* Don't do 26. Do three: name your S&C method, your AI-knowledge method, and the bridge between them. [FARRICE: draft three named frameworks. Alliteration helps recall; a drawing helps more — *"our brains are always looking for a picture."*]
- [ ] Draw each one. One whiteboard photo per framework. The drawing is the obsession signal.

**The metric:** you have ≥2 named, drawn frameworks you can teach from memory, each backed by ≥3 real client stories. If you can't draw it without notes, you haven't mined it yet.

**The unlock → Phase 1:** at least one framework named, drawn, and tied to a story you can tell in 90 seconds. Without this, Phase 1 produces generic advice anyone could give — *"commoditized volume builds nothing."*

One long-form per month minimum. [FARRICE: pick your long-form home — YouTube or Substack. You already have Parallax; if it carries this audience, use it. One channel, deep.]
- **Short-form (LinkedIn / IG) → empower to ONE next thought.** Omar via Dan Martell: *"empowered to go and attack [the thing]... empowered meaning one thing."* Each post shifts one belief, names one piece of the WHAT, and stops.

**Mechanism — the content pipeline (only-you-can-make):**

## PHASE 2 — LEAD CONVERSION (teach what the business can do for them, and they buy)

> Omar: *"you teach them about what your business could do for them. And then they buy."* Still mostly free/low-friction context. The job is belief-shift, not pitch. *"Making it seem easy IS the close."*

**The teach:** the three belief-shifts — **possible · easy · trust.** *"You shift beliefs by making people feel like it's possible, by making people feel like it's easy, by making people feel like they can trust you."* For the Invisible Expert these are precise:
- **Possible:** "You already have the IP. You don't need to become an influencer." (Dissolves the identity threat — the core block in your ICP.)
- **Easy:** "You talk, the system translates." The make-it-easy move pre-sells: *"it's going to be easy if I work with them."*
- **Trust:** you are the Invisible Expert who escaped — proof, not promise. *"Prove to people you can help them by actually helping them"* (Frank Kern, via Omar). Your free content already helping them IS the trust.

**Build sequence:**
- [ ] Set the objective for EACH touch — master class objective ≠ challenge-day objective ≠ Day-5 offer objective. Omar: *"if you don't know why you're teaching, you won't know what to teach."*

## PHASE 3 — FULFILLMENT (whatever they bought, you teach them — NOW the HOW)

> Omar: *"then whatever they bought, you then teach them."* This is the paid, committed, anyone-can-do-it container. **This is the only phase where you teach actual HOW.** *"When somebody pays you, they'll be more receptive of the how-to."* The post-transaction consumer pays attention differently — *"where your treasure is, there your heart is also."*

**The teach:** the full implementation HOW you parked in Phases 1–2 — your prompts, your system architecture, your S&C programming, your translation method, delivered step-by-step in a context where attention is paid for. For the Invisible Expert client, fulfillment is the literal *"you talk, we translate"* engine running on their expertise. [FARRICE: define your delivery format — cohort calls? async loom reviews? a workshop? Omar even flies people to Vegas and *"shows them everything"* — and warns they'll still get it wrong, so build for repetition, not one-shot transfer.]

**The discipline that prevents churn:** fulfillment fails not from under-delivering but from over-teaching. Omar: over-teaching ≠ over-delivering. Drowning a paying client in everything-at-once overwhelms them — *"an overwhelmed person never takes action."* Keep delivery belief-led, one implementable thing at a time. *"It's always mindset"* — wrap each tactic in the belief-shift that lets it land, or the client won't execute it.

**Build sequence:**
- [ ] Sequence the delivery so each session is ONE implementable thing → client implements → returns with data → next thing. *"There is so much revelation in implementation."*
- [ ] Deliver the HOW openly now — no gatekeeping inside the paid container. The free content earned the right; the payment created the attention.
- [ ] Over-deliver on value while teaching one thing at a time. The goal: a client who feels they got a fortune and ships results.
- [ ] Capture the win the moment it happens — it becomes Phase-1 content and a Phase-4 ascension trigger. Each fulfilled client *"defied the odds"* and adds a story to the rolodex only you can make.

**The metric (the phase is working when):** clients *implement and get results* (good soil produces a harvest — *"those who do with what they hear"*), and they refer / re-buy. Specifically:
- Client implementation rate (are they doing the homework?). Low implementation = you're over-teaching, not the client failing.
- Testimonial / case-study yield per client. This feeds the pricing flywheel: results → testimonials → premium clients.
- Refund/confusion rate near zero. Confusion = fire hose; fix the delivery, not the product.

**The unlock → Phase 4:** at least one client who got a real result and would say yes to a next level. *"Fulfillment sells the next thing — deliver so well they renew."*

**The teach:** the next-level teach for graduates. The Invisible Expert who got visible now needs scale, a deeper system, or the *other* asset (your S&C ↔ AI-systems bridge — a graduate of one becomes a lead for the other). [FARRICE: name your ascension offer — what does a successful client buy next? A retainer? A higher-tier mastermind? The second framework? Omar's machine works because *every* graduate has a next rung; design it before you need it.]

**Mechanism — the next teach:** a higher-context, higher-price container (mastermind, retainer, or done-with-you build). Same teach-to-sell physics, raised one level. Omar's whole edifice: *"at this point, we run ads, we teach. The group that came to the teach jumps into the next teach."* For you, run from existing clients first — they already trust you and have results.

**Build sequence:**
- [ ] Design the ascension offer BEFORE your first cohort graduates (so there's a next rung waiting).
- [ ] Teach the next level to graduates as their natural step — they self-select, you don't pitch. The belief-shift is *"you've proven this works; here's the next ceiling."*
- [ ] Feed every graduate's win back into Phase 1 as your most uncopyable content — closing the flywheel.

**The metric (the phase is working when):** graduates re-buy and you have a predictable next-offer take-rate. Omar's end-state: *"it's crazy how predictable it can get if you just keep at it"* — and his model needs only *"a few hundred people."* Your solo math: one offer can carry you (Omar: *200 × $5K = $1M*; your cold-start version — *e.g., 20 clients × $3K = $60K*, then raise price as the play maxes). [FARRICE: write your first 12-month revenue target and the simple math that gets there. Keep it one offer until it's maxed — *"have you maxed out the play you're already running?"*]

**The unlock → next cycle:** a working flywheel — Phase-4 graduates and their wins become Phase-1 magnets. Then you *"just run the play"* again, raise the price, improve execution. You do not chase a new platform, offer, or funnel until this one is maxed.
