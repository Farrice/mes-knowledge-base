## #2 — SUNDAY 4AM ↔ MONDAY 9AM

**Prompt (primary):**
Create a premium black streetwear T-shirt design for MyBPM called "Sunday 4AM / Monday 9AM". The buyer is the raver sitting in a Monday morning stand-up meeting whose body is still quietly running last night's warehouse set — coworkers oblivious. The design is a minimal typographic split: "SUN 04:00 AM" and "MON 09:00 AM" stacked or mirrored, with ONE continuous thin heartbeat/kick-pattern trace running underneath both timestamps, connecting them — the same pulse under both lives. Electric blue trace on black, white timestamps, generous negative space, chest-center placement. Small "MY.BPM" mark only. The design should feel like a quiet inside joke in office-safe clothing: restrained, premium, collectible. Avoid: DJ decks, headphones, crowd silhouettes, neon chaos, glitch effects, office clip-art (laptops, coffee cups), festival clichés.

**Variants (rendering only — person locked):** (V2) timestamps side-by-side split-screen with a hairline divider · (V3) tiny calendar-grid motif where only SUN and MON cells carry the pulse trace.

**Composition brief (Satori lens):** one focal system (the dual timestamp + trace), horizontal eye-flow left-to-right ending on the pulse; passes the 5-word test ("two timestamps, one heartbeat").
**Type brief (Kittl lens):** timestamps in a tabular/mono numeral face (boarding-pass energy), trace hand-drawn-imperfect; legible at thumbnail.

## #1 — STILL SYNCED / 03:17 AM (prior-art direction, refined)

**Prompt (primary):**
Create a premium black streetwear T-shirt for MyBPM called "Still Synced". The buyer is the person leaving a 3:17 AM warehouse set whose body still feels locked to the kick pattern after the lights come up — half asleep on the walk to the car, fully synced. Center the design on a precise pulse-grid graphic: thin electric-blue BPM gridlines, magenta phase-shift markers, and a subtle green heartbeat trace that feels like music data becoming body memory. Integrate small text: "MY.BPM" and "STILL SYNCED / 03:17 AM". Modern rave identity, not EDM decoration: restrained, wearable, high-contrast, collectible, one main graphic system with enough negative space to look premium on a real shirt. Avoid: DJ decks, crowd silhouettes, festival clichés, random neon chaos, overcomplicated fractals, smiley faces, mushrooms.

**Variants:** (V2) the pulse-grid compressed into a small left-chest "vital signs monitor" card with big 03:17 AM underneath · (V3) back-print version — full pulse grid between shoulder blades, front left-chest timestamp only.

**Composition brief:** grid = texture, timestamp = focal point; the eye lands on 03:17 AM first or the 50ms gate fails.
**Type brief:** clinical mono numerals against organic trace lines — the tension IS the concept (data vs body).

## #5 — RESTING HEART RATE (5-genre family)

**Prompt (primary — Dubstep 140 first, family-consistent system):**
Create a premium streetwear T-shirt design for MyBPM called "Resting Heart Rate: 140". The buyer is the dubstep loyalist whose smartwatch thinks something is wrong and whose body knows nothing has ever been more right. The design mimics a minimal fitness-tracker vitals card: "RESTING HEART RATE" in small clinical label type, a giant "140" as the dominant element, and an EKG heartbeat trace that morphs mid-line into a dubstep kick-and-wobble pattern. Below in small text: "BPM: NORMAL (FOR ME)" and "MY.BPM". One color accent per genre on black (dubstep: electric green). Clinical-meets-warehouse aesthetic: restrained, tongue-in-cheek, collectible. The number must be readable across a room. Avoid: actual medical logos, smartwatch brand UI, skulls, neon chaos, DJ equipment, crowd scenes.

**Family system:** same layout, number + accent color swap per genre — HOUSE 124 (warm orange) · TECHNO 130 (steel grey-blue) · TRANCE 138 (violet) · DUBSTEP 140 (electric green) · HARDSTYLE 150 (red). One Shopify PDP, 5 variants — the system IS the collection cohesion.

**Composition brief:** the number is the design; everything else whispers. **Type brief:** oversized condensed numerals, clinical micro-labels; genre name appears ONLY in the variant selector, never on the shirt (the number is the inside joke — if you know, you know).
