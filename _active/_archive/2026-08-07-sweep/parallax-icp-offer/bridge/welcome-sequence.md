# Parallax Welcome Sequence (3 Emails)

**Principle (Tyler Denk):** The moment after opt-in is the highest-intent moment. Capture buyer-qualification data when the reader is warm. Do NOT waste this sequence on generic "welcome to the newsletter" filler.

**Principle (Grace):** Every email has a district job. Email 1 is Trust (over-deliver on the first touch). Email 2 is Trust (authority proof via story). Email 3 is Revenue (soft invitation — still indirect).

**Voice rules:** No AI tells. No "Here's what" openers. Max 2 em dashes per email. Reader-as-protagonist. Would-Farrice-say-this-to-a-friend test on every line.

**Sequencing:** Email 1 fires immediately post-subscribe. Email 2 fires Day 3. Email 3 fires Day 7. These sit alongside the regular Tuesday/Thursday publication cadence, so subscribers read ~2 Parallax editions during the welcome window.

### Why this email works

**Trust district job:** Email 1 over-delivers before asking for anything. The Interest Audit prompt pack is a tangible asset the reader can run tonight. That's the first Tyler Denk "zero-cost referral lever" — the asset itself is shareable.

**Post-Subscribe Intelligence Capture:** The one question — *"what are you trying to see differently this quarter?"* — is the qualification filter. Avatar 01 answers something about their publication. Avatar 02 answers something about their practice. Avatar 03 answers something about the mission. The answers self-sort the list.

**Voice test:** *"I'll stop emailing you. Both are fine."* is a line Farrice would actually say. No funnel-speak.

**Reader-as-protagonist:** The reader is the one with the stone in their hand. Farrice is the one asking the question. Writer enters as the inquirer, not the subject.

**No hard CTA:** No "book a call." No "upgrade to paid." The CTA is a reply or a 3-question survey — both feel editorial, not transactional.

**Word count:** ~260 words (reader-friendly length).

## Email 2 — Day 3

**Subject:** The teacher who ran a 9-year nonprofit nobody had heard of

**Preview text:** What he didn't have wasn't a content problem.

**From name:** Farrice Cain

---

**Body:**

Javier Payano is a music teacher at Lane Tech in Chicago. He's still in the classroom every Monday morning. He also runs Human Values Collective — a character-education nonprofit he's been building since 2017, which the 3Arts Foundation recognized with a Make a Wave award.

Nine years of work. Real classroom outcomes. A program — Sunday Dinners, where parents and teachers eat together and talk about raising kids — that does something most programs don't.

A website visitor couldn't tell what HVC did in five seconds. The homepage scored 1/3 on the StoryBrand quick-test. The most compelling program in the portfolio had zero digital footprint. Seven podcast episodes in four years, 476 total listens. Five different things a visitor could click. No path.

He didn't have a content problem. He had an *articulation* problem.

And articulation problems are the ones experts can't fix for themselves, because they're the ones who know the work too well to see how a stranger hears it.

I spent 9 days rebuilding HVC's positioning from the inside. Three parallel research agents. 100+ web queries. 30+ sources. Then I built nine interconnected documents — each one feeding the next. Grounding document → brand foundation → StoryBrand rewrite → Grace blueprint → newsletter flywheel → growth strategy. The homepage went from "Five CTAs, no path" to "the parent is the hero, HVC is the guide."

Javier called the result truly magical.

But the real lesson wasn't the nine documents. The real lesson was this:

> **A 9-year-old organization with real outcomes and no distribution is not a marketing problem. It's a translation problem. The work is already there. The words aren't.**

I've been thinking about that a lot.

How many people reading this have real work that nobody has heard of — because the articulation layer was never built?

No CTA. Just a question. Reply if you want.

Talk Thursday.

— Farrice

*Parallax — see everything from more than one angle.*

---

### Why this email works

**Authority proof via story, not via claim:** The email never says "I'm good at this." It tells the Javier story and lets the reader make the inference themselves. This is the Grace Andrews move — the story IS the proof.

**Parallax voice, not sales voice:** Told in the same register as the Manifesto or the AI Emotions edition. Specific details (Lane Tech, 3Arts, 476 listens, 1/3 on StoryBrand quick-test). No superlatives.

**The closing question is a mirror, not a hook:** *"How many people reading this have real work that nobody has heard of?"* — this is the Lulu Articulation Gap. It names the feeling before the reader has words for it. It does NOT say "if that's you, here's my offer."

**The landing page link is absent — by design:** If Email 2 had a CTA, it would break the Trust district frame. The reader who resonates with this story will find their way to the landing page on their own (via Edition 05 soft-CTA, the footer link in any edition, or Email 3).

**Word count:** ~330 words.

### Why this email works

**Revenue district job, done indirectly:** Email 3 is the first (and only) welcome-sequence email that explicitly names the offer. It does so in the same editorial voice as the newsletter itself — no subject lines like "YOUR LAST CHANCE," no urgency manufacturing, no benefit-stacking.

**"A handful of readers have already written back asking":** This is social proof by frame, not by claim. Positions the offer as *demanded by readers* rather than *pushed by Farrice*.

**Three-tier presentation, compressed:** The three tiers get one sentence each. No comparison table. No feature list. The landing page does the detailed sell — the email only needs to open the door.

**The Javier question is the close:** Same question from the offer one-pager, same question from the case study. Consistency across surfaces. Readers who have been following the thread recognize it.

**The soft exit:** *"If not, no worries — next Parallax edition drops Tuesday"* gives the reader permission to stay on Parallax without feeling pitched to. This is the retention protect.

## Email Sequence Summary

| Email | Day | District | CTA | Residue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Immediate | Trust | Reply with one question (or short survey) | *"He asked me a real question. And he sent a prompt pack."* |
| 2 | Day 3 | Trust | Reply if the question lands | *"That guy built a real thing for a real teacher. He sees articulation problems other people don't."* |
| 3 | Day 7 | Revenue | Visit landing page (if curious) | *"He finally told me what he sells. It sounds like what I need. Let me look."* |

The sequence works for subscribers who NEVER see LinkedIn or any other Parallax channel. It's self-contained. The ICP offer never gets hard-sold. The reader who buys after Email 3 is buying because the story has been told in editorial voice, not because they've been funneled.

## Avatar-Match Variants (advanced — Phase 2)

If the Post-Subscribe Survey identifies an Avatar match, the welcome sequence can branch:

- **Avatar 01 (Stalled Substack Operator):** Email 2's Javier story gets swapped for a Michelle-Welsford-style publication story (when a real subscriber case study exists). Email 3 leads with Crystal tier.
- **Avatar 02 (Signal Bleeder):** Email 2 gets a consultant-practice story (when one exists). Email 3 leads with Architecture tier.
- **Avatar 03 (Mission Founder):** Email 2 keeps the Javier story (it's the Avatar 03 match anyway). Email 3 leads with Launch tier.

For launch, use the single Javier version across all avatars. Branch variants ship in Phase 2 after 10+ buyers produce usable case studies.

## Voice Check (mandatory before sending)

For each email, read aloud and ask:
1. Is there an em dash count >2 anywhere? — cut.
2. Is there a "Here's what / here's why / here's how" opener? — rewrite.
3. Could this have been written by any newsletter consultant? — rewrite (fails One-of-One).
4. Does any sentence sound like Farrice talking at a prospect instead of to a friend? — rewrite.
5. Is the reader the protagonist of the email, or is Farrice? — if it's Farrice, flip to reader.
