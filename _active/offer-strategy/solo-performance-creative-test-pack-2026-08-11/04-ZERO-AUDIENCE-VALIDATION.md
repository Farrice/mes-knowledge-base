# Zero-Audience Validation Plan

## Objective

Collect the first paid pilot without pretending that owned audience, content engagement, or a polished offer document equals demand.

The acquisition order is:

1. Existing-demand marketplaces
2. Warm introductions
3. Controlled teardown-led outbound
4. Public content as proof exhaust, not the primary dependency

No outreach should be sent without explicit approval. The copy below is prepared, not deployed.

## Step 1: Build one specimen

Choose one DTC health or performance brand with:

- A public Meta Ad Library footprint
- One clear SKU
- Enough existing ads to identify repetition
- Public product, review, and category material

Produce a reduced specimen:

- One-page public-input account read
- Three creative hypotheses
- Three finished sample statics
- One test map

Label it clearly:

> Speculative demonstration built from public information. No private account data was used, and no performance result is claimed.

Do not make the full 15-ad pack for free.

## Step 2: Enter existing demand

### Marketplace search terms

- `creative strategist Meta ads DTC`
- `performance creative strategist UGC`
- `Meta ad creative briefs`
- `DTC static ad creative`
- `creative testing framework ecommerce`
- `AI video creative strategist Meta`

### Qualification filter

Apply only when the buyer:

- Explicitly mentions Meta, TikTok, or paid social
- Requests concepts, hooks, briefs, finished assets, testing, or iteration
- Has an existing product and active campaigns
- Shares a real budget or documented spending history
- Values performance evidence, not only visual style

### Proposal

> You do not need another designer waiting for requests. You need someone who can read the account's existing signal, decide the next meaningful change, and turn that decision into assets your media buyer can launch.
>
> I would start by mapping your current winners and repeated patterns, then build five distinct test hypotheses and turn them into 15 finished Meta statics. Each asset maps to a specific learning question, so the next batch produces decisions, not just more files.
>
> I have attached a speculative sample showing the structure. It uses public inputs and claims no performance result.
>
> Founding pilot: $1,000 prepaid [MODELED], one SKU, seven business days, one revision round, and one post-launch readout.
>
> If you send your product page and three current ads, I will tell you whether the account has enough signal for this sprint before you hire me.

Do not claim DTC case studies that do not exist. Lead with the specificity of the approach and the quality of the specimen.

## Step 3: Request warm introductions

Send this only to people who already know Farrice's judgment or work:

> I am testing a fixed creative service for established health and performance brands already running Meta ads.
>
> I take their current ad data and customer language, identify five materially different things to test next, and deliver 15 finished static ads plus the testing map in seven business days.
>
> I am looking for three founding pilots at $1,000 [MODELED]. Do you know one founder, growth lead, media buyer, or agency owner who already spends on paid-social creative and might give me a candid look?
>
> I am not asking you to endorse results I have not produced. An introduction for fit-checking is enough.

The last sentence protects the relationship and keeps the ask truthful.

## Step 4: Run a small outbound test

Do not blast a list. Select ten brands that visibly meet the qualification criteria.

### Prospect evidence required

- Brand and product
- Meta Ad Library link
- Observable repetition or coverage gap
- Named decision-maker or agency owner
- Why the pack fits now

### Message

> I looked through [Brand]'s current Meta creative. Most of the live ads appear to cluster around [observable pattern], while [specific audience/message/proof gap] looks under-tested.
>
> I mocked up one test direction as a concrete example. It is based only on public information, so I am not claiming it will outperform your controls.
>
> If the diagnosis is useful, I run a seven-day pack that turns five test hypotheses into 15 finished statics and a launch map. Want me to send the one-page example?

Permission-first replies are not sales. Count them separately.

## Step 5: Publish proof exhaust

After each specimen or authorized client delivery, publish one lesson:

- The repeated pattern the account was overusing
- The missing creative variable
- The difference between a cosmetic variation and a learnable test
- One before/after brief or first-frame decision
- The limits of the test and any result it did **not** prove

The purpose is to make judgment inspectable. Do not wait for content to create an audience before pursuing buyers already in market.

## Thirty-day operating cadence

### Week 1

- Finish one specimen
- Submit five qualified marketplace proposals
- Request five warm introductions

### Week 2

- Submit five more qualified proposals
- Send five permission-first teardown offers
- Hold fit conversations and record objections verbatim

### Week 3

- Complete ten more qualified exposures across the channels that produced substantive replies
- Sell and begin the first pilot if fit exists
- Do not rewrite the whole offer based on one objection

### Week 4

- Deliver or complete the first pilot
- Record economics and buyer comprehension
- Decide: keep, revise one component, or kill the motion

## Scoreboard definitions

- `qualified`: meets the written buyer and problem criteria
- `sent`: the offer or proposal reached the buyer
- `reply`: any response
- `substantive reply`: question, objection, referral, request for details, or fit discussion
- `held`: an actual fit conversation occurred
- `sold`: buyer agreed to scope and payment
- `collected`: money received
- `delivered`: assets handed off
- `launched`: buyer placed assets into an active campaign
- `repeat`: buyer purchased or requested the next batch

## Decision gates

These are operating rules, not predicted conversion benchmarks.

- **After 10 qualified exposures:** if there are zero substantive replies, revise targeting or the opening, not the entire service.
- **After 20 qualified exposures:** if there are zero held conversations, pause the motion and interview buyers before producing more assets.
- **After 20 qualified exposures and several held conversations:** if there are zero sales, diagnose trust, proof, scope, and price separately.
- **After the first three deliveries:** if buyers do not launch, the service is failing at usability even if they praise the work.
- **Before any retainer:** require a repeat purchase, an explicit continuity request, or two buyers asking for the same recurring need.

## Discovery questions

1. How many new creatives do you launch in a typical month?
2. Who decides the next production slate?
3. Which three ads currently carry significant spend?
4. Where does your team feel stuck: research, concepts, copy, production, approvals, or learning from results?
5. How much internal time and external spend does the current pipeline consume?
6. Which product and audience need the next test?
7. Can you launch a new batch within seven days of delivery?
8. Who owns claims approval?

If the buyer cannot answer questions 2, 3, 6, or 7, do not force the sale. The service depends on a functioning launch and learning environment.
