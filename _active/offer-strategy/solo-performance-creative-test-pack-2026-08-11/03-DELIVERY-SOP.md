# Delivery SOP: The 15-Ad Creative Test Pack

## Operating objective

Deliver a test-ready batch that creates five distinct learning opportunities without exceeding solo capacity. Preserve source integrity, claim safety, and interpretability. Do not drift into media buying, full-funnel strategy, UGC management, or unlimited production.

## Capacity model

- One active client at a time
- Target personal workload: 12–18 hours per pilot
- One SKU
- Five concepts
- Fifteen meaningful static variants
- One revision round
- One readout

If the first three pilots repeatedly exceed 18 hours, the scope or price is wrong. [MODELED capacity gate] Do not solve the problem by silently working more hours.

## Intake gate

Do not start the delivery clock until all required inputs exist.

### Required

- Product page and checkout path
- Brand kit: logo, fonts, colors, visual rules
- Product images or permission to create agreed AI-assisted imagery
- Recent creative-level results or top-five/highest-spend and bottom-five/weakest creative screenshots
- Meta Ad Library link
- Existing customer reviews, survey data, or authorized customer material where available
- Approved claims, disclaimers, and substantiation boundaries
- Primary goal for this batch
- Named approver and named launch owner

### Intake classification

Label every input:

- `SUPPLIED`: directly provided by client
- `OBSERVED`: visible in a public or authorized source
- `INFERRED`: strategist interpretation
- `UNKNOWN`: missing or not supported

No inferred customer quote, testimonial, result, or claim may appear as fact in final creative.

## Phase 1: Account and evidence read

### Output: one-page account read

Record:

- Highest-spend and strongest-performing creative patterns
- Weakest or fatigued patterns
- Current coverage by persona, message, proof type, format, offer, and funnel job
- What the account cannot currently tell us
- One primary coverage gap the pack should close

Avoid generic conclusions such as “test more hooks.” Name the actual missing test.

## Phase 2: Customer and category signals

Review only authorized or public sources:

- Client reviews and testimonials
- Product-page language
- Customer comments and support language supplied by the client
- Competitor ads and product pages
- Category-native formats and trust cues

Create a short signal ledger with source link or client-file reference. Preserve unusual verbs, rituals, objections, and social details. Do not clean the life out of customer language.

## Phase 3: Build the five-bet slate

Each concept card must contain:

1. Concept ID
2. Audience/situation
3. Awareness level
4. Funnel job
5. Customer or account evidence
6. Message hypothesis
7. Visual opening-frame direction
8. Three meaningful variants
9. Required proof or disclaimer
10. The single learning question

The five concepts should create coverage diversity. They should not be five executions of the same feature claim.

## Phase 4: Write and design

### Copy check

- A cold viewer can understand relevance without knowing the brand
- The headline states or earns a clear message
- Product features connect to an audience consequence
- Claims stay inside supplied evidence
- Each variant changes one purposeful persuasion variable
- CTA matches the landing-page job

### Visual check

- First takeaway is legible in one second
- Eye path is intentional: hook, qualifier or proof, product, then action
- Product remains recognizable
- Layout survives agreed placements
- Visuals feel native to the audience rather than generically polished
- AI-generated imagery is inspected for product, body, text, label, and anatomy errors

### Silent-view check

Hide the logo and supporting copy. Confirm that the intended viewer can still tell why the ad might be relevant. If not, revise the first frame.

## Phase 5: Handoff

Recommended folder structure:

```text
/01-account-read/
/02-signal-ledger/
/03-test-map/
/04-master-ads/
/05-placement-exports/
/06-readout/
```

Filename convention:

```text
[concept-id]_[variant-id]_[placement]_[short-message].png
```

Every filename must map back to the test map. The media buyer should not have to guess which asset tests which hypothesis.

## Revision boundary

The buyer submits one consolidated revision request within two business days.

Included:

- Copy clarification
- Brand-compliance corrections based on supplied rules
- Agreed format corrections
- Minor visual adjustments

New scope:

- New product or SKU
- New target audience
- Replacement of an approved concept
- New claims, testimonials, or source material
- New creative format
- Additional revision rounds

## Post-launch readout

Before interpreting results, record:

- Launch date
- Spend and impressions by creative
- Account baseline where available
- Whether the media buyer isolated variables sufficiently
- Any campaign, audience, offer, or landing-page changes that confound the read

Label the conclusion:

- `SUPPORTED`: enough directional evidence for the next decision
- `MIXED`: signal conflicts or confounds exist
- `INSUFFICIENT`: not enough delivery, spend, time, or isolation
- `UNKNOWN`: data unavailable

Never call an ad a winner because the client likes it or because it received early clicks.

## Pilot receipt

After every delivery, record:

- Collected revenue
- Personal hours
- Tool costs
- Number of client questions
- Revision count
- Assets launched
- Time to launch
- Buyer-valued element
- Buyer-confusing element
- Performance state
- Repeat purchase requested: yes/no

The service becomes repeatable only when the receipt shows it can be delivered profitably and understood by the buyer.
