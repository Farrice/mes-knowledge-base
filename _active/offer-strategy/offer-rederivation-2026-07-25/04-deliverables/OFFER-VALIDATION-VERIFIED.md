# Offer Validation: Verified Findings

**Depth:** deep · **Date:** 2026-07-26 · **Stage:** verify phase plus synthesis, run on the corpus at `.tmp/research/offer-validation-deep/` · **Prior label:** RECON-GRADE (finder-labeled, no independent attack)

Twenty load-bearing claims came out of twelve findings files. Each was attacked by a separate refuter that had not found it, briefed to break the claim rather than confirm it, and told that silence grades UNCONFIRMED. The verdicts came back 6 VERIFIED, 5 LIKELY, 8 UNCONFIRMED, 1 REFUTED.

---

## The finding

**The price band survived. The buyer behavior did not.**

Claims about what competitors *charge* held up when their live pages were re-read today. Claims about how buyers *behave*, meaning reply rates, audit-to-retainer conversion, channel reach, and whether AI has absorbed static production, came back unverified, downgraded, or refuted.

So the pricing decision is safe to make on this corpus. The go-to-market decisions are not. They are hypotheses to test with live sends, not conclusions to plan a runway around.

Three claims survived verification with their numbers intact and their *reasoning* dead. Those sit in the section below on what verification broke, and they are the sharpest material here.

## What survives, and what it licenses

The $950 to $1,200 band is real as an asked price. Two direct comps were re-read today and matched word for word: CREVARI at $1,000 for a research artifact carrying a credited-to-pilot term (https://crevaricreative.com/), and DTC.SUPPLY at $950 for 15 finished statics (https://dtc.supply). Both refuters flagged the same limit independently. Neither site carries named clients, testimonials, or any third-party footprint. A live price shows the price is *asked*, not that anyone pays it.

That gives a deliverable benchmark: 15 statics at $950 (https://dtc.supply). Five to eight statics at the same price would be under-scoped against the closest comp.

The credited-audit-to-retainer structure turns out to exist across three sellers spanning two categories. Veza runs $4,500 credited toward an $8,000/mo retainer (https://www.vezadigital.com/services/ai-search-visibility-audit), and the refuter found MV3 Marketing and Allocera on 30-day credit terms. None of the three publishes conversion data. The structure is available to copy. Its conversion rate is not a known quantity.

GEO stays parked, now on primary-source evidence rather than a secondary write-up. SparkToro's own report puts AI Mode at 0.34% of searches against a 68.01% zero-click rate (https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/). AI Overviews are reported separately there at 20%+ of searches, so the two are not conflated at the source. No commercial-intent breakdown exists, which is the one cut that would matter for a supplement buyer.

## What verification broke

Three claims came back with the specifics intact and the inference dead. This is why the verify phase was worth running.

**1. Meta's own numbers argue for automated variation, not human angle briefs.** The 11% CTR and 7.6% conversion lift are verbatim on Meta's page (https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-creative-advantage-unlocking-the-power-of-diversification-with-meta-andromeda). Both figures measure Meta's *own AI image-generation feature*. The corpus used them to argue that brands need more distinct human-researched angles. Read at the source, they support the opposite: Meta's tooling now generates the variation itself. The 3-to-50 creatives case study turns out to be one vendor testimonial, not tied to Andromeda at all.

**2. The "tools display, humans analyze" differentiation is gone.** Motion and Foreplay prices confirmed verbatim (https://www.foreplay.co/pricing), and both now ship AI-generated insight rather than raw display. Motion has an AI brain, AI tags, automatic iteration-finders, and scheduled reports. Foreplay has an AI script generator, emotional analysis, and persona detection. A manual teardown cannot be differentiated on the analysis axis against a $59/mo subscription. Differentiation has to rest on judgment, context-specificity, or depth, and that case has not been built yet.

**3. LinkedIn as a route to DTC brand-side buyers is REFUTED.** Visible comment counts of 10 to 23 against a self-reported 36K following work out to a 0.03% to 0.06% engagement rate, and the visible commenters read as media buyers and growth marketers, meaning peers rather than brand-side buyers (https://www.linkedin.com/in/daradenney/). Her YouTube reach runs 3x to 4x her LinkedIn reach, so LinkedIn is not her own lead channel either. This claim is dropped from the evidence base and appears here labeled REFUTED.

Two downgrades also change numbers already in circulation.

On pricing: the $2,000/mo floor is not a market floor. A live Upwork posting for a DTC performance-ads creative strategist sits at $1,500 (https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Creative-Strategist-DTC-Performance-Ads-Part-Time-Full-Time_~022064891501473411734/). Denney's own quote could not be re-read, since YouTube blocked transcript retrieval.

On conversion: the audit-to-retainer planning number drops. The 6-or-7-in-10 figure has no readable source and no denominator. The better-supported independent range is 40% to 60% (https://theblueprint.training/agency-pricing-models/), itself seller-reported. Roast My Landing Page's roughly 1-in-4 is the more conservative anchor, and that product is defunct: the domain redirects now and its copy has gone past tense (https://wayfront.com/case-studies/oliver-meakings-landing-page-roast). Plan on 25% and treat anything above it as upside.

## Structural limits of this corpus

**Reddit is fetch-blocked to this crawler.** Five of the twenty load-bearing claims (A4, A5, A6, C2, C3) rest on Reddit threads that no refuter could re-read. Those claims are not merely uncorroborated. Their primary sources are unreachable, so they cannot be promoted later without a different capture method. Any Reddit-sourced number in the working notes should be treated as a lead, not a datum.

**The Meta Ad Library buyer filter is not independently reproducible.** This is the sharpest operational finding in the run. The refuter reached the live library and confirmed Omni Creatine as a real active US advertiser with specific Library IDs, but could not obtain a per-advertiser active-ad total for any named brand. The public interface exposes no per-page count, the page-ID lookup returned no matching ads, and a keyword check on Legion returned about five results against a claimed 330. Keyword search undercounts, so this is inconclusive rather than disproof. Even so, establishing one brand's true count took over fifteen minutes, which already contradicts the 10-minute claim. The 15-active-ads buyer filter is a method that has not been shown to repeat, and it is the operational spine of prospecting. Fix the counting method before building a prospect list on it.

**The empty-slot positioning bet is the corpus's own synthesis, not an attested finding.** Twelve queries across agency sites, Fiverr, Upwork, Whop, Gumroad, Contra, Dribbble, and Behance surfaced near-misses only: Diligent Studios at $2,700/mo (https://www.diligentstudios.com/), merren.io competitive creative audits at $5K to $15K, Angled at $5K to $10K/mo. Each sits either above the threshold or on monthly rather than one-time terms. That supports LIKELY rather than VERIFIED. X and LinkedIn DM offers and Stan-store products went unswept, and one counter-example would refute it.

**Three claims deferred by the contract cap, not dropped.** Extraction surfaced 23 distinct load-bearing claims and the depth contract caps verification at 20. Deferred: C1 (the "who will actually work on my account" vetting filter), C4 (SF Marketing at $7,500 and ProductQuant at $2,997 for one-time sprints), C7 (Profound's B2B-weighted customer wall). All three stay finder-labeled and carry no verified status.

## What this document does and does not license

Decide now on the price point and the deliverable scope. Size the sprint against 15 statics at $950 and price the research artifact against $1,000, both re-read today.

Treat every reply-rate, conversion-rate, and timeline number as a hypothesis and test it with live sends. Independent benchmarks put cold-email reply rates at 3.4% to 5.8% overall and around 4.2% for case-study-structured campaigns (https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/). Those figures assume the sender *has* a case study. No benchmark surfaced for a sender with no proof in the vertical, which is the actual starting position. The 500-sends-per-day account cannot be re-read, and it would burn domain reputation at solo scale regardless, where the safe limit runs 25 to 50 sends per day per mailbox.

Rework the differentiation story before it goes into copy. Two of its three supports inverted under verification (Meta's angle-scarcity argument and the tools-versus-humans distinction), and the third is refuted (LinkedIn reach).

Do not cite: the 13-brand ad-count list, the 750/820/720/330 counts, the $2,000/mo floor as market fact, 6% to 8% reply rates, 60% to 70% audit conversion, $20/static as the market anchor, or Meta's lift figures as evidence for human angle research.

### Counterpoint worth holding

Here is the strongest case against this document's caution. Asked prices on live pages are the one class of evidence that verified cleanly, and pricing is the decision with the shortest feedback loop. A first live send answers the reply-rate question inside a fortnight for the cost of an afternoon, which is cheaper than another research round. The risk in over-reading these limits is treating "not yet verified" as "do not proceed." The evidence supports proceeding on price while instrumenting the mechanics, and that is a different instruction from waiting.
