## 2. What the swarm is actually for

Five questions. Every one is empirical, and none can be answered from the existing artifacts, which is exactly why those artifacts failed.

**Q1. What are these buyers purchasing right now, in what package, at what price?**
Real evidence only: live agency and freelancer service pages, published rate cards, job postings, marketplace listings. Not "industry typical." Named sources or it doesn't count.

**Q2. Does the hollowed-out-by-AI pain have budget attached?**
The POV is emotionally true. The open question is whether "our marketing sounds like everyone else's now" is a line item anyone pays to fix, or a feeling nobody funds. If it's the second, the POV stays as the content wedge and the offer sells something else. That distinction decides the whole run.

**Q3. What sells at a speed a solo operator can actually collect on?**
Productized services in the range a DTC or SaaS buyer approves without a procurement cycle, deliverable by one person in under a week. This is the 18-day constraint made into a research question.

**Q4. Who already runs teardown-as-marketing in these verticals, and what happens after the teardown?**
The teardown is decided. The mechanism that converts one into money is not. Find operators doing it, map their actual funnel, locate where it breaks.

**Q5. What does a first buyer need to see from someone with no case studies in their vertical?**
The portfolio-with-metrics gate is real and named in his own prior research. Find how solo operators clear it without prior client results.

## 3. Output contract

Three paths. Each one carries every field or it isn't a path:

- **Buyer**: specific enough to build a list from
- **The problem, in their words**: sourced from real language, not inferred
- **Mechanism**: why this works, stated so a skeptic can attack it
- **Deliverable**: what physically ships, and in how many days
- **Price**: with the evidence behind the number, or marked PROJECTED
- **Proof required to sell it**, and whether Farrice can produce that in 18 days
- **First send**: named channel, named list-building method, a message he could send Monday
- **Falsification test**: the cheapest thing that proves this wrong, and the kill number

Plus, mandatory:

- **A claim ledger.** Every load-bearing claim gets VERIFIED, LIKELY, or UNCONFIRMED with its source. That discipline saved the Path A brief and is not optional here.
- **Dissent preserved.** If agents disagree on which path is strongest, the disagreement ships intact. No blending to consensus.
- **A named loser.** At least one path the research kills, with the reason. A run that likes everything found nothing.

## 4. Scale and shape

Roughly ten to twelve agents across three phases. Not a fleet, not a token burn.

**Phase 1, evidence sweep (parallel, ~6 agents).** One per research question above, plus one running the DTC and B2B verticals separately on Q1. Each returns sourced findings with URLs and no synthesis.

**Phase 2, path construction (~3 agents).** Each builds one candidate path from the Phase 1 evidence, working from a different angle: fastest-to-cash, highest-ceiling, best POV alignment. They don't coordinate, which is what keeps the three genuinely different.

**Phase 3, adversarial pass (~3 agents).** Each tries to kill one path, prompted to refute rather than grade. A path surviving a real attempt is worth something. A path nobody attacked is a wish.

**Budget discipline:** WebSearch and the free tier only. Perplexity is dead, no paid APIs. A question that can't be answered free comes back UNCONFIRMED rather than guessed.

## 5. How this run fails

Naming the failure modes in advance, because the last four offers all came out of a process that felt exactly this productive.

1. **Three beautiful paths, none sent.** The only defense is that every path ships with a Monday-executable first send. A path that can't name one isn't a path.
2. **A dead offer comes back wearing new vocabulary.** Guard: the Dead row in § 1 gets checked against every output.
3. **Q2 gets answered by assumption.** The hollowing-out pain is emotionally compelling, which makes it easy to assert budget that doesn't exist. Q2 takes a hard VERIFIED-or-UNCONFIRMED verdict, no soft language.
4. **Research comes back instead of an offer.** The output contract is the guard. Anything without a price, a first send, and a kill number is incomplete.
5. **The run becomes the work instead of the unblock.** One sitting. If it overruns, ship what exists and go.
