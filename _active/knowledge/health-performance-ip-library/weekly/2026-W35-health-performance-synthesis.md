## Audience-state movement

The buyer is trying to reduce decision load while remaining self-directed. On
Thursday, buyers increased creatine doses on their own after creator content,
often without naming a brand. On Friday, a current stack photo became a
370-comment joke while brands launched daily products that compress several
jobs into one routine. The buyer wants autonomy and fewer steps at the same
time. That rewards products that are easy to use and messages that are easy to
judge.

## Promises-not-kept pattern

The category keeps turning a buyer's desire for clarity into a larger
specification. Thursday's answer was a bigger dose. Friday's answer was more
jobs inside one scoop. Both are easy to print and easy to compare. Neither
settles what the product should own after a competitor matches the number or
ingredient list.

Strongest buyer line: “This is why people eventually simplify.” It is community
text, not a medical verdict.

## Repeated buyer fears

- I am taking too much and cannot tell what matters.
- I am paying for decorative doses or better branding.
- I want fewer steps without giving up the benefit I care about.
- I do not know which claim deserves belief.
- A creator or community may explain my stack more clearly than the brand.

## Source-backed claims and weak claims

Strong enough to use with attribution: P&G's filed acquisition amount; P&G's
public rationale; live product-page ingredients, prices and comparisons; dated
launch reporting; exact community text read in context; NIQ and PwC findings
reported as published research.

Keep out: claims that multi-benefit products work worse; clinical judgments
about ingredient combinations; claims that broad formulas necessarily perform
worse in marketing; company ARR treated as audited; AI impressions treated as
recommendations or sales.

## Positioning and demand read

The Angle Map is strongest when the product already has too many defensible
truths. Three angles and one lead recommendation become more valuable as
formulation breadth increases.

Demand is forming around decision compression. New brands are spending
formulation money to simplify routines. Mature capital is buying trust systems.
The service opportunity sits between them: decide which belief the product
should own before another creative round turns the formula into a list.

## Education synthesis

Formula architecture asks what can coexist safely, legally and usefully in the
product. Message architecture asks which buyer, occasion and belief should
lead. One system can be broad while the other stays narrow. Confusing them makes
the ingredient list perform the strategist's job.
