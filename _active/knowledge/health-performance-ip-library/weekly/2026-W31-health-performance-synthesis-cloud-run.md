## Repeated Buyer Fears

- "This brand's website organizes itself by product, not by the actual moment I'm standing in when I need an answer." (07-27)
- "A study got flattened into a scary headline about my supplement, and the brand said nothing." (07-28)
- "My reviews page has a discount popup on it. Is that a compliance problem I didn't know about?" (07-29)
- "The lab paperwork I'm pointing to was earned on a different format than the one I'm actually shipping." (07-30)
- "If a big store carries it, it must have been checked." (07-31)

The throughline across all five: a buyer or founder is leaning on a proxy for trust (a wearable reading, a headline, a review count, a COA, a retail endcap) that was never built to answer the safety or efficacy question they're actually asking it.

## Weak Claims To Avoid

- Treating a retailer's decision to stock a product as a safety or clinical review (07-31).
- Framing a review-request incentive as fine so long as it isn't labeled an incentive (07-29).
- Assuming a "third-party tested" badge automatically covers every format a brand ships, gummy included (07-30).
- Treating a single flattened or contradictory headline as settled science in either direction (07-28).
- Stating that ashwagandha broadly "causes liver damage," or overcorrecting into "the case-report signal is nothing to track" (07-31).
- Making an AI-search citation claim without live observation, screenshots, or prompt logs this week (standing rule, no exceptions surfaced).
