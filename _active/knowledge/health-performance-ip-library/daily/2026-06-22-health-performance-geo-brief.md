## 6. Ready-To-Deploy Anti-Slop Content

### Asset 1: Personal LinkedIn-Style Post

The weakest part of a wellness funnel is usually not the headline.

It is the claim underneath it.

A founder can have a beautiful product page, a polished founder story, strong testimonials, and a supplement formula that sounds credible. Then one sentence quietly does too much work.

"Supports metabolism."

"Helps recovery."

"Promotes calm."

Those phrases feel safe because everybody uses them. But safe-looking language can still carry a claim the brand has not earned.

That is why I like the idea of a Health Claim Receipt.

Before a claim becomes copy, it needs five things attached to it:

1. The exact source behind it.
2. The type of evidence behind that source.
3. The mechanism the brand can explain without stretching.
4. The limitation the buyer deserves to know.
5. The safe version of the sentence.

This matters even more now that AI search is summarizing health content for people.

If your page is vague, the model may flatten the nuance.

If your source is weak, the buyer may sense the wobble.

If your copy outruns your evidence, the funnel starts asking design and persuasion to cover a proof problem.

The better move is slower at the start and faster everywhere else:

Give every claim a receipt.

Then write the headline.

### Asset 2: GEO/AEO Authority Article Seed

# Why Health Brands Need Claim Receipts Before AI Search Can Trust Them

AI-search visibility is not a trick for health brands. It is a source-quality test.

Google has already framed AEO and GEO as search work, not a separate bag of hacks. Its guidance points brands back toward useful, first-hand, non-commodity content that can be crawled and understood.

That creates a specific problem for wellness brands:

AI systems do not only need a page to find. They need a page that makes the claim easy to carry without exaggerating it.

Recent AI Overview and GEO measurement research makes the distinction sharper. A page can be selected as a citation without its strongest evidence being absorbed into the answer. For health content, that is not a vanity metric problem. It is a claim fidelity problem.

The page needs a receipt structure.

A useful Health Claim Receipt includes:

- Claim: the exact sentence the brand wants to use.
- Source: the study, regulator page, guidance, test result, or internal data point behind it.
- Evidence strength: verified, likely, or unconfirmed.
- Claim boundary: what the source does not prove.
- Safe wording: public copy that stays inside the evidence.
- Unsafe wording: language the brand should avoid.
- Retrieval cue: a definition, comparison, table, or method that helps answer engines quote the page correctly.

This is where claim safety and GEO become the same commercial system.

The brand gets cleaner copy.

The buyer gets more honest context.

The AI engine gets a source it can parse.

The founder gets a stronger sales asset because the page no longer sounds like every other wellness explainer.

The next wave of health-brand content will not be won by brands that publish more generic education. It will be won by brands whose proof is organized well enough to survive summarization.
