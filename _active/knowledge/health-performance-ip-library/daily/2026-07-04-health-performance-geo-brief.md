Recovery has become a scene.

It shows up in recovery rooms, wearable scores, red-light beds, breathwork prompts, and now a chamber at Wimbledon where an athlete lies down, gets light, sound, electromagnetic fields, and hydrogen, then says he felt rested after falling asleep.

That is a powerful market signal.

It is also where recovery copy gets sloppy.

Because a feeling can be real.

A luxury experience can be worth buying.

A wearable score can be useful.

An elite-athlete scene can be persuasive.

But those are not the same kind of truth.

The recovery buyer is not only asking:

"Does this work?"

They are asking:

"What exactly am I supposed to believe?"

Is this a relaxation claim?

Does it come from a readiness signal?

Is the brand making a general wellness claim?

Does a study support the outcome?

Has the product itself been tested for that result?

Is the page drifting into a medical-sounding promise?

Most brands collapse those into one soft cloud of "reset, recharge, recover."

That language feels safe because it is familiar.

But familiar is not always clear.

FTC guidance expects health-related product claims to be truthful, not misleading, and supported by science.

FDA's general wellness device guidance gives low-risk wellness products a frame, but that does not turn every performance or health outcome into a free claim.

Google says its AI features may pull from multiple related searches and sources.

So the page has to make the claim easy to repeat without making it bigger.

I would audit recovery copy with one simple map:

Claim line.

Truth type.

Source needed.

Safe wording.

Unsafe wording.

Answer-page asset.

Example:

"I felt rested after the session" is a user experience.

"May support relaxation" is general wellness language that still needs careful context.

"Improves recovery" needs a stronger proof path.

"Reduces inflammation" gets much closer to a health claim and needs serious evidence.

"Prevents injury" should not be casually sitting on a studio page.

This is not about making recovery brands boring.

The best experiences should still feel desirable.

But the more expensive and science-coded the category becomes, the more the buyer needs a translator.

The premium move brings the caveat into view and makes the truth type visible.

The reset can be real without pretending to be proof.
