# GLP-1 Movement Receipt

## A Claim-Safe Content Rubric For Wellness Brands

Audience: health, wellness, supplement, recovery, fitness, nutrition, and performance brands that want to speak to GLP-1-adjacent buyer questions without crossing into medical advice.

Status: public-safe demo asset, not legal, medical, or regulatory advice.

## The Short Version

Enter the GLP-1 conversation with a receipt before you enter with a claim.

A Movement Receipt is a source-backed content asset that separates what happened, what the source actually supports, what the brand can safely say, what the buyer might infer, and what still needs expert review.

## Why This Exists

GLP-1 medications are changing how some consumers think about appetite, food, energy, movement, strength, identity, and support.

That creates a real content opening for wellness brands.

It also creates a claim-safety problem.

Soft phrases can still imply a lot:

- "Support your GLP-1 journey"
- "Maintain your results"
- "Fight muscle loss"
- "Stay energized while losing weight"
- "Natural GLP-1 support"

Some of those phrases may be harmless in one context and risky in another. Risk depends on the sentence, source, product, audience, page context, and likely buyer takeaway.

## What The Source Actually Says

### Claim 1: Movement behavior signal

Label: LIKELY

Public reporting on conference-presented research said 753 adults with obesity who started GLP-1 medication had lower average step counts and lower moderate-to-vigorous activity after starting treatment. The reported figures were about 5,047 to 4,487 daily steps, and 28 to 22 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity per day.

Why this stays LIKELY: this is useful market intelligence from conference reporting, but it should not be treated as a peer-reviewed clinical conclusion or a causal claim.

Source: People, June 16, 2026, reporting on ENDO 2026 and the Endocrine Society release: https://people.com/people-on-glp-1s-cut-back-on-physical-activity-research-says-11998688

### Claim 2: Health advertising substantiation

Label: VERIFIED

FTC business guidance says objective health-product claims need adequate support before they are used in advertising. For health benefit or safety claims involving foods, supplements, drugs, and related products, the guidance points to competent and reliable scientific evidence as the general standard.

Source: FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance

## The Movement Receipt Rubric

Use this before publishing GLP-1-adjacent wellness copy.

| Receipt Field | What To Check | Safe Direction | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claim type | Is this clinical, ingredient-based, behavior-support, anecdotal, or market-observation? | Label the claim before writing copy. | Treating every wellness sentence as harmless lifestyle language. |
| Exact source match | Source supports the exact wording. | Match claim strength to the source. | Citing a weak or adjacent source for a stronger claim. |
| Limitation | Source limits are visible. | State the boundary in plain language. | Turning early or observational data into certainty. |
| Net impression | What might a reasonable buyer hear? | Rewrite for the likely takeaway, not just the technical sentence. | Avoiding disease words while implying treatment support. |
| GLP-1 adjacency | Are you speaking to a context or advising medication users? | Discuss general behavior support and tell readers to consult qualified professionals for personal medical decisions. | Positioning a product as medication support, side-effect support, or treatment companion without proper review. |
| Movement language | Are strength, energy, movement, or recovery claims tied to proof? | Keep language general unless the exact claim is supported. | "Prevents muscle loss," "fixes fatigue," or "protects results." |
| Retrieval structure | Can a human or AI system see the source, claim, boundary, and update date? | Add dated context, source links, definitions, and claim boundaries. | Publishing a generic tips article with no receipt trail. |

## Agent-Readable Work Primitive

### Purpose

Help a brand create GLP-1-adjacent content that separates source-backed claims, buyer-language observations, safe wording, risky implication, and update rules.

### Inputs

- Product page, landing page, post, or draft copy.
- Claim list.
- Source list.
- Intended audience.
- Channel: website, LinkedIn, email, ad, or sales page.
- Review boundary: internal strategy, public copy, or legal/regulatory review.

### Source Of Truth

- Current brand-approved claim library.
- FTC and FDA guidance where applicable.
- Peer-reviewed evidence when available.
- Clearly labeled conference research or public reporting when peer-reviewed evidence is not available.
- Internal legal, medical, or compliance review for final claims.

### Decision Rules

- Claims that imply treatment, side-effect management, disease prevention, or medication support require escalation before publication.
- Conference reporting and consumer research should stay directional and should not become clinical certainty.
- Buyer takeaway controls the rewrite when the likely impression is stronger than the literal words.
- Content that names GLP-1 users should keep the brand's role to education, general support, or source-backed context unless stronger review exists.
- AI-search citation claims require direct testing and a dated log.

### Examples

- Good: "A dated guide that explains what a source does and does not prove, then gives safe wording examples."
- Good: "A product page section that labels movement support as general wellness support and avoids medication-specific promises."
- Bad: "A supplement page that uses a GLP-1 trend headline to imply treatment support."
- Bad: "A blog post that cites early reporting but writes as if causation is proven."

### Quality Tests

- Can a reviewer identify the exact claim and exact source?
- Is the evidence grade visible?
- Is the limitation visible?
- Copy avoids medical advice.
- Review date is visible.
- Retrieval readiness is clear without claiming untested AI citations.

### Failure Modes

- Treating buyer conversation as clinical proof.
- Treating soft language as automatically safe.
- Publishing a tips article without source boundaries.
- Writing for AI search before writing for buyer trust.
- Letting AI draft claims without a source hierarchy.

### Update Rule

Review monthly, or sooner if new peer-reviewed research, FTC/FDA guidance, product claims, or platform citation tests change the risk profile.
