## Why This Sample Exists

The GLP-1 Movement Receipt needed one original proof layer: five real public pages reviewed through the Source-to-Search Trust Layer method.

This sample shows how a wellness, nutrition, supplement, or performance brand can separate:

- what the page says
- what a buyer might infer
- what the source support makes visible
- where the claim can drift into medical-risk language
- what answer-page or trust-asset opportunity sits underneath the copy

Do not claim that any page is cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, or Google AI Overview. No live citation test was run for this sample.

## Audit Method

Each page was reviewed for six fields:

| Field | Review Question |
|---|---|
| Page observation | What does the public page appear to emphasize? |
| Claim type | Is the claim product, ingredient, nutrition, behavior-support, medication-adjacent, or market-positioning? |
| Buyer net impression | What might a reasonable buyer take away? |
| Risk read | Low, medium, or high based on GLP-1 adjacency, medical implication, proof visibility, and wording strength. |
| Safer direction | How the same idea can be framed with clearer boundaries. |
| Source-to-Search opportunity | What trust asset, answer page, or claim receipt would make the page easier to inspect. |

## Page 1: Pendulum GLP-1 Probiotic

Source: https://pendulumlife.com/products/glp-1-probiotic  
Status: VERIFIED that the public page was available during this review.

### Page Observation

The page sells a product named GLP-1 Probiotic and presents it around naturally increasing GLP-1, appetite, cravings, post-meal glucose response, and food noise. It also shows footnoted support such as preclinical research and a consumer survey.

### Claim Type

- Product claim
- Microbiome and ingredient-mechanism claim
- GLP-1-specific claim
- Appetite and craving-adjacent claim

### Buyer Net Impression

A buyer may read the product name and page framing as a direct GLP-1 support promise, even if the page uses dietary-supplement framing rather than medication language.

### Risk Read

Medium to high.

The stack creates the strongest risk: product name, GLP-1 language, appetite language, food-noise language, and hormone-mechanism framing. That stack makes the page a good candidate for a visible claim receipt.

### Safer Direction

Keep product claims tightly matched to the cited evidence and make the evidence grade visible next to the claim.

Safer public language pattern:

> This product page explains the strain, the proposed GLP-1-related mechanism, what the cited research supports, and where the evidence should not be stretched.

### Source-to-Search Opportunity

Build a product-page claim receipt with:

- claim-by-claim source grade
- human vs preclinical evidence labels
- plain-language limits
- buyer FAQ on GLP-1, appetite, cravings, and food noise
- update date and review owner

### Page Observation

The page offers smoothies designed for people on a GLP-1 routine, with protein, fiber, zero grams added sugar, and FAQ-style education around hydration, protein, digestion, and nutritional routine.

### Claim Type

- Menu and food-positioning claim
- Nutrition-support claim
- GLP-1 routine-adjacent claim

### Buyer Net Impression

A buyer may read the page as a practical food-routine companion for GLP-1 users. The page is not selling a pill, which lowers risk, but direct GLP-1 and side-effect language still raises review needs.

Medium.

## Pattern Findings Across The Five Pages

### 1. The product category changes the risk

Food pages can often speak in nutrition-routine language. Supplement pages need tighter claim boundaries because a capsule, powder, or ingredient stack can imply a more direct physiological effect.

### 2. The product name can make the claim before the copy does

If the product name includes GLP-1, the claim receipt should review the name, headline, page structure, disclaimer placement, and likely buyer takeaway together.

### 3. Disclaimers help, but they do not erase net impression

A disclaimer at the bottom cannot carry all the safety work if the headline and product frame imply a stronger promise.

### 4. GLP-1 support is not one claim

It can mean appetite, cravings, food noise, digestion, muscle maintenance, hydration, protein adequacy, portion fit, routine support, or medication-adjacent support. Each needs its own source grade.

### 5. The strongest public asset is not another tips article

The stronger asset is a retrieval-ready answer page with visible claims, sources, limits, examples, and update rules.

## Common Risk Patterns

| Risk Pattern | Why It Matters | Safer Move |
|---|---|---|
| Product name carries the medical context | The buyer reads the name before the disclaimer. | Audit the name and headline as claims, not just the body copy. |
| General nutrition proof becomes GLP-1-specific proof | Protein, fiber, and hydration evidence may not prove medication-specific outcomes. | Label general evidence separately from GLP-1-specific evidence. |
| Side-effect language enters marketing copy | GI or medication-related language can sound clinical. | Keep it educational and escalate for review. |
| "Support" does too much work | Support can mean routine, symptom, outcome, or mechanism. | Define the support type on-page. |
| AI-search claims get ahead of testing | Retrieval-ready is not the same as cited. | Say "built for AI-search inspection" unless live testing is logged. |
