# How to Create Supplement Marketing Angles Without Overclaiming: Proof-To-Angle Visual Brief

**Status:** Local visual brief; generation and publication `NO_PERMISSION`  
**Format:** 4:5 LinkedIn carousel plus one embeddable article table  
**Visual role:** Make the evidence-to-angle decision inspectable without turning it into a legal-approval badge.

## Core Visual Idea

One source fact moves through six visible frames. Every frame narrows what the next one is allowed to say.

Make the proof chain inspectable in one frame: exact proof, evidence limit, buyer tension, defensible belief, hook direction, and reviewer boundary.

## Proof-To-Angle Receipt

Constructed example: a fictional electrolyte powder has a current finished-product batch certificate reporting label accuracy for 500 mg sodium per serving within the laboratory's stated tolerance.

1. **Exact proof:** Current batch certificate reports label accuracy for 500 mg sodium per serving. Design note: a small document crop keeps the “current batch” date field visible.
2. **Evidence limit:** Label accuracy only. No hydration, safety, performance, or superiority outcome. Boundary box: muted red.
3. **Buyer tension:** “Third-party tested” tells the buyer almost nothing by itself. Treatment: fade the generic badge behind the actual test field.
4. **Defensible belief:** A trustworthy brand shows what was tested and what was not. Layout: a two-column “tested / not tested” card.
5. **Angle and hook:** *Show The Test, Not The Halo.* “This certificate proves one thing. We show you exactly which one.” Hierarchy: angle name large, hook smaller, no product-performance imagery.
6. **Reviewer boundary:** Marketing read complete. Qualified product and claim review still required. Status field: empty reviewer name with a `REVIEW REQUIRED` chip.

## Carousel Frames

### Frame 1: The Failure

**Headline:** Your evidence does not become an angle when you add louder words.

**Composition:** Warm-white field, one oversized generic “SCIENCE-BACKED” badge crossed by a thin charcoal evidence line. Keep the visual quiet, not alarmist.

### Frame 2: The Decision Chain

**Headline:** Proof has to survive six decisions.

**Visual:** Horizontal chain with unequal box widths: exact proof -> limit -> tension -> belief -> hook -> reviewer.

### Frame 3: The Constructed Receipt

Use the six-row receipt above. Highlight the evidence limit before the angle name. The order is the teaching.

### Frame 4: The Net-Impression Risk

**Headline:** The claim is bigger than the sentence.

**Visual:** One center card surrounded by five labeled satellites: words, images, customer quote, citation, and context. A sixth smaller satellite reads “disclosure.”

**Risk callout:** A soft word can still sit inside a hard implied claim.

### Frame 5: The Boundary

**Headline:** Farrice reads for signal. The qualified reviewer keeps the pen.

**CTA:** Send me the live page or campaign. I will point to one consequential message or proof gap using your public words—nothing invented.

## Art Direction

1. **Composition:** Muji-clean evidence sheet with the grit of an annotated studio proof. Avoid the SaaS dashboard look.
2. **Palette:** Warm white, charcoal, muted oxide red for evidence limits, and muted moss for verified source fields. No neon AI gradient.
3. **Typography:** Condensed sans for field labels; readable grotesk for body; one serif italic only for the named angle if it helps separation.
4. **Frame behavior:** One decision per frame. No decorative icon should imply medical approval, certification, efficacy, or platform endorsement.
5. **Visual hierarchy:** Evidence limit appears before the creative payoff.
6. **Accessibility:** Minimum 4.5:1 body-text contrast. Do not rely on red and green alone. Every icon has a text label.
7. **Export intent:** Editable source first. No paid generation in this run.
