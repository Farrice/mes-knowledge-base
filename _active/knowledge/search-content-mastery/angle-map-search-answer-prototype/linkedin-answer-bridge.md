# How to Create Supplement Marketing Angles Without Overclaiming: LinkedIn Post

**Status:** Local production packet; publication `NO_PERMISSION`

## Post Copy

Thirty hooks can still be one weak angle.

I see the same move all over supplement marketing.

The evidence says one narrow thing.

The campaign stretches it into a bigger promise because the narrow thing does not feel loud enough.

Then review kills the line.

So the team replaces it with softer words and calls the problem solved.

It isn't.

The whole impression still counts: headline, body in the image, testimonial, study link, and the sentence hiding under the button.

A safer adjective list does not solve the decision. Show how one exact source fact becomes one buyer belief, one angle, and one review boundary.

The chain I use looks like this:

Exact proof  
Evidence limit  
Buyer tension  
Belief worth testing  
Named angle  
Hook direction  
Express claim  
Implied claim  
Editorial risk line

Constructed example:

A batch certificate confirms label accuracy for one fictional electrolyte product.

That does not prove better hydration, better performance, or better safety.

But it can support a real belief:

A trustworthy brand shows what was tested and what was not.

Angle: **Show The Test, Not The Halo.**

Hook: “This certificate proves one thing. We show you exactly which one.”

Now the creative team has somewhere to go.

The reviewer has something precise to inspect.

And the evidence is not carrying a promise it never made.

That is what The Angle Map is built to settle before production gets expensive.

Send me the live page or campaign. I will point to one consequential message or proof gap using your public words—nothing invented.

## What Is The Claim Boundary?

This is marketing and source-organization guidance. Legal, medical, and regulatory review sit outside this scope. Market effects remain `UNTESTED`.

The example is constructed. A real supplement brand must use its current product truth, evidence, surface, and qualified reviewer.
