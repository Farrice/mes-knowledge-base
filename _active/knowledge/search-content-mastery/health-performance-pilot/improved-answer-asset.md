# How Should a Supplement Brand Structure Claims for Human Trust and AI Inspection?

Meta description: Put the claim, source, limit, reviewer, and update date together so buyers and search systems can inspect what is known without mistaking readiness for proof.

Suggested URL: `/supplement-claim-receipts/`  
Canonical: verify against the live site before any publication.

## Direct answer

A supplement brand should place five things together: **the exact claim, its strongest approved source, what that source does not prove, who reviewed the wording, and when the record was last checked**.

That structure makes the evidence easier for a buyer, reviewer, or retrieval system to inspect. It does not guarantee that Google or an AI answer engine will rank, cite, or send traffic to the page.

This answer uses a **direct answer -> claim receipt -> worked example -> limits -> action** pattern.

## The five-field Claim Receipt

1. **Claim:** the exact public sentence, not a vague topic.
2. **Source:** the approved label, study, technical file, official guidance, or first-party record supporting it.
3. **Limit:** what the source cannot establish and what implication the copy must avoid.
4. **Reviewer:** the qualified person who owns that class of claim under the brand's review process.
5. **Updated:** the review date and trigger for checking it again.

This is the information gain: **make the claim record inspectable as five adjacent fields—claim, source, limit, reviewer, and update date—and explicitly separate retrieval readiness from observed citation performance.**

## Worked example: composition fact, not outcome promise

**Claim:** “Each serving contains 5 g of Ingredient X.”

**Source:** The brand's currently approved product label and specification sheet.

**Limit:** The label verifies composition. It does not establish a treatment, safety, tolerance, or performance outcome.

**Reviewer:** The product owner and the qualified claim reviewer required by the brand's process.

**Updated:** Use the date of the current label approval. Review it again when the formula, label, source, or guidance changes.

For example, a composition statement and a performance promise are not the same claim type. The source that supports one may not support the other.

## What should happen before drafting?

1. Confirm the entity, product, audience, and offer facts.
2. Collect the approved source hierarchy and prohibited claim patterns.
3. Capture the founder or subject-matter expert's actual explanation.
4. Review current buyer questions and live result structures.
5. Write the `SearchBrief` with one information-gain requirement and one risk boundary.

## What should happen after drafting?

1. Fact-check every claim against the attached receipt.
2. Review the CTA and its destination as part of content quality.
3. Record any operator override without deleting the original score.
4. If the asset is later published, record `PUBLISHED`, `INDEXED`, `RANKED`, `CITED`, `TRAFFIC`, `CONVERTED`, and `COLLECTED` as independent dated events.
