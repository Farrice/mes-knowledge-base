## POSITIONING THESIS
**For actors and high-stress performers:** "Training designed around your chaos, not against it."

The core insight: Most coaches force clients into perfect conditions. Josh designs programs *within* real-world constraints (on-set schedules, unpredictable nutrition, high-stress seasons, medication timing, body-composition deadlines).

**Why this angle resonates:** Performers have money, they need a transformation for specific reasons, and they're tired of coaches who don't understand their life.

---

## LINKEDIN HOOK STRATEGY
**Goal:** Get an actor or performer to a DM conversation. One LinkedIn post per week from the hooks below. Rotate them. Let the algorithm learn which resonates.

---

### HOOK 1: The Constraint-is-Feature Angle
**Headline:** "I coach actors who can't afford a generic coach"

**Body:**

On-set schedule is chaos. You need your body ready by Friday. You can't afford to look soft for a role.

Most coaches hand you a template and tell you to make it work.

I do the opposite. I build training *around* your actual life — not the idealized version.

That means:
- Workouts that fit 10 minutes on set OR 60 minutes at home
- Programs that work through medication shifts and irregular nutrition
- A coach who understands performance pressure isn't the same as bodybuilding ambition

If your schedule has never fit into a standard training program, let's talk.

**Call-to-action:** Reply here or DM "training"

---

### HOOK 2: The "What You Think vs. What You Actually Need" Angle
**Headline:** "Most actors cut by eating less and training more. That's not what actually works."

**Body:**

You think: "I need to eat chicken and broccoli, hit a hard deficit, train 6×/week, and suffer for 8 weeks."

You actually need: A program that keeps your strength while your body composition shifts. Nutrition you can actually stick to while on set. A coach who knows that a body falling apart mid-shoot costs you the role *and* your confidence.

I've coached actors through this. The pattern is always the same: the ones who keep their strength are the ones who book the role.

The muscle is the leverage.

**Call-to-action:** DM if you've tried the "chicken and broccoli" approach and it didn't work

---

### HOOK 3: The Authority Angle (Josh's Own Win)
**Headline:** "I used to think I couldn't stay strong while cutting. Then I figured out how."

**Body:**

I was an actor. I understood the pressure: the role depends on your body. Miss the body comp deadline and you miss the booking.

I tried the standard playbook: hard deficit, high volume, suffer.

Didn't work. Lost strength. Lost confidence. Felt like I couldn't win.

Then I learned a different approach: periodized training that defends muscle, strategic nutrition timing, a system that *works with* your chaos instead of against it.

I kept my strength. Lost the fat. Booked better roles.

Now I coach other actors through the same thing.

If you're tired of the "suffer or fail" narrative, let's build something different.

**Call-to-action:** DM "coaching" and let's talk about your goal

---

### HOOK 4: The Niche Credibility Angle
**Headline:** "I'm not a generic strength coach. I coach performers."

**Body:**

There's a difference between "getting strong" and "getting strong for a reason."

Actors, musicians, high-stress professionals — they need bodies that perform under pressure. That means a coach who understands:

- You can't afford to lose strength when cutting
- Your schedule isn't predictable
- You need results by a specific deadline, not "eventually"
- The body is part of your livelihood

I coach for that. Not for beach season or general fitness.

If you're a performer and you've felt misunderstood by coaches who treat you like a weekend gym warrior, that ends here.

**Call-to-action:** DM if this resonates
