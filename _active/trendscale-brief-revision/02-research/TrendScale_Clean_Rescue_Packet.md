## 3. Template Constraints

The TrendScale template has:

- 28 paragraphs.
- 1 script table.
- Script table columns:
  - `Visual / editing notes`
  - `Section`
  - `Script`
  - `On-screen text`

Fill the template fields directly. Keep the brief native to the provided format.

Field rules:

- `Strategist`: Farrice Cain.
- `Client`: product name and brand.
- `PDP`: actual product page URL, not a pending note.
- `Media buyer notes`: if used, make it client-facing account context only.
  Exclude Codex notes, internal debate, and launch-placeholder language.
- `Concept type`: Iteration.
- `Iterated from`: the original named concept.
- `Avatar`: specific buyer state, not a generic demographic paragraph.
- `Hypothesis`: one testable creative bet.
- `Concept inspo`: concrete visual objects and scenes.
- `Editing notes`: production direction only.
- `Note`: claim and compliance guardrails only.

Script table rules:

- Rows 1 to 3: `Hook 1`, `Hook 2`, `Hook 3` as alternate openers.
- Body rows: buyer wound, mechanism, proof or label fact, product reveal, CTA.
- `Script` column must be exact spoken VO only.
- `Visual / editing notes` can carry production detail.
- `On-screen text` should be 6 words or fewer when possible.

## 5. Claim Guardrails

Treat supplement claims as structure-function and label-inspection copy.

Allowed if sourced and softened:

- L-carnitine helps transport long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria.
- Magnesium supports more than 300 enzyme systems.
- Less than 1 percent of total body magnesium is in serum.
- Magnesium forms may differ in absorption.

Avoid:

- Direct diagnosis of the viewer.
- Disease language.
- Guaranteed outcomes.
- Before-and-after or body transformation framing.
- "You have this problem" personal-attribute framing.
- Broad competitor accusations.
- Certainty language such as "actually depleted" or "almost certainly low."

## 8. Failure Notes From The Bad Iteration Loop

Primary failure:

- The brief got structurally cleaner while the script column got less usable.

Repeated failure modes:

- Strategy language leaked into spoken VO.
- Product reveal rows explained why the ad works instead of saying the ad.
- Hooks became scientific statements instead of buyer-recognition moments.
- Mechanism arrived before the wound.
- Claim safety edits flattened the psychological tension.
- PDP facts became notes instead of production-ready fields.
- Internal notes to Codex or media-buyer-style notes appeared in client-facing
  fields.
- Later versions optimized for gates rather than taste.

Do not use:

- "That is the click."
- "The form list is the story."
- "The metaphor works."
- "This ad should..."
- "The reason to click..."
- "Recommended control is..." inside any spoken script row.
- Any visible reference to Codex, AI slop, internal process, routing, or gates
  inside the client-facing DOCX.

## 9. Exact Writing Standard

The script must sound like paid-social VO the first time it is read aloud.

Hook standard:

- First line creates private recognition, not product education.
- Cold buyer should feel seen before being taught.
- Use an object, scene, or unresolved prior behavior.
- Mechanism-first hooks are blocked.
- Supplement-category openers need a human object or scene.

Body standard:

- Row 1 after hooks names the buyer wound.
- Mechanism comes after recognition.
- Proof is one clean fact, not a mini lecture.
- Product reveal is plain and earned.
- CTA is low pressure: inspect, read, compare, decide.

Sentence standard:

- Hook VO: usually 6 to 18 words per line.
- Body VO: usually 8 to 24 words per row.
- One idea per row.
- Spoken rows must be recordable as-is.
- If a narrator would sound like they are reading notes, the row fails.

On-screen text standard:

- 2 to 6 words when possible.
- Concrete, not explanatory.
- It should reinforce the spoken line, not repeat the whole line.

Tone standard:

- Quiet confidence.
- Editorial restraint.
- Keep out hype cadence, guru cadence, doctor-coat authority theater, and
  urgency theater.

Language constraints:

- Em dashes are blocked.
- Block the generic curiosity opener about what nobody tells you.
- Remove binary reveal crutches.
- Cut truth-reveal phrasing.
- Reject numbered-guru setup lines.
- Strip abstract consultant phrasing.
- Keep internal process language out of client-facing copy.

## 10. Script Row Acceptance Test

Every `Script` cell must pass these questions.

1. Could a HeyGen narrator record this exact line without changing a word?
2. Does the line sound like a human paid ad, not a brief note?
3. Does the hook create recognition before mechanism?
4. Does the body sequence move from wound -> mechanism -> proof -> product ->
   low-pressure CTA?
5. Is every product or health claim either sourced, softened, or removed?
6. Is the line safe from obvious Meta personal-attribute framing?
7. Is there zero internal process residue?

If any answer is no, the row is blocked.
