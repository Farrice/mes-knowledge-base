TRENDSCALE BRIEF
Client-facing production brief. Built for founder review and editor handoff.
Strategic header
Strategist: Farrice Cain
Client: JCKED - Liquid L-Carnitine 4000mg
PDP: Final product page URL pending. Use the JCKED Liquid L-Carnitine 4000mg PDP before launch.
Media buyer notes: Primary buying angle: dose contrast. JCKED gives 4,000mg liquid L-carnitine; low-dose 500mg category products become the foil. Test the mechanism-first version before any body-transformation or urgency-led variant.
Concept type: Iteration
Iterated from: The Locked Vault control concept.
Avatar: Men 35 to 50 who still train, diet, and buy supplements, but feel the same routine gives less back than it used to. He is not new to L-carnitine. He has probably tried a lower-dose bottle and written the category off.
Awareness level: Problem aware to Solution aware
Hypothesis: A locked-vault metaphor gives this viewer an honorable explanation for a stalled routine: before long-chain fats can be used as fuel, they have to reach mitochondria. JCKED then owns specificity through dose contrast. By the click, he should be thinking: “Maybe I tested the wrong dose story.”
Inspiration and editing
Concept inspo: A locked vault, a key entering a gate, cinematic mitochondria, and a calm dose comparison. The world is science-documentary, not gym-bro transformation ad.
Cast type: HeyGen narrator + AI b-roll. Use a polished presenter and cinematic biology visuals, not handheld UGC.
AI avatar look: Male, 38 to 45, understated, clean but lived-in. Neutral kitchen or study. Dark overshirt or simple crewneck. No doctor coat, no fitness influencer styling.
Pacing: Medium-fast. Calm voiceover, tight mechanism cuts, slower product/dose reveal.
Main emotion: Vindication first, then curiosity, then confidence in the dose difference.
Voiceover: Plain-spoken strategist voice. Direct, controlled, and specific. The narrator should sound like he has seen the category, not like he is hyping a supplement.
Music: Sparse low pulse at minus 18dB. Drop to silence on the final decision line.
Caption style: Manual burned-in captions, bottom third, six words per frame maximum. Amber only for gate, key, and dose callouts.
Overall direction: Lead with the locked-gate mechanism, then make 4,000mg the proof-of-difference. Product enters once dose becomes the obvious question.
Editing notes: Keep the mitochondria sequence metaphorical and premium. Avoid body photos, scale imagery, before-and-after comparisons, doctor visuals, panic urgency, and CapCut-style effects. Product shot should use supplied brand assets or real product photography.
Script
Format: Video, 9:16, 30 seconds. Optional 6-card static carousel using the same dose-contrast angle.
Notes
Note: Production guardrail: structure-function language only. Confirm final label/PDP dosage before launch. Avoid direct fat-burn phrasing, disease claims, hard weight-loss promises, and personal-attribute callouts.

TRENDSCALE BRIEF
Client-facing production brief. Built for founder review and editor handoff.
Strategic header
Strategist: Farrice Cain
Client: Puravita - 12-Form Magnesium Complex
PDP: Final product page URL pending. Use the Puravita 12-Form Magnesium Complex PDP before launch.
Media buyer notes: Primary buying angle: invisible signal plus form differentiation. Lead with the phone-battery metaphor, then move into serum-magnesium limits and form-dependent absorption. Avoid deficiency diagnosis and unapproved influencer references.
Concept type: Iteration
Iterated from: The Battery You Can’t See control concept.
Avatar: Men 38 to 62, with a 45 to 55 sweet spot, who have normal routines and still feel off. He is not shopping for magnesium yet. He is looking for an explanation that does not make him feel dramatic or broken.
Awareness level: Unaware to Problem aware
Hypothesis: A phone-battery metaphor makes an invisible mineral story instantly legible. Serum-magnesium limits give the ad a credible reason to continue. The 12-form product story then makes Puravita more specific than another generic sleep-magnesium ad.
Inspiration and editing
Concept inspo: Phone at 5 percent, normal morning light, lab panel closing, soft body-map glow, and a clean formula reveal. The tone is magazine science, not exhausted-person stock footage.
Cast type: HeyGen narrator + AI b-roll. Use a polished presenter and cinematic metaphor visuals, not handheld UGC.
AI avatar look: Male, 45 to 50, understated, slight gray at temples, simple sweater or overshirt, warm kitchen light. Calm and credible without influencer polish.
Pacing: Medium. Slow first beat, tighter proof sequence, quiet final hold.
Main emotion: Private recognition first, then relief that there is a concrete signal to inspect.
Voiceover: Quiet, grounded, and observant. Narrator names the pattern without diagnosing anyone.
Music: Sparse piano or low ambient bed at minus 18dB. Silence under the final decision line.
Caption style: Manual burned-in captions, bottom third, six words per frame maximum. Use sage for magnesium/form callouts and warm amber only for proof cards.
Overall direction: Make this an invisible-dashboard ad. The viewer should feel that the body can be giving incomplete signals, then see why magnesium form matters.
Editing notes: Use hand-only b-roll for the phone. Avoid stock exhausted-person footage, doctor coats, disease language, literal anatomical diagrams, and urgency text. The body glow should feel like an editorial metaphor, not a diagnosis.
Script
Format: Video, 9:16, 45 seconds. Optional 4:5 static thumbstop using the phone-battery line.
Notes
Note: Production guardrail: use “supports” language and label/PDP-approved claims only. NIH-backed proof supports more than 300 enzyme systems, less than 1% of total magnesium in serum, and form-dependent absorption. Avoid inflated enzyme-count claims, deficiency diagnosis, and unapproved named-influencer usage.
