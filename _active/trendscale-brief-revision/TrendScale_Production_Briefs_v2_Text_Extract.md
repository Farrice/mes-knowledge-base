# TrendScale_JCKED_Production_Brief_v2.docx

TRENDSCALE BRIEF
Client-facing production brief. Cold-traffic v2 pass for founder review and editor handoff.
Strategic header
Strategist: Farrice Cain
Client: JCKED - Liquid L-Carnitine 4000mg
PDP: Final product page URL pending. Use the JCKED Liquid L-Carnitine 4000mg PDP before launch.
Media buyer notes: Primary test angle: right ingredient, wrong dose. Make the ad look like a dose and mechanism teardown rather than another fat-loss claim: 500mg category reference point vs JCKED 4,000mg, with L-carnitine transport as the reason the dose matters.
Concept type: Iteration
Iterated from: The Locked Vault control concept.
Avatar: Male, 35 to 50. He trains, tracks, and has already bought supplements. He is not naive. The useful tension is that he may have tried L-carnitine before, but the old bottle did not give him a reason to believe the dose or mechanism mattered.
Awareness level: Problem aware to Solution aware
Hypothesis: Cold traffic will pay attention if the ad gives him a more precise explanation than “try harder.” The strongest entry is the half-used bottle in his cabinet: a familiar object that lets us introduce the dose gap, then the transport mechanism, then JCKED as the version with a sharper reason to click.
Inspiration and editing
Concept inspo: Half-used supplement bottle, cabinet mirror, locked vault, amber key, mitochondrial gate, clean dose cards. The world should feel like a calm teardown, not a transformation promise.
Cast type: HeyGen narrator + AI b-roll. Use a polished presenter and cinematic mechanism visuals, not handheld UGC.
AI avatar look: Male, 38 to 45, understated, clean but lived-in. Neutral kitchen or study. Dark overshirt or simple crewneck. No doctor coat, no fitness influencer styling.
Pacing: Fast first 5 seconds, then measured mechanism. Keep the product reveal slower so the 4,000mg number lands.
Main emotion: Vindication. He should feel less blamed and more interested in the dose distinction.
Voiceover: Direct, plain, and slightly forensic. It should sound like Farrice walking a founder through why this angle can beat category fatigue.
Music: Sparse low pulse at minus 18dB. Cut the bed under the final decision line.
Caption style: Manual burned-in captions. Six words per frame maximum. Use amber only for key, gate, and dose-card moments.
Overall direction: Start with the failed-prior-product tension, then explain why dose and transport change the story. The ad should make JCKED feel like the first L-carnitine creative that respects a skeptical buyer.
Editing notes: Open on a real object, not a body. Use cabinet, label, dose card, vault, and mitochondria visuals. Avoid body photos, scale imagery, before-and-after comparisons, doctor visuals, panic urgency, and template effects.
Script
Format: Video, 9:16, 30 seconds. Optional 6-card static carousel using the dose-contrast angle.
Notes
Note: Production guardrail: structure-function language only. Confirm final label/PDP dosage before launch. Avoid direct fat-burn phrasing, disease claims, hard weight-loss promises, and personal-attribute callouts.

# TrendScale_Puravita_Production_Brief_v2.docx

TRENDSCALE BRIEF
Client-facing production brief. Cold-traffic v2 pass for founder review and editor handoff.
Strategic header
Strategist: Farrice Cain
Client: Puravita - 12-Form Magnesium Complex
PDP: Final product page URL pending. Use the Puravita 12-Form Magnesium Complex PDP before launch.
Media buyer notes: Primary test angle: the body has no battery icon. Lead with the phone-battery metaphor, then sharpen the proof with serum magnesium limits and form-dependent absorption. The script should create curiosity without diagnosing deficiency.
Concept type: Iteration
Iterated from: The Battery You Can’t See control concept.
Avatar: Men 38 to 62, with a 45 to 55 sweet spot. He has normal routines, normal labs, and a private sense that something is off. He is not searching for a magnesium brand yet, which is why the ad must sell the missing signal before it sells the bottle.
Awareness level: Unaware to Problem aware
Hypothesis: The first five seconds should feel more like a pattern interrupt than a supplement ad. The strongest entry is a normal lab report or a phone at 5 percent because both create the same question: what if the signal I trust is incomplete? Once that question is open, the magnesium proof and 12-form story have a reason to matter.
Inspiration and editing
Concept inspo: Phone at 5 percent, normal lab panel, kitchen morning, soft body-map glow, formula cards, and a quiet product reveal. Think magazine science with a human problem underneath.
Cast type: HeyGen narrator + AI b-roll. Use a polished presenter and cinematic metaphor visuals, not handheld UGC.
AI avatar look: Male, 45 to 50, understated, slight gray at temples, simple sweater or overshirt, warm kitchen light. Calm and credible without influencer polish.
Pacing: Slow first beat, quick proof reveal, quiet product hold. Let the lab-report line breathe.
Main emotion: Private recognition. He should feel that the ad is naming something he has not been able to explain cleanly.
Voiceover: Quiet, grounded, and observant. The narrator should sound like someone noticing a pattern while staying far away from cure language.
Music: Sparse piano or low ambient bed at minus 18dB. Silence under the final decision line.
Caption style: Manual burned-in captions. Six words per frame maximum. Use sage for serum/form callouts and warm amber only for proof-card emphasis.
Overall direction: Make the first half an invisible-signal story and the second half a form-difference story. The viewer should click because the label suddenly feels worth reading.
Editing notes: Use hand-only b-roll for the phone and clean document-style lab visuals. Avoid stock exhausted-person footage, doctor coats, disease language, literal anatomical diagrams, and urgency text.
Script
Format: Video, 9:16, 45 seconds. Optional 4:5 static thumbstop using the lab-report or phone-battery hook.
Notes
Note: Production guardrail: use “supports” language and label/PDP-approved claims only. NIH-backed proof supports more than 300 enzyme systems, less than 1% of total magnesium in serum, and form-dependent absorption. Avoid inflated enzyme-count claims, deficiency diagnosis, and unapproved named-influencer usage.
