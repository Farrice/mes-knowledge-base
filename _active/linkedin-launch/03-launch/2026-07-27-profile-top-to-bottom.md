```
The best product in a category almost never has the best marketing. There's a structural reason for that, and it isn't talent.

When you actually have the data, you get careful. Careful sounds like maybe. And maybe loses to a competitor who has nothing to protect and will say anything.

I learned that in the worst version of it.

I sold supplements for three years, behind a counter, watching people read the back of a label and decide whether to believe it. Before that I was the guy buying it. Pre-workouts, fat burners, whatever the magazine said, back when the ad and the article were the same thing. They promised the physique, the look, the feel, the confidence. Mostly I was after that last one.

Then eighteen years as a trainer, and the question never stopped. Nutrition plans, program design, and every conversation on the gym floor. What should I be taking. Is this stack worth it. Does any of it do anything.

I told a lot of people to save their money.

What bothered me is that some of it was real. Good research, honest formula, worth every dollar. It lost anyway, to worse products with better marketing.

Supplements are just the extreme version. The pattern shows up anywhere the truth is complicated, which is most places worth working. Software, services, devices, anywhere the honest claim needs a paragraph and the lie fits on a banner.

You know if this is you. You paid for the study, then watched somebody with half your evidence outsell you on a line you'd never be allowed to write.

So that's the job. Take a product that actually does something, decide what it should say and who it should say it to, and get it standing on something instead of hedging.

Three ways in.

Free teardown. I pull your public messaging apart the way your most skeptical customer would, tell you which claim isn't landing, and send it to you before I publish it.

A sprint. You get the decision, not a document. Who it's for, what it proves, what order it goes in, and the angles that come out of it. Your team runs it from the brief.

A retainer. I own the message and the angle direction, and every concept traces back to a real person and a real reason.

I don't build the ads, run the account, or write your calendar. You've got people for that. I decide what they should be making, and why.

No revenue guarantees, and I won't imply science you haven't earned. Eighteen years around this stuff cured me of that.

Somewhere right now a kid's in an aisle, reading a label, hoping it's true. This time I'd like him to be right.

If your product actually works and nobody can tell, DM PROOF.
```
