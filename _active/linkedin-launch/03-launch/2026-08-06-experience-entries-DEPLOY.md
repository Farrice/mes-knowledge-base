## 4. Independent coaching practice — positioning + draft

### The angle you're missing

Everything you sell rests on one claim: **I was standing there when your buyer decided.** Every entry above
is past tense. Vitamin Shoppe ended. Equinox ended in October.

The private practice makes it **present tense**, and that is worth more than anything else it could say. You
are not a man who used to know this buyer. You had the conversation this week.

Three things it proves that nothing else on the profile can:

**It's the practitioner test, made visible.** Your own standing rule is *actively doing it → share it; not
doing it → don't post it.* This entry is the receipt for your right to speak. Without it, an eighteen-year
career quietly becomes an eighteen-year-old career.

**The client tier is the trust proof, not the status proof.** Presidents, VPs, actors — people who can
afford any trainer, any doctor, and any supplement on the shelf. They can ask anyone. They ask you. That is
not a flex about who you know; it is evidence that your judgment survives contact with people who have
unlimited options and no reason to settle.

**It's a focus group that never closes.** Brands pay agencies for a room of strangers who know they're being
watched. You have a standing appointment with high-discretionary-income buyers who tell you the truth
because you have never had a rack to move or an affiliate link to protect.

**Do not position it as "I also do personal training."** That reads as a side hustle and splits the
profile. Position it as the reason the strategy work is current.

**The turn** is *"I had that conversation this week."* Five words, all Saxon, and it's the entry's whole
reason for existing. Everything before it establishes that the practice is real; everything after cashes it.

**Two craft notes:**

  inside an entry dated 2018. Without it, an eighteen-year claim under an eight-year role reads as stretched.

  the podcast right"* — because executives and actors ask different questions than the gym floor does, and
