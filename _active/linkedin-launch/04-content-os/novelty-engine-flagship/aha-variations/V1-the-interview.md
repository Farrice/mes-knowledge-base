There's a job interview happening for your brand right now, and you weren't invited to it.

A founder I know found out by accident. She typed her own product into ChatGPT, "best magnesium for sleep," half expecting to see her name. The machine named four other brands, wrote two tidy sentences vouching for each, and never reached hers. Two years building human trust, and the thing deciding her shortlist had read none of it.

For two years she'd been answering the wrong interviewer. So have most of us.

We were told AI was a tool, the thing that writes the posts and drafts the emails. What slipped past while we used it: AI also became the panel deciding who gets in the room. In March, OpenAI rebuilt ChatGPT around product discovery and made eligible Shopify stores findable by default, no setup required. The vetting now happens inside the chat, before a buyer ever loads your site. By the time they reach you, the interview is over.

This interviewer doesn't grade what we spent two years building. It reads structured proof and earned authority, not ad spend, not brand warmth, not how many humans already love you. A 5W study across five engines this spring found four supplement brands took more than 47% of every citation it tracked. AG1, reportedly the largest greens brand alive by revenue, placed third. The biggest budget in the room missed the cut.

There's a strange mercy underneath this. On June 17, a tool called Peec AI shipped a way to see your placement on that shelf: visibility, win rate, position, per product, inside ChatGPT and Perplexity. Their line: you can't win shelf space you can't see. Which means most brands aren't looking yet. Whole categories still return zero names, the machine lecturing about ingredients because nobody claimed the seat. That seat stays empty for weeks, not years.

The founder stopped being a marketer the moment she read that. She's a vendor now, interviewed by a buyer who can't yet see her, and she finally knows which questions it's asking.
