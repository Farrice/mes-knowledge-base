A brand I know spent eighteen months on a forty-page brand book. The founder origin film. The lifestyle photography that makes you feel something. The case for why they were, genuinely, the better product in the category.

Then a buyer asked ChatGPT to recommend one, and the machine read none of it.

Not because the work was bad. Because none of it was written anywhere a model could quote. A photograph isn't a sentence. A founder's story, told beautifully on a homepage, is not a claim an agent can lift and repeat. The machine doesn't admire your taste. It looks for a line it can cite, and when it can't find one, it moves to the brand that left one lying around.

The biggest brand in the category already learned this the expensive way. AG1, reportedly the largest greens brand on the planet by revenue, asked the same engines to recommend a supplement and came in third. Behind Thorne. Behind Seed. 5W's research ran 3,800 prompts across five engines this spring, and four brands took more than 47% of every citation it tracked; the biggest spender by revenue wasn't first. Loyalty and ad budget didn't buy the recommendation. A quotable, machine-readable claim did.

So the old faith quietly broke. The category now rewards what a machine can read and repeat, and a superior product it cannot parse loses to a plainer one that left a clean line lying around. In March, OpenAI rebuilt ChatGPT around product discovery and made eligible Shopify stores findable by default, no setup. Consideration now happens inside the chat, before a human ever reaches you. And whole sub-categories still return zero brand names, because nobody has written a quotable claim into the slot yet. On June 17, a tool called Peec AI began showing brands their placement per SKU inside that answer. You can finally check whether the machine can quote you.

Humans still shop, and you still have to win them. But there is now a second buyer in the room who only reads, and it has never once seen your beautiful PDF.
