# 03 - Signature Series Bible

## Purpose

Signature series make the market remember the judgment. They also reduce weekly decision fatigue.

Launch with one primary series, one proof series, and one flexible support series. Do not launch all of these at once.

## Series Selection Rule

A series is worth running only if it:

- proves the offer
- names a real buyer pressure
- can repeat for 10 or more editions
- creates an audit or proof object
- gives the reader one useful decision

## Series 1 - Teardown Tuesday

Role: flagship weekly adjudication.

Promise:

"I take one claim, brand, product page, AI answer, or content asset and show what the proof can carry, what it cannot carry, and what I would build next."

Best format:

- carousel for durable teardowns
- text post for speed
- article when the topic deserves a full proof trail

Default structure:

1. The claim or AI answer.
2. Why it matters to a buyer.
3. Bull case: what is actually strong.
4. Bear case: what is weak, missing, or risky.
5. Verdict: what the brand should say or build.
6. Receipt: source, caveat, or prompt log.
7. One practical GET.

CTA posture:

Default no CTA. Every fourth edition can softly bridge to audit.

First 3 editions:

- "I ran a magnesium sleep claim through the two-gate test."
- "Why this recovery brand's best proof is trapped in the wrong place."
- "ChatGPT flattened a supplement category. The missing source surface is the real story."

Kill condition:

If it becomes roast content, stop. The series should feel useful, not smug.

## Series 2 - The AI-Search Visibility Test

Role: free top-of-funnel and self-diagnosis.

Promise:

"Run these prompts and you will know whether AI search can see, name, and explain you."

Best format:

- LinkedIn text post
- downloadable/checklist post
- Featured section asset
- DM reply template

Default structure:

1. State the buyer fear.
2. Give one prompt.
3. Show what failure looks like.
4. Explain what it means.
5. Give the next prompt or action.

First 3 editions:

- "Ask ChatGPT your category's number-one buying question."
- "Ask three engines the same question. Watch how they disagree."
- "Ask who the experts are. If your founder is absent, your proof is not traveling."

CTA posture:

This is the most natural CTA surface. Use: "Send me your site, category, and one competitor if you want me to run the first five prompts."

Kill condition:

Do not claim the brand is absent unless you actually ran the prompt and saved the result.

## Series 3 - Proof Vs Claim

Role: claim-safety authority and buyer education.

Promise:

"I separate what a brand wants to say from what the source can safely carry."

Best format:

- text post
- one-page visual
- carousel

Default structure:

1. The phrase buyers see.
2. The proof it would need.
3. The claim boundary.
4. The safer rewrite.
5. The retrieval opportunity.

First 3 editions:

- "Clinically backed is not a receipt."
- "Supports sleep is not the same as treats insomnia."
- "The caveat is not legal clutter. It is what makes the claim citable."

CTA posture:

Mostly no CTA. Let the repeated judgment create trust.

Kill condition:

Do not drift into legal advice. Farrice is showing claim discipline, not replacing counsel.

## Series 4 - Founder Proof Port

Role: convert Marcus into a believer.

Promise:

"I turn buried founder judgment into public authority without making the founder perform."

Best format:

- founder voice memo rewrite
- before/after post
- short article
- direct-to-camera explanation

Default structure:

1. The founder's rough truth.
2. The market's weaker version.
3. The proof or lived mechanism.
4. The post-ready authority line.
5. The buyer belief it moves.

First 3 editions:

- "Voice memo to authority: the founder's throwaway caveat was the whole moat."
- "The product page hid the best mechanism because it sounded too technical."
- "The expert did not need a personal brand. He needed his proof translated."

CTA posture:

Soft and founder-specific: "This is the work I do for founders who have the proof but not the public language yet."

Kill condition:

If it starts sounding like creator coaching, stop. This is expert translation, not influencer training.

## Series 5 - Source-To-Search Lab

Role: make the GEO method visible.

Promise:

"I show how one source-backed insight becomes a piece humans trust and AI can retrieve."

Best format:

- article
- carousel
- build-in-public post

Default structure:

1. Source truth.
2. Buyer question.
3. Claim boundary.
4. AI-search surface.
5. LinkedIn asset.
6. Offer bridge.

First 3 editions:

- "From FDA warning to content asset: the claim boundary that should travel."
- "From Reddit doubt to answer-page opportunity."
- "From founder interview to AI-search source surface."

CTA posture:

Use after a tangible example exists. Do not over-explain the service before the proof object is visible.

Kill condition:

If it becomes a process flex with no buyer consequence, cut it.

## Series 6 - Soft-Line Audit Demo

Role: zero-proof bridge and first case-study substitute.

Promise:

"A small, anonymized audit that shows the read without pretending to have a client result."

Best format:

- one-page screenshot-style visual
- LinkedIn text post
- article section
- proof tracker entry

Default structure:

1. Brand archetype.
2. Claim or prompt.
3. What the machine/human likely sees.
4. What is missing.
5. The safer stronger line.
6. The asset to build next.

First 3 editions:

- "The sleep supplement with proof trapped in a PDF."
- "The recovery tool that explains the vibe, not the mechanism."
- "The founder-led brand with a strong POV hidden in podcast clips."

CTA posture:

Strong bridge allowed after the demo: "This is the shape of the Claim-Safe Citation Audit."

Kill condition:

Do not make an anonymous example so vague that it cannot prove judgment.

## Launch Recommendation

First 30 days:

- Run Teardown Tuesday every week.
- Run one AI-Search Visibility Test post each week.
- Build one Soft-Line Audit Demo by week 3.

Do not add another named series until Teardown Tuesday has four consecutive editions.
