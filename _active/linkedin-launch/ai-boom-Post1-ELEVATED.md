A client read her work last month, looked up, and asked who actually wrote it.

She runs content for three wellness brands. She didn't have a clean answer, and the pause told him everything.

The trust had been thinning for months before that meeting, across forty posts that were 85% her and 15% nobody, that smooth, faintly-too-polished voice every brand now wears. Her content had gotten cheaper to make. That was the whole problem. When words cost almost nothing, sounding like everyone else costs more than it ever has.

You are running two funnels now, and AI is doing the opposite thing to each.

At the top, discovery, the machines moved in. Someone asks ChatGPT which magnesium to take, and the answer gets written before you reach the room. A 5W study (January to April 2026) ran 3,800 supplement prompts across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI overviews. Four brands took almost half the citations it tracked. AG1, reportedly the largest greens brand by revenue, landed third. The winners published proof a machine could read.

At the bottom, the moment someone trusts you with their body and their money, it flips. Trust got expensive. Your buyer arrives having read AI-written everything, and they smell it by the second line.

Most founders saw content get cheap and bought more of it. They poured hours into the half AI already flattened, then automated the one thing it cannot fake: visible proof that a real person stands behind the claim. The brands pulling ahead ran it backwards. They let AI flood the top, then spent the saved hours making the bottom unmistakably human.

The cheaper your content gets, the more your humanity is worth.

Run the test this week. Ask an AI your ten category questions and see whether your brand shows up. Then read your last ten posts and count how many a stranger could prove a human made.

One of those numbers is too low. You already know which one.
