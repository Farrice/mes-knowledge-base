| Tier | Who | Reality |

## When they buy (reactive triggers — content and DMs time to these)

1. **LTV:CAC collapse** — under 3:1 the model breaks; they go looking for "positioning triage."
2. **The scaling wall** — $1–5M on one hero product + founder hustle; portfolio/retail expansion exposes that no brand architecture exists.
3. **New exec or funding announcement** — highest-reliability window; new VPs/CMOs re-evaluate partners in their first 60–90 days (response rates halve after day 90).
4. **The angle bottleneck** — media buyer in-house, ads managed, and new converting concepts stopped arriving.

## Their world (the pressure stack)

The founder is expected to produce new campaign angles while keeping claims inside the evidence the brand can defend. More AI-generated volume does not settle that decision. Agencies can supply execution and positioning firms can supply decks; the opening is the layer between them: a buyer decision, proof hierarchy, and usable angle direction.

## Their words (mirror these; never define them)

unit economics · CM3 · creative fatigue · ad-library sameness · "what's the angle" · angle library · hook rate · variants/week · Founder Fog · positioning triage · claims matrix · friction tax · the Data Mirage (distrust of agency-reported ROAS) · fractional operator / growth partner · "everyone has access to the same AI models."

Emotional register underneath: *"We did everything right and we're losing to worse products." · "The copy got prettier and kept saying nothing." · "I can explain the product perfectly on a call; none of that survives into the marketing."*

## What makes them say YES to a no-logo outsider

1. Published, dated, NAMED teardown work ("if yes, interview; if not, the resume is marketing copy")
2. A small paid test before a big commitment ($500–$1,500 tactical engagements are their documented de-risk behavior)
3. A permissionless read of THEIR actual brand (the free teardown DM)
4. Niche compliance fluency, demonstrated not claimed
5. A grounded "what AI does well / where it fails" POV — they are allergic to "AI theater"

## Disqualifiers (no pitch, no exceptions)

- Pre-revenue / no real sales yet
- >$1M/mo ad spend with a full in-house creative team (they hire FTEs, not projects)
- Any product Farrice wouldn't personally defend — one indefensible client kills the claim-honesty position
- Buyer wants legal certification, guaranteed revenue, rankings, retailer acceptance, or AI citations
- Brand cannot provide its approved evidence and claim boundaries

## The one belief every piece of content shifts

FROM: "our copy problem is a writing/volume problem" → TO: **"it's an unmade decision — what we prove, who must believe it, what we'll defend."**
