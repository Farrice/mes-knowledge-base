# Offer Canon — verbatim promises, prices, boundaries

*Prices and promises come from this document only. No AI may discount, bundle, or invent guarantees beyond what's here.*

## The ladder (temperature-staged: cold gets a bounded paid diagnostic, never the priced core)

### Rung 0 — The free teardown (the door)
"Send me your homepage. I read it, two product pages, your science page, and your last ten reviews, then write up what you're claiming, what you're actually proving, and where the gap is. Quotes from your own site, nothing invented. You get it before anyone else does, and if I've got something wrong you tell me and I fix it. No call required. DM PROOF."
Protocol: sent privately to a named decision-maker BEFORE publishing; publishes publicly ~72h later. Constructive, credit-where-real first, never a hit piece.

### Rung 1 — The Angle Map, $750 prepaid (the cash rung; wargame-ruled 2026-07-28)
**Promise:** a 60-minute live read of the brand's message, then **3 named angles with hooks and the claim-safety line under each, delivered inside 48 hours.**
Price is one number: $750, prepaid, no range, no discounting. Make-right clause: if it doesn't change what they ship, he keeps working until it does.
Why it exists (context for the AI, not for copy): founders buy $500–$1,500 tactical engagements to de-risk an unknown before bigger commitments; this rung is the documented de-risk mechanism, and it closes inside a week where the $2,500 sprint cannot.

### Rung 2 — The Proof-to-Market Sprint, $2,500 (10 business days)
For the founder with a dated occasion (raise, launch, retail push, repositioning) selling on instinct-written or borrowed positioning. Five stages: Extract → Diagnose → Decide → Translate → Align. **Terminal deliverable: decided positioning, messaging, and proof order, ENDING IN FEEDABLE ANGLES a team can run** — never "the decision" alone, never a deck that gathers dust (buyers' documented complaint about positioning firms: the "execution vacuum"). Capacity: 1 sprint at a time, 2/month max.

### Rung 3 — Retainer
He owns creative strategy: message, angle direction, concepts — each tied to a real person and a real reason. Team executes.

## The claim-safety wedge (embedded in every rung, never standalone)

He maps each claim to evidence strength and proposes language that survives compliance scrutiny — but the client's attorney has the pen. Say it as: *"I read for signal, not law."* He never certifies claims, never implies science the brand hasn't earned. This wedge is a growth lever, not a legal service: the words that keep the FTC away are the same words a skeptical buyer trusts.

## Objections and the standing answers

- **"Why should I trust a stranger with no logos?"** → The teardown IS the interview: published, dated, specific, sent to the brand first. Plus the make-right clause. (He has zero direct-response performance receipts yet — never fake them; artifact quality and the guarantee carry the load.)
- **"Is this just another positioning deck?"** → Everything ends in named angles with hooks — the thing a media buyer can run Monday.
- **"We already have an agency."** → He doesn't replace the agency; he makes the decision the agency keeps asking them for. "I don't build the ads, run the account, or write your calendar. I decide what they should be making, and why."
- **"AI can do this now."** → Never argue AI capability. "Everyone has the same AI models. You have someone who's lived the physiology your ad is selling — so the angle is true before it's scaled."

## Hard boundaries (violating any of these is off-canon)

- No revenue guarantees, ever. Quality make-right only.
- No science claims the brand hasn't earned; no compliance certification.
- No work for products he wouldn't defend — the entire position is claim-honesty.
- Prepaid only on paid rungs (bank-clear lag makes invoiced work phantom revenue).
- Never lead outreach with "your better product is losing" (a seller's belief) — lead with the metric-shaped trigger the buyer already feels (angles fatigued, LTV:CAC, sameness).
