## 1. The Avatar in One Paragraph

He is the most knowledgeable person in almost every room he walks into, and almost no one outside those rooms knows his name. He built real skill the hard way — a decade-plus in the body, thousands of hours actually changing how people move, feel, and perform — and he quietly believes that skill should be enough on its own, that the work should speak for itself, that the people worth having will find him. So he has spent years perfecting the craft and starving the signal, watching louder and thinner operators win the clients, the partnerships, and the "expert" seat he knows he deserves. He is not lazy and he is not confused about his value in private; he is *misaligned* — the outside doesn't match the inside — and every attempt to fix it has felt like becoming someone he doesn't respect. That contradiction, not a skills gap, is the entire buying trigger.

## 2. Identity & Resistance Map (McRaney deep-canvass level)

**Core identity he is protecting:** *The real one. The practitioner who gets results, not the guy who talks about getting results.* His self-worth is fused to being substantively good — and to being visibly *not* one of "those people" (the loud, the salesy, the all-hat-no-cattle content crowd). Being seen as self-promotional isn't just uncomfortable; it threatens the one identity he's proud of.

**Identity-level resistance (the unstated belief):** *"If I were actually good enough, I wouldn't have to market myself. The people who post are compensating for work that can't stand on its own."* So the better he gets, the *less* he promotes — because promotion feels like a confession that the work isn't enough. Self-promotion = fraud tax. This traces to a practitioner culture where "let your results talk" is the highest virtue and hype is the mark of the phony, and often to a personal history of being rewarded for competence and punished (or embarrassed) for wanting attention.

**The contradiction (behavioral evidence of the belief):** He says he wants more of the right clients and to be known for his work. He *does* the opposite: rewrites his headline for the tenth time and posts nothing, tells himself referrals are "the purest form" while the referral well runs dry, buys the cohort and doesn't finish it, hires a VA whose posts "don't sound like me" and quietly kills the account. The energy goes into the craft (safe, identity-affirming) and away from the signal (exposing, identity-threatening).

**The belief that must shift:** From *"marketing = self-promotion = bragging = fraud"* to *"the signal is just alignment — making the outside finally match the inside."* He never has to become a promoter. He has to become *accurately visible.* That reframe is the whole sale, because it lets him act without betraying the identity he's protecting.

**Pre-contemplation reality (~60%):** Most of this market does not think they have a marketing problem. They think they have a *"those people are cringe"* problem, or a *"I just need to be more consistent"* problem, or no problem at all ("word of mouth is fine"). They will not respond to "grow your brand." They respond to a specific, dated observation that their outside is underselling their inside — shown, not told.

## 3. Audience State (Cimorelli — where he is when he meets Farrice)

**Awareness stage:** Problem-aware at best, solution-*unaware*. He feels the friction (peers with half his skill getting the seat) but has not connected it to a fixable positioning problem — he's filed it under "I'm just not a marketing person."

**Felt experience right now (11pm, scrolling):** A low, private burn. He sees a former colleague's post doing numbers and thinks *"that's not even good."* Then the second feeling, worse: *maybe they figured out something I can't.* A flicker of shame that a man this competent can't make the machine work. He closes the app telling himself he'll "get to the content thing" after the next program build.

**What he just tried (the accumulated disappointment):**
- Posted consistently for a stretch, got crickets, decided posting doesn't work for people like him.
- Hired a social/VA or bought an AI tool; the output "didn't sound like me" and he pulled the plug.
- Bought a course/cohort on personal brand; didn't finish it; it made him feel more like a fraud, not less.
- Rewrote his headline and bio repeatedly, chasing a version that finally "feels right." None ever do.

**What he believes is true about the domain:** That LinkedIn is where everyone performs; that good content = clever hooks he finds embarrassing; that being visible means being loud; that AI content is slop (he's mostly right, which is exactly the opening). He is convinced the game rewards noise over substance — so he's opted out and called it integrity.

### Wince-test anchors (never in copy to him)

## 5. Bridge Message (the one message that meets him where he is)

> **"You're not bad at marketing. You're misaligned — what people see doesn't match what you can actually do. Let's fix the outside so it finally matches the inside."**

Why it lands: it validates (you're not bad), removes the shame (it's alignment, not a character flaw), sidesteps every wince word (no "brand," no "visible," no "authority"), and uses his own frame (*outside vs. inside*, *scattered vs. aligned*). It reframes the entire act from self-promotion (identity threat) to alignment (identity-affirming — the practitioner making things *correct*).

**Short-form triad (for copy, mirrors the Invisible-Expert "You talk / We translate" cadence):**
> **"You do the work. I fix the signal. The right people stop scrolling past you."**

## 7. Five Content Angles for Inbound

> Each is I-narrative (his story as the vehicle, the reader as the hero). All CTAs are soft, DM-shaped, never "book a call."

1. **Hook:** *"For 18 years I was the best trainer in the building and the worst at getting known for it. Here's the belief that kept me broke."*
   **Proves:** he lived the exact wound — instant credibility with the avatar; names "the work should speak for itself" as the lie.
   **CTA shape:** *"If you've ever told yourself the same thing, tell me in the comments what you're actually great at — I'll tell you the first thing I'd change about how you say it."*

2. **Hook:** *"The coach with half your skill is getting the clients you deserve. It's not because they're better. It's because their outside matches their inside and yours doesn't."*
   **Proves:** reframes the resentment he feels into a diagnosable, fixable gap (alignment, not talent).
   **CTA shape:** *"Drop your headline below. I'll show you the one word costing you the right people."*

3. **Hook:** *"I can tell within 30 seconds of reading your profile whether you're underselling yourself. Here are the 3 tells."*
   **Proves:** the diagnostic skill itself — this is the audit, demonstrated free, dated and specific.
   **CTA shape:** *"Want me to run the 3 tells on yours? DM me 'ALIGN.'"*

4. **Hook:** *"'It doesn't sound like me.' Every expert says this about their own content — and they're right. Here's why the writing feels fake and what actually fixes it."*
   **Proves:** he understands the exact reason they killed their last VA/AI attempt; positions his AI-plus-lived-expertise method as the opposite of slop.
   **CTA shape:** *"Send me a post that didn't sound like you. I'll show you where the real voice got lost."*

5. **Hook:** *"You don't have a marketing problem. You have an alignment problem. There's a difference, and it changes everything about what you should do next."*
   **Proves:** installs the core reframe (the belief-shift) as a standalone idea people can adopt and repeat.
   **CTA shape:** *"If 'alignment, not marketing' just clicked for something you've been stuck on, tell me what — I read every reply."*

## 8. DM Opener Frame (helpful, not nagging)

**The rule that kills the nag fear:** lead with a *gift specific to them*, not a question about you. A pitch asks for their time; a gift gives them something before asking anything. Structure:

1. **Named, specific observation (the gift, unsolicited):** *"Hey [Name], read your profile after your comment. You've clearly got the [specific real skill you can see], but your headline is doing about 20% of the job it could. It says what you are, not what you fix for people."*
2. **One concrete example (prove you actually looked):** *"Right now it reads '[their headline]'. A stranger can't tell who you're for or what changes because of you."*
3. **Permission-based offer, zero pressure:** *"Happy to send you the aligned version — takes me two minutes and there's no ask on the other side. Want it?"*

Why it can't feel like nagging: he never pitched, he already gave value, and he handed the other person the yes/no. If they say yes, the aligned headline *is* a live proof of the audit. If they go quiet, nothing was lost and nothing was begged for. (Mirrors the canonical brief's "no pitch, only invite" teardown-DM.)

### B. Core Wound (Ontological refraction)
The market's fear refracts through **abandonment/insignificance**: *"I will do excellent work my whole life and remain unseen — competent and invisible, passed over by people with a fraction of my substance."* Rub-salt line (use sparingly, with love): *"You could be the best in your field and die the best-kept secret in it. Skill was never the thing standing between you and the seat you want."*

### C. Resonance Hierarchy (build bottom-up, sell top-down)
- **Experiences:** crickets on posts, VA drafts that "aren't me," the lesser peer winning the client.
- **Beliefs:** "the work should speak for itself"; "marketing = bragging = fraud"; "AI content is slop."
- **Values:** substance over noise, integrity, mastery, being *actually* good.
- **Identity:** "the real one — the practitioner, not the performer." **Never clash with this in the lead.** The offer must let him stay the real one *and* be seen.

### D. Epiphany Threshold (the 7–9 Goldilocks belief)
- *Below threshold (too obvious):* "You should post more." (He's heard it; it shames him.)
- *Over-BS (unbelievable):* "This one hook will make you go viral." (Fires every distrust alarm.)
- *Goldilocks (surprising + believable):* **"You don't have a marketing problem, you have an alignment problem — your outside doesn't match your inside, and that's a 15-minute fix to see, not a personality to overcome."** Thesis (I'm bad at marketing) → antithesis (no, you're misaligned) → synthesis (fixable, and it doesn't require becoming someone you'd hate).

### E. Suffering archetype
**The Overlooked Craftsman** — proud, competent, quietly resentful, allergic to hype, opted out of the game and called it integrity. Tone that works: peer-to-peer, unhurried, a little blunt, zero cheerleader energy. Tone that fails: hype, urgency, "you've got this," any exclamation-point optimism.

### F. Ejection triggers (do-not-say list)
"personal brand," "thought leader," "authority," "influencer," "guru," "go viral," "content pillars," "funnel," "monetize," "scale," "get visible," "provide value," "build your audience," any exclamation-point hype, any promise of numbers/followers/reach.
