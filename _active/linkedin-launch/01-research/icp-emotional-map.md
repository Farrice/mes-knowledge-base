## 1. Who They Are — Identity + Worldview

**Meet Dana Reyes, 34. Head of Growth at an $8M DTC supplement brand.** Three years ago she was the scrappy media buyer who 3x'd a brand on a shoestring and got promoted into the room. Now she sits between a founder who Slacks her competitor screenshots at 10pm and a board that reads CAC like a polygraph. Title says "Head of Growth." On bad weeks it feels like "Head of Explaining Why The Number Moved."

**How she sees herself:** sharp, scrappy, *taste*. She's proud she can smell a winning angle before the data confirms it — that's her superpower, the thing the spreadsheet can't replicate. She claims the labels **operator, builder, performance-minded, brand-builder-not-just-buyer**. She rejects, hard, the labels **"growth hacker"** (cringe, 2019), **"influencer,"** and anything that smells of guru-LinkedIn-thread-bro. The founder claims **visionary**; the CMO claims **brand steward**; the media buyer claims **the one who actually ships the ad that won.**

**The status game:** in this world, status is *"my creative is in everyone's feed and they don't know it's mine."* It's being the brand other operators screenshot and say "who's running their creative?" It's the angle that travels. Losing status looks like becoming **"generic Shopify brand #48"** — invisible in the scroll, indistinguishable from the bottle next to it. [V: "sea of sameness," Gartner]

## ★ 2. Broken Promises

They have been *sold to* relentlessly, and the receipts don't add up. Be a little angry on their behalf — because they are.

- **The agency that ran a $500M-spend deck** and then, three months in, was "just renaming ad sets" and asking for *more testing budget* when CAC went **up**. The promise was "we'll cut your CAC in half in 90 days." The delivery was five variations of the same ad with different stock footage and subtitles. [L]
- **The "we've worked with 100+ brands" pitch** — cool, *have you seen OUR economics?* The promise was pattern-recognition. The delivery was a template that ignored their margin, their compliance reality, their actual customer.
- **The AI tool stack.** The average $2M DTC brand pays for **8–12 AI tools** and "none of them replace the thinking, the creative judgment, or the execution." [L, admetrics] The promise was *infinite content, lower cost*. The delivery was a faster way to make more mid ads — which just **fatigues the audience faster.** They bought a fire hose and what they needed was a point of view.
- **The "just use AI" crowd / the board member who discovered ChatGPT.** Promised the creative problem was solved. Delivered ad scripts that "sound like corporate LinkedIn posts, not something a real human says" — and the quiet, insulting implication: *what do we need you for?* [L; aligns with V: AI-creator enthusiasm fell 60%→26%]
- **The platform itself.** Meta promised scale. It delivered a **slot machine** — $38 CAC Monday, $92 Wednesday, "I can't take this to the CEO with a straight face." The unkept promise underneath all of them: *that more volume, more tools, more spend would make them feel safe.* It made them feel replaceable.

## ★ 3. Their Deepest Problems — All Three Levels

**Surface (what they say in the brief / on the call):**
> "We need more creative. More variants per week. We're fatiguing too fast — ads die in 5–7 days. We need a better hook. Can you get us to 8–12 angles a week?"
It's framed as a *throughput* problem. Make more, make it faster, make it not die.

**Deep (what they admit once they trust you, over the second drink):**
> "We don't actually *have* an angle. We have 'pretty ad with product in it.' We've tested twelve — pain, aspiration, founder rant — and nothing scales past $1k/day. I don't know if we have a creative problem, a media problem, or a *business-model* problem, and I'm terrified it's the last one." The real fear under the brief: **they've run out of things to say about their own product, and volume is how they hide that they've stopped having a point of view.** "More creative" is a request for someone to *think* — they just can't say that out loud, because thinking is supposed to be *their* job.

**Unspoken (the 2am truth they would never say to a colleague, a founder, or a board):**
> *"It might not be the brand that's replaceable. It might be me."*
> If AI can crank the variants and the agency can "handle creative," what is her actual edge? She used to feel like she had a superpower on Meta. Now she feels like **"the platform owns my career."** She is one bad quarter from the board deciding to "bring in a real CMO," and she knows it. The supplement might be commoditized — but so, she fears, is *she*. The deepest wound: she got into this because she had **taste**, and the entire industry is now organized to prove taste doesn't matter, only volume does. The work she's proudest of is the work the machine is trying to make worthless.

*(This is the McRaney core: the behavior — buying more tools, demanding more variants — is driven not by a throughput belief but by an existential one. Content that speaks to throughput bounces off. Content that names the replaceability fear and hands her back her taste as the irreplaceable asset is the one that makes her stop scrolling and reply.)*

Ours looks staged.* She carries home the spreadsheet she can't will into existence, the founder's screenshots, and the quiet question: **what is my edge, actually.** That's the chair she's sitting in when Farrice's post shows up in her feed.

## 5. The Emotional Landscape — Farrice's Content Target

- **What makes them STOP scrolling:** a *named mechanism* they're living but haven't articulated — "the variant treadmill is making your fatigue worse, not better." Recognition, not motivation. (Niche brief format #1/#3 — mechanism teardown + counterintuitive truth. [V])
- **What makes them feel SEEN:** naming the *gap between volume and a point of view*, and the replaceability fear under it — without pity. "You don't need more ads. You need one thing that's true before it's scaled."
- **What earns TRUST:** proof and substance over polish. Cited mechanism, real teardown of a real in-category ad, a compliance-aware POV, and a *credible "where AI falls short"* take — the explicit ask in this market. [V] Lived physiology that survives the authenticity gate.
- **What triggers ACTION (DM / reply / hire):** handing them back their taste as the *defensible asset* + a low-risk way to test him (audit / teardown / paid case). The wedge: *"Everyone has the same AI models — you have something they can't prompt: someone who knows what works in a body."* [V]

**Emotions to EVOKE:** recognition ("that's exactly it"), relief (someone finally separated signal from noise), validated taste (your judgment is the moat, not the bottleneck), earned hope. **Emotions to AVOID:** shame/lecture ("you're doing it wrong"), hype ("10x your creative with AI!" — they're allergic), and *threat* — never make her feel more replaceable than she already does. Reassure the human, indict the machine.
