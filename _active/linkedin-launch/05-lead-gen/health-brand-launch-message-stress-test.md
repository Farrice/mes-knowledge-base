# The Health Brand Launch Message Stress Test
### 12 questions that reveal unclear positioning, weak proof, and internal story gaps — before you invest in creative.

**How to score:** For each question — **0** = no clear answer · **1** = partially clear or disputed · **2** = clear, specific, and supported.

**1. Can the team name one primary customer?**
Not a broad demographic. A person in a recognizable situation.

**2. Can the team explain the customer's problem in plain language?**
No category jargon. No product language.

**3. Can five internal stakeholders give the same reason to choose the product?**
Ask separately: founder, brand, product, growth, agency.

**4. Is the competitive alternative clear?**
It may be a direct competitor, a current behavior, doing nothing, a different category, or a home solution.

**5. Is there one central launch message?**
Not five equal claims. One idea everything else supports.

**6. Does the strongest proof support the central message?**
The most impressive fact is not always the most useful proof.

**7. Is proof prioritized?**
Can the team distinguish headline proof, supporting proof, deep-detail proof, and proof requiring review?

**8. Are the founder, website, product team, and agency telling the same story?**
Alignment must exist before production multiplies inconsistency.

**9. Does the team know the boundaries of the message?**
What can be said, what requires qualification, what requires review, what should not be implied.

**10. Is "why now" clear?**
The launch needs a credible reason for relevance.

**11. Are the main objections mapped?**
The launch should address the doubts that block the decision.

**12. Could an implementation team use the brief without another two-hour explanation?**
A strategy is not useful when it only makes sense to its author.

## Scoring

| Score | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **0–10** | **Not ready for execution.** The team risks multiplying confusion through more assets. |
| **11–18** | **Important decisions remain unresolved.** Production may begin, but revisions and internal disagreement are likely. |
| **19–24** | **Ready for leadership and reviewer alignment.** The message is strategically coherent enough to move into execution. |
