# Founding Source Role Conferral

## The Role Definition

**Founding Source** is a named, numbered membership tier in the Proof-to-Market practice.

- **What it means:** The founder is a pilot client who built the first proof run of the Sprint methodology. They earned access to method refinements, evidence updates, and case-study protocol before those become public.
- **What it signals to them:** You're not a discount buyer. You're a collaborator who shaped the practice. Your feedback matters. You're in.
- **What it signals to the market (later):** The case study was earned through commitment, not purchased through a cheap tier.

## How It Works

### At offer stage (before sale)

**Language used in sales conversation:**

> *"The Proof-to-Market Sprint is $2,500 for a 10-business-day intensive. I'm taking three founding pilots — brands that want to shape the methodology while we build it. Each founding pilot becomes a named 'Founding Source' in the practice. That means you have access to method updates, evidence refinements, and case-study protocol before those become public. It's membership earned through commitment, not a discount tier."*

### At contract stage (after payment)

**Contract or written confirmation includes:**

> *"By paying, you become Founding Source № [1/2/3 of 3] in the Proof-to-Market practice. This means: (1) you have access to future method updates and evidence refinements before public release; (2) your feedback shapes the protocol and is incorporated into the case-study method; (3) permission to share your case study (anonymized or attributed) is part of the founding-pilot terms."*

### In the deliverable (frontmatter)

**The Blueprint's frontmatter includes:**

> *"Your role in this proof: You are **Founding Source № [1/2/3 of 3]** in the Proof-to-Market practice. As a named source, you have access to strategy updates, method refinements, and case-study protocol before public release. This is membership earned through early commitment, not a discount tier."*

### Post-delivery (ongoing)

**Founder receives:**

1. **Access to method updates** — when the workflow changes or refines, Founding Sources get early notification + updated templates
2. **Monthly case-study protocol calls** — optional monthly calls (15-30 min) to discuss proof wins, evidence updates, and new angle bank additions
3. **Case-story pre-release** — when a case study is written, the Founding Source sees it first, provides feedback, approves before publication
4. **Priority on future sprints** — Founding Sources can refer other brands at the founder-pilot rate for 90 days (a reward for commitment, not a referral fee)

## The testimonial ask (with role-conferral)

**Sent when the founder has results to share:**

> *"We're documenting the founding-pilot results. As Founding Source № [X], would you be willing to share a 1–2 sentence note on what shifted in your positioning or proof strategy? No script — just your words. I'll send a few options if you prefer. Permission: would you want this attributed to you and [Brand], or would anonymized be better?"*

**Key difference:** The ask acknowledges their role and frames it as building proof, not "testimonial for marketing."

## Case-study template (for later)

When case studies are written, each includes:

> *"[Brand] was Founding Source № [X] in the Proof-to-Market practice, piloting the 10-business-day intensive to clarify positioning, proof hierarchy, and campaign angles before launch."*

This tells the market:
- They were early (founder tier)
- They helped shape the methodology
- The results are from the proven protocol, not a one-off

## Anti-cringe calibration

**For Dana/Marcus (regulated category) types:**

- No performative energy required. The role is structural, not cultural.
- No "exclusive club" language. It's a practical tier: you pilot → you get access to updates.
- No mandatory testimonial. Share if you want; the relationship survives if you don't.
- No obligation to promote. The Founding Source status is theirs to publicize or keep quiet.

## Capacity and allocation

- **Total slots:** 3 Founding Sources
- **Allocation rule:** First-to-commit gets first slot. Once three are committed, new buyers are offered recurring-implementation tiers (post-pilot, higher price, no Founding Source status)
- **Timeline:** The founding-pilot window is open until all three slots are filled. Once filled, communicate: "Founding pilot tier is booked. Next intake is [date] for recurring implementation at $[price]."

## The offer language (for sales)

Use this exact framing in all cold outreach and conversations:

**In email or DM:**

> *"The Proof-to-Market Sprint is $2,500 for 10 business days. Positioning, proof hierarchy, and messaging blueprint before you fund execution. I'm taking three founding pilots — each becomes a 'Founding Source' in the practice with access to method updates and case-study protocol before public release. If that matters to you, let's talk."*

**In sales conversation:**

> *"You'd be Founding Source № [X]. That means you help shape how this methodology evolves, and your feedback gets built in. You also get early access to new angles, proof strategies, and case studies. It's a membership tier, not a discount."*

---

## Why this matters (the frame)

Without role-conferral:
- Founders feel they're getting a discount because you need pilots
- Conditions feel like limitations, not membership
- They're incentivized to negotiate price
- Case studies feel like you're extracting proof of concept from them

With role-conferral (Founding Source):
- Founders feel they're being invited into a practice, not used as a beta tester
- Conditions feel like membership requirements
- They're invested in the method's success, not fighting the deal
- Case studies feel like documenting a collaboration, not mining testimonials

The fantasy this frames is: **"You're building this with me. Your insight shapes what others get later."**

That's the exact fantasy the Proof-to-Market offer sells in positioning. The role-conferral lives it.
