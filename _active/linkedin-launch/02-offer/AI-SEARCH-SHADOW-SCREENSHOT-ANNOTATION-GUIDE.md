# AI-Search Shadow — Screenshot Annotation Guide

## Why Annotation Matters

A raw Ahrefs screenshot is data. An *annotated* screenshot is a story. You're showing the prospect not just "you're absent from AI," but "here's exactly where the machine steers buyers away from you."

**Goal:** Annotation should make them think: *"Oh. I didn't know the AI did that. That's costing me [visible customer]."*

---

## Annotation Technique #1: Red Arrow (The Absence)

### What It Shows
Prospect brand is missing from top-3 AI recommendations for a typical buyer query.

### How to Annotate

1. **Screenshot the AI response** (ChatGPT answer to "best [category] for [buyer intent]")
2. **Highlight the top-3 brands** the AI recommends (box them)
3. **Add red arrow pointing to the gap** where the prospect's brand *should* appear
4. **Add label:** "Your brand isn't in this conversation"

### Example

```
ChatGPT: "For casual fitness, I'd recommend:
1. Optimum Nutrition CreatinePowder (most popular)
2. Jenerise Creatine (targets women) 
3. Legion Creatine (premium quality)

[RED ARROW pointing to space below]
↓
← Your brand appears here (position #8, if at all)
```

### When to Use
- Repositioning brands (Create Wellness missing from "creatine for women")
- Innovation launches (GNC The Drop not mentioned for "next-gen creatine")
- Competitive setbacks (losing ground to named rival)

### Prospect Discomfort Level
**Medium-High.** They see the absence clearly; the label ("Your brand isn't in this conversation") makes it personal.

## Annotation Technique #2: Yellow Box (The Wrong Language)

### What It Shows
Prospect's brand is mentioned, but described using language that *doesn't* match their actual positioning.

### How to Annotate

1. **Screenshot the AI response**
2. **Yellow-highlight the description** of the prospect's brand
3. **Add bracket note:** "[This is how the AI describes you, not how you describe yourself]"
4. **Footnote** the actual positioning they're trying to own

### Example

```
ChatGPT: "Create Wellness offers quality creatine powder for general fitness enthusiasts."

[YELLOW BOX around this text]
Note: "Create Wellness actually says 'creatine for daily wellness, not just gym performance.' The AI is two steps behind your repositioning."
```

### When to Use
- Brands mid-rebrand (AI hasn't caught the new narrative)
- Tone/messaging gaps (AI uses generic language; brand is trying to sound premium/boutique/science-forward)
- Target audience mismatch (AI says "for athletes"; brand says "for women" or "for casual users")

### Prospect Discomfort Level
**High.** The AI is *speaking about them*, but wrong. This is more uncomfortable than absence because it's present but inaccurate.

## Annotation Technique #3: Side-by-Side Comparison (The Competitive Gap)

### What It Shows
Two brands answering the same buyer question; AI describes one as innovator, the other as follower (or generic).

### How to Annotate

1. **Create a split-screen screenshot** (or paste AI responses side-by-side in an image editor)
2. **Competitor A (left):** How the AI describes the rival brand
3. **Prospect Brand (right):** How the AI describes the prospect
4. **Color-code differences:**
   - Green highlight = prestigious/innovative language (competitor)
   - Gray highlight = generic language (prospect)
5. **Label:** "Same category. Different positioning in the AI's mind."

### Example

```
SIDE 1: Jenerise
"Jenerise Creatine is specifically formulated for women, 
addressing the unique fitness goals of female athletes. 
It combines creatine with targeted micronutrients."

[GREEN BOX: "specifically formulated," "targeted," "unique"]

SIDE 2: Create Wellness
"Create Wellness offers creatine powder for fitness users 
who want a clean supplement option."

[GRAY BOX: "offers," "clean," "fitness users"]

← Prospect sees: "We're all selling creatine. Jenerise is selling *purpose*."
```

### When to Use
- Competitive positioning wars (who owns the narrative?)
- Market-share concerns (why is a named competitor winning in AI-search?)
- Repositioning proof (shows exactly how far behind the AI reading is)

### Prospect Discomfort Level
**Very High.** Side-by-side shows they're *losing the positioning battle in the AI layer* before they've even won it in the market.

## Annotation Technique #4: Timeline Annotation (The Recency Problem)

### What It Shows
Brand launched something new; AI is still describing the old positioning because training data hasn't updated.

### How to Annotate

1. **Screenshot the AI response**
2. **Find outdated language** (old category, old target audience, old product description)
3. **Add timeline label:**
   ```
   AI Training Data: [Last update ~X months ago]
   Your Launch: [Date brand actually launched new positioning]
   Gap: [X months of market momentum the AI doesn't know about]
   ```
4. **Highlight the stale language** (yellow box)

### Example

```
ChatGPT: "GNC offers a wide range of supplements you can buy 
at their stores or online, including popular brands like 
Optimum Nutrition and Muscletech."

[YELLOW BOX: "wide range," "buy at stores"]

Timeline Label:
- AI Last Updated: ~April 2026
- GNC Launched "The Drop" Innovation Hub: June 2026
- Gap: AI doesn't know about your innovation yet
```

### When to Use
- Recent pivots or launches (product exists, but AI doesn't know yet)
- Category-new entries (the AI has no frame for the new category)
- Retail/channel updates (AI still routes to old channels)

### Prospect Discomfort Level
**Medium.** It's not their fault (training lag), but it highlights the *marketing cost* of waiting for AI to catch up.

## Annotation Technique #5: Feature Comparison (The Missing Proof)

### What It Shows
AI describes competitor's product with proof/claims; describes prospect's product generically.

### How to Annotate

1. **Screenshot AI responses** side-by-side (or full page)
2. **Extract the claims** each brand gets in AI descriptions
   - Competitor: "clinically proven," "third-party tested," "targets specific goal"
   - Prospect: "quality," "clean," "good option"
3. **Create a simple comparison table** overlay:
   ```
   | Feature          | Competitor      | Your Brand  |
   |------------------|-----------------|-------------|
   | Specificity      | Targets women   | General     |
   | Proof language   | Clinically ✓    | Clean ✓     |
   | Positioning      | Performance +   | Fitness     |
   |                  | Wellness        |             |
   ```
4. **Label:** "The AI knows their story. It's guessing at yours."

### When to Use
- Claims differentiation gaps (competitor has more specific proof language)
- Positioning saturation (multiple competitors with clear angles; prospect sounds generic)
- Evidence-tier mismatches (competitor positioned as science-backed; prospect sounds lifestyle)

### Prospect Discomfort Level
**Very High.** It shows a *specific* claim they're losing (not abstract "visibility," but concrete "the AI doesn't believe you as much").

## Annotation Technique #6: The Null Annotation (Powerful Understatement)

### What It Shows
Brand is simply absent. No words, no positioning, no mention.

### How to Annotate

1. **Screenshot the AI response**
2. **Add ONE small note:** "I asked ChatGPT [exact query]. Your brand doesn't appear in the top 5."
3. **That's it.** No red arrows, no boxes. The absence speaks.

### Example

```
[Screenshot of ChatGPT response]

Query: "Best supplement for muscle recovery after workouts"
Platform: ChatGPT
Date: July 18, 2026
Result: [Brand name] doesn't appear in the top 5 recommendations.
```

### When to Use
- Highest-impact scenarios (absence is already dramatic; don't over-design it)
- Founder meetings (restraint > aggression; let them feel the discomfort)
- Stealth positioning problem (they don't realize they're completely absent yet)

### Prospect Discomfort Level
**Extreme.** One sentence, one screenshot. The silence is louder than all the annotations.

## Color-Coding Standard (Consistent Branding)

Use the same colors across all screenshots for quick pattern recognition:

| Color | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Red** | Absence, missing opportunity, what's broken |
| **Yellow** | Inaccuracy, mismatch, what's wrong with the current description |
| **Green** | Competitor strength, what they're doing right, what you need to own |
| **Blue** | Timeline/context, when/why the gap exists |

## Tools for Annotation

### Quick (Farrice's workflow)
1. **Take screenshot** (Cmd+Shift+4 on Mac, screenshot tool on Windows)
2. **Open in Preview** (Mac) or Paint (Windows)
3. **Use shape tools** to add boxes, arrows, text labels
4. **Export as PNG**
5. **Paste into DM or attachment**

### Professional (If scaling to deck/presentation)
1. **Figma** (free tier; comment tools + screenshot annotation)
2. **Loom** (record + annotate in one flow)
3. **Skitch** (lightweight Mac/Windows annotation tool)

### Fastest (If super time-constrained)
1. **Screenshot**
2. **Use default OS markup tool** (Markup in Preview on Mac; Snip & Sketch on Windows)
3. **Add arrow + text label**
4. **Send as-is** (authenticity > polish)

### Step 1: Pull the Screenshot
```
Go to Ahrefs Brand Radar or ChatGPT directly
Query: "best creatine supplement for women"
Platform: ChatGPT
```

```
Ask: "What's the gap I want the prospect to *feel* when they see this?"

```

## Common Annotation Mistakes (Avoid)

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Over-annotating (5+ arrows, 8+ boxes) | Looks desperate; prospect tunes out | Limit to 2 annotations max per screenshot |
| Vague labels ("This is bad") | Prospect doesn't know what you're pointing at | Be specific: "Positioned for women; described as generic" |
| Using prospect's emotional language ("You're losing!") | Triggers defensiveness | Use neutral language: "The AI describes you this way; you're trying to own that way" |
| Adding competitor brand logos/names in red | Looks like attack; alienates prospect | Compare positioning language, not brands |
| Screenshot without date | Feels old; loses credibility | Always add: "Query: X. Date: YYYY-MM-DD" |
| Annotation in prospect's brand colors | Looks like their own asset (confuses context) | Use neutral colors (red, yellow, blue) or Farrice's brand colors |

## Annotation Checklist (Before Sending)

- [ ] Screenshot is recent (within 7 days)
- [ ] Query is clearly labeled ("Best creatine for women", not "creatine query")
- [ ] Platform is noted (ChatGPT, Perplexity)
- [ ] Annotation is simple (2 marks max; clear intention)
- [ ] Labels are specific (not "this is bad"; say what the gap is)
- [ ] Color-coding matches standard (red = problem, yellow = wrong language, green = competitor strength)
- [ ] File is saved with date (proof-assets/[brand]-[query]-[YYYY-MM-DD].png)
- [ ] Copy in DM matches the annotation story (one-liner explanation)
- [ ] Prospect's name is in the DM (personalization matters)

## When to Send (Timing)

- **Day 1:** Screenshot + DM (cold outreach)
- **Day 3:** If no response, follow-up with a *different* annotation of the same brand (Show "this is pattern, not one-off")
- **Day 7:** If still no response, soft pivot ("Maybe this angle doesn't land; let me show you [different problem]")
- **Day 10:** Last attempt or move to next prospect
