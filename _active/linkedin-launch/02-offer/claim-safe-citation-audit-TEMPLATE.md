## SCORED SECTION 1 — AI-Citation Visibility

**What it answers:** When a buyer asks AI the category's real questions, does the brand get *named*? Or does the answer name the competitor and three brands that aren't even bigger?

**The wince line that opens it:** *"Your rankings stayed flat but the clicks vanished."*
Then the reframe: ranking is no longer the scoreboard. Inside an AI answer, users click a traditional link 8% of the time and click *inside* the summary just 1% of the time **[VERIFIED — Pew, July 2025]**. Being *named in the answer* is now worth more than ranking, because the click is mostly gone either way. And AI Overviews cut organic clicks 38%, pushing zero-click from 54% to 72% **[VERIFIED — Agarwal & Sen RCT, Apr 2026]**. The traffic isn't soft. It's being intercepted upstream.

### The exact method to check (run this live, log every result)

Build the **buyer-question set** first: 6–10 questions a real buyer types, in three shapes — *category* ("best magnesium for sleep"), *comparison* ("[brand] vs [competitor]"), and *decision* ("is [ingredient] worth it for [outcome]"). The question shape matters: question-form queries trigger AI answers at a higher rate **[VERIFIED directionally — AIO measurement research, 2026]**.

For each question, check **all three engines as separate citation markets** — they do not agree, and that disagreement is the durable insight, not any single percentage **[VERIFIED — Otterly, 2026: Reddit is the #1 cited domain across all three]**:

| Engine | How to run it | What to record |
|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT** | Ask the buyer question cold (fresh chat, search/browse on). | Is the brand named? Which domains are cited? Is a competitor named instead? |
| **Perplexity** | Same question. Read the citation list, not just the answer. | Same. Note any Reddit/forum citation — it's frequently the source AI trusts over the brand's own site. |
| **Google AI Overview** | Same question in Google; capture the AI box. | Same. Note whether the AI box even appears (health queries get restricted/rolled-back AIOs). |

**Run each query at least three times.** A single screenshot is not proof — citation behavior varies run to run **[LIKELY — GEO measurement research, 2026]**. Record "named" only when the brand actually appears; mark everything untested `[VERIFY LIVE]`.

**The disagreement to look for and name out loud:** in healthcare queries, ChatGPT cites elite hospital sources ~1% of the time while Google AI Overviews cite them 33% **[VERIFIED — BrightEdge, Oct 2025–Jan 2026]**. ChatGPT leans .gov and medical-specialty orgs; Google leans elite institutions. *The brand's own marketing page is almost never the answer on either engine — unless it carries institutional-grade proof.* That sentence is the whole sale.

### Score it (0–10)
- **9–10:** Named in the answer on 2+ engines for category-level questions; cited domains include the brand's own substantiated proof, not just a reseller or a Reddit thread.
- **5–7:** Named occasionally (usually comparison/branded queries only); category-level questions name the competitor or aggregators instead.
- **0–4:** Invisible across all three engines on category questions. The answer exists, the buyer reads it, the brand isn't in it. *If you're not in the AI answer, you're not on the shortlist.*

**Verdict sentence (template):** "On [N] of [M] buyer questions, [brand] was not named; [competitor] was named on [N]. The engine answering the most buyers ([ChatGPT/Google]) is the one [brand] is most invisible on."

## SCORED SECTION 2 — Claim-Safety Map (the gate only a body-expert can grade)

**What it answers:** Of the brand's top public claims, which ones could draw an FTC letter, and which ones are *under-claiming* proof they actually have? Both are failures. One is a $53,088-per-violation failure.

**The wince line that opens it:** *"You're paying more for the marketing than for what's actually in the bottle."* That's what the customer's BS-detector says when claims outrun the science. The FTC says it with a number: **$53,088 per violation, effective Jan 17, 2025 [VERIFIED]**. Supplements may only *support* normal structure/function — never *treat, cure, or prevent* — and structure/function claims carry the **21 CFR 101.93** disclaimer requirement and the "competent and reliable scientific evidence" substantiation bar **[VERIFIED — FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance]**.

**Why this is baked UNDER the authority headline, never sold as a "legal audit":** the same content that survives FTC substantiation is the content AI cites. The compliance gate and the AI-trust gate are the same gate. Reading the actual evidence behind a physiology claim — knowing whether the study supports the exact wording or a weaker one — is the move a generalist GEO shop and a legal reviewer both miss. The first doesn't know the science; the second doesn't know retrieval.

### Method: inventory 5–8 claims, grade each on three axes

Pull the brand's top claims verbatim from the homepage + product page. For each, fill one row:

| # | Claim (verbatim) | Claim type | FTC-substantiation risk | Net-impression risk | AI-citation-worthiness | Safe rewording |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (paste) | clinical / structure-function / ingredient / behavioral / market-observation | High / Med / Low | What a buyer *hears* vs what's literally said | Carries a receipt AI can lift? Y/N | (rewrite to safe + citable) |

**The three grading axes, defined:**

1. **FTC-substantiation risk** — Does the claim require "competent and reliable scientific evidence," and does the brand visibly have it? *High* = disease/treatment implication or a benefit claim with no proof shown. *Med* = structure/function claim missing the 21 CFR 101.93 disclaimer or with thin support. *Low* = clearly framed support claim with cited evidence.
2. **Net-impression risk** — The FTC judges the *whole impression*, not the literal sentence. A page can avoid the word "treat" and still imply treatment. Flag anything where the buyer's reasonable takeaway exceeds the evidence.
3. **AI-citation-worthiness** — The same claim, scored for retrieval. Claims backed by a citation, a number, or a credentialed expressed mechanism are exactly what lifts generative visibility up to 40% **[VERIFIED — Princeton/Georgia Tech GEO paper]**. An unsupported benefit line is both an FTC risk AND uncitable. The safe rewrite usually *raises* both scores at once — that's the seam.

**Safe-wording rule (give them the pattern, not just the flag):** move from *promise* to *support + receipt*. "Cures your sleep problems" → "Magnesium glycinate supports normal muscle relaxation and sleep quality; [cited mechanism / study]. *This statement has not been evaluated by the FDA. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.*" The safe version is the citable version.

### Score it (0–10)
- **9–10:** Every top claim is a support claim with visible substantiation and a carryable receipt; disclaimers present; net impression sits inside the evidence.
- **5–7:** Claims are mostly defensible but several are under-supported (uncitable) or carry net-impression drift; disclaimers inconsistent.
- **0–4:** One or more claims imply treat/cure/prevent, or make a benefit claim with no proof shown. *Every claim needs a receipt before it deserves a headline.*

**Guardrail (state this in every audit, verbatim):** *This audit grades advertising claims for substantiation and citation strength. It is not legal advice and not medical advice. A High flag is a "have your counsel look before this goes wider," not a verdict. No claim here is a diagnosis, treatment recommendation, or representation about any individual's health.*

## SCORED SECTION 3 — Proof-Carry Gap

**What it answers:** Is the brand's proof *organized so the machine can carry it* — or is it good proof trapped where the engine can't reach it? This is the literal Bridge Message, made testable.

**The reframe:** "The brands getting cited aren't bigger. Their proof is organized so the machine can carry it." Check three carry-paths:

| Carry-path | What to look for | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Entity authority** | Does the brand read as a distinct, consistent entity across the web (consistent name, founder, credentials, structured "about the science")? Is the founder/expert a citable source in their own voice? | AI search rewards founder content because it carries semantic authority; brand pages read as marketing and get filtered. **[LIKELY]** |
| **Third-party + community presence** | Is the brand present where AI actually pulls from — Reddit, forums, independent reviews, .gov/medical-org adjacency? Or does its authority live *only* on its own site? | Reddit is the #1 cited domain across all three engines **[VERIFIED — Otterly]**. "If your authority only lives on your own site, you're invisible." |
| **Structured substantiation** | Are claims paired with a liftable proof unit — a stat, a study link, a named mechanism, a dated definition — in clean, crawlable form? | Citations + statistics + expert quotations lift generative visibility up to 40% **[VERIFIED — Princeton/GA Tech]**. Proof the machine can't parse is proof the machine can't carry. |

### Score it (0–10)
- **9–10:** Distinct entity, founder is a citable voice, real off-site presence, claims paired with liftable proof units.
- **5–7:** Good proof exists but lives mostly on-site and unstructured; little off-site presence; founder silent.
- **0–4:** Proof is real but completely uncarryable — locked on the marketing site, unstructured, no off-site footprint, no founder voice. *Good proof, invisible machine.*

## SCORED SECTION 4 — Sameness / Angle Gap

**What it answers:** Does the brand sound like a voice the machine can't synthesize for free — or like the edgeless template everyone else ships?

**The wince line that opens it:** *"AI didn't flatten your brand voice — it revealed you never had one."* And the moat-setter: *"It reads like it was written by someone who's never actually done the thing."*

**Method:** read the brand's last 5–10 pieces of public copy (site, social, blog) against the competitor's. Score for:
- **Synthesizability** — could a free model generate this in 10 seconds? If yes, it's commodity and AI has no reason to cite *them* over the aggregator.
- **Lived specificity** — is there a real mechanism, a real number, a real practitioner POV the machine can't fabricate? Or is it "support your journey / fuel your transformation"?
- **Founder voice present** — is there an unfakeable human point of view, or anonymous brand-speak?

### Score it (0–10)
- **9–10:** Distinct, substantiated, lived voice — a mechanism and POV no free model would produce.
- **5–7:** Competent but generic; defensible facts wrapped in interchangeable phrasing.
- **0–4:** Pure template slop; indistinguishable from the competitor and from a free model's output.

**Verdict sentence (template):** "Read blind, [brand] and [competitor] are indistinguishable — which means AI has no reason to carry one over the other, or over the free aggregator that summarizes both."

## SCORED SECTION 5 — Divergence Angles + the Named Position to Own

**What it produces:** 3–5 concrete divergence angles, then the single **named position** the brand should own — the spot in the category where its real proof, said in its real voice, becomes the thing AI carries.

**How to build the angles:** for each top buyer question where the brand is invisible (Section 1) and has real but uncarried proof (Sections 2–3), write the angle that fuses (a) a buyer wince, (b) the brand's substantiated proof, (c) a claim-safe frame, (d) a retrieval cue (definition / number / named mechanism). The angle is the seed of a citable, claim-safe asset.

**The named position** is one line the brand can own: not a tagline, a *category coordinate*. "The [outcome] brand whose [ingredient/mechanism] claims are the ones the science actually supports." It's the intersection where being cited and being claim-safe are the same move.

### Score it (0–10)
- This section scores **opportunity density**, not current state: 9–10 = 3+ wide-open angles with real proof behind them and a clean named position; 5–7 = angles exist but proof is thin or the position is contested; 0–4 = saturated, no proof edge, no ownable coordinate.
