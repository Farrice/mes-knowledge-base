## Executive Answer

No official Google source provides a "final export" or comprehensive public list of Google Business Profile (GBP) ranking factors, often called a "ranking source final export." Google's official guidance limits disclosure to three core factors—**relevance**, **distance** (proximity), and **popularity** (prominence)—without detailing sub-factors or weights[7][8].

### Verified Evidence (Directly Stated in Sources)
- Google's local algorithm uses **relevance** (how well a GBP matches the query via categories, keywords, reviews), **distance** (proximity to searcher), and **popularity** (prominence via reviews, links)[1][2][7][8].
- Specific GBP fields confirmed to influence rankings include: business title (keywords matching search), address/proximity, primary category, hours (open status), reviews (star rating, volume, content), attributes, services, website link[4][5].
- No Google document exports or lists all factors; official support pages emphasize the three pillars without specifics[7][8].

**Source URLs for Verified Claims**:
- Official Google: https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en [7]; https://support.google.com/business/thread/239976426 [8].

### Directional Signals (Unofficial Surveys/Expert Consensus Indicating Likely Influence)
Industry studies (e.g., 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey) rank GBP elements highly:
- **GBP** is the #1 Local Pack factor (32% weight), ahead of reviews (20%)[1].
- Top GBP signals: primary category (#1), keywords in title (#3), address in search city (#4), open status (#5), star ratings (#6)[1][4].
- Reviews impact via rating, volume, and snippets[1][4].
- Completeness (NAP consistency, photos, posts) boosts relevance[1][2][3].
No pricing, market, competitor, or social listening data in results.

### Inference (Logical Extension from Evidence)
- Lack of "final export" suggests Google withholds details to prevent manipulation, focusing public advice on basics while sub-factors (e.g., category precision) emerge from patterns in surveys[1][7].
- High survey weights for GBP (32%) imply it's foundational, but proximity often overrides others[1][2][4].
- "Smoke" (slang for unconfirmed signals) aligns with keyword-stuffing in titles working per video, but risks penalties[5].

### Unverified Assumptions (Speculative or Lacking Direct Support)
- Exact weights (e.g., 32% GBP) from third-party surveys, not Google-confirmed; may reflect correlation, not causation[1][3].
- NAP consistency yields "40% more local pack appearances"—survey claim without raw data[1].
- Centroids (city/industry hubs) as factors—historic, possibly outdated[4].
- No evidence for "check-ins," "near me" as 7% weighted signals beyond general mentions[3].
