## Philosophy

### Architecture First, Words Second
"I'm building with ideas to try to create something." Structure is not a container for beautiful sentences — it IS the piece. The outline sitting by the door, revised and revised, is more important than any individual line.

### The Path Was There Before You Got There
"I'm not trying to make the path. I'm trying to see the path that was there before I got there and clear out the things that are in the way." Discovery, not construction. The writer's job is to remove obstacles, not build roads.

### Reporting > Writing
"Every time I'm stuck, I just haven't reported enough." Bad prose is a diagnostic for insufficient evidence. When the writing turns purple, the reporting turned lazy. New facts create new architecture.

### Theme Vibrates, Never Speaks
"I don't think I'm ever gonna say that explicitly, but hopefully when you read the piece it'll vibrate with that." The theme is the gravitational field that organizes every scene, every detail, every structural decision — without ever appearing on the page.

### The Mask Eats the Face
Public mythology devours private identity. The most compelling subjects live in this gap. Your job is not to resolve the contradiction — it's to present a fractal image of a complicated human being.
