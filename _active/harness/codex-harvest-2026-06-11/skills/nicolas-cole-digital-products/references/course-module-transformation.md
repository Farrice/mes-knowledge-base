## Operating Thesis

An information product wins when it converts the buyer from "I understand" to "I did the thing." Course modules are not topic containers. They are action environments that sell the next action, remove avoidable mistakes, break work into micro-steps, pre-answer questions, prove applicability, add perceived value through assets, and end with a crystallized assignment.

## The 7-Section Course Module Stack

| Section | Function | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Reasons Why | Sell the buyer on why this action matters before asking them to act. | Motivation and urgency for the action. |
| 2. Mistakes To Avoid | Remove second-layer objections and make the buyer feel safer moving forward. | Confidence and guardrails. |
| 3. Micro-Steps | Break the action into the smallest executable path. | Step-by-step completion sequence. |
| 4. Questions And Uncertainties | Pre-answer likely quit-point questions. | FAQ, edge cases, troubleshooting, objections. |
| 5. Walkthrough Or Proof | Show the method working through case study, screen walk-through, or personal example. | Applicability proof. |
| 6. Bonus Asset | Pull out a template, checklist, prompt, worksheet, or example as a named value asset. | Perceived value and execution support. |
| 7. Assignment | Turn the main action into a final task the buyer completes. | Visible artifact, submission, or before/after evidence. |

## Results-First Product Rules

1. Define the final outcome before the content.
2. Define the buyer's current state and proficiency level.
3. Choose a coherent archetype band instead of trying to speak to everyone.
4. Convert the path into actions, not topics.
5. Use as many steps as the buyer needs to reach the outcome without unanswered questions.
6. Treat each module as one task with one visible output.
7. Match accountability to price: self-check below $350, guided review or live support above $350.

## Anti-Patterns

- Topic-first curriculum: "Here is everything I know."
- Understanding-first modules: "The buyer will understand X" without a task.
- Expert sprawl: speaking to beginners and elite experts in the same promise.
- Hidden asset burial: useful templates buried as ordinary lesson material.
- No quit-point coverage: questions answered only after learners get stuck.
- Vague homework: "Reflect on this" instead of "complete and submit this artifact."
