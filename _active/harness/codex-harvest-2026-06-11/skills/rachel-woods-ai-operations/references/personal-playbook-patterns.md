# Rachel Woods - Personal Playbook Patterns

Personal playbooks should reduce friction, capture preferences, and create repeatable execution. They can be lighter than client-facing playbooks, but they still need steps, standards, and feedback loops.

## Pattern 1: Weekly Planning And Delegation

**Promise**: Convert goals, calendar, open loops, and energy constraints into a realistic week plan with delegable tasks.

**Trigger**: Start or reset of the week.

**Inputs**:
- Current goals.
- Calendar.
- Open tasks.
- Deadlines.
- Energy and focus constraints.
- Active clients/projects.

Core steps:
1. Collect goals, deadlines, meetings, active projects, and constraints.
2. Identify must-do outcomes.
3. Rank by consequence and leverage.
4. Build a weekly plan.
5. Classify tasks into do, delegate, defer, delete.
6. Offer to draft delegated outputs or handoff briefs.

Delegation map:
- Objective: collect calendar constraints and deadlines.
- Good Enough: draft the weekly plan, sequence work blocks, identify tasks to defer.
- Expert: choose tradeoffs when goals conflict or client commitments are at risk.

Examples:
- Strong output gives a realistic week, names constraints, protects deep work, and produces handoff-ready delegation briefs.
- Weak output creates an idealized schedule that ignores calendar reality.

Feedback loop:
- At week's end, log what slipped, what was overplanned, what got delegated, and what rule should change next week.

## Pattern 2: Meeting Preparation

**Promise**: Prepare for a meeting with agenda, context, likely decisions, questions, and follow-ups.

**Trigger**: Meeting is scheduled or preparation block begins.

**Inputs**:
- Attendees.
- Meeting purpose.
- Prior notes.
- Desired decision.
- Relationship context.
- Materials to review.

Core steps:
1. Collect attendees, purpose, prior notes, desired outcome, and stakes.
2. Identify decisions needed.
3. Draft agenda.
4. Create prep notes and likely objections.
5. Produce follow-up templates.

Delegation map:
- Objective: gather notes and attendee context.
- Good Enough: draft agenda, questions, and follow-up templates.
- Expert: decide negotiation stance, sensitive language, or final commitments.

Feedback loop:
- After the meeting, add missed objections, better questions, and follow-up improvements.

## Pattern 3: Daily Execution Reset

**Promise**: Turn scattered tasks into one realistic operating plan for the day.

**Trigger**: Start of workday or midday reset.

**Inputs**:
- Today's calendar.
- Task list.
- Energy level.
- Deadlines.
- Messages or urgent requests.

Core steps:
1. Collect tasks, calendar, energy level, and deadlines.
2. Identify the few tasks that matter.
3. Sequence work blocks.
4. Define minimum viable progress.
5. Identify tasks AI can draft or clear.

Delegation map:
- Objective: list time blocks, deadlines, and urgent requests.
- Good Enough: draft daily plan and identify AI-draftable work.
- Expert: decide what matters most when priorities conflict.

Feedback loop:
- At day end, patch planning rules based on what was unrealistic or repeatedly ignored.

## Pattern 4: Inbox And Follow-Up Triage

**Promise**: Decide what needs a reply, what can be delegated, what should wait, and what should become a task.

**Trigger**: Inbox review block.

**Inputs**:
- Email/message list.
- Relationship context.
- Current projects.
- Response standards.
- Calendar and task constraints.

Core steps:
1. Collect message list or pasted emails.
2. Classify by urgency, importance, and relationship risk.
3. Draft replies where criteria are clear.
4. Flag messages needing human judgment.
5. Create task list and follow-up schedule.

Delegation map:
- Objective: categorize messages and extract dates/tasks.
- Good Enough: draft routine replies and summarize decision requests.
- Expert: approve sensitive replies and relationship-risk decisions.

Feedback loop:
- Log rewritten replies, missed relationship context, and new canned response rules.

## Personal Quality Rules

- Optimize for realistic execution, not ideal planning.
- Prefer clear next actions over long analysis.
- Preserve personal preferences and energy constraints.
- Turn recurring friction into a playbook patch.
- Keep the run prompt short enough to use repeatedly.
