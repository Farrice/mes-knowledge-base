# Quality Rubric

| Criterion | Score 4 - Acceptable | Score 7 - Good | Score 10 - Savant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feeling Clarity | Names a broad emotion | Names a precise emotional target and why it matters | Names the feeling, the memory, and the exact artifact that will carry it |
| Product Legibility | Explains the feature | Makes the use case clear | Makes the feature feel obvious, inevitable, and worth sharing |
| Memorability | Adds a hook or detail | Creates one remembered moment | Engineers emotional residue across first impression, action, and aftertaste |
| Restraint | Avoids obvious clutter | Rejects weak additions | Protects the product's soul through sharp scope decisions |
| Delight | Adds charm | Adds contextual surprise | Adds a tiny signature detail users retell without sacrificing usability |
| Presentation | Shows screens | Uses context and pacing | Sells the idea through story, device context, rhythm, and emotional payoff |
| Usability | Does not break core use | Improves clarity | Makes the product feel easier because emotion, structure, and function align |
| Stackability | Mentions related experts | Routes to useful companion skills | Creates a clear multi-expert sprint with handoffs and quality gates |

## Fail Conditions

- The output explains principles but does not produce the artifact.
- The design becomes cute at the expense of clarity.
- The feeling is generic: "delightful," "premium," "modern," or "clean" with no specific memory.
- The recommendation adds features instead of improving the product's emotional center.
- The launch or demo shows screens without context.
- The agent workflow improves instructions but not user experience.
