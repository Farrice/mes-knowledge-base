# Phrase Library

This file captures the 23 sequence families as deployable decision tools. Use the mechanics, not rote copy. Keep wording natural to the speaker, the relationship, and the stakes.

| # | Sequence Family | Primary Job | Use When | Avoid When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I'm not sure if it's for you, but..." | Rejection-free introduction | You need to introduce an idea softly | You are hiding a hard sell |
| 2 | "How open-minded would you be..." | Identity-safe agreement | You need openness to an alternative | The listener has already consented |
| 3 | "What do you know/understand about..." | Soft certainty challenge | They think they know enough | Your tone may sound superior |
| 4 | "How would you feel if..." | Future-feeling trigger | Emotion is missing from the decision | You are dramatizing false stakes |
| 5 | "Just imagine..." | Mental movie installation | They need to picture the outcome | The picture is vague or unrealistic |
| 6 | "When would be a good time..." | Assumptive scheduling | Time is the stated barrier | You have no legitimate next step |
| 7 | "I'm guessing you haven't got around to..." | Face-saving follow-up | They delayed action | You need accountability language |
| 8 | Simple swaps | Default-answer redesign | A yes/no question stalls progress | The current question already works |
| 9 | "You have three options..." | Choice reduction | Too many choices create delay | There are not three honest options |
| 10 | "There are two types of people..." | Identity contrast | Standards or action identity matter | It would sound condescending |
| 11 | "I bet you're a bit like me..." | Likeness bridge | You need rapport before the ask | You cannot truthfully claim similarity |
| 12 | "If... then..." | Conditional logic | You need consequence or promise clarity | The condition is not credible |
| 13 | "Don't worry..." | Anxiety reduction | They are nervous or apologetic | The concern is serious and unresolved |
| 14 | "Most people..." | Social permission | They need norm reassurance | You do not know what most people do |
| 15 | "The good news is..." | Positive reframe | A concern needs constructive context | There is no credible good news |
| 16 | "What happens next is..." | Next-step control | You need to close or proceed | They have not received enough information |
| 17 | "What makes you say that?" | Objection diagnosis | They state resistance | You already know the full reason |
| 18 | "Before you make your mind up..." | No-to-maybe recovery | A decision is premature | The decision is final or sensitive |
| 19 | "If I can, will you?" | Conditional close | A barrier may be removable | You cannot or will not explore the condition |
| 20 | "Enough" | Scope sufficiency | Quantity, proof, or resources need a threshold | You are guessing at what matters |
| 21 | "Just one more thing..." | Columbo add-on | The conversation is complete and rapport is high | The extra ask is too large |
| 22 | "Could you do me a small favor?" | Help request | You need a small action or referral | The favor is actually big |
| 23 | "Just out of curiosity..." | Post-no diagnosis | They delay or decline | You plan to argue with the answer |

## Phrase Stack Rules

- Use one lead phrase, one support phrase, and one next-step phrase.
- Keep the listener's dignity intact.
- Prefer a shorter phrase said with truthful intent over a long scripted monologue.
- When in doubt, ask the softer diagnostic question first.
