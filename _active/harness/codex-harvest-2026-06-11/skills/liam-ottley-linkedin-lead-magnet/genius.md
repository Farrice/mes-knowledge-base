# Liam Ottley - LinkedIn AI Lead Magnet Genius Context

Source: `extractions/video-context/3iR3kHxCwfo/` from Liam Ottley's "How to Turn LinkedIn into a Personal Brand Lead Magnet with AI."

This skill system extracts the operating method from Liam Ottley's framing and Lara Acosta's demonstrated LinkedIn method: use AI to help an expert turn what they know into LinkedIn content that attracts decision-makers, builds authority, and routes attention into a lead magnet or offer.

## Core Thesis

LinkedIn is valuable for B2B and service businesses because decision-makers are dense on the platform. The game is not "AI versus humans." It is people using AI well versus people using AI badly.

The source argues that most AI LinkedIn content fails because it asks a model for generic drafts before the strategy is clear. The better order is:

1. Clarify the buyer outcome.
2. Extract monetizable expertise.
3. Find strategic arbitrage angles.
4. Generate hooks and rehooks.
5. Draft with a proven structure.
6. Bridge the post to a lead magnet or offer.
7. Be present for the engagement window.

## Source-Grounded Patterns

### Pattern 1: Monetizable Expertise First

Monetizable expertise is knowledge that directly changes someone's outcome in a way they would pay for. The content does not begin with "what I know." It begins with "what changes for the buyer if they understand this?"

Use this before any AI drafting. If the expertise cannot name a buyer outcome, payment reason, or business consequence, it is not ready to become lead-generating content.

### Pattern 2: Strategic Arbitrage

Strategic arbitrage means borrowing the structure, timing, or attention of what already works while applying the creator's story, skill, or offer. It can come from trends, authority figures, popular post structures, buyer anxieties, industry shifts, or high-performing swipe examples.

The goal is not to copy ideas. The goal is to reuse attention patterns with a different insight and commercial bridge.

### Pattern 3: Hook Plus Rehook

The first line stops the scroll. The second line keeps the reader from bouncing before the "see more" click. Weak posts jump from a generic hook into a list. Strong posts extend the hook with specificity, proof, tension, or an outcome.

Good hook raw material:

- exact result
- specific number
- named mechanism
- "how I" rather than generic "how to"
- buyer pain with commercial consequence
- timely trend translated into a useful take

### Pattern 4: Broad-Narrow-Niche

A post should be broad enough to catch a larger professional audience, narrow enough to be relevant to a buyer problem, and niche enough to prove the creator is not generic.

Default sequence:

1. Broad hook: recognizable business desire, result, fear, or trend.
2. Narrow setup: specific buyer segment or problem.
3. Niche body: creator's method, evidence, mechanism, or example.
4. Commercial bridge: lead magnet, DM prompt, booking path, or offer.

### Pattern 5: Story Plus Education

The strongest personal brand posts both storytell and educate. Story earns attention and retention; lesson creates usefulness; actionable advice creates trust; a buyer-centered CTA turns the post into a lead magnet surface.

Stories are not about the creator. They are about what the reader sees in themselves, their goal, their fear, or their future self.

### Pattern 6: SLAY Structure

Use SLAY as a post structure:

- Story: open with a specific lived moment or concrete setup.
- Lesson: name the business or buyer insight.
- Advice: give tactical steps the reader can use immediately.
- You: point the ending back to the reader with a question, CTA, or next action.

This is best for authority, founder story, personal lesson, and educational posts that need retention before the teaching begins.

### Pattern 7: PAS For Sales Content

Use problem-agitate-solution when the post must sell or route into a lead magnet:

- Problem: name the buyer's current stuck state.
- Agitate: show the cost of leaving it unresolved.
- Solution: teach the method or offer the diagnostic/asset that helps them move.

The CTA should be clear, not needy: download, comment, DM, audit, checklist, walkthrough, or book.

### Pattern 8: AI Needs Identity And Proof Before Drafting

The AI workflow starts with context, not a blank prompt. Give the model:

- what the expert sells
- who they serve
- proof and results
- voice preferences and banned language
- offer advantages
- monetizable expertise
- strategic arbitrage topics
- swipe structures or saved posts

Ask for hooks before full posts. Pick the strongest hook, then ask for a full draft using the selected structure.

### Pattern 9: Same But Different Remix

Use swipe files as structure libraries. A good remix keeps the shape that created attention but replaces the story, examples, expertise, proof, and CTA.

The output must explain why the structure works so the creator learns, not just receives a draft.

### Pattern 10: First 30 Minutes Guard

Do not treat scheduling as "done." The source emphasizes that the first engagement window matters. The operating plan should reserve time immediately after posting for replies and high-quality comments.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic AI draft before strategy.
- "How to" hooks with no proof, result, or rehook.
- Content that teaches but never points to a next action.
- Personal stories with no business relevance.
- Trend jacking with no point of view.
- Lead magnets that collect attention but do not bridge to the paid offer.
- Scheduling posts and disappearing.
