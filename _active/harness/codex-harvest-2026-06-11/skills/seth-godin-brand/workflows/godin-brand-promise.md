# Godin Brand Promise Architecture

> **Expert**: Seth Godin | **Skill**: seth-godin-brand | **Tier**: Foundation
> **Produces**: Brand Promise Definition + Trust Engineering Protocol
> **Slash Command**: `/godin-brand-promise`

---

## Purpose

Define what your brand actually promises — not your tagline, not your logo, not your mission statement, but the *expectation* people carry when they hear from you. Then engineer the trust system that keeps that promise, especially when it's hard.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Brand/Business Name** — What entity are we diagnosing?
2. **Current Positioning** — How do you describe what you do today?
3. **Target Audience** — Who specifically are you serving?
4. **Recent Touchpoints** — Last 3-5 interactions customers had with you (support calls, deliveries, content, etc.)

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: The Nike/Hyatt Diagnostic

Apply Godin's brand-vs-logo test:

> **Test**: If [your brand] announced it was launching [a product in a completely different category], would people know exactly what it would be like?

- If YES → You have a brand. Proceed to Step 2.
- If NO → You have a logo. The promise is undefined or inconsistent.

**Scoring**:
| Score | Verdict |
|-------|---------|
| 🟢 Immediate clarity | Strong brand promise — refine and protect |
| 🟡 Partial recognition | Brand promise exists but is fuzzy — needs sharpening |
| 🔴 No idea | Logo, not brand — must build promise from scratch |

### Step 2: Promise Excavation

Answer Godin's two diagnostic questions:

1. **"Who do you want to help your customers become?"**
   - Not what you sell — what TRANSFORMATION you enable
   - Not demographics — identity shift

2. **"What are your customers hiring you to do?"**
   - Jobs-to-be-done through Godin's lens
   - These two answers are "probably related" — find the intersection

**Output**: One-sentence Brand Promise Draft:
> "When you hear from [brand], you can expect [specific experience/transformation], and we will deliver it [how] even when [hardest scenario]."

### Step 3: Trust Audit

Score current trust using Godin's formula:

| Trust Element | Question | Score (1-10) |
|---------------|----------|--------------|
| Promise Made | Is the promise explicit and clear? | |
| Promise Kept | Do you keep it consistently? | |
| Hard-Mode Kept | Do you keep it when it's HARD? | |
| Wizard Test | Do you make audacious promises you can't keep? | |
| Response Speed | When a customer raises their hand, how fast do you respond? | |

**The Eyeglasses Standard**: 20-minute response, optician interaction, making it right. "Worth more than a Super Bowl ad."

**The Anti-Pattern**: "Due to unusually heavy call volume, our phone trees have changed. Please leave a message and the AI will call you back." = Clear statement about marketing priorities.

### Step 4: Promise Stress Test

Identify the 3 hardest scenarios where your promise could break:

1. **Scale Pressure** — What happens when you're overwhelmed?
2. **Cost Pressure** — What happens when keeping the promise is expensive?
3. **Failure Pressure** — What happens when you actually make a mistake?

For each, design a **Promise-Keeping Protocol**:
- What's the response within 20 minutes?
- Who's authorized to make it right?
- What does "making it right" look like?

### Step 5: Brand Promise Architecture Output

Produce the final document:

```
BRAND PROMISE ARCHITECTURE
===========================

Brand: [Name]
Promise: [One-sentence promise]
Nike/Hyatt Score: [🟢/🟡/🔴]

TRANSFORMATION ENABLED:
- Customer becomes: [identity shift]
- Customer hires us for: [job-to-be-done]

TRUST ENGINEERING:
- Hard-mode protocol: [how we keep promise under pressure]
- Response standard: [time + method]
- Failure recovery: [what "making it right" looks like]

WIZARD OF OZ AUDIT:
- Promises we make: [list]
- Promises we keep: [list]
- Gap: [what's audacious but undelivered]
```

---

## Quality Gate

| Dimension | Minimum Standard |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Promise Clarity | A stranger could predict your next move |
| Trust Design | Hard-mode protocol defined for 3+ scenarios |
| Wizard Audit | No undelivered audacious promises |
| Specificity | Promise is one sentence, not a paragraph |
