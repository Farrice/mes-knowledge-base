# Oren Taste Development — Hidden Knowledge

## Non-Obvious Rules
- Taste is not an identity claim; it is an active practice of noticing, choosing, articulating, and revising.
- Quality evaluation should separate composition, effectiveness, and vibe before collapsing into a verdict.
- The knowing-acting gap is usually the real blocker: people can recognize better choices before they can consistently make them.
- Budget does not excuse bad judgment; the thrift test forces taste under constraint.
- A taste recommendation should include what to stop doing, not only what to admire.
- A strong critique must be specific enough that the next revision becomes obvious.
- Taste improves fastest when the operator studies exemplars, explains why they work, and then makes decisions in the real world.

## Diagnostic Questions
1. What exactly makes this good or bad: composition, effectiveness, vibe, or fit?
2. What would a more tasteful operator notice that the current operator missed?
3. Where is the user's taste stronger than their execution?
4. What constraint would expose whether this is real taste or just expensive preference?
5. What single decision would raise the quality floor fastest?

## Quality Bar
The answer should produce a usable judgment, not just admiration language. It should name the specific quality gap, the standard being used, the next decision, and the practice loop that will build better taste over time.
