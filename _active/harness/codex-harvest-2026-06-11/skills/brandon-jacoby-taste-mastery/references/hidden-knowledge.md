# Hidden Knowledge: Brandon Jacoby Taste Mastery

## 1. The Real Skill Is Balance

The key move is not "break rules" or "follow patterns." It is balance: knowing which battle deserves depth and which part should quietly use the known path.

## 2. AI Rewards People Who Already Run Through Walls

AI amplifies initiative more than it creates it. People who already questioned blockers, built around constraints, and sought new paths before AI get the largest gain.

## 3. Good Enough Is Often Commercially Fine

Many products only need good enough. Taste mastery begins with honesty about whether the project actually requires greatness, then chooses where greatness is worth the cost.

## 4. The Canvas Has Intelligence

Some discoveries cannot be prompted directly because the designer does not know how to ask for them yet. Manual tinkering, visual friction, and bad variants reveal possibilities.

## 5. Wrong Artifacts Are Social Tools

A wrong artifact is useful because it gives a founder, client, or team something concrete to reject. Rejection sharpens hidden criteria.

## 6. Taste Is Environmental

Taste is shaped by environment, curiosity, references, people, and the problems someone chooses to work on. It is not only an internal eye.

## 7. Design Mentorship Often Comes From Non-Designers

Business, product, founder, and operator mentors can make a designer stronger than another design critique because they teach consequence.

## 8. High Craft Is Not Luxury

Apple, Uber, and similar mass products show that taste can make an experience broadly accessible. Greatness can be simplification and distribution, not just premium rarity.

## 9. First Versions Being Bad Is a Professional Expectation

The professional difference is not that experts start better. It is that they trust the process enough to make the bad version and keep going.

## 10. The Signature Is the Moat

Abundance creates sameness. A recognizable signature, expressed through product decisions rather than surface styling alone, is what fights slop.
