# Oren Content-Team Architecture — Hidden Knowledge

## Non-Obvious Rules
- A solo social media manager is usually a structure problem, not a talent problem.
- "Media company" is an operating model, not a platform mix.
- Organic content, paid creative, landing pages, email/SMS, and external creator content should share faces, stories, and assets.
- A strategist's capacity is bounded by relationship and feedback load; too many contributor relationships quietly collapse quality.
- Production value becomes negative when it slows the publishing loop.
- Signature series should be locked only after repeatable evidence, not after the first clever idea.
- Product and content roadmaps should be built together; content that starts after product launch becomes reactive.
- Founder content is often a core brand asset, not a vanity channel.
- Agency partners are useful only when they understand how creator output becomes paid, owned, and conversion assets.

## Diagnostic Questions
1. What point of view does the brand express beyond selling the product?
2. Who owns strategy, who owns creation, and who owns publishing?
3. What weekly rhythm turns raw ideas into reviewed, scheduled, measured output?
4. Which assets move across organic, paid, conversion, and email/SMS?
5. What is the one anchor competitors cannot copy?
6. What production habit is slowing publish speed without improving business outcomes?
7. Which pod or contributor relationship is above capacity?
8. What proof would justify scaling into another pod?

## Quality Bar
The answer should leave the operator with a team map, cadence, production constraint, and next operating move. Avoid vague calls for "more content," "better storytelling," or "stronger creative" unless they are converted into owners, rituals, standards, and asset flow.
