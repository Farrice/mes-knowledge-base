# Envelope Architecture Builder

## Pre-Flight Gate
Inputs required:
- Project type and intended reader.
- Raw notes, transcript, research dump, outline, or idea pile.
- Desired ending, if known.

Load `../genius.md` and use the fragment-sorting method.

## Skill Acquisition
Use Martel's envelope logic:
- gather before drafting
- break material into fragments
- assign each fragment a job
- sequence toward a known destination
- preserve room for discovery

## Execution
1. **Inventory the Hodgepodge**: Split the input into discrete fragments. Keep facts, phrases, scenes, questions, images, claims, symbols, and ending signals separate.
2. **Assign Fragment Jobs**: Label each fragment as `scene`, `fact`, `language`, `symbol`, `character pressure`, `question`, `ending`, or `discard`.
3. **Build Envelopes**: Group fragments into envelopes by scene, chapter, argument movement, or emotional stage.
4. **Sequence the Journey**: Arrange envelopes from entry to ending. Mark fixed points and movable points.
5. **Name the Drafting Use**: For each envelope, write the first drafting action the writer should take when opening it.

## Content Type Adaptations
| Type | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Fiction | Group by scene and pressure shift |
| Essay | Group by question, example, counterpoint, and ending |
| Founder Story | Group by before-state, fracture, decision, cost, proof, and changed worldview |
| Brand Narrative | Group by problem, symbol, customer scene, proof, and belief shift |
| Speech | Group by opening image, story beats, audience turn, and final charge |

## Output Requirements
Produce:
- `Fragment Inventory`
- `Envelope Map`
- `Sequence`
- `Fixed vs. Movable Beats`
- `Drafting Instructions`
- `Discard / Parking Lot`

## Quality Gate
- No envelope is just a vague theme.
- Every retained fragment has a job.
- The ending pressure is visible.
- The writer can draft from the map without starting from nothing.
