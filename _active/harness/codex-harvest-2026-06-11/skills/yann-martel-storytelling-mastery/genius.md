# Yann Martel Genius Context

## Core Philosophy
Martel's craft treats story as a human exchange, not a delivery mechanism. The writer prepares deeply, arranges material carefully, and then withdraws enough control for the reader to co-create. The best story gives closure without full possession: the reader feels guided, then released with a living question.

## Master Method

### 1. Gather Before Drafting
Collect research, words, facts, images, scene flashes, structural ideas, and ending signals. Do not rank too early. The raw mass matters because unexpected details later become doors.

### 2. Turn Research Into Envelopes
Break the mass into fragments. Sort each fragment by story function:
- scene
- fact
- language
- symbol
- character pressure
- question
- ending

The envelope is a thinking tool. It prevents blank-page panic and forces material to take shape.

### 3. Know the Destination
Martel does not worship beginnings. He cares more about where the reader lands. Before drafting, name the final feeling, the final unresolved question, and the final proof that the journey meant something.

### 4. Draft With Local Freedom
The plan is a departure point. During drafting, fragments can move, shrink, grow, or disappear. The writer is prepared enough to improvise.

### 5. Make the Reader Work
Books beat passive media by giving the reader space. Withhold enough description, explanation, and emotional labeling that the reader must supply inner images and meaning.

### 6. Use Allegorical Distance
Animals, masks, footnotes, altered forms, and strange containers let the writer approach heavy subjects without dullness or sermonizing.

### 7. Score the Prose
Punctuation is not grammar housekeeping. It is rhythm, breath, emphasis, acceleration, and pause.

### 8. End With Resolved Mystery
The ending must complete the reader's core expectation but not answer the work into death. A good ending satisfies and still glows.

## Signature Moves

### Sort the Hodgepodge
Before writing, turn research into discrete fragments and assign each to a job.

### Protect the Reader's Imagination
Remove surplus description and emotional instruction. Keep vivid cues; delete controlling explanations.

### Let Animals Carry Human Weight
Use animals when direct human representation would trigger prejudice, fatigue, or excessive heaviness.

### Keep the Character Open
When the subject is the pressure, make the character ordinary enough for reader entry.

### Elevate a Humble Form
Footnotes, envelopes, chapter counts, reports, and other practical forms can become story architecture.

### Break One Rule at a Time
Formal experiment works when the reader still has handrails.

### Release the Gift
After full effort, the work belongs partly to readers. Their interpretation is not a bug.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Over-Supplied Story**: Describes, explains, and resolves until the reader has no role.
- **Clever Formatting Without Need**: Experimental layout that advertises author cleverness instead of serving story.
- **Fact Hoarding**: Research displayed because it was collected, not because it earns story value.
- **Symbol as Costume**: Animal or allegorical figure chosen decoratively rather than functionally.
- **Character Caricature**: Physical description so specific it becomes cartoonish.
- **Mystery as Fog**: Withholding clarity where the story actually needs coherence.
- **Genre Betrayal**: Violating the reader's core contract without giving a better contract.
- **AI Sincerity Fraud**: Delegating intimate human feeling to a machine while pretending it came from the heart.

## Quality Gate
Before shipping any Martel-style output, answer:
1. What fragments did we gather before drafting?
2. What is the destination?
3. What does the reader get to imagine?
4. What has been withheld for good reason?
5. Does the chosen form carry meaning?
6. Does punctuation control tempo?
7. Which reader expectation is fulfilled?
8. Which mystery remains alive?
9. Does the output feel authored by a person?
