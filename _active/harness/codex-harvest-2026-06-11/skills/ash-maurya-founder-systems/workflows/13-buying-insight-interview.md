# Buying Insight Interview

## Pre-Flight Gate

Use when the founder needs to know what customers actually want, what they will buy, or which message/offer should move them. Do not use this to ask for feature requests. The customer is not responsible for inventing the solution.

## Execution

1. **Define The Old Way**
   - Name the current alternative: tool, spreadsheet, email, consultant, course, habit, workaround, or doing nothing.
   - Recruit people actively using that old way.
   - Avoid interviewing people who only say they might like the new solution.

2. **Use The One Useful Question**
   - Ask: "Walk me through the last time you encountered this situation and tell me where you struggled."
   - Keep the customer in a specific recent scene.
   - Ask "what happened next?" until the full journey is reconstructed.

3. **Reconstruct The Buying Journey**
   - Trigger: what happened right before they looked for a solution?
   - Old-way use: when, why, and how did they use the existing solution?
   - Search: what else did they consider?
   - Fired alternatives: why did each option fail?
   - Chosen alternative: what made the winning option good enough?
   - Tradeoffs: what did they accept even though it was imperfect?

4. **Detect Hidden Problem Cues**
   - Pet peeves: small annoyances they have normalized.
   - Struggles: time-consuming or costly friction.
   - Workarounds: improvised systems that reveal unmet demand.
   - Repetition: patterns that appear across roughly 10 interviews.

5. **Map Buying Criteria**
   - Hiring criteria: what must be true for a solution to get chosen?
   - Firing criteria: what makes an alternative unacceptable?
   - Anxiety criteria: what could stop the switch?
   - Value criteria: what cost, delay, risk, or upside justifies payment?

6. **Audit The Insight Against Facts**
   - Separate quote/fact from interpretation.
   - Mark any founder inference as unproven.
   - Reject insights that only confirm what the founder already wanted to build.

7. **Frame The Commitment Ask**
   - Replay the customer's last-time story.
   - State the old-way failure.
   - Present the promise against the buying criteria.
   - Ask for the smallest meaningful commitment: paid diagnostic, pilot, deposit, data access, calendar slot, or referral.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Context | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Startup | Focus on old alternatives, switching trigger, and paid pilot criteria |
| Service | Mine prior vendors, failed DIY attempts, and willingness to pay for diagnosis |
| Digital product | Validate repeated workarounds before building curriculum or software |
| B2B | Capture stakeholder pressure, budget trigger, approval anxiety, and deadline cost |

## Output Requirements

- Old-way target definition
- Interview script
- Behavior reconstruction map
- Pet peeve/struggle/workaround table
- Buying criteria map
- Fact-vs-interpretation audit
- Commitment ask script

## Quality Gate

Pass only if the output predicts buying behavior from a specific past journey. Fail if it relies on feature requests, compliments, or future-tense intent.
