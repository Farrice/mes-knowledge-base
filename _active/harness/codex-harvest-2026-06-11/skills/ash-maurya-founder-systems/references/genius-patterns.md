# Genius Patterns

## 1. Business Model As Product

Treat the business model as the first thing to design. The product is only one component inside a larger system of customer segment, problem, promise, price, channel, cost, and traction.

## 2. Customer Forces Physics

Customers switch when push plus pull overcomes inertia plus friction. Loss aversion means the negative forces often outweigh product benefits, so the pitch must break the old way and reduce switching anxiety.

## 3. Switching Story Extraction

Every purchase has three acts: trigger, search, and outcome. Extract the moment that broke the old solution, the alternatives that were fired, the criteria that mattered, and the tradeoffs the buyer accepted.

## 4. 5P MVP Design

MVP design is five ordered decisions: problem, prospects, promise, price, and packaging. If one is guessed, the MVP may work technically and fail commercially.

## 5. Alternative-Anchored Pricing

Pricing starts from the current alternative. If the customer already pays with money, delay, labor, or risk, the new price is judged against that existing cost.

## 6. Demo-Sell-Build

Do not build first and ask later. Design the offer, demo the promised outcome, ask for commitment, and only then build the minimum package that customers effectively bought.

## 7. Behavior Over Opinion

The system privileges actions: last time the problem happened, what they tried, what they paid, what they rejected, and what they commit to now.

## 8. Sequential Risk Reduction

Problem discovery precedes solution design. Solution design precedes offer delivery. Offer delivery precedes demand validation. Each phase earns the next.

## 9. Learning Velocity Advantage

The founder's real speed is not shipping speed. It is the rate of validated learning per week.

## 10. Triggered Early Adopter Segmentation

The best first customer is not the biggest market. It is the segment with the strongest push, active workaround, visible cost, and willingness to switch now.

## 11. Mafia Offer Construction

An irresistible offer restates the exact problem, promises a specific outcome, demonstrates the path, prices against value, and removes the largest switching anxiety.

## 12. Traction-First Sequencing

Traction is the goal. Everything else is judged by whether it increases evidence, commitment, revenue, or learning clarity.

## 13. The One Useful Interview Question

Ask customers to reconstruct the last real situation: what happened, where they struggled, what they did, and what followed. This reveals buying behavior better than opinions about the future.

## 14. Old-Way Mining

Study current users of existing alternatives. New products usually win by breaking a limitation in the old way that customers already tolerate.

## 15. Buying Criteria Extraction

Ask what made the customer choose one solution over another. The answer reveals what they value enough to buy.

## 16. Pet Peeve, Struggle, Workaround Detection

Listen for recurring small annoyances, time-consuming struggles, and improvised workarounds. These are often the earliest form of a commercial problem.

## 17. Fact-Audited Interview Analysis

Every insight must be checked against what the customer actually said or did. This prevents the founder from turning a keyword into the feature they already wanted to build.
