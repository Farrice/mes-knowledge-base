# Genius Patterns

1. **Story-job matching**: Pick the story by the prospect's resistance: confusion, uncertainty, objection, low trust, low desire, or lack of familiarity.
2. **Puzzle-piece persuasion**: Use multiple short stories in sequence instead of forcing one story to carry the whole sale.
3. **Plainspoken authority**: Write the way a believable person would speak in business, not like a narrator auditioning for drama.
4. **Familiar-then-different bridge**: Make novelty acceptable by starting with what the prospect already understands.
5. **Open-loop pain**: Describe lived pain without resolving it yet, so the offer can become the missing ending.
6. **Proof-loaded origin**: Make the origin explain why the person or offer can credibly deliver.
7. **Future desire with guardrails**: Make the future attractive without outrunning evidence.
8. **Mechanism simplification**: Explain the process only far enough to make the benefit believable and different.
9. **Trust by witness**: Let customers, experts, certifications, tests, and case studies carry belief.
10. **Believability repair**: Remove inconsistency, vagueness, exaggeration, and stiff language before final output.
11. **Research-backed empathy**: Use prospect language from interviews, reviews, support logs, comments, and forums.
12. **Compression by strongest facts**: Write long to find the material, then cut to the smallest story that still persuades.
