# David Garfinkel - Persuasion Story Code

This skill turns raw business material into short, true, conversational persuasion stories. It is based on David Garfinkel's `The Persuasion Story Code`, extracted from the local 140-page PDF in `extractions/persuasion-story-code/source/`.

The core distinction: dramatic stories entertain and teach broad life lessons; persuasion stories move a prospect toward agreement. The skill is built for entrepreneurs who need stories that sell, reassure, explain, build trust, and travel across sales copy, posts, emails, pitches, webinars, and video.

## Decision Framework

Use this skill when the business asset needs one of these jobs:

- **Credibility**: Why you, this product, or this idea deserves belief.
- **Familiarity**: Show the prospect you know their pain, world, pressure, or situation.
- **Future desire**: Make a true future promise feel visible and desirable.
- **Reassurance**: Calm doubt about novelty, usability, endurance, risk, or refund.
- **Explanation**: Make a process, product, mechanism, or alternative choice clear.
- **Trust**: Prove that other people, authorities, or customers already validate the claim.
- **Objection handling**: Answer concern through story rather than argument.

Use another skill first when the job is pure brand strategy, funnel math, market research, cinematic production, or platform growth mechanics. Use Story Code after those inputs exist to turn them into persuasive human communication.

## Anti-Patterns

- Do not turn every message into a hero's journey.
- Do not write long dramatic arcs when a three-paragraph persuasion story will do.
- Do not invent facts, results, testimonials, client stories, or proof.
- Do not use vague claims where a concrete sequence of events is needed.
- Do not exaggerate past what can be defended.
- Do not use corporate, academic, or ornamental language.
- Do not make the story responsible for the entire sale; persuasion stories usually work as precise puzzle pieces inside a larger message.
