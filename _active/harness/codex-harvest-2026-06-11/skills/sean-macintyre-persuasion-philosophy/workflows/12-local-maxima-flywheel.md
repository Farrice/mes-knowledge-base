# Sean Macintyre × Authority Flywheel — Local-Maxima Career Diagnostic

## Role

You are running a stack: Sean's local/global maxima reasoning feeds Authority Flywheel deployment. The Authority Flywheel skill builds compounding authority assets (newsletter, content, IP, voice memo workflow). It's powerful — but deployed at the wrong career position, it cements you at a local maximum you should be leaving.

The diagnostic: before you double down on building authority where you are, classify whether *where you are* is worth doubling down on.

Sean's quote: *"You at a local maxima cannot know if you've reached a global maxima. The only way to do is to explore. And if you are at a local maxima, all that you can do is exploit it until you realize that's all you're going to get from this particular plateau of your life."*

This workflow is intentionally uncomfortable. It produces three possible outputs:
1. **"Yes, deploy the Flywheel here"** — you're at a worth-exploiting local maximum
2. **"Deploy the Flywheel, but plan an exit"** — you're at a plateaued local maximum and the Flywheel will buy time/cash to fund exploration
3. **"Don't deploy the Flywheel here yet"** — you're already exploring, and authority-building in the current niche would cement a position you're trying to leave

**Before executing**: Read `genius.md` § "Genius Pattern 5: Local-Maxima vs. Global-Maxima."

## Input Required

1. **Current Position**: Niche, role, business model, audience, income level, time investment.
2. **Trajectory**: Income growth, audience growth, satisfaction trajectory over the past 6-18 months.
3. **Restless / Settled Signal**: Honest gut answer — does the current position feel like home or like a waystation?
4. **Adjacency Awareness**: What other niches / business models / roles are visible from your current position? What "what ifs" surface regularly?
5. **Authority Flywheel Plan** (if any): The specific Flywheel deployment plan — what asset, what cadence, what positioning — that's about to launch or recently launched.

> **Pre-Flight Gate**: This requires honest self-assessment. If the user's input is performative ("everything is amazing, growth is exponential"), the diagnostic will be inaccurate. Probe gently for restlessness signals.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Position Classification

Apply the local/global maxima framework:

| Question | If yes → | If no → |
|---|---|---|
| Are you currently growing materially in this position (revenue, audience, capability)? | Possibly local max **exploiting** (good) | Possibly local max **plateaued** (warning) |
| Has the growth rate flattened in the past 3-6 months? | Local max plateaued | Continue diagnostic |
| Do you regularly fantasize about a different niche, role, or business model? | Restlessness signal — possibly should be exploring | Probably settled — local max exploiting |
| If you doubled down for 3 more years, would you be a higher version of yourself or a more entrenched version of the same self? | "Higher" → exploit. "More entrenched" → explore. | |
| Have you tried 1-2 explorations and found them disappointing? | You may have already found the global maximum (Sean's "scary thought") | More exploration may be warranted |

Output one of three classifications:
- **Local max — exploiting (growing)**: deploy the Flywheel hard
- **Local max — plateaued**: deploy the Flywheel as exit-funding, plan exploration
- **Pre-exit / exploring**: hold the Flywheel; don't cement a position you're leaving

### Phase 2: The "Goalposts Will Move" Inoculation

Matthew's observation: *"The goalposts will always move no matter how far you keep improving."* This applies to every classification — meaning even at local-max exploitation, the writer should expect to want more eventually.

Build into the recommendation:
- **Current horizon**: how long is this exploitation worth before goalposts move predictably?
- **Pre-planned exploration window**: when (specifically) will you re-run this diagnostic?
- **Alarm signals**: what specific metrics, feelings, or external events trigger an early re-diagnosis?

### Phase 3: Authority Flywheel Calibration (per classification)

#### If "Local max — exploiting":
Deploy the Flywheel at full intensity. The Flywheel compounds — its value scales with time-in-niche. You're at the right time to start.

Calibration:
- **Newsletter / pole asset**: positioned for the *current* niche; lean into it
- **Content cadence**: aggressive
- **Identity claim**: "the [specific thing] expert" — own the niche

#### If "Local max — plateaued":
Deploy the Flywheel with an *exit-financing* frame. The Flywheel will produce cash and credibility while you fund the next exploration. But its positioning should leave room for adjacency.

Calibration:
- **Newsletter / pole asset**: positioned slightly broader than the current niche; "the [adjacent broader category]" rather than the precise current role
- **Content cadence**: sustainable, not aggressive (you'll be redirecting energy outward)
- **Identity claim**: "[role] who thinks like a [next role]" — bridge identity
- **Exit plan**: write down the date / signal that triggers the pivot

#### If "Pre-exit / exploring":
Hold off on the heavy Flywheel deployment. Cementing authority in a position you're leaving is a waste.

Calibration:
- **Light Flywheel**: minimal asset (single newsletter or podcast) framed around *exploration itself*
- **Content cadence**: experimental
- **Identity claim**: "in transition" — explicit about exploration
- **Reset trigger**: when the exploration produces a new clear position, re-run this diagnostic to greenlight the full Flywheel there

### Phase 4: The Pro-Divorce Frame (Sean's Provocation)

Sean's actual statement: *"This is why I'm so pro-divorce. And so very in favor of starter marriages. Most entrepreneurs and most copywriters — we get into the profession because we wanted something more."*

This is the local/global maxima reasoning applied to *commitments* — including career commitments, niche commitments, and business model commitments.

**The frame**: it's not failure to leave a position. It's the only way to know whether the position was the global maximum or just the highest peak you'd seen so far. *Starter niches* are valid. The wrong move is staying out of fear of having "wasted" the time invested.

If the user's diagnosis is "plateaued" but they resist the recommendation to plan exit, surface this frame explicitly. The exit isn't disloyalty to past investment; it's the only honest move under uncertainty.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Career situation | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Solo creator / freelancer** | Heavy weight on restlessness signal + adjacency awareness; less weight on income trajectory (which can lag) |
| **Agency owner** | Add: is the agency growing or revenue-stagnant despite hours growing? Revenue-stagnant + hours-growing = plateaued |
| **Course creator / coach** | Add: are student outcomes still improving, or have they stabilized? Stabilized outcomes + churn-and-burn dynamics = plateau warning |
| **Newsletter / Substack writer** | Trajectory metrics: subscriber growth, paid conversion, open rate over time |
| **In-house copywriter** | Position is often pre-defined; the diagnostic is about whether to stay employed vs. go independent |
