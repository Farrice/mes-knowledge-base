# James I. Bond — Brain Glue Genius Context

## Core Genius

James I. Bond's Brain Glue method treats persuasion as **memory engineering for buying decisions**. The central move is to stop arguing harder and start shaping the prospect's emotional, sensory, and pattern-recognition systems so the message becomes easy to understand, hard to forget, and natural to repeat.

The method is especially useful for entrepreneurs because most offers fail before the proof is evaluated: the buyer does not grasp the idea fast enough, does not feel it, or cannot remember it later. Brain Glue fixes the message at the level of first impression, cognitive simplicity, emotional charge, and verbal stickiness.

## Decision Framework

Before executing any workflow, answer these in order:

1. **What must stick?** A product name, core promise, sales argument, hook, metaphor, offer, campaign idea, or behavior change.
2. **Who must repeat it?** Buyer, audience, referral source, salesperson, founder, customer, investor, or team.
3. **What is blocking stickiness?** Confusion, blandness, abstraction, mistrust, wrong tribe, weak sensory image, no surprise, no verbal pattern.
4. **Which Brain Glue lever fits the block?**
   - Confusion: understanding ease, analogy, perspective, demonstration.
   - Blandness: trigger words, odd/unexpected, sensory elevation.
   - Weak recall: rhyme, alliteration, chiasmus, anchor word ownership.
   - Wrong audience feel: tribal alignment, rejection to attract.
   - Low trust: expectation setting, honest admission, demonstration.
   - Low desire: metaphor, sensory benefit, humor, oxytocin, social identity.
5. **Can the phrase carry a business job?** It must name, clarify, differentiate, reassure, provoke, qualify, or convert.

## STEAM ATTRACTORS

Brain Glue's master framework is STEAM ATTRACTORS:

| Lever | Job |
|---|---|
| Setting Expectation | Control the first frame so the buyer sees what you need them to see |
| Tribal Alignment | Connect to identity, belonging, pride, language, values, and shared enemies |
| Easing Understanding | Make the offer cognitively simple enough to grasp in a second |
| Analogies and Metaphors | Turn abstract claims into pictures, objects, scenes, and comparisons |
| Anchoring | Attach the idea to something already known, valued, feared, loved, or owned |
| Toning Voice | Express calm confidence, authority, and emotional control |
| Triggering Oxytocin | Use humor, fascination, warmth, or emotional safety to lower resistance |
| Rhyming | Create completion pressure and memory through sound patterning |
| Alliteration | Use repeated sounds to make names and phrases easier to process |
| Chiasmus | Reverse structure or meaning to make a statement feel profound and repeatable |
| Trigger-Words | Wake attention with emotionally charged words, used ethically and precisely |
| Odd and Unexpected | Break the expected pattern so the brain has to pay attention |
| Rejecting to Attract | Increase desire by naming who it is not for and who qualifies |
| Sense Elevation | Add sight, sound, smell, taste, touch, gesture, or demonstration |

## Signature Moves

- **Start with the obvious asset**: Mine the existing name, category, audience phrase, competitor, feature, or customer behavior before inventing something clever.
- **Find the one feature that deserves the spotlight**: Do not cram all benefits into the first frame. Hook with the most memorable buying reason, then earn the rest.
- **Add perspective**: Compare the claim to something the audience already knows so scale, speed, risk, or value becomes visible.
- **Turn logic into a scene**: Replace abstract claims with visuals, demonstrations, gestures, sounds, and sensory language.
- **Create completion pressure**: Use rhyme, repeated sound, and reversible phrasing so the phrase feels finished and wants to be repeated.
- **Tell the truth early**: An honest admission or clear expectation can make the buyer relax and trust the rest of the message.
- **Use the edge carefully**: Surprise, trigger words, rejection, and line-approaching names work only when they serve the buyer's understanding and the brand can tolerate the heat.

## Quality Rubric

| Criterion | 4: Acceptable | 7: Strong | 10: Brain Glue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | The idea is understandable | The idea is easy to explain | The buyer can repeat it after one exposure |
| Emotional Charge | The words are competent | The words create feeling | The phrase triggers an involuntary reaction |
| Memory | The line is readable | The line has a pattern | The line sticks like a jingle, proverb, or nickname |
| Business Function | Sounds good | Helps the asset perform | Directly clarifies, differentiates, qualifies, or sells |
| Audience Fit | Generic buyer language | Uses audience cues | Feels native to the buyer's tribe |
| Sensory Vividness | Abstract | Some concrete detail | The buyer sees, hears, feels, tastes, or smells it |
| Ethical Force | Persuasive | Strong but fair | Powerful without deceiving or manufacturing false claims |

## Anti-Patterns

- **Clever but jobless**: A phrase that sounds witty but does not clarify, differentiate, or sell.
- **All tools at once**: Stuffing rhyme, alliteration, metaphor, and shock into the same line until it feels fake.
- **Wrong tribe mimicry**: Borrowing identity markers without evidence of real audience understanding.
- **Trigger-word abuse**: Using intense words for cheap attention when the offer cannot support the emotion.
- **Metaphor fog**: A metaphor that creates a picture but makes the actual offer less clear.
- **Feature pileup**: Trying to say every benefit before the buyer understands the main one.
- **Edgy mismatch**: Using rejection, shock, or line-approaching language for a brand that needs trust, safety, or discretion.

## Voice DNA

Brain Glue execution should sound direct, commercial, vivid, and practical. It should not sound academic. The output should move quickly from diagnosis to usable language. Favor short candidate lines, clear rationales, and immediate deployment notes.

When producing assets, use punchy labels:
- **Sticky Core**
- **Buyer Memory Hook**
- **Tribal Cue**
- **Perspective Shift**
- **Sensory Upgrade**
- **Rejection Line**
- **Campaign Spine**

## Ethical Boundary

Brain Glue can make weak, misleading, or manipulative ideas more persuasive. Do not use it to hide defects, invent proof, exploit protected vulnerabilities, or make false claims. Strong persuasion should make a true value easier to understand and remember.
