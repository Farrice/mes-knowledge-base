# Brain Glue Genius Patterns

**Source:** Book PDF, 204 pages, approximately 45k extracted words.

**Domain:** Sales messaging, persuasive language, naming, advertising, social content, video scripting, brand memory.

## Genius Patterns

### 1. First-Frame Control
- **What Bond does:** Sets the expectation before the buyer forms their own frame.
- **Executable behavior:** Open with the frame you want believed: the honest reason for contact, the standard to expect, the surprising truth, or the main lens.
- **Deploy when:** Sales calls, landing pages, video opens, pitch decks, webinar intros.
- **Success metric:** The audience interprets later claims through the intended frame.

### 2. Tribal Belonging Before Argument
- **What Bond does:** Looks for identity affiliation before persuasion.
- **Executable behavior:** Map buyer tribes, pride markers, language, heroes, enemies, rituals, and caution zones.
- **Deploy when:** Founder-led content, niche offers, community campaigns, high-trust sales.
- **Success metric:** The audience feels "this is for people like us" before the pitch.

### 3. Cognitive Simplicity
- **What Bond does:** Compresses complex ideas into something the prospect can picture or compare.
- **Executable behavior:** Replace abstract explanation with one feature, one perspective comparison, one demonstration, or one analogy.
- **Deploy when:** Technical products, new categories, expert offers, AI services, consulting.
- **Success metric:** A non-expert can explain the value accurately after one pass.

### 4. Perspective Translation
- **What Bond does:** Makes numbers, claims, and scale meaningful by comparing them to known references.
- **Executable behavior:** Add "compared to what," "near what," "as fast as," "as easy as," "enough to," or "the equivalent of."
- **Deploy when:** Stats, ROI claims, speed claims, market size, transformation promises.
- **Success metric:** The buyer understands why the claim matters, not just what the claim is.

### 5. Metaphor as Product Interface
- **What Bond does:** Uses metaphor to make products feel tangible, named, and ownable.
- **Executable behavior:** Ask "what does this remind me of?" then turn the answer into a name, tagline, visual, or pitch frame.
- **Deploy when:** Naming, book titles, offer categories, ad concepts, video hooks.
- **Success metric:** The metaphor clarifies the product and heightens emotion.

### 6. Anchoring to Existing Mental Real Estate
- **What Bond does:** Borrows familiarity from something already loved, feared, repeated, or known.
- **Executable behavior:** Anchor to a famous phrase, category leader, cultural object, competitor, award, passion topic, or word you want to own.
- **Deploy when:** New products, challenger positioning, category creation, underdog campaigns.
- **Success metric:** The new idea inherits faster understanding from the existing anchor.

### 7. Calm Confidence
- **What Bond does:** Treats voice and delivery as persuasion assets.
- **Executable behavior:** Strip strain, desperation, and over-explaining. Use controlled pacing, clear emphasis, and grounded certainty.
- **Deploy when:** Video scripts, sales calls, webinars, founder content.
- **Success metric:** The delivery makes the message feel easier to trust.

### 8. Oxytocin Before the Ask
- **What Bond does:** Uses laughter, fascination, and human warmth to lower resistance.
- **Executable behavior:** Add a gentle joke, surprising observation, self-aware admission, or vivid curiosity beat before asking for commitment.
- **Deploy when:** Negotiation, video opens, sales emails, live presentations.
- **Success metric:** The buyer becomes more receptive without feeling manipulated.

### 9. Redintegration Through Sound
- **What Bond does:** Uses the brain's need for completion to make phrases memorable.
- **Executable behavior:** Generate rhyme, rhythm, matched syllable counts, repeated sound, and phrase completion loops.
- **Deploy when:** Slogans, hooks, product names, personal brand phrases, short-form video lines.
- **Success metric:** The phrase is easy to finish, repeat, and remember.

### 10. Chiasmus as Instant Wisdom
- **What Bond does:** Reverses a phrase so it feels deeper than a plain statement.
- **Executable behavior:** State the idea, identify its opposite, then reverse structure or meaning.
- **Deploy when:** Thought leadership, speeches, captions, campaign slogans, belief-shift content.
- **Success metric:** The line feels quotable without needing explanation.

### 11. Trigger Word Wake-Up
- **What Bond does:** Uses emotionally charged words to interrupt half-attention.
- **Executable behavior:** Insert a precise charged word at the moment the audience needs to wake up: worry, stink, naked, vomit, danger, ugly, dead, etc.
- **Deploy when:** Hooks, subject lines, sales diagnosis, ads, cold outreach.
- **Success metric:** The word increases attention while staying connected to a real point.

### 12. Pattern Violation
- **What Bond does:** Breaks expected language patterns to force attention.
- **Executable behavior:** Twist a cliche, create a strange combination, approach a boundary, or give an unexpected ending.
- **Deploy when:** Crowded feeds, ads, email subject lines, book/product names.
- **Success metric:** The phrase interrupts the scan and makes the audience want the next line.

### 13. Rejection as Qualification
- **What Bond does:** Makes desire stronger by naming who should not buy.
- **Executable behavior:** Define ideal buyer, non-buyer, disqualifying traits, minimum standard, and "not for you if" lines.
- **Deploy when:** Premium offers, consulting, communities, high-ticket products, contrarian brands.
- **Success metric:** Good prospects lean in; bad-fit prospects self-select out.

### 14. Sense Elevation
- **What Bond does:** Adds sight, sound, smell, taste, touch, gesture, or demonstration to make the idea physical.
- **Executable behavior:** Convert abstract benefit into sensory language, a visual symbol, a sound cue, tactile phrase, demonstration, or buyer participation.
- **Deploy when:** Video, sales pages, brand identity, product demos, presentations.
- **Success metric:** The audience experiences the message instead of merely reading it.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

These are paraphrased calibration anchors, not source reproduction.

### Exemplar 1: The Demonstration That Explains the Future
- **Context:** A new product or technology is hard to understand.
- **Move:** Replace explanation with a small physical demonstration that acts as a symbol of the whole promise.
- **Why it works:** The prospect does not need to understand every technical detail. The demonstration becomes the memory handle for the opportunity.

### Exemplar 2: The One-Feature Name
- **Context:** A product has many features, but one feature drives the buying decision.
- **Move:** Name and headline the product around the one feature that matters most.
- **Why it works:** Buyers do not remember feature inventories. They remember the vivid reason the thing exists.

### Exemplar 3: The Reversible Quote
- **Context:** A plain argument needs to become a belief.
- **Move:** Reverse the words or concepts so the point feels inevitable.
- **Why it works:** Chiasmus gives the audience a complete mental object. It feels finished, wise, and repeatable.

## Anti-Exemplar

### The Logical Feature Dump
- **What mediocre looks like:** A page, pitch, or script lists every feature, proof point, audience benefit, and explanation at once.
- **Why it fails:** The prospect has no memory handle. Brain Glue requires one sticky path into the idea before detail expands.
