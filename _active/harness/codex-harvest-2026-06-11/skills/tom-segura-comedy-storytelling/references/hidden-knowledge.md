# Tom Segura Hidden Knowledge

## 1. The Joke May Be Inside The Reason, Not The Event

Many stories fail because the event is interesting but the reason for telling it is missing. The "way in" gives the audience pressure before they hear the anecdote.

## 2. The Dumb Version Is Often A Doorway

Early bad ideas should be tested, not judged. The stage equivalent for writing is a rough draft, voice note, peer riff, or low-stakes post.

## 3. Obvious Does Not Mean Useless

An obvious observation can be powerful if it is widely felt and rarely articulated. The danger is not obviousness; the danger is saying it without specificity or turn.

## 4. Text Needs Built-In Delivery

When writing loses voice, face, and live timing, it must compensate through line breaks, sentence length, syntax, and word choice.

## 5. Collaboration Should Spark Your Own Angle

A writers' room is valuable when other angles unlock your angle. It fails when everyone averages into a committee voice.

## 6. Callback Charm Has A Shelf Life

A callback proves the writer is paying attention. Repetition past that point turns the same signal into neediness.

## 7. Comedy Mode Matters

Storytelling, social commentary, traditional bits, pure jokes, and alt angles operate differently. The same material can fail if forced into the wrong mode.

## 8. Humor For Marketing Needs A Job

In business writing, the humor must prove shared world, insight, competence, warmth, or confidence. Cleverness alone is not a good enough reason.
