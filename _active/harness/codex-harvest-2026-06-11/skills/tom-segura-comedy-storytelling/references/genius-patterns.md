# Tom Segura Genius Patterns

## 1. Conversation Premise Detection

Tom treats talking as a testing environment. Offstage conversation reveals when a phrase, complaint, or observation has life.

Deploy when a draft feels polished but lifeless. Before rewriting, generate raw versions in a conversational register and mark anything that feels like "that's a thing."

## 2. Annoyance As Signal

He distinguishes charged opinion from indifference. Being bothered, delighted, grossed out, or suspicious gives the joke a direction.

Deploy when writing sounds neutral. Ask what the writer actually feels about the situation.

## 3. Say-The-Obvious Ownership

His observational mode prizes the familiar thing everyone has seen but no one has said plainly. The laugh comes from recognition, not novelty alone.

Deploy when a social post needs a "yes, exactly" moment.

## 4. Editor-Delay Reps

Tom's advice is to write down and try even the dumb thoughts. The editor should not decide before the premise has contact with pressure, conversation, or audience response.

Deploy when brainstorming is too tasteful too early.

## 5. Reluctant-Laugh Surprise

He values the moment where the audience is pulled somewhere unexpected and laughs despite their judgment.

Deploy when the draft is safe but the audience would tolerate a sharper truth after trust is established.

## 6. Goodwill-Before-Edge Sequencing

Provocative material works better after rapport. The writer earns more range by first proving shared world and warmth.

Deploy in authority, sales, and client-facing contexts.

## 7. Clear Premise Deconstruction

Tom admires comics who state the premise clearly, then break it down from multiple angles until the idea is exhausted.

Deploy in thought leadership, social commentary, and explanation-heavy writing.

## 8. Way-In Before Story

The reason for telling a story can matter more than the event. The story needs pressure, stakes, or gratitude before the anecdote begins.

Deploy when a personal story reads as "this happened" instead of "this matters."

## 9. Long-First Compression

For story work, Tom recommends telling the long version first, then cutting unnecessary information, then adding punchlines or tags.

Deploy when the writer is trying to produce the tight version before discovering the material.

## 10. Word-Choice Timing

Some accurate words are funnier than other accurate words because of sound, image, force, or social flavor.

Deploy during line edits after the structure is working.

## 11. Callback Restraint

Callbacks create shared world and signal attention, but they become annoying when repeated too often.

Deploy in long posts, threads, speeches, and essays with one or two strong recurring details.

## 12. Written-Comedy Exposure

Written comedy loses the performer's control over cadence, facial expression, and immediate feedback. The text must carry timing on its own.

Deploy for posts, essays, emails, and ghostwritten material.
