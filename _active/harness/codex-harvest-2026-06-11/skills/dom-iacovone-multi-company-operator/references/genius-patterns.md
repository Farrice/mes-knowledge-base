# Genius Patterns

## GP-1: Four-Block Annual Compression

Turn a messy business into four annual priorities, then make every weekly operating check answer whether those priorities are on pace.

Use when: the founder is running many initiatives with no clear ranking.

## GP-2: Finance-First Course Correction

Treat revenue mix, CM1, gross-to-net, and trade spend as truth surfaces that reveal strategy quality before the story does.

Use when: the team is emotionally attached to a launch or campaign.

## GP-3: Stage-Gate Before Launch Energy

Every product must pass strategic sense, category improvement, margin creation, and channel fit before it deserves launch resources.

Use when: a new product idea is exciting but unproven.

## GP-4: Founder Absence As Team Leverage

The founder creates higher-quality executive debate by leaving meetings where their presence suppresses candor.

Use when: the team waits for founder approval or avoids disagreement.

## GP-5: Buyer Feedback Is Not Product Truth

Retail buyers can provide useful constraints, but the brand must not let buyer requests rewrite product strategy.

Use when: a channel partner asks for product, price, or SKU changes.

## GP-6: Channel Incentive Mapping

Distribution choice depends on incentives: DTC, TikTok, Amazon, direct retail, and DSD each create different execution behaviors.

Use when: a founder is choosing channels by prestige instead of sell-through mechanics.

## GP-7: Leakage To Valuation Translation

Trade spend, 3PL costs, freight, and logistics choices should be translated into valuation impact, not only operating expense.

Use when: a growth company is preparing for diligence or scaling retail.

## GP-8: Exception With Evidence

Broad defaults can be overridden when the category, partner, buyer, margin, and channel conditions justify the exception.

Use when: a plan seems to contradict best practice but may be correct in context.
