# Genius Patterns

## 1. Temporary North Star

Use a short-lived event narrative as the filter for all content. It should translate the brand's larger belief into the specific moment.

## 2. Sets Before Posts

Inventory the physical space first. A set is any area where action, people, props, and camera position can repeat.

## 3. Itinerary Reverse Engineering

Treat every scheduled experience as a content opportunity, then decide whether it is reach, immersion, proof, or product content.

## 4. Modular Hooking

Generate many openings from one set by changing the first action, prop, person, question, sound, or visual detail.

## 5. Scalable Concept Filter

Choose ideas that can produce multiple clips with low friction. A speedrun concept should survive real event pressure.

## 6. Character-Led Sets

Find the person who naturally creates energy and put a camera around them. Characters make a space feel alive.

## 7. Friction-Light Participation

Make guest participation easy: one prompt, one short action, one clear reason to say yes.

## 8. Top and Middle Funnel Split

Top funnel earns attention from the widest relevant audience. Middle funnel makes viewers feel present inside the brand world.

## 9. Live Upgrade Loop

When the environment offers a better idea, stop and upgrade the plan. Add action, background detail, or story compression.

## 10. Execution Stations

Assign director, shooters, editor, handlers, and approval roles before the event. The system fails if footage sits unused.

## 11. Bridge Building

Use guests, partners, and collaborators as bridges into other audiences, but make the clip useful to them too.

## 12. Story Compression

Use props, action, and micro-conflict to tell a complete story in seconds.
