# /offer-positioning-north-star - Business Architecture Elevation Pipeline

Build a launch-ready business architecture package that clarifies who the business serves, what it exists to change, how it should be positioned, what it sells, how the service is delivered, how prospects convert, and which claims are safe to use.

Use this when a plan has good raw material but needs the same kind of truth-grounded business upgrade produced for Coach Cooz: clear north star, buyer-facing positioning, premium offer architecture, service delivery map, Triage Audit conversion logic, and proof discipline.

## Usage

```text
/offer-positioning-north-star [client/brand/project + source files or folder]
/offer-positioning-north-star "Coach Cooz V3 avatar/content + V6 source files"
```

## Core Principle

Do not polish the offer first.

Lock the truth first.

The final package should make the business easier to run, easier to explain, easier to sell, and harder to overclaim.

The buyer should be able to answer:

1. "Is this for me?"
2. "What problem does this solve?"
3. "Why is this different from the alternatives I already know?"
4. "What happens if I say yes?"
5. "What proof makes this credible?"

## Workflow Stack

Run the stack in this order. Use named workflows/skills when available; otherwise apply the protocol locally.

### 1. Strategic Clarity

Load and apply:

- Seth Godin strategic clarity
- smallest viable audience logic
- problem versus situation distinction

Purpose:

- Choose the smallest viable audience.
- Define what the business exists to change.
- Separate the market shelf from the deeper mission.
- Name the internal north star, public promise, service north star, sales north star, and delivery north star.

Quality gate:

- The public mission must be plain enough for a cold buyer.
- The deeper mission can carry meaning, but it cannot replace clarity.

### 2. Positioning Context Lock

Load and apply:

- April Dunford positioning
- alternatives and competitive context
- buyer risk reduction

Purpose:

- Choose the category the buyer already understands.
- Name the target buyer in practical language.
- List the alternatives the buyer compares against.
- Define differentiated value against those alternatives.
- Separate plain buyer language from brand-layer language.

Quality gate:

- A cold buyer should understand what the business does before seeing poetic, branded, or internal language.

### 3. Offer And Economics Architecture

Load and apply:

- Jason Fladlien offer stack and identity-offer logic
- Vincent Hu / offer economics
- price-resistance and ascension logic

Purpose:

- Build a buyer-facing offer ladder.
- Explain fit, non-fit, promise, delivery, proof, price logic, and decision rules.
- Correct unrealistic revenue math.
- Mark public offers versus invite-only validation paths.

Quality gate:

- Do not claim that a few standard clients create a high monthly revenue target if the math does not support it.
- Do not publicly scale an offer before demand, delivery, and close data validate it.

### 4. Service Design

Load and apply:

- service design logic
- premium delivery logic
- client experience mapping

Purpose:

- Show what happens after someone buys.
- Map onboarding, first 30 days, weekly cadence, touchpoints, boundaries, measurements, and premium markers.
- Make the premium price visible through service design, not adjectives.

Quality gate:

- The buyer should see why this costs more than commodity support.
- The operator should see what must happen every week.

### 5. Triage And Conversion System

Load and apply:

- audit-to-client conversion logic
- objection handling
- fit and path recommendation logic

Purpose:

- Define the diagnostic doorway.
- Prevent menu selling.
- Prevent free consulting.
- Recommend one path from diagnosis.
- Define follow-up, close quality, and no-fit exits.

Quality gate:

- The call must end with one clear path or a clear no-fit decision.

### 6. Proof And Claims Discipline

Load and apply:

- proof map logic
- permission review
- measurement review
- claim softening and validation rules

Purpose:

- Classify claims as verified, permission-sensitive, measurement-sensitive, hypothesis, operational target, or avoid.
- Match proof to buyer lane and offer path.
- Flag claims that need interviews, data, client approval, or source checks.

Quality gate:

- Every public claim must answer: Is this true? How do we know? Can we say it this way?

### 7. Final Clarity And Anti-Slop Audit

Load and apply:

- authenticity audit
- anti-slop audit
- audience language check

Purpose:

- Remove strategist jargon.
- Remove buyer-confusing language.
- Remove broad claims.
- Remove emotional language that has no practical meaning.
- Keep the voice plain, serious, and alive.

Final question for every section:

> Would a high-fit buyer understand this, trust this, and know the next step?

If the answer is weak, revise.

## Quality Gates

Before finalizing, check:

- Cold-buyer clarity: buyer understands what it is before branded language.
- Revenue math: price, capacity, and timeline are realistic.
- Premium service: the support level justifies the price.
- Triage fit: one path is recommended, not a menu.
- Proof safety: risky claims are softened or held.
- Voice: plain, serious, emotionally alive, no generic consultant language.
