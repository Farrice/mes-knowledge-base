## Uniqueness Audit: What This Source Adds That We Don't Already Have

The existing April Dunford skill is strong on **execution mechanics** (5-component positioning, sales pitch architecture, champion enablement, GTM bowling pin). What it lacks — and what this source delivers — falls into three categories:

### 1. Positioning Diagnostics & Failure Modes (ENTIRELY NEW)

The existing skill has no diagnostic capability. This source provides:

- **The "Back It Up" Test** — how to detect positioning failure from live sales call behavior (prospect confusion, wrong-competitor comparisons, "why would I pay for that?" responses)
- **The Three Positioning Failure Modes**: (1) not thinking about it at all, (2) treating it as a marketing-only exercise, (3) premature category creation
- **The Distinction Engine**: positioning ≠ messaging ≠ branding ≠ taglines — a disambiguation protocol that prevents the most common conflation errors
- **When Positioning ISN'T the Problem**: explicit diagnostic for separating positioning problems from lead gen problems, sales execution problems, and product-market fit problems

### 2. The Fear Architecture of B2B Decision-Making (CRITICAL GAP FILL)

The existing skill mentions indecision but doesn't operationalize the psychology. This source delivers:

- **The JOLT Effect Integration** — 40-60% of B2B deals end in no decision, not competitor loss. This reframes the entire sales narrative.
- **Fear > Emotion** — in B2B, the dominant emotion is career-preservation fear, not aspiration. This is a PARADIGM-LEVEL insight the existing skill acknowledges but doesn't weaponize.
- **The Incumbent Gravity Problem** — "nobody gets fired for picking Salesforce" is a structural force, not a competitor. The source provides the counter-strategy (niche domination → credibility escalation).
- **B2B vs. B2C Value Bifurcation** — explicit framework for why consumer positioning rules (Liquid Death, vibes, aesthetics) categorically fail in B2B considered purchases.

### 3. Category Creation Anti-Patterns & Market Vocabulary Control (PARTIALLY NEW)

The existing skill has Pattern 11 (Anti-Category-Creation Default) but only as a guard rail. This source provides:

- **The 90/10 Rule**: 90% of the time, enter an existing category and dominate a subsegment. 10% of the time, you're genuinely creating a category.
- **Vocabulary Ownership Protocol**: when you ARE doing category creation, naming the category and defining key concepts in a way that advantages you is a competitive weapon.
- **The Analyst/Influencer Mapping**: understanding who has influence on your buyers (Gartner, system integrators, podcasters, industry experts) and investing in educating those entities.

## New Genius Patterns to Add (8)

1. **The Opening Scene Protocol** — Positioning as "the opening scene of a movie" — set where, when, who, vibe before plot details
2. **The CRM-to-Investment-Banking Pivot** — The signature case study of niche domination → expansion, used as the primary teaching vehicle
3. **The Grandmother Test Rejection** — "Would your grandmother understand this?" is the WRONG test for B2B. Resonance with specialized buyers > universal clarity
4. **The Fear-First Emotion Map** — In B2B, fear of career consequences > all other emotions. Not "looking cool" — career survival
5. **The 40-60% No-Decision Reality** — The most fearsome competitor is not another vendor — it's inaction driven by decision paralysis
6. **The Liquid Death Fallacy** — Consumer marketing "best practices" (vibes, emotions, branding) systematically fail in B2B considered purchases
7. **The Vocabulary Weapon** — In emerging categories, naming key concepts advantages the namer. Vocabulary = competitive moat
8. **The "Problem Inside the Problem" Detection** — Great CEOs see a problem within the obvious problem — and that inner problem becomes their positioning insight

## New Hidden Knowledge to Add (6)

1. **The Sales Call as Positioning Sensor** — The fastest way to assess positioning quality isn't focus groups or surveys — it's listening to first sales calls for confusion, wrong-competitor references, and "why would I pay for that?"
2. **The Investor Pitch Contamination** — Founders use their investor deck (future-oriented, trend-based) as their sales deck (present-oriented, value-based). Different audiences, different pitch physics
3. **The Customer Discovery Paradox** — Customers will tell you they'd buy something they won't actually buy. The only reliable test is the sales pitch, not the interview
4. **The Cross-Functional Non-Negotiable** — Positioning created by marketing alone ALWAYS fails because sales ignores it. Must be co-created with product, sales, CS, and CEO
5. **The Academic Marketing Blindspot** — All university marketing research is done on consumer packaged goods. Professors stretch CPG findings to B2B without testing. Most "best practices" are B2C artifacts
6. **The Point-of-View Imperative** — Great positioning requires a founder's unique perspective on the market — "we see the problem inside the problem." Without a POV, positioning becomes generic
