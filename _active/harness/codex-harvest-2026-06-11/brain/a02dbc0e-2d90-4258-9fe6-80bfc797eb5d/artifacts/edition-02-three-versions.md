# Edition 02: The Coachella Reckoning — 3 Final Versions

> **Writers Room**: Miller (zero-load), Kallaway (open loops / retention), Roth (cinematic structure), Wright (scene craft)
> **Subject line**: I took my two-year-old to Coachella and my judgment of it broke.

---

# VERSION A: "The Open Loop"

**Strategy**: Opens with the JJ/Bieber hook as the first line. Pairs dashstar with Shelter early. Self-reflective turn before the signature line.

---

A two-year-old and Justin Bieber taught me the same thing at Coachella last weekend.

I'll get to both.

Saturday evening, Madeon played "Shelter." He'd retired that song. It's one of my most cherished. When the intro started building, my wife Jen screamed "BABE!" — I grabbed JJ, pulled him up on my shoulders, and we shared a moment I'd filed under fifteen-years-from-now.

Jen walked out to "Shelter" at our wedding. Acoustically, on keyboard, the whole song. I didn't know it was coming. It's carried a specific weight for me ever since.

Later that night, Knock2 played "dashstar\*" — the song from JJ's gender reveal. At the drop, we'd popped cannons that revealed what I already knew in my heart. Our first born was going to be a boy.

Two songs that carry my life, same night, shared with my son. At Coachella of all places.

---

I've been judgmental of Coachella for a long time. Not the music — some of my favorite DJs play every year. What I couldn't stand was the culture. Outfits as a whole personality. Strangers pushing past you for the photo. The whole thing feeling less about what was on stage than about being seen near it.

We went to Weekend 2 for a friend's birthday. Me, Jen, JJ in a wagon, and our best friend Ryan. Not the demographic they put on the flyer.

When music hit JJ, he'd want up. Arms out. Eyes on the stage. Moving with the bass. When it didn't, he'd point at the phone. Ten minutes of whatever two-year-olds watch to refill. Then he'd hear something and want back in.

Nothing about it was performance. He was there when he was there and checked out when he wasn't. Nobody grading him on the transitions.

By 10:20 he was out cold in the wagon as we walked toward Bieber.

---

The internet was deciding, in real time, that Bieber's set was a disaster. "Phoned in." Lazy. Disrespectful.

Most of my attention was on JJ asleep in the wagon. But I watched enough to see what was actually happening.

Bieber walked to the front and let fans touch him. Made eye contact with people who'd been screaming his name since grade school. During "One Less Lonely Girl," Hailey pushed Billie Eilish out on stage and let Billie have her crush moment. The crowd got their old songs. Then Bieber played the new music he actually wanted to share.

He gave them all of himself. In the parts he wanted to give.

---

That's what JJ and Bieber had in common, though I didn't see it until the drive home.

Both showed up as themselves. Gave what was real. Didn't perform anything they weren't feeling. JJ slept when he was tired. Bieber played old songs without pretending to love them and new songs without pretending the crowd wanted them.

Neither got read as present by the people watching from outside.

I'd been one of those people. Scrolling past Coachella posts for years, deciding what I was looking at from photos I wasn't in.

Next to the girl filming her face to check her makeup, and the guy mouthing "we need more shots" — past the guy in a fit that screams *I paid a lot for this* — was Jen, quietly dancing to a song from our wedding with our son asleep in a wagon three feet away.

**You can't tell presence from performance from the outside.**

---

We drove home Sunday with JJ out before we hit the freeway. Jen looked at me and said, "Well, that worked better than expected."

I laughed. We drove with the music down.

---
