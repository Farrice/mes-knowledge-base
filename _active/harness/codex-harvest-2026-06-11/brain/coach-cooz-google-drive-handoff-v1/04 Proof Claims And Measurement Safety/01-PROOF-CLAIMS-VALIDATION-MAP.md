# Coach Cooz Proof And Claims Validation Map V3
## Executive Read

Coach Cooz has unusually strong proof for a private coaching business.

The risk is not lack of proof.

The risk is using real proof in ways that overclaim, confuse, or create permission problems.

This map separates:

- safe public claims
- permission-sensitive claims
- measurement-sensitive claims
- strategic hypotheses
- claims that need buyer interviews or sales data before becoming public truth

## Claim Status Key

| Status | Meaning | Public Use |
|---|---|---|
| Verified | Supported by internal source or named proof, still check permission if personal | Use with care |
| Permission-sensitive | May be true, but named-client use requires approval | Use anonymized until cleared |
| Measurement-sensitive | Depends on measurement method or body composition proof | Soften or cite method |
| Hypothesis | Strategic belief not yet proven by market behavior | Test, do not present as fact |
| Operational target | Internal goal, not a buyer promise | Keep internal |
| Avoid | Too risky, confusing, or unsupported | Do not use |

## Safe Claim Alternatives

### Mari

Risky:

> Mari gained 16 lbs of pure muscle in 3 months.

Safer:

> Mari gained 16 lbs in 3 months while getting visibly stronger and following the protocol with exceptional discipline.

If measurement method is credible:

> Mari gained 16 lbs in 3 months. If we use the body-composition method from that period, the result showed a major lean-mass increase. I treat it as an exceptional case, not a universal promise.

### Long-Arc Proof

Risky:

> My clients stay forever.

Safer:

> Some clients have stayed 3-9 years because the work keeps paying them back.

### 90-Day Proof

Risky:

> Real change in 90 days.

Safer:

> The first win can show up in 90 days when the foundation is built right. The deeper proof is whether the work still holds later.

### Premium Pilot

Risky:

> I offer Executive System Architecture for `$5k-$10k/mo`.

Safer:

> I am validating one invite-only premium pilot for a buyer whose body has become a serious bottleneck to work, family, and life.

## Real-Call And Composite Claims

Never say:

> I had a call last week...

Unless:

- the call literally happened last week
- identifying details are removed or permission-safe
- the claim is not a composite

Use instead:

> I have heard this pattern enough to recognize it.

Or:

> A prospect said a version of this recently.

Or:

> This is a composite of a pattern I see often.

## Claims To Validate Before Public V4

### Buyer Interviews

Run 10-15 current/past client interviews:

- Why did you buy?
- What almost stopped you?
- What did you think you needed?
- What did you actually need?
- Why did you stay?
- How would you describe Cooz to a peer?
- What would sound fake if Cooz claimed it publicly?

### Permission Checks

For each named client:

- Can we use first name?
- Can we use initials?
- Can we use profession?
- Can we use company/show?
- Can we use years trained?
- Can we use photos/video?
- Can we use numbers/lifts?
- Can we quote directly?

### Measurement Checks

For Mari and any body recomposition claim:

- starting weight
- ending weight
- measurement date
- method used
- who measured
- margin of error
- photos available
- what can be responsibly claimed

### Sales Data Checks

Before expanding offer claims:

- Triage Audit show rate
- close rate
- path recommended
- objection by path
- hours per close
- revenue by source
- remote support satisfaction
- pilot willingness to pay

## Public Proof Rules

1. One proof claim per asset is usually enough.
2. Match proof to the buyer lane.
3. Do not use proof as a flex; use it to teach.
4. Exceptional cases must be labeled as exceptional.
5. Use years and consistency carefully; retention means renewal evidence, not contract lock-in.
6. Named clients need permission.
7. Job titles, shows, records, and measurements need accuracy checks.
8. Avoid saying "guarantee" unless there is a real guarantee with terms.

## Proof Quality Gate

Before any public claim ships, answer:

- Is this true?
- How do we know?
- Does the client approve?
- Is the number measured or remembered?
- Is the claim typical or exceptional?
- Could a skeptical buyer challenge it?
- Is there a safer way to say it?
- Does this proof help the buyer understand why Cooz is the right choice?

If any answer is unclear, soften or hold the claim.
