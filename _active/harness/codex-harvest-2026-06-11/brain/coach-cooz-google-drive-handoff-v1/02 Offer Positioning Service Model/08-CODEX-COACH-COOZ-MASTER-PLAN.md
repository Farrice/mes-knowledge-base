# Coach Cooz - Codex Master Plan

Research date: 2026-05-03
Purpose: A comparison-ready alternative to the Antigravity V6 package.

## The Big Difference

I would not lead cold buyers with "The Resurrection Coach."

I would keep Resurrection as the identity and brand world, but I would lead with plain buyer language:

> Strength and energy rebuilds for professionals over 40 whose body is starting to fall behind the life they built.

That line does three jobs faster:

- It tells cold buyers what this is.
- It names who it is for.
- It puts the emotional idea inside a practical category.

"Resurrection" is powerful once they are warm. Cold buyers need to know if this is fitness, executive coaching, therapy, spirituality, or health support. The first line should remove that confusion.

## The Positioning

### Market context

Most successful professionals over 40 are not looking for a trainer because they forgot how to work hard.

They are stuck between bad alternatives:

| Alternative | What It Promises | Why It Fails This Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Local personal trainer | Workouts and accountability | Often too tactical; no life-context diagnosis |
| Luxury gym | Access and environment | The gym is not the relationship |
| Fitness app | Convenience and low price | Easy to ignore when life gets hard |
| Biohacking stack | Data and optimization | More information, not more follow-through |
| 12-week transformation | Fast visible change | Often does not hold when the schedule breaks |
| Executive coach | Performance and mindset | Solves the wrong layer if the body is the bottleneck |
| Doing nothing | No cost today | Becomes expensive later |

### Category

Use this market category:

> Long-arc strength and energy coaching.

Do not try to create a new category from day one. Dunford rule: enter an existing mental shelf first, then sharpen the subcategory.

### One-line positioning

> Coach Cooz rebuilds strength, energy, and consistency for professionals over 40 whose body is starting to fall behind the life they built.

### Brand identity layer

Use this after the plain-language line:

> The deeper work is Resurrection: the person who stops adjusting to the diminished version and comes back to a body they recognize.

## The Buyer

The buyer is not "high-performing professionals" in general. That phrase is too broad by itself.

The sharper buyer:

> A 40-60 year-old professional with money, responsibility, and body memory. They used to be strong, athletic, consistent, or at least physically confident. Now they are tired of pretending the slide is temporary.

### What they know

- They are not where they used to be physically.
- Their energy is less predictable.
- Their recovery is worse.
- Their calendar keeps winning.
- They have tried to restart more than once.

### What they do not want

- A cheap trainer.
- A motivational persona.
- A bro-fitness room.
- A wellness sermon.
- A generic app.
- A 12-week sprint that disappears when work spikes.

### What they want but may not say

- "I want to feel like myself again."
- "I want my body to stop being the weak link."
- "I want someone serious enough to help me stay with it."
- "I do not want to be sold like a beginner."

## The Offer Ladder

I would simplify the buyer-facing offer to three options plus one invite-only pilot.

The public message should not be "four pathways." It should be:

> Start with the Triage Audit. If there is a fit, Cooz recommends one of three rebuild paths.

### Path 1 - Burbank Private Rebuild

For local clients who need the room, the schedule, and the in-person relationship.

- Delivery: 3-4 in-person sessions/week
- Includes: training, nutrition targets, recovery targets, weekly check-in
- Price: `$1,500/mo`
- Founding option: first 5 local clients can lock `$1,275/mo` for 12 months
- Minimum: 12 weeks
- Best proof: Mike, Sam, Karima, Buzz

### Path 2 - Remote Daily Rebuild

For travel-heavy or non-local clients who need daily support but not a gym appointment.

- Delivery: programming, nutrition/accountability, 5-day/week async support, weekly review
- Price: `$1,500/mo`
- Standing target: `$2,000/mo` after proof validates
- Minimum: 12 weeks
- Best proof: Corey-style travel/schedule clients

Important: this path must prove value against much cheaper remote coaching. The daily relationship has to be concrete, not just "Voxer access."

### Path 3 - 90-Day Rebuild Sprint

For buyers who want a defined first commitment before ongoing work.

- Delivery: 12-week training, nutrition, recovery, and decision-practice protocol
- Price: `$3,500`
- Upgrade path: continue into Path 1 or Path 2
- Best use: skeptical buyers, event-deadline buyers, "I need a first win" buyers

### Invite-Only Pilot - Executive Body System

Do not sell this broadly yet. Validate it with 5 founder/VP conversations first.

- Delivery: full schedule/body/energy architecture
- Price: `$5,000/mo`
- Minimum: 3 months
- Capacity: 1 pilot client
- Requirement: buyer already sees the body as a business/life bottleneck, not a training problem

## Revenue Path

### 90-day realistic target

The practical 90-day target is:

> `$6k-$10k/mo` in new recurring revenue, with a shot at `$10k+` if one premium pilot closes.

### 6-month target

The `$10k-$15k/mo` goal is more credible as a 6-month target:

| Mix | Monthly Revenue |
|---|---:|
| 4 local clients at `$1,275` + 2 remote at `$1,500` | `$8,100/mo` |
| 4 local clients at `$1,500` + 3 remote at `$1,500` | `$10,500/mo` |
| 3 local at `$1,500` + 3 remote at `$1,500` + 1 pilot at `$5,000` | `$14,000/mo` |
| 4 local at `$1,500` + 4 remote at `$2,000` | `$14,000/mo` |

This plan should stop pretending that 3-4 normal clients is enough for `$10k-$15k/mo`.

## The Acquisition System

### For 30 days, only run three channels

1. Warm referrals
2. Google Business Profile/local proof
3. LinkedIn profile + 3 posts/week

Do not add YouTube, blog, podcast, or paid ads until this core loop produces booked audits.

### Channel 1 - Warm referrals

Run 20 direct asks in 30 days.

The ask is not "do you know anyone who needs coaching?"

The ask:

> Who do you know who is 40-plus, successful, and quietly frustrated that their strength, energy, sleep, or consistency is not where it used to be?

Target:

- 20 asks
- 12 named prospects
- 4 sent intros
- 3 booked Triage Audits
- 1 close

### Channel 2 - GBP/local proof

The local search target should be fitness/search-intent language, not "executive coach."

Track:

- personal trainer Burbank
- private personal training Burbank
- strength coach Burbank
- fitness coach Burbank
- online fitness coach Los Angeles

Refresh the profile around relevance, distance, and prominence: accurate category, complete services, reviews, photos, owner replies, and local landing-page match.

### Channel 3 - LinkedIn

LinkedIn's job in the first 30 days is not "go viral."

Its job:

- make referrals trust the recommendation
- make profile visitors understand the offer
- create enough recognition that a few qualified DMs happen
- show Cooz is real, specific, and not another coach with AI language

Post cadence:

- 3 posts/week
- 1 client proof story
- 1 diagnostic utility post
- 1 belief/identity post

## The Triage Audit

The 90-minute structure is good. The operational change is gating.

### Free vs paid

- Warm direct referrals: free Triage Audit
- Cold LinkedIn/GBP traffic: application first
- If call quality is low after 5 calls: charge a diagnostic fee credited toward enrollment

### Flow

1. Listen for what is breaking.
2. Read back their own language.
3. Diagnose the pattern.
4. Recommend one path.
5. Ask if they want the next step.

Do not over-explain all offers. The diagnosis chooses the path.

## What I Would Cut From V6

- The claim that online alone can produce 2-4 clients in 60 days.
- The broad Week 5+ expansion into YouTube/blog unless calls are happening.
- The `$197` paid trial.
- The repeated "That's the work" closer on almost every post.
- Any "real call last week" copy unless literally true and permission-safe.
- Any universal claim from Mari's exceptional case.
- "Burbank executive coach" as the main GBP target.
- Over-locking the category before buyer language has been tested.

## What I Would Keep From V6

- Brave Choice as methodology.
- Long-arc proof as the trust spine.
- Triage Audit as the conversion mechanism.
- Burbank/Hollywood as referral context.
- The refusal to compete as a commodity trainer.
- The focus on real client stories instead of credentials.
- The 90-day first-win frame, if presented carefully.

## The 30-Day Win Condition

At the end of 30 days, we should know:

- Which language gets referrals to name people.
- Whether LinkedIn creates profile movement or DMs.
- Whether the Triage Audit closes at premium prices.
- Whether Cooz has enough capacity for the delivery model.
- Whether ESA is a real buyer category or just strategist logic.

If those are not known, the plan should not expand.
