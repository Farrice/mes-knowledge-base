Controlled demo for AI Misfire Founding Proof

The AI output sounded right. It still missed the work.

Use this board for a Loom, screenshots, carousel slides, or a LinkedIn document post. It shows the gap between a clean AI answer and an agent-readable workflow.

Normal prompt

The input looked reasonable.

Write a follow-up DM to a founder-led consultant who asked about using AI to improve their content workflow.

Goal: get them to send one failed AI output, prompt, SOP, workflow, or content process for review.

Tone: clear, useful, not hypey.
Polished output

The output sounded fine.

Hey [Name], great connecting with you.

I help consultants use AI to produce more content without losing their voice.

If you are interested, I would be happy to hop on a quick call and show you how AI can transform your workflow.

Let me know if you want to chat.

Guess #1

It assumed the goal was a call.

The real next step was one submitted artifact. Proof should come before a call.

Guess #2

It used generic AI benefits.

The buyer pain was not more content. The pain was AI being close but still needing rescue.

Guess #3

It implied transformation before diagnosis.

That is false confidence. The honest move is to inspect one workflow first.

Rewritten with operating judgment

The stronger version asks for the misfire first.

Hey [Name], your point about AI getting close but still needing review is exactly the pattern I am mapping.

Quick question:

Where is AI already useful in your workflow, but still wrong enough that you or someone senior has to correct it before it can be trusted?

If you send one prompt, SOP, workflow, or failed output, I can point out the first three places it is being forced to guess.
The point

The model did the task.

It did not understand the work.

That gap is the audit.

Agent-readable fix

What the workflow needed before AI wrote anything.

  • Purpose: move a relevant buyer from vague AI frustration to one submitted artifact.
  • Authority: do not promise transformation before seeing the workflow.
  • CTA hierarchy: ask for one misfire, return three observations, bridge only if the problem is real.
  • Quality test: the buyer should know exactly which output to send.
