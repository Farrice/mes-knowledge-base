## Post 1 - Flagship For Today

Lane: Meta-proof / AI Misfire Recognition
Role: publish first
CTA: soft invitation to send one misfire
Quality score: 43/50

I read the LinkedIn posts we made for this offer and got mad.

Not because they were bad.

That would have been easier.

They were clean.
Structured.
Useful.
Reasonable.

The kind of thing that makes you say, "Yeah, this is probably fine," right before you post something nobody remembers.

And that was the problem.

The content was committing the exact sin the offer is supposed to fix:

AI did the task.
It did not understand the work.

It explained the idea:

"Your AI keeps guessing because the judgment behind the work is trapped in your head."

True.

But it did not make you feel the tension of that.

It did not show the moment where a founder reads a draft and thinks:

"This is technically right, but I would never say it like this."

It did not show the late-night correction.

The little jaw clench.

The weird frustration of having to fix something that was supposed to save you time.

It did not sound like me.

And if it does not sound like me, why would anyone trust me to help them find the invisible judgment in their own work?

That is the part people miss about AI content.

The issue is not always accuracy.

Sometimes the issue is that the output has no fingerprints.

No scar tissue.
No taste.
No pressure.
No little human detail that proves somebody actually lived the lesson.

Clean is not the same thing as true.

Finished-looking is not finished.

So we rebuilt the posts from the misfire itself.

The proof is not "look how good AI made this."

The proof is:

I caught the place where the work was missing.

That is what I want to help founder-led experts do with their own AI outputs.

Not polish the sentence.

Find the spot where the system was guessing because the real rule lived in your head.

If you have one output like that - a prompt, SOP, proposal, sales follow-up, content draft, workflow, whatever - send it.

Messy is fine.

Honestly, messy is usually where the truth is.

### Alternate Hooks

1. I almost published the exact kind of AI content I warn people about.
2. The draft was clean. That was the problem.
3. I caught my own offer making the mistake it is supposed to diagnose.

### First Comment Option

The test I am using now: if the post explains the idea but does not reveal the judgment behind it, it is not done yet.

### Why This Works

- Personal proof: Farrice admits the internal misfire.
- Non-obvious frame: the content itself becomes the case study.
- Buyer recognition: "technically right, but I would never say it like this."
- Offer pull-through: send one misfire, map the guess-point.

## Post 2 - Ethical Persuasion And Customer Language

Lane: Farrice lived lens / customer language
Role: trust builder
CTA: no hard CTA
Quality score: 41/50

I had this uncomfortable thought the other day:

"I just manipulated my two-year-old son, and I do not know how to feel about it."

Not in some dark, evil way.

In the normal parenting way that nobody wants to call marketing because it makes the room feel weird.

He was overtired.

He needed to nap.

We recently moved him from the crib to the bed, so the bed still has play energy attached to it.

If you have ever negotiated with a toddler, you know the vibe.

You are not giving a TED Talk.

You are trying to help a tiny human do the thing that will make their life better in about 11 minutes.

And what hit me was this:

I could not say the pretty thing.

I had to say the thing in the way he could actually receive it.

That is where the moral tension lives.

Because the tactic is not the whole story.

Intent matters.
Presence matters.
Outcome matters.

If I am using persuasion to make my life easier while ignoring what is good for him, that is manipulation.

If I am using persuasion to help him get the thing he genuinely needs, in the language he can actually understand, that is care with a spine.

This is why so much AI-assisted marketing sounds fake.

It says the pretty thing.

"Streamline your workflow."
"Unlock productivity."
"Transform your business."

Meanwhile the customer is thinking:

"I thought this would save time, but now I have to review everything."

That gap is not a copywriting problem first.

It is a presence problem.

The system was not close enough to the customer's reality.

It did not know what they needed to hear.

It knew what marketers usually say.

Those are not the same.

This is the standard I keep coming back to:

If you will not say it the way the person needs to hear it, you may not care as much as you think.

That applies to parenting.

It applies to marketing.

And it definitely applies to the AI outputs we keep calling "almost there."

Almost there is where the real work starts.

### Alternate Hooks

1. Parenting is the most uncomfortable marketing class I have ever taken.
2. The line between manipulation and empathy is thinner than most marketers want to admit.
3. My toddler taught me why most AI marketing sounds fake.

### First Comment Option

The standard is not "did it sound good?" The standard is "did it meet the person inside their actual reality?"

### Why This Works

- Personal proof: fatherhood scene with privacy guardrails.
- Perspective shift: customer language becomes care, not style.
- Donald Miller clarity: simple human story before mechanism.
- Oren pass: premium because it names an uncomfortable moral distinction.

## Post 3 - The Founder Rescue Tax

Lane: Buyer mirror / founder judgment capture
Role: strong pain recognition
CTA: small diagnostic action
Quality score: 40/50

There is a type of AI cost nobody puts in the budget.

It shows up at 10:47 p.m.

Laptop open.
Shoulders tight.
One eye on the draft.
One eye on the clock.

The proposal is almost right.

The follow-up is almost right.

The content draft is almost right.

Which means it is still yours to fix.

That is the founder rescue tax.

It is the time you spend saving the output that was supposed to save you time.

And the expensive part is not just the minutes.

It is the role you quietly accept.

You become the safety net.

The tone checker.
The customer-language translator.
The "we would never say it like that" department.
The final layer of taste no one knows how to explain.

(Very prestigious. Terrible use of your nervous system.)

Most people respond by rewriting the prompt.

Sometimes that helps.

But if you keep making the same correction, the prompt is not the whole problem.

The correction is the map.

When you change a sentence, ask:

- What did I know that the system did not?
- What rule did I apply without naming it?
- What customer reality did the draft miss?
- What proof did I reject as too weak?
- What would have made this pass on the first try?

That is where the real asset lives.

Not in the AI output.

In the judgment you used to rescue it.

The goal is not to remove your taste from the process.

The goal is to stop making you rescue the same mistake every night.

### Alternate Hooks

1. The hidden AI cost is not the subscription. It is the rescue work.
2. If AI saves you 20 minutes and costs you 35 minutes of correction, that is not leverage.
3. Founder-led businesses need to track the AI rescue tax.

### First Comment Option

Try this today: open the last AI output you corrected and highlight the first sentence you changed. That sentence is usually not random. It is a missing rule.

### Why This Works

- Buyer language: "almost right," "we would never say it like that."
- Proof as story: late-night founder scene.
- Tactically implementable: highlight the first correction.
- Offer pull-through without over-selling.

## Post 4 - A Plan Is Not The Work

Lane: Human judgment / personal training bridge
Role: authority builder
CTA: no hard CTA
Quality score: 39/50

I spent years watching people ask for plans that were not going to save them.

Workout plans.
Meal plans.
Morning routines.
Accountability systems.

Some of them were good plans.

That was not the issue.

The issue was the person trying to follow the plan on the day they were tired, embarrassed, stressed, impatient, and quietly looking for a way out.

That was the work.

Not the PDF.

The human inside the PDF.

I see the same thing happening with AI now.

People ask for the better prompt.

The better SOP.

The better automation.

And sometimes, yes, the prompt is sloppy.

But a lot of the time the real problem is that the system has been given the plan without the person.

It does not know:

- what the buyer is afraid to admit
- what proof will actually move them
- which detail carries the trust
- what tone crosses the line
- when "clear" becomes too flat
- where the expert would pause before deciding

A human can fill in those blanks from experience.

AI fills them in from probability.

That is why a clean SOP can still produce strange work.

It followed the steps.

It missed the judgment.

This is the shift I think more founder-led experts need to make:

Stop treating the prompt like the work.

The prompt is the visible request.

The work is the invisible operating context underneath it.

And until that context is captured, every new tool is just a shinier plan for the same old gap.

### Alternate Hooks

1. A workout plan was never the work. Neither is your prompt.
2. People keep asking AI for better plans when the missing piece is judgment.
3. I learned this in personal training before I ever saw it in AI.

### First Comment Option

This is why I care less about "what can we automate?" and more about "what judgment is the expert still applying manually?"

### Why This Works

- Cross-domain proof: personal training -> AI workflows.
- Perspective shift: plan/prompt/SOP is not the work.
- Buyer relevance: founder-led experts know the work depends on judgment.
- Reduces jargon while preserving authority.

## Post 5 - Fingerprints Are The Moat

Lane: Taste / anti-slop / memorability
Role: differentiation and shareability
CTA: reflective prompt
Quality score: 41/50

Metal Gear Solid taught me something most AI content still gets wrong.

If you never played it, stick with me.

There is a boss fight where the game makes you solve the problem outside the expected interface.

Not a better weapon.
Not a better combo.
Not "try harder."

You literally had to change the frame.

As a kid, that stuck with me because it did not feel like content.

It felt like the game noticed me.

That is what most AI-assisted work is missing right now.

It can imitate the category.

It can produce the post.
The sales email.
The proposal.
The SOP.
The carousel.

But it often removes the weird little detail that makes the work feel authored.

The fingerprint.

The line only you would say.

The story your competitor would cut because it seems inefficient.

The reference that makes one person think, "Oh, this person is one of us."

The tiny human choice that proves there is a real mind behind the machine.

In an AI-saturated market, polished is getting cheaper by the day.

Fingerprints are getting more valuable.

That does not mean being random.

It means knowing which authored details carry trust.

For me, that might be fatherhood, personal training, gaming, spiritual language, or the pressure of building inside a nap window.

For you, it might be the customer story you keep sanitizing.

The unpopular belief you keep softening.

The sentence you delete because it feels "too specific."

That might be the exact thing the market remembers.

If AI removes the fingerprints from your work, it did not make you faster.

It made you easier to forget.

### Alternate Hooks

1. The future of AI content is not more output. It is more fingerprints.
2. Small weirdness is a moat.
3. The detail your competitor would remove might be the thing your audience remembers.

### First Comment Option

Quick audit: what is one detail in your work that feels "too you" to include? That might be the signal, not the flaw.

### Why This Works

- Farrice taste signal: gaming reference with inclusive bridge.
- Non-obvious frame: memorability through frame-change.
- Market proof: AI saturation makes authorship more valuable.
- Practical action: identify the detail you keep deleting.

## Post 6 - Bridge Post: Send One Misfire

Lane: Offer bridge / AI Misfire Express
Role: conversion after trust posts
CTA: DM `MISFIRE` or send one artifact
Quality score: 42/50

I am opening a small batch of AI Misfire Express audits.

This is not an "AI automation roadmap."

Not yet.

That is the mistake I see a lot of people make.

They try to build the agent before they know where the work is breaking.

So the agent gets faster at producing the same almost-right output.

Clean.
Confident.
Wrong in the way only the founder can explain.

AI Misfire Express starts smaller.

You send one thing:

- a prompt
- an SOP
- a workflow
- a proposal draft
- a sales follow-up
- a content process
- a failed AI output
- a client delivery step

I map where the system was forced to guess.

That means I am looking for:

- the missing customer language
- the invisible taste rule
- the source of truth it should have used
- the proof standard it did not know
- the decision it should not have made alone
- the moment a human should have been pulled back in

You will leave with one of four answers:

1. This needs a better prompt.
2. This needs a clearer SOP.
3. This needs an agent-ready work primitive.
4. This is not ready to automate yet.

That last answer matters.

Because faster wrong output is not leverage.

It is just a cleaner mess.

If you have one artifact that keeps coming back almost right, DM `MISFIRE`.

Messy is fine.

I am not looking for your polished system.

I am looking for the place it breaks.

### Alternate Hooks

1. Do not build the agent yet.
2. Faster wrong output is not leverage.
3. If your AI output keeps coming back almost right, start there.

### First Comment Option

Good fit: founder-led experts already using AI but still rescuing the output manually. Bad fit: someone who wants a giant automation build before diagnosing the work.

### Why This Works

- Clear offer after trust-building posts.
- Bounded scope: one artifact, one diagnosis.
- No overpromising: includes "not ready to automate yet."
- Concrete buyer action.

## DM Follow-Ups

Use manually. No automated LinkedIn messaging.

### If someone comments with recognition

### If someone asks what a misfire is

A misfire is any AI output that looks usable at first but still needs human rescue before you would trust it. Could be a prompt result, sales follow-up, SOP-generated task, proposal draft, content draft, or client delivery step.

### If someone is interested but vague

The easiest way to start is not a call. Send one artifact: the prompt/output, SOP, workflow, or draft. I will look for the first place AI had to infer something your actual judgment normally handles.

### If someone worries the artifact is messy

Messy is actually better. Polished examples hide the gap. I am looking for the moment where the system guessed, not a perfect documentation sample.

### If someone asks what they get

You get a Misfire Map: where the output guessed, what context or source of truth was missing, what decision rule should exist, and whether the fix is a prompt, SOP, agent-ready work primitive, or "do not automate this yet."
