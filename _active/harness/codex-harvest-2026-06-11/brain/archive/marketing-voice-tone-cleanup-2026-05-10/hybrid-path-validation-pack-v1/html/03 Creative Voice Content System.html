03 Creative Voice Content System

Why This Document Exists

The early content was structurally sound but not strong enough. It explained the idea, but did not always make the reader feel the tension.

That failure matters because it is the exact problem AI Misfire Express is supposed to diagnose:

The output can be correct on paper and still miss the work.

The content system must not sound like generic AI consulting. It needs Farrice's lived voice, taste, tension, proof, and perspective change.

Voice Standard

Farrice's strongest public voice should feel like:

  • candid but not careless
  • sharp but not condescending
  • premium but not stiff
  • psychological without being academic
  • strategic without hiding behind jargon
  • human enough to prove there is taste behind the thinking

Default voice:

Oracle insight plus Catalyst action.

Oracle:

  • names the hidden pattern
  • makes the reader feel seen
  • explains the deeper mechanism
  • resists hype

Catalyst:

  • gives a next move
  • creates urgency without panic
  • turns insight into action
  • collects signal

The Content Problem To Avoid

Bad version:

"AI needs context engineering and semantic documentation for agentic workflows."

Better version:

"Your AI is not failing because it is dumb. It is failing because the part of the work that makes you good at this is still invisible."

Best version:

"I read one of my own AI-assisted drafts and had that annoying little pause. Structurally, it was fine. The point was clear. The logic was there. But I would not post it. It sounded like the kind of content that knows the topic but has never had to win the room."

Content Pillars

Pillar 1: AI Misfire Diagnosis

Purpose: make the pain visible.

Core question:

Where is AI being forced to guess?

Useful formats:

  • misfire breakdowns
  • before/after examples
  • "close but wrong" posts
  • workflow diagnosis
  • prompt/SOP autopsy

Pillar 2: Human Judgment As The Operating System

Purpose: elevate Farrice's authority without making the work mystical.

Core question:

What judgment is the founder still carrying manually?

Useful formats:

  • founder rescue stories
  • hidden-rule extraction
  • customer-language translation
  • taste/quality distinctions
  • "the correction is the data" posts

Pillar 3: Buyer Language And Positioning

Purpose: connect AI work back to marketing, offers, content, and revenue.

Core question:

What does the buyer need to understand before the system can persuade or execute?

Useful formats:

  • positioning teardown
  • plain-English translation
  • content resonance audits
  • StoryBrand-style clarity edits
  • offer language diagnosis

Pillar 4: Agent-Ready Operating Systems

Purpose: introduce the moat once the buyer understands the pain.

Core question:

What would make this workflow clear enough for AI to execute without inventing?

Useful formats:

  • semantic document examples
  • work primitive breakdowns
  • AI-ready workflow maps
  • quality gates
  • failure mode analysis

Post Architecture

Most strong posts should follow this structure:

  1. Start with a scene, confession, contradiction, or specific moment.
  2. Let the reader recognize themselves before naming the mechanism.
  3. Show the misfire or tension.
  4. Name the hidden pattern.
  5. Give one useful diagnostic lens.
  6. End with a reflective line or light CTA.

Avoid opening with:

  • "In 2026..."
  • "AI is changing everything..."
  • "Here are 5 ways..."
  • "Most people do not understand..."
  • "The future belongs to..."

Use hard CTAs only when the post is designed as a bridge to AI Misfire Express.

Proof Rules

Every claim needs proof beside it:

  • personal scene
  • observable workflow
  • real before/after
  • market source
  • buyer language
  • internal build proof
  • diagnosis example

No naked claims like:

  • "This will transform your business."
  • "This is the future of work."
  • "Every founder needs this."
  • "AI agents are the next gold rush."

Luke Insight Vectors To Add Punch

Use these vectors when rewriting posts:

  • Specificity: name the artifact, workflow, output, correction, or missed rule.
  • Contrast: show the difference between "did the task" and "understood the work."
  • Proof adjacency: place proof next to the claim, not three paragraphs later.
  • Mechanism: explain why the problem happens.
  • Minimum action: give the reader one thing to inspect in their own workflow.

Rory Sutherland Perception Layer

Use perception strategy carefully:

  • Reframe the cleanup as hidden business intelligence.
  • Make one misfire feel like a valuable diagnostic object, not a failure.
  • Make "send me one failed output" feel lower-risk than "book a strategy call."
  • Position the audit as finding invisible judgment, not fixing bad AI use.
  • Make the buyer feel more sophisticated for noticing the gap.

Core perception shift:

The annoying correction you keep making is not wasted time. It is the clearest evidence of what your AI system cannot yet see.

Donald Miller Clarity Pass

The reader should always know:

  • what problem Farrice solves
  • who it is for
  • what to send
  • what happens next
  • why the diagnosis matters

Do not require the reader to understand:

  • semantic documents
  • context engineering
  • agentic governance
  • Antigravity
  • work primitives

What To Challenge

External reviewer should identify:

  • which posts still sound like generic AI content
  • which hooks lack tension
  • where the writing over-explains
  • where proof is implied but not shown
  • whether the voice is distinct enough for LinkedIn trust-building

Non-Negotiable Content Standard

The reader should leave with one of these:

  • a new distinction
  • a diagnosis they can apply
  • a phrase for a problem they already felt
  • a belief shift
  • a reason to submit one misfire

If the post only educates, it is not done.
