Of course, this show wasn't really about truth. Not being able to answer
an obscure, bonehead question does not make you a liar. And the silly
stunts you had to perform were hardly anything like real-world
consequences for getting caught in a lie.
But, the show was fun—if you like that kind of thing.
Now I bring "Truth or Consequences" up in these two versions-the
how and the town-because the question of truth in persuasive storie
is not often all that well understood. Nor are the consequences. But this is
important. Many people choose to lie, sometimes without even realizing
it. Why? Because often, it's so much easier to persuade someone with an
untruth, than with the truth itself.
But as you will see, selling by using a lie is often a short-lived gain. And while
selling with the truth does require more effort going in, it's a sale that
won't come undone in the way a sale based on a lie can and often does.
Let me show you what I mean:
Everywhere around the world, persuaders play their own version of
"Truth or Consequences." Every day.
It's not always nearly as much fun as the radio (and, later, TV) show. But
it is far more meaningful.
