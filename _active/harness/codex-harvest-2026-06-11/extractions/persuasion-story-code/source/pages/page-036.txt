that shows you turned things around.
So you'll need at least one big matching contrast, or a set of matching
contrasts (like Karbo did). Start out with a piece of paper in landscape
mode and a line drawn down the middle (or a page in your word
processor with two columns that cover the whole page, in landscape
mode).
In the left column, or on the left side of the page, write out a symptom of
your failure. For example, don't write "I was broke" or "I couldn't make
any money." Instead, a symptom would be "My wife phoned me, crying,
because the landlord had shown up at the house-and we didn't have
money to pay it."
What you want to do is write as many symptoms down on the left-hand
side as you can.
Then, see how many contrasting examples you can come up with. These
examples show that you had achieved success where you were failing
before. For not being able to afford to pay rent, the contrasting example is
owning multiple pieces of real estate outright. (Karbo actually had that
and a lot more in the ad, but quoting the whole ad would have taken up
way too much space.)
When you have put all the symptoms on the left you can think of, and
matched up as many contrasting examples as you have on the right, take
a look and see:
1. Would your story work better to have a series of examples, the
way Karbo did, or
2.
Would one, longer composite description of your failures,
followed but one longer, composite description of your successes,
work better?
Choose the one you think will work best, and write that story.
A Failure/Success Story is great to open a speech, to include in a pitch,
and of course to start an ad or sales letter.
