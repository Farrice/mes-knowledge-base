There's no single "best place" to use these stories. In copy, you might use
one to start your message and another right before you introduce your
solution or offer. You could do the same in spoken presentations. In a
live conversation with a prospect, you can sometimes use these stories to
great effect as an answer to a question or an objection.
Stories That Predict The Future (Chapter 5)
The celebrated British writer Samuel Johnson said it best: "Promise, large
promise, is the soul of an advertisement." Though he said it in the 1700s,
it is still true today. But it's true in more than advertising—it's true in any
persuasive effort.
If you are making a business proposal... if you are pitching a business
idea... if you are trying to sell anyone on anything... then a large
promise (especially one that is believable, and that you can fully deliver
on) is the fuel that sends your prospect towards a willingness to agree.
Persuasion stories that predict the future are the kind of promises that
gives extra momentum to your ads and presentations. One of them, the
Unexpected Benefit Story, can work in almost any persuasion effort. The
other three types are more specialized and will work best in particular
kinds of situations, which we cover in detail in Chapter 5.
Here are the four kinds:
• Unexpected Benefit Story
• Predicted Transformation Story
• Improved Status Story
• Future Health Improvement Story
The key thing to remember about this kind of persuasion story is that
you use it to make the most legitimate, attractive appeal regarding a
future that your prospect will want. You can use it right at the beginning
of an ad or a pitch, or right before the point where you spell out your
offer.
