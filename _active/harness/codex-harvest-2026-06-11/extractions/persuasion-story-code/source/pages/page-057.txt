And that's what a Predicted Transformation Story is about. Promising to
deliver that kind of superpower to your prospect.
To put one of these stories together, you need to figure out something
that is true... plausible... something that you/your product/ your service
can actually deliver... and, most important of all, will be perceived as a
real, true, no-kidding superpower by your prospect. Something unique
and awe-inspiring. Previously thought impossible to obtain.
Let's create one right now.
Suppose you offered a service called "Automatic Rapport Coaching."
Your main promise would be that, by learning and practicing certain
special skills, you could get into rapport with anyone, anywhere,
anytime. A pretty big promise, but, by itself, hardly the promise of a
superpower.
Let's look at the ordinary powers your course will deliver first. As you
attempt to persuade someone to engage your Automatic Rapport
Coaching services, you might talk about features and benefits. Learning
to hold non-threatening eye contact (feature), which would create trust
(benefit). Learning to listen better (feature), which would make other
people relax when they talk to you (benefit). Learning mirroring and
matching behavior (feature), which puts you and the other person "in
synch" (benefit).
All of these are good, but none is rare and unusual enough to come
across as a superpower.
What would be? How about this:
After only a few coaching sessions, and sometimes after just one,
you will notice a stunning change in the way people respond to
you.
People who used to ignore you will seek you out, and many will
ask for your opinion and try to get your approval.
