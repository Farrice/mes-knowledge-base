None of the things Abacus promises to do are unusual by themselves.
What makes Abacus unusual is the combination of things you don't
usually find all in one business. And some things you'd never expect to
get from a travel agency.
What did Abacus do that you wouldn't expect?
Things like: Keeping its staff up to date on "insider" skills (as opposed to
hiring cheerful but mainly unskilled order takers); "white glove"
treatment from start to finish of a trip (like having a limo waiting for you
at the travel destination); scouting meeting sites in advance for clients.
Altogether, this combination of special services seems pretty spectacular.
Abacus knew how to do each of these things but had never put together
a story that let people see in advance all the unique services Abacus
offered. That's what I was able to help them with.
Did you get the sense that, after reading this story, a prospect would
already understand a lot about the "new" kind of travel management
service Abacus offers, even before they meet with Bonnie or Duane?
Tips For Creating Your Own Story About Something New Or
Unfamiliar
To put together your own story like this, make two lists:
1) What you do that is familiar and expected
2) What you do that is different from what everyone else does. Be sure to
refine this list so the benefit of each item on this list is immediately
obvious-to the prospect who knows nothing about your business.
Write your story combining both—and starting with what's "normal" for
Peiferent, your business to offer. The combination of "familiar" and
will make it easier for your prospect to accept what's new
about what you do.
Remember the purpose of your story is to show that what you're doing
that's familiar is already comfortable and accepted by your prospect...
and everything you've added that's new only makes your offer better
and more desirable!
