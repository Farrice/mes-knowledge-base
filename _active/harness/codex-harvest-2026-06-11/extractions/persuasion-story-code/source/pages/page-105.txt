CHAPTER 9
TRUTH OR CONSEQUENCES
We've spent a lot of time going over the many different kinds of
persuasion stories you can use. But there's one vitally important topic we
haven't really covered in full. And that's truth.
We need to talk about this. Because to get the trust of the people you are
persuading, they must believe that what you are telling them is true.
And I can't think of a better way to start this chapter than to take a quick
trip to the town called "Truth or Consequences."
You start in El Paso. Drive north 119 miles on Interstate 25, and you
arrive in the village originally known as Hot Springs.
In 1950, NBC announced a contest: They would broadcast one of their
shows from the first municipality that named itself after that show.
And so, on March 31, 1950, Hot Springs city commissioners voted to
rename their town Truth or Consequences. As a result, they won NBC's
contest. And that is how the town's new name came about.
And in the newly renamed town, The Truth or Consequences game show
itself was goofy. Contestants had only a couple seconds to answer a fairly
impossible trivia question (giving the right answer would be considered
"truth"), and if they didn't give the correct answer (most didn't), they
had to embarrass themselves by performing a silly stunt before a national
audience ("consequences").
