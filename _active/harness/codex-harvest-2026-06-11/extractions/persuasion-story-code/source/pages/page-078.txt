CHAPTER 7
STORIES THAT EXPLAIN
When I took my first sales training more than 30 years ago, I got a strict
warning: "Sell now, educate later."
What I got from that is: Let your client do most of the talking. Don't say
too much. Hear what your prospect has on their mind.
But when I got deep into the study of direct-response copywriting a few
years later, I frequently heard this advice: "The more you tell, the more
you sell."
That is, provide details. Make your complete sales pitch. Don't hold
important information back.
These two rules seem to contradict each other. But do they?
I came to a nuanced understanding that includes both of them. Iť's true
that if you spend a lot of time educating without using persuasion stories,
vou'll have a hard time winning someone else over. And it s also true
that you've got to tell your prospect something, because nearly all of the
time they're not going to persuade themselves with no input from you.
So what's the sweet spot?
Carefully-crafted stories that explain while persuading at the same time.
It's not that they don't educate. It's that they educate your prospect about
reasons to buy (benefits) and desired outcomes (results). And, they give
your prospect an advance look at the experience of using your product
and service.
