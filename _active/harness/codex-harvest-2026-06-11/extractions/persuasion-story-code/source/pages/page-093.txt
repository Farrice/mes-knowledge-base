CHAPTER 8
STORIES THAT BUILD TRUST
It's perplexing, but it's real: The more someone wants something, the
more they are going to resist your efforts to persuade them.
Think about your own life. Maybe you were planning a special vacation
for a long time. The closer it came, the more excited you got. But at the
same time, so dia this persistent sense of dread: What it something goes
wrong? What it the airline loses my luggage? What if it rains all week
and we can'tgo anywhere?
Most people experience this twin sense of excitement and dread,
especially when they need to make a decision about whether or not to
buy something.
It doesn't make much sense. Until you realize that most people have had
a version of this happen to them: They got excited about a new idea or
about something they were going to buy... they tried it out... and for one
reason or another, they were bitterly disappointed.
Of course, this doesn't always happen. Sometimes things work out just
the way they expected. Occasionally, things exceed expectations.
But it does happen sometimes. So, to protect themselves against the same
kind of disappointments they have suffered in the past, people get wary.
Suspicious. Skeptical.
This leads to their having a natural reluctance to believe a persuader. It's
realistic, therefore, to assume people don't fully trust you, or what you're
saying.
