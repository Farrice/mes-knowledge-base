And in fiction, telling all these lies works-under one very important
condition. Mark Twain put it this way:
It's no wonder that truth is stranger than fiction. Fiction has to make

Yes, real life doesn't always make sense. Sometimes it makes no sense at
all. Fiction may contain some of the messiness of real life. But overall,
fiction needs to make sense for a reader or viewer to enjoy it and accept it.
For our purposes, when Twain says,
"Fiction has to make sense,"
what he means is:
Fiction needs to be believable.
Believability. Novelists, screenwriters, directors and actors all strive for it

• Now we get to our third category: Persuasive stories.
These stories, at their best, are the ones that have a double requirement:
They need to be both true and believable.
Truth comes from starting with facts.
And not abandoning them when you tell your story.
But rather, building upon them.
Getting facts requires digging. It's worth noting that a number of
bestselling fiction authors started their careers as journalists: Ernest
Hemingway, Joan Didion, Michael Connelly, Anna Quindlen, Susan
Sontag, and, of course, Mark Twain.
