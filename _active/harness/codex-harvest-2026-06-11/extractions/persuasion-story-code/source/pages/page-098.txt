One thing to consider with these stories is how well known the
organization providing the certification is. If a journalist wins a Pulitzer
Prize, that's so well known it requires no further explanation.
But the George Polk Award, while known among journalists, is not
generally as well known. If someone had such an award, it would be
good to add that it's given by Long Island University to recognize
achievement in print and broadcast journalism. Now it makes more
sense to a non-journalist.
Back to our software example from before. Microsoft is the world's
largest software company, and thus any certification with the Microsoft
name on it doesn't require any further explanation. But if our software
developer who got the two Al certifications wanted to mention a
certification from an organization that's somewhat obscure, more detail
would be a good idea.
