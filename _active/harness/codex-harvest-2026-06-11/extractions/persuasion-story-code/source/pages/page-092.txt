Then Kim found a book called The Persuasion Story Code. She read
the book and started adding in persuasive stories to key parts of
her pitch. What she found was that customers started to perk up,
every time she would tell a story-even a very quick one. She was
surprised by the turnaround: The number of sales she closed kept
improving until she became the top-closing salesperson on her
team.
This is a classic problem/solution story. It starts out with the problem:
Kim wasn't closing enough sales. In the second paragraph, it rolls out the
solution: Kim read the book and started including persuasive stories in
her pitch. Finally, the end result: Kim's closing rate improved, and
eventually she became the top-producing performer on her sales team.
You've seen stories like this before. They definitely work. But I saved
this one until last because there are so many other kinds of proven, easy-
to-use stories in concert with a problem-solution Story that help you
increase your persuasion success rate even more.
And I wanted you to be aware of others so you didn't settle on this and
miss the rest. They can help you add to your persuasive power.
You'll almost certainly want to use a Problem/Solution Story in your
pitch. Probably at the beginning, possibly right after you have
established what problem your solution solves-and that the prospect
has this problem.
Just about every product or service of any value in the marketplace
solves a problem. If it doesn't, chances are it's not going to sell very well.
And if it does solve a problem, then you'll be way ahead of the game
early on in your persuasion efforts if you can clearly identify and
describe the problem—and then, put it into a Problem/Solution Story.
