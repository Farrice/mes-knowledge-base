It was true when Honest Abe said it, and it's just as true today.
With that in mind, you can see the need for, and the value of, experience
testimonials. Having a customer or client describe their experience is
welcome information for someone considering trying something new.
What's even better, for you as a persuasive storyteller, is to find out what
the most common worries people have in advance are. And then address
the most pressing ones with an experience testimonial.
Here's one for a hypothetical yoga instructor named Sarah:
"I was always a little intimidated by yoga people-the equipment,
the clothing, the terminology, and, really, the sense of being
overwhelmed whenever people started to talk about it.
"After my first private session with Sarah, I can't begin to tell you
how relieved I felt. I didn't feel any pressure at all. Actually, I felt
relaxed and more confident that I could finally learn yoga."
-Katie Linwood
People do care about the quality of their experiences. As a persuader,
you may find it all too easy to get tangled up in documented as well as
possible future results-so tangled up that you forget people are also
seeking a certain kind of experience.
But for sure, people are. As one example, it's natural to assume people
buy luxury cars for the performance or the prestige. Or, on an even more
practical note, the resale value.
But people also buy the same cars for the pleasure and comfort of little
touches. The quality of the leather upholstery. The smoothness of lights
and audio systems that gently fade in and out, rather than come on or off
abruptly.
Whatever it is you are setting out to persuade someone about, remember
the importance of experience. If you can get a testimonial that conveys
the experience in an appealing way—get it.
