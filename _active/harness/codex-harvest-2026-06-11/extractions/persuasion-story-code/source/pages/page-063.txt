When you do, be careful about not going too deep.
Because, for many people, detailed science is something they'd just as
soon not hear about. Ever. U.S. Congressman Don Beyer, from Virginia,
said recently in a TV interview that when he first got to Congress, he
happily volunteered to be on the House Science, Space and Technology
Committee-and everyone else in the room laughed.
How come? Seems like no one—or at least no one else in Congress—
wanted to get near the topics this committee would cover.
I get it.
Having grown up with a father who was a professional scientist-and
living a hop, skip and a jump from Silicon Valley—I'm probably more
comfortable with science and tech talk than most people are. But it's not
my favorite thing. And I know a lot of highly intelligent people who go
into instant brain-freeze when the conversation gets nerdy.
That's why you have to use science talk sparingly when you put it in a
persuasion story.
So, with those warnings in mind, understand that a little science can go a
long way in persuading your prospect that what you have to offer is
valuable and unique.
Master copywriter "Million Dollar Mike" Morgan put together a
promotion for a skincare product that makes age spots disappear. The

And these microbes are defenders of the largest entrance to your
body... your skin.
Without getting too scientific, this brand new Swiss solution is
activated by this skin layer.
