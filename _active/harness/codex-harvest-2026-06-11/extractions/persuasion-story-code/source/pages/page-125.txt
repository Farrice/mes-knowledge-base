The important takeaway here for a persuasion storyteller is that when
you have a good relatable story with relatable people in it, listeners and
viewers are drawn to it like iron filings are drawn to a magnet. Of course,
some movies and TV shows do this better than others and to be sure,
some characters are larger than life. But even the superheroes have
relatable qualities. Writers have learned to include characteristics like
these:
• Relatable characters are determined to reach their goals, and keep
trying in the face of frustration.
• They care deeply about their own families.
• They take responsibility for themselves, and solve their own
problems.
• They're authentic in what they say and how they react to
situations.
• They persevere, in spite of their own doubts and fears.
When you're putting together a persuasion story of any kind, as simple
as it may be, see if you can show a bit of the flawed humanity of your
characters. Show their admirable qualities, too. Show these things no
through adjectives or descriptions, but by what your character or
characters say and what they do. Stories about ordinary people in
ordinary situations with ordinary problems who discover unusually
good solutions to those problems, make for great persuasion stories.
