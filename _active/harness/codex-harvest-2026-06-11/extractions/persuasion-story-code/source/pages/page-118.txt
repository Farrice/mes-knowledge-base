STRATEGY 1: POWERFUL STORIES USE WORD-PICTURES THROUGHOUT.
Words that evoke specific imagesknown as word-pictures-have a
much stronger impact than words that describe concepts, abstractions
and generalizations.
Here's an example. If you were describing wetland animals in the
Sunshine State, you could say, "the scary reptilian life inhabiting the
Florida Everglades," and that would be pretty abstract. Hard to visualize,
based on the words alone.
But change your description to word-pictures— "huge crocodiles with
monstrous teeth jutting out of their mouths, slithering through Florida's
Everglades" —and now, you've got words that create pretty clear images
of these terrifying creatures.
Let's use word-pictures in a less frightening context. Compare these two
stories about a hypothetical baseball pitcher training product, The Strike-
Zone Method, and notice how you react to each story:
1. The Strike-Zone Method gives you unparalleled accuracy and
control. Batters will hold you in high esteem but have little affinity
for you. Your pitching style will be consistent and fruitful, and
any variation you encounter will not detract from your objectives.
When strike-outs occur, this will only be a predictable result that
favors your team and keeps the other team at bay. Your team will
appreciate the power of your pitches.
