What To Focus On In A Mechanism Story
The first thing to do is think about the question: Does your
product/service have a mechanism worth highlighting? Remember, not
every good product does.
Two examples:
Alka-Seltzer has a mechanism, described in just four words: "Plop-plop!
Fizz-fizz!"
But Coca-Cola (another carbonated drink) does not have a mechanism—
at least not one they use in their advertising. (There are rumors that the
secret recipe to make Coke is in a well-guarded safe at company
headquarters in Atlanta.) Despite not using a mechanism in marketing,
the company pulled in over $40 billion a year in 2022. So, I guess they're
doing alright.
Again, if you don't have a mechanism you can use in your persuasion
package, not to worry.
But if you believe you do, then think about how to describe it simply
and visually. That's the first step. Describe it so a child could understand.
Then, make each step of the process as interesting and exciting as
possible. Notice in the example story that the copywriter is talking about
adding 20 years to a person's life, or firming up a woman's hips and
thighs. Those are immediately recognizable-and, for most people-
desirable benefits. Also, very tangible and easy to visualize.
