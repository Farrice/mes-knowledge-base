These stories are the cure to the widespread human oddity that we often
have more than one emotion at a time-and one of those emotions is
doubt. That is, someone can have a very strong desire to agree with you
or to buy something, but at the same time they can also have a nagging
doubt that maybe they shouldn't.
Most of the time, doubt wins. It's always easier to do nothing than to do
something you're not quite sure about.
Until, that is, there's no doubt remaining. Then, there's really no choice
but to move forward on something you clearly want.
Ihus, reassurance stories. They help ease, diminish, and remove a
prospect's doubt without you having to resort to being too pushy or
obvious about it.
We cover four types:
• Stories About Something New Or Unfamiliar
• Stories About How Others Have Made It Work
• Stories About A Hard-To-Believe Claim Of Endurance
• Stories About If You Want A Refund
The best place to use a reassurance story is where doubts come up. In a
live presentation, you can often tell by body language or questions. In
sales copy, this is something you learn from experience.
Stories That Explain (Chapter 7)
Many persuaders believe that if you find yourself forced to explain
yourself, you've already lost the persuasion battle. What I've found is
that's only true when your explanation itself is not persuasive. A long,
bland recitation of facts comes across like a boring lesson, which is the
opposite of an intriguing piece of persuasion.
Nevertheless, you can't avoid explaining entirely as part of the
persuasion process. Because sometimes you do need to explain to get a
prospect comfortable with what you're selling. Why? Because if they
don't understand it, they can't get on board with you.
