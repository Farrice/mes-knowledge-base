A Story About How Others Have Made It Work can be long or short. The
TFT story is relatively long: eight paragraphs, 252 words. Depending on
your product and how easy it is to use and to understand, you could tell
this kind of story in 100 words or less. The point of a story like this is not
to be as long and detailed as possible.
Just the opposite. You want to include the key facts and describe, as
concisely as you can, something that happened. Something that will
reassure your prospects that they, too, can use what you are offering and
get the result they're looking for.
STORIES ABOUT A HARD-TO-BELIEVE CLAIM OF ENDURANCE
Sometimes people don't doubt the performance of a product so much as
the claim of how strong and durable it is.
When you can make a presentation live and in person—or on video—
you can provide this kind of reassurance through a demonstration. I'm
sure you've seen infomercials showing how strong a glue is, or how
unbreakable an advanced form of duct tape is.
But you can't always do a demonstration. That's where Stories About A
Hard-To-Believe Claim Of Endurance comes in handy.
This story is from an old mail-order ad for "Forever" socks, which are
supposed to never wear out!
"Always come out like new," barked the manufacturer.
"What's the catch?"
"No catch, no secrets!" said the manufacturer happily. "8-ply
DuPont nylon—instead of the usual 4. Woven so closely, they
make ordinary socks seem like they're three-quarters air, by
comparison. Styled by one of the world's great sock designers—
for perfect proportion and fit. Guaranteed to wear forever, in
normal use. That 'normal use' simply means don't burn holes in
them deliberately, or try to cut them with scissors or razor."
