Another example, and perhaps even more universal: From Showtime's
hit series Your Honor, in 2023. One scene really caught my eye. It includes
Michael, a former judge, and Elizabeth, a state senator. With their
vaunted titles, they're not exactly the easiest characters to relate to.
Michael's daughter-in-law, Robin, had been murdered. Robin was also
Elizabeth's daughter. (So she and Michael were related by the marriage
of their children.)
The murder is unsolved. In this scene, Michael and Elizabeth have
teamed up to do some investigating on their own and see if they can find
out who the murderer was. We're inside a corner store in New Orleans.
Now, check out what happens:
They have figured out that the person who can help them identify the
murderer is a man named KJ, who used to work at the corner store. And
the cashier working there now would know how to reach KJ.
So they go up to the counter. Michael tries to start a conversation with
the cashier, but she's not interested. Tells him he sounds like a cop.
Important point: To the cashier, Michael and whoever he's with, are
enemies from a higher social status. Thus, not at all relatable.
Elizabeth has an idea. She asks Michael to move over. Then she hunches
down, and says to the cashier,
"Hi. What's your name?"
