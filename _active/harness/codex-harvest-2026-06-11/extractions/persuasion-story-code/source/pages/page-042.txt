And though there is motion, one thing is missing from these stories: an
ending.
I don't mean these are eternal stories, that go on for centuries. What I
mean is: they're open-ended. On purpose. Because when you have a story
about a problem without a fixed ending, your reader or listener really
wants to know what the ending is... and that gives you an opportunity
to present your solution later as an offer, which they will be more likely
to buy (since it's something they've been wanting).
So, to be clear: These stories are not self-contained ads or sales pitches.
But they are integral components of a larger persuasive message, and they
make the message that much stronger.
One more thing, before we get to the stories themselves. And that's
research.
Making stuff up without any specific knowledge or personal experience
will usually create a story that does not come across as authentic at all.
Make sure this doesn't happen by doing some research ahead of time.
The best kind of research is simply talking to people suffering from the
problem. But that's not always possible or practical to do. Almost as
good is going onto sites that have customer comments, like Amazon. Or
discussion forums, like Reddit.
What you are looking for is what people describe and how they describe
it. Reading what they say will give you a lot of valuable information to
create your story.
