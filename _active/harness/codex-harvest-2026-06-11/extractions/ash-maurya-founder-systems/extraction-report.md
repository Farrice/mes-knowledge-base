### Delta Insights

1. The most useful question is a past-tense behavior reconstruction: "Walk me through the last time you encountered this situation and tell me where you struggled."
2. Future-tense questions create false positives. "Would you use this?" produces politeness; "Would you pay?" produces lowballing; "What features do you want?" produces wish lists.
3. What customers want is hidden inside what they already use. New problems worth solving come from old solutions and their limitations.
4. The best interview target is a current user of the old way, not someone who says they might like the new way.
5. Buying criteria are revealed by triggers, the journey to the chosen solution, fired alternatives, and the reasons one option won.
6. Useful pain often appears as pet peeves, struggles, and workarounds before it appears as a named "big problem."
7. Interview analysis must be audited against facts in the transcript, not the founder's preferred interpretation.
8. Repeated pet peeves, struggles, and workarounds across roughly 10 interviews are a signal to sell a solution before building it.
