# Kishotenketsu Client-Facing Service Package

## Service Position

This service turns flat, generic, or over-agitated writing into a contrast-driven narrative system.

The core promise:

> We help a brand, founder, offer, product, or service stop forcing fake conflict and start communicating through world, process, contrast, perspective shift, and integration.

Use this when a client has material that is technically clear but emotionally thin, too generic, too adversarial, too AI-clean, or too dependent on problem-agitation.

## Best-Fit Clients

| Best Fit | Why This Works |
|---|---|
| Founder-led brands | The method extracts lived texture, rituals, scenes, phrases, and memories. |
| Premium services | The method creates desire without shouting urgency or over-villainizing the status quo. |
| Creator-led businesses | The method turns lived experience into posts, essays, scripts, and ghostwritten thought leadership. |
| AI, SaaS, consulting, education, and expert businesses | The method explains changed realities, new variables, and process-based mechanisms. |
| Brand or category strategy projects | The method makes positioning feel like a world the audience can enter. |
| Campaigns, ads, and media scripts | The method creates attention through contrast and reframe instead of rage-bait. |

## Poor-Fit Clients

Do not sell this as the primary service when:

- the offer is not defined
- the audience is unknown
- the client has no proof, examples, process, or lived source material
- the work needs urgent crisis communication or legal/compliance precision
- the client wants generic high-volume content instead of sharper narrative quality
- the client expects Kishotenketsu to replace conversion, proof, positioning, or platform fit

## Intake

### Intake Decision

Before accepting a project, score the client on five questions:

| Question | Pass Signal | Risk Signal |
|---|---|---|
| What is the current material? | A page, post, deck, script, offer, ad, or founder story exists. | The client only has a vague idea. |
| What changed in the audience's world? | A real market, workflow, identity, category, or cultural shift is visible. | The client only says "we are better." |
| What process can be shown? | There is a real ritual, workflow, product use, customer journey, or founder practice. | The client has only claims and adjectives. |
| What proof exists? | Case studies, screenshots, testimonials, examples, data, or credible lived details exist. | The story would need to carry unsupported claims. |
| Where will this deploy? | Clear surface: site, ad, social, ghostwriting, deck, email, script, launch. | No deployment surface or decision-maker. |

Green-light the service when at least four pass signals are present. If proof or positioning is missing, sell a diagnostic sprint first.

### Intake Questions

Ask these before writing:

1. What are we rewriting or building?
2. Where will it be used?
3. Who needs to believe, feel, understand, or do something after reading it?
4. What does the current version overstate, flatten, or fake?
5. What old reality did the audience used to live in?
6. What changed in their world?
7. What process, ritual, object, scene, or repeated behavior proves the change?
8. What details would only someone inside this world know?
9. What should the brand, product, service, or founder integrate, rather than defeat?
10. What claims need proof, restraint, or legal/compliance caution?
11. What voice samples should be protected?
12. What would make the final piece worth keeping?

## Proof Examples

### Example 1: Founder Ghostwriting

Before:

> Consistency compounds. If you show up every day, you eventually win.

After:

> I used to think consistency meant never missing. Then I found an old notebook from a year I thought I had wasted. Half the pages were grocery lists. A few had bad headlines. But every few pages, there was a sentence I still believed. Consistency was not the spotless calendar. It was leaving enough traces that future me could find the thread again.

Why it works:

- concrete object
- lived memory
- perspective shift
- integrated lesson

### Example 2: Brand Or Product Page

Before:

> We help teams save time with AI workflows.

After:

> The work is not disappearing because your team is lazy. It disappears in handoffs: the Slack reminder, the copied note, the client detail buried in a call. Our system gives the company a new ritual: capture once, route once, prove once, reuse forever.

Why it works:

- world before claim
- process as proof
- changed operating ritual
- product as integration layer

### Example 3: Ad Or Media Hook

Before:

> Stop wasting time with generic AI tools.

After:

> The tool is not the variable. The missing operating memory is.

Why it works:

- no fake enemy
- category reframe
- compact variable
- opens a proof path

### Example 4: Positioning

Before:

> We are an AI productivity platform for founders.

After:

> The old category was "make more content faster." The new variable is operational memory: source, route, proof, reuse. This is not a chatbot. It is a narrative and workflow memory layer for teams that want their best context to compound.

Why it works:

- changed category
- boundary line
- mechanism language
- buyer-world relevance

These are directional service shapes, not fixed market claims. Adjust after buyer conversations, proof, speed, and demand.

|---|---:|---|
| Diagnostic Sprint | $750-$1,500 | Raise when the before/after proof reliably creates follow-on work. |
| Core Narrative Package | $2,500-$6,000 | Raise when it includes positioning, offer clarity, interviews, or high-stakes site/campaign copy. |
| Campaign Or Content System Package | $6,000-$12,000+ | Raise when it includes ads, sales page sections, ghostwriting, multiple stakeholders, or revenue-critical launch work. |
| Retainer / Narrative Desk | $2,000-$6,000 per month | Raise when volume, approval complexity, or revenue impact increases. |

Pricing rationale:

- price the transformation and decision value, not word count
- charge more when proof, conversion, positioning, and strategy are included
- keep a lower diagnostic offer so clients can experience the before/after delta
- never sell high-ticket narrative work without a source packet and proof boundary

Recent pricing guides show copywriting and messaging projects ranging from low hundreds for simple assets to several thousand for landing pages, sales pages, email sequences, and brand messaging packages; brand strategy engagements can climb much higher when positioning, messaging, and implementation are bundled. Treat those as reference terrain, not a command to copy the market.

## Quality Gates

Every paid delivery must pass these gates:

| Gate | Pass Standard | Fail Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Source Boundary | Claims are backed by client source, proof, or clearly labeled inference. | The story makes unsupported claims feel true. |
| World Specificity | The audience can feel the environment, ritual, or process. | The piece is still abstract and adjective-heavy. |
| Variable Test | The changed variable is nameable in one sentence. | The piece depends on a villain or vague problem. |
| Process-As-Plot | The process moves the reader, not just decorates the copy. | Details are pretty but do not change meaning. |
| Ten Shift | The reader sees the earlier world differently after the turn. | The turn is a random twist or just a slogan. |
| Integration | The ending invites a new state, ritual, or action. | The ending becomes hype, victory, or generic motivation. |
| Proof And CTA | Revenue-facing assets keep proof and next action explicit. | The story becomes beautiful but commercially soft. |
| Voice Fidelity | Ghostwritten work preserves real phrases, cadence, and worldview. | The piece sounds like a polished template. |
| Platform Fit | Posts, ads, pages, scripts, and decks fit their surface. | The same copy is pasted everywhere. |
| Cultural/Source Caveat | Kishotenketsu is presented as one useful structure, not a universal superiority claim. | The service uses exoticizing or overbroad claims. |

## Sales Page Skeleton

### Headline

Turn generic brand, content, and copy into a world people can feel.

### Subhead

A contrast-driven narrative service for founders, creators, and teams whose message is clear but not yet compelling.

### Problem Frame

Most weak writing is not missing more urgency. It is missing a world.

The audience cannot feel the old reality. They cannot see the process. They cannot name the variable that changed. So the copy reaches for fake enemies, louder pain, or generic transformation language.

### Method

We rebuild the piece through four movements:

- Ki: establish the world
- Sho: deepen through process
- Ten: introduce the variable
- Ketsu: integrate the new reality

### Deliverables

You leave with a rewritten asset, a narrative map, proof examples, and a reusable method for future posts, pages, ads, scripts, campaigns, and ghostwritten pieces.

### CTA

Start with a Diagnostic Sprint when the source material is messy. Start with the Core Narrative Package when the asset and deployment surface are already clear.
