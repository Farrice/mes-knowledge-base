# Tom Segura Before/After Proof Lab

## Purpose

This proves the Tom harvest changes a real writing behavior. The test is not whether the system can summarize comedy. The test is whether it can turn a flat draft into something more recognizable, specific, and useful without imitating Tom's persona.

## Weak Draft Fixture

Audience: consultants, creators, and operators who use AI to write public content but keep getting generic drafts.

Desired reader action: feel seen enough to consider an AI content workflow repair.

Trust risk: medium. Humor can help, but forced jokes would weaken credibility.

Weak draft:

```text
AI can help you create better content faster. But if your prompts are not clear, your posts may sound generic. A better content workflow can help you write with more personality, improve your messaging, and connect with your audience.
```

## Diagnosis

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Weak link | Recognition and charge. |
| Primary failure | The draft explains the problem from above instead of naming the lived frustration. |
| Secondary failure | No reason-for-telling; it reads like advice that could come from anyone. |
| Tom mechanics | Opinion over indifference, say the obvious, way-in before anecdote, word-choice laugh lever, text timing compensation. |
| Next gate | Sam Parr humor-fit check, then `/publishable-copy-gate` before public use. |

## Rewritten Copy

```text
Your AI draft is not bad because the model lacks personality.

It is bad because your workflow handed it a grocery bag of half-thoughts and asked for a dinner party.

So the post comes back technically correct, emotionally vacant, and wearing the little blazer all AI drafts seem to own.

The fix is not "write funnier prompts."

It is a workflow that preserves the messy human part: the thing you were actually annoyed by, the sentence you almost deleted, the proof you forgot to include, and the detail that makes the reader think, unfortunately, yes, that is exactly me.
```

## Behavior Delta

| Before | After | Behavior Change |
|---|---|---|
| Starts with a generic AI benefit. | Starts with a recognizable misdiagnosis. | Reader has a clearer reason to keep reading. |
| Says "sound generic" abstractly. | Shows the generic-draft feeling through a concrete image. | The problem becomes felt, not merely understood. |
| Uses neutral advice language. | Names annoyance, deletion, forgotten proof, and recognition. | The copy has charge and a lived-world texture. |
| Tries to add personality as a promise. | Explains the workflow failure behind flat personality. | Trust increases because the mechanism is clearer. |
| No next gate. | Sends conversion use to Sam Parr and publishable gates. | Humor stays subordinate to proof and action. |

## Source Mechanics Used

- `00:02:13`: funny needs opinion; indifference is dead.
- `00:07:46`: name the thing everyone has experienced.
- `00:49:26`: find the reason for telling the story before telling it.
- `00:58:16`: stronger word choices carry more comedic energy.
- `01:10:40`: written comedy must encode cadence in the words.

## Remaining Risk

The rewrite is stronger as a post opener or offer bridge, but it still needs real proof before becoming a sales asset: a named workflow repair, before/after client example, or measurable reduction in rewrite time.
