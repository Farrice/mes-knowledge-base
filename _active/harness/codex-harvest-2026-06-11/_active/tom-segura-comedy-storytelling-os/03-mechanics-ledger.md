| Mechanic | Evidence Anchor | Deploy In | Failure If Misused |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conversation premise detector | `00:01:22` | Voice notes, draft warmups, hook mining. | Polishing before any living phrase appears. |
| Opinion over indifference | `00:02:13` | Social posts, essays, product opinions, brand voice. | Neutral corporate language with no charge. |
| Say the obvious | `00:07:46` | Observational posts, category naming, relatable hooks. | Treating obviousness as disqualification instead of potential. |
| Dumb-first reps | `00:10:02` | Brainstorming, creative sessions, joke generation. | Editing out the premise before it gets tested. |
| Surprise and reluctant laugh | `00:11:20` | Punchlines, hooks, reframes, uncomfortable truths. | Shock without rapport or surprise without meaning. |
| Goodwill before edge | `00:12:31` | Authority comedy, client-facing humor, sharper opinions. | Leading with the riskiest line before trust exists. |
| 200-rep standard | `00:13:27` | Signature stories, keynote bits, recurring content themes. | Calling a draft "done" after one pass. |
| Premise deconstruction | `00:15:13` | Thought leadership, social commentary, sales narratives. | Jumping to tags before the premise is clear. |

## Quality Rubric

| Dimension | Pass |
|---|---|
| Charge | The draft has a real opinion, irritation, delight, embarrassment, or tension. |
| Recognition | At least one line names something the audience already knows but has not articulated. |
| Way in | The story has a reason for being told before the anecdote takes over. |
| Compression | The draft has been expanded first and cut second. |
| Surprise | The punch, turn, or final line moves somewhere adjacent but unexpected. |
| Word music | The strongest verbs and nouns are chosen for sound, image, and meaning. |
| Trust | Humor does not weaken credibility or obscure the action. |

## Anti-Patterns

- Asking the model to "make it funny" without naming audience, stakes, and trust risk.
- Adding one-liners after the draft instead of finding the joke inside the draft's actual tension.
- Mistaking edginess for surprise.
- Telling a story because it happened, not because the reader needs it.
- Overusing callbacks because one callback worked.
- Imitating Tom's voice, profanity, or persona instead of using his method.
