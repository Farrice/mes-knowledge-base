# LinkedIn Validation Sprint - AI Operating Partner Offer

## Intent Lock

**Goal:** Position the AI Operating Partner / Agent-Ready Operating System offer through Farrice's current LinkedIn profile without turning the profile into a generic AI consultant page.

**Primary validation question:** Can the current audience and near-network understand, feel, and respond to the problem: "Your best knowledge is trapped in human heads, so AI is forced to guess"?

**Success is not a prettier profile.** Success is proof of signal:

- profile visitors understand the new offer direction
- posts create comments, DMs, saves, or profile views from relevant buyers
- at least 3 real people submit a workflow, SOP, failed AI output, or "this is my problem" reply
- at least 1 person accepts a paid or serious diagnostic conversation

## Positioning Verdict

Do not lead with "semantic document libraries" on LinkedIn.

That language is valuable inside the offer and delivery system, but cold profile visitors will not feel it yet.

Lead with the sharper buyer pain:

> Your best customer knowledge, founder judgment, and delivery rules are trapped in human heads. That is why your AI keeps producing work that looks finished but still feels wrong.

Then name the mechanism:

> I turn that hidden knowledge into agent-ready operating systems: customer language libraries, work primitives, command surfaces, and validation loops.

This preserves the bridge from your current profile:

**Customer language -> founder judgment -> agent-readable marketing/work systems.**

That bridge is the positioning sweet spot.

- marketing consultant with AI tools

The category should feel like you are combining taste, psychology, and systems:

4. **What should I do?** Send one SOP, workflow, failed AI output, or marketing asset for a misfire diagnosis.

## First Three Posts

### Post 1 - Problem Declaration

Your AI is not bad.

It is blind.

It sees the prompt.
It sees the document.
It sees the SOP.

But it does not see the customer language that makes your offer land.

It does not see the founder judgment that tells the team when to stop.

It does not see the edge cases, approval rules, or quality standard living in someone's head.

So it guesses.

Sometimes the guess is obvious.
Usually it is worse:

The output looks finished, but still feels wrong.

That is the gap I am paying attention to now.

Not better prompts.
Not more tools.
Not another automation dashboard.

The missing layer is agent-readable work.

If your AI keeps producing work that is almost useful but still needs you to rescue it, send me one example.

I will show you where it guessed.

### Post 2 - Proof Demo

I gave AI a normal workflow instruction.

It gave me a normal answer.

That was the failure.

The instruction was reasonable:

"Start the session, figure out the task, choose the right workflow, and give useful next steps."

A human understands what that means.

An AI has to infer everything underneath it:

- how deep to go
- what tools to route
- when to validate
- what authority it has
- when to stop
- what a good next step looks like

So the answer becomes generically helpful.

Not wrong.

Just not operationally alive.

Then I rewrote the same workflow as a semantic work primitive:

- purpose
- inputs
- outputs
- authority
- decision rules
- examples
- failure modes
- quality tests

Same task.
Different operating layer.

Now the AI knows when to act, when to ask, and when it is guessing.

That is the difference between documentation and agent-readable work.

### Post 3 - Founder Judgment

The founder is still the operating system.

That is the part most AI consultants skip.

They look at your business and ask:

"What can we automate?"

I think the better question is:

"What does the founder keep explaining?"

Because that is usually where the real system lives.

The client nuance.
The quality bar.
The exception logic.
The customer language.
The thing a senior person knows in 12 seconds that the SOP never says.

If that stays trapped in the founder's head, AI can only imitate the surface.

It can write the email.
It can summarize the call.
It can draft the proposal.

But it cannot reliably know what matters.

That is why the next wave of AI work is not just automation.

It is making the work agent-readable.

## Offer Assumption Test

### Problem

Founder-led teams are using AI, but the best business knowledge still lives in conversations, intuition, customer language, and founder judgment. AI outputs look polished but require human rescue.

### Current Alternative

- write better prompts
- try a new model
- hire an automation person
- create more SOPs
- keep correcting the AI manually

### Forces

| Force | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| Push | Founder/team is tired of re-explaining context and correcting "almost useful" AI |
| Pull | A system where AI can execute from clearer meaning, authority, and validation |
| Inertia | Current manual correction is annoying but familiar |
| Friction | Buyer may not believe "semantic work" is practical or urgent yet |

### Mafia Offer

Send me one failed AI output, SOP, prompt, or workflow.

I will return a one-page AI Misfire Map showing:

- where the AI was forced to guess
- what meaning was missing
- what authority or source of truth was unclear
- what the first agent-ready version should include

If the map is useful, the paid next step is a focused audit or sprint around that workflow.
