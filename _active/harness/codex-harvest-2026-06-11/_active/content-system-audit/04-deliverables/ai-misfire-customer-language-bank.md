# AI Misfire Customer Language Bank

## One Problem To Own

The public problem:

> AI output looks finished, but you still cannot trust it without rewriting, correcting, or explaining what it missed.

The deeper problem:

> The AI is being asked to do work that depends on judgment, customer language, examples, boundaries, and taste that were never made visible.

The offer promise:

> Send one prompt, SOP, workflow, or failed AI output. I will show where the AI was forced to guess.

## What The Buyer Phrase Really Means

| What They Say | What It Often Means | What To Map |
|---|---|---|
| "It sounds generic." | The AI lacks customer language, point of view, proof, and exclusions. | Buyer language, examples, anti-examples, tone boundaries. |
| "It missed the point." | The purpose of the work was not defined beyond the task. | Purpose, intended outcome, strategic role of the asset. |
| "It is close, but I cannot use it." | The quality bar is implicit. | Quality tests, final approval criteria, examples of usable output. |
| "It keeps needing my edits." | Founder judgment has not been captured as rules. | Decision rules, escalation boundaries, edge cases. |
| "It used the wrong angle." | The source of truth or buyer stage is unclear. | Source hierarchy, audience state, buying belief, offer context. |
| "It made things up." | The AI did not know what it may infer versus what it must verify. | Authority boundaries, evidence rules, "do not invent" constraints. |
| "It did too much." | The AI lacked scope and stopping rules. | Task boundary, output length, when to ask for help. |
| "The SOP is clear, but AI still gets it wrong." | The SOP is human-readable, not agent-readable. | Inputs, outputs, source of truth, examples, counterexamples, failure modes. |

## Jargon Rules

- First-touch content should use buyer language before expert language.
- Use one authority term only after the plain problem is obvious.
- Define the term immediately in normal language.
- Never stack multiple insider terms in one sentence.
- If a sentence needs "semantic," "agentic," and "governance" to sound important, rewrite it.
- Let the market sources inform the point of view, but do not turn posts into citations.

Good pattern:

> The enterprise term for part of this is context engineering. Plain English: AI cannot make a good decision if the context behind the decision is invisible.

Bad pattern:

> We install semantic context infrastructure for agentic workflow governance.

## Objection Language

### "Is this just prompt engineering?"

```text
Sometimes the fix is a better prompt. But if the same correction keeps happening, the prompt is usually where the symptom shows up, not where the problem lives.

The audit maps the missing judgment behind the prompt.
```

### "I do not have a clean workflow."

```text
That is fine. Messy is often better.

The goal is not to judge the document. The goal is to find the moment AI has to fill in blanks a human would fill from experience.
```

### "Can AI really handle this?"

```text
Maybe. Maybe not.

That is why I start with a misfire map before an implementation. Some work needs a prompt fix. Some needs a clearer SOP. Some is not ready for automation yet.
```

### "We already use AI."

```text
That is exactly who this is for.

The question is not whether AI is in the workflow. The question is where it still needs expert rescue before the output can be trusted.
```

## Do Not Say List

Avoid these in public posts, profile copy, and first-touch outreach:

- "Unlock the power of AI"
- "Revolutionize your workflow"
- "AI transformation for modern businesses"
- "10x your productivity"
- "Future-proof your business"
- "Harness cutting-edge agentic systems"
- "Semantic document infrastructure"
- "AI-native business architecture" as a first line
- "Automate everything"
- "Replace yourself with AI"
- "Prompt engineering secrets"
- "Done-for-you AI automations" as the main offer
- "Thought leadership" as a buyer promise
- "Operating system" without explaining the concrete workflow it affects

## High-Resonance Lines

- The most dangerous AI output is not the bad one. It is the one that looks finished.
- A human fills in blanks from experience. AI fills in blanks from probability.
- The correction is the data.
- The prompt is where the symptom shows up. The missing judgment is where the problem lives.
- Your AI did the task. It did not understand the work.
- Faster wrong output is not leverage.
- The first fix is not another tool. It is finding where the tool had to guess.
- If AI keeps needing you to rescue the same output, that is not a productivity problem. That is a missing-system problem.
- The more autonomy you give AI, the less vague your work can be.
- A good AI workflow does not remove judgment. It makes the right judgment reusable.

## Customer-Language Quality Gate

Before publishing or sending, check:

- Can a non-technical founder understand this without knowing "context engineering"?
- Does the copy name a moment they have actually felt?
- Does the CTA ask for one concrete artifact, not a vague call?
- Does it avoid pretending the problem is solved before seeing the workflow?
- Does it reinforce Farrice as Oracle/Catalyst: clear-eyed diagnosis plus practical next step?
