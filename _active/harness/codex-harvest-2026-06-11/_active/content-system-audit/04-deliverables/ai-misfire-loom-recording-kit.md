# AI Misfire Express Loom Recording Kit

## Purpose

Record one clear proof-first Loom that makes the AI Misfire Express offer obvious in under five minutes.

The viewer should leave thinking:

> "I have an AI output, prompt, SOP, or workflow that keeps doing this. I should send it to Farrice."

## Recording Setup

### Recording length

- Primary Loom: 4 to 6 minutes.
- Short version: 60 to 90 seconds.
- One take is fine if the message is clear. Do not chase a perfect performance.

### Screen setup

Open three tabs or windows before recording:

1. A normal prompt, SOP, or workflow instruction.
2. A sample "almost useful but wrong" AI output.
3. The AI Misfire Map intake page or Notion form.

If you do not have a real client example yet, use this controlled demo:

```text
Prompt:
Write a follow-up email to a founder-led consultant who asked about improving their AI content workflow.

Bad output pattern:
The email sounds polished, but it does not know the buyer's current belief, what they already tried, what source of truth controls the offer, or when to invite a paid audit.

Missing operating meaning:
Audience: founder-led expert, not generic business owner.
Goal: get one failed output submitted, not book a broad strategy call.
Quality bar: specific, restrained, no AI hype.
Authority boundary: do not diagnose deeply before seeing the workflow.
CTA: send one prompt, SOP, workflow, or failed output.
```

## Five-Minute Loom Script

### 0:00-0:20 - Hook

Say this directly:

```text
If your AI output looks finished but you still have to rescue it, the model may not be the problem.

The problem may be that the work is not legible enough for AI to execute without guessing.
```

Pause for half a second.

```text
I call this an AI misfire.
```

### 0:20-0:45 - Who this is for

```text
This is for founder-led experts, consultants, coaches, small agencies, and AI-forward service businesses already using AI inside content, sales, proposals, delivery, or client workflows.

You are not starting from zero.

You are already using AI.

But the output still needs your judgment before it can be trusted.
```

### 0:45-1:35 - Show the ordinary input

Screen-share the normal prompt or SOP.

```text
Here is the normal version of the work.

On the surface, this instruction looks reasonable. A human can read it and fill in the blanks because the human already knows the business, the buyer, the tone, the offer, the edge cases, and what "good" means.

AI does not know that automatically.

So when the instruction is missing source of truth, quality standards, decision rules, and examples, the AI still produces something.

It just has to guess.
```

### 1:35-2:35 - Show the misfire

Screen-share the sample output.

```text
This is the dangerous part.

The output is not obviously terrible.

It has structure. It sounds professional. It may even be grammatically clean.

But this is where the founder or senior operator starts correcting it:

"That's not what we mean."
"We would not say it that way."
"It missed the real objection."
"It did the task, but not the work."

That correction is not a small edit.

That correction is evidence.

It shows the operating knowledge that was missing.
```

### 2:35-3:40 - Explain the operating fix

Screen-share a simple before/after outline.

```text
Most people respond by rewriting the prompt.

Sometimes that helps.

But if the correction keeps happening, the prompt is not the system.

The hidden judgment is the system.

The fix is to make the work agent-readable.

That means defining:

purpose,
inputs,
source of truth,
authority boundaries,
decision rules,
examples,
counterexamples,
failure modes,
and quality tests.

That is what turns "write this for me" into work an agent can actually execute.
```

### 3:40-4:35 - Name the paid diagnostic

```text
That is what I map inside AI Misfire Express.

You send me one prompt, SOP, workflow, content process, sales follow-up, proposal process, or failed AI output.

I map where the AI is being forced to guess, what operating meaning is missing, and whether the fix is a prompt tweak, an SOP rewrite, or a deeper agent-ready work primitive.

This is not a full automation build.

It is the diagnostic before the build.

Because faster wrong output is not leverage.
```

### 4:35-5:00 - CTA

Screen-share the Notion intake form.

```text
If this is happening in your business, send one example through the AI Misfire Map.

Messy is fine.

Paste the prompt, SOP, workflow, or failed output.

I will look for the first places AI is guessing.

If it is worth a paid audit, I will tell you. If it is not, I will tell you that too.
```

## Recording Checklist

- [ ] First sentence names the pain: polished output that still needs rescue.
- [ ] The viewer sees a before/after or controlled demo.
- [ ] No claim implies guaranteed revenue, automation success, or model superiority.
- [ ] You say "diagnostic before the build."
- [ ] You preserve human judgment instead of promising replacement.
- [ ] CTA asks for one asset, not a broad call.
- [ ] End screen shows the intake path.

| Voice | Direct, restrained, pattern-recognition tone |

## Red-Team Notes

- Do not overexplain semantic documents in the Loom. Sell the pain first.
- Do not make the viewer feel behind or stupid. The issue is hidden work meaning, not incompetence.
- Do not position this as "better prompting." That collapses the category.
- Do not pitch full operating systems in the first Loom. The cash path is the diagnostic.
