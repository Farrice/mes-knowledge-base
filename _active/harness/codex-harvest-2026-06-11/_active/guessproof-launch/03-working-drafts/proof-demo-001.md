# Proof Demo 001: The Follow-Up That Looked Ready

This is demo proof, not client proof.

Use this as the first public proof artifact until a real client-approved example exists. It shows the method without claiming client results.

## Scenario

An expert consultant used AI to draft a follow-up email after a strategy call with a warm lead. The draft looked polished, but the consultant could not send it. It sounded generic, skipped the real tension from the call, and moved to a proposal too quickly.

## Input

### Original Prompt

> Write a follow-up email after a discovery call. Thank them for their time, recap the conversation, and ask if they want to move forward with a strategy sprint.

### AI Output Before

Subject: Great speaking with you

Hi Jordan,

Thanks again for taking the time to speak with me today. I enjoyed learning more about your business and the goals you are working toward.

As discussed, it sounds like you are looking for ways to improve your current marketing process and create a clearer strategy for growth. I believe a strategy sprint could help you identify opportunities, clarify priorities, and build a plan to move forward.

If you are interested, I would be happy to send over more information about how the sprint works and what the next steps would look like.

Best,

Farrice

## Why It Misfires

| Misfire | Where AI Guessed | Missing Human Judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Generic recap | "Improve your current marketing process" | The actual pain was that the founder keeps rewriting AI-generated sales content before it goes to clients. |
| Soft problem | "Create a clearer strategy for growth" | The real cost is expert rescue work, quality anxiety, and slow client delivery. |
| Weak next step | "Send over more information" | The buyer needs a concrete artifact review, not more information. |
| No signal capture | No reference to the specific moment from the call | The call revealed a repeatable workflow failure: AI drafts proposals that sound right but miss the founder's judgment. |
| No risk boundary | The email implies a broad strategy sprint | The right scope is one recurring workflow, one proof artifact, one validation pass. |

## Hidden Judgment

The consultant was not rejecting the email because of style. They were rejecting it because it failed to preserve the buyer's real decision logic.

The missing judgment:

- Name the exact rescue loop the buyer described.
- Show that the rewrite is evidence, not failure.
- Avoid selling a broad sprint before the buyer sees the misfire pattern.
- Ask for the artifact that reveals the problem.
- Keep the next step low-friction and diagnostic.

## Guessproof Rules Captured

| Rule | Why It Exists |
|---|---|
| Name the specific rescue loop in the first three lines. | The buyer must feel recognized before being asked to act. |
| Replace vague growth language with the exact workflow failure. | Generic value props hide the reason the buyer cares. |
| Ask for one artifact before proposing a call. | The offer is proof-led; the artifact is the sales conversation. |
| Use "where AI was forced to guess" instead of "bad prompt." | This protects the buyer's status and reframes the issue as missing context. |
| Promise a bounded diagnosis, not a full free build. | Free triage should create trust without giving away the paid audit. |

## Repaired Output After

Subject: The rewrite is the signal

Hi Jordan,

The part that stood out from our call was not that AI is failing you. It was that your team keeps using AI, then you still have to rescue the final 20 percent before anything can reach a client.

That rewrite is the signal.

It usually means the workflow is missing your decision rules: what to include, what to leave out, what tone protects the relationship, and what proof has to be present before a recommendation is safe to send.

For the first step, do not send me the whole process. Send one AI-assisted follow-up, proposal section, sales email, or client-facing draft that looked close but still needed your rescue.

I will map the first three places the AI was forced to guess and tell you whether this is a prompt issue, an SOP issue, a workflow issue, or not ready to systemize yet.

If the pattern is worth fixing, I can turn that one recurring misfire into a reusable execution kit with source-of-truth rules, decision boundaries, and validation.

Best,

Farrice

## Reusable Protocol

1. Collect one real AI-assisted output the buyer had to rescue.
2. Ask for the original prompt, source notes, and intended audience.
3. Mark every place the AI invented, softened, generalized, or skipped judgment.
4. Convert each misfire into a rule, boundary, source requirement, or human review point.
5. Repair the output once.
6. Package the repair as a reusable workflow kit.
7. Deliver a Guessproof Receipt showing before, diagnosis, after, rules captured, checks added, and remaining human review.

## Validation Receipt

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Does the repaired version name the real pain? | Pass: "final 20 percent" and "client-facing" rescue are named. |
| Does it avoid blaming the buyer? | Pass: "AI was forced to guess" reframes the failure. |
| Does it ask for an artifact? | Pass: asks for one AI-assisted follow-up, proposal section, sales email, or client-facing draft. |
| Does it avoid overpromising? | Pass: offers a diagnosis and fit verdict before implementation. |
| Does it create a paid bridge? | Pass: "If the pattern is worth fixing..." opens the sprint conversation. |
