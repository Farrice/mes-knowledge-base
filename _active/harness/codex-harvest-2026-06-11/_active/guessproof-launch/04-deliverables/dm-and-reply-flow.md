# DM And Reply Flow

Use this to move from public interest to artifact submission to free triage to paid audit. Do not send messages without explicit approval.

## Public Comment Reply

When someone comments with interest:

> Send me the actual misfire in DM. The best example is an AI output that looked close but still needed your rewrite before it could go out. I will map the first three places it was forced to guess.

## DM Intake Reply

> Perfect. Send whatever you have:
>
> 1. The AI output you had to rescue.
> 2. The original prompt or instruction.
> 3. Who it was for.
> 4. What you changed before sending, if you know.
> 5. What made the first version unsafe, weak, generic, or unusable.
>
> I will return the first three places it was forced to guess.

## If They Ask "Can I Just Describe It?"

> You can, but the artifact is better. The misfire usually hides in the exact wording, missing source material, and decision boundary. If you can send even a rough excerpt, I can give you a much sharper read.

## If They Ask "What Do You Charge?"

> The free triage is three bullets on one misfire. If it looks like a recurring workflow worth fixing, the paid AI Misfire Map is $750. That is a 48-hour audit of one workflow: full misfire map, hidden-judgment diagnosis, first repair, and a Guessproof Receipt. If you move into a workflow sprint within 7 days, part of the audit can credit toward the sprint.

## Free Triage Delivery

> I mapped the first three places this was forced to guess:
>
> 1. [Where AI guessed.]
> 2. [What judgment was missing.]
> 3. [Fit verdict: prompt issue, SOP issue, workflow issue, or not ready.]
>
> My read: [one-sentence recommendation.]

## Paid Audit Bridge

Use when the workflow is real, recurring, and valuable.

> This looks like a workflow issue, not just a prompt issue.
>
> The repeated rescue point is [specific pattern]. A better prompt may improve the surface, but the real fix is to capture the source-of-truth rules, decision boundaries, and validation checks that your judgment is currently supplying manually.
>
> The paid AI Misfire Map is the right next step if you want me to map the full pattern. It is $750, delivered in 48 hours, and includes the full diagnosis, first workflow fix, and Guessproof Receipt.

## Soft Close

> If you want, I can do the paid map on this workflow. I would keep the scope tight: one workflow, one misfire pattern, one first repair, and a clear verdict on whether it should become a sprint.

## If They Are Not Ready

> I would not pay to build this yet. The missing piece is source material. Before systemizing, capture two or three examples of the version you would actually approve, plus the rules you used to decide what changed. Once that exists, this becomes much easier to map.

## Objection: "Is This Just Prompt Engineering?"

> Sometimes the verdict is a prompt issue, but often the prompt is only where the deeper problem shows up. The bigger question is: what judgment was the AI forced to invent? If the answer involves source material, risk, client context, proof, or review standards, it needs more than a better prompt.

## Objection: "Can AI Really Handle This?"

> I do not assume it can. That is why the first step is a misfire map. Some workflows should become reusable. Some only need a prompt or SOP patch. Some are not ready because the source material is not clear enough yet.

## Objection: "I Do Not Want To Share Client Info"

> Redact names, numbers, and sensitive details. I only need enough structure to see where the AI guessed and what judgment had to be supplied manually.

## Disqualifiers

Do not push paid work if:

- They cannot share even a redacted artifact.
- The workflow does not recur.
- The output does not matter to revenue, delivery, trust, or client quality.
- They want guaranteed leads or revenue.
- They want broad automation before proving one workflow.
- They are looking for a cheap prompt pack.
