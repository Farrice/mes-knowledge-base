# Hybrid LinkedIn Oracle Content Brief

## Positioning Lock

Use the Hybrid Sequence:

> Creative Strategist + AI Operating Partner.

The public wedge:

> Your AI keeps guessing because the judgment behind the work is still trapped in your head.

The backend moat:

> Customer language, founder judgment, source-of-truth rules, and work primitives that make marketing and operating workflows agent-ready.

Do not lead with "semantic document library." Lead with the pain people can recognize in 10 seconds:

> The output looks finished, but you still have to rescue it.

## Archetype Lock: Oracle Primary, Catalyst Secondary

### Oracle Role

The Oracle names the hidden pattern the market is feeling but cannot yet articulate:

- AI is not just a tool problem.
- Prompt quality is not the whole problem.
- The real bottleneck is invisible judgment.
- Founder corrections are not annoying edits; they are evidence.
- The next edge is not "more output." It is work clear enough for AI to execute without guessing.

### Catalyst Role

The Catalyst makes the audience feel capable of acting now:

- Send one misfire.
- Map where it guessed.
- Turn the correction into a rule, example, boundary, or source of truth.
- Fix one workflow before selling the grand operating system.

### What To Avoid

- Oracle without proof: abstract insight, no concrete artifact.
- Catalyst without depth: generic motivation, no diagnosis.
- AI consultant voice: tools, automations, and efficiency claims with no buyer psychology.
- Overexplaining the mechanism before the buyer feels the problem.

## Offer Diagnosis

### What The 2026 Market Validates

The 2026 evidence strongly validates the direction:

- AI is moving into daily work and professional workflows.
- Teams are under pressure to redesign how work gets done.
- Agents and AI workflows need governance, context, accountability, and human validation.
- Buyers prefer low-friction, proof-oriented paths and need value clarity.

### What The Market Does Not Yet Validate

The market evidence does not prove that founder-led experts will pay immediately for AI Misfire Express.

That must be tested through:

- comments with buyer language
- DMs that mention a real AI failure
- submitted prompts, SOPs, workflows, or outputs
- paid audit interest
- implementation upsell interest

### Cash-First Offer For This Sprint

Offer name:

> AI Misfire Express

Plain-English offer:

> Send me one prompt, SOP, workflow, or failed AI output. I will show where AI was forced to guess and what needs to become clearer before it can be trusted.

Price ladder:

- $500 starter audit for first proof buyers
- $750 standard audit
- $1,000 express audit
- $1,500-$2,000 implementation sprint for one workflow that clearly needs a primitive

### What Not To Pursue Yet

- Full AI Operating Partner retainers as the first public CTA
- Enterprise governance language as the first impression
- "Semantic document libraries" as the headline offer
- Automated LinkedIn outreach or fake engagement
- A full profile overhaul before signal collection

## Social Strategy

### Week 1 Job

Do not try to explain the whole category.

Make one problem unavoidable:

> AI output that looks finished but still needs human rescue.

Then ask for one artifact:

> Send the prompt, SOP, workflow, or failed output.

### Content Mix

- 2 perspective-shift posts
- 1 practical diagnostic post
- 1 story/field-note post
- 1 explicit offer post
- 20 comments that join existing AI/workflow/customer-language conversations
- 10 manual warm DMs only after real interaction or clear relevance

### Posting Cadence

| Day | Asset | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Flagship post | Name the AI misfire problem and collect signal. |
| Day 2 | Founder rescue post | Turn correction into evidence. |
| Day 3 | Customer language post | Bridge creative strategist identity to AI operating partner. |
| Day 4 | Prompt vs. primitive post | Teach the fix without jargon. |
| Day 5 | Offer post | Invite paid audits from the warmest readers. |

## Buyer-Language Rules

Lead with:

- "looks finished but still needs rescue"
- "not what we mean"
- "I still cannot send it"
- "the last output you had to rewrite"
- "AI is close, but not trustworthy"

Then explain:

- "where AI guessed"
- "what judgment was missing"
- "what source of truth it needed"
- "whether this is a prompt issue or a workflow issue"

Save for later:

- context engineering
- semantic document libraries
- work primitives
- agentic operating model

## First-50-Word Gate

Each LinkedIn post must pass:

1. The first sentence names a felt problem or surprising truth.
2. The first 50 words do not require technical knowledge.
3. The reader can tell whether the post is for them before the first line break.
4. No "as AI transforms the future" phrasing.
5. No more than one abstract noun in the opening.

## Measurement Rules

Track these daily:

- comments from founder-led experts, consultants, agencies, operators, or AI-forward service providers
- DMs mentioning a specific AI output or workflow
- number of submitted prompts, SOPs, workflows, or failed outputs
- number of people asking "how much?"
- number of people who say "this is exactly what is happening"
- number of paid audit objections
- number of qualified workflows that could become proof demos

Decision after 14 days:

- Continue if 3+ qualified artifacts are submitted and at least 1 paid audit is strongly interested or closed.
- Adjust language if posts get likes but no submitted artifacts.
- Adjust buyer segment if agencies respond but founder-led experts do not.
- Pause paid push if people understand the content but cannot name a workflow.

## Oren Pass

- Archetype fit: Strong Oracle with practical Catalyst action.
- Commercial connection: Strong if every post asks for one misfire, not a broad strategy call.
- Originality: Strong when framed around "the correction is the data" and "AI did the task, not the work."
- Execution reality: Strong because it asks for one artifact and one small diagnostic.
- Anti-bloat: Strong if "semantic document library" stays behind the curtain until the buyer is warm.

## Donald Miller Pass

- The customer is the hero: yes, the founder is already trying to make AI useful.
- The guide is clear: Farrice diagnoses the hidden judgment and shows the first fix.
- The plan is simple: send one misfire, map guess-points, decide the fix.
- The CTA is concrete: DM `GUESS` or send one failed output.
- The language is understandable without knowing "context engineering."

## Red-Team Pass

- Risk: The offer may sound like a prompt audit if not framed carefully.
  - Fix: Always include judgment, source of truth, decision rules, and quality bar.
- Risk: The content may sound too enterprise if over-citing 2026 sources.
  - Fix: Use sources to inform claims, not to dominate posts.
- Risk: The public CTA may attract messy low-quality submissions.
  - Fix: That is acceptable for signal, but paid audit qualification must stay strict.
- Risk: The broader AI Operating Partner offer may distract from cash.
  - Fix: Keep Week 1 focused on AI Misfire Express.
