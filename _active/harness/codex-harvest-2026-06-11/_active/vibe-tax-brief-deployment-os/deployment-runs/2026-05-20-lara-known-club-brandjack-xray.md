## LinkedIn Draft

Everyone copied Lara's format.

Almost nobody copied the actual move.

I looked at The Known Club page because it would be easy to file it under "LinkedIn growth."

That would miss the useful part.

The page is not really selling "post more."

It is selling a safer way to become visible.

There is a difference.

Most people trying to grow on LinkedIn are not short on advice.

They are drowning in it.

They have saved the hooks.

Copied the spacing.

Rewritten the first line six times.

Built a little graveyard of "good posts" they will never actually publish.

Ask me how I know.

The uncomfortable part is not writing.

It is pressing publish while you still feel self-appointed.

That is where Lara's offer gets interesting.

The Known Club stacks the pieces that make visibility feel earned:

live coaching,

operator proximity,

AI and content infrastructure,

feedback loops,

community witnesses,

and proof that moves from views to calls to clients.

That is not random.

It answers the quiet fear underneath most personal brand advice:

"Who am I to say this publicly?"

Templates do not fix that.

A better hook does not fix that.

Posting three times a week does not fix that.

Those are signals.

Proof is different.

A signal says:

"People noticed."

Proof says:

"The right buyer understood enough to move."

That distinction matters because visibility can lie to you.

A post can get comments and still create no buyer trust.

A profile can look active and still make buyers do all the translation.

A creator can sound like an expert and still leave people unsure what they would actually buy.

That is the expensive part.

Not being unknown.

Being visible in a way buyers still cannot use.

That gap is what I call the Vibe Tax.

The hidden cost of trusting signals that feel good but do not reduce buyer confusion.

This is also why I think Lara's category is bigger than "LinkedIn growth."

At the surface:

followers,

content,

AI tools,

positioning.

Underneath:

status safety.

A way for experts to become known without feeling like they crowned themselves in public.

That is the move most people copy wrong.

They copy the short lines.

They miss the trust architecture.

They copy the cadence.

They miss the proof ladder.

They copy the confidence.

They miss the part that makes confidence believable.

The next question is not:

"How do I get more visible?"

It is:

"When people see me, do they know what I am safe to trust?"

That is the diagnostic.

What signal are you over-trusting right now:

views, comments, followers, compliments, or sales calls?

## First Comment

The proof ladder I would use:

1. Attention: people noticed.
2. Comprehension: people can repeat what you do.
3. Trust: people believe your method.
4. Intent: the right people ask sharper questions.
5. Conversion: buyers take the next step.
6. Memory: your phrase travels without you.

Most content advice gets people stuck at 1-2.

Strong offers build from 3 onward.

Which rung is your content actually stuck on?
