## Core Rafa Principles From The Transcript

### 1. Sell The Idea, Not The Mockup

Rafa's line: if you have a feature idea, do not just share mockups or a prototype. Make an ad. Sell the idea.

Farrice translation: do not just explain an AI workflow, offer, or content system. Stage the feeling of what it does for the person. Show the before-state, the emotional tension, the reveal, and the payoff.

### 2. First Experiences Create Mythology

The first MacBook setup video made the computer feel like a movie, a sci-fi object, and a polished world. The first run framed everything after it.

Farrice translation: the first interaction with an AI Brain, proof run, content sprint, client audit, or agent workflow should set the emotional frame. The client should feel, "This is different. This gets me. This is not another generic AI thing."

### 3. Metal Gear Solid As Memory Engineering

Two mechanics stood out:

- Meryl's codec frequency hidden on the physical game box.
- Psycho Mantis reading the player through the controller, then being defeated by moving the controller to port two.

These were not just clever mechanics. They made the player step outside the expected box. The friction made the solution unforgettable.

Farrice translation: great content and offers sometimes need the audience to do a little cognitive work. Not confusion for its own sake, but a designed moment where they feel the click.

### 4. Surprise Needs Meaning

"Surprise and delight" is overused, but the underlying truth is still powerful. People remember moments that made them say, "I did not think that was possible," or "How did they do that?"

Farrice translation: AI content should not be faster slop. It should create a moment where the reader realizes their old frame was too small.

### 5. Authored Software Has Fingerprints

Rafa points to indie apps, Panic, Hand Mirror, side projects, humor, about pages, settings labels, haptics, video, sound, and awkward little details that make people feel the human behind the product.

Farrice translation: your brand needs fingerprints. Gaming, fatherhood, anti-guru skepticism, spirituality, and AI craft are not decorations. They are proof there is a person behind the system.

### 6. The Motif Makes Decisions Easier

Retro's onboarding started as a slideshow plus haptics. The team realized the haptics made users feel the pacing. Once they found the motif - fast world slowing down into what matters - sound, pacing, visuals, and haptics aligned.

Farrice translation: every campaign, post, offer, or workflow needs a motif. Once the motif is found, copy, examples, visuals, and CTAs become easier.

### 7. Design For The People You Want To Keep

Rafa accepts that some high-feeling moments may create drop-off. The design question is not only "Did fewer people continue?" It is "Who stayed, and what kind of relationship did the moment create?"

Farrice translation: some content should filter. If the work is true to the people you want, it may repel people who were never going to care deeply anyway.
