# Conde Creative Thinking Sprint - Rafa Transcript For Farrice Content

Date: 2026-05-05  
Source: [Rafa Conde inspiration capture](../inspiration-sources/2026-05-05-rafa-conde-memorable-design.md)  
Workflow: `/conde-creative-thinking-sprint`  
Purpose: Generate memorable content, offer, and workflow ideas from Rafa's feeling-led design ethos, with Farrice's gamer identity and AI with Heart positioning.

## 1. Boring Default Diagnosis

### Obvious Solution

Summarize the YouTube transcript into "lessons from Rafa Conde" and make generic posts about surprise and delight.

### Category Habit

Turn the source into productivity advice:

- "Make your work more memorable."
- "Add delight to your product."
- "Use storytelling in design."
- "Sell ideas with video."

### Why It Will Be Forgotten

Those angles remove the voltage. The real power is not "design better." It is the collision between:

- a gamer remembering impossible moments,
- a creator trying to build taste,
- AI making everyone faster but more forgettable,
- and the desire to make content, workflows, and offers feel authored by a real person.

## 2. Feeling Prompts

- Wouldn't it be funny if an AI workflow had a "Psycho Mantis" moment where it proved it understood the user's private pattern?
- It should feel like the first time a game breaks the fourth wall and you realize the designer thought about you.
- The content should seem to know the audience's hidden frustration before it names the solution.
- Readers should remember, "Oh, I have been making mockups when I should have been making the idea felt."
- The first time someone experiences a Farrice workflow should feel like a system with a soul, not a form with automation behind it.
- The content should feel like the controller port two moment: the solution was outside the frame the whole time.

5. **The Box Back Reveal** - Use an overlooked external detail as the key to solving a marketing problem.

9. **The Authored Workflow** - Add a small signature moment to every Farrice workflow handoff.

## 4. Top Five Concepts

### 1. Controller Port Two Content

Interaction: A post or essay starts with a familiar problem, then reveals the solution is outside the expected interface.  
Emotional payoff: "I was solving the wrong layer."  
Build shape: LinkedIn post, newsletter section, or content prompt template.  
Risk: Too abstract if the game reference carries the whole point.  
Test: Can a non-gamer still understand the lesson?

### 2. Psycho Mantis ICP Hook

Interaction: Open by naming the private, slightly uncomfortable audience thought, then show why the system knows it.  
Emotional payoff: "How did he know?"  
Build shape: Hook bank for AI consulting, content audits, and offer pages.  
Risk: Can feel manipulative if not grounded in empathy.  
Test: Does the hook make the right person feel seen, not exposed?

### 3. Make The Idea Felt Audit

Interaction: Review a post, offer, demo, or workflow and classify it as mockup-level or felt-idea-level.  
Emotional payoff: "Now I see why this technically works but does not land."  
Build shape: Productized diagnostic or content quality gate.  
Risk: Could become another abstract rubric.  
Test: Does it produce concrete edits within 15 minutes?

### 4. The Authored Workflow

Interaction: Add small, human, named details to agent workflows: handoff copy, progress states, first-run setup, artifact naming, completion ritual.  
Emotional payoff: "This feels like Farrice built it, not like a generic automation."  
Build shape: Antigravity workflow upgrade pass.  
Risk: Decorative details could slow down serious work.  
Test: Does the detail reduce coldness or increase clarity?

### 5. The Moment Library

Interaction: Every time Farrice feels "that was cool," he stores the moment, mechanism, feeling, and possible use.  
Emotional payoff: "My taste is becoming reusable."  
Build shape: New ideation-bank sub-system for inspiration sources.  
Risk: Hoarding inspiration without deploying it.  
Test: Every source must produce at least three content seeds or one workflow improvement.

## 5. Build Path For Top Three

### Controller Port Two Content

1. Save the Metal Gear mechanic as a reusable content frame.
2. Draft one LinkedIn post using the frame.
3. Add a non-gamer bridge line so the value lands beyond the reference.
4. Route deeper versions through `/story-code-content-engine`.

### Psycho Mantis ICP Hook

1. Build a hook bank from audience private thoughts.
2. Tag each hook by offer: AI Brain, content sprint, voice capture, ICP intelligence.
3. Use `/content-sprint` to turn the best hook into a post.
4. Add ethical guardrails: seen, not shamed.

### Make The Idea Felt Audit

1. Turn Rafa's "make an ad" principle into a 5-question audit.
2. Apply it to one existing Farrice offer page or demo workflow.
3. Convert the before/after into a content proof post.
4. If useful, productize it as part of the AI Brain or content audit.

## 6. Gimmick Risks

- Game references can become nostalgia wallpaper if the business insight is weak.
- "Surprise and delight" can become decorative instead of meaningful.
- Fourth-wall moves can feel try-hard if they do not resolve into the user's problem.
- Friction can become bad UX if the reward is not immediate and worth it.
- Humor and weirdness should serve recognition, not distract from trust.

## 7. Recommended First Experiment

Create one publish-ready LinkedIn post from **Controller Port Two Content**:

Thesis: "Most AI content is forgettable because it optimizes the mockup, not the memory. Metal Gear Solid understood this decades ago: the unforgettable moment happens when the player has to change frames."

Use the post to introduce the larger content direction: AI systems with human fingerprints.
