## Verdict (Full)

**Antigravity is world-class-aspiring with two genuinely state-of-the-art components and three structural debts that hold it back from being world-class.**

The Knowledge Compiler (240 files, 1.8M words, 6-stage pipeline at `execution/knowledge_compiler.py`) is on the actual frontier — a real implementation of the Karpathy LLM-Wiki pattern that most teams only talk about. The tiered loading architecture (Tier 0 → Tier 1.5 Recall grounding → Tier 1 SKILL → Tier 2 genius → Tier 3 sub-agent) and Recall-as-Tier-1.5 grounding solve the right problem (token/quality tradeoff with semantic gating) more sophisticatedly than 95% of agent systems published in 2026. The Chain protocol with a factual-grounding veto (Dimension 4 < 6 blocks delivery) is principled. These are not décor.

But the system suffers from the same disease most ambitious agent stacks have in 2026: **the scaffolding has outpaced the evals.** With 110 skills, 95 agents, 58 directives, and four evolution phases, but only 16 ground-truth benchmark experts and no calibrated quality rubric, the system literally cannot tell whether it's getting better. Evolution loops that don't close are not evolution loops — they're logging. Quality scores without rubric calibration drift toward "everything is a 9/10." A routing layer that doesn't enforce its own bindings (the writers-room/parallax incident, 2026-04-21) is decoration. **More feature-area than truth-loops to keep that feature-area honest.** That's the gap between aspiring and world-class — and it's fixable in 4-6 weeks of disciplined work, not by adding anything new.

## Best-Practice Anchor (2026)

8 principles defining world-class AI orchestration today, drawn from Anthropic's published guidance, Karpathy's LLM-Wiki/context-engineering work, and the broader 2026 production-agent literature:

1. **Simplicity bias.** Start with the simplest workflow that works. Add agentic complexity only when evals prove it pays off. (Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents")

2. **Evals are the moat, not prompts.** Eval-driven development beats prompt tuning. 20-50 real-failure tasks with rubrics where two domain experts agree pass/fail beats elaborate scoring rubrics nobody calibrates. (Anthropic "Demystifying Evals"; LangChain State of Agent Engineering 2026)

3. **Multi-agent only for breadth-first parallel exploration.** Multi-agent systems used 15× tokens vs single-agent in Anthropic's research system. They lose when tasks require shared context, tight interdependencies, or sequential reasoning. Sub-agents help when isolated context windows enable parallel compression; they hurt when used reflexively.

4. **Context engineering > pure RAG.** Karpathy's LLM-Wiki pattern (compile raw notes into structured, interlinked, navigable wiki with an index.md the agent can traverse) is the emerging best practice over pure vector retrieval. Antigravity is already on this path.

5. **Self-improvement only in verifiable domains.** Self-evolving agents work where outputs are objectively gradeable (code compiles, math proves, latency drops). In subjective domains (copy quality, brand fit) without a ground-truth judge, evolution loops drift toward "judge overfitting" and grade inflation.

6. **Three-layer memory under one retrieval substrate.** Episodic / semantic / procedural is correct, but production systems fail when these are siloed without unified retrieval. The 2026 failure mode is not "no memory" — it's "five memories, none load at the right moment."

7. **Transparent decision-making and human checkpoints.** Routing decisions, tool selections, and quality scores must be inspectable. Silent fallbacks (e.g., the 6 Recall failure modes) are 2026 anti-patterns.

8. **Orchestrator-worker with strong agent-computer interfaces.** The lead agent maintains plan state externally; workers get clear, narrow contracts. Most production failures trace to vague task delegation, not model capability.

## What's Structurally Weak (9 Findings)

### 1. Evolution loops are aspirational, not closed
**Evidence**: Phase 1-4 scripts exist (`log_performance.py`, `skill_evolution_protocol.py`, `gap_analysis.py`, `evolution_tracer.py`) but no `evolution_orchestrator.py` runs them as a continuous cycle. `chain_runner.py finalize()` logs scores to Notion but no automated process reads those logs and triggers Phase 2 skill updates or routing weight adjustments. Phase 3 (cross-pollination) has no dedicated execution module visible at all.
**Impact**: The "autoresearch loop" described in CLAUDE.md is logging, not learning. The system accumulates evidence about its own performance but takes no automatic action on that evidence.
**Severity**: HIGH

### 2. Quality gate scoring is uncalibrated
**Evidence**: CLAUDE.md Step 6 specifies 4-dim scoring (Intent / Expert Standard / Adversarial / Factual Grounding, 1-10 each, composite ≥7) with veto on Factual <6. But there is no rubric document with worked examples (3/6/9 anchors per dimension), no regression test suite calibrating the rubric across intent categories, and no evidence the prose_classifier.py auto-injection actually catches AI-prose grade inflation. The scoring is whatever the model decides it should be in the moment.
**Impact**: Risks systematic grade inflation. Without a rubric, "8/10 Expert Standard" from one session may correspond to "5/10" by external benchmark. The quality gate becomes ceremonial.
**Severity**: HIGH

### 3. Skill quality variance is unmeasured
**Evidence**: 110 skills in `skills/` directory, ~95 agents in `agents/`, but `knowledge/expert-benchmarks/` has only 16 registered experts across 7 domains. Estimated tier distribution: A-tier 6-12 / B-tier ~80-120 / C-tier ~90-130 — but this is inference, not measurement.
**Impact**: Cannot distinguish "world-class workflow with real expert thinking encoded" from "generic prompt with expert label." Routing to the wrong tier silently degrades output. The chain may be loading C-tier skills with confidence.
**Severity**: HIGH

### 4. Routing enforcement is weak
**Evidence**: 2026-04-21 incident (writers-room executed when /parallax was correct workflow) documented in CLAUDE.md "Mandatory Workflow Routing" section. The fix was to add a bindings table to CLAUDE.md as text — but no runtime validation in `chain_runner.py` or `expert_router.py` (80KB) prevents the same class of error from recurring. Enforcement depends on the model reading CLAUDE.md and obeying.
**Impact**: A single missed binding produces 6/10 output (per the incident). The routing layer is sophisticated but advisory, not enforcing.
**Severity**: MEDIUM-HIGH

### 5. Directive proliferation without hierarchy
**Evidence**: 58 directive files in `directives/` with no precedence rules, no INDEX.md, no staleness detection. `directives/_archived/` exists but is unmanaged — unclear which directives are load-bearing vs. dormant. CLAUDE.md references ~14 of them as load-bearing; the other 44 sit at the same hierarchical level.
**Impact**: New context requires reading 58 files to understand decision logic. Dead directives create cognitive load and false choices. Karpathy's index.md pattern not applied to directives even though it's applied to knowledge/.
**Severity**: MEDIUM

### 6. Recall grounding lacks observability
**Evidence**: `directives/recall-grounding-protocol.md` mandates silent skip on 6 failure modes (disconnected, <2 cards, low relevance, >5s timeout, --no-ground flag, non-grounding domain). No degradation logging. No evidence anywhere that grounding correlates with higher Expert Standard scores.
**Impact**: A core architectural feature (Tier 1.5) cannot be measured. If Recall becomes unreliable, the system degrades silently to skill-file-only output with zero visibility. The most dangerous pattern in the system: a feature you can't measure that you've architected the chain around.
**Severity**: MEDIUM-HIGH

### 7. Memory siloes
**Evidence**: 5 separate memory layers — `/Users/farricecain/.claude/projects/-Users-farricecain-Google-Antigravity/memory/` (user auto-memory), `.memory/sovereign.db` (3-tier Ebbinghaus episodic/semantic/procedural), `agents/[name]/memory/` (per-agent), `knowledge/` (wiki), Notion Knowledge Vault. No unified retrieval facade. Each is queried (or not) by different code paths.
**Impact**: Information goes in but doesn't reliably come out at the right moment. Textbook 2026 failure mode: "5 memories, none load together."
**Severity**: MEDIUM

### 8. Skill-vs-agent duplication
**Evidence**: ~110 skills + ~95 agents with parallel naming (e.g., `lara-acosta` exists as both skill and agent). Stated intent: "skills = workflows, agents = personas." But no cross-reference audit has been done to verify that's what's actually happening, or whether agents are thin duplicates of skills.
**Impact**: Unclear how much real coverage exists vs. what's double-counted. Maintenance burden inflated.
**Severity**: LOW-MEDIUM

### 9. MCP configuration mismatch
**Evidence**: `.mcp.json` registers only Recall as an MCP server. Other tools (Notion, Canva, Gemini, Perplexity, Tavily, Gmail, Drive, etc.) are accessed via execution-layer Python clients, not MCP.
**Impact**: Not broken, but configuration is not the source of truth. New developers can't read `.mcp.json` to understand tool surface. Architectural clarity issue, not a functional gap.
**Severity**: LOW

## Resist These Temptations

### 1. Don't add more skills
The marginal skill at 110 is almost certainly C-tier and dilutes the average. Until tier-grading (Fix 3) gives you an A-tier floor, every new skill makes the variance problem worse, not better. **The growth curve has flipped from accretive to dilutive.** New domain expertise should enter as additions to existing A-tier skills (genius.md enrichment), not as new SKILL.md files.

### 2. Don't add more directives
58 flat files with no precedence is already a navigation problem. Adding the 59th directive to "fix" routing — as was done after the writers-room incident — is a patch over a structural issue. **Fix 2 (runtime enforcement) is the right answer; another markdown file is not.** Every new directive that solves a routing/quality problem is evidence that the runtime layer is too thin.

### 3. Don't pursue cross-pollination (Phase 3) or autonomous self-modification before Fix 1
Cross-pollination between skills sounds impressive but is the highest-risk evolution mode in the 2026 literature — judge-overfitting and behavior drift compound fastest there. Without a calibrated rubric and ground-truth set, cross-pollination will move the system in unpredictable directions you cannot detect. **Phase 3 should remain dormant until Fixes 1, 3, 4 are complete.**

## Audit Methodology Note

This audit was produced by:
1. Three parallel Explore sub-agents (architecture / workforce / substrate) that read the actual files and produced independent reports
2. One Plan agent that benchmarked findings against external best-practice
3. Synthesis at the orchestration layer

The findings are triangulated — every weakness in the report appeared in at least 2 of the 3 audit reports independently. Strengths are likewise triangulated. Where audit reports disagreed, the more conservative (more critical) finding was kept.

**Confidence level**: HIGH on architectural findings (file paths verified, incidents documented, scripts exist or don't). MEDIUM on quantitative claims (110 skills, 16 benchmarks, 240 wiki files — these are claimed by the system but not independently re-counted in this audit).

**What this audit does NOT cover**:
- Performance/latency profiling
- Cost-per-deliverable analysis
- Client-deliverable quality blind comparison (would require Fix 1 to do properly)
- Security/credential hygiene (separate concern)
- Specific skill-by-skill quality rating (covered by Fix 3 if executed)
