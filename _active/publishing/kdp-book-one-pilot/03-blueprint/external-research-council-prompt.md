# Independent KDP Opportunity Research Mission

Copy everything below into Gemini Deep Research, Genspark, Qwen/QMK, or another model with live web research. Run it independently in each model. Do not give one model another model's report.

---

You are the lead analyst for an independent, evidence-led Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP) opportunity study. Your job is to identify the strongest first-book opportunity for Farrice Cain, challenge the current working concept, and produce a decision packet that another analyst can verify.

This is research and product strategy only. Do not publish, buy ads, contact anyone, fabricate credentials, or begin writing the full manuscript.

## 1. Capability disclosure

Before researching, state:

- whether you have live web search and page-opening access;
- whether you can inspect Amazon product pages, samples/Look Inside pages, bestseller rank, formats, prices, page counts, reviews, author pages, and public off-Amazon footprints;
- whether you can access historical rank or keyword tools;
- which sources are blocked, paywalled, dynamic, or unavailable;
- the current date and the freshness window you will use.

Do not claim access to the CoAuthor.ai Chrome extension unless you can actually see its overlay. If you cannot refresh CoAuthor data, use the supplied snapshot as dated, user-provided evidence and label its sales and royalty figures `MODELED`, not actual income. If you cannot browse, stop after identifying the capability gap; do not simulate current research from memory.

## 2. Decision to support

Determine:

1. Which narrowly defined nonfiction book opportunity gives Farrice the best reachable chance of selling a premium, useful product on Amazon while building durable authority or reusable content?
2. Should the incumbent concept—military-style calisthenics for men over 50—be kept, reframed, or killed?
3. What is the smallest paid-demand or behavior test that should happen before the full book is produced?

Do not optimize for a clever topic. Optimize for reachable paid demand, defensible usefulness, ethical authority, feasible production, and a distribution path Farrice can actually sustain.

## 3. Operator context

Farrice is strongest where health, human performance, evidence, content, and systems meet. He values commercial reality, inspectable proof, durable intellectual property, claim-safe work, visual taste, and one clear next move. He can orchestrate research, writing, product design, and a content engine, but he will not pretend to hold military, medical, or coaching credentials he does not hold.

The book may serve two jobs: direct KDP revenue and an authority/content asset. Do not let a low-probability book distract from higher-probability client revenue without evidence.

The largest known production constraint is safe, consistent instructional imagery. Exercise books may require original human photography, multiple movement frames, designed overlays, video demonstrations, model releases, IP assignments, and qualified program review. Treat that burden as a real cost and schedule constraint. Also deliberately search for strong opportunities with a lower visual and credential burden.

## 4. Incumbent concept and prior evidence

**Concept:** a large-format, paperback-led field manual for civilian men over 50 using military-style calisthenics, movement-specific starting levels, short at-home sessions, and original human instructional imagery.

**Current working title:** *The Civilian Strength Manual After 50*.

**Current working subtitle:** *A 6-Week Military-Style Calisthenics Plan for Men Over 50 to Rebuild Strength, Balance, Mobility, and Work Capacity at Home*.

**Title status:** `UNTESTED`. It may be ownable but less searchable than a literal keyword title. Test it rather than defending it.

**Current art direction:** Cover A's authentic, age-matched human photography combined with Cover B's modern field-manual information system. Preserve this as a promising direction, not final art.

## 5. Research doctrine

Use these rules throughout:

- Search snippets are discovery, not verified evidence. Open the strongest pages.
- Prefer current primary or official sources for KDP rules, policies, pricing formulas, platform restrictions, and health guidance.
- For market claims, triangulate independent source types: Amazon/retailer data, trend/search behavior, public reader language, creator/community activity, and visible distribution.
- Every meaningful number must have a URL, source title, source class, observation date, and retrieval date.
- Separate `VERIFIED`, `TRIANGULATED`, `DIRECTIONAL`, `INFERENCE`, `UNVERIFIED`, and `CONTRADICTED`.
- Use `NO EVENT` for revenue, conversion, or buyer behavior that has not happened.
- Run counterevidence searches for every finalist.
- Agreement among low-quality summaries is not triangulation.
- A fast-growing topic is not automatically a reachable KDP market.
- “Easy to generate with AI” is a competition risk, not a durable advantage.
- Do not accuse authors of using AI, pen names, fake reviews, or manipulation without direct evidence. Similarities may be labeled only as observations or inference.
- Do not copy protected interiors, workouts, titles, or distinctive expression. Summarize architecture and reader jobs. Keep any direct excerpt under 25 words from a source.

## 6. Wide opportunity farm

Do not narrow early. Build a universe of 30–40 candidate book opportunities across multiple opportunity families, including but not limited to:

- healthy aging, strength, mobility, recovery, or behavior change;
- practical performance systems for busy adults;
- evidence-led consumer health education with conservative claims;
- visual field manuals, workbooks, checklists, trackers, decision guides, and reference products;
- lower-illustration products that can still justify a premium price;
- content-to-book opportunities where public content can create demand;
- authority books that support Farrice's health/performance positioning;
- adjacent problems discovered from search, reviews, communities, YouTube, TikTok, Reddit, forums, Google Trends, retailer pages, or public keyword tools.

At least 15 candidates must sit outside “fitness for men over 50.” At least 8 must have materially lower imagery requirements than an exercise-instruction manual. Include at least one viable route in each of these classes:

1. KDP cash-flow product;
2. authority-building book;
3. content-to-book flywheel;
4. visually rich premium manual;
5. lower-production-burden guide or workbook.

Do not seed the list only with your own prior knowledge. Show the queries and signals that caused each candidate to enter the universe.

## 7. Funnel and hard gates

First eliminate candidates that fail a non-negotiable gate:

- no observable paid-demand signal;
- demand appears to depend on one anomalous title or one famous author;
- false credentials or unsafe claims would be needed to compete;
- required expertise or rights cannot realistically be obtained;
- production burden destroys likely economics;
- title/category is dominated by free content with no clear paid utility;
- opportunity is an obvious AI-commodity cluster with no defensible mechanism;
- realistic distribution cannot reach the buyer;
- it would consume the main revenue lane without a proportionate proof path.

Then score the survivors out of 100 using this decision model:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Reachable paid demand | 20 |
| Competition reachability, not merely low competition | 15 |
| Buyer pain, urgency, and job clarity | 10 |
| Ownable mechanism or product differentiation | 12 |
| Production feasibility | 10 |
| Proof, safety, and claim defensibility | 8 |
| Rights-cleared visual feasibility; higher score means easier/safer | 8 |
| Organic content and distribution potential | 8 |
| Farrice's authentic authority and interest fit | 5 |
| Format and unit economics | 4 |

Scores are analyst aids, not proof. For every score above 7/10, cite the evidence that earns it. Penalize missing evidence instead of filling it with optimism.

Shortlist 8–12 candidates, then select five for deep research. The incumbent concept must compete in this field even if it would not naturally rank first.

## 8. Top-five dossier requirements

For each finalist, provide:

- narrow reader, painful job, promise, and buying moment;
- exact Amazon and web queries used;
- 10–20 relevant competing titles when available, with ASIN, author/publisher, date, format, page count, price, paid BSR/rank type, ratings/reviews, and visible recency;
- whether demand is broad, concentrated, seasonal, fad-driven, or stable;
- review and community language: desired outcomes, objections, confusing points, missing features, and trust concerns;
- product architecture from lawful samples, contents pages, descriptions, and reviews;
- price ladder and conservative unit-economics scenarios using current official KDP formulas;
- incumbent strength, imitation risk, and credible wedge;
- author and publisher footprints on Amazon, Google, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Reddit, newsletters, websites, podcasts, and Goodreads where discoverable;
- likely acquisition paths, clearly separated into observed, inferred, and unknown;
- imagery, illustration, video, reviewer, legal, safety, and IP requirements;
- a smallest viable prototype and a pre-manuscript demand test;
- the three facts most likely to reverse the recommendation.

Use at least three independent source classes per finalist and at least one counterevidence source or query.

## 9. Title and positioning challenge

For the incumbent concept and the two strongest alternatives:

1. Generate 12 title/subtitle pairs across three strategies: search-max, ownable-brand, and human-outcome.
2. Check exact-title and confusingly similar collisions on Amazon, Google, Goodreads, major retailers, and the USPTO database where accessible. This is preliminary screening, not legal clearance.
3. Inspect whether the core phrase appears in actual customer searches, competitor subtitles, reviews, video titles, Reddit language, and trend data.
4. Evaluate thumbnail readability, specificity, credibility, emotional pull, expansion potential, and false-authority risk.
5. Do not declare a title a winner from aesthetics alone. Recommend a title test: Amazon-style thumbnail preference, keyword relevance, landing-page behavior, or a small audience test.

Treat *The Civilian Strength Manual After 50* as the incumbent. Explain whether it should remain the title, become a series/imprint concept, move to a subtitle, or be replaced.

## 10. Imagery and instructional-quality fork

For any exercise or movement-based finalist, compare at least four executable production systems:

1. original age-matched human photography plus designed overlays;
2. original photography converted into consistent line art or silhouettes;
3. licensed professional illustration or 3D anatomy/motion assets;
4. a redesigned product that reduces the amount of movement instruction required.

For each, estimate required asset count, shoot/production days, consistency controls, rights needed, safety review, accessibility, likely cost range, schedule, failure modes, and suitability for print/Kindle/video reuse. Do not recommend AI-generated anatomy as final exercise instruction unless a qualified human reviewer verifies every frame and the legal/rights position is clear; even then, compare it against original photography.

If the visual burden makes the incumbent unattractive, propose a genuinely different book architecture rather than merely lowering quality.

## 11. Distribution research

Determine whether category leaders are only listing on Amazon or appear to use a broader engine. Look for evidence of:

- KDP Select/Kindle Unlimited;
- Amazon Ads or sponsored placement, without guessing the buyer;
- Author Central and A+ Content;
- websites, email capture, lead magnets, QR funnels, video libraries, and social content;
- podcasts, newsletters, affiliates, communities, media mentions, bundles, or courses;
- launch spikes versus durable rank where history is accessible.

Separate what is `OBSERVED`, `LIKELY`, and `UNKNOWN`. Build a 90-day organic distribution hypothesis for each finalist and identify the attribution needed to know whether it works. Do not treat views, likes, rank, or free downloads as verified book revenue.

## 12. War game and falsification

Attack the top three recommendations from these perspectives:

- skeptical buyer;
- category incumbent;
- Amazon/KDP policy reviewer;
- qualified health or exercise reviewer where relevant;
- production manager;
- copyright/IP reviewer;
- cash-flow operator protecting Farrice's main revenue lane;
- distributor trying to acquire buyers without an existing audience.

For each candidate, state:

- why it could look attractive but fail;
- weakest assumption;
- early-warning metric;
- countermove;
- stop condition;
- what new evidence would change the verdict.

Include at least five rejected candidates with a clear evidence-based reason. Negative controls are required; a report in which every niche looks promising has failed.

## 13. Required final deliverable

Produce one source-linked decision packet in this order:

1. **Executive verdict:** best opportunity, runner-up, incumbent verdict (`KEEP`, `REFRAME`, or `KILL`), confidence, and one immediate test.
2. **What changed from the prior thesis:** confirmed, contradicted, or still unknown.
3. **30–40 opportunity universe table.**
4. **8–12 candidate shortlist with gate results and evidence-linked scores.**
5. **Top-five market dossiers.**
6. **Production and imagery feasibility comparison.**
7. **Distribution map and 90-day hypotheses.**
8. **Title/positioning lab for the top three concepts.**
9. **War game, stop conditions, and negative controls.**
10. **Recommended product concept:** one reader, one problem, one promise, mechanism, format, page range, price hypothesis, visual system, reviewer needs, and prototype.
11. **Seven-day validation sprint:** exact searches, prototype, landing-page or content tests, success thresholds, attribution, budget assumptions, and decision rule. Keep it research/prototype only unless separately authorized.
12. **Evidence ledger:** claim ID, claim, label, source URL, source title, source class, published date, retrieval date, excerpt or field observed, and implication.
13. **Contradictions and unverified gaps.**
14. **Machine-readable appendix:** CSV or JSON containing the candidate table, score inputs, source ledger, and confidence labels.

End with exactly:

- `LOCKED:` evidence-supported decisions;
- `PARKED:` decisions that need taste, private facts, or more evidence;
- `NEXT ACTION:` the single smallest reversible step;
- `WHAT I COULD NOT VERIFY:` explicit gaps;
- `COMMERCIAL EVENT:` `NO EVENT` unless actual buyer or revenue evidence was observed.

## 14. Quality gate

Before delivering, audit your own report:

- Did you actually open pages, or rely on snippets?
- Did you search counterevidence?
- Did the incumbent compete fairly rather than receive confirmation bias?
- Did at least 15 candidates sit outside men-over-50 fitness?
- Did at least 8 candidates reduce the visual-production burden?
- Does every decisive claim have a source and retrieval date?
- Are modeled estimates labeled modeled?
- Are rank types distinguished?
- Are present facts separated from inference?
- Are distribution claims based on evidence rather than absence assumptions?
- Are the safest, most feasible tests placed before manuscript production?
- Is there one clear recommendation and one reversible next action?

If any answer is no, repair the report or mark it `DEGRADED`. Do not call it deep research merely because it is long.
