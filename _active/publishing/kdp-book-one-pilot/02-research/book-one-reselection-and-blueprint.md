### The beatable opening

Own the intersection of **military simplicity + civilian honesty + movement-specific progression after 50**. Do not repeat the saturated 28-day, “joint-friendly,” one-track formula. The book should provide:

- a separate starting rung for each movement pattern;
- illustrations showing setup, action, common error, and regression;
- a six-week progression followed by a maintenance path;
- readiness and stop rules that do not pretend to diagnose pain;
- simple sessions with meaningful progression rather than random daily variety;
- an explicit civilian author note and no invented military story;
- named sources and a qualified exercise-program reviewer.

## Proposed Outline

### Introduction: Strong Again Without Pretending You Are 25

Who this is for, what “military-style” means, the civilian-author boundary, medical clearance language, and what six successful weeks can honestly establish.

### 1. The Military Principle Worth Keeping

Keep simplicity, consistency, bodyweight mastery, and work capacity. Discard punishment, nostalgia, combat claims, and younger-body recovery assumptions.

### 2. Find Your Starting Rung

Assess each movement independently, choose the right variation, and learn the green/yellow/red readiness rules. This is the recommended **gold chapter** because it tests the book's distinct mechanism, safety, clarity, and visual requirements.

### 3. The Five Movement Families

Squat and step; push; pull or upper-back alternative; trunk; balance and locomotion. Each family receives setup, common-error, regression, progression, and stop-rule panels.

### 4. The 20-Minute Session Architecture

Warm-up, skill-strength block, work-capacity block, and downshift. Include shorter minimum sessions and rules for adding time without adding junk volume.

### 5. Weeks 1–2: Restore the Patterns

Build consistency, technique, range tolerance, and repeatable effort. No transformation promises.

### 6. Weeks 3–4: Build Strength and Capacity

Add reps, mechanical difficulty, density, or complexity one variable at a time. The reader progresses each movement separately.

### 7. Weeks 5–6: Integrate and Demonstrate

Combine patterns into simple circuits, repeat the baseline, and compare capability without promising a specific physique or health outcome.

### 8. Recovery, Pain Boundaries, and Missed Weeks

Fatigue traffic lights, soreness versus warning signs, missed sessions, travel, plateaus, and when to stop and seek qualified help.

### 9. Your Next Six Weeks

How to repeat, progress, or transition to a strength, walking, rucking, or hybrid goal without abandoning the core practice.

### Reader Toolkit

- baseline and week-six scorecard;
- weekly calendar template;
- exercise substitution matrix;
- movement-specific progression map;
- fatigue traffic-light card;
- six-week training log.

## Evidence and Originality Standard

Every factual or technical claim should enter a source ledger. Record the claim and source, then its evidence strength, approved language, intended chapter, and reviewer state.

The manuscript must distinguish:

- `VERIFIED`: directly supported by a reliable source or live observation;
- `LIKELY`: supported but incomplete or time-sensitive;
- `SOURCE_REPORTED`: Sean Dollwet's own reported results, timelines, or tactics;
- `UNCONFIRMED`: not strong enough to publish as fact;
- `NO_EVENT`: no sales or reader-behavior event yet.

Competitor books supply market language and unmet needs only. Do not reproduce their titles or distinctive table-of-contents structures. Their examples, stories, prose, art, and proprietary devices are also off-limits.

AI disclosure is mandatory on KDP for AI-generated text, images, or translations, even if substantially edited. AI-assisted work does not require disclosure, but Farrice remains responsible for rights, quality, and accuracy. Human authorship and copyright protection are case-specific, so Farrice's original analysis, examples, editorial decisions, and revisions must be documented.
