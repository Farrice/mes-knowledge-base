# Objection Handles — Content System Audit

> **Source**: 5 from `/recommend` plan + 4 added based on existing dm-script-playbook objection bank
> **Rule**: Always isolate the variable. If they're vague ("not sure"), force them to name what specifically is blocking the yes.

---

## 1. "I don't want to sound like ChatGPT."

> Neither do I — that's the entire point.
>
> The audit builds a constraint-heavy system that forces the AI to use your vocabulary, your sentence patterns, your anti-patterns. We're not trying to make AI sound human. We're building a fence around it so it can only sound like you.
>
> Default ChatGPT writes for everyone. The system we map writes for one person — you.

---

## 2. "I don't have time for a consulting call."

> If you don't have 45 minutes to fix the system, you'll keep bleeding 10 hours a week running a broken one.
>
> 45 min upfront. 10 hours back per week. The math is the entire pitch.

---

## 3. "Can't I just buy a prompt pack for $20?"

> Prompt packs give you generic inputs. Generic inputs produce generic outputs.
>
> What I'm building is a custom operational architecture based on your specific business, your voice samples, your content bottlenecks. A prompt pack is a hammer for sale. The audit is a workshop designed around the project you're actually working on.

---

## 4. "Is this just going to mean paying for another expensive software?"

> No. I prioritize lean stacks.
>
> We'll likely use what you already have — Notion, your existing Claude or ChatGPT subscription. This is about process, not new software. If anything, it usually means you can cancel one or two tools you stopped using.

---

## 5. "Why should I pay for an audit when I just need to write more?"

> Because relying on willpower is a failing strategy. Professionals rely on systems.
>
> The audit diagnoses exactly where your friction is so we can eliminate it permanently. "Just write more" is the advice that got you stuck in the first place.

---

## 6. "I should be able to do this myself."

> Totally fair question.
>
> Quick thought: do you write your own contracts? Build your own website? Edit your own videos?
>
> No — because that's not where your $150/hour brain creates leverage. This is just translating that brain into content production without you spending 10 hours a week on it.

---

## 7. "I've tried AI tools before and they all sound terrible."

> What did you try?
>
> Let me guess — you typed your idea into ChatGPT and asked for a LinkedIn post. The output was generic motivational filler.
>
> That's not an AI problem. That's a prompt problem. Default prompts get default outputs. The system we build gives the AI 5-10 layers of constraint before it can write a single sentence. Different inputs, different outputs.

---

## 8. "I need to think about it."

> Absolutely. What specifically are you weighing?
>
> Is it the money, the process, or whether content is even the right channel for you right now?
>
> Let's isolate the variable so you're actually deciding instead of delaying.

---

## 9. "Why is it $99 / $249 — what's the catch?"

> No catch. The price is intentionally low because I'm building proof.
>
> First 3 clients are at $99 — full beta pricing in exchange for a testimonial and the chance to use your system as a case study. Standard goes to $249 from client #4. By the end of the year it'll be $500+ once I have 10 testimonials.
>
> You're not getting a worse version. You're getting the same thing earlier.

---

## 10. "Can you guarantee results?"

> I can guarantee one thing: if after the call you don't think the workflow we mapped will save you the time I promised, full refund. Same day.
>
> What I can't guarantee is that you'll execute the system. That's on you. But the system itself works — I run my own newsletter and LinkedIn off the same architecture.
