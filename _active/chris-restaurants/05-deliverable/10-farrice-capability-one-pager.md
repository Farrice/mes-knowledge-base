## What I do

**Diagnostic engagements**. The work in this folder. A 5–14 day deep read on a concept (or three concepts), produced as documents your operators can act on without my involvement. Pricing intelligence, competitive set analysis, operational bottleneck mapping, brand-asset activation briefs, AI opportunity scoping. The output is the work, not a slide deck about the work.

**90-day implementation sprints**. Taking three to six recommendations from a diagnostic and shipping them inside the existing operating budget of one or more concepts. Tools deployed, SOPs written, staff trained, a measurable result at the end. The Phase 1 quick wins in the 90-day roadmap (`08-90-day-ai-transformation-roadmap.md`) are the kind of work that ships in a sprint like this.

**Working AI prototypes**. Built on demand for a specific operational moment. The manager voice log → daily digest tool described in the roadmap is a working example I can demo live in five minutes on a phone. I build what the corporate roadmap can't justify building.

**Cross-portfolio infrastructure**. The harder work. Cross-location BI dashboards, inventory variance integration across non-uniform POS systems, the kind of multi-concept reporting layer that lets a single owner read four locations on Sunday night the way an enterprise reads four regions. The concrete example sits inside this folder: Wasabi at CityWalk runs Digital Dining, a legacy POS that no cloud AI vendor builds a plug-and-play connector for. Building the ODBC or CSV bridge that brings Wasabi into the same analytical layer as the cloud-POS concepts is exactly the kind of single-location engineering project a corporate BI roadmap built for 1,650 Panda Express stores cannot justify allocating analyst hours to. Phase 2 and Phase 3 work in the 90-day roadmap; W1 names the bridge as a specific deliverable.

**Honest limit-setting**. The credibility move. Restaurants get pitched on AI weekly. Most pitches conflate things that work (phone reservation AI, review response, inventory variance, scheduling forecast) with things that don't (drive-thru voice AI, kitchen automation at independent scale, AI menu development). The honest limits section in the 90-day roadmap is the kind of work I lead with. Naming what AI can't do is how I earn the right to recommend what it can.

You don't get a generic playbook. Every recommendation in this folder is calibrated to a specific concept, a specific competitive set, a specific operational reality. The Yakiya beverage program math is a Yakiya-Pasadena number; the Panda Inn founding myth is a Panda Inn Glendale activation; the Wasabi throughput observation is specific to a captive theme-park market. **Nothing in here is reusable copy from another client.**

You don't get someone trying to replace your team. The recommendations in this folder are sized for execution by Chris, by the GMs, by the corporate teams that already exist. My job is to build the thing that gets handed back; it isn't to take over the operation. **Anything in an engagement that proves valuable becomes a deliverable your corporate data team can absorb and scale across other concepts at zero marginal friction.**

You don't get vendor sales. I don't take commission from any of the tools mentioned in the 90-day roadmap. Marqii, MarketMan, Lineup.ai, Tenzo, 7shifts, Toast, Bikky, Slang.ai. Every vendor in the roadmap is recommended on the basis of fit and documented case-study evidence, not on a referral arrangement. If a tool isn't right for your specific POS, your specific concept, or your specific operational priorities, it doesn't go in the recommendation. The 90-day roadmap is built that way on purpose.

## How I work

I work in documents. The artifacts in this folder are the way I think. Long-form, sourced, structured for the reader to act on without my presence in the room. Every dollar figure in the deliverable is anchored in a citation I can defend. Every recommendation has a dependency, a cost range, an effort estimate, and a known failure mode named explicitly.

I work fast. This folder was built in 2–3 days from a starting point of zero domain knowledge in the restaurant industry. I do not know the restaurant industry the way Peggy Cherng knows the restaurant industry. That comparison is not on the table for any consultant who has not sat inside a restaurant for 30 years. What I bring is a research-and-execution velocity that can produce defensible work product on a new domain inside a week, and the discipline to show the work so the operator can verify the math at every step.

I work in the open. Drafts, sources, raw data, the working files. Everything I produce can be reviewed at any phase. There is no black-box engagement where the output appears at the end and the client has to trust the process. The 03-research files in the source folder for this deliverable are the working notes I built the artifacts from. They are available to any operator who wants to verify a number, challenge a finding, or pull a competitor data point I didn't surface.

I work small. Most engagements I build are scoped to fit inside a single decision an operator would make in a single meeting. A 30-day pilot at one location, a 90-day sprint across two concepts, a focused diagnostic on a specific operational problem. The first thing I deliver in an engagement is something installed, not something presented. **This entire folder is the proof of work.** The next phase is a 30-minute working session to choose one decision worth testing.
