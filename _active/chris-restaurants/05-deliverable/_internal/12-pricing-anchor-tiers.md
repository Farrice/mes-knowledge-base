# 12 — Pricing Anchor Tiers

**Audience:** Farrice (internal reference) + reusable language blocks for the capability one-pager and the follow-up conversation.
**Purpose:** Three-tier pricing structure that serves the hybrid mandate. The same folder supports a Chris-level close at Tier 1 AND a Gigi-Cheung-level close at Tier 2 or 3. Reader self-selects depth.
**Calibration:** Anchored against a $5B private enterprise buyer, not an indie restaurant owner. Ceiling is set by PRG scale, not freelancer math.

---

## The Pricing Philosophy (read first)

Three principles govern every number below.

**1. Price against found money, not hours.**
A consulting hour doesn't mean anything to Gigi Cheung. $500K of unmonetized beverage revenue at Yakiya does. Every tier is priced as a fraction of the margin it unlocks — not as a rate card. Sticker shock dies when the reader does the math and realizes Tier 2 costs less than the first month of Yakiya beverage capture.

**2. The first dollar is friction, not revenue.**
Tier 1 exists to remove the friction of saying yes. The goal of Tier 1 is not the fee — it's the permission slip to do Tier 2. Price it low enough that the decision feels like a calendar problem, not a budget problem.

**3. Reader-as-protagonist in every price frame.**
Never describe what Farrice is doing ("strategic assessment," "AI transformation roadmap"). Describe what the buyer gets at the end of month 1, month 3, month 12 — in specific, dollar-attributed outcomes. The price is the bridge between the reader's current state and the outcome. Nothing else.

## Tier 1 — Audit Completion

**Price:** $4,500 flat project fee.
*(Range considered: $2,500–$7,500. Landed at $4,500 because below $4K the reader assumes this is a favor and the friend-tax problem activates; above $5K triggers a formal budget cycle Chris can't approve without escalation. $4,500 sits in the "corporate card without a meeting" zone at Chris's level.)*

**Duration:** 10–14 days from kickoff to final delivery.

**What Farrice delivers:**
- The full Google Drive folder Chris already has — formalized as a contracted deliverable with signed scope.
- Two additions not in the gift folder: (1) a **POS audit** on each of the three locations to identify the data hygiene issues that would block any downstream AI work, and (2) **custom competitive pricing dashboards** for each of the three restaurants refreshed with a second-pass dataset.
- **One 90-minute working session** with Chris and whichever internal stakeholder he selects to walk through findings.
- The live **manager voice log demo** installed on Chris's phone for 14 days as a working prototype.
- A sharp 2-page "what's next" brief that lays out Tier 2 without pressure.

**Buyer persona this targets:** Chris himself, or a PRG middle manager who can approve $5K on a corporate card without escalating to Gigi or CFO review. This is the "Chris uses it himself" tier.

**Narrative rationale:**
This tier exists so Chris can do what he originally asked Farrice to do — formalized and expanded — without having to sell anything upward. Chris gets the analysis, the POS audit, and the voice log demo as a working asset in his pocket. If he never walks it up to Gigi, Farrice still gets paid, and Chris still looks like the operator who brought a solution to a problem nobody asked him to solve. Everyone wins.

The POS audit is the quiet enterprise hook. It costs almost nothing to run, and the findings are what unlock Tier 2 when Chris decides to escalate.

**What Farrice says when presenting this tier:**

> "Chris, you asked for menu analysis. I built that, plus a few things I think you'll want once you see them. The formal version includes the POS audit — I need to check what data quality looks like on each of your three locations before I'd recommend any tool for any of them, because bad POS data makes AI look stupid fast. Fee is $4,500 flat. No retainer, no subscription, no commitment past delivery. If this is useful, we talk about what's next. If it isn't, I still want to know — tell me what missed."

**Sticker shock absorbers:**
- "Less than one weekend's labor overage at a single location."
- "Same as running one Meta ad burst for Wasabi during spring break."
- "Cheaper than the first month of a Slang.ai subscription for one restaurant — and this covers all three."

**ROI math (honest):**
Tier 1 is not priced on ROI — it's priced on friction removal. The ROI justification is downstream in Tier 2. If Chris pushes on ROI, the answer is: *"The audit itself won't pay for itself. What the audit finds will pay for itself ten times over — and the audit is the only way to know what we're working with. If that math doesn't feel worth $4,500 to you, we don't do this tier. We do a conversation instead."*

## Tier 2 — Legacy Concept Engagement

**Price:** $24,000 flat for 90 days. Option to structure as $8,000/month × 3 months for buyers who prefer monthly billing cadence.
*(Range considered: $15K–$30K. Landed at $24K because it's the highest number that still clears "VP-level signature authority at a $5B private company" — typically $25K per purchase order without CFO escalation. $24K anchors under that ceiling with a buffer. Monthly $8K sits comfortably under the $10K/mo procurement threshold most enterprises trigger a second sig on.)*

**Duration:** 90 days, with four defined deliverable checkpoints at days 14, 30, 60, 90.

**What Farrice delivers:**

**Weeks 1–2 (Foundation + Quick Win #1):**
- Manager voice log → daily digest deployed to Chris across all three concepts. Live, in production.
- Review response automation (Marqii or Owner.com, whichever fits the legacy brands' voice) stood up on Panda Inn Glendale with auto-drafting approved by Chris before send.
- First weekly "Legacy Concept Intelligence Brief" — 2 pages, three concepts, one read.

**Weeks 3–4 (The Yakiya Beverage Lever):**
- A specific, testable beverage program intervention at Yakiya designed to move attach rate from 6% to an initial 10% target inside 30 days of the intervention.
- Scoped menu layout change, server script, and pricing adjustment — ready to pilot. Measurement framework defined before the test runs.
- Phase 1 of the POS data hygiene work, scoped from the Tier 1 audit.

**Weeks 5–8 (The Panda Inn Founding Myth Activation):**
- A customer-facing storytelling asset that surfaces the "birthplace of Panda Express" fact through Panda Inn Glendale's website, menu inserts, and Google Business Profile. Zero new menu cost. This is Dai Media founder-posture work applied to the heritage concept.
- Draft review response voice calibrated specifically to each concept (Panda Inn = legacy warmth, Yakiya = premium restraint, Wasabi = theme-park hospitality).
- Second "Legacy Concept Intelligence Brief" with month-1 results on voice log adoption and review response time reduction.

**Weeks 9–12 (The Wasabi Throughput Assessment + Digital Dining Bridge):**
- Ticket time (OTD) diagnostic at Wasabi CityWalk to identify the two or three throughput bottlenecks that cost captured revenue during Universal's peak hours.
- **Digital Dining → analytics bridge scoped and built (W1 in the 90-day roadmap).** Wasabi runs Digital Dining (Menusoft Systems / Heartland Payment Systems), a legacy POS that no cloud AI vendor connects to natively. Tier 2 includes the procurement conversation with Heartland (week 9), the build of either an ODBC direct-database connection or a CSV export pipeline (weeks 10–11), and validation against the back-office terminal's own reports (week 12). Wasabi exits Tier 2 with the same daily P&L visibility Yakiya and Panda Inn had on day one. **This is the named engineering deliverable that justifies the Tier 2 fee against found-money math alone — without it, Wasabi stays operationally orphaned for another fiscal year.**
- Third "Legacy Concept Intelligence Brief" with aggregated results and a scoped Tier 3 proposal.
- **Final readout:** 60-minute session with Chris and whichever PRG stakeholders he's brought into the engagement by week 10. Three slides. Results, not narrative.

**Buyer persona this targets:** Gigi Cheung (VP of Concept) is the sign-off. Chris is the internal champion. The math clears her signature authority without escalating to CFO.

**Narrative rationale (the sentences Farrice uses verbatim):**

> "Tier 2 is 90 days, $24,000, one measurable result per month across the three concepts. Month 1: the Yakiya beverage attach rate moves from 6% to 10% — that's roughly a $200,000 annualized revenue unlock at one location, verified by Yakiya's own POS report. Month 2: Panda Inn's founding-myth story activates across the website, menu inserts, and Google profile — zero new menu cost, measurable lift in Google Business Profile clicks. Month 3: Wasabi's OTD comes down by three minutes at peak, which recovers captured revenue during Universal's busy windows. The engagement is priced against the month-1 beverage lift alone — everything in months 2 and 3 is gravy on the ROI math."

**Sticker shock absorbers:**
- "Priced under one weekend's labor cost at a single Express store."
- "Less than 5% of the annualized Yakiya beverage recovery alone."
- "The same fee a boutique design agency charges for a single-concept visual refresh that doesn't ship any revenue."
- "90 days, four checkpoints, scope fixed at signature. No hours, no scope creep."

**ROI math (concrete):**

| Line item | Conservative estimate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Yakiya beverage attach gap (6% → 10%, month 1 target) | $80,000–$150,000 annualized at current cover count | WS1 Yakiya receipts + PPA math |
| Yakiya beverage attach gap (6% → 18%, month 3 stretch) | $300,000–$700,000 annualized | WS1 Yakiya (verified) |
| Panda Inn founding-myth Google CTR lift (5% → 12%) | $30,000–$80,000 in recaptured dinner covers/year | WS1 Panda Inn |
| Wasabi OTD reduction (3 min at peak recovers lost covers) | $50,000–$120,000/year | WS3 benchmarks + Universal attendance math |
| **Total estimated 12-month margin unlock at completion** | **$460K–$1.05M** | Triangulated across WS1 + WS3 |

| **First-year ROI ratio** | **19:1 to 43:1** | — |

The fee is 2–5% of the lowest-case annualized margin unlock. That math is the defensibility — Gigi doesn't sign a $24K PO because she likes Farrice. She signs it because the smallest believable number on the page is still 19x the fee.

**What Farrice says when presenting this tier:**

> "This is the tier where the work actually ships. 90 days, fixed scope, one measurable result per month. The first month's result — Yakiya beverage attach — is designed to pay for the entire engagement with change left over. If it doesn't, I refund the final month and we part friends. Gigi, I've structured this so you can sign it on your existing signature authority without routing through David's team."

## Tier 3 — Specialty Portfolio Partnership

**Price:** $22,500/month, 12-month commitment. Quarterly review cadence built in.
*(Range considered: $15K–$50K/mo. Landed at $22,500/mo because it clears the "serious strategic retainer" threshold without triggering the $25K/mo procurement category that typically forces CFO co-sign at a $5B enterprise. Annualizes to $270K — a number that reads as "strategic partnership line item," not "consulting expense." Upgrade path to $35K or $50K/mo exists if scope expands to include Ippudo, Just Salad, Pieology, Uncle Tetsu.)*

**Duration:** 12 months, with a 90-day out clause after month 3 if either side wants to walk.

**What Farrice delivers:**

**Embedded capacity across the full legacy portfolio:**
- Panda Inn (4 units), Yakiya (2 units), Wasabi (1 unit), Hibachi-San — all four concepts under one weekly intelligence cadence.
- **Month 1 of the retainer ships the Digital Dining bridge live across Wasabi.** The W1 bridge from the 90-day roadmap (and from Tier 2 if Tier 2 ran first) becomes a production data feed inside the first 30 days of the partnership. This is the proof-of-model month: the bridge stays live, the cross-location dashboard becomes real, and the side-quest specialist thesis becomes a working production system instead of a slide. Every concept added to the partnership after that gets the same treatment — find the legacy POS or workflow gap, name the engineering work, ship it inside the first month of coverage.
- Weekly 2-page "Legacy Portfolio Intelligence Brief" delivered to Chris, Gigi, and whichever additional stakeholders PRG adds to the distribution list. Pre-compressed, decision-grade.
- Monthly strategic review with Gigi's team (60 minutes, three decisions max per meeting).
- Quarterly readout to Tom Davin or the Cherng family if desired.

**Ongoing work the partnership covers:**
- All review response automation across the portfolio, drafted and sent by Farrice's system, approved by GMs before publication.
- Monthly menu engineering analysis per concept with a single recommendation per concept per month — the "one decision" rule.
- Quarterly competitive set refresh across all concepts.
- Active experimentation: one testable hypothesis per concept per quarter, designed, instrumented, measured, reported.
- The manager voice log → daily digest system running across all concepts as production infrastructure.
- Ad-hoc strategic requests from Gigi's team (up to 8 hours/month).

**Expansion triggers:**
The partnership explicitly reserves the option to expand coverage to Ippudo, Just Salad, Pieology, or Uncle Tetsu at an additional $5,000/month per concept added, with a minimum 6-month commitment on the additional concept. This is how the Tier 3 floor becomes a Tier 4 ceiling ($50K/mo at full portfolio coverage).

**Buyer persona this targets:** Requires David Landsberg (CFO) co-sign or Cherng family awareness. Gigi Cheung is the internal champion who walks it up. Tom Davin is the ratifier.

**Narrative rationale (the specific sentences):**

> "Tier 3 is what we do once the Tier 2 engagement has shipped three wins and we both agree it's worth making permanent. It's a $270K annual strategic partnership across the legacy portfolio — four concepts at launch, expandable to eight as trust compounds. Think of it as a fractional internal function. You get the equivalent of a dedicated operations analyst, a data engineering capacity, and a strategic advisor — all focused exclusively on the concepts your Panda Express-calibrated corporate queue can't prioritize — for less than the fully-loaded cost of one senior hire. And unlike a hire, the scope flexes with the quarter. Slow month, I'm writing the intelligence brief. Big month, I'm running three experiments in parallel."

**Sticker shock absorbers:**
- "Less than the fully-loaded cost of a $150K corporate analyst (salary + benefits + overhead + seat), and the analyst would never be able to prioritize these four concepts over Express."
- "The Yakiya beverage recovery alone covers the first 18 months of the retainer."
- "Annualized, this is roughly 1/500th of what PRG's announced 2025 $200M automation investment — for the portion of the portfolio that investment won't touch this decade."
- "Fractional at 1/3 the cost of a 'digital transformation consultant' like Kearney or Bain boutique practices, with work that ships instead of slides that don't."

**ROI math (the CFO test):**

| Panda Inn founding-myth CTR lift | $50K | $130K |

Every month there's a new decision across one of the four concepts that deserves the same treatment we gave Yakiya. Making that permanent is what Tier 3 is for. It's the version of this where I become the person on your team who's always thinking about Panda Inn, Wasabi, Yakiya, and Hibachi-San — because nobody else at PRG has the bandwidth to. That's it."

## Price Sequencing — How The Tiers Show Up In The Deliverable

**In the Executive Brief (01):** Only Tier 2 and Tier 3 are mentioned, framed as "two ways to make this real." Tier 1 is NOT mentioned in the Executive Brief — it's reserved for the Chris-level conversation, because naming it upward cheapens the enterprise framing.

**In the Capability One-Pager:** All three tiers mentioned, with Tier 2 as the recommended first step. The page structure: Current State → 90-day Engagement (Tier 2) → Ongoing Partnership (Tier 3). Tier 1 is a single line at the bottom: *"Smaller scope available on request for internal-only deliverables."*

**In the Chris Handoff Script:** Tier 1 is the opening frame. "Farrice said he'd put this together for me — the expanded version is $4,500 and includes a POS audit across all three." Chris leads with the low number so the conversation with leadership starts from "safe scope," not "big bet."

**In the in-room conversation with Gigi:** Tier 2 is the primary anchor. Tier 1 gets offered only if Gigi asks for "a smaller first step." Tier 3 gets named only after Tier 2 has been discussed and Gigi has engaged with the Yakiya beverage finding specifically.

## A Final Warning on Pricing Discipline

Do not negotiate below these floors.

If Chris or Gigi pushes for a number lower than Tier 1, the answer is: *"If the fee is the problem, that tells me the value isn't clear yet. Let me show you the voice log demo on your phone — 10 minutes. If after that you still want to negotiate the fee, I'll walk."*

If Gigi pushes for Tier 2 at a discount, the answer is: *"Tier 2 is priced against the first month's ROI at the lowest-case beverage recovery number. Discounting means assuming the recovery won't hit. If you don't believe it'll hit, we shouldn't do this engagement at all. If you do believe it'll hit, the discount is trivial compared to what's on the table."*

Anchor the math. Let the reader argue with the arithmetic, not with Farrice.
