# 15 — Follow-Up Sequence

**Audience:** Farrice (internal playbook).
**Purpose:** Day-by-day choreography after Chris sends the folder. This document removes decision fatigue from the post-delivery window — Farrice runs the sequence, not his anxiety.
**Core principle:** *"The person most able to walk away is the person most able to sell. Every day of this sequence is built to keep Farrice in that posture."*

---

## Reading the sequence

Each day below has the same structure:

- **What happens** — the expected state on that day, plus contingencies if the room is warm or cold.
- **What Farrice does** — the single action he takes (if any). Most days: nothing.
- **What Farrice does NOT do** — the actions he must resist. More important than "what he does" on most days.
- **The template** — if a message is sent, the exact language.
- **The reasoning** — why this move at this moment, so Farrice doesn't freestyle.

---

## Day 0 — Send Day

**What happens:** Chris receives the folder from Farrice, opens it, skims it, ideally texts back something like "holy shit" or "this is way more than I expected." Chris may or may not look at the manager voice log demo.

**What Farrice does:**
One short text to Chris, sent at the moment of delivery. Nothing more.

**Template (text to Chris):**

> "Folder's in. Take your time with it — the Executive Brief is the only thing I'd say read first. Everything else is in there when you need it. Let me know if anything lands."

**What Farrice does NOT do on Day 0:**
- ❌ Ask Chris for a response.
- ❌ Highlight specific findings in the text ("make sure you see the Yakiya thing!").
- ❌ Suggest Chris forward it to leadership.
- ❌ Ask if Chris wants to schedule a call.
- ❌ Send any follow-up that day.

**Reasoning:**
Day 0 is about letting the deliverable do its work without interference. The text above does three things: (1) confirms delivery, (2) names the single highest-leverage entry point (Executive Brief), (3) invites feedback without demanding it. "Let me know if anything lands" is intentionally passive. It keeps Farrice's posture calm and confident.

The anti-actions matter more than the action. Every impulse Farrice has to "follow up" on Day 0 is anxiety, not strategy. Resist.

---

## Day 1 — The Quiet Day

**What happens:** Chris has either read it overnight (possible but unlikely given how long the folder is), or more realistically he's planning to read it this evening after his shift. He may have started with the Executive Brief and stopped there.

**What Farrice does:**
Nothing. Absolutely nothing. Do not text, do not email, do not check in.

**What Farrice does NOT do:**
- ❌ Send "any thoughts?" messages.
- ❌ Ask if Chris has read it.
- ❌ Post about the project anywhere public.
- ❌ Tell anyone the folder is "out there."

**Reasoning:**
Day 1 is Farrice's test of his own posture. The temptation will be enormous. Resist. Chris has not had enough time to read the folder yet. Any Day 1 touch reads as anxiety, which cheapens the entire frame. The person who built something worth $24K doesn't follow up on day 1 — because the work is clearly worth the time it takes to read.

*(If Chris proactively reaches out on Day 1 with a specific question or reaction, Farrice answers warmly and briefly. But he does not initiate.)*

---

## Day 2 — The First Check-In

**What happens:** Chris has now had 48 hours with the folder. He has probably read the Executive Brief, skimmed at least two other documents, and formed a preliminary opinion. He may or may not have shown anything to anyone at PRG yet.

**What Farrice does:**
One text to Chris. Not a "did you read it yet" text — a conversation opener that implies Farrice assumes Chris has read it and is now thinking about what to do next.

**Template (text to Chris):**

> "Hey — spent some time with the folder yet? Curious what hit first, and what you'd change if you were picking one thing to show somebody."

**What Farrice does NOT do:**
- ❌ Ask if Chris has forwarded it.
- ❌ Mention specific findings (forces Chris to remember which pages they're on, which activates the "I haven't finished reading" guilt response).
- ❌ Pitch the tiers.
- ❌ Ask if Chris wants to set up a call with leadership.

**Reasoning:**
"What hit first" is a better question than "what do you think." It invites Chris to tell a story instead of passing judgment, which is always easier and more revealing. "If you were picking one thing to show somebody" plants the forward-motion seed without asking Chris to commit to forwarding it. This is the first day it's appropriate to gently surface the escalation question.

*(Chris's answer to "what hit first" is strategic intel. If he names the Yakiya beverage finding, the deliverable has landed. If he names the founding-myth story, he's engaged with the emotional layer. If he names the tools section, he's in pragmatic mode. Farrice adjusts his next move based on which layer hit.)*

---

## Day 3–4 — The Reaction Read

**What happens:** Chris has replied (or not). Three outcomes to prepare for:

**Outcome A — Chris is excited and has already forwarded it.**
Response includes phrases like "I sent it to my regional manager this morning" or "Gigi said she's going to look at it this week." This is the green light scenario.

**Outcome B — Chris is interested but hasn't forwarded yet.**
He's reading, thinking, not sure what to do. He might say "this is great, I need to figure out who to show it to."

**Outcome C — Chris is quiet or noncommittal.**
Short, uncertain replies. "Yeah it's cool, I'll take a look when I have time." This is the frost scenario.

**What Farrice does:**

**For Outcome A (green light):**
One text, short, and then radio silence until Chris brings news:

> "Appreciate you moving on it. Let me know if anybody has questions — happy to jump on a call anytime this week. Otherwise I'll sit tight and let you run point."

The "sit tight and let you run point" language is critical. It tells Chris he's in the driver's seat, which strengthens Chris's political position inside PRG (he's not a broker, he's an operator with good taste).

**For Outcome B (interested but frozen):**
Farrice offers a gentle frame-break, not a push:

> "If it helps — the audience who'd get the most from the Yakiya beverage finding is whoever owns concept P&L. That's probably Gigi Cheung on your side based on what's public, but you know the room better than I do. No rush either way. The work doesn't expire."

This does three things: (1) Farrice names a specific person (Gigi) without pretending to know more than Chris about the internal org chart, (2) the phrase "you know the room better than I do" preserves Chris's expertise, (3) "the work doesn't expire" releases all time pressure.

**For Outcome C (frost):**
Farrice does not push. He pivots to the long game:

> "All good — take whatever time you need. I had an idea for a small add-on I'm going to build anyway. Will send it over in a few days as a thank-you for the original ask."

This is the setup for Day 7–14's "demonstrate by doing" move. Farrice is telling Chris he's not waiting for a commitment — he's going to keep producing value regardless. This dissolves the pressure and often flips Outcome C into Outcome B within a week.

**What Farrice does NOT do on Day 3–4:**
- ❌ Ask for a meeting with Chris's boss.
- ❌ Ask Chris to introduce him to anyone by name.
- ❌ Send a "summary" of the folder to make it easier for Chris to forward. (If Chris can't forward the original, he won't forward a summary either.)
- ❌ Follow up twice in the same day.

## Day 5–7 — The Warm Window

**What happens:** If Day 3–4 was Outcome A or B, Farrice is now waiting to hear whether leadership has engaged. Chris may or may not have news. The fastest feedback loop is Chris reporting back spontaneously when leadership responds.

**What Farrice does:**
Nothing, unless Chris initiates. The exception: if Chris reports that a meeting has been scheduled with Gigi or any PRG stakeholder, Farrice prepares the meeting brief.

**The meeting prep, if invited:**

If Chris says "Gigi wants to meet" (or any PRG executive), Farrice prepares:

1. **The manager voice log demo**, loaded and ready to run on his phone in under 60 seconds.
2. **Two printed copies of the Executive Brief** — one for the exec, one for Farrice to mark up during conversation.
3. **One printed copy of the Tier 2 pricing page** (not Tier 3 — keep the conversation anchored on the nearer tier).
4. **A 3x5 notecard in his pocket with the opening line:** *"You can do this. Your CIO can do this. Peggy built the original system in 1975. The reason this folder exists isn't that we know more — it's that we're unencumbered by the arithmetic keeping your team on Express."*
5. **One question he plans to ask in the first 10 minutes:** *"What's the decision you'd need to make in the first 30 days of working together that would tell you whether this is worth continuing?"*

**The opening 90 seconds of the meeting:**

Farrice does not pitch. He says:

> "I appreciate you taking the time. Before I say anything about what I do, I want to show you the thing Chris has been using for the last two weeks. It's about 90 seconds on my phone. After that I'm happy to answer whatever questions you have about the folder or about how I'd run the work."

Then he opens the manager voice log demo. Live. No slides. No positioning. The demo is the pitch.

**The positioning comes at minute 5, not minute 1.** Only after the demo has landed does Farrice say the 1975 reframe out loud.

**What Farrice does NOT do during the meeting:**
- ❌ Walk through the folder page by page. (Insulting — Gigi has read it or she hasn't.)
- ❌ Open with capability summary. (The demo replaces the capability summary.)
- ❌ Ask for the sale in the first meeting. (He asks the "one decision" question instead, which does the selling without asking.)
- ❌ Quote a number unprompted. (If Gigi asks about price, he points to the Tier 2 page and says "the fee is on page 2; what I care about in this conversation is whether the math works for you.")

## Day 7–14 — The "Demonstrate By Doing" Move

**What happens:** Two scenarios govern this window:

**Scenario 1: Farrice has had a meeting with PRG leadership in Days 5–7.**
He's in active conversation about next steps. The follow-up sequence becomes specific to that conversation. Skip to Day 14–30 planning.

**Scenario 2: No leadership meeting yet. Chris is still deciding whether/how to escalate, OR leadership has seen the folder but not responded.**
This is where the "demonstrate by doing" move activates. Farrice builds a second small asset without being asked and sends it to Chris as a gift. This is leverage without asking.

**The second asset options (pick one):**

1. **Review Response Starter Pack for Panda Inn Glendale.** Farrice writes 20 review responses for the Panda Inn Glendale's most recent Google reviews, drafted in a warm-legacy-concept voice that preserves the founder-era pride. He sends the Word doc to Chris with a note saying "I noticed Panda Inn had some unreplied reviews — built this for Chris to use or pass along. No strings."

2. **A Sichuan Brand Activation One-Pager for Panda Inn.** Riffs on the Sichuan adjacency opportunity (Lao Sze Chuan permanently closed) and sketches what a single-page menu insert might look like surfacing the Cherng family's Sichuan lineage. Zero new menu cost.

3. **A Yakiya Beverage Test Protocol.** A 1-page write-up of exactly how the Yakiya beverage attach test would run — server script, A/B structure, measurement method, 14-day timeline. Turns the "6% to 20% opportunity" into a specific, runnable document.

**What Farrice does:**
Build the asset (2–4 hours of work), then send it to Chris with the following message:

**Template (text or email to Chris with the asset attached):**

> "Couldn't stop thinking about the [Panda Inn review gap / Sichuan lineage thing / Yakiya beverage test]. Built this over the weekend. No agenda — just wanted to send it to you as a thank-you for the original ask. Use it or pass it along, whatever feels right. If your team finds it useful, I'll build the next one too."

**What Farrice does NOT do with the second asset:**
- ❌ Attach an invoice or a "scope this into Tier 2" note.
- ❌ Ask Chris to forward it to leadership explicitly.
- ❌ Include a call-to-action.
- ❌ Send it the same day as the original folder — the second asset must feel like a different gift, not a follow-up.

**Reasoning:**
The second asset is the single most effective move in the entire follow-up sequence. Four reasons:

- It demonstrates that Farrice is not transactional. He's going to produce value whether or not PRG pays him. That changes the buyer's posture more than any pitch language could.
- It gives Chris a second gift to bring into his PRG conversations. Chris can now say "he's kept building stuff — check out this second thing" which is a natural escalation without Chris having to chase.
- It creates a scarcity dynamic in reverse. Most consultants pressure after delivery. Farrice gives more after delivery. The contrast is disorienting in a way that makes PRG leadership curious about what's behind the generosity.
- It's a trust-bank deposit. If Farrice does land the engagement, the second asset becomes part of the "first 2 weeks of free work" origin story that cements the relationship.

**Critical:** This move only works if the second asset is actually good. If Farrice phones it in, the move reads as desperate. The second asset must be portfolio-quality.

## Day 14–30 — The Conversion Window

**What happens:** Two weeks after delivery, Farrice now has clarity on which path the opportunity is on. Three scenarios govern the conversion window:

**Scenario 1: Warm. Meetings have happened or are on the calendar. Leadership is engaged.**

Farrice moves to conversion. The move is a time-bound anchor:

**Template (email to Chris OR directly to Gigi if the relationship is now direct):**

> "Wanted to share what the next step looks like on my side so we're not talking about a moving target.
>
> I've held time for a 90-day Tier 2 engagement starting [specific date — ideally the first of the next month]. If we're going to run it, the kickoff needs to happen by [date], which means we'd want the green light confirmed by [date minus 5 business days]. After that, the next open window I have is [6–8 weeks later], which delays the Yakiya beverage work into the next quarter.
>
> Whichever way you want to go is fine — I'd rather know which so I can plan the calendar. Let me know what's easiest on your side for the decision call."

**Why this works:**
- "Wanted to share what the next step looks like" frames Farrice as organizing his own calendar, not pressuring theirs.
- Two specific dates creates a real deadline without inventing urgency.
- "Whichever way you want to go is fine" releases all pressure. Farrice is not emotionally attached to the outcome.
- "Decision call" replaces "sales call" — the frame is about making a choice, not being convinced.

**Scenario 2: Ambiguous. Chris has shown it to people but nothing concrete has emerged. Polite interest, no commitment.**

Farrice does not chase. He writes the single gentlest possible closing move:

**Template:**

> "Hey — wanted to close the loop. I know the folder has been making its way around. If there's a conversation worth having in the next couple weeks, I'm around. If the timing isn't right, that's completely fine — nothing changes on my side, and I'll keep thinking about the concepts because I can't help it. Let me know either way."

This is the "polite release" version. It preserves the relationship, it acknowledges reality, and it leaves the door unlocked without pretending it's wide open.

**Scenario 3: Cold. Chris has not forwarded the folder, or it's been ghosted, or the room is clearly not engaged.**

Farrice does NOT send a goodbye message. He does one of two things:

- **If the relationship with Chris is healthy:** he pivots the conversation to something unrelated. He texts Chris about a totally different topic (an article, a restaurant, a shared interest). The deliverable becomes dormant, not dead. Farrice revisits in 30–60 days with a new reason.
- **If the relationship with Chris feels strained:** he sends one last message — *"All good. Let me know if anything changes, otherwise I'll get out of your hair. The folder is yours to use or share whenever."* — and then fully disengages for 60 days.

**What Farrice does NOT do in Scenario 3:**
- ❌ Ask for a reason.
- ❌ Discount the price.
- ❌ Offer to do additional free work.
- ❌ Reach out to anyone at PRG directly around Chris.
- ❌ Send a "last chance" message.

## Day 30+ — The Long Game

**What happens:** The initial window has closed. Farrice either landed the engagement (in which case this document becomes irrelevant and the delivery playbook takes over), or the room went cold (in which case Farrice enters long-game mode).

**Long-game moves (if cold):**

Every 30–45 days, Farrice takes ONE small action to stay warm with Chris without being a nuisance. Rotate through:

- **A short relevant article** — an industry piece about Panda Express, PRG, or the legacy concepts. Sent with a single-line comment. No agenda.
- **A small new asset** — built in 2–3 hours. Fresh angle. Different from the last one. Sent with "no agenda" framing.
- **A personal check-in** — about Chris, not about the folder. The relationship comes first.
- **An industry reframe** — if something newsworthy happens in AI + restaurants (a case study, a failure, a launch), Farrice sends Chris a 3-sentence take on what it means for the legacy concepts.

The compounding logic: Chris stays warm for 6–12 months with 4–8 micro-touches. At some point during that window, something at PRG changes — Gigi gets a new mandate, the CFO asks about specialty concept performance, a Yakiya review goes bad in a visible way, a competing consultant pitches something similar, Panda Inn Glendale has a bad Google sentiment month. When that moment arrives, Farrice is the first person Chris thinks of, because Farrice never stopped being useful.

**The rule that governs everything:**

> *"The folder is yours to use whenever. Nothing expires."*

This sentence appears in Day 0, in Day 14, in every long-game check-in. It is the sentence that keeps the door open without ever being pushed through.

---

## The Posture Rules (governing the entire sequence)

1. **Farrice is the person most able to walk away.** Every message above is written from that posture. The moment Farrice's language starts to sound needy, the room cools.

2. **Anxiety is the enemy.** Most of the Day-by-Day guidance is about what Farrice does NOT do. The anti-actions matter more than the actions.

3. **The folder has a long half-life.** The Yakiya beverage finding that's true today will still be true in 90 days. Urgency is not real. Speed comes from buyer readiness, not seller pressure.

4. **Chris is a friend first, a channel second.** Every move has to preserve the Chris relationship even if the PRG opportunity dies. Never ask Chris to carry weight that costs him political capital. The handoff script is designed so he never has to.

5. **"No" is not a permanent state.** A cold Day 14 can be a warm Day 60 if Farrice's long-game discipline holds. Consultants who close hard on Day 14 lose the Day 60 opportunity.

6. **The best follow-up is a second deliverable.** When in doubt, build something new. Every small asset Farrice ships during the follow-up window deepens the proof base and changes the buyer's frame. The second asset is the most powerful move in the whole sequence.

7. **Never touch PRG directly around Chris.** Chris is the channel. Even if Farrice knows Gigi's email, even if LinkedIn makes it tempting — do not reach out to anyone at PRG without Chris in the loop. The relationship integrity with Chris is worth more than any direct-channel shortcut.

## Summary Timeline

| Day | Action | Who speaks |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Delivery text to Chris | Farrice |
| 1 | Nothing | — |
| 2 | "What hit first" check-in | Farrice |
| 3–4 | Respond to Chris's reaction, branch on outcome | Farrice / Chris |
| 5–7 | Quiet, unless meeting offered | Chris |
| 7 | **If meeting:** full prep move | Farrice |
| 7–14 | **If no meeting:** second asset delivered | Farrice |
| 14–30 | Conversion anchor OR polite release | Farrice |
| 30+ | Long game — micro-touches every 30–45 days | Farrice |
| 60 | Re-open window with new reason if still cold | Farrice |

The discipline of this sequence is its own form of proof of work. A consultant who can execute this cadence without wavering signals something no pitch can signal: *this person has done this before, and he's not desperate.*

That signal alone changes the buyer's posture more than any sentence in any artifact.
