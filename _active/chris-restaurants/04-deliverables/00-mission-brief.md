## WASABI POS CONFIRMED: DIGITAL DINING (by Menusoft Systems)

**Source:** User confirmed via Chris, 2026-04-06.

**What this is:** Digital Dining is a legacy Windows-based POS system dating to the late 1980s, made by Menusoft Systems (now part of Heartland Payment Systems / Global Payments). It is NOT a modern cloud POS. It is a full-service-restaurant workhorse with decades of deployment in independent and multi-unit operators, but it sits in the "legacy" category alongside Aloha, Micros 3700, and Restaurant Manager.

**Integration reality:**
- Limited native APIs
- Primary integration path is ODBC direct-to-database connections (if Heartland/Menusoft license permits)
- Secondary path is file-based CSV/TSV exports
- Modern AI restaurant tools (Owner.com, Marqii, Lineup.ai, MarketMan, Popmenu) have Toast/Square/Clover connectors first and Digital Dining last, if at all
- Third-party middleware (Restaurant365, MarginEdge, Avero) offers some Digital Dining support but adds a layer of complexity and cost
- Any POS-native AI integration at Wasabi should assume 2–3x the timeline and 1.5–2x the cost vs a Toast/Square location

**Strategic implication — why this is a GIFT for the deliverable, not a liability:**

Digital Dining at Wasabi is the **literal embodiment of the side-quest specialist thesis**. PRG corporate IT will NEVER prioritize building a Digital Dining integration for a single location — the ROI math doesn't justify the engineering work when you have 1,650 Panda Express stores sitting on modern cloud infrastructure. This means Wasabi is structurally *orphaned by arithmetic* from any analytical improvements Yakiya and Panda Inn can tap into with a normal cloud POS.

**This is the most forensically-defensible "side-quest" example in the entire deliverable.** The Executive Brief, the Wasabi menu intelligence file, the 90-day roadmap, and the Farrice capability one-pager should all reference Digital Dining explicitly as the exemplar case. ## WS2 — THE JARGON PALETTE (credibility at 60 seconds)

Three terms that must appear in the deliverable used correctly:

1. **"Prime cost"** + the **"60% rule"** — the single most-watched number in restaurant ops. Using it correctly in the first 60 seconds signals native fluency. Prime cost = food cost + labor cost. 60% rule = prime cost should stay under 60% of sales for a healthy operation. High-end restaurants fight to stay at 65% and survive.

2. **"PPA"** (Per Person Average) — upscale operators talk in PPA. A consultant who says "average ticket" sounds retail. A consultant who says "your Yakiya PPA is underperforming the Pasadena $120+ tasting-menu set despite the $128 sticker" sounds like a restaurant person.

3. **"OTD"** (out-the-door / ticket time) — the shift-level obsession. Using it in context on Wasabi at CityWalk — where theme park psychology forces speed on a full-service price point — shows Farrice understands the specific P&L constraint that location fights every night.

**Bonus intimacy signal:** "In the weeds" used correctly once about Chris's Friday night reality. Small. High leverage. Signals Farrice has spent time talking to operators, not reading LinkedIn posts about operators.
