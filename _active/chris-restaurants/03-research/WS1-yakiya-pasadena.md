### Cuisine Positioning
**Tableside-grill yakiniku tasting menu in chef's pre-fixe format.** Pasadena-specific feature: the tabletop grill swaps for a shabu-shabu hot pot. Not a sushi restaurant — closer to "Japanese steakhouse meets omakase," but executed buffet-style by waitstaff (see Critical Operational Issue below).

The cuisine sits in a narrow lane: **higher-end than Korean BBQ chains** (Gyu-Kaku, Chris' Korean BBQ), **more structured than à la carte yakiniku**, **more casual than true omakase counter dining** (Sushi Enya, Sushi Karen). Yakiya is trying to operate in a category gap — "yakiniku omakase" — which barely exists as a recognized concept in U.S. dining.

### Menu Engineering Observations (Visible from Structure Alone)

1. **Beverage attach rate is dangerously low.** Verified party of 4 paid $700 — beverages were $42 (6%). Cocktails capped at $13–$15 in a $128/pp tasting concept. This is below the L.A. fine-dining floor by 30–40%. Yakiya is leaving money on the table because the cocktail pricing reads "casual" and the bottle ladder jumps from $24 sake → $130 Dassai with no $40–$80 anchors.

2. **Single-menu dependency = no upsell ladder.** Once a guest commits to $128, there's no "chef's add-on," no "premium cut supplement," no $40 caviar course, no chef's counter premium. Sushi Enya extracts $200/pp through omakase escalation; Yakiya tops out at $128 + bottle.

3. **The 90-min table cap math is hostile.** 50 covers × 90 min × $128 PPA on a 5-hour service window = ~3 turns = ~150 covers/night maximum. There's no 2nd seating premium, no late-night conversion to a la carte, no chef's bar.

4. **Sunday is the only daypart that opens at 11:30 AM.** Lunch revenue is unmonetized 6 days a week. Hacienda Heights does Sat/Sun lunch; Pasadena does not. That's structural — it's not a market difference, it's an operating choice.

5. **The "switch grill for shabu" is a Pasadena-only feature** but isn't visibly priced at premium. Shabu and yakiniku both run $78. The Pasadena differentiation isn't being captured as price.

## Key Insights

### Insight 1: Yakiya Pasadena is operating in a category gap that Pasadena doesn't recognize.
"Yakiniku omakase" is not a category L.A. diners search for. Pasadena's premium Japanese customer searches "omakase," "sushi," or "wagyu" — not "yakiniku tasting menu." The result: **Yakiya gets discovered as a Japanese steakhouse (Benihana category) by people expecting hibachi, and as a substandard omakase by people expecting Sushi Enya.** Both populations leave disappointed for opposite reasons. The 88 Google reviews after 10 years vs. KAVIAR's 386 in the same market is the data point. Recommendation: Yakiya's category needs to be SOLVED before any menu engineering matters.

### Insight 2: The cooking execution gap is the existential issue, not the menu.
Multiple reviewers (Parkzer in detail, others in passing) report that the wagyu is cooked by waitstaff who are visibly inexperienced — and the result is overcooked tongue, undercooked ribeye, and no salt provided. **At $128/pp for a wagyu-centric concept, this is the equivalent of an omakase bar serving day-old fish.** The food cost on A5 Miyazaki is north of $40/lb. Burning it on a tabletop grill in front of a paying customer is the worst possible failure mode. This is not a menu engineering problem — this is a labor/training/SOP problem that AI can actually help solve (POS-driven cooking timers, video-based training modules, photographic doneness checklists).

### Insight 3: Yakiya is leaving 25–35% of beverage revenue on the table.
Cocktails priced at $13–$15 in a $128/pp tasting concept is below market floor. KAVIAR, Sushi Roku, Sushi Karen all run cocktail prices in the $16–$22 range. Yakiya has the bottle ladder ($960 Dassai exists) but no mid-tier $40–$80 sake/wine bridge. The verified party of 4 had a 6% beverage attach. Industry benchmark for upscale Asian tasting menus is 20–30%. **Closing this gap alone would add $20–$40 PPA at zero food cost — that's $300K–$700K/year on a 50-cover restaurant doing 4 nights/week of solid bookings.**

### Insight 4: The cross-pollination opportunity across PRG's portfolio is the actual prize.
This dossier was supposed to be about Yakiya. The bigger story is that **PRG runs three structurally distinct concepts** (theme-park captive at Wasabi, legacy ethnic at Panda Inn, imported premium at Yakiya) and there is enormous AI leverage in cross-portfolio operations: shared sourcing intelligence (A5 wagyu enters at Yakiya, could be tested as a Panda Inn premium SKU), shared customer data (a Yakiya special-occasion booking signals the family will hit Panda Inn for casual brunch within 30 days), shared menu R&D (Panda Inn's bone marrow rice could become a cross-concept signature). **No single-restaurant menu analysis touches this.**

### Insight 5: The "Hastings Ranch monopoly" is Yakiya's strongest asset and they're not using it.
Yakiya is the only premium Japanese restaurant for 2.5 miles in any direction. The closest competitor that could challenge it is in Old Pasadena, ~4 miles away. East Pasadena has the demographic to support premium dining (Hastings Ranch is one of the most affluent ZIPs in 91107) but no other operator has built for it. Yakiya should be DOMINATING the East Pasadena premium-Japanese category — instead it has 88 Google reviews. The marketing/positioning gap is massive. Recommendation: hyperlocal Hastings Ranch geo-targeting + a Sunday brunch / family-tasting offer would unlock the entire East Pasadena daypart with no competitive response possible.
