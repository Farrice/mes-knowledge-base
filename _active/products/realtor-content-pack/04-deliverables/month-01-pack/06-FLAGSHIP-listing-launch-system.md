# The Listing Launch System

This is the flagship. Run it every time you take a listing. It turns one property into a coordinated launch: a shoot sheet, an announcement, a three-post sequence, a reel, and a forwardable summary you (or your marketing person) can execute from a phone. The whole system runs on one rule: **every claim traces to your MLS sheet, the seller's disclosures, or your own eyes on a walkthrough. If you can't source it, you don't say it.**

---

## Part 1 — The Shoot Sheet (fill this before you film anything)

One page. Fill it at the listing appointment or the photo walkthrough. Everything downstream copies from here, so errors here become errors everywhere.

```
LISTING: [ADDRESS] · [NEIGHBORHOOD], [CITY]
PRICE: $[PRICE] · [BEDS] bed / [BATHS] bath · [SQFT] sqft · [LOT SIZE] lot
KEY DATES: photos [DATE] · live on MLS [DATE] · open house [DATE/TIME]

THE ONE ARGUMENT (a single sentence: why this home at this price —
the line every post in the launch supports):
"____________________________________________"

THE TRIFECTA:
1. Pattern interrupt (the thing that stops the scroll): ______________
2. The real pitch (the substantive reason to buy):    ______________
3. Bonus proof (the feature that seals it):           ______________

THE LIKELY OBJECTION (what makes a buyer hesitate — street, zip,
age, layout — named honestly in 8 words or fewer): ______________
THE FLIP (the verifiable fact that answers it):     ______________

TOP 5 VERIFIED FEATURES (from MLS sheet / disclosures only):
1. ____________  2. ____________  3. ____________
4. ____________  5. ____________

VERIFY BEFORE CLAIMING (check each box or strike the claim):
[ ] Renovation year / permits — per seller docs, not the flyer
[ ] Solar / systems: owned vs leased (never say "paid solar" unverified)
[ ] Any extra unit: permitted? legally rentable? (no "income unit" until yes)
[ ] Price history: NO "price cut" talk unless MLS shows a real change event
[ ] Sqft and bed/bath split match MLS exactly
[ ] Any commute/transit claim: currently true and operating

DON'T-SAY LIST for this listing (fair-housing set + anything
that failed verification above): _____________________________

SHOT LIST (tie each to a claim you'll make):
[ ] Approach shot (the opening frame of the reel)
[ ] The pattern-interrupt feature, from its best angle
[ ] The flip (the shot that proves the objection wrong)
[ ] Kitchen in motion (walking, not static)
[ ] The golden-hour hero spot (film your CLOSE take here)
```

## Part 2 — The Launch Sequence (3 posts + announcement)

Timing: Coming Soon 2–4 days before live (only where your MLS rules allow pre-marketing; check yours) → Just Listed on day one → Open House 1–2 days before the event. First-day engagement matters most: post the Just Listed the morning the listing goes live, not the weekend after.

### Post A — Coming Soon (curiosity gap, no address)

*Photo: one detail shot. The gate, the doorway, the view. Not the full exterior.*

Caption:
> Something's coming to [NEIGHBORHOOD] this week that I've been sitting on for [TIMEFRAME].
> [ONE LINE of honest anticipation tied to the Trifecta's pattern interrupt: "I don't get many with [FEATURE] at this price." Only say it if it's true.]
> [BEDS] beds. [ONE MORE SPEC, held back deliberately.] That's all you get until [DAY].
> Want the full details the minute it's live, before the portals have it? DM me "[KEYWORD]."
> [AGENT NAME] · [BROKERAGE] · [LICENSE #]

### Post B — Just Listed (the announcement, full reveal)

*Photos: 6–10, ordered: approach shot → pattern interrupt → best interior → the flip → outdoor → hero.*

Caption structure (this is the workhorse; write it in your speaking voice):
> [WALK-IN NARRATIVE, 2–4 short paragraphs: take the reader on the physical path through the property, street to back fence. Write it as a lived scene, not a spec list. The test: every sentence puts the reader somewhere specific. "Stand at the kitchen island and you're looking straight down the hall to the [FEATURE]" beats "open floor plan."]
>
> ✔️ $[PRICE]
> ✔️ [BEDS] bed / [BATHS] bath · [SQFT] sqft
> ✔️ [VERIFIED FEATURE]
> ✔️ [VERIFIED FEATURE]
> ✔️ [VERIFIED FEATURE]
> ✔️ [LOT / OUTDOOR LINE]
>
> [ONE CONTRAST LINE — the objection-flip fact in one sentence: "at this price in [CITY], you usually give up [X]. Not here."]
>
> DM me "[KEYWORD]" and I'll send the full details and get you in this week.
> [ADDRESS]
> Listed by [AGENT NAME] · [BROKERAGE] · [LICENSE #]
> . . .
> [HASHTAGS: your neighborhood + 2–3 ADJACENT, higher-search neighborhoods + #justlisted + your handle tag]

### Post C — Open House (logistics + one reason)

*Photo: the single best shot not used as the Just Listed cover.*

Caption:
> [LISTING ADDRESS] is open [DAY], [TIME].
> If you only have ten minutes, come see [THE PATTERN INTERRUPT]. Photos don't do it justice and I'm tired of trying.
> [ONE PRACTICAL LINE: where to park / enter through the side gate / I'll have the disclosure package printed.]
> Can't make [DAY]? DM "[KEYWORD]" and we'll set a private time.
> [AGENT NAME] · [BROKERAGE] · [LICENSE #]

## Part 3 — The Listing Reel (HOOK → TOUR → CLOSE)

Three separate filming takes, assembled into one 20–40 second reel. The hook lands in the first 2 seconds, no walk-up. Hook goes on screen as text, word for word. Native captions on.

**HOOK (one sentence, filmed at the pattern-interrupt spot):**
Pick the lane that fits the listing:
- *Objection flip:* "Saw [THE OBJECTION] and almost kept scrolling?"
- *Rarity:* "[THE RARE FACT] doesn't come up in [NEIGHBORHOOD]." (Only if your MLS search actually backs it.)
- *Authority POV:* "[A CALM MARKET THESIS the property proves — 'real privacy in [CITY] isn't a bigger fence. It's this.']"

**TOUR (2–4 sentences, VO over moving footage — the facts live here):**
> "[Walk the viewer through the Trifecta in order: interrupt → real pitch → bonus proof. Every claim from the shoot sheet's verified list. Speak the price cleanly. One lived-scene line minimum: put the viewer at a spot in the house and show them what they see from it.]"

**CLOSE (separate take, hero spot, golden hour if you can — this take is reusable):**
> "$[PRICE], [NEIGHBORHOOD]. I'm [AGENT NAME] with [BROKERAGE]. DM me '[KEYWORD]' and I'll send you everything and get you in this week."

**Filming notes:** movement in frame from second one (walk-in or push-in, never a static hero shot) · on-screen text for every number · post the morning it goes live · cover text on the reel: 2 short lines, each provable by the frame behind it ("half an acre." / "one level.").

## Part 4 — The Seller Update Text (forwardable)

Your marketing is also a listing tool: sellers hire agents they can see working. After launch day, send the seller one text they can forward to anyone:

> Hi [SELLER FIRST NAME], [ADDRESS] is live. Here's the launch recap:
> **THE NUMBERS:** $[PRICE] · [BEDS]/[BATHS] · [SQFT] sqft
> **LIVE NOW:** MLS + [PORTALS] + my channels ([N] posts + 1 video). Links: [LINKS]
> **THIS WEEK:** open house [DAY/TIME] · [N] private showings booked · buyer inquiries so far: [N]
> **NEXT UPDATE:** [DAY]. If anything moves before then, you'll hear from me first.
> Forward this to anyone who's curious about the listing. The DM word for full details is "[KEYWORD]".

Numbers in this text are real counts, always. An inflated inquiry count to impress a seller is the fastest way to lose the trust the whole system builds.

## The Fair-Housing Floor (applies to every part above)

- Market the property, never the buyer. No "perfect for families," "great starter for young couples," "ideal for professionals," or any description of who should live there.
- Banned descriptors: "safe," "family-friendly," "exclusive neighborhood," "great for kids." Substitute the physical fact: gated entry, fenced yard, cul-de-sac, guest suite, flexible bonus room.
- Schools stay out of your videos and hooks. If you reference schools at all, link official district/rating sources in written form and let buyers draw conclusions.
- Lifestyle framing is fine when it describes activities the property enables ("morning walks to [PARK]"), not the people doing them.
- When unsure, run the swap test: would the sentence still make sense if any person of any background bought the home? If not, rewrite it.
