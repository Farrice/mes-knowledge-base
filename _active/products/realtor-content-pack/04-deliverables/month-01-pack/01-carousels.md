# Carousels — 8 Templates

Format: hook slide (large text, minimal design), 4–7 content slides (one idea per slide), CTA slide. Keep slide text under ~25 words. The hook slide decides everything; write it last.

---

## Carousel 1 — Buyer Education: "What your rent is actually buying"

**Data to pull first:** a current mortgage payment estimate for a typical entry-level home in your market (use your lender partner's calculator with today's rate; label it an estimate) and the going rent for a comparable unit (check active rental listings).

- **Slide 1 (hook):** Your rent: $[RENT]/mo. A mortgage on [STREET-LEVEL EXAMPLE, e.g. "a 3-bed in [NEIGHBORHOOD]"]: about $[PAYMENT]/mo.*
- **Slide 2:** Rent buys you 30 days. Then the meter resets.
- **Slide 3:** A mortgage payment splits three ways: interest, taxes, and principal. That last slice is money you keep.
- **Slide 4:** On a $[PRICE] home, roughly $[PRINCIPAL]/mo of the payment goes to your own balance. (Ask me or any lender to run your exact number.)
- **Slide 5:** The trade: you take on maintenance, insurance, and commitment. Owning is not automatically better. It's better *when you plan to stay put.*
- **Slide 6:** The honest question is not "can I afford to buy." It's "what does 5 more years of renting cost me vs. 5 years of owning."
- **Slide 7 (CTA):** Want that 5-year math run on your actual rent and budget? DM me "[KEYWORD]" and I'll send it. No pressure, just the numbers. — [AGENT NAME], [BROKERAGE]
- *Footnote on slide 1: "Estimate based on [RATE]% rate, [DOWN]% down. Your numbers will differ."*

## Carousel 2 — Buyer Education: "The 5 costs nobody budgets for"

- **Slide 1 (hook):** The down payment is not the only check you'll write. Budget these 5 before you shop.
- **Slide 2:** 1. Closing costs. Typically 2–5% of the price, due at signing. On $[PRICE], plan for $[LOW]–$[HIGH]. (Your lender's Loan Estimate gives the real figure.)
- **Slide 3:** 2. The inspection. A few hundred dollars, paid whether or not you buy. The cheapest insurance in the whole process.
- **Slide 4:** 3. Moving + immediate fixes. First locksmith visit, blinds, that one leaky valve the inspection flagged. First-month cash, not loan money.
- **Slide 5:** 4. Reserves your lender wants to see. Many loans require months of payments in savings *after* closing.
- **Slide 6:** 5. Property taxes + insurance escrow setup. Often prepaid at closing. Ask your lender to show you this line before you're surprised by it.
- **Slide 7 (CTA):** I keep a one-page "real cost of buying in [CITY]" worksheet. DM me "[KEYWORD]" and it's yours.

## Carousel 3 — Seller Education: "What actually sets your sale price"

- **Slide 1 (hook):** Your home's price is not set by what you paid, what you need, or what Zillow says.
- **Slide 2:** It's set by one thing: what a buyer will pay TODAY, proven by what buyers paid for the closest recent sales.
- **Slide 3:** What counts: sold (not listed) prices of similar homes nearby in the last 90ish days, adjusted for condition, size, lot.
- **Slide 4:** What doesn't count: your renovation receipts from [YEAR], the neighbor's asking price (asking ≠ getting), your online estimate (it hasn't walked your house).
- **Slide 5:** Pricing high "to leave room to negotiate" usually backfires: the best offers come in the first two weeks, and a stale listing invites lowballs.
- **Slide 6:** The strategy conversation is really about your timeline and your risk tolerance. That part is personal. The comps are not.
- **Slide 7 (CTA):** Want to see the actual comps for your street, no listing appointment required? DM me "[KEYWORD]."

## Carousel 4 — Seller Education: "Fix this, skip that"

- **Slide 1 (hook):** Before you spend a dollar getting your house ready to sell, read this.
- **Slide 2:** FIX: anything a buyer's inspector will flag anyway. Leaks, dead outlets, the water heater sticker from two owners ago. Cheap now, expensive in negotiation.
- **Slide 3:** FIX: paint and light. Fresh neutral walls and working bulbs in every fixture return more than almost any project.
- **Slide 4:** FIX: the first 10 feet. Front door, porch light, house numbers, mulch. Buyers decide their mood before they're inside.
- **Slide 5:** SKIP: the full kitchen remodel. You'll pick finishes for a buyer you've never met and rarely get the money back.
- **Slide 6:** SKIP: replacing things that merely look dated but work. Price it in instead. Buyers would rather choose their own countertops than pay for yours.
- **Slide 7 (CTA):** I do a walk-through where the only agenda is "spend here, not there." DM me "[KEYWORD]" to set one up.

## Carousel 7 — Myth-Busting: "You don't need 20% down"

- **Slide 1 (hook):** "I can't buy. I don't have 20% down." Read this before you decide that.
- **Slide 2:** 20% down avoids mortgage insurance. That's all it does. It is not the entry ticket.
- **Slide 3:** Conventional loans exist from 3% down. FHA from 3.5%. VA and USDA from 0% for those who qualify. (Your lender confirms which you fit.)
- **Slide 4:** Mortgage insurance on a smaller down payment is a real monthly cost, not a scam. The question is whether paying it beats [MORE YEARS] more years of renting while you save. That's math, not a rule of thumb.
- **Slide 5:** Also real: down payment assistance programs in [STATE/COUNTY]. Ask a local lender which ones are currently funded. (Programs change. I don't list them here so you never act on stale info.)
- **Slide 6:** The move: talk to a lender a year before you think you're ready. Worst case, you leave with a checklist. Best case, you're closer than you thought.
- **Slide 7 (CTA):** Want intros to the two lenders my buyers actually like? DM me "[KEYWORD]."

## Carousel 8 — Process Explainer: "Offer accepted. Now what?"

- **Slide 1 (hook):** Your offer got accepted. Here's the next 30–45 days, in order, with the parts nobody warns you about.
- **Slide 2:** Days 1–3: earnest money deposited, escrow opened. Your money is held by a neutral third party, not the seller.
- **Slide 3:** Week 1: inspection. You're not looking for a perfect house. You're looking for expensive surprises.
- **Slide 4:** Week 2: negotiate repairs or credits. This is a second negotiation. Most buyers don't know they get one.
- **Slide 5:** Weeks 2–4: appraisal + loan underwriting. Rule of the quiet weeks: buy nothing on credit. Not a couch. Not a car. Underwriters re-check everything.
- **Slide 6:** Final week: clear-to-close, final walkthrough (verify repairs, verify nothing left behind), then signing.
- **Slide 7:** Keys. Usually at recording, not at signing. Plan your movers for the day after, not the day of.
- **Slide 8 (CTA):** I give every buyer this as a printed timeline with your actual dates filled in. DM me "[KEYWORD]" to see it.
