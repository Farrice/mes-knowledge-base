# Local Signal Playbook

This playbook helps you turn the life of your market into original content without copying another agent, pretending to have an opinion you do not hold, or turning every local post into a listing ad.

The goal is **qualified local attention**: the right people recognize the place, the question, or the tradeoff. Views are useful, but they are not leads. A lead begins when a real person starts a relevant conversation.

The Listing Launch System remains this pack's flagship. Use this playbook between listings to build the local familiarity and trust that make listing content land better.

## The loop

```text
Local signal → your real take → simple content → human conversation → learning
```

## Step 1: Build your Agent Fit Card

Fill this once and update it when your business changes.

| Field | Your answer |
|---|---|
| Market | [CITY / NEIGHBORHOODS] |
| Niche | [who you most often help] |
| One person this content should feel written for | [a specific situation, not a catch-all audience] |
| Three things you genuinely notice or care about locally | [INTERESTS] |
| Three phrases you naturally use | [VOICE MARKERS] |
| Three phrases you would never say | [VOICE EXCLUSIONS] |
| Easiest production format | [TALKING HEAD / VOICEOVER / FACELESS / GREEN SCREEN / MIXED] |
| Realistic weekly capacity | [number of posts] |
| Low-pressure offer | [GUIDE / LIST / CONVERSATION / TOUR / PLAN] |
| Who answers replies | [NAME] |

If you cannot complete the “genuinely notice or care about” row, do not force an opinion. Start with sourced reporting or a question you are honestly exploring.

## Step 2: Collect five signals

You need choices before you need scripts. Gather at least five candidates from these three lanes:

### Current local sources

- City agendas and planning documents
- Local news outlets
- Business openings or closures
- Community events
- Transportation or infrastructure changes
- A current MLS or board report you are authorized to use

Record the URL, publisher, date, and the exact fact the source supports. A development announcement does not automatically prove higher property values.

### Saved content patterns

Save a post because of its structure, not because you want its words. Write down what may transfer:

- Question headline
- Foreground cutout over local footage
- Ranking or bracket
- “What changed and who notices?” structure
- Field voiceover

Another creator's topic, opinion, views, and audience do not automatically transfer to your market.

### Lived observations

- A question you heard twice this week
- Something buyers keep noticing or missing
- A block, business, commute, or routine you personally use
- A moment from an open house or showing
- A local change you would text a friend about

This lane is often the strongest because no other agent has your exact experience.

## Step 3: Reject before you rank

Discard an idea when:

- The factual claim has no source.
- It says or implies who belongs in a neighborhood.
- It uses phrases such as “safe neighborhood,” “perfect for families,” “great schools,” or “ideal for young professionals.”
- It copies another creator's wording or persona.
- You would never discuss the subject outside content production.
- The audience is impressive but not local or relevant to your business.
- No one is prepared to answer the CTA.

## Step 4: Score the survivors

Give each idea 0, 1, or 2 points in five areas:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local specificity | Could belong anywhere | City-level | Block, place, event, or locally recognizable detail |
| Audience relevance | No clear person | Broad local interest | One plausible local person feels seen |
| Your conviction | Borrowed or neutral | Curious | You have a real take or lived experience |
| Conversation potential | Passive fact | Worth saving/sharing | Naturally invites a relevant reply |
| Production fit | You will avoid making it | Possible with friction | Fits your easiest format and capacity |

Highest total wins. If two ideas tie, choose the one you care about more. If they still tie, choose the easier one to make.

### Production choices

- **Talking head:** best when your expression and opinion carry the piece.
- **Foreground cutout over local B-roll:** useful for a current source or visually recognizable place.
- **Voiceover:** useful when you want the place to lead.
- **Faceless:** valid when your footage, sourcing, and point of view are still specific.
- **Mixed:** begin on camera, then let the place or property take over.

Daily posting is not required. The right cadence is the one you can sustain without becoming a worse agent or a fake creator.

## Step 6: Approve the human take

Before filming or posting, confirm:

- I would actually say this.
- This opinion is mine.
- The source still supports the factual line.
- I described property, place, or process, not the protected identity of who belongs there.
- A human owns the reply after the CTA.

If any answer is no, the card is a draft.

## Step 7: Run the conversation bridge

```text
Useful local content
→ low-pressure CTA
→ one routing question
→ human reply
→ one question about the person's real situation
→ useful next step or CRM follow-up
```

Example routing question:

> Glad this was useful. Are you here for the local updates, or are you also thinking about a move?

Do not punish a content-only follower with a sales sequence. Help first. Let an actual need earn the real-estate conversation.

## Keep two scoreboards

| Attention | Pipeline |
|---|---|
| Local reach | Relevant DMs |
| Watch time or completion | Qualified conversations |
| Saves | Appointments |
| Shares | Signed clients |
| Profile visits | Collected revenue |

Do not combine the columns. A high-view post can be a poor business asset. A modest local post can create the right conversation. Until something is published, record `NO EVENT`.
