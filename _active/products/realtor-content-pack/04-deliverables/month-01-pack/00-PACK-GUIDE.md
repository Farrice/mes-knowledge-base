# Month 1 Content Pack — Start Here

Welcome. This pack contains a full month of social content built for working agents: 8 carousels, 8 reel scripts, 15 captions, 2 emails, 2 faceless video scripts, the Listing Launch System (the per-listing engine you'll run every time you take a new listing), and the Local Signal Playbook for creating original content between listings.

## How the merge fields work

Every template uses [BRACKETED FIELDS]. Replace each one with your own details before posting. The common ones:

- **[CITY] / [NEIGHBORHOOD]** — your market. Always use the specific neighborhood over the metro name when you can. "Maple Grove" beats "the Minneapolis area."
- **[AGENT NAME] / [BROKERAGE] / [LICENSE #]** — you. Include your license number wherever your state requires it on advertising.
- **[PRICE], [BEDS], [BATHS], [SQFT], [LISTING ADDRESS]** — pull straight from your MLS sheet. Never estimate a spec from memory.
- **[LOCAL STAT], [DOM AVERAGE], [MEDIAN PRICE]** — your market numbers. Get these from your MLS market report, your board's monthly stats, or a title rep. Each template that uses a data field tells you exactly which number to pull and where to get it. **Never guess a statistic and never reuse last quarter's number.** A specific, current, sourced number is what makes you look like the local expert. A wrong one does the opposite.
- **[KEYWORD]** — a single word people can DM you to get something (a guide, a list, the full details). Pick one word per post, keep it in caps, and actually reply when people send it. If you use a DM automation tool, set the trigger before you post.

**The 10-minute rule:** each template should take about 10 minutes to customize. Fill the fields, read it out loud once, cut anything you personally would never say, post. If you're rewriting whole paragraphs, stop. Pick a different template that fits you better.

## Fair housing (read this once, apply it always)

Describe the property and the lifestyle it offers. Never describe the people who should live there. That means: no "perfect for families," "great for young professionals," "ideal starter home for couples," "safe neighborhood," or any language that welcomes or steers a protected group. Say "guest suite," not "in-law suite for grandma." Say "walkable to parks and coffee," not "family-friendly." Leave school ratings out of your videos; if you share school information at all, link the district's own data and let buyers evaluate it themselves. Every template in this pack is written to this standard. Keep it that way when you customize. When in doubt, describe the house, the lot, the light, and the location. Never the buyer.

## Suggested month

- **Weeks 1–4:** 2 carousels + 2 reels + 3–4 captioned posts per week, one email mid-month.
- **Any week you take a listing:** pause the calendar and run the Listing Launch System (file 06). A live listing outranks everything else in this pack.
- **Between listings:** use the Local Signal Playbook (file 07) to turn current local sources and lived observations into voice-compatible ideas. The Listing Launch System remains the flagship.
- Post reels with your face when you can. Use the faceless scripts (file 05) for weeks you can't film.

## One posting principle

Every post here does one of three jobs: shows you know the market (authority), starts conversations (engagement), or gives people a reason to DM you (lead gen). The captions file labels each one. Keep a rough balance across the month, and always know which job a post is doing before you hit publish.

Keep attention and pipeline separate. Reach, watch time, saves, shares, and profile visits tell you whether the content earned attention. Relevant DMs, qualified conversations, appointments, clients, and collected revenue tell you whether it moved the business. Do not call the first column the second.

## Original local content

File 07 adds a source-first creation loop. It requires a real local source or lived observation, your own point of view, a format you can sustain, and a human response path. It rejects copied wording, unsourced local claims, fair-housing steering, generic AI voice, and viral examples from the wrong market.
