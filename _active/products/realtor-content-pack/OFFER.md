**Replaces** the subscription framing in `PRODUCT.md` and `03-storefront/01-sales-page.md` as the go-to-market shape. The month-1 pack and the flagship system stay exactly as built; what changes is what gets promised, to whom, and for how long.
**Cleanup mandate (owner, 2026-08-24):** deliverable by one person, sellable without inventing proof, premium over volume, and a Priestley signal test before any further build.

## 1. The Offer

**Who it's for.** Listing-side operators who market more than one property a month: small agencies and VA firms producing realtor content by hand (20 of the 50 on the outreach list), and listing-heavy agent teams (the top slice of the other 30). Not the solo agent with two listings a year. Value here scales with listing count, so the buyer must have listing count.

**The promise, in their words.** *Every listing you take goes live with a full launch attached: shoot sheet, coming-soon, just-listed, open-house, the reel, and the seller text they forward. You run it in about an hour instead of building it from scratch, and you own it forever.*

**What they get:**

1. **The Listing Launch System**, editable, theirs permanently. Shoot sheet with the verification checklist, the three-post launch sequence, the HOOK/TOUR/CLOSE reel structure, the forwardable seller update, and the fair-housing floor baked into every line.
2. **A 60-minute install call** where we fill the shoot sheet on a listing they actually have.
3. **Their first launch, produced with them.** One real property, one real MLS sheet, launch copy written alongside them so the system is running before they're alone with it.
4. **A 30-minute debrief** two weeks later: what landed, what to change, handoff.
5. **The starter library**: 8 carousels, 8 reel scripts, 15 captions, 2 sphere emails, 2 faceless-video scripts, for the weeks between listings.
6. **Agency license** where the buyer is an agency or VA firm: rebrand it and run it inside their client accounts.

**Price.** $950 founding. $1,800 after the founding cohort closes. One time, not a subscription.
**Optional after install:** the Listing Desk, $390/year, four quarterly refresh drops plus system updates. Offered at the debrief, never bundled into the install.

**Capacity cap: 6.** Not a marketing number. Each install costs about 4.5 working hours (call, first-launch production, debrief, support window). Six is 27 hours of delivery, which lands at roughly 9 hrs/wk across a three-week delivery window while 5 to 8 hrs/wk of outreach is still running. Seven breaks the week. Six is the ceiling, and the ledger in section 2 is where the number comes from.

**Risk reversal a solo can honor.** Half at signing, half after their first launch is live. If the first launch ships and they don't want the system, the second half is never billed and they keep the launch. Downside is capped at six half-payments, which is survivable, and it is honest because this is a finished engagement rather than a subscription that has to keep being fed.

**Proof-light selling, honestly.** No case studies exist in this niche yet, so nothing pretends they do. The founding cohort *is* the proof mechanic: the first-launch deliverable produces six documented launches, which become the case studies the $1,800 price is sold on. Scarcity is real because capacity is real.

**After the signal.** Proves: raise to $1,800, open cohort two at eight, add the pure white-label license (section 5) for buyers who don't need the install. Fails: the assets stay on the shelf, nothing has been promised that has to keep being delivered, and the exit costs one page and six weeks.

## 3. What We Deliberately Do Not Promise

- **No monthly content drop, forever.** Twelve production events a year for an open-ended subscriber list is the obligation a solo cannot carry, and month two is where these products die. Quarterly refreshes, built once, are what can actually be shipped.
- **No "built by a working team."** One operator built it and runs it. The current sales page says otherwise and that line has to go before anything sends.
- **No results guarantee.** Listings won, leads generated, followers gained: none of it is controllable from here. Guaranteeing it would be the first thing a buyer catches.
- **No custom content per client.** Custom means linear hours, which is the trap the owner named by name. Templates with merge fields, or nothing.
- **No community, group calls, office hours, or Slack.** Community management is the explicit disqualifier. Two scheduled calls and a 14-day email window, both named in the agreement.
- **No compliance guarantee.** The fair-housing floor is built into every template, and final responsibility sits with the buyer and their broker, same as any marketing they run. Saying more than that would be false.
- **No unlimited revisions on the first launch.** One listing, one production pass, one revision round.
- **No software.** This is a system, not a tool. No portal to maintain, no logins to support.
- **No claim of prior clients in this niche.** There are none yet. The founding cohort exists because of that, not in spite of it.

## 5. The Alternate: The Pure White-Label License

**Shape.** $2,500 one time, perpetual rebrand-and-resell rights to the Listing Launch System plus the starter library, delivered as a file set with a written license. No calls, no first launch, no debrief. Sold only to the 20 agencies and VA firms. Roughly 0.5 hrs per sale and near-zero after.

Hours per dollar, it beats the install by a wide margin, and it is where this goes in month four.

**Why it lost, in one line:** it removes the first-launch deliverable, which is the only mechanic in either offer that manufactures the proof the owner does not yet have. Selling a $2,500 file set cold, with no case study and no name in the niche, asks a stranger to trust a promise nothing backs. Proof buys the right to sell the file set later.

## 6. Test Plan (Priestley)

**Signal mechanic.** No checkout. A one-page brief plus an application, "The Founding Six," sent by hand to the 50-contact list (27 email, 12 phone, 6 form, 5 IG DM per `05-outreach/02-list-notes.md`). **Sends stay human.** Agencies and VA firms go first. Listing-heavy teams follow.

**Five glass-slipper questions** (each disqualifies, none describes): How many listings did you market in the last 90 days? Who writes the launch copy today and how long does it take them? Have you paid for a content system before, and what happened? Do you have a listing going live in the next 30 days? Who signs off, you or someone else?

**Cohort cap: 6.** Application target: 8 to 10, from an expected 12 to 15 replies. Priestley's 10x waitlist does not survive contact with a 50-name cold list, so the honest target is stated rather than the textbook one.

**Decision rule, pre-registered, 21 days from first send:**
- **Proceed** at 6 or more applications and 3 or more paid deposits. Deliver the cohort, then reprice.
- **Iterate** at 3 to 5 applications and 2 or fewer deposits. Interest without urgency means the promise or the price is wrong. Fix the offer, not the send volume.
- **Kill** at fewer than 3 applications after 50 personalized sends and two follow-ups. The buyer does not feel this pain at this price, and the answer is a different buyer, not a different page.

**Timeline.** The first seven days go to formatting the deliverable, writing the one-page brief and the application, and the first 20 sends. Days 8 to 14 carry the remaining 30 sends plus follow-up on the earlier batch. Day 15 through day 21 is second follow-up, deposit closes, and the decision. Delivery runs the three weeks after that, and repricing (or shelving) happens the week the last debrief lands.

**Open decisions carried forward:** product/brand name still unchosen (cultural-connotation check required before locking); domain and landing host pending the name.
