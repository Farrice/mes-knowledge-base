"Here's where I want to be straight with you. I put real time, real energy, and real infrastructure into this. My AI system, my development hours, my research — this wasn't a weekend project. I need to be compensated for what I've already built, and we need to figure out who pays for what going forward."

"And I want to say this because I know we're family — that's exactly WHY I want to do this right. If I build this for free and it doesn't work, I'm resentful. If you pay me and it does work, we both win and the relationship is stronger. Business done well between family is a flex, not a risk. ### Frame the Ongoing Costs

"There are two kinds of costs here. One is my build fee — paying me for what I've already created. The other is infrastructure — API keys, data feeds, hosting. Those are like the electricity bill for the system. I'm not asking you to pay for my personal AI tools. I'm asking you to fund the tools the trading system needs to operate. Think of it like: I built the restaurant, you're paying for the ingredients and the rent."

### Close (Give Him Options, Not an Ultimatum)

"I put together three options. Each one is fair. Pick the one that matches how you want to approach this. There's no wrong answer — I just need us to be clear before I keep building."

## PART 3: OBJECTION HANDLING

---

### "Can't you just set it up and let it run?"

"I wish. But this isn't a Roomba — it's more like a race car. It needs a driver. Markets change their fee structures overnight — Polymarket literally did this on March 30th and killed an entire category of profitable bots in one day. Strategies that work this month might not work in three months. Someone needs to monitor performance, evolve the strategies, and make sure the risk controls are calibrated. That's what the maintenance covers. Without it, the system will make money for a while and then slowly stop — not because it's broken, but because the market moved and nobody adjusted."

---

### "Why do I need to pay you AND pay for APIs?"

"Think of it like this: I'm the chef, and the APIs are the ingredients. You're paying me for my expertise — knowing what to build, how to build it, and how to keep it working. You're paying for the APIs because those are the raw materials the system needs to operate — weather data, sportsbook odds, AI analysis calls. I'm not double-dipping. My Claude Code subscription, my development tools, my research infrastructure — that's on me. The APIs the trading system uses to actually find trades? That's the system's operating cost, and it runs on your behalf."

---

### "What if it doesn't make money?"

"That's a real possibility and I respect you for asking it. 92% of all Polymarket wallets lose money. This system is built to be in the top 8%, but I can't promise that. Here's what I CAN promise: we test with paper money first. Zero real dollars at risk. After 30-60 days of simulation against live market data, we'll have hard numbers — win rate, profit factor, edge size by strategy. If the numbers don't work, you're out $130 in API costs and a build fee for a system that still exists and can be retested when market conditions change. You're not out $25,000. That's the whole point of how I designed this."

"And honestly — if I was the kind of person who said 'guaranteed profits,' you should run. Nobody who's honest about trading says that. What I built is a system that gives you a structural edge, not a money printer."

---

### "You're family, why are you charging me?"

"BECAUSE you're family. If I do this for free and burn 100+ hours and it doesn't pan out, I'm going to feel some type of way. And you'd feel it too — that weird energy where one person gave too much and the other didn't realize it. I don't want that between us. A clear deal means clear energy. You know exactly what you're getting, I know exactly what I'm getting, and neither of us is guessing or keeping score."

"Also — and I say this with love — if you went to a developer on Upwork and asked them to build a multi-strategy AI trading platform with two exchange integrations, risk management, and paper trading... you'd get quoted $25-50K and it would take 3-6 months. I'm charging a fraction of that because this is us, and because I want it to work. But 'family discount' and 'free' are two different things."

---

### "The briefing said $75-130 to test — why is this $3-5K?"

"The $75-130 is the API and data cost to run the paper trading test. That's like the gas money. The $3-5K is for the car I already built. Those are two different things."

"The system that runs those tests — the 7,248 lines of code, the four AI strategies, the risk management, the cross-platform matching, the paper trading simulator — that took real time and real skill to build. The $75-130 gets the system running. The $3-5K pays for the fact that the system exists in the first place."

"Think of it this way: if I hand you a loaded gun and say 'bullets are $2,' you'd still expect to pay for the gun."

---

### "Can I just use ChatGPT to do this myself?"

"You could try. Seriously — I'm not going to gatekeep. But here's what you'd be building from scratch:"

"First, you'd need to figure out Polymarket's API — it uses a CLOB (Central Limit Order Book) with specific authentication, order types, and a Gamma Markets wrapper. Then you'd need to build a contract matching engine that catches false arbitrage — research shows 60-70% of apparent cross-platform opportunities are fake because the contracts have different resolution criteria. Then weather data integration with exact airport station coordinates. Then sportsbook odds aggregation from 348 sources. Then risk management with position sizing, kill switches, and circuit breakers. Then paper trading infrastructure that simulates realistic fills."

"ChatGPT can help you write code. It can't architect a trading system, debug API authentication, or know that Polymarket weather markets resolve from airport stations, not city centers. That knowledge came from me spending weeks doing deep extraction research on every profitable bot strategy that's been documented."

"The short answer: you could rebuild this in 3-6 months if you're a developer. Or you can have it now, battle-tested, for a fraction of what it would cost to hire someone."
