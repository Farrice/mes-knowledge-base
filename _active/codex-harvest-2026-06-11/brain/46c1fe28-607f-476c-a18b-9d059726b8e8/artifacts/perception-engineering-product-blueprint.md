## Part 2: Best Use Cases — When to Fire Each Workflow

### Scenario 1: "My Sales Page Converts at 0.8% and I Don't Know Why"

**Fire**: `/behavioral-copy-audit`

**Why this workflow**: Most people rewrite bad copy by adding more words. This workflow diagnoses the *specific failure mode* across three lenses simultaneously. Is the copy failing because it describes features instead of engineering a perception? Because the dopamine architecture is flat? Because it makes claims without creating epiphanies? You get a scored diagnosis + the top 5 rewrite prescriptions ranked by impact.

**Real scenario**: A course creator selling a $497 program on "building systems." The copy talks about templates, SOPs, and time savings. The `/behavioral-copy-audit` reveals:
- **Perception gap**: The course is filed in the "productivity tool" mental bin. Needs reframing as "freedom architecture."
- **Dopamine gap**: No mechanism — no named "Missing 1%" that makes the method feel proprietary.
- **Insight gap**: Zero reversed causation vectors. The copy assumes the audience already believes systems matter.

**Output**: A diagnosis that tells you exactly WHERE to spend rewrite effort — not starting from scratch, but surgical fixes.

**Real scenario**: A fitness coach posting "5 exercises for shoulder mobility" content. Gets 50K views, 3 DMs. The blind spot engine reveals:
