## Genius Patterns

### 1. Prescription Without Diagnosis Is Malpractice
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Refuses to discuss solutions until the problem is fully mapped and quantified. Treats premature solution-selling as professional misconduct.
- **Executable Behavior**: Before ANY recommendation, complete the full diagnostic cycle: interview executives AND operators, map every click in every process, quantify time/cost waste with the ROI formula.
- **Deployment Context**: Every new client engagement, every sales conversation, every internal project scoping.
- **Success Metric**: Client can see their exact annual waste figure before hearing a single solution.

### 2. The Lamborghini Chassis Principle
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Identifies that companies bolting AI tools onto broken processes creates "a Toyota Corolla with Lamborghini parts" — it doesn't become a Lamborghini.
- **Executable Behavior**: Before any implementation, build the centralized knowledge base (the "chassis"). Every company needs a single brain where all data lives before AI agents can function.
- **Deployment Context**: When a client wants to "add AI" to their existing operations. When a company has fragmented data across 20+ tools.
- **Success Metric**: Company has a single source of truth that AI systems can query before any agent is deployed.

### 3. The "Then What?" Interview Cascade
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Asks "walk me through exactly what you did yesterday morning" then relentlessly follows with "and then what?" to surface hidden inefficiencies that employees skip over because they're normalized.
- **Executable Behavior**: Sit with every operator (not just executives). Get them to narrate their entire day. Probe every click, every tab switch, every manual step. They will intuitively skip inefficiencies — that's where the gold is.
- **Deployment Context**: Process mapping phase of every audit. The gap between what executives *think* happens and what *actually* happens is where the ROI lives.
- **Success Metric**: Process maps show 3-7x more steps than executives described.

### 4. The ROI Quantification Formula
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Converts every inefficiency into a dollar figure using: `Time Wasted × People × Days/Year × Loaded Hourly Cost = Annual Waste`
- **Executable Behavior**: For every bottleneck identified, plug in the four variables. Present the aggregate waste figure to the business owner before discussing solutions.
- **Deployment Context**: Every diagnostic presentation. This formula is the core selling mechanism — business owners buy ROI, not workflows.
- **Success Metric**: Every identified inefficiency has a dollar figure attached. Total annual waste exceeds proposed engagement cost by 3-10x.

### 5. Operators Know the Road, Executives Know the Destination
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Interviews BOTH the C-suite (for strategic goals) and the frontline employees (for actual daily workflows). Never relies on executive descriptions alone.
- **Executable Behavior**: Split every audit into two interview tracks: executives for vision/goals/priorities, operators for actual minute-by-minute processes. The delta between these two narratives is where the most expensive problems hide.
- **Deployment Context**: Every consulting engagement. CEOs are "too far away" from core problems.
- **Success Metric**: Discovery of 2-3 major waste areas the CEO didn't know existed (like the SDR team wasting 2hrs/morning on manual prospecting).

### 6. Quick Wins as Springboard
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Prioritizes solutions on a 2×2 matrix (value × difficulty). Always starts with "quick wins" (high value, low cost) to build trust and fund the "big swings."
- **Executable Behavior**: Build an opportunity matrix from the audit findings. Deliver quick wins first to demonstrate measurable ROI. Use that proven ROI as the springboard to sell larger implementation contracts.
- **Deployment Context**: Post-audit prioritization. Client relationship management. Upselling from diagnostic to implementation.
- **Success Metric**: First deliverable produces measurable ROI within 30 days, financing the next phase.

### 7. The Change Management Gate
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Identifies that strategy + implementation without adoption = failure. Requires an "AI Champion" (senior leader who is bought in and will push through hurdles).
- **Executable Behavior**: Before accepting an implementation engagement, verify: Is there a senior-level AI champion? Is the team willing to adopt? If not, prescribe change management/training first.
- **Deployment Context**: Pre-engagement qualification. Implementation planning. The #1 predictor of project failure is lack of internal championship.
- **Success Metric**: Identified AI champion exists with org-chart authority before implementation begins.

### 8. Bottom-Line Focus (Profit Delta, Not Revenue Growth)
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Positions consulting as improving EBITDA/net profit, not growing top-line revenue. "You're at $10M ARR with $1.5M profit. We'll raise that to $2.2M."
- **Executable Behavior**: Frame every proposal in profit terms, not revenue. Quantify savings (reduced labor waste, reduced tool waste, increased efficiency) rather than growth promises.
- **Deployment Context**: Sales conversations, diagnostic presentations, ROI calculations.
- **Success Metric**: Client immediately understands the profit impact without needing to "believe in" revenue projections.

### 9. Non-Technical Positioning as Advantage
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Leans INTO being non-technical. "I struggle to turn on my computer." This becomes a trust signal — he's not selling tools, he's selling clarity that any business can understand.
- **Executable Behavior**: Never lead with technical complexity. Lead with business outcomes. Translate every technical solution into profit/time language.
- **Deployment Context**: Every client interaction. Positioning materials. Sales conversations. The moment you start explaining N8N flows, you've lost the executive.
- **Success Metric**: Client understands every recommendation without any technical knowledge.

## Hidden Knowledge

### 1. The 9-to-5 Narration Technique
Employees will skip over inefficiencies because they're habituated. The trick is to make them narrate their *entire* day chronologically, not ask about specific processes. "Show me every click of your mouse during the day" surfaces waste that targeted questions miss.

### 2. The Assumption Decomposition
"We follow up with leads" sounds like one task. It's actually 7: open CRM → filter leads → check ICP match → read submission → draft response → send email → set reminder. Every "one task" is 4-10 sub-tasks. Decompose every assumption.

### 3. The Backend Deal for First Clients
For your first engagement: charge nothing upfront. Say "if you're satisfied, it'll be a couple thousand. If not, just give me a case study, testimonial, and reference." This eliminates buyer risk while guaranteeing you get the three assets you actually need (social proof > cash at this stage).

### 4. The Technical Partnership Leverage Model
Start with referral partnerships (10-15% of implementation contract). Graduate to white-label (50% margins — they do the technical work, you own the client relationship). Never need to become technical yourself.

### 5. The 12-Month Payback Home Run
Enterprise companies consider 10-year payback acceptable. AI consulting typically delivers under 12 months. This is a "home run" by enterprise standards. Frame every ROI calculation against the 12-month benchmark — it makes even $100K+ engagements feel conservative.

### 6. The Recurring Maintenance Lock-In
Once you've implemented, you own the maintenance. AI evolves so fast that what you built 3 months ago needs updating. This creates natural recurring revenue ($3-5K/mo advisory retainers) that mirrors the IT MSP model — clients stay for years because switching costs are prohibitive.

## Methodology: The 6-Step Vibe Consulting Framework

### Phase 1: Problem Identification (Discovery)
- Interview executives for strategic goals and destination
- Interview operators for actual 9-to-5 workflows
- Use "Then What?" cascade to decompose every task
- Surface the gap between what leadership thinks happens and what actually happens

### Phase 2: Process Mapping
- Map every process end-to-end (from strategic decision to task completion)
- Document every click, tab switch, manual handoff
- Identify bottlenecks, redundancies, and "we've always done it that way" patterns
- Use the 4-question filter: Structured input? Rule-based decisions? Predictable output? Repeated often?

### Phase 3: ROI Quantification
- Apply formula: `Time Wasted × People × Days/Year × Hourly Cost = Annual Waste`
- Quantify every identified inefficiency in dollars
- Calculate total annual waste across all processes
- Frame against 12-month payback benchmark

### Phase 4: Prescriptive Solution
- Build opportunity matrix (value × difficulty)
- Prioritize quick wins (high value, low cost) as springboard
- Map big swings (high value, high effort) as Phase 2
- Present diagnostic deck with current state → target state → measurable delta

### Phase 5: Implementation
- Options: self-implement (if technical), partner/referral (10-15%), white-label (50% margin)
- Ensure AI Champion exists before proceeding
- Deploy change management alongside technical implementation
- Upskill workforce — don't just install tools

### Phase 6: Maintenance & Advisory
- Recurring retainer ($3-5K/mo) for maintaining implementations
- Advisory role: filter AI noise, surface relevant innovations, recommend when to upgrade
- Technology evolves fast — what you built 3 months ago needs updating
- This creates MSP-style lock-in (clients stay for years)
