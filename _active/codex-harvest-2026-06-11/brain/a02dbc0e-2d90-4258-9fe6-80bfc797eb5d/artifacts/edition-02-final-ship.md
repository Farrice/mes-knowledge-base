## The Essay

I've been judgmental of Coachella for a long time.

Not the music — some of my favorite DJs play every year. What I couldn't stand was the culture. Outfits as a whole personality. Strangers pushing past you for the photo. The festival feeling less about what was on stage than about being seen near it.

Every year I'd scroll past the posts and think: not for me.

We went to Weekend 2 for a friend's birthday. Me, my wife Jen, our two-year-old JJ in a wagon, and our best friend Ryan. Not the demographic they put on the flyer. I was there to survive the weekend. I wasn't expecting what happened Saturday night.

---

Madeon played "Shelter."

He'd retired that song. It's one of my most cherished. Jen walked out to "Shelter" at our wedding — acoustically, on keyboard, the whole song. I didn't know it was coming. It's carried a specific weight for me ever since.

I had it filed under songs I'd have to wait fifteen years to share with my son. Then the intro started building at Coachella on a Saturday evening, and Jen screamed "BABE!" — I grabbed JJ, pulled him up on my shoulders, and we got to share something I wasn't expecting.

Later, Knock2 played "dashstar\*" — the song from JJ's gender reveal. At the drop, we'd popped cannons that revealed what I already knew in my heart. Our first born was going to be a boy. Two songs that carry my life, same night, shared with my son.

But the songs weren't the lesson. The lesson was watching my son.

---

When music hit JJ, he'd want up. Arms out. Eyes on the stage. Moving with the bass. When it didn't, he'd point at the phone. Ten minutes of whatever two-year-olds watch to refill. Then he'd hear something and want back in.

Nothing about it was performance. He was there when he was there and checked out when he wasn't. Nobody grading him on the transitions.

By 10:20 he was out cold in the wagon as we walked toward Bieber. I'd been hearing the noise all weekend — social media had already decided what this set was going to be. I had no expectations. I'm not a Bieber fan. But the internet, mostly non-fans, was deciding in real time that his set was a disaster. Phoned in. Lazy. Disrespectful.

I watched enough to see what was actually happening.

Bieber walked to the front and let fans touch him. Made eye contact with people who'd been screaming his name since grade school. During "One Less Lonely Girl," Hailey pushed Billie Eilish out on stage and let Billie have her crush moment. The crowd got their old songs. Then Bieber played the new music he actually wanted to share.

He gave them all of himself. In the parts he wanted to give.

---

JJ and Bieber had the same night.

Both showed up as themselves. Gave what was real. Didn't perform anything they weren't feeling. JJ slept when he was tired. Bieber played old songs without pretending to love them and new songs without pretending the crowd wanted them.

Neither got read as present by the people watching from outside.

I went into the weekend expecting to survive a festival I'd judged from 150 miles away. I came out realizing I'd been deciding what Coachella was from posts I'd scrolled past.

Somewhere in the middle of all of it was Jen, quietly dancing to a song from our wedding with our son.

**You can't tell presence from performance from the outside.**

When's the last time you showed up somewhere and spent more energy proving you were there than being there?

---

Jen looked at me in the car Sunday and said, "Well, that worked better than expected."

I laughed. We drove home with the music down.
