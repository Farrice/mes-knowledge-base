## Executive Summary

- **Core Genius**: Writing hooks so emotionally violent that the reader *physically cannot scroll past*. Not hook templates — hook **physics**. The 8 principles are a quality checklist that separates dead-on-arrival hooks from vicious ones.
- **What Makes This Different**: Existing Luke Iha hook tools generate hooks by *type* (contrarian, confession, etc.) or *collect* them from organic sources. This extraction captures the **quality layer** — *how* to make any hook vicious regardless of its type.
- **Deployable Skills**: Write, audit, and rewrite hooks using the Vicious Hook principles. Every hook produced by any Luke Iha workflow can be run through a "vicious pass" to elevate it.
- **Hidden Knowledge Captured**: The Germanic word root insight, the "3-sentence hedging" diagnostic, the three categories of relevance as a hook architecture selector, the "consequence first / mechanism later" ordering rule.

## Genius Patterns

### GP1: The Viciousness Mandate
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Evaluates every hook by asking "Would this make someone flinch, gasp, or feel personally called out?" — anything that doesn't is rejected
- **Executable Behavior**: First line must be emotionally violent. No warm-up, no throat-clearing, no intro. Go for the gut in the first 10-20 words.
- **Deployment Context**: Every hook, every platform, every awareness level
- **Success Metric**: If you can remove the first sentence and the ad still works, your hook was filler

### GP2: The Three Relevance Categories
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Classifies all hooks into three relevance architectures before writing
- **Executable Behavior**: Choose your relevance type first: (1) Pain/Condition Callout, (2) Belief Callout, (3) General Openness/Soap Opera
- **Deployment Context**: Hook strategy selection — determines risk/reward trade-off
- **Success Metric**: Pain hooks = high reliability, low ceiling. Belief hooks = medium risk. Openness hooks = grand slam bets with high failure rate.

### GP3: Emotionally Charged Language / Germanic Word Roots
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Selects words from Anglo-Saxon/Germanic roots over Latin/French equivalents
- **Executable Behavior**: Replace: perspire → sweat, fabric → cloth, excessive → too much, insomnia → sleepless, synthetic → man-made. Deploy words like: banned, stink, naked, killing, terrifying, mortal, beg, die, sexless, lying
- **Deployment Context**: Every word in the hook (and the first 2-3 sentences of the body)
- **Success Metric**: Hook reads at a 5th-grade level with visceral, earthy, concrete language

### GP4: Open Loop Architecture
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Constructs hooks where the reader cannot make an educated guess about the answer
- **Executable Behavior**: The hook must promise information the reader CANNOT predict. If they can guess the answer from the hook alone, the loop is too loose. Test: Can a stranger guess what comes next? If yes → rewrite.
- **Deployment Context**: All copy, but especially unaware and problem-aware ads
- **Success Metric**: Zero information leakage — the hook creates pull through the copy

### GP5: Stakes & Rubbernecking
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Ensures something heavy is at risk — death, marriage, identity, health
- **Executable Behavior**: Every hook must contain implicit or explicit stakes. What bad thing could happen? What is being risked? What is being destroyed?
- **Deployment Context**: All hooks, especially story-format and confession-format
- **Success Metric**: The reader feels a compulsion to look, like passing a car wreck

### GP6: Specificity as Spice
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Adds concrete details (numbers, timeframes, body parts, named conditions) — but doesn't overdo it
- **Executable Behavior**: Treat specificity like seasoning. "26 years of marriage" beats "years of marriage." "Guest bedroom" beats "room." But don't overload.
- **Deployment Context**: Particularly powerful in story hooks and proof hooks
- **Success Metric**: Details make it feel real without feeling cluttered

### GP7: Anti-Ad Disguise
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Writes hooks that read like confessions, gossip, insider reveals, or stories — never like advertisements
- **Executable Behavior**: The hook should feel like something you stumbled onto — a story, a confession, a piece of information promising new insight. If it reads like an ad, it's dead.
- **Deployment Context**: Critical for unaware audiences; beneficial for all awareness levels
- **Success Metric**: Reader cannot predict the product from the hook alone

### GP8: Consequence First, Mechanism Later
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Leads with the consequence/pain they already care about; saves the mechanism for the body
- **Executable Behavior**: Never lead with your mechanism unless it's already widely known and believed. Lead with the consequence: "spikes cortisol" beats "causes electrical imbalance" because they already know cortisol is bad.
- **Deployment Context**: All hooks that involve a mechanism or method
- **Success Metric**: The first line references something the reader already cares about — not something they need to learn

## Hidden Knowledge

1. **The 3-Sentence Hedge Diagnostic**: Most copywriters start their *real* ad 3-4 sentences in. Luke diagnoses this as "throat clearing" — the psychology of thinking you have time to warm up. The fix: delete your first 3 sentences and see if the ad improves.
2. **The 70/80 Rule**: Luke spends 70-80% of his writing time on the hook alone. Everything else is secondary.
3. **The Prediction Test**: "If they can make an educated guess and feel confident in their guess of what you're going to say, then they have no business to actually read the ad." This is the litmus test for open loop quality.
4. **Relevance ≠ Pain Callout**: Most people interpret "relevance" as "call out their pain." Luke distinguishes three categories — pain, belief, and general openness — and treats general openness as the highest-risk/highest-reward play.
5. **Germanic Word Selection as AI Prompt**: Luke explicitly suggests copying his word lists into AI and asking for "the most vicious possible words" — he uses AI as a word-level upgrade tool, not a writing tool.
6. **The Transgressive Edge**: The best hooks feel "borderline dangerous, like you're not supposed to see this story." They touch topics others avoid. This is not recklessness — it's calibrated boundary-pressing.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

### Exemplar 1: The Gay Husband Hook
- **Context**: Unaware-style story hook for a health/wellness product
- **The Example**: "My husband came out as gay after 26 years of marriage and it nearly killed me."
- **What makes this excellent**: Contains ALL 8 principles — vicious first line (no hedging), emotionally charged ("gay," "killed"), open loop (what happened?), stakes (death, marriage destroyed), specificity ("26 years"), anti-ad disguise (reads like a confession), consequence first (no product hint), and general openness relevance

### Exemplar 2: The Identity Wake-Up Hook
- **Context**: Unaware-style beauty/skincare ad
- **The Example**: "I went to sleep as a black woman and I woke up as a white woman."
- **What makes this excellent**: Pure transgressive openness — unbelievable, visceral, identity-shattering. You MUST know the story. Zero product leakage.

### Exemplar 3: The Sexless Marriage Hook
- **Context**: Unaware-style lifestyle/relationship ad
- **The Example**: "When I cleaned out my father's guest bedroom, I realized I inherited his sexless marriage."
- **What makes this excellent**: Open loop + stakes + specificity ("guest bedroom") + charged language ("sexless") + anti-ad disguise (reads as confession) + consequence first

### Anti-Exemplar: The Mechanism-First Mistake
- **What mediocre looks like**: "This weird morning habit causes electrical imbalance in your body"
- **Why it fails**: Leads with a mechanism ("electrical imbalance") the reader doesn't know or care about. No existing relevance. Violates GP8 (consequence first) and GP1 (viciousness) — it's polite and informational, not visceral.

## Signature Moves

1. **The Hedge Execution**: Delete the first 3 sentences of any ad. If the 4th sentence is the real start → those 3 lines were throat-clearing. → **Deploy when**: Reviewing any first draft
2. **The Flinch Test**: Read the hook aloud. If it doesn't make you slightly uncomfortable → rewrite. → **Deploy when**: Final hook validation pass
3. **The Germanic Swap**: Post-draft, identify every Latinate/French word in the hook and swap for the Anglo-Saxon/Germanic equivalent → **Deploy when**: Word-level polish phase
4. **The Prediction Block**: Show the hook to someone. Ask "What do you think comes next?" If they have a guess → the open loop leaks → **Deploy when**: Testing hook tightness
5. **The Consequence Flip**: If any hook leads with a mechanism → flip it so the consequence comes first → **Deploy when**: Health, supplement, and mechanism-based offers

## Expert-Specific Quality Rubric

| Criterion | Score 4 (Acceptable) | Score 7 (Good) | Score 10 (Savant) |
|-----------|---------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Viciousness** | Has a clear first line, but it's safe | First line provokes curiosity and mild emotional response | First line makes the reader flinch, gasp, or feel personally called out |
| **Word Charge** | Uses clear language, no jargon | Some emotionally charged words, mostly concrete | Every word is visceral, earthy, Germanic — language from the gut |
| **Open Loop Tightness** | Creates curiosity but answer is guessable | Strong curiosity, hard to predict | Impossible to predict what comes next; reader dragged through the copy |
| **Stakes** | Something matters but it's abstract (e.g., "success") | Something concrete is at risk (health, money) | Life, death, identity, marriage — something that makes you rubber-neck |
| **Anti-Ad Feel** | Doesn't scream "ad" but feels a little polished | Reads like a story or confession with slight marketing tell | Indistinguishable from organic gossip, confession, or breaking news |
