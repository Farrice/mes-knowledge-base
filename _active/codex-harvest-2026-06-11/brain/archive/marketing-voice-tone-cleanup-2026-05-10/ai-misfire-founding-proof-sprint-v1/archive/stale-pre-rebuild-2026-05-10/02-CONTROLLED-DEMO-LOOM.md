# Controlled Demo Loom

## Purpose

Create one proof asset before asking for money.

The viewer should think:

> I have an AI output like this. I should send it.

## Demo Rule

Call this a controlled demo, not a client case study.

Do not imply:

- client results
- revenue outcomes
- guaranteed automation success
- private client data

## Screen Setup

Open four panes:

1. The normal prompt.
2. The polished-but-wrong output.
3. The three guess points.
4. The first agent-ready fix sketch.

## Controlled Demo Input

```text
Prompt:

Write a follow-up DM to a founder-led consultant who asked about using AI to improve their content workflow.

Goal:

Get them to send one failed AI output, prompt, SOP, workflow, or content process for review.

Tone:

Clear, useful, not hypey.
```

## Polished-But-Wrong Output

```text
Hey [Name], great connecting with you.

I help consultants use AI to produce more content without losing their voice.

If you are interested, I would be happy to hop on a quick call and show you how AI can transform your workflow.

Let me know if you want to chat.
```

## Three Guess Points

### Guess Point 1: It Assumed The Goal Was A Call

Problem:

The desired action is not a broad strategy call. The desired action is one submitted artifact.

Why it matters:

A call ask creates friction before proof. The buyer does not yet know whether the diagnosis is real.

Operating fix:

Define the CTA hierarchy:

1. ask for one misfire
2. return a 3-bullet diagnosis
3. bridge to paid audit only if the problem is real

### Guess Point 2: It Used Generic AI Benefits

Problem:

"Produce more content" and "without losing your voice" sound like commodity AI content consulting.

Why it matters:

The buyer pain is not "I want AI." The pain is "AI is close but still wrong enough that I have to rescue it."

Operating fix:

Use buyer pain language:

> Where is AI already close to useful, but still wrong enough that someone senior has to correct it?

### Guess Point 3: It Missed Authority Boundaries

Problem:

The message implies broad transformation before seeing the workflow.

Why it matters:

That creates false confidence. The right move is diagnostic humility.

Operating fix:

Add scope boundary:

> Send one example. I will tell you whether it is a prompt issue, SOP issue, or deeper operating-layer issue.

## Rewritten Output

```text
Hey [Name], your point about AI getting close but still needing review is exactly the pattern I am mapping.

Quick question:

Where is AI already useful in your workflow, but still wrong enough that you or someone senior has to correct it before it can be trusted?

If you send one prompt, SOP, workflow, or failed output, I can point out the first three places it is being forced to guess.
```

## First Agent-Ready Work Primitive Sketch

```text
Workflow: AI Misfire intake DM

Purpose:
Move a relevant buyer from vague AI frustration to one submitted artifact.

Inputs:
- buyer segment
- public context
- artifact type if known
- relevant pain phrase

Output:
A short DM asking for one concrete artifact.

Authority:
Do not promise transformation before seeing the workflow.
Do not ask for a broad strategy call first.
Do not diagnose more than three points for free.

Quality test:
The message should make the buyer think, "I know exactly which output I could send."
```

## Five-Minute Loom Script

### 0:00-0:20 Hook

```text
If your AI output looks finished but you still have to rescue it, the model may not be the problem.

The problem may be that the work is not clear enough for AI to execute without guessing.

I call this an AI misfire.
```

### 0:20-0:50 Who This Is For

```text
This is for founder-led experts, consultants, small agencies, and service businesses already using AI inside content, sales, proposals, delivery, or internal workflows.

You are not starting from zero.

You are already using AI, but the output still needs expert judgment before it can be trusted.
```

### 0:50-1:50 Show The Normal Prompt

```text
Here is a normal prompt.

It looks reasonable to a human because the human fills in the missing context automatically.

But AI does not know the real goal, the buyer's current state, the source of truth, the approval rule, or when to stop.

So it produces something polished.

But it guesses.
```

### 1:50-3:10 Mark The Guess Points

```text
This output is not terrible.

That is why it is dangerous.

It sounds professional, but it made three unauthorized decisions.

It assumed the next step was a call.
It used generic AI benefit language.
It implied transformation before seeing the workflow.

Those are not style issues. Those are operating issues.
```

### 3:10-4:20 Show The Fix

```text
The fix is not "make the prompt longer."

The fix is to make the work more legible:

- purpose
- inputs
- output standard
- authority boundaries
- source of truth
- decision rules
- quality tests

That is what turns a vague task into a workflow AI can execute without guessing.
```

### 4:20-5:00 CTA

```text
That is what I map inside AI Misfire Founding Proof.

You send one prompt, SOP, workflow, or failed output.

I map where AI is guessing, what judgment or source of truth is missing, and what the first agent-ready version should include.

If your AI output looks finished but still needs rescue, send one example.
```

## Short 90-Second Version

```text
If your AI output looks finished but still needs rescue, the model may not be the problem.

A human reads a messy prompt or SOP and fills in the blanks from experience.

AI fills in the blanks from probability.

Those are not the same thing.

That is why the output can sound polished and still feel wrong.

It did the task, but missed the work.

Inside AI Misfire Founding Proof, you send me one prompt, SOP, workflow, content process, sales follow-up, proposal process, or failed AI output.

I map where AI was forced to guess, what operating meaning was missing, and whether the fix is a prompt tweak, SOP cleanup, or agent-ready work primitive.

If this is happening in your business, send one example.

Messy is fine.
```
