# Social Domination Playbook

## Operating Principle

Domination here does not mean posting everywhere at maximum volume.

It means becoming the most specific person in the buyer's mind for this problem:

> AI output looks finished, but still needs expert rescue.

The goal is to own a phrase, a moment, and a diagnostic behavior.

Phrase:

> AI did the task. It did not understand the work.

Moment:

> "That is not what we mean."

Behavior:

> Send one misfire first.

## Daily 45-Minute Operating Loop

### 0-5 Minutes: Signal Check

Review:

- yesterday's comments
- DMs
- submitted artifacts
- saved buyer phrases
- prospect queue
- one relevant market signal

Pick one lane for the day.

### 5-20 Minutes: Publish Or Engage

If posting:

- publish one post
- stay present for 30 minutes after if possible
- reply to every relevant comment

If not posting:

- leave 5-8 thoughtful comments on ICP or adjacent market posts
- use a useful distinction, not a pitch

### 20-35 Minutes: Manual DMs

Send 3-5 human-reviewed DMs only when there is a reason:

- they commented
- they posted about AI/workflows/content/ops
- their business visibly depends on expert judgment
- their content hints at AI frustration or operational complexity

No automation.

### 35-45 Minutes: Capture And Route

Log:

- buyer phrase
- pain signal
- workflow mentioned
- CTA used
- next follow-up
- paid audit fit
- proof candidate status

Then choose tomorrow's lane.

## Comment Strategy

Comment types:

1. **Diagnostic comment**
   - "The question I would ask is: where did AI make a decision it was never authorized to make?"

2. **Language comment**
   - "When people say 'AI sounds generic,' I usually hear 'the system does not have our customer language yet.'"

3. **Workflow comment**
   - "Before automating this, I would map the exact point where a human still has to rescue the output."

4. **Proof comment**
   - "The fastest proof is not a deck. It is one before/after workflow teardown."

5. **Boundary comment**
   - "Some workflows are not ready for AI yet. That is not failure; that is useful scoping."

Comment rule:

> Be useful enough that the reader would want you to diagnose their workflow.

## LinkedIn Rules

- First 50 words must name a felt problem.
- No links in post body unless required.
- One CTA per post.
- Reply fast in first 30 minutes when possible.
- Ask for one artifact, not a call.
- Do not overuse AI jargon.
- Alternate between recognition, diagnosis, proof, and offer.

## Instagram Rules

Instagram is for recognition and shareability.

Best formats:

- 7-slide carousel
- 30-45 second talking-head reel
- screenshot-style diagnostic note
- "AI phrase vs buyer phrase" carousel

Carousel structure:

1. Strong recognition hook.
2. One felt moment.
3. Why it happens.
4. What it costs.
5. The diagnostic question.
6. The first fix.
7. CTA: send one misfire.

## YouTube/Loom Rules

YouTube/Loom is for proof.

Best assets:

- 5-minute AI Misfire Map demo
- 90-second short version
- before/after teardown
- "prompt vs primitive" walkthrough
- "human-readable vs agent-readable SOP" demo

Rule:

> Do not explain the category for five minutes. Show the misfire in the first minute.

## Newsletter Rules

Newsletter is for authority and durable trust.

Best structure:

1. One market shift.
2. One buyer moment.
3. One useful distinction.
4. One small diagnostic.
5. One CTA.

Example issue:

> AI is moving into workflows. The problem is that workflows were written for humans who already know the work.

## Blog Rules

Blog is for search/referral and proof storage.

Best topics:

- What is an AI misfire?
- Why better prompts stop working.
- Human-readable SOP vs agent-readable workflow.
- How to find where AI guessed.
- What to audit before building an AI agent.
- AI audit vs AI Misfire Map.

## Proof-Demo Process

1. Choose one artifact:
   - failed output
   - prompt
   - SOP
   - sales follow-up
   - proposal
   - content workflow

2. Mark three guess-points:
   - missing audience state
   - missing source of truth
   - missing rule/boundary/example

3. Translate each into:
   - what AI guessed
   - why it matters
   - what rule would fix it

4. Show a before/after:
   - before: vague instruction
   - after: clearer work primitive section

5. End with:
   - "If you have one like this, send it."

## Weekly Review Questions

- Which phrase made people stop?
- Which post produced the most specific DMs?
- Which buyer segment had the clearest workflow pain?
- Which objection came up more than once?
- Which workflow type looks easiest to turn into a proof demo?
- Did the content attract buyers or only AI peers?
- Did any CTA create action, not just praise?

## Anti-Slop Rules

Reject content if:

- it sounds like any AI consultant could have written it
- the first line could fit any trend
- the post explains too much before naming a buyer moment
- the CTA asks for a call before giving proof
- it uses source stats as the whole post
- it makes claims the offer has not earned yet
