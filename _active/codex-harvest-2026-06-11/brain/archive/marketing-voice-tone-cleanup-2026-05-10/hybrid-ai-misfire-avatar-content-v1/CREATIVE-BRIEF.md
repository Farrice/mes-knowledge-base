# Creative Brief

## Campaign Name

AI Misfire Map

## Strategic Role

Create market recognition and signal for AI Misfire Express by making founder-led experts feel the specific pain of AI output that looks finished but still needs expert rescue.

This is not an awareness campaign about AI.

This is a recognition campaign for one painful moment:

> "That is not what we mean."

## Big Idea

> AI did the task. It did not understand the work.

## Enemy

Faster wrong output.

Secondary enemies:

- generic prompt packs
- AI automation before diagnosis
- tools layered on unclear work
- "AI transformation" language
- polished outputs that hide weak judgment
- documentation that humans understand but AI cannot execute

## Audience

Primary:

> Founder-led experts paying the AI cleanup tax.

They already use AI. They do not need to be convinced AI exists. They need to understand why it keeps producing almost-right work that still needs them.

Secondary:

- AI-forward agencies that need a better diagnostic before building.
- Creator-consultants turning expertise into repeatable systems.
- Premium local/service businesses with repeated intake, follow-up, proposal, or delivery workflows.

## Buyer Tension

They want leverage without losing judgment.

They want AI speed without generic output.

They want to delegate without becoming the quality-control bottleneck forever.

They want practical help, but they do not want to sound like they got tricked by AI hype.

## Single-Minded Proposition

If your AI output looks done but still needs expert rescue, send one example and we will map where AI was forced to guess.

## Proof Logic

Use proof in this order:

1. Familiar moment: "It looks finished, but I still cannot send it."
2. Diagnosis: the AI guessed because the work depended on invisible judgment.
3. Mechanism: turn the correction into a rule, example, source of truth, boundary, or quality test.
4. Small proof: before/after misfire map.
5. Offer: AI Misfire Express.

## What We Want The Reader To Think

Before:

> "AI is useful, but it is still weirdly unreliable."

After:

> "The output keeps failing because the work behind the task is not clear enough yet."

Action:

> "I should send Farrice one output and see where it guessed."

## Tone

Oracle/Catalyst.

Oracle:

- precise
- calm
- pattern-aware
- not hype-driven
- willing to name the hidden issue

Catalyst:

- practical
- energizing
- specific
- makes the next move feel doable

Voice should feel:

- conversational
- exact
- grounded
- slightly surgical
- warm but not fluffy
- premium without being precious

## Visual Direction

Use:

- diagnostic markup
- before/after frames
- margin notes
- high-contrast text blocks
- clean document screenshots
- prompt/output teardown visuals
- simple arrows from "guess" to "missing rule"
- restrained palette with accent color for misfire points

Avoid:

- robots
- cyberpunk imagery
- glowing brains
- generic AI grids
- gradient blobs
- stock photos of people pointing at screens
- dark "future of work" mood boards
- overdesigned dashboards with no real work shown

## Creative Territories

### 1. The Almost-Right Draft

The dangerous output is not obviously bad. It is polished enough to pass until an expert reads it.

Use for:

- LinkedIn flagship posts
- Instagram carousels
- Loom intro
- short reels

### 2. The Correction Is The Data

Every founder edit points to a missing rule, source, example, boundary, or quality test.

Use for:

- proof demos
- newsletter
- blog
- follow-up DMs

### 3. Human-Readable Is Not Agent-Readable

Most SOPs are written for people who already know the work.

Use for:

- educational posts
- YouTube/Loom proof
- carousel
- paid audit explanation

### 4. No Call First. Send The Misfire First.

Buyers need a low-friction proof path.

Use for:

- featured asset
- CTA
- DMs
- outreach

## Messaging Rules

Lead with buyer language:

- "It is close, but I still cannot send it."
- "That is not what we mean."
- "It missed the point."
- "I keep fixing the same thing."

Then introduce mechanism:

- "where AI guessed"
- "what judgment was missing"
- "what source of truth it needed"
- "what rule or example should exist"

Save expert language for warm buyers:

- context engineering
- semantic document library
- work primitive
- agent-ready operating system

## Campaign CTA

Primary:

> DM `GUESS` with one prompt, SOP, workflow, content process, sales follow-up, proposal, or failed AI output.

Short:

> No call first. Send the misfire first.

Paid bridge:

> If this is bigger than a prompt tweak, I can map the full misfire pattern inside AI Misfire Express.

## Offer Role

AI Misfire Express is the diagnostic before the build.

It is not:

- a generic AI audit
- a prompt pack
- a full automation build
- a giant AI transformation plan

It is:

- one artifact
- one diagnosis
- one map of where AI guessed
- one recommendation: prompt fix, SOP rewrite, work primitive, or not ready to automate

## Mandatory Quality Bar

Every asset must answer:

> What does the reader get from this besides knowing Farrice is smart?

Acceptable answers:

- a distinction
- a diagnosis
- a checklist
- a sharper question
- a belief shift
- a minimum action
- proof they can inspect

Reject:

- clever but non-useful posts
- abstract AI takes
- generic AI-consultant language
- claims without proof
- posts that explain the mechanism before the buyer feels the problem
