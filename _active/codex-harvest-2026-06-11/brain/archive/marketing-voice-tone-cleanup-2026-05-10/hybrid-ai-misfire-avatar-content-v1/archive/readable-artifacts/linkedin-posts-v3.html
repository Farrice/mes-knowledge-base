Post 2 - Ethical Persuasion And Customer Language
Trust builderCustomer language41/50

I had this uncomfortable thought the other day:

"I just manipulated my two-year-old son, and I do not know how to feel about it."

Not in some dark, evil way.

In the normal parenting way that nobody wants to call marketing because it makes the room feel weird.

He was overtired. He needed to nap. We recently moved him from the crib to the bed, so the bed still has play energy attached to it.

If you have ever negotiated with a toddler, you know the vibe.

You are not giving a TED Talk.

You are trying to help a tiny human do the thing that will make their life better in about 11 minutes.

And what hit me was this:

I could not say the pretty thing.

I had to say the thing in the way he could actually receive it.

That is where the moral tension lives.

If you will not say it the way the person needs to hear it, you may not care as much as you think.

That applies to parenting. It applies to marketing. And it definitely applies to the AI outputs we keep calling "almost there."

Alternate hooks

  1. Parenting is the most uncomfortable marketing class I have ever taken.
  2. The line between manipulation and empathy is thinner than most marketers want to admit.
  3. My toddler taught me why most AI marketing sounds fake.

Post 4 - A Plan Is Not The Work
Personal training bridgeAuthority39/50

I spent years watching people ask for plans that were not going to save them.

Workout plans. Meal plans. Morning routines. Accountability systems.

Some of them were good plans.

That was not the issue.

The issue was the person trying to follow the plan on the day they were tired, embarrassed, stressed, impatient, and quietly looking for a way out.

That was the work.

Not the PDF.

The human inside the PDF.

I see the same thing happening with AI now.

Stop treating the prompt like the work. The prompt is the visible request. The work is the invisible operating context underneath it.

Post 5 - Fingerprints Are The Moat
TasteAnti-slop41/50

Metal Gear Solid taught me something most AI content still gets wrong.

If you never played it, stick with me.

There is a boss fight where the game makes you solve the problem outside the expected interface.

Not a better weapon. Not a better combo. Not "try harder."

You literally had to change the frame.

That is what most AI-assisted work is missing right now.

It can imitate the category. It can produce the post, sales email, proposal, SOP, or carousel.

But it often removes the weird little detail that makes the work feel authored.

If AI removes the fingerprints from your work, it did not make you faster. It made you easier to forget.
