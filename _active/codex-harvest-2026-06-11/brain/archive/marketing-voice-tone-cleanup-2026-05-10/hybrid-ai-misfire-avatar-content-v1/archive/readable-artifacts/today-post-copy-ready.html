Publish first Meta-proof AI Misfire Express

Flagship Post: Final Polish

I almost published the exact kind of AI content I warn people about.

I was reading the LinkedIn posts for this AI Misfire Express offer.

They were clean.

Structured.

Useful.

The sort of post that gets a polite nod and dies quietly.

That annoyed me because it was the whole problem in miniature.

The content was not bad.

It was worse.

It was finished-looking.

It had the thesis:

"Your AI keeps guessing because the judgment behind the work is trapped in your head."

True.

Also not enough.

Because the draft did not carry the pressure behind that sentence.

It did not show the founder at 10:47 p.m. fixing an output that was supposed to save them time.

It did not show the little taste decision.

The customer phrase that matters.

The proof that feels too weak.

The line you delete because it sounds like a person who read your About page once and then got promoted to CMO.

It did not sound like me.

And if it does not sound like me, why would anyone trust me to help them find the invisible judgment in their own work?

That is the misfire.

You think AI made the content generic.

But a lot of the time, the content is generic because the real operating rules never made it into the system.

AI did not erase the fingerprints.

It was never handed them.

So the fix is not "make it sound more human."

That is too vague.

The fix is to find the missing judgment:

  • What would you never say?
  • What customer phrase matters?
  • What proof is not strong enough?
  • Where would you pause before deciding?
  • What detail feels too specific, but is actually the signal?

This is what I mean by an AI misfire.

AI did the task.

It did not understand the work.

And weirdly, my own draft proved it.

If you have one of these - a prompt, SOP, proposal, follow-up, content draft, workflow, whatever - send me the messy version.

I will show you the place where the system started guessing.

Because clean is not proof.

Sometimes clean is where the real work got hidden.

Luke + Rory Punch Pass

Insight vector: You do not have generic content because AI wrote it. AI wrote generic content because your real operating rules were never made visible.

Rory perception shift: Clean no longer means quality. In an AI-saturated feed, clean can be camouflage for missing judgment.

Mechanism name: The Finished-Looking Misfire.

Metric shift: Do not measure words generated. Measure rescue minutes, trust-before-send, and repeated corrections.
