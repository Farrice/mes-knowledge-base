## Angle 1: The Duolingo Lesson

**Brandjack:** Duolingo  
**Theme:** Recognizable voice  
**Recommendation:** Strongest option for today

Duolingo did not become memorable by sounding more professional.

It became memorable because it had a point of view.

A little weird.

A little too specific.

Obviously itself.

That is the part most people miss when they use AI for content.

They ask the system to make the draft cleaner.

But clean is not the same as recognizable.

I learned that the uncomfortable way with my own writing.

I had a post about AI content that was technically fine.

Clear thesis.

Good structure.

Useful point.

And I hated it.

Because it sounded like a smart consultant explaining a problem from a safe distance.

Not like a dad trying to build a business in the margins while his two-year-old teaches him, in real time, that attention is not optional.

That is the part AI could not invent.

JJ having an outburst.

Me realizing cheap dopamine had become a parenting shortcut.

The pressure of needing to put money on the table.

The rule I keep coming back to:

When he is awake, be present.

When he sleeps, execute.

That is not a personal anecdote.

That is the operating system.

And most AI content fails because the operating system never makes it into the prompt.

It gets the task.

It misses the taste.

It writes the sentence.

It misses the life behind the sentence.

So the real question is not:

"How do I make this sound more human?"

The better question is:

"What part of my judgment did I leave out because I was moving too fast to notice it?"

That is where the voice lives.

That is where the trust lives.

That is where the misfire starts.

### Why This Works

Duolingo gives the post instant recognition without turning the post into a brand summary. The payload is still Farrice: presence, fatherhood, AI craft, and recognizable judgment.

## Angle 2: The Apple Lesson

**Brandjack:** Apple  
**Theme:** Taste over utility  
**Best for:** Premium positioning

Apple never won because it explained technology better.

It won because it made technology feel like taste.

That is the difference most people miss with AI.

They think the job is to make the output more useful.

Useful is the floor.

The real work is making the output feel like it came from someone with judgment.

Someone with a life.

A standard.

A reason they would say this and not that.

I had to relearn that from a two-year-old.

A few days ago, JJ had one of the biggest outbursts I have seen from him.

And it forced me to look at how often I let convenience pretend to be strategy.

Screens.

Cheap dopamine.

Work pressure.

The very real need to make money and still be present.

It is easy to call that sacrifice.

Sometimes it is.

Sometimes it is just absence with a better name.

That hit me because I see the same thing in AI content.

People call it efficiency.

But sometimes efficiency is where the fingerprints disappeared.

The little phrase only you would use.

The tension you are actually living through.

The taste decision nobody else can outsource.

The human cost behind the clean framework.

AI can help you move faster.

But if it helps you skip the moment where your judgment forms, you did not save time.

You removed the reason anyone should trust you.

That is the standard I am holding my own work to now:

If the draft is clean but unrecognizable, it is not done.

### Why This Works

Apple frames the work as taste and judgment, which supports premium creative-strategist positioning. It is slightly more polished and less raw than Angle 1.

## Angle 3: The Netflix Lesson

**Brandjack:** Netflix  
**Theme:** Re-earned attention  
**Best for:** Retention and attention angle

Netflix understands something most AI content misses:

Attention is not earned once.

It has to be re-earned every few seconds.

That is not just true for shows.

It is true for writing.

Parenting.

Marketing.

Any moment where another human being can feel whether you are actually there.

A few days ago, JJ had a massive outburst.

And I had one of those dad moments where the lesson was not cute.

It was inconvenient.

I saw how quickly screens can become the easy button.

I saw how cheap dopamine hits different when it is shaping a two-year-old's attention instead of numbing an adult's.

And I had to admit something I did not love admitting:

Sometimes I let the pressure to build the business become an excuse to be less present.

That has been sitting with me.

Because the same pattern shows up in AI work.

We outsource the hard attention.

Then act surprised when the output has no pulse.

The system gives us structure.

A hook.

A neat little framework.

A sentence that sounds like it belongs on LinkedIn.

But it does not know what we were avoiding.

It does not know the guilt.

The standard.

The line we would never say.

The detail that feels too personal but is actually the proof.

That is the part we have to bring.

AI can draft.

It cannot be present on our behalf.

And the more I do this work, the more I think presence is the real creative advantage.

Not polish.

Presence.

Because presence is where judgment comes from.

### Why This Works

Netflix turns the post toward attention and retention. It is the best option if the next content arc is about presence as a creative advantage.
