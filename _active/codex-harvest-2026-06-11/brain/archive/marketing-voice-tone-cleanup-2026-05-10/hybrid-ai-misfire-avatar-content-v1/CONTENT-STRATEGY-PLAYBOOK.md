# Content Strategy Playbook

## Content Thesis

Founder-led experts do not need more AI noise. They need to see why AI keeps producing almost-right work and how to make one workflow clear enough for AI to support without guessing.

The content should educate the market while collecting signal.

Signal means:

- comments naming a real misfire
- DMs with a specific output or workflow
- submitted prompts/SOPs/workflows
- questions about audit price
- offers to let you use anonymized proof
- paid audit interest

## Semantic Lanes

### Lane 1: AI Misfire Recognition

Reader state:

> "AI is close, but still wrong."

Reader payoff:

- They can name the problem.

Angles:

- The output looked finished.
- "That is not what we mean."
- AI did the task, not the work.
- The dangerous draft is the polished one.

CTA:

> Send the last output you had to rewrite.

### Lane 2: Founder Judgment Capture

Reader state:

> "It only works when I fix it."

Reader payoff:

- They see their correction as reusable data.

Angles:

- The correction is the data.
- Your taste is a system.
- The founder's pause is a rule.
- AI cannot apply judgment it cannot see.

CTA:

> Show me where you keep correcting it.

### Lane 3: Customer Language As Source Of Truth

Reader state:

> "AI makes us sound generic."

Reader payoff:

- They see language as infrastructure, not decoration.

Angles:

- Generic output comes from generic source material.
- AI cannot hear customers unless the system contains their words.
- Buyer language beats prompt tricks.

CTA:

> Send one prompt that keeps flattening your voice.

### Lane 4: Agent-Readable Work

Reader state:

> "How do I fix this?"

Reader payoff:

- They understand the difference between task, prompt, SOP, and work primitive.

Angles:

- Prompt vs. primitive.
- Human-readable is not agent-readable.
- Source of truth before automation.
- Work needs rules, examples, counterexamples, and stopping points.

CTA:

> I will tell you if this needs a prompt, SOP, or primitive.

### Lane 5: Proof-First Diagnostic

Reader state:

> "Can Farrice actually help?"

Reader payoff:

- They see the diagnostic in action.

Angles:

- Before/after misfire map.
- Three places the AI guessed.
- The hidden rule behind the correction.
- Why one workflow is not ready for automation yet.

CTA:

> DM `GUESS` for the AI Misfire Map.

### Lane 6: Market Compass

Reader state:

> "What is actually happening with AI at work?"

Reader payoff:

- They get a grounded 2026 interpretation.

Angles:

- AI adoption is moving into workflows.
- Governance and context are the bottlenecks.
- Agents create more need for human judgment, not less.
- The audit market is growing because buyers do not trust vague builds.

CTA:

> If this is showing up in your workflow, send one example.

## Hook Rules

Use:

- "The most dangerous AI output is not the bad one."
- "AI did the task. It did not understand the work."
- "A human fills in blanks from experience. AI fills them in from probability."
- "The correction is the data."
- "Your SOP may be human-readable, but not agent-readable."

Avoid:

- "AI is changing everything."
- "The future of work is here."
- "Unlock the power of AI."
- "10x your productivity."
- "Semantic document infrastructure."

Weekly:

- language worth adding to the bank
- whether to keep, narrow, or pivot

## Decision Rules

Continue the current strategy if:

- 3+ qualified artifacts submitted in 14 days.
- 1+ paid audit closed or seriously discussed.
- Comments include real buyer language, not just peer praise.

Adjust language if:

- posts get likes but no DMs or submissions.
- people understand AI but not the offer.
- comments focus on tools instead of misfires.

Narrow the buyer if:

- one segment sends most artifacts.
- agencies respond faster than founder-led experts.
- local services show cash readiness sooner.

Pause expansion if:

- no one can name a real workflow.
- the content attracts only AI builders, not buyers.
- the offer feels like prompt cleanup.
