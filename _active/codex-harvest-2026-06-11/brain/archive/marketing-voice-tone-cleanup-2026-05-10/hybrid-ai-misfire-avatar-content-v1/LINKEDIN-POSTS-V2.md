It is data.

When you say:

"We would never say it that way."

you are not just editing.

You are exposing the rule the system did not have.

## Post 3: Prompt Vs. Primitive

Lane: Agent-Readable Work  
Value: framework  
CTA: prompt or primitive diagnosis

A prompt asks AI to do the task.

A work primitive explains the work.

That distinction matters.

A prompt says:

"Write a follow-up email."

A work primitive says:

- who the follow-up is for
- what the person already believes
- what source of truth controls the message
- what the AI may infer
- what it must not invent
- what a good response must include
- what would make it unusable
- when the AI should stop and ask a human

The first one produces text.

The second one gives AI operating meaning.

If you have a prompt that keeps disappointing you, DM `GUESS`.

I will tell you whether it needs a prompt fix or a clearer work primitive.

## Post 5: Human-Readable Is Not Agent-Readable

Lane: Agent-Readable Work  
Value: useful distinction  
CTA: send one SOP

Most SOPs are written for humans who already know the work.

That is why AI misreads them.

A human reads:

"Follow up with the lead."

and automatically knows:

- which offer they asked about
- what objections matter
- what tone fits
- what not to promise
- when to escalate
- where the source of truth lives

AI sees the same step and fills in the blanks.

Not from experience.

From probability.

That is how a clean SOP can still produce messy AI output.

The fix is not prettier documentation.

The fix is making the work clear enough that AI knows what it can decide, what it cannot invent, and when it should stop.

If you have an SOP a person understands but AI keeps flattening, send it over.

I will show you where the ambiguity lives.

## Post 7: Do Not Build The Agent Yet

Lane: Market Compass  
Value: contrarian filter  
CTA: send one workflow

Do not build the agent yet.

Not if the workflow is still unclear.

Not if the source of truth is implied.

Not if no one has defined what the AI is allowed to decide.

Not if the output still depends on one expert quietly fixing everything at the end.

An agent on unclear work does not create leverage.

It creates faster ambiguity.

The better first move is smaller:

Find one place AI is already almost useful.

Then ask:

- where did it guess?
- what did it need to know?
- what rule was missing?
- what source should have controlled the output?
- what should have triggered a human check?

If those answers are clear, build.

If not, diagnose first.
