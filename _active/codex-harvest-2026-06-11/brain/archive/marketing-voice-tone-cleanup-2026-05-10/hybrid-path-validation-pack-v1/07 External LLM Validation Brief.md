# 07 External LLM Validation Brief

## Copy/Paste Prompt For External Validation

You are reviewing a business strategy and validation pack for Farrice Cain.

Do not rubber-stamp the strategy. Your job is to pressure-test it for cash speed, buyer comprehension, positioning clarity, proof gaps, content quality, and execution realism.

## Context

Farrice is choosing the Hybrid path:

> Creative Strategist plus AI Operating Partner.

The public wedge:

> customer language, founder judgment, positioning, content, and buyer-readable creative strategy.

The backend moat:

> agent-ready operating systems, semantic document libraries, AI-consumable work primitives, source-of-truth rules, quality gates, and workflow protocols.

The near-term cash/signal offer:

> AI Misfire Express.

Plain-English offer:

> Send one failed AI output, prompt, SOP, workflow, content process, proposal, sales follow-up, or delivery step that is close but still wrong. Farrice diagnoses where AI is being forced to guess and maps the missing judgment, rules, examples, source of truth, and quality checks.

## Primary Buyer Hypothesis

Primary buyer:

> Founder-led experts paying the AI cleanup tax.

Examples:

- coaches
- consultants
- creator-consultants
- expert businesses
- small agencies
- AI-forward solo operators
- premium local/service businesses with repeated sales, proposal, intake, or delivery workflows

They already use AI, but the outputs still require founder rescue.

## Market Thesis

2026 evidence suggests AI adoption is moving from experimentation into workflow redesign, but trust, context, governance, human judgment, and proof are now bottlenecks.

Buyer translation:

> AI is already in your workflow, but you still do not trust the output.

Offer translation:

> Your AI keeps guessing because the judgment behind the work is still trapped in your head.

## What Is Already Strong

- The Hybrid path is clearer than pure AI documentation and more differentiated than pure creative strategy.
- The first offer is concrete enough to test quickly.
- The buyer pain is real if framed as "AI output that looks finished but still needs rescue."
- The backend semantic library is a plausible moat if proven through before/after examples.
- The best first proof story is Farrice catching weak AI-assisted content in his own work.

## Weak Spots To Challenge

Please challenge these directly:

1. Is "founder-led experts" too broad for fast cash?
2. Which buyer segment has the highest probability of paying in the next 72 hours?
3. Does "AI Misfire Express" sound premium enough?
4. Is the $500-$1,000 diagnostic price believable without external case studies?
5. Is the offer distinct from prompt consulting and AI automation agencies?
6. Does the LinkedIn post direction create enough trust, tension, and proof?
7. Is the semantic library backend useful now, or should it stay hidden until demand is proven?
8. What is the biggest reason this could fail?
9. What is the fastest honest proof asset to create?
10. What exact 14-day validation sprint would you recommend?

## Required Output

Return:

1. Verdict: proceed, adjust, or pivot.
2. Strongest buyer segment.
3. Strongest first offer name and price.
4. Strongest one-sentence positioning.
5. Main proof gap.
6. Three things to remove or simplify.
7. Three things to strengthen.
8. Revised 14-day validation plan.
9. First LinkedIn post recommendation.
10. Red-team warnings.

## Scoring Rubric

Score each 1-10:

- speed to cash
- buyer clarity
- differentiation
- proof strength
- content resonance
- operational feasibility for 1-3 hours/day
- long-term moat
- risk of sounding generic
- risk of overbuilding
- overall path quality

## Boundaries

No external publishing, outreach, scraping, profile edits, paid tools, or automated engagement should be recommended unless explicitly approved by Farrice.

Assume the first sprint must be human-reviewed and manual.
