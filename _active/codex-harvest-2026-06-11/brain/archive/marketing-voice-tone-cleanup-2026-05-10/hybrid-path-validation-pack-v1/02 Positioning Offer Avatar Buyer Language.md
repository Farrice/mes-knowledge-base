# 02 Positioning Offer Avatar Buyer Language

## Positioning Verdict

Use the Hybrid Sequence:

> Creative Strategist plus AI Operating Partner.

The buyer-readable wedge:

> customer language, founder judgment, positioning, content, offer clarity, and strategic taste.

The differentiated backend:

> semantic document libraries, agent-ready workflows, AI-consumable work primitives, source-of-truth rules, quality tests, and operating protocols.

## The Offer

First cash offer:

> AI Misfire Express.

Plain-English promise:

> Send one AI output, prompt, SOP, workflow, content process, proposal, sales follow-up, or delivery step that is close but still wrong. Farrice will show where AI is being forced to guess and what needs to be made explicit so the work can run cleaner.

What the buyer receives:

- misfire diagnosis
- missing-context map
- hidden-judgment extraction
- source-of-truth gaps
- decision-rule gaps
- quality-test gaps
- first recommended fix
- optional implementation sprint

What it is not:

- not a prompt cleanup service
- not generic AI automation
- not a tool setup call
- not a full enterprise transformation
- not a branding-only strategy session

## Pricing Ladder

Use pricing as a signal tool while proof is early:

| Tier | Price | Use Case |
|---|---:|---|
| Starter Misfire Map | $500 | First proof-building audits, smaller asset, quick turnaround. |
| Standard AI Misfire Express | $750 | One workflow or asset with written diagnosis and Loom walkthrough. |
| Express Priority | $1,000 | Faster turnaround, tighter action map, higher-touch review. |
| Implementation Sprint | $1,500-$2,000 | Convert the diagnosis into the first agent-ready work primitive or workflow layer. |

Later, after proof:

- $3,000-$7,500 for deeper workflow system build.
- Higher only after case studies and demand clarity.

## Primary Avatar

Name:

> The Founder-Led Expert Paying The AI Cleanup Tax.

They are not AI beginners. They are already using AI, but the outputs still require too much rescue.

They might say:

- "It gets me 70% there, but the last 30% is where the real work is."
- "The output is not wrong exactly. It is just not something I would send."
- "It sounds polished, but it does not sound like me."
- "It misses the nuance."
- "It does not understand the customer."
- "I still have to rewrite the whole thing."
- "I keep giving it more context, but it still does not get the judgment."
- "I do not want more AI content. I want better thinking."

## Trigger Events

This buyer is most receptive when:

- an AI-generated proposal sounded generic
- a content draft was polished but had no tension
- a sales follow-up missed the real buyer psychology
- a client-delivery SOP failed to capture edge cases
- a prompt worked once but could not be repeated
- a founder spent an hour rescuing a supposedly time-saving output
- the team started using AI informally without shared rules
- the buyer sees AI adoption accelerating and feels behind, but does not want hype

## Belief Map

Current belief:

> AI is useful, but I still cannot trust it with my real work.

Frustration:

> I keep having to rescue outputs that look finished.

New belief:

> The output is not the real problem. The real problem is that my judgment, examples, customer language, and quality rules are not visible to the system.

Offer belief:

> If we diagnose one misfire, we can find the hidden rules that make the work easier to repeat.

## Messaging Hierarchy

First-touch:

> Your AI keeps guessing because the judgment behind the work is still trapped in your head.

Second-touch:

> The fix is not always a better prompt. Sometimes the work itself needs a clearer operating layer.

Warm-buyer language:

> We convert the workflow into an agent-ready work primitive: purpose, inputs, constraints, source of truth, decision rules, examples, counterexamples, quality tests, failure modes, and update rules.

## Objections And Responses

Objection:

> "I do not know if this is worth paying for."

Response:

> Start with one misfire. If the diagnosis does not reveal a repeated cleanup pattern, do not build anything bigger.

Objection:

> "Can I just use better prompts?"

Response:

> Sometimes, yes. But if the same issue keeps coming back, the prompt is usually pointing at a missing rule, example, source of truth, or judgment standard.

Objection:

> "I am not technical."

Response:

> Good. This starts with the work, not the tool. Send the messy output and explain what it was supposed to do.

Objection:

> "We already have SOPs."

Response:

> Human-readable SOPs are useful, but AI often needs clearer decision rules, boundaries, examples, counterexamples, and quality tests.

Objection:

> "I do not want my systems exposed."

Response:

> The first audit can use one artifact. No internal system access is required. Sensitive material can be redacted.

## What To Challenge

An external reviewer should pressure-test:

- Is the primary buyer narrow enough?
- Is "founder-led expert" too broad for fast cash?
- Should the first buyer be AI-forward agencies instead?
- Is "AI Misfire Express" compelling or too cute?
- Is $750 a better first price than $500 or $1,000?
- Does the offer promise diagnosis, transformation, or implementation clearly enough?

## Strategic Constraint

Do not reposition into generic "AI consultant."

The money is in the specific diagnosis:

> AI did the task. It did not understand the work.
