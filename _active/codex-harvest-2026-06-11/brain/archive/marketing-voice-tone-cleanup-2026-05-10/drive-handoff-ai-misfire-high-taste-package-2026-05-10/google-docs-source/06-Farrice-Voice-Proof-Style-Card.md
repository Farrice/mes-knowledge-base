# Farrice Voice + Proof Style Card

## Purpose

This style card prevents the Hybrid Sequence content from becoming the same problem the offer diagnoses.

The standard:

> Do not let AI finish the task while missing the work.

For LinkedIn, that means no clean educational post is good enough unless it carries lived tension, human judgment, proof, rhythm, and a line only Farrice would write.

## Voice DNA

Farrice writes like:

- a sharp friend who can name the thing without making you feel stupid
- a dad under real pressure, not a content machine playing founder online
- a pattern-recognition nerd who can connect AI, parenting, training, gaming, and business without forcing it
- a strategist who cares about the human outcome more than sounding clever
- an operator building under a compressed work window, where wasted content is not harmless

The tone is:

- conversational authority
- warm but direct
- self-aware
- specific enough to sting a little
- premium by restraint, not polish

The writing should feel like Farrice is thinking in public.

Not performing.
Not teaching from a mountaintop.
Not doing the "AI consultant thought leadership voice" thing.

(That voice already has enough interns.)

## Proof Rules

Every strong claim needs proof beside it.

Acceptable proof types for this stage:

- Personal proof: "I caught this in my own content."
- Process proof: before/after diagnosis, correction notes, internal audit.
- Lived proof: fatherhood, compressed work window, personal training, coaching, creative strategy.
- Market proof: 2026 source or platform observation.
- Demonstrable proof: screenshot, Loom, audit map, post teardown, client artifact, internal workflow example.

Do not say:

- "This offer is validated" until buyer signal proves it.
- "AI Misfire Express fixes your workflow" without showing the diagnosis.
- "This is the future" unless the claim is grounded in a 2026 source or framed as a thesis.

Use this rule:

> If a sentence asks the reader to trust Farrice, add proof or make the claim smaller.

## The V3 Post Architecture

Most posts should follow this sequence:

1. Scene, confession, contradiction, or specific moment.
2. Reader mirror.
3. Hidden mechanism.
4. Proof or lived example.
5. Perspective shift.
6. Small next action or open loop.

Use hard CTAs only on bridge posts.

For most posts, the stronger ending is:

- a line that keeps the thought open
- a question that sharpens the reader's diagnosis
- a small action they can do without asking permission
- a quiet bridge to "send me one misfire"

## Required Tension

Every publish-ready post needs at least one of these tensions:

- I almost shipped generic content while building an anti-generic offer.
- AI made the output cleaner and less true.
- The founder is not saving time; they are becoming the unpaid QA department.
- The prompt is not the problem; the invisible judgment is.
- Pretty language is sometimes a way to avoid saying what the customer needs.
- AI can imitate a category, but it cannot invent your fingerprints.
- A tool can do the task and still miss the work.

If no tension exists, the post is probably just a lesson.

Lessons are fine.
But lessons do not stop the scroll anymore.

## Voice Moves To Use

### The self-indicting opener

Start by implicating Farrice before critiquing the market.

Examples:

- "I read the posts we made for this offer and got mad."
- "I almost published the exact kind of content I warn people about."
- "The draft was clean. That was the problem."

### The painfully specific detail

Replace broad language with moments the reader recognizes.

Weak:

> Your AI output sounds generic.

Strong:

> It sounds like a person who read your About page once and then got promoted to CMO.

### The human-operating-system bridge

Use personal training, coaching, fatherhood, and creative work as proof that the insight comes from human behavior, not AI trend watching.

Example:

> A workout plan was never the work. The work was the person trying to follow it on the day they were tired, embarrassed, and quietly looking for a way out.

### The taste line

Use restraint and precision.

Examples:

- "Clean is not the same thing as true."
- "Finished-looking is not finished."
- "The detail your competitor would remove for efficiency might be the thing your audience remembers."

### The soft knife

Say the uncomfortable thing with care.

Example:

> If you keep rewriting the same AI output, you do not have a prompt problem yet. You have an invisible judgment problem.

## Personal Story Guardrails

Family and fatherhood can be used when the story reveals Farrice's responsibility, not JJ's private life.

Allowed:

- Farrice's realization about presence.
- Farrice's compressed work window.
- The moral tension of persuasion, screens, and attention.
- A general parenting moment used to teach care, language, and responsibility.

Not allowed:

- Making JJ the spectacle.
- Over-sharing details that do not serve the reader.
- Turning a family moment into a manipulative credibility play.
- Using fatherhood as a pasted metaphor.

Rule:

> The story must make Farrice more accountable, not make JJ more visible.

## Banned Patterns

Do not publish if the post:

- opens with a generic AI trend
- says "in today's fast-paced world"
- explains the mechanism before the reader feels the problem
- uses "semantic documents" or "context engineering" before buyer recognition
- ends every post with "DM GUESS"
- sounds like it could be posted by any AI consultant
- has no scene, no confession, no proof, or no pressure
- treats jargon as a substitute for authority

## Quality Gate

Score each post before publishing.

Fresh Voice:

- 2+ moments that create a laugh-exhale, wince, or "damn, true"
- no guru energy
- no interchangeable AI consultant language
- reads naturally out loud

Luke Proof:

- every claim has proof within two sentences
- proof tier matches claim size
- no fake validation

Kallaway:

- relevant and relatable: 8+
- small effort to large reward: 7+
- non-obvious frame: 8+
- validated with proof: 7+
- tactically implementable: 7+

Donald Miller:

- clear problem in the first few lines
- simple plan
- no cognitive toll booths

Oren:

- hidden pattern named
- premium restraint
- practical next move
- buyer feels like Farrice sees the work under the work

LinkedIn:

- first 50 words create tension, recognition, or curiosity
- mobile spacing
- one core idea
- no fake engagement bait
