# Coach Cooz Triage-To-Client Conversion System V3
## Executive Read

The Triage Audit is not a free consultation.

It is the premium diagnostic doorway.

Its job is to:

1. qualify fit
2. make the buyer feel accurately heard
3. name what is breaking
4. recommend one path
5. create a clear next step

If the audit becomes a menu pitch, the premium frame weakens.

If the audit becomes free coaching, Cooz gives away the value without creating commitment.

## The Conversion Principle

> The diagnosis chooses the path.

Everything in the call should reinforce that.

## Audit Entry Rules

### Free Triage Audit

Use for:

- direct warm referrals from trusted people
- current or past relationship with Cooz
- clearly high-fit inbound with budget, urgency, and serious intent

### Application First

Use for:

- cold LinkedIn traffic
- GBP/search traffic
- Instagram traffic
- people asking price before context
- anyone vague about urgency or budget

### Paid Diagnostic Trigger

If after 5 audits:

- show-up quality is weak
- budget fit is poor
- prep + call + follow-up exceeds 3 hours often
- audit-to-close is below 20%
- prospects leave with free value but no serious next step

Then test a paid diagnostic fee credited toward enrollment.

Do not frame it as a cheap trial.

Frame it as a serious diagnostic.

## Pre-Call System

### Intake Questions

Keep it short. The goal is not to diagnose before the call.

Ask:

1. What made you book this now?
2. What is slipping: strength, energy, sleep, consistency, body confidence, or something else?
3. What have you tried that worked for a minute and then stopped?
4. What does a normal week actually look like?
5. Are you looking for in-person support in Burbank, remote support, or unsure?
6. If Cooz recommends a path, are you prepared to invest at least `$1,500/mo` or `$3,500` for a defined first sprint if the fit is right?

The budget question should not sound apologetic.

It protects both sides.

### Cooz Pre-Call Notes

Before the call, choose:

- likely buyer lane
- likely proof analogue
- likely objection
- likely path
- exact phrases to listen for

## Call Architecture

### Phase 1: Listen

Time: 0-25 minutes

Goal:

Hear the actual pattern before diagnosing.

Default opener:

```text
Before I tell you anything about how I work, I want to hear what is actually happening in your body, your schedule, and your life right now.

Take a few minutes.

What made you book this call?
```

Prompts:

- What changed in the last year?
- Walk me through a normal Tuesday.
- What does your body feel like at 4 PM?
- What have you tried that worked for a minute and then stopped?
- What are you tired of hearing yourself say?
- What does your family notice, if anything?
- What are you not saying out loud yet?

Do not diagnose early.

### Phase 2: Read Back

Time: 25-40 minutes

Goal:

Prove he heard them.

Template:

```text
Here is what I am hearing.

You said [exact phrase].

You said [exact phrase].

And the pattern I hear is this: the body carrying [career/family/life] is starting to fall behind it.

You have tried [alternatives].

Some worked for a minute.

None became something you could keep.

Tell me where I am wrong.
```

If they correct him, good. That means they are collaborating.

### Phase 3: Diagnose

Time: 40-60 minutes

Goal:

Name 2-3 patterns, not the whole framework.

Use only what fits:

- under-recovered, not under-trained
- inputs unstable before training problem
- calendar winning because practice is not installed
- self-reliance turning into a blind spot
- sprint solution for long-arc problem
- body signaling, not failing
- schedule is the design condition
- support layer missing

Do not teach everything.

The buyer should leave thinking:

> He named the pattern.

Not:

> I got enough free coaching to try this alone.

### Phase 4: Recommend One Path

Time: 60-80 minutes

Goal:

Recommend one path with clear reason.

Template:

```text
Based on what you told me, I would not recommend all options.

I would recommend [path].

Here is why.
```

Then explain only that path.

Use comparable proof:

- Local room needed -> Mike, Robin, Karima, Sam, Buzz
- Travel/schedule moving -> Corey, Sam, Buzz
- Defined first win -> Mari with caveat, plus any verified sprint proof
- Premium complexity -> Mike, Corey, Carron, Robin

### Phase 5: Close Or Next Step

Time: 80-90 minutes

If fit is clear:

```text
If you want to do this, the next step is simple.

We pick the start date, set the first 12-week target, and I send the agreement.

Do you want me to hold the spot for you?
```

If they need time:

```text
That is fair.

I do not want you deciding from pressure.

Think about the right question:

Is the pattern you described likely to change without a different structure around it?

If yes, you should not hire me.

If no, then the decision is whether this is the right structure.
```

If no fit:

```text
I do not think this is the right fit right now.

Here is what I would do first.
```

Then give one clean recommendation.

## Path Recommendation Logic

| Signal | Recommend |
|---|---|
| Local, can train 3-4x/week, needs room/accountability | Burbank Private Rebuild |
| Local but cannot hold 3-4 sessions | Remote Daily Rebuild or 90-Day Rebuild Sprint depending on urgency |
| Travel-heavy or non-local | Remote Daily Rebuild |
| Skeptical but serious, wants defined first commitment | 90-Day Rebuild Sprint |
| High-complexity leader, body as life/business bottleneck, clear budget | Invite-only premium pilot |
| Wants cheap training or menu shopping | No fit or lower-support referral |

## Objection Handling

### "It is expensive."

```text
It is.

And I do not want to make it sound cheaper than it is.

The question is whether this is a training expense, or whether the body falling behind your life is already costing you more than that.
```

### "I need to think about it."

```text
That is fair.

Think about the pattern, not the price alone.

Based on what you told me, is this likely to change with the same structure you have now?
```

### "Can I start next month?"

```text
Maybe.

But I want to be honest: next month can be real, or it can be the same story that has kept this pattern alive.

If next month is real, we can put the date down now.

If it is avoidance, it is better to name that.
```

### "Can I do fewer sessions?"

```text
Maybe.

But I would not recommend less support if the diagnosis says support is the missing piece.

The goal is not to buy the smallest version.

The goal is to choose the version that has a chance of holding.
```

### "I have tried coaching before."

```text
That matters.

I want to know what did not hold.

Was it the workouts, the schedule, the recovery, the relationship, or the fact that the plan had no answer when life moved?
```

### "I should be able to do this myself."

```text
I understand that.

And I am not questioning whether you know how to work hard.

What I am questioning is whether self-reliance has turned into the thing keeping the pattern alive.
```

## Follow-Up System

### Same-Day Follow-Up

Send a short recap:

```text
Good talking today.

Here is what I heard:

- [pattern 1 in their words]
- [pattern 2 in their words]
- [pattern 3 in their words]

My recommendation: [path].

Why: [one paragraph].

Next step: [agreement/start date or decision deadline].
```

### 48-Hour Follow-Up

If no response:

```text
Quick check-in.

The main question I would stay with is the one from the call:

Is the pattern likely to change with the same structure around it?

If yes, you probably do not need me.

If no, then we should decide whether [path] is the right structure.
```

### 7-Day Close Or Release

```text
I do not want this sitting open forever.

If you want to move, we can set the start date.

If not, no problem. I would rather release the spot than keep pretending indecision is clarity.
```

Review weekly:

- audits booked
- show rate
- close rate
- close rate by source
- close rate by path
- hours per close
- objections by path
- revenue by source
- number of no-fit calls

## Audit Quality Gate

A good audit should produce:

- a clear diagnosis
- one recommended path
- a buyer who feels heard
- a next step
- tracking data

A bad audit produces:

- a menu pitch
- free coaching without commitment
- vague "let me know" follow-up
- unclear path recommendation
- no data captured

## Red Flags

Pause or redesign the audit if:

- Cooz is doing 90-minute calls for low-fit people
- prospects keep asking for cheaper versions
- too many people leave with useful advice but no action
- the recommendation feels improvised
- remote path objections repeat
- ESA prospects like the idea but will not pay

## The One-Line Rule

Before every audit, Cooz should remember:

> My job is not to prove how much I know. My job is to name what is breaking and recommend the right path.
