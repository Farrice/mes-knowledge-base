# Coach Cooz Offer Architecture V3
## Executive Read

The buyer-facing offer should be simple:

> Start with the Triage Audit. If there is a fit, Cooz recommends one rebuild path.

Do not lead with a menu.

Do not ask the buyer to self-select from four options.

The offer architecture can have four internal paths, but the public sales motion is one doorway and one recommendation.

## The Offer Promise

### Plain Promise

> Rebuild strength, energy, and consistency when life keeps breaking the plan.

### Premium Promise

> Build the body that can carry the next chapter of the life you already built.

### Delivery Promise

> The plan will be built for your real week, not the week you wish you had.

### What Not To Promise

- guaranteed `$10k-$15k/mo` for Cooz from a few clients
- guaranteed client transformation in 90 days
- universal body recomposition results
- ESA as a public broad offer
- "pure muscle" claims without measurement context

## Public Offer Ladder

The buyer sees:

1. Triage Audit
2. One recommended path
3. Clear next step

The public page can mention that different paths exist, but it should not make the buyer shop the menu.

## Path 1: Burbank Private Rebuild

### Buyer-Facing Name

Burbank Private Rebuild

### Best For

Local professionals who need the room, the appointment, and the in-person relationship.

### Core Problem

They have already proven that unsupervised plans disappear when work, family, stress, or fatigue gets loud.

### Promise

> Private in-room coaching that rebuilds strength, energy, and consistency under your actual week.

### Delivery

- 3-4 private sessions per week
- strength training and movement work
- nutrition targets
- recovery targets
- weekly review
- minimum-version planning for bad weeks
- direct adjustment when the week changes

### Price Logic

- Founding rate: `$1,275/mo` only if still strategically useful and capacity-limited.
- Standing rate: `$1,500/mo`.
- Minimum: 12 weeks.

The price is justified by high-touch delivery, local private attention, and the relationship layer. This should not be compared to gym access.

### Fit Signals

- Lives or works close enough to show up consistently.
- Can train 3-4 times per week.
- Wants the room, not just programming.
- Has money and urgency.
- Does not want a cheap trainer.

### Non-Fit Signals

- Wants one session per week and no between-session work.
- Wants bargain pricing.
- Cannot protect appointment time.
- Wants to pick exercises instead of be coached.
- Needs medical care beyond Cooz's scope.

### Proof To Use

Mike, Robin, Karima, Sam, Buzz.

## Path 2: Remote Daily Rebuild

### Buyer-Facing Name

Remote Daily Rebuild

### Best For

Travel-heavy, non-local, or schedule-moving professionals who need daily support but not an in-person appointment.

### Core Problem

Their life moves too much for a static plan. Most remote coaching feels too easy to ignore.

### Promise

> Daily coaching support for the professional whose schedule will not sit still.

### Delivery

- remote programming
- weekly review
- nutrition/accountability targets
- 5-day/week async support window
- travel-week minimums
- missed-week recovery planning
- video or voice check-ins as needed
- monthly progress review

### Required Service Standard

This path must be concrete.

"Voxer access" is not enough.

The buyer should know:

- when Cooz responds
- what they send
- what Cooz reviews
- how travel weeks are adjusted
- what gets tracked
- how progress is judged
- what happens after a miss

### Price Logic

- Founding rate: `$1,500/mo`.
- Standing target: `$2,000/mo` after delivery proof validates.
- Minimum: 12 weeks.

Remote must earn premium pricing by being higher-touch and more diagnostic than cheaper remote coaching.

### Fit Signals

- Travels often.
- Has unpredictable hours.
- Needs accountability without being local.
- Can communicate consistently.
- Values response and guidance.

### Non-Fit Signals

- Wants an app plan only.
- Does not send check-ins.
- Cannot communicate honestly.
- Wants low-cost macros and workouts.
- Needs in-person accountability but refuses to admit it.

### Proof To Use

Corey, Sam, Buzz, travel-week content, schedule-moving stories.

## Path 3: 90-Day Rebuild Sprint

### Buyer-Facing Name

90-Day Rebuild Sprint

### Best For

Skeptical buyers, event-deadline buyers, or people who need a defined first win before they trust a longer arc.

### Core Problem

They need enough structure to prove change is possible, but they may not be ready to commit to long-arc support yet.

### Promise

> A 12-week first-win protocol to name what is breaking, rebuild the first layer, and decide the right long-term path from evidence.

### Delivery

- 12-week training, nutrition, recovery, and consistency protocol
- starting assessment
- weekly targets
- weekly review
- minimum-version plan
- final review and next-path recommendation

### Price Logic

- Founding: `$3,500`.
- Standing: `$4,500` when proof and demand justify.
- Best positioned as a defined first commitment, not a discounted way into coaching.

### Fit Signals

- Has a time-bound goal.
- Has been burned by programs and needs a first win.
- Wants to test the work seriously.
- Can follow structure for 12 weeks.

### Non-Fit Signals

- Wants a quick aesthetic promise.
- Believes 90 days solves everything.
- Needs daily high-touch support but wants a cheaper path.
- Wants to avoid the ongoing work.

### Proof To Use

Mari only with measurement caveat, plus any 90-day first-win clients once validated.

## Path 4: Invite-Only Premium Pilot

### Buyer-Facing Name

Use carefully.

Recommended public language:

> Invite-only premium rebuild pilot.

Avoid making "Executive System Architecture" the public headline until buyer language validates. ESA can remain an internal label.

### Best For

One founder, VP, senior creative, or business owner who already sees the body as a business, family, and life bottleneck.

### Core Problem

The problem is bigger than workouts. Their schedule, travel, decision patterns, recovery, food, sleep, and physical capacity are all interacting.

### Promise

> Full-body, schedule, and energy architecture for the person whose body has become a bottleneck to the life they are responsible for.

### Delivery

- 90-minute Triage Audit
- deeper schedule/body audit
- training, nutrition, recovery, and travel architecture
- weekly executive-style review
- daily decision support
- spouse/family schedule considerations only if appropriate and consented
- monthly strategic review
- direct support around high-risk weeks

### Price Logic

- Pilot: `$5,000/mo`.
- Minimum: 3 months.
- Capacity: 1 pilot.
- Do not scale publicly until 5 founder/VP conversations validate language, delivery, and close logic.

### Fit Signals

- Budget is not the main objection.
- They see their body as a serious life bottleneck.
- Their schedule complexity is high.
- The cost of inaction is obvious.
- They want a specialist, not a trainer.

### Non-Fit Signals

- Wants luxury attention but not behavior change.
- Wants concierge access without doing the work.
- Sees this as a status purchase only.
- Has no measurable pain or urgency.
- Needs medical, therapeutic, or clinical intervention outside Cooz's lane.

### Proof To Use

Mike for long-arc professional proof, Corey for schedule complexity, Carron for input/output precision, Robin for identity and Brave Choice.

Do not use ESA as a broad public claim until validated.

## Offer Incomparability

Cooz should not try to be "better personal training."

He should be harder to compare:

| Component | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Triage Audit | Creates diagnosis before path recommendation. |
| One recommended path | Reduces menu-shopping and frames Cooz as specialist. |
| Private room or daily support | Gives the buyer a structure that matches their life. |
| Training + nutrition + recovery | Solves the body as a system without using jargon. |
| Minimum-version planning | Makes consistency possible under real weeks. |
| Brave Choice | Gives decision language between sessions. |
| Long-arc proof | Shows the work holds beyond a sprint. |

The incomparable statement:

> I have seen trainers, apps, gyms, and programs. I have not seen someone start with a 90-minute diagnosis, recommend one path, and build the relationship that keeps the work alive years later.

## Offer Decision Rules

### Public Rule

Start with the Triage Audit.

### Internal Rule

The diagnosis chooses the path.

### Capacity Rule

Do not sell more local clients than Cooz can coach well.

### Price Rule

Do not discount below the premium floor. If price resistance is high, improve diagnosis, fit, and proof. Do not cheapen the offer.

### Remote Rule

Remote only works if daily support is defined and tracked.

### Sprint Rule

The sprint is a serious first commitment, not a cheaper version of ongoing coaching.

### Pilot Rule

ESA/premium pilot is not real until founder/VP conversations produce demand, language, and at least one qualified paid pilot.

## Buyer-Facing Offer Page Structure

Use this order:

1. Name the problem:
   > Your strength, energy, and consistency are starting to fall behind the life you built.
2. Set the context:
   > The old restart is not working because the plan was built for a calmer week.
3. Explain the doorway:
   > Start with the Triage Audit.
4. Explain what happens:
   > Cooz listens, diagnoses, and recommends one path.
5. Show the paths lightly:
   > In-person Burbank coaching, remote daily support, a 90-day first-win sprint, or invite-only premium support if the problem is bigger than training.
6. Show proof:
   > Clients stay 3-9 years because the work keeps paying them back.
7. CTA:
   > Book the Triage Audit.

## Offer Quality Gate

Before publishing or selling any path, check:

- Does the buyer understand what they get?
- Does the price make sense against the support level?
- Is the proof matched to the path?
- Is the non-fit clear?
- Is the next step simple?
- Does this path protect Cooz's capacity?
- Does this attract the right buyer or a cheaper one?
