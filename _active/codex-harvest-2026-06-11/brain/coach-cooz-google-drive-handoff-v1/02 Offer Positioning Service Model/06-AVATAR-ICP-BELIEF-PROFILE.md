# Coach Cooz V3 Avatar, ICP, And Belief Profile

Status: evidence-informed, provisional until buyer interviews and platform data validate it.

## Executive Read

The buyer is not "any busy professional who wants fitness."

The buyer is the person whose life got bigger while their physical support system got smaller.

They are not looking for more motivation. They are trying to decide whether the slide they feel in their body is temporary, normal aging, or the beginning of a version of themselves they do not want their family, team, or peers to adjust to.

The strongest market language is still plain:

> Rebuild strength, energy, and consistency for professionals over 40 whose body is starting to fall behind the life they built.

"Resurrection" can stay as the brand world. The cold buyer needs the practical door first.

## Primary Avatar

### Reference Name: Evan

Evan is 47.

He lives in Burbank, Studio City, Toluca Lake, Sherman Oaks, or another nearby pocket where work, family, and status all sit close together.

He has money. He has responsibility. He has body memory.

He used to be strong, athletic, lean, or at least confident in his body. He can still talk like that version of himself is nearby. That is part of the problem. His language has not caught up to the evidence.

He is not in a dramatic collapse. That is why he keeps delaying.

He is in the quieter season where the data is getting harder to ignore:

- The 4 PM fog is showing up more often.
- The gym keeps losing to the calendar.
- Sleep is worse than he admits.
- He avoids certain mirrors.
- His lifts do not feel like his memory of them.
- He makes the same Sunday promise and hates hearing himself make it.
- He still performs at work, but the performance costs more than it used to.

### Occupation, Activity, Thought Process

**Occupation:** The load-bearing adult.

Not his job title. His role in the world.

He is the person other people assume will be fine. He may be a senior creative, business owner, director, producer, private-practice professional, district leader, or local operator with a serious calendar. What matters is the role pressure: people depend on his output, presence, decisions, and composure.

**Activity:** He manages decline with tiny negotiations.

He drinks coffee before water. He skips breakfast and calls it focus. He tells himself the second drink is decompression. He checks Slack or email at night even when there is nothing urgent enough to justify it. He cancels the gym for a meeting that could have moved. He buys a device, supplement, scan, app, or program because buying a tool feels less confronting than changing the week.

**Thought process:** "I should be able to handle this."

That sentence is the lock.

He does not resist Cooz because he hates coaching. He resists because buying coaching threatens his self-image. He built a life by being capable. If he needs help with his body, it feels like the one domain where his identity stopped working.

## Secondary Buyer Lanes

These are not separate ICPs. They are entry paths into the same buyer.

### Lane 1: The Burbank Room-Needed Professional

This person needs the physical appointment.

They are local enough to train in person and affluent enough to pay for 3-4 sessions a week if the diagnosis lands. The room matters because they have already proven that unsupervised plans disappear when work gets loud.

**Best message:** "You do not need another plan you can ignore. You need the room, the appointment, and the relationship that keeps the work alive."

**Best proof:** Mike, Robin, Karima, Sam, Buzz.

**Best offer path:** Burbank Private Rebuild.

### Lane 2: The Schedule-Moving Creative Or Leader

This person travels, writes, produces, speaks, runs teams, or works in bursts. They cannot promise a stable week.

They have failed with programs that assumed stable access, stable sleep, stable meals, and stable work hours. They need coaching that travels with the pattern.

**Best message:** "Your schedule does not break the plan. Your plan breaks because it keeps pretending the schedule will sit still."

**Best proof:** Corey, Sam, Buzz.

**Best offer path:** Remote Daily Rebuild or 90-Day Rebuild Sprint.

### Lane 3: The Performance-Proof Buyer

This person is not convinced by soft transformation language. They trust numbers, lifts, records, adherence, and visible proof.

They may be an athlete, former athlete, serious lifter, or simply a buyer who needs evidence before emotion.

**Best message:** "The output gets more honest when the inputs match the demand."

**Best proof:** Carron, Mari, Cody.

**Best offer path:** Remote Daily Rebuild, Burbank Private Rebuild, or the 90-Day Sprint if the goal is time-bound.

## Buyer Journey

### State 1: Quiet Avoidance

He has not admitted this is a coaching problem yet.

Content job: make the private moment visible.

Best hooks:

- "The mirror tells the truth before most people are ready to say it."
- "The 4 PM fog is usually not mysterious."
- "The calendar keeps winning because the plan was built for a week you do not have."

### State 2: Problem Aware

He knows something is off and has tried the obvious fixes.

Content job: separate diagnosis from effort.

Best hooks:

- "Some people are under-recovered, not under-trained."
- "The third Monday restart is data."
- "Your body cannot keep producing outputs your inputs do not support."

### State 3: Solution Aware

He knows he needs help, but he is comparing Cooz to personal trainers, apps, gyms, and programs.

Content job: reframe the category.

Best hooks:

- "A workout is not the relationship that keeps the workout alive."
- "The plan has to survive the week, not the other way around."
- "The strongest proof is not always a before-and-after. Sometimes it is year nine."

### State 4: Coach Aware

He is checking whether Cooz is credible, specific, and safe to talk to.

Content job: reduce booking anxiety.

Best hooks:

- "The Triage Audit is not a menu pitch."
- "I listen first. Then I diagnose. Then I recommend one path."
- "If I cannot name what is breaking, I have no business selling you a plan."

## Buying Triggers

- A physical moment embarrasses them: mirror, stairs, pool, back catching, shirt fit, photo.
- A family moment lands: kid asks why they are tired, spouse stops asking, vacation photos hurt.
- Work exposes the gap: they cannot focus, travel breaks the plan again, the 4 PM fog costs them.
- A medical or performance marker scares them.
- A peer referral makes it feel safe to ask for help.
- A content post names their exact pattern without shaming them.

## Failed Attempts

- 12-week programs that worked until life moved.
- Fitness apps that were easy to ignore.
- Cheap trainers who counted reps but did not diagnose the life pattern.
- Boutique gyms where access did not equal follow-through.
- Biohacking tools that created data but not action.
- Sunday restarts.
- "Just eat better" promises.
- Workouts copied from their younger body.

## Trust Bridges

- Specific read-back of the buyer's own language.
- Clear distinction between diagnosis and selling.
- Long-arc client proof: 3-9 years.
- Proof by different lanes: local, travel-heavy, performance, family, remote.
- Admission of fit and non-fit.
- Plain language before brand language.
- One useful tool before any CTA.

## Language Bank

### Use Often

- "strength, energy, and consistency"
- "body starting to fall behind the life you built"
- "when life keeps breaking the plan"
- "the plan has to survive the week"
- "the relationship that keeps the work alive"
- "the 4 PM fog"
- "the third Monday restart"
- "under-recovered, not under-trained"
- "the minimum version"
- "the body that has to carry your life"
- "name what is actually breaking"
- "one path, not a menu pitch"

### Use Carefully

- "Resurrection" only as brand world, not as the cold explanatory layer.
- "Brave Choice" when the post is about decision moments.
- "90 days" only as a first-win frame, never as a guarantee.
- "16 lbs" only with measurement context or softer language until verified.

### Avoid

- "crush it"
- "level up"
- "CEO body"
- "founder mode"
- "wellness journey"
- "holistic reset"
- "transformation" as generic filler
- "executives, founders, and entrepreneurs" as a list
- technical language that a normal buyer would never say in a DM

## Avatar In One Paragraph

Evan is the 47-year-old professional whose life works on paper and is getting expensive in his body. He has enough money to hire help, enough pride to delay it, enough body memory to know what he lost, and enough responsibility that the people around him have already started getting the tired version. He does not need a trainer to tell him to work hard. He needs a serious diagnostic relationship that can name what is breaking, rebuild the foundation, and help the work hold when his actual life shows up.
