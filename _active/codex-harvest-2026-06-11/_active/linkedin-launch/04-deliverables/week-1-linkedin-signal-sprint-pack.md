## Post 2 - Founder Rescue

The first symptom of a bad AI workflow is not bad output.

It is founder rescue.

The founder reads the draft and says:

"That's not what we mean."

"We would never say it like that."

"It missed the real objection."

"It did the task, but not the work."

That correction is not a small edit.

It is evidence.

It shows the AI was missing something the founder knew automatically:

- customer language
- quality bar
- edge case
- decision rule
- source of truth
- strategic restraint

Most teams respond by rewriting the prompt.

Sometimes that helps.

But if the correction keeps happening, the prompt is not the system.

The hidden judgment is.

That is what has to become agent-readable.

If you have one output you keep rescuing, send it to me.

I will map the missing layer.

## Post 3 - Prompt vs Primitive

Most prompts ask for the task.

Work primitives explain the work.

That distinction matters.

A prompt says:

"Write a follow-up email."

A work primitive says:

- what the follow-up is for
- what source of truth controls the message
- what the customer already believes
- what the AI may infer
- what it must not invent
- what a good email must include
- when to stop and ask a human

The first one produces text.

The second one gives the agent operating meaning.

That is why better prompts eventually hit a ceiling.

If the work itself is not legible, the AI just becomes faster at guessing.

Send me one prompt that keeps disappointing you.

I will tell you whether it needs a prompt fix or a work primitive.

## Post 4 - SOP Gap

Most SOPs are written for humans who already know the work.

That is why AI misreads them.

A human reads a vague step and fills in the blanks from experience.

AI fills in the blanks from probability.

Those are not the same.

The dangerous part is that the AI can still sound confident.

It will complete the checklist.
It will format the output.
It will sound helpful.

But the missing meaning is still missing.

That is why founder-led teams need a different layer:

agent-readable work.

Not prettier documentation.
Not a bigger prompt library.
Not more automation on top of unclear rules.

A layer that defines:

- purpose
- inputs
- outputs
- authority
- source of truth
- examples
- failure modes
- quality tests

If you have an SOP that a human understands but AI keeps flattening, send it over.

I will show you where the ambiguity lives.
