# Sam Parr Ad Breakdown Protocols

## Purpose

The strongest section of the source is the live bad-ad rescue. This document turns that critique style into protocols future agents can actually run.

## Protocol 1: Proof-First Rescue

Use when the copy leads with product category, benefit language, or vague superiority.

1. Mark every claim that asks the reader to believe something.
2. Find the strongest available proof object:
   - quote,
   - before/after,
   - metric,
   - screenshot or artifact,
   - test,
   - comparison,
   - visible result.
3. Rewrite the opener from the proof object instead of the product.
4. Add one payoff line that explains why the proof matters.
5. Remove claims that cannot be supported.

Evidence anchors: `00:33:11`, `00:34:17`, `00:34:46`.

## Protocol 2: Visual Proof Translation

Use when a fact is true but too abstract to feel.

1. Name the abstract fact.
2. Ask what the reader would see, hold, compare, count, or notice if the fact were visible.
3. Convert the fact into a concrete comparison.
4. Check whether the comparison is accurate, not just dramatic.
5. Put the visual proof near the claim it supports.

Evidence anchors: `00:38:29`, `00:40:30`.

## Protocol 3: Objection By Detail

Use when the reader's doubt is predictable.

1. Name the objection in plain language.
2. Avoid a direct rebuttal unless the format requires it.
3. Find a lived detail, test, usage moment, or anecdote that makes the objection smaller.
4. Insert the detail where the doubt would naturally appear.
5. Keep the tone observational, not defensive.

Evidence anchors: `00:40:30`, `00:42:16`.

## Protocol 4: Price Desire Sequence

Use when the copy asks for action before value is felt.

1. Delay price or CTA until the outcome has emotional and practical weight.
2. Use story, contrast, proof, or consequence to raise desire.
3. Make the price feel like the natural bridge to the result.
4. Remove pressure language that tries to compensate for weak desire.

Evidence anchor: `00:42:30`.

## Protocol 5: Humor Fit Check

Use only when humor fits the brand, audience, and stakes.

1. Decide what the humor must prove:
   - humanity,
   - confidence,
   - shared world,
   - pattern break,
   - warmth.
2. Remove jokes that only prove cleverness.
3. Check whether a serious buyer would trust the offer more or less after reading it.
4. Keep humor out of claims, legal-sensitive sections, and proof unless the brand has earned that tone.

Evidence anchors: `00:43:56`, `00:44:05`, `00:45:15`.
