# Tom Segura Operating Model

## Core Thesis

Tom's comedy method is not "add jokes." It is a disciplined way of noticing the ordinary thing everyone recognizes, finding the emotional charge inside it, giving the audience a reason to care, and then cutting the story until the surprise lands cleanly in text or speech.

For Antigravity, this becomes a writing layer for:

- social posts that need recognition and shareability
- marketing copy that needs personality without losing trust
- essays and stories that need stakes before anecdote
- comedy punch-up that diagnoses why a line is flat
- AI writing cleanup where the draft is correct but emotionally inert

## The Method

### 1. Activate The Conversation Muscle

Comedy begins before drafting. Talk through the idea, riff with trusted people, and notice the moment where the conversation produces "that's a thing." Capture that phrase before editing it.

Output: raw premise list, not polished jokes.

### 2. Find The Charge

Indifference is rarely funny. Strong like, dislike, irritation, embarrassment, and specific frustration create the voltage. The question is not "what joke can I add?" It is "what do I actually care about here?"

Output: charged observation with a point of view.

### 3. Say The Obvious

The best observational joke often names something everyone has experienced but no one has articulated. If it feels too obvious, check whether it is actually unsaid. The first person to say it owns the recognition.

Output: plain-language recognition line.

### 4. Delay The Editor

The early filter kills usable premises. Write the dumb versions, say the rough versions, and let pressure or audience response reveal what has life.

Output: unfiltered attempts and rough tags.

### 5. Choose The Comedic Mode

Before rewriting, choose the mode:

| Mode | Use When | Output Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Observational story | A real experience has a reason for telling. | Setup, stakes, turn, payoff. |
| Social commentary | A cultural behavior needs deconstruction. | Premise, angles, tension, release. |
| Traditional bit | A premise needs compact joke structure. | Setup, misdirect, punch, tag. |
| Pure joke | A line needs maximum compression. | Clean setup and punch. |
| Alt/odd angle | The point should feel surprising or weird. | Unexpected frame, not tidy logic. |

Output: selected writing mode and why.

### 6. Find The Way In

A story is not enough. The reader needs to know why this story is being told now. A weak story says "this happened." A strong story creates stakes before the event, so the event has pressure.

Output: reason-for-telling line.

### 7. Expand Before Compression

Tell the full version first. Then remove anything the reader does not need for the payoff. Only after the story is lean should punchlines, callbacks, and word-level substitutions be added.

Output: long draft, cut draft, punch draft.

### 8. Encode Timing In Text

Written comedy cannot rely on face, voice, pause, or stage energy. Timing has to live in sentence length, line breaks, word choice, specificity, and where the punchline lands.

Output: text draft with rhythm decisions visible.

### 9. Use Callbacks Sparingly

Callbacks work because they prove attention and build a small shared world. Overuse turns charm into annoyance.

Output: one or two placed callbacks, not a callback swarm.

### 10. Send Trust-Sensitive Humor Through The Gate

If the writing is marketing, client-facing, or authority-building, humor must prove shared world, competence, warmth, or confidence. It should not prove only cleverness.

Output: humor-fit check and next gate.

## Success Standard

A Tom-style pass succeeds when the revised writing has:

- a specific point of view
- a reason for telling
- one recognition moment
- one cleaner surprise or turn
- tighter story path
- better word-level laugh levers
- no forced persona imitation
- a clear behavior delta for the reader
