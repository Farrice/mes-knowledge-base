## Three Angles For Today

### The correction tax

- **Why the ICP cares**: Founder-led experts lose time when AI gets close enough to use but not good enough to trust.
- **Obvious take to avoid**: Do not frame this as 'AI is bad.'
- **Farrice angle**: The correction is evidence of hidden founder judgment that needs to become agent-readable.
- **Post hook**: The most expensive AI output is not the terrible one. It is the one that looks finished but still needs rescue.
- **Comment angle**: The useful audit question is not 'what can we automate?' It is 'what do we keep correcting?'
- **CTA**: Send one output you had to rescue and I will show where it guessed.

### Prompt library ceiling

- **Why the ICP cares**: Teams keep rewriting prompts when the real issue is unclear source of truth, authority, and examples.
- **Obvious take to avoid**: Do not sell prompt packs.
- **Farrice angle**: Prompts ask for the task. Work primitives explain the work.
- **Post hook**: Most prompt libraries eventually hit the same ceiling: the work itself is still not legible.
- **Comment angle**: A prompt can request output. It cannot magically supply missing judgment, edge cases, or approval rules.
- **CTA**: Send one prompt that keeps disappointing you. I will tell you if it needs a prompt fix or a deeper primitive.

### Founder judgment bottleneck

- **Why the ICP cares**: The founder becomes the hidden approval layer for every content, proposal, and delivery workflow.
- **Obvious take to avoid**: Do not promise to replace the founder.
- **Farrice angle**: The win is making founder judgment reusable without flattening it.
- **Post hook**: If every AI draft still needs the founder's eye, the founder is not the blocker. The missing operating layer is.
- **Comment angle**: The goal is not removing senior judgment. The goal is deciding which parts can become rules, examples, and escalation boundaries.
- **CTA**: Send one workflow that still needs founder rescue.
