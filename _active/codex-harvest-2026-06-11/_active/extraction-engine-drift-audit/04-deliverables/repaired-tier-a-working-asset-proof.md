## After Asset

### LinkedIn Post

Most AI workflow problems do not look broken.

They look impressive, then vanish.

The team gets a good answer in chat. A useful prompt lands in a doc. Someone
builds a small automation. A report looks polished enough to share.

Then two weeks later, nobody knows what source it came from, which route produced
it, whether it was verified, or where the reusable version lives.

That is the quiet failure mode I keep seeing.

The problem is not output volume. The problem is operating memory.

In a recent internal repair audit, the global checks looked healthy at first.
But the individual source packages told a different story: some assets were
trusted, some needed proof, some had to be rebuilt from source, and one needed
to stay out of the trusted arsenal until repaired.

The useful fix was not a bigger AI stack.

It was smaller and stricter:

- source boundary,
- route owner,
- first-run path,
- before/after proof,
- verifier coverage,
- one place to find the result later.

That is what a Workflow Repair Audit does.

We take one AI-enabled workflow that already produces useful output, then make
it trustworthy enough to reuse:

- what source it is allowed to use,
- where it should route,
- what proof it must leave behind,
- what counts as a pass,
- what should be patched, quarantined, or rebuilt.

The goal is not "more AI."

The goal is fewer impressive artifacts that disappear.

If your team has useful AI work scattered across prompts, docs, automations, and
half-finished systems, start with one workflow. Make it source-backed,
findable, and reusable.

That is where the leverage starts compounding.

CTA: I am opening a small number of Workflow Repair Audits for founder-led teams
or solo operators who already have useful AI workflows, but cannot fully trust,
find, or reuse the outputs yet. DM me "repair" and I will send the diagnostic
questions.

### Offer Section

#### Workflow Repair Audit

For founder-led teams, operators, and service businesses whose AI workflows are
producing useful work that still gets lost, duplicated, mistrusted, or rebuilt.

In one focused diagnostic, we inspect one workflow and classify its operating
state:

| Area | Question |
|---|---|
| Source hygiene | What evidence is the workflow allowed to rely on? |
| Route owner | Which workflow or operator owns the result? |
| Proof | What before/after, cold-start, or transformed artifact proves the system works? |
| Firing | Does natural language route to the intended capability? |
| Reuse | Where does the trusted result live so it compounds? |
| Risk | What should be kept, patched, quarantined, or rebuilt from source? |

You leave with:

- a one-workflow trust ledger,
- the smallest repair plan,
- one proof artifact,
- a reusable first-run path,
- and a clear verdict: keep, patch, quarantine, or redo.

Not included: broad stack rebuild, publishing, outreach, hidden automation,
paid-data scraping, or claims the evidence cannot support.
