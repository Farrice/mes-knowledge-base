## 1. Delight Opportunity Map

| Surface | User Emotion Before | Desired Emotion After | Moment Type | Why It Is Not A Gimmick |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First AI Brain / content audit handoff | Skeptical, busy, expecting generic AI output | "This was built around how I actually think" | Personal recognition | Proves the core promise of voice/thinking capture |
| Content ideation bank capture | Raw, scattered, unsure where the idea goes | Relief and creative momentum | Tiny ritual | Turns messy thoughts into reusable assets |
| Offer demo | Curious but not convinced | "I can feel what this does" | Feature ad | Sells the idea instead of showing workflow machinery |
| LinkedIn post hook | Scrolling, guarded | "How did he know I think that?" | Surprise | Creates recognition before teaching |
| Workflow completion | Mentally loaded | "I know the next move" | One-step-ahead help | Reduces coldness and gives momentum |
| Empty state in future idea bank | Blank, low energy | "I know what to add" | Prompted ritual | Helps the user create source material |
| Public content series | Curious but non-gamer | "The game reference made the business lesson sharper" | Story bridge | Uses nostalgia as mechanism, not decoration |

## 2. Candidate Moments

### 1. "Controller Port Two" Reframe Prompt

Location: Inside idea-processing workflows when the obvious angle is weak.  
Before: User thinks they need a better tactic.  
After: User realizes they need a different frame.  
Detail: Add a prompt: "What is the controller port two move here? What solution lives outside the expected interface?"  
Safeguard: Explain the reference in one sentence for non-gamers.

### 2. "Make The Ad" Handoff

Location: When a feature, offer, workflow, or service idea is ready to show.  
Before: User has a description or mockup.  
After: User has a persuasive story artifact.  
Detail: Workflow asks for scene, stakes, reveal, payoff, and remembered line.  
Safeguard: Keep the ad under 90 seconds or one screen of copy.

### 3. "Psycho Mantis" Hook Check

Location: Content sprint hook stage.  
Before: Hook is clever but external.  
After: Hook names a private audience thought.  
Detail: Add a check: "Does this feel like the audience's mind was read ethically?"  
Safeguard: Seen, not shamed. Never expose sensitive personal material without care.

### 4. "Fingerprint Pass"

Location: Final content/offer/workflow review.  
Before: Output is structurally good but generic.  
After: Output has a Farrice-specific signal.  
Detail: Require one fingerprint from lived experience, gaming, fatherhood, anti-guru critique, spirituality, or AI craft.  
Safeguard: The fingerprint must clarify the point, not replace it.

### 5. "Moment Library" Capture Template

Location: Ideation bank inspiration sources.  
Before: User likes something but does not know how to use it.  
After: Source becomes mechanisms, hooks, and workflow routes.  
Detail: Capture: moment, feeling, mechanic, why it worked, Farrice translation, content use.  
Safeguard: Every source must produce deployable seeds.

### 6. "Slow Down The Slop" Sequence

Location: AI with Heart onboarding, offer page, or video.  
Before: The market feels noisy, fast, generic.  
After: The user feels grounded and seen.  
Detail: Structure copy/video from fast noise to slow clarity, echoing Retro's onboarding motif.  
Safeguard: Do not delay action too long; the sequence must resolve quickly.

### 7. "Back Of The Box" Easter Egg

Location: Newsletter or content series.  
Before: Reader expects the answer inside the post.  
After: Reader notices the answer was hidden in a detail they overlooked.  
Detail: Include a repeated phrase or image that becomes meaningful at the end.  
Safeguard: Keep it solvable and satisfying, not obscure.

### 8. "Human Behind The System" Closing Ritual

Location: Client deliverables and internal artifacts.  
Before: Output feels automated.  
After: Output feels authored.  
Detail: Add a short "What I noticed" section with one specific human observation.  
Safeguard: Keep it useful and concise.

## 3. Ranked Shortlist

1. **Moment Library Capture Template** - highest long-term value because it upgrades the ideation bank.
2. **Fingerprint Pass** - strongest protection against generic AI voice.
3. **Controller Port Two Reframe Prompt** - most ownable gamer-to-strategy mechanic.
4. **Psycho Mantis Hook Check** - strongest content performance angle.
5. **Make The Ad Handoff** - best for offers, demos, and client buy-in.

## 4. Build Specs For Top Three

### Moment Library Capture Template

Use for every future inspiration source:

- Source and link
- Moment remembered
- Feeling created
- Mechanic underneath
- Why it worked
- Farrice translation
- Content hooks
- Offer/workflow uses
- Routing commands

Test question: "Can this source produce at least three specific content seeds or one workflow improvement?"

### Fingerprint Pass

Add to final content checks:

- Could this have been written by any AI consultant?
- Where is the lived detail?
- Where is the point of view that might repel the wrong person?
- Which Farrice signal sharpens the idea: gamer, dad, anti-guru, spirituality, AI craft, biracial outsider, pattern-breaker?
- If the fingerprint is removed, does the piece lose memory?

Test question: "Would someone who knows Farrice recognize him in this?"

### Controller Port Two Reframe Prompt

Reusable prompt:

> What is the controller port two move here? If the obvious interface is the wrong place to solve the problem, what outside-the-frame shift makes the whole thing easier, clearer, or more memorable?

Use in:

- content ideation
- offer repositioning
- client audit insight generation
- AI workflow design
- sales narrative

Test question: "Does this produce a better frame, or just a clever reference?"

## 5. Annoyance Risks And Safeguards

- Do not force a game reference into every piece.
- Do not make workflows slower just to feel cute.
- Do not hide important information behind Easter eggs in client work.
- Do not use "mind reading" language where privacy or sensitivity is involved.
- Do not let delight replace clarity, speed, or trust.

## 6. User Test Questions

- What line or moment do you remember 24 hours later?
- Did the reference make the idea clearer or just more stylish?
- Did the workflow feel more human without becoming slower?
- Did the surprise resolve into a useful truth?
- Would you retell this idea to someone else?
