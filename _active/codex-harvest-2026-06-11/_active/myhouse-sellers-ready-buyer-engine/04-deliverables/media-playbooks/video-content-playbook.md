# Video Content Playbook

## Asset Status

Ready to review. Not live. Any numbers, payment examples, program references, buyer-agreement claims, or legal/financing language require review before posting.

## Content Thesis

The best video lane is not "look at this beautiful home." It is Jen becoming the agent who says what serious buyers are already thinking:

"I make real money. Why does this still feel impossible?"

Every video should pull the buyer from anxiety into a next step: READY, MAP, OFFER, SELL, or SOUNDCHECK.

## 20 Short-Form Scripts

### 1. The Buyer Is Not Behind

**Hook:** You are not behind because you did not buy at 29.

**Script:** A lot of first-time buyers now have careers, kids, family obligations, student loans, and years of rent behind them. That does not make you unserious. It means the purchase needs more precision. Before we look at homes, we need payment comfort, cash-to-close, financing route, and area tradeoffs on the table.

**CTA:** Comment READY for the first-home readiness scorecard.

### 2. Stop Starting With Listings

**Hook:** Listings are the most emotional way to start a practical decision.

**Script:** I love a good listing. But if we start there, the house starts driving the conversation. For serious buyers, I want the math to drive first: monthly comfort, cash available, lender status, and whether the neighborhood actually fits your life.

**CTA:** Comment MAP for the Valley Match Map.

### 3. The $850K Reality Check

**Hook:** An $850K budget can be real and still need a reality check.

**Script:** At this price point, we are not just asking what you can buy. We are asking what makes the purchase responsible enough to act on: payment comfort, cash-to-close, lender review, and the right tradeoff between home type and neighborhood.

**CTA:** Comment READY for the scorecard.

### 4. Family Gift Money

**Hook:** Family help can help. It can also slow everything down if it shows up late.

**Script:** If gift money may be part of your purchase, start that conversation early. The lender may need documentation, timing, and clarity on whether the money is a gift or loan. Do not wait until the house is found to figure this out.

**CTA:** Comment READY for the checklist.

### 5. Co-Buying Is Not Weird

**Hook:** Buying with a parent, sibling, or friend is not weird anymore. But it does need a plan.

**Script:** Co-buying can make the path possible. It can also create problems if nobody talks about exit plans, payment responsibility, decision authority, and what happens if one person wants out later.

**CTA:** Comment OFFER for the prep kit.

### 6. The Quiet Fear

**Hook:** The most common buyer question is not "what house can I buy?" It is "is this even possible for me?"

**Script:** That question deserves math, not hype. The answer starts with income, savings, payment comfort, programs that may apply, and what your lender can actually support.

**CTA:** Comment READY.

### 7. The Tour Trap

**Hook:** Touring too early can make you feel less ready, not more ready.

**Script:** If the numbers are unclear, every showing becomes emotional evidence that the market is impossible. A better path is readiness first, tours second.

**CTA:** Comment READY.

### 8. The Neighborhood Tradeoff

**Hook:** The house is not the only thing you are buying.

**Script:** You are buying the commute, parking, noise, HOA rules, resale path, grocery run, family rhythm, and how the home feels at night. That is why the Valley Match Map matters before the tour list.

**CTA:** Comment MAP.

### 9. Would I Let My Friend Buy This?

**Hook:** Pretty is not the same as smart.

**Script:** When I look at a home for a friend, I am not just asking whether it photographs well. I am looking at layout, deferred maintenance, resale, noise, disclosures, and whether the tradeoff fits their life.

**CTA:** Comment OFFER.

### 10. The Offer Room

**Hook:** Sellers do not only look at price.

**Script:** Price matters. But terms, timelines, financing strength, contingencies, and communication also affect how an offer feels. Buyers should understand the offer room before they are desperate to win one house.

**CTA:** Comment OFFER.

### 11. Seller Side Receipt: Showing Quality

**Hook:** A busy open house does not always mean strong demand.

**Script:** Showing quality matters. Who came? How qualified were they? What did they ask? Did agents follow up? Seller-side work teaches you how to read buyer seriousness on the other side too.

**CTA:** Comment SELL.

### 12. Seller Side Receipt: Calm Close

**Hook:** The calm close starts before the listing goes live.

**Script:** The prep, pricing, showing strategy, offer review, and communication rhythm all affect how calm the close feels. That is the same operational lens I want serious buyers to benefit from.

**CTA:** Comment SELL.

### 13. Home Soundcheck: Noise

**Hook:** Tour a home with your ears open.

**Script:** Street noise, neighbor rhythm, parking, AC hum, backyard sound, and nighttime feel all matter. A house can look right online and feel wrong in real life.

**CTA:** Comment SOUNDCHECK.

### 14. Home Soundcheck: Hosting

**Hook:** If your home is also your creative recovery space, tour differently.

**Script:** Think through sound, flow, guest parking, sleep, work zones, storage, and whether the layout supports how you actually live after a long week.

**CTA:** Comment SOUNDCHECK.

### 15. The Adult Buyer

**Hook:** You do not need a dream-home speech. You need a serious plan.

**Script:** A lot of first-time buyers do not want to be sold a fantasy. They want to know what works, what does not, and whether the next step is responsible.

**CTA:** Comment READY.

### 16. What Makes A Consult Useful

**Hook:** A buyer consult should not be a guessing session.

**Script:** The best consults happen when we know timeline, area, budget range, lender status, cash picture, decision makers, and what would make buying feel calm.

**CTA:** Comment READY.

### 17. The Valley Map

**Hook:** If the Valley feels impossible, your search may be too flat.

**Script:** The right move may be a different property type, adjacent area, commute tradeoff, or renovation tolerance. The map matters because your life is not a filter menu.

**CTA:** Comment MAP.

### 18. The Payment Conversation

**Hook:** Approval is not the same as comfort.

**Script:** A lender may help you understand what is possible. You still need to know what feels sustainable in your actual life. Both matter.

**CTA:** Comment READY.

### 19. The Saturday Rule

**Hook:** Your Saturday should not be the first qualification tool.

**Script:** If a lead is not clear on timeline, financing, and decision authority, they need education before showings. Serious buyers deserve speed. Browsers need structure.

**CTA:** Comment OFFER.

### 20. The Calm Buyer Path

**Hook:** The goal is not to make buying feel easy. The goal is to make it clear enough to act.

**Script:** If the math works, move. If the math needs review, review it. If the timing is wrong, nurture the plan. Clarity is the win before the keys.

**CTA:** Comment READY.

## Filming Notes

- Open with the tension in the first sentence.
- Keep each video to one buyer problem, one insight, one CTA.
- Use real local texture: SFV streets, commute, parking, open house setup, kitchen table math, laptop search, buyer checklist.
- Avoid protected-class targeting. Speak to readiness, behavior, timeline, and financial questions.
- Do not post payment examples or program claims without review.
