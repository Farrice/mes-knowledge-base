# Daniel Priestley 24 Assets OS - Genius Patterns

## 1. Income Follows Assets
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Separates income from effort and tracks what the business owns that creates income repeatedly.
- **Executable Behavior**: Convert every revenue goal into an asset gap: IP, brand, market, product, systems, culture, or funding.
- **Deployment Context**: Revenue plateau, founder bottleneck, low valuation, or inconsistent sales.
- **Success Metric**: More revenue per person without proportional headcount growth.

## 2. Asset, Not Activity
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Rejects work that disappears after the task is done.
- **Executable Behavior**: Ask what remains after the work is finished: a reusable method, audience, product, process, proof asset, dataset, or operating document.
- **Deployment Context**: Content, sales, delivery, hiring, training, admin, and client work.
- **Success Metric**: Each project leaves behind a reusable asset.

## 3. Ecosystem Over Hero Asset
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Treats business value as the interaction of all 24 assets, not one strong component.
- **Executable Behavior**: Score every category and fix the weakest constraint before over-investing in the strongest one.
- **Deployment Context**: Businesses with strong brand but weak systems, good products but no channels, or good delivery but poor funding story.
- **Success Metric**: No asset category remains below commercial level.

## 4. Revenue Per Person as Truth Serum
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Uses RPP to cut through founder stories and diagnose business shape.
- **Executable Behavior**: Divide annual revenue by people whose primary career focus is the business, then interpret team stage and asset maturity.
- **Deployment Context**: Deciding between lifestyle and performance ambition.
- **Success Metric**: RPP rises while stress and founder dependency fall.

## 5. Lifestyle vs Performance Design
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Forces the ambition decision before prescribing assets.
- **Executable Behavior**: Choose the company shape first, then set asset standards, hiring, funding, and system depth accordingly.
- **Deployment Context**: Founder wants freedom, scale, saleability, or category impact.
- **Success Metric**: The build plan matches the desired company, not borrowed advice from another path.

## 6. Soft Assets as Modern Wealth
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Values content, data, methods, brand, systems, and culture as business infrastructure.
- **Executable Behavior**: Move tacit knowledge into files, workflows, dashboards, media, legal registrations, and products.
- **Deployment Context**: Expert businesses, agencies, creators, consultants, SaaS-enabled services, and training companies.
- **Success Metric**: The company's most valuable assets can be inspected digitally.

## 7. Asset Creation Cycle
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Normalizes the ugly first version and pushes through to remarkable.
- **Executable Behavior**: Move each asset through idea, brief, supplier/prototype, beta, commercial version, and remarkable version.
- **Deployment Context**: New products, methods, reports, websites, onboarding, SOPs, brand assets, and client deliverables.
- **Success Metric**: Assets improve through market feedback instead of stopping at acceptable.

## 8. Supplier Leverage
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Uses excellent specialists to raise the business standard.
- **Executable Behavior**: Create a sharp brief, invite critique, select suppliers above the current comfort zone, and coordinate iteration.
- **Deployment Context**: Brand, design, legal IP, systems, product, finance, and automation work.
- **Success Metric**: External talent upgrades asset quality faster than founder DIY.

## 9. Asset Zero
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Looks for the unique missing force that animates the otherwise correct system.
- **Executable Behavior**: Identify the founder's obsession, worldview, taste, unfair insight, or mission that makes the 24 assets feel alive.
- **Deployment Context**: The business has decent components but low pull, low emotional energy, or generic positioning.
- **Success Metric**: The asset ecosystem gains a clear reason to exist beyond optimization.

## 10. Build-to-Buy Game
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Builds as if a serious buyer will inspect the business.
- **Executable Behavior**: Treat every asset as diligence evidence: clear ownership, transferability, documented proof, and reduced key-person risk.
- **Deployment Context**: Exit planning, financing, partnerships, acquisitions, or premium positioning.
- **Success Metric**: The business becomes easier to value, fund, buy, partner with, or inherit.

## 11. Cross-Industry Remarkability
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Steals standards from unrelated industries rather than copying direct competitors.
- **Executable Behavior**: Audit remarkable businesses outside the category and translate their asset standards into the user's business.
- **Deployment Context**: The category is stale, everyone copies everyone, or assets feel ordinary.
- **Success Metric**: The business looks unusually high-standard for its market.

## 12. Quarterly Asset Inspection
- **What He Does Unconsciously**: Treats asset creation as a recurring operating rhythm.
- **Executable Behavior**: Run a quarterly 24-asset inspection, select the weakest high-leverage assets, and assign build owners.
- **Deployment Context**: Ongoing business operations, client retainers, or productized service delivery.
- **Success Metric**: Asset scores compound quarter over quarter.
