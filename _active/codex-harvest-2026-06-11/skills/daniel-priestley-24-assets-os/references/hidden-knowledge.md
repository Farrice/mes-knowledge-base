# Hidden Knowledge

- **A business can be busy and asset-poor**: Full calendars, sales calls, delivery work, and team meetings can hide the fact that little transferable value is being created.
- **The founder is not the asset**: The founder's image, content, methods, recorded expertise, proof, and systems can be assets. The person alone is a risk.
- **RPP tells the truth faster than profit**: Profit can be distorted by accounting choices. Revenue per person quickly reveals whether assets or people are doing the work.
- **A weak asset category taxes every other category**: Poor data makes sales harder. Weak culture makes systems brittle. Weak product assets make brand work expensive.
- **Most entrepreneurs stop at commercial**: The payoff comes from pushing a working asset to remarkable, where sharing, referrals, valuation, and pricing improve.
- **The brief is the first real asset**: A clear briefing document turns fuzzy desire into something suppliers, team members, investors, and AI agents can improve.
- **Supplier choice is strategy**: The quality of designers, lawyers, developers, operators, and advisors becomes part of the company's asset ceiling.
- **Digital transfer is mandatory**: If an asset cannot be transferred through files, tools, recordings, dashboards, code, or media, it will struggle in a fast-changing market.
- **The product ladder is an asset ecosystem**: Gifts, P4P, core product, and P4C should move buyers through trust, proof, commitment, transformation, and expansion.
- **Funding assets are not just for fundraising**: Plans, structure, valuation logic, and risk controls improve lender trust, partner trust, buyer trust, and founder decision quality.
- **Asset zero cannot be templated**: The missing asset is usually a founder-specific worldview, mission, taste, community, obsession, or timing advantage.
- **Quarterly inspection prevents drift**: Asset systems decay when uninspected; a recurring scorecard makes the business improve without waiting for crisis.
