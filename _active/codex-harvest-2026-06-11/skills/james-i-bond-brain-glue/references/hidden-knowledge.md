# Brain Glue Hidden Knowledge

## Tacit Insights Made Deployable

- **The first impression is a command, not a greeting.** Before the prospect evaluates proof, the opening frame tells them what kind of experience they are having.
- **A name can do more selling than a paragraph.** A sticky name compresses benefit, category, mood, and memory into one object.
- **Clarity is not enough.** A message can be clear and still die. It must also create a feeling, picture, sound, or pattern.
- **Prospects rarely misunderstand everything.** They misunderstand one important thing, and that one misunderstanding blocks the sale.
- **A single sensory cue can outperform a logical argument.** The brain remembers sounds, images, textures, smells, and gestures faster than it remembers claims.
- **Rhyme and alliteration are not childish.** They are pattern technologies. They make a phrase feel complete and easy to retrieve.
- **Chiasmus creates authority by compression.** A reversed structure makes a thought feel like a principle instead of an opinion.
- **Shock works only when it points to truth.** Odd, edgy, or unexpected language must reveal the product's real value or audience identity.
- **The best hook is often already inside the asset.** The name, founder, category, main feature, objection, competitor, or customer phrase usually contains the raw material.
- **Qualification is attraction.** Saying "not for everyone" makes the ideal buyer feel seen and reduces generic persuasion pressure.
- **Humor lowers the drawbridge.** A small laugh can soften resistance before the buyer receives the serious claim.
- **Anchoring is mental shortcut design.** The right comparison lets the buyer borrow understanding from something they already trust, love, fear, or use.
- **Perspective beats magnitude.** A large number means little until the audience can compare it to a concrete world.
- **The buyer should be able to finish the line.** A phrase that invites completion is easier to remember and repeat.
- **Brain Glue is a sequencing method.** Do not use every tool. Diagnose the missing mechanism, add the smallest sticky device that solves it, then stop.

## Decision Heuristics

| If the asset feels... | Use... | Because... |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate but dull | Trigger words, odd/unexpected, sensory elevation | Attention is the bottleneck |
| Smart but confusing | Understanding ease, perspective, analogy | Comprehension is the bottleneck |
| Useful but generic | Metaphor, anchoring, rejection | Differentiation is the bottleneck |
| Polished but forgettable | Rhyme, alliteration, chiasmus | Recall is the bottleneck |
| Pushy | Expectation setting, honest admission, oxytocin | Trust is the bottleneck |
| Too broad | Tribal alignment, reject to attract | Fit is the bottleneck |

## Practitioner Constraints

- Produce multiple candidates, but recommend one winner.
- Explain why the winning line works in one or two sentences.
- Never let clever phrasing obscure the offer.
- Treat all Brain Glue devices as hypothesis-driven. The best line should be tested against actual audience response.
- Avoid long verbatim quotes from source material. Use the extracted mechanics, not the book's prose.
