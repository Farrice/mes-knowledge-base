## Genius Patterns

### Pattern 1: The Attention Redirection Principle
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Never tries to create attention from scratch. Every growth post borrows from an entity (brand, person, news event, consensus belief) that already has an audience paying attention.
- **Executable Behavior**: Before drafting any growth post, identify the specific entity with pre-existing audience attention. The entity IS the hook mechanism.
- **Deployment Context**: Every growth-bucket post. Skip only for authority/conversion/personal buckets.
- **Success Metric**: Post reaches beyond your existing follower count by 3x+.

### Pattern 2: The Boomerang Effect
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Structures posts so the referenced subject is compelled to engage, which triggers algorithmic amplification.
- **Executable Behavior**: Write something sharp enough about a brand/person that the subject notices and engages. Their engagement pushes your post to THEIR entire audience.
- **Deployment Context**: Brandjacking and namejacking posts where the subject has active LinkedIn presence.
- **Success Metric**: The referenced brand/person or their team member comments on the post.

### Pattern 3: The "So What?" Gate
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Kills every news summary that doesn't have a clear "so what" before the draft is finished.
- **Executable Behavior**: After writing a newsjack, answer "so what?" in one sentence. If you can't, you're summarizing, not contextualizing. Keep going until you can.
- **Deployment Context**: All newsjacking and trend-commentary posts.
- **Success Metric**: Your post contains a position, not a summary. Readers can tell whether they agree or disagree.

### Pattern 4: The LinkedIn Lag Advantage
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Sources news from X, Google, and LLM conversations 2-4 days before it trends on LinkedIn.
- **Executable Behavior**: Scan X, Google Trends, and ChatGPT/Claude for industry news daily. By the time you post on LinkedIn, you're "first" even though it happened days ago.
- **Deployment Context**: Newsjacking — timing is the variable that makes or breaks it.
- **Success Metric**: Post published within 24-48 hours of the story appearing on X, before LinkedIn catches up.

### Pattern 5: The 4-Bucket Funnel
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Every single piece of content is assigned a job (Growth, Authority, Conversion, Personal) before a word is written.
- **Executable Behavior**: Before drafting, declare the bucket. Growth = reach new people. Authority = prove expertise. Conversion = ask for business. Personal = deepen connection.
- **Target Ratios**: Default to Growth 40% / Authority 30% / Conversion 20% / Personal 10% from the 2026 full-system video. Use Growth 35% / Authority 35% / Conversion 20% / Personal 10% when an established expert needs more trust-building than reach.
- **Success Metric**: Monthly content audit shows all 4 buckets represented at roughly these ratios.

### Pattern 6: Body-First Writing
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Writes the substance of the post first, then mines the body for the hook.
- **Executable Behavior**: Never start with the hook. Write the body — the frameworks, numbers, examples, value. Then read what you wrote and pull the most surprising, provocative, or specific line to the top. The best hooks already exist in your content.
- **Deployment Context**: Every post. This eliminates the "blank page" problem.
- **Success Metric**: Hook is a direct extraction from the body content, not a manufactured attention-grab disconnected from the substance.

### Pattern 7: The Steal Protocol
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Systematically collects from three sources (sales calls, internal docs, expert conversations) and never creates content from scratch.
- **Executable Behavior**: Record sales/strategy calls. Mine transcripts for objections, pain points, and questions — each one is a post. Study internal SOPs for the "why" behind processes. Interview subject matter experts for their "obvious" expertise that's invisible to them.
- **Deployment Context**: Content ideation — when you don't know what to write about.
- **Success Metric**: 100+ ideas in your content bank before you ever feel "stuck."

### Pattern 8: The Infrastructure Trinity
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Runs content production through three interconnected tools with zero improvisation.
- **Executable Behavior**: 
  1. **Notion Kanban**: Ideas → In Progress → Design → Edits → Posted (every card tagged by format)
  2. **Google Drive**: Meeting transcripts, SOPs, conference decks, sales call recordings — connected to Claude
  3. **Claude Project**: Custom instructions with role/identity/story/opinions/banned words/tone, knowledge base with best posts + call transcripts + case studies
- **Deployment Context**: Always. This is infrastructure, not optional tooling.
- **Success Metric**: You can sit down to write and have something drafted within 30 minutes because the system feeds you.

### Pattern 9: Engagement as Distribution
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Treats strategic commenting as a distribution channel equal to posting.
- **Executable Behavior**: Build an engagement list (spreadsheet: category, name, LinkedIn recent-activity URL — NOT profile URL, to avoid stalker optics). Spend 30 minutes daily commenting on posts from people whose audiences match your ICP, especially before opening drafts in the first 90 days. Comments must add genuine value — a new angle, a story, or a respectful challenge. Never "great post."
- **Deployment Context**: Daily, especially when starting out (comments > posts when audience is small).
- **Success Metric**: Psychological reciprocity kicks in — people start engaging back on your posts.

### Pattern 10: The Content-Market Fit Timeline
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Expects and plans for 90 days of iteration before content "works."
- **Executable Behavior**: Posts 1-20 = practice (get reps, test hooks). Posts 21-40 = find your voice and community. Posts 41+ = momentum and content-market fit. Commit to 3-5 posts/week for 90 days minimum.
- **Deployment Context**: Any new LinkedIn brand build.
- **Success Metric**: By post 41+, you can predict which topics/formats your audience will engage with.

### Pattern 11: The Format Test Escalation
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Uses text posts as cheap experiments, then escalates winners.
- **Executable Behavior**: Start with text posts — fastest to create, cheapest to test. When a text post hits, turn it into a carousel. When a carousel hits, turn it into a video. Double down on winners. Your data is the truth; best practices are starting points.
- **Deployment Context**: Format selection and repurposing decisions.
- **Success Metric**: Format promotion is data-driven, not gut-driven.

### Pattern 12: The North Star Alignment
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Checks every post against content north stars — guiding principles that keep all content pointed in the same direction.
- **Executable Behavior**: Define 3-5 north stars (core beliefs, positioning statements). Every post must align with at least one, preferably multiple. If it doesn't align, it confuses your audience and delays content-market fit.
- **Deployment Context**: Content review gate before publishing.
- **Success Metric**: Stranger can read your last 10 posts and accurately describe what you believe and who you serve.

### Pattern 13: The 5-Field Author Signal
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Optimizes the five fields that travel with every piece of content into the AI retrieval system: name, headline, company, industry, and title. These are the only author-level signals the unified model uses to decide WHO should see your content.
- **Executable Behavior**: Before optimizing post content, audit these 5 fields. Your headline isn't a vanity tagline — it's metadata the AI uses to match you to audiences. Headline should contain the skill/domain your ICP cares about, not your job title. "LinkedIn Growth Strategist for B2B SaaS" beats "Founder & CEO at Distinctiva."
- **Deployment Context**: Profile optimization, client onboarding, any time reach drops without content quality changing.
- **Success Metric**: Profile views from target ICP increase within 7-14 days of headline rewrite. Posts reach new audience segments that match updated fields.

### Pattern 14: The 60-Token Audition
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Front-loads the first ~50 words of every post with topic-specific, semantically rich language because that's all the retrieval model reads to decide candidate-pool inclusion.
- **Executable Behavior**: Write the post body-first (Pattern 6), then audit the first 50 words. Ask: "If the AI only read these words, would it know EXACTLY what topic this is about and WHO it's for?" Remove throat-clearing, generic openers, and vague setups. The first 50 words are your audition for the candidate pool — not for human readers.
- **Deployment Context**: Every post, after body-first writing is complete. This is the final filter before publishing.
- **Success Metric**: First 50 words contain at least 3 topic-specific terms the AI can use for semantic matching. No filler openers like "I've been thinking about..." or "Here's the thing about..."

### Pattern 15: The Depth-Over-Breadth Principle
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Commits to deep coverage of 2-3 topic lanes rather than scattering across 10+ topics. The unified LLM has "world knowledge" — it connects related subtopics semantically without needing exact keyword matches.
- **Executable Behavior**: Define your 2-3 topic lanes (e.g., "LinkedIn growth systems" + "content strategy for B2B SaaS" + "building an agency from zero"). Post deeply within those lanes. The AI will AUTOMATICALLY connect your "LinkedIn algorithm" post to someone who engaged with "social media marketing" — you don't need to cover every adjacent topic yourself.
- **Deployment Context**: Content strategy architecture. Replace the instinct to "cover everything" with commitment to depth.
- **Success Metric**: Your last 20 posts can be grouped into 2-3 clear topic lanes. The AI starts surfacing your content to increasingly precise audiences.

### Pattern 16: The Save Economy
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Architects content for saves (reference value) not just likes (momentary agreement). Based on Authored Up data analysis, 1 save ≈ 5x the reach impact of 1 like.
- **Executable Behavior**: Before publishing, ask: "Would someone save this to reference later?" If not, restructure around a framework, checklist, data point, or step-by-step process that has revisit value. Saves signal to the AI that this content has durable value, not just fleeting engagement.
- **Deployment Context**: Authority and Growth bucket posts especially. Any post that teaches, frameworks, or provides data.
- **Success Metric**: Save-to-like ratio above 1:10. Posts with high save rates should show disproportionate reach expansion within 24-48 hours.

### Pattern 17: The Percentile Threshold
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Understands that the AI converts raw engagement numbers to percentiles — your 50 likes are compared to what's typical for YOUR follower count, not to a 500K-follower creator's 5,000 likes.
- **Executable Behavior**: Stop comparing raw numbers to bigger creators. Focus on your own engagement VELOCITY and PERCENTILE position. A post that gets 3x your average engagement in the first hour is algorithmically equivalent to a viral post from someone with 100x your audience — both signal "unusually high engagement" to the percentile model.
- **Deployment Context**: Performance analysis, content audit, client reporting. Kills the "my numbers are too small to matter" objection.
- **Success Metric**: Track engagement as a multiple of your 30-day average, not raw counts. Identify your percentile breakpoints.

### Pattern 18: The Small Account Advantage
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Leverages the structural fact that LinkedIn's new retrieval system matches by INTEREST, not by network size. Data shows a 3.29% revenue increase for smaller accounts under the new system.
- **Executable Behavior**: Tell new creators and small accounts: the old system favored big networks. The new AI system favors relevant content for specific interests. If your content precisely matches someone's professional learning arc, the AI will surface it regardless of your follower count. This is the best time in LinkedIn's history to start from zero.
- **Deployment Context**: New brand builds, client onboarding when account is small, 90-day sprint framing.
- **Success Metric**: New accounts seeing impression growth within first 30 days that outpaces network growth — signal that AI discovery is working.

### Pattern 19: The Profile-First SEO Funnel
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Treats the profile as the landing page for every piece of content. The post wins the click; the profile has to convert the attention into trust, follow, booking, newsletter, or lead magnet action.
- **Executable Behavior**: Before writing a post, audit headline, banner, about, services, featured section, and custom CTA. Headline uses buyer-search language. About follows problem, solution, proof, CTA. Featured sends visitors into an owned channel or booking path.
- **Deployment Context**: Before a new LinkedIn sprint, after reach grows but conversion lags, or whenever content traffic is being sent to a vague founder/resume profile.
- **Success Metric**: A stranger can understand what the person does, who it is for, and what to do next within 10 seconds.

### Pattern 20: The 72-Hour Landscape Study
- **What She Does Unconsciously**: Studies a niche before posting into it, using active accounts as market research rather than inspiration.
- **Executable Behavior**: Spend 72 hours finding accounts with ICP-matched engagement. Log recurring topics, hooks, formats, endings, design patterns, and outlier posts. Rebuild proven structures around the creator's own experience rather than copying ideas.
- **Deployment Context**: New niche, new offer, new client, stalled growth, or any reset where the creator does not yet know the unwritten rules of the feed.
- **Success Metric**: The first 20 posts are built from observed niche patterns plus original experience, not blank-page guesses.

## Hidden Knowledge

Tacit expertise Diandra demonstrates but doesn't explicitly teach:

- **The Obscurity Advantage**: When you're unknown, obscurity is your asset, not your problem. No one is watching your failures. Every flopped post is free research data. Every small win is private validation. Use this window before anyone is paying attention.

- **Extraction Over Creation**: The distinction between "creating" content (exhausting, starts from zero) and "extracting" content (sustainable, starts from existing material). All content already exists — it's buried in calls you've had, presentations you've given, documents you've written. The system makes it accessible.

- **The Recent-Activity-Link Trick**: When building an engagement list, never add profile URLs. Instead, go to the person's profile → "Show all posts" → copy THAT URL. This avoids showing up repeatedly in their "Who viewed your profile" and looking like a stalker.

- **The Expert's Invisible Knowledge**: The most valuable content comes from things subject matter experts consider "obvious" — the stuff that's become automatic to them. Ask "Why do you do it that way?" and "What's the mistake you see most often?" — 9/10 times they'll say something brilliant they'd never think to post.

- **The Conversion Content Dependency**: Conversion posts only work if growth + authority have done their job. Never lead with "buy my thing" to an audience that hasn't been warmed. The buckets are sequential in trust, not random.

- **The Profile Landing-Page Dependency**: Content traffic is wasted if the profile cannot answer "what do you do, is it for me, and what should I do next?" Treat banner, headline, featured, about, services, and CTA as one funnel.

- **The 40/30/20/10 Operating Mix**: The full-system video moves the monthly default toward Growth 40%, Authority 30%, Conversion 20%, Personal 10%. Do not erase the older 35/35/20/10 pattern; use it when the strategy calls for equal reach and trust building.

- **Visual = Better**: According to Diandra's data analysis, posts with visuals (images, one-pagers, screenshots, framework diagrams) consistently outperform text-only posts. Always try to include a visual, even if it's a simple framework diagram or screenshot.

- **Claude Over ChatGPT for LinkedIn**: Diandra specifically recommends Claude over other LLMs for LinkedIn writing — it handles conversational tone, contraction usage, and banned-word avoidance better than alternatives.

- **The 500+ Word Context Dump**: When setting up any AI writing tool, provide 500+ words about your service, wins, "musts," and cliché blacklist. Generic inputs produce generic outputs. The quality ceiling is set by the quality of context you provide.

- **The Negative Signal Trap**: LinkedIn tested including negative signals (hide, unfollow, report) in the retrieval model and it made the AI WORSE at recommendations. They stripped negative signals entirely. The system only amplifies positive engagement. This means being ignored is neutral — not punishing. You can't get "algorithmically buried" by bad posts. You just don't get amplified.

- **The Generative Recommender Sequence**: The AI treats your engagement history as a SEQUENCE — a professional learning journey, not random data points. What you liked on Monday shapes what the AI shows you Tuesday and Wednesday. It's reading your curiosity arc. This means your audience's feed is a moving target — the content they need TODAY is shaped by what they consumed yesterday.

- **The Pod Detection Layer**: LinkedIn is actively building dwell-time analysis to distinguish real reading from scripted pod engagement. Short dwell times followed by immediate comments signal automation. Pod members are getting systematically deprioritized because the AI can detect "rapid comment, no actual reading" behavioral patterns. Authentic engagement with real dwell time is now the only safe engagement strategy.

- **The Adam Bird Correction**: A common myth is that engaging with other posts gives YOUR content a "boost." Diandra clarifies via Adam Bird's research: your outbound engagement shapes YOUR feed (what you see), not your content's reach (what others see). However, what you see shapes what you CREATE — so strategic engagement is still critical for inspiration, not algorithm manipulation.

### Exemplar 1: The Liquid Death Brandjack + Boomerang
- **Context**: A client used Liquid Death as the entity, breaking down how their creative team generates billions in earned media without a traditional ad budget.
- **The Example**: Post structured as a brandjack with genuine creative analysis of Liquid Death's advertising strategy. The VP of Creative at Liquid Death showed up in the comments and corrected a detail. That single correction comment got 190 likes. The algorithm read the VP's engagement as a signal that "something real is happening" and pushed harder.
- **What makes this excellent**: Demonstrates the boomerang effect in action — borrowed attention returned with compound interest. The "correction" was actually better than agreement because it proved the post was substantial enough to warrant a real response from the subject.

### Exemplar 2: The ChatGPT × Google Search Newsjack
- **Context**: Client covered the revelation that ChatGPT pulls directly from Google search results and contextualized what it means for SEO strategy.
- **The Example**: 293 comments, 234 reposts, 28,000 impressions. Posted within the 24-48 hour window. Not a summary of the news — a clear position on what the downstream effects would be for B2B SEO practitioners.
- **What makes this excellent**: Passed the "So What?" gate perfectly. Every reader could tell what the poster believed and had to decide whether they agreed or disagreed. Timing + position = distribution.

### Exemplar 3: The "Original Research" Hot Take
- **Context**: Client posted a direct contrarian claim: "Original research is outranking AI-written content. Not by a little, by a lot. And that gap is widening."
- **The Example**: 390 comments, 300 reposts, 3,198 likes. Binary engagement — people either agreed strongly or disagreed strongly. No "it depends" hedging. A clear position that forced a reaction.
- **What makes this excellent**: Passed the anxiety test — the poster was genuinely nervous to publish it. That nervousness is the signal that the take is contrarian enough to move people. The specificity ("not by a little, by a lot") prevented fence-sitting.

### Exemplar 4: The Algorithm Post That Saved Itself
- **Context**: Diandra's own post breaking down the 2026 LinkedIn algorithm changes — specifically the "saves are worth 5x likes" data from Authored Up.
- **The Example**: The post itself was architected as a save-worthy reference piece: a numbered list of the 8 algorithm changes with one actionable takeaway per change. The irony — a post about saves being the most important engagement signal became a heavily-saved post. Engagement velocity in the first hour was 4x her 30-day average.
- **What makes this excellent**: Meta-demonstration of Pattern 16 (Save Economy). The content format (numbered reference list with actionable data points) is exactly what triggers saves. She practiced what she preached, and the AI rewarded it because save-heavy engagement pushes content into broader candidate pools.

### Exemplar 5: The Small Account Client Win
- **Context**: New client with 1,200 followers, B2B fintech niche. First month using the 5-field optimization + semantic lane commitment strategy.
- **The Example**: Client rewrote headline from "CEO | Fintech | Builder" to "Making compliance painless for startup CFOs | Fintech regulatory strategy." Within 3 weeks, posts were reaching CFOs and compliance officers who had zero connection to the client — pure AI-driven discovery. Impressions went from 800 average to 4,200 average without follower growth.
- **What makes this excellent**: Proves Pattern 18 (Small Account Advantage) and Pattern 13 (5-Field Author Signal) in combination. The headline rewrite gave the AI semantic data to work with. The topic-lane posts gave the AI consistent signal. Small account, big reach — exactly what the new system enables.

### Anti-Exemplar: The Generic Industry Summary
- **What mediocre looks like**: "Big news! [Company] just announced [thing]. Here are 5 takeaways..." followed by bullet points that summarize the press release without any original analysis or position.
- **Why it fails**: No "So What?" answer. No original insight. The 200 people who already follow the creator might skim it. The algorithm sees low engagement velocity and kills distribution. The creator is reporting, not analyzing — and nobody needs another reporter when the press release is already public.

### Anti-Exemplar 2: The Pod-Boosted Post
- **What mediocre looks like**: Post gets 40 comments in 12 minutes, all from the same pod group. Comments are generic: "So true!" "Great insight!" "Absolutely agree!" Average dwell time: 3 seconds per commenter.
- **Why it fails**: The 2026 retrieval model actively detects this pattern — rapid engagement with near-zero dwell time signals automation, not genuine interest. LinkedIn's anti-pod layer deprioritizes these posts from candidate pools. The short-term engagement spike produces long-term reach suppression. Authentic engagement with real dwell time is the only sustainable strategy.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The Summary Trap**: Summarizing what a brand did / what a person said instead of adding your own insight. If you're reporting, not analyzing, the post dies.
2. **Forced Controversy**: Hot takes that are provocative for clicks but not genuinely held beliefs. If posting it doesn't make you nervous, it's not contrarian enough.
3. **Bucket Confusion**: Trying to make one post do all four jobs (reach + trust + convert + connect). Each post has ONE primary job.
4. **Infrastructure Neglect**: Relying on motivation instead of systems. Without the Notion calendar, Drive connection, and Claude project, you're starting from zero every time.
5. **The Profile-Link Stalk**: Adding profile URLs to your engagement list instead of recent-activity URLs — makes you look creepy.
6. **Premature Conversion**: Running conversion content before growth + authority have built trust. You can't convert an audience that doesn't trust you yet.
7. **The "Just Start" Fallacy**: Posting without researching competitors, outlier content, or ICP behavior first. Research before writing is non-negotiable.
8. **Inspiration Dependency**: Waiting for ideas to come to you instead of extracting from existing material (calls, docs, expert conversations).

## Voice DNA

- **Tone**: Direct, practical, confident without arrogance. Zero "guru energy." Speaks as someone who built from nothing and has the receipts.
- **Vocabulary**: "Content system" not "content strategy." "Content bank" not "ideas list." "Bucket" for content types. "Extract" not "create."
- **Sentence Structure**: Short, declarative. Frequent use of "So what?" and "Why?" as structural transitions. Conversational contractions.
- **Banned Phrases**: unlock, leverage, game-changer, dive deep, at the end of the day, thought leader (use "recognizable voice" instead), skyrocket, 10x
- **Cultural Markers**: Colombian-born, agency-founder perspective, speaks from builder experience not consultant theory. References getting fired as origin story, not trauma — it's fuel.
