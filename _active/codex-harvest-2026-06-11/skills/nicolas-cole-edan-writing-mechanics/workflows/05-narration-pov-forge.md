# Narration POV Forge

## Pre-Flight Gate

Use this when writing feels competent but generic, lacks worldview, overstates the moral, or hides the real belief.

## Skill Acquisition

Load:

- `skills/nicolas-cole-edan-writing-mechanics/genius.md`
- `skills/nicolas-cole-edan-writing-mechanics/references/hidden-knowledge.md`

## Execution

1. Identify the surface topic.
2. Extract the hidden belief underneath the piece:
   - What does the writer think most people misunderstand?
   - What judgment is implied but not said?
   - What does the narrator believe about the theme?
3. Draft 5 possible narration lines:
   - Quiet.
   - Sharp.
   - Reflective.
   - Contrarian.
   - Personal.
4. Place narration after a setup that earns it:
   - After description.
   - After action.
   - After necessary explanation.
5. Remove generic moralizing.
6. Protect scarcity: keep only the line that changes how the reader sees the piece.
7. Check fit with the writer/client voice.

## Content Type Adaptations

| Type | Narration Use |
|---|---|
| Fiction | Reveal character/narrator worldview without breaking scene spell |
| Memoir | Convert experience into earned perspective |
| Newsletter | Turn topic into a point of view |
| Thought leadership | State the belief behind the strategy |
| Ghostwriting | Match client worldview, not generic authority voice |
| Brand story | Make the brand's operating belief concrete |

## Output Requirements

Return:

1. Hidden belief.
2. Five narration candidate lines.
3. Recommended placement.
4. What setup the line needs.
5. Final narration line.
6. Warning if the line is too generic or too loud.

## Quality Gate

Narration must reveal a specific worldview. "This is important" is not narration; it is weak explanation.
