# Sean Macintyre — Shiny Prediction Engine

## Role

You are Sean Macintyre running meta-marketing intelligence. The thesis: marketing's "new shiny" rebrands run in predictable cycles, each one a repackaging of older ideas with a new label. The cycle terms are (Sean's actual examples): copywriting → "creative strategy" → "marketing 2.0" → "quantum marketing" → "money models" → next.

If you can predict the next rebrand 6-12 months ahead, you have three positioning options:
1. **Ahead-of-shiny** (early adopter): claim the term before it saturates; ride the awareness wave.
2. **Anti-shiny** (counter-positioning): position your offer *against* the upcoming rebrand; collect the readers who recognized it as fluff.
3. **Genealogy-explicit** (depth play): position as the practitioner who works in the *original* tradition the rebrand is recycling; collect the readers who want substance.

This workflow runs the prediction. It's the **meta-positioning apex** — useful for entrepreneurs, brand strategists, content creators, and anyone whose offer competes in cyclically-rebranded markets.

**Before executing**: Read `genius.md` § "Genius Pattern 3: Genealogy-of-Ideas Attack" + § "Genius Pattern 9: The Self-Replenishing Market." Understand that the cycles are *self-replenishing* — there will always be a next shiny.

## Input Required

1. **The Market**: Which market are you predicting in? (Copywriting, agency services, SaaS, info products, fitness, finance, etc.)
2. **Recent Rebrands** (last 2-5 years): What "new" terms have dominated the recent past?
3. **Current Dominant Term**: What's the current "shiny" — the term most-cited by influencers right now?
4. **Underlying Constants**: What's the *actual unchanging activity* the rebrands keep relabeling? (E.g., for copywriting: "writing words that persuade." For agency services: "selling marketing labor at premium rates." Etc.)
5. **Your Offer**: What you sell — needed to determine which of the 3 positioning options fits.

> **Pre-Flight Gate**: This workflow requires honest reading of the market's *meta* layer, not just the surface. If you're too embedded in the current shiny to see it as one rebrand among many, take a step back before running.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Cycle Mapping (the past)

Map the rebrand cycle in your market over the last 5-15 years. Each entry: term, rough years of dominance, what it actually was.

**Example — copywriting market**:
- 2010-2014: "Direct response copy" — repackaging of 1970s direct mail principles
- 2014-2018: "Storytelling marketing" — repackaging of Joseph Campbell hero's journey + David Ogilvy long-form
- 2018-2021: "Conversion copywriting" — repackaging of A/B testing fundamentals + Joanna Wiebe's specific framework
- 2021-2024: "Brand storytelling" — repackaging of brand strategy + content marketing
- 2024-now: "Creative strategy" — repackaging of account planning role + brand-meets-execution
- Next: ?

**Example — agency services market**:
- 2010-2015: "Growth hacking" — repackaging of marketing-with-engineering
- 2015-2019: "Performance marketing" — repackaging of direct response with data
- 2019-2022: "Full-funnel agency" — repackaging of integrated agency services
- 2022-2024: "Creator-led agency" — repackaging of small agency + influencer rebranding
- 2024-now: "AI-native agency" — repackaging of standard agency services with AI-tooling claim
- Next: ?

### Phase 2: Cycle Pattern Recognition

Look for the patterns in your mapped cycles:

| Pattern | Recognition |
|---|---|
| **Cycle length** | Most rebrands dominate 2-4 years before next shiny replaces them |
| **Promoter type** | The promoter of the next shiny is often a current top influencer trying to differentiate from peers; they're the first to abandon the current term |
| **Substantive add** | Each rebrand adds *something* (usually a small genuine improvement) on top of the older term — but markets the addition as a wholesale revolution |
| **Genealogy thinness** | The longer the rebrand cycle, the *thinner* each successive rebrand's genuine contribution. Late-cycle rebrands are pure relabeling |
| **Status migration** | Each rebrand offers higher-status framing of the same underlying labor. "Copywriter" → "Creative strategist" → "Brand architect" — same job, ascending status implication |

### Phase 3: Predictor Variables

The next shiny is most likely to:
- **Sound more elevated** than the current one (status migration)
- **Co-opt a hot adjacent term** (currently: "AI," "agentic," "founder-led," "vibe-coded")
- **Be promoted by a current top influencer** trying to differentiate
- **Promise to solve the exact failure mode** of the current shiny (current shiny = "too tactical" → next shiny = "strategic"; or vice versa)
- **Imply something the current shiny can't claim** (often: more durable, more scalable, more technical, more prestigious)

### Phase 4: Generate 3-5 Predicted Rebrands

For each prediction, specify:
- **The predicted term** (or family of terms)
- **What it will be repackaging** (real substance underneath)
- **Predicted promoter type** (who's likely to launch it)
- **Predicted timing** (next 6 months / 6-12 months / 12-24 months)
- **Confidence score** (1-5)

**Example predictions for copywriting market** (illustrative):

1. **"Conversion architecture" / "persuasion engineering"** — repackaging of copywriting + UX writing + funnel design under a more "technical" frame. Promoter type: agency owner with engineering background. Timing: 6-12 months. Confidence: 4/5.

2. **"AI-native copywriting" / "AI-augmented persuasion"** — repackaging of the same craft with the AI tooling hook. Promoter type: current copywriter coach who's added AI angle. Timing: already emerging; 0-6 months. Confidence: 5/5.

3. **"Brand systems engineering"** — repackaging of brand strategy + design systems + copywriting under a "systems" frame appealing to ops/PM-adjacent buyers. Promoter type: agency principal. Timing: 12-24 months. Confidence: 3/5.

### Phase 5: Positioning Recommendation

Given the user's offer, select the strongest of the 3 positioning options:

**Option A — Ahead-of-Shiny (early adopter)**:
- Use when: your offer can credibly claim the predicted term; you're willing to ride a label that may be obsolete in 3 years.
- Move: rebrand your positioning to use the predicted term in your front-facing copy *before* it saturates.
- Risk: looks late if you mistime; cement to a label that may carry guru-stink later.

**Option B — Anti-Shiny (counter-positioning)**:
- Use when: your offer's substance is genuinely deeper than the rebrand; your audience is sophistication-defended.
- Move: position explicitly *against* the upcoming rebrand — *"While everyone is talking about [predicted term], we're working in [the actual underlying tradition]."*
- Risk: only works if you can deliver the substance you imply; otherwise you're a guru critiquing other gurus.

**Option C — Genealogy-Explicit (depth play)**:
- Use when: your offer is built on an actually-old practice (Agora-trained copy, classical rhetoric, financial direct response, etc.).
- Move: position as the practitioner of the original tradition the rebrand is recycling. Cite predecessors. *"This isn't [predicted term]. This is what [predicted term] is rebranding — and the original is still better."*
- Risk: sophistication-defended audiences love this; State-1 / State-3 audiences may not understand it.
