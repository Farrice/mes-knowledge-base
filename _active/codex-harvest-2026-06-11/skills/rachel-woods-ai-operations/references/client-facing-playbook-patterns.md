# Rachel Woods - Client-Facing Playbook Patterns

Client-facing playbooks must protect trust. They need stricter review, source control, brand fit, and escalation rules than internal personal playbooks.

## Pattern 1: Monthly Client Performance Report

**Promise**: Turn raw performance data into a client-ready narrative with insights, risks, and next actions.

**Trigger**: Reporting period closes and source data is available.

**Inputs**:
- Reporting period.
- Client goals.
- Previous report.
- Campaign/channel data.
- Known launches, anomalies, or business context.

Core steps:
1. Collect source data and campaign context.
2. Validate freshness, completeness, and anomalies.
3. Identify wins, concerns, and unexplained changes.
4. Interpret against client goals.
5. Draft report narrative.
6. Score against client voice and evidence standards.
7. Flag claims needing human review.
8. Produce client-ready report plus internal notes.

Human gates:
- Before sending.
- When data conflicts.
- When recommending budget, offer, or strategy changes.

Delegation map:
- Objective: validate data freshness, calculate deltas, assemble tables.
- Good Enough: draft narrative, identify likely explanations, summarize wins and concerns.
- Expert: approve recommendations, handle relationship-sensitive language, decide strategic changes.

Example standard:
- Strong output ties every metric to a client goal, names the likely cause, states confidence, and gives a next action.
- Weak output lists metrics without interpretation or recommends changes without evidence.

Feedback loop:
- Track client questions, human rewrites, missing context, and unsupported claims.
- Patch the playbook after each report cycle with new client preferences, approved phrasing, and recurring data caveats.

## Pattern 2: Client Onboarding Intelligence Brief

**Promise**: Convert intake materials into a useful internal client brief.

**Trigger**: New client signs or kickoff is scheduled.

**Inputs**:
- Proposal or scope.
- Intake form.
- Sales notes.
- Website and public assets.
- Call transcript or notes.

Core steps:
1. Collect intake forms, website, CRM notes, proposal, and call notes.
2. Extract business model, audience, offer, objections, goals, and constraints.
3. Identify unknowns.
4. Draft onboarding brief.
5. Create kickoff questions.
6. Assign follow-up tasks.

Human gates:
- Before kickoff.
- Before making strategic claims.

Delegation map:
- Objective: collect and summarize provided materials.
- Good Enough: identify likely goals, risks, questions, and open loops.
- Expert: approve strategic diagnosis and client-sensitive interpretation.

Feedback loop:
- After kickoff, add missed questions, misunderstood context, and better intake prompts back into the playbook.

## Pattern 3: Client Deliverable Quality Review

**Promise**: Use the agency's quality standards as a first-pass reviewer before work reaches the client.

**Trigger**: Draft deliverable is ready for review.

**Inputs**:
- Draft deliverable.
- Client context.
- Deliverable rubric.
- Brand voice or style guide.
- Prior approved examples.

Core steps:
1. Identify deliverable type.
2. Load the relevant rubric and client context.
3. Score the deliverable.
4. Separate must-fix issues from preference notes.
5. Produce revision instructions.
6. Re-check the revised version.

Human gates:
- Before client delivery.
- If score is below the acceptable bar.

Delegation map:
- Objective: check required sections, formatting, and source presence.
- Good Enough: score against rubric and draft revision notes.
- Expert: approve final delivery and resolve taste or strategy calls.

Feedback loop:
- Compare AI review to human review and patch rubric gaps after every miss.

## Pattern 4: Client Strategy Audit

**Promise**: Produce a structured audit that finds missed opportunities without generic recommendations.

**Trigger**: Client requests audit, renewal review, or new strategy review.

**Inputs**:
- Audit scope.
- Client goals.
- Current assets.
- Performance data.
- Competitor or market context where available.

Core steps:
1. Define audit scope and client objective.
2. Collect assets and current performance.
3. Compare current state to standards.
4. Identify bottlenecks.
5. Prioritize recommendations by impact, effort, confidence, and risk.
6. Draft client-facing audit and internal implementation notes.

Human gates:
- Before recommendations are presented.
- When data is incomplete.

Delegation map:
- Objective: collect assets, check completeness, summarize current state.
- Good Enough: identify bottlenecks, rank opportunities, draft audit narrative.
- Expert: approve recommendations and handle strategic tradeoffs.

Feedback loop:
- Track recommendation acceptance, client objections, and implementation results; patch scoring rules quarterly.

## Client-Facing Quality Rules

- Never invent metrics, quotes, or facts.
- Mark unsupported assumptions.
- Preserve client relationship context.
- Avoid overpromising.
- Separate internal critique from client-facing language.
- Include confidence levels for major recommendations.
- Create a human approval gate before anything leaves the team.
