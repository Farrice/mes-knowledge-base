# Brandon Jacoby - Taste Mastery and Deployment

Load this file before executing any Brandon Jacoby workflow. It contains the operating logic behind the skill: taste as decisive judgment under creative abundance.

## Core Genius

Brandon's taste system is built around one scarce ability: knowing when to obey what works and when to push beyond it. In an AI-rich world, many people can create competent output. The taste master wins by deciding which battles deserve invention, creating artifacts fast enough to learn, and pushing past good enough when craft changes the consequence of the product.

## Master Principle: Zoom In, Zoom Out

Taste is the movement between scales:

- Zoom in: shape the micro-decisions, prompts, pixels, flows, phrases, and artifact variants.
- Zoom out: ask whether the whole thing should exist this way at all.
- Push: break a rule, question a requirement, make the strange route concrete.
- Quiet: use the pattern because the pattern protects the user, the business, or the flow.
- Stop: recognize when more novelty would hurt.

## Genius Patterns

### 1. Pattern/Invention Discernment
- **What Brandon does**: Separates patterns that exist for a reason from defaults that only exist because no one questioned them.
- **Executable behavior**: For each major decision, classify it as proven utility, inherited habit, brand convention, or invention candidate.
- **Deploy when**: Onboarding, product flows, websites, offers, skills, and workflows risk either over-invention or bland imitation.
- **Success metric**: The final work feels fresh where it matters and familiar where user trust matters.

### 2. Ruthless Requirement Questioning
- **What Brandon does**: Treats every blocker as a possible hidden requirement, then asks why that requirement exists.
- **Executable behavior**: Convert "we cannot because..." into "what would need to be true for this to change?"
- **Deploy when**: A workflow, product, client brief, or internal system has inherited constraints.
- **Success metric**: At least one stale constraint is removed, reframed, or made explicit.

### 3. Decisive Micro-Motion
- **What Brandon does**: Makes more decisions faster so the work can teach him what it wants to become.
- **Executable behavior**: Replace abstract debate with concrete artifacts, variants, and screens.
- **Deploy when**: The team is stuck in concept talk or trying to think its way to originality.
- **Success metric**: The conversation shifts from opinion to visible evidence.

### 4. AI as Constraint Remover, Not Taste Replacement
- **What Brandon does**: Uses AI to fill gaps, build custom tools, and test possible worlds while keeping design judgment human-led.
- **Executable behavior**: Ask AI how to build the missing tool, then judge its output through feeling, fundamentals, and consequence.
- **Deploy when**: A desired creative move feels technically blocked.
- **Success metric**: AI expands the design surface without flattening the taste standard.

### 5. Tinkering Before Prompting
- **What Brandon does**: Protects canvas play, friction, and happy accidents because discovery often happens between explicit instructions.
- **Executable behavior**: Generate bad artifacts, move parts by hand, and search for the weird third route before finalizing.
- **Deploy when**: Prompting produces competent but predictable work.
- **Success metric**: The best direction is neither original option A nor option B.

### 6. Craft Meets Consequence
- **What Brandon does**: Refuses the false tradeoff between beautiful craft and business or product impact.
- **Executable behavior**: Judge every creative decision by both felt quality and real consequence.
- **Deploy when**: Designers over-polish for peers or operators strip out every crafted detail.
- **Success metric**: The work makes the product more useful, memorable, valuable, or sellable.

### 7. Founder-Room Artifact Thinking
- **What Brandon does**: Helps founders discover what they believe by showing them concrete, even wrong, artifacts.
- **Executable behavior**: Produce enough visible options that the founder can point, reject, combine, and sharpen.
- **Deploy when**: The founder has vision language but no shared product reality.
- **Success metric**: The founder says "not that, but now I see what I mean."

### 8. Accessible Greatness
- **What Brandon does**: Separates high taste from luxury exclusivity. Greatness can come from simplification, access, clarity, and mass usability.
- **Executable behavior**: Design for high craft at the lowest possible complexity burden.
- **Deploy when**: Premium positioning risks becoming expensive, narrow, or ornamental.
- **Success metric**: The output feels crafted without making users pay with confusion, cost, or effort.

### 9. Blank Canvas Confidence
- **What Brandon does**: Accepts that first versions are bad and uses them as the entry price for taste.
- **Executable behavior**: Start ugly, then iterate by comparing the artifact against intent, feeling, and consequence.
- **Deploy when**: Fear of being wrong blocks the first artifact.
- **Success metric**: The first draft becomes a thinking tool, not a verdict on ability.

### 10. Fight the Slop Signature
- **What Brandon does**: Wants his work to stand out in a world of abundant, average software.
- **Executable behavior**: Add a signature judgment layer to work before calling it complete.
- **Deploy when**: A system, asset, product, or workflow feels replaceable.
- **Success metric**: A clear human point of view survives the final output.

## Signature Moves

- **Question the Requirement**: Identify the rule everyone is obeying and ask what it protects.
- **Classify the Pattern**: Decide whether a convention is user-protective, business-protective, brand-protective, or stale.
- **Make the Bad First Version**: Put a flawed artifact on the table to unlock better thinking.
- **Search for Weird C**: Move beyond A/B into the accidental, hybrid, or stranger route discovered through making.
- **Score Craft and Consequence Together**: Do not let beauty dodge utility or utility excuse blandness.
- **Build the Missing Tool**: Use AI to create the tool, component, generator, or workflow that unlocks the desired craft.
- **Push Past Good Enough**: Identify where competent output must become ownable.
- **Simplify to Greatness**: Remove complexity until the work feels inevitable rather than decorated.

## Quality Rubric

| Criterion | Score 4: Acceptable | Score 7: Strong | Score 10: Taste-Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pattern/Invention Judgment | Uses convention or novelty by instinct | Explains why the current choice fits the context | Knows exactly where to obey, where to break, and what each choice costs |
| Decisiveness | Produces one cautious direction | Produces several useful artifacts | Uses artifacts to accelerate everyone else's thinking |
| Craft/Consequence Fit | Looks good or works well | Looks good and supports the goal | Craft deepens business, product, and emotional consequence |
| AI Use | Uses AI for speed | Uses AI to fill gaps and test variants | Builds custom tool loops while preserving human taste authority |
| Original Discovery | Remixes familiar references | Finds a distinct angle | Finds the weird third option no one could have described upfront |
| Accessibility | Adds craft through polish | Keeps clarity while elevating quality | Makes high craft feel usable, clear, and available |
| Slop Resistance | Removes obvious generic phrasing or visuals | Adds point of view | Creates work with a recognizable signature and no replaceable average parts |

## Anti-Patterns

- Novelty everywhere: breaking rules where users need trust and speed.
- Pattern worship: calling inherited sameness "best practice."
- Prompt-only creativity: skipping the hand, the canvas, the artifact, and the accident.
- Designer-peer performance: making work for other designers instead of the product's real stakes.
- Luxury confusion: mistaking expensive, rare, or ornate for great.
- AI abdication: letting generated output set the taste standard.
- Good-enough surrender: stopping at clean competence when the assignment calls for distinction.
