# Donald Miller Cognitive Load Hidden Knowledge

## Source Package

Primary expansion source: `extractions/donald-miller/storybrand-message-clarity-system/`

## Hidden Knowledge

- Customers almost never report confusion; they simply disengage.
- The business owner is usually blind to the weight of their own language.
- Clever language feels valuable internally but often adds work for the customer.
- A sound bite is not a caption idea; it is a reusable sales asset.
- Simplicity can look less impressive to the creator and more useful to the buyer.
- If a message cannot survive a distracted scroll, it is too heavy for curiosity collateral.
- The five sound bites should act as a campaign foundation before copy variants are generated.
- Repetition is not inauthentic when it reduces customer confusion.
- A beautiful design cannot rescue a high-load message.
- Every channel should carry the same core words so the market can memorize and repeat them.

## Anti-Patterns

- Multiple customer problems in one campaign.
- Abstract category language before the felt problem.
- Founding dates, mission statements, or company pride in curiosity collateral.
- Sound bites rewritten differently for each channel.
- Treating cognitive-load scoring as subjective taste instead of a concrete rewrite gate.
