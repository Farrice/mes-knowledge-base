# Lucas Alpay Storytelling - Kishotenketsu Contrast Story System

## Role

Use this workflow when the material is being weakened by fake conflict, problem-agitation, forced villains, or a rigid three-act frame. The job is not to remove tension. The job is to find a better engine: contrast, texture, process, perspective shift, and integration.

## Inputs

- Draft, idea, scene, post, page, ad, campaign, media script, messaging note, positioning note, product/service/concept brief, client-facing service package, or ghostwriting source.
- Audience or reader.
- Desired action, feeling, or interpretation.
- Domain: fiction, social, content, ads, media, creative strategy, brand, marketing, messaging, positioning, copy, ghostwriting, product/service/concept, or general writing.
- Proof assets or voice source when the output is public, revenue-facing, or ghostwritten.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Engine Diagnosis

Decide whether the current engine is conflict, contrast, proof, clarity, or voice.

Flag:

- fake villain
- fake urgency
- problem-agitation relapse
- atmosphere with no turn
- process summarized as filler
- inspirational-template ghostwriting
- brand as feature list instead of world
- messaging as benefit soup instead of changed-world explanation
- positioning as competitor comparison without a category variable
- product/service/concept prose as abstract promise without a lived use-case scene

If proof, offer clarity, or voice fidelity is the real blocker, route there before rewriting.

### Phase 2: Ki World

Build the state of being:

- where the reader/protagonist/customer is
- what rules govern the world
- what rituals, tools, materials, settings, and repeated actions matter
- what contrast is already present but unclaimed

### Phase 3: Sho Process

Turn process into movement.

Render the relevant sequence with sensory and emotional weight. The process must reveal pressure, identity, world rules, trust, or desire. It cannot be decorative.

### Phase 4: Ten Shift

Introduce the turn:

- a variable forcing adaptation
- a different point of view
- a memory
- a time jump
- new information
- a changed context
- an unrelated element that later connects

The turn must recontextualize earlier details. It does not need to be a shock twist. In this workflow, Ten means a turn, change of plane, or new relation that makes the established world newly meaningful.

### Phase 5: Ketsu Integration

End by reconciling the earlier world with the shift.

Use coexistence, adaptation, changed meaning, a new ritual, or a clearer next action. Do not force victory/defeat unless the material actually wants a battle.

### Phase 6: Domain Gate

Apply only the necessary next gate:

| Domain Need | Next Gate |
|---|---|
| Lived texture or ghostwriting | Eric Roth visual prose or ghostwriter elevation |
| Social retention | Shaan Puri/Kallaway frame and hook checks |
| Brand world | Dai/Oren/Alex Copper world-building checks |
| Ads or media deployment | Platform pull, conversion clarity, proof boundary, and publishable-copy gate |
| Messaging or positioning | April Dunford/StoryBrand clarity plus category-variable and boundary-line checks |
| Product, service, or concept narrative | Brand-world gate plus mechanism, use-case scene, buyer constraint, and proof boundary |
| Revenue copy | Sam Parr story desire, Nicolas Cole sentence craft, Harry Dry audit, then publishable-copy gate |
| General taste | High-taste writing OS |

## Quality Gate

Fail if:

- the output simply labels Ki/Sho/Ten/Ketsu without a felt turn
- removing the villain removes all stakes
- texture is decorative rather than structural
- copy loses proof, mechanism, or CTA clarity
- ghostwriting loses the client's voice
- cultural claims exceed the source boundary

Pass only when the rewrite visibly improves reader movement: inhabit, deepen, reframe, integrate.
