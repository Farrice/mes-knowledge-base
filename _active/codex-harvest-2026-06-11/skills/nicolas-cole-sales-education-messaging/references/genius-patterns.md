# Genius Patterns

## 1. Sales Is Education

The sale is not created by pressure. It is created by moving the buyer from uninformed default-no to informed decision.

## 2. Objections Are Not Facts

An objection may be stated with confidence and still be wrong. Treat it as evidence of a missing belief, missing mechanism, missing context, or wrong comparison.

## 3. Questions Reveal Hidden Resistance

When a buyer asks logistical, mechanism, cost, timing, or fit questions, they are revealing the objections that make them default to no.

## 4. Educated Buyers Self-Select

Once buyers understand the problem and category of solution, they either sell themselves or say no with clarity. Both are healthier than a pressured yes.

## 5. Category Before Offer

Do not rush to "my product" or "my service." First teach the category of solution and why that category is the right response to this problem.

## 6. Specific Knowledge Beats General Status

The seller does not need to be smarter than the buyer in every domain. They need deeper evidence and pattern recognition in the tiny specific domain being sold.

## 7. Problem-Side Depth Creates Urgency

Problem awareness alone is weak. Urgency comes from reasons, consequences, opportunity cost, and second-order emotional impact.

## 8. Solution-Side Depth Creates Confidence

A buyer needs to understand the category, why it works, the benefits it creates, and the ultimate positive outcome before the seller becomes the obvious executor.

## 9. Education Compounds

Even when someone does not buy now, education makes the seller memorable as the person who clarified the issue. That can turn into later demand.

## 10. Medium-Agnostic Sales Arc

Calls, DMs, sales pages, and launch emails all need the same logic: problem education, category education, and a clean next step.

## 11. Wrong Metric Diagnosis

Some objections appear rational because the buyer is using the wrong metric. Diagnose whether they are measuring one category with another category's rules.

## 12. Buyer Agency Is The Trust Engine

The goal is not to force agreement. The goal is to help the buyer make an informed decision they can stand behind.
