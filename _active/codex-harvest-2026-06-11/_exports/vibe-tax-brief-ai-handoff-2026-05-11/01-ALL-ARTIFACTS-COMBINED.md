## Buyer Reality Map

| Buyer Reality | What It Sounds Like | What The Brief Must Reveal |
|---|---|---|
| Offer is interesting but not urgent | "People like it, but they do not book." | what is missing from pain, stakes, or proof |
| Buyer language is off | "I know what I do, but I cannot explain it simply." | the words buyers already use |
| Competitors sound identical | "Everyone says strategy, clarity, growth, AI." | the claims that are now category wallpaper |

## Claim Labeling Standard

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified | Source-backed or directly evidenced by buyer language |
| Likely | Strong inference from credible evidence, but not directly confirmed |
| Assumption | Useful working hypothesis to test |
| Do Not Claim | Too broad, unsupported, or too confident for public copy |

## Market Truth Gate

Before a brief ships:

- Identify the buyer's real alternative if they do nothing.
- Name at least 3 substitute options or competitor frames.
- Capture at least 3 buyer-language phrases.
- Label every major claim as Verified, Likely, Assumption, or Do Not Claim.
- Add a contradiction note when sources or buyer language disagree.

# Human Resonance Gate

The Vibe Tax Brief must feel human-to-human. The buyer should not feel targeted by a machine or diagnosed by a distant consultant. They should feel that someone paid attention to the real emotional cost of guessing.

## Buyer Fear Map

| Fear | Human Translation | Brief Response |
|---|---|---|
| Making decisions from bad signal | "What if I am acting on the wrong evidence?" | separate observed buyer evidence from trend/content noise |
| Looking smart but not moving buyers | "People engage, but the market is not actually moved." | rank conversion signal above engagement signal |
| Looking naive | "What if the market already knows something I do not?" | show category assumptions and hidden comparisons |
| Wasting momentum | "What if I spend another month polishing the wrong thing?" | identify false signals and first test |
| Being ignored | "What if my work is good but invisible?" | map shortlist readiness and social repeatability |
| Sounding generic | "What if AI and competitors made my offer sound like everyone else?" | expose language sameness and proof gaps |
| Choosing wrong | "What if I pick the wrong audience, angle, or message?" | create evidence-backed angle options |
| Losing client trust | "If I advise from false signal, I look careless." | include client-cost-if-wrong as a diagnostic field |
| Being sold to | "What if this is just another strategy call disguised as help?" | give useful diagnostic value before asking for money |

## Buyer Desire Map

| Desire | Human Translation | Package Must Provide |
|---|---|---|
| Relief | "Tell me what is actually going on." | plain-English diagnosis |
| Certainty | "Give me enough proof to act." | claim labels and next test |
| Language | "Give me words I can use tomorrow." | buyer phrases, one-liners, sales openers |
| Direction | "Show me what to fix first." | priority order |
| Recognition | "Make me feel understood." | buyer language before expert language |

## Six Diagnostic Scores

Use a 0-5 score per dimension.

| Score | What It Measures | 0-1 False Signal Risk | 2-3 Partial Signal | 4-5 Buyer-Ready Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Channel / Context Fit | whether signal comes from the same arena where buyers decide | wrong arena: vanity platform, peer applause, generic trend | some overlap but weak buyer intent | same context where buyers search, compare, decide, or pay |
| Buyer Precision | whether the audience is tied to a real situation | broad, demographic, or vibe-based | buyer type named but not decision-specific | real trigger, constraint, situation, and buying moment |
| Pain Point Specificity | whether pain has consequence and emotion | generic clarity/growth/overwhelm | plausible but emotionally soft | external problem, internal feeling, and cost of delay |
| Solution Novelty | whether the answer escapes commodity sameness | obvious, everywhere, or template-like | useful angle with weak mechanism | non-obvious mechanism or memorable reframe |
| Walk-Away Value | whether the buyer leaves with a decision | "interesting" only | one useful insight | clearer decision, sharper language, or visible correction |
| Signal Consistency | whether the pattern repeats across evidence | one loud comment, viral post, or hunch | pattern appears but mixed intent | repeats across buyer words, objections, alternatives, response, and commercial behavior |

## Score Bands

| Score | Meaning | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 24-30 | strong enough to build from | Keep |
| 18-23 | usable, but needs proof | Revise |
| 12-17 | risky hypothesis | Test |
| Under 12 | likely Vibe Tax | Stop |

## Human-to-Human Copy Gate

Public copy must answer:

1. What does this buyer secretly suspect is not working?
2. What are they afraid would be embarrassing to admit?
3. What have they already tried?
4. What signal have they mistaken for truth?
5. What would make them feel safer taking one paid step?
6. What phrase would they repeat to a peer?

Fail copy that sounds clever but does not make a real person feel seen.

Hard fail: the brief makes the buyer feel judged by a consultant instead of seen by someone who understands the decision pressure.

## How It Works

The buyer answers 12 questions. Each question is scored 0-5.

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0-1 | false signal risk |
| 2-3 | partial signal |
| 4-5 | buyer-ready signal |

Total raw score: 60. Convert to 100 if needed.

## Questions

### Channel / Context Fit

1. Are you using signals from the same context where buyers actually decide, search, compare, or pay?
2. Can you name which signals you are ignoring because they do not predict buyer action?

### Buyer Precision

3. Is your buyer tied to a real trigger, constraint, and buying moment?
4. Can you describe the decision pressure the buyer feels when the problem becomes urgent?

### Pain Point Specificity

5. Does your message name the external problem, internal frustration, and consequence of delay?
6. Does your copy use phrases buyers would actually say under stress?

### Solution Novelty

7. Does your offer reveal a non-obvious mechanism or reframe buyers remember?
8. Can a buyer explain why your approach is different from the obvious alternative?

### Walk-Away Value

9. Would the buyer leave with a clearer decision, sharper language, or visible correction?
10. Does every finding lead to keep, revise, stop, or test?

### Signal Consistency

11. Does the pattern repeat across buyer words, objections, alternatives, content response, and commercial behavior?
12. Can you show receipts or confidence labels for the signal you are trusting?

This offer is not suffering from low AI awareness. The likely gap is buyer specificity.

# Delivery SOP - Cash-First Services

## Universal Delivery Boundary

Keep every first project small enough to complete within 48 hours and 1-4 hours/day.

If the buyer asks for a bigger build, sell the small diagnostic or first workflow first. Do not accept a broad custom implementation before trust, scope, and proof exist.

## Intake

Ask only for what the first version needs:

- desired outcome
- current process
- example input
- example output
- tools involved
- what breaks today
- what must be true for the first version to count as useful

Do not request sensitive account access unless the buyer has paid, the scope requires it, and a safer alternative is unavailable.

## Delivery Flow

### AI Workflow Integration Sprint

1. Map current process.
2. Identify the smallest repeated task.
3. Build before/after workflow map.
4. Draft prompt pack.
5. Draft SOP.
6. Run acceptance checklist.
7. Send handoff and optional walkthrough.

### Lead List + Outreach Starter Pack

1. Define buyer fit.
2. Define disqualifiers.
3. Build source map.
4. Manually qualify prospects.
5. Write first-message angles by segment.
6. Add follow-up prompts and objections.
7. Deliver tracker-ready table.

### The Vibe Tax Brief

1. Clarify offer and buyer.
2. Identify the decision the brief must protect.
3. Map false signals, alternatives, and substitutes.
4. Gather buyer language.
5. Map hidden objections, proof gaps, and pain.
6. Create offer-angle shortlist and sales openers.
7. Recommend keep, revise, stop, or test actions.
8. Deliver brief plus next action.

### AI Content Misfire Audit

Use `_active/content-system-audit/fulfillment-sop.md`.

## Handoff Template

```text
Here is the first version.

What I fixed:
- [fix 1]
- [fix 2]
- [fix 3]

How to use it tomorrow:
1. [step]
2. [step]
3. [step]

What I would watch:
- [risk]

If you want the next layer installed:
- [small upsell option]
```

## Proof Capture

After delivery, ask:

```text
Was this useful enough to keep using? If yes, what specific part saved time, made the decision clearer, or made the next action easier?
```

Track:

- before state
- after state
- buyer quote
- implementation request
- paid upsell
- delivery hours
