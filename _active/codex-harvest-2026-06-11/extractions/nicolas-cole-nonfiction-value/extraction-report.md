# Nicolas Cole - Nonfiction Value Architecture Extraction Report

## Core Thesis

The value of nonfiction writing can be reverse engineered before drafting. A reader values concrete deliverables: tips, stats, steps, lessons, benefits, reasons, mistakes, examples, questions, and personal proof/story. The writer's job is to choose which value type is in the spotlight, list the exact deliverables, then increase specificity, objectivity, and instruction until the piece clearly gives the reader something.

## Genius Patterns

1. Value Before Prose: the core decision comes before sentence structure, adjectives, or style.
2. Ten Value Modes: nonfiction value repeatedly resolves into tips, stats, steps, lessons, benefits, reasons, mistakes, examples, questions, or story.
3. Spotlight Discipline: one value mode must be primary for the piece, even if secondary modes support it.
4. List-Level Architecture: the writer does not need 3,000 draft words to discover value; they need the list of what they are giving.
5. Specificity as Proficiency: writing quality rises with specificity, objectivity, actionability, and instruction.
6. Bundled Term Detection: weak nonfiction hides inside broad terms like love, push, accountability, discipline, mindset, or consistency.
7. Moment Translation: abstract advice becomes valuable when translated into a literal moment the reader can picture.
8. Skim-First Delivery: readers decide value by skimming title and subheads before reading.
9. Promise-Delivery Alignment: subheads reveal whether the piece delivers the thing the title promised.
10. Same Idea, Higher Resolution: mediocre and useful writing can say the same thing, but useful writing says it at a more specific level.

## Hidden Knowledge

- The outline is not a container for the draft; it is the value proof.
- A subhead is a receipt for the title's promise.
- Most weak nonfiction is not wrong. It is too bundled to be useful.
- "What am I giving the reader?" is stronger than "What am I writing about?"
- The list is the first product. Prose is the packaging.
- Specificity is reader trust. A measurable moment signals the writer actually understands the problem.

## Hall of Fame Exemplars

### 1. High Agency Kids Tips

Weak version: "Love your kids. Push them. Hold them accountable."

Cole's upgraded version turns the same idea into moments: no phones at the dinner table, full attention, no escape hatch after failure, and consequences when promises are broken. The value rises because the reader can imagine, measure, and execute the advice.

### 2. Four-Second Subhead Test

If a title promises "tips to raise high agency kids," the reader skims for the specificity of the tips. Generic subheads tell the reader the writer is not giving much. Specific subheads prove delivery before the body copy is read.

### 3. Value Spotlight Decision

Before drafting, the writer chooses whether the piece is giving tips, mistakes, reasons, examples, questions, or another value mode. That choice determines what the outline must contain and prevents meandering opinion writing.

## Signature Moves

1. Ask "which value mode is in the spotlight?"
2. Convert topic into a list of reader deliverables.
3. Identify broad or bundled terms.
4. Translate each broad term into a literal moment.
5. Make the moment measurable.
6. Check whether the subheads deliver the title promise in four seconds.
7. Add secondary value modes only after the primary value is clear.
8. Use examples and stories to create confidence, not decoration.

## Quality Rubric

| Criterion | Weak | Strong | Savant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value Clarity | Topic is named, but reader value is unclear | One primary value mode is clear | The title, outline, and subheads all prove the same value promise |
| Specificity | Uses bundled words | Converts broad claims into concrete actions | Uses literal moments the reader can imagine, measure, and repeat |
| Objectivity | Advice is subjective | Includes visible criteria | Reader can judge whether they did the thing correctly |
| Actionability | Reader feels inspired | Reader knows a next action | Reader can execute without needing another explanation |
| Skim Value | Subheads are vague labels | Subheads show useful points | Subheads alone make the piece feel worth saving |
| Promise Delivery | Body drifts from title | Most sections support title | Every section is a receipt for the promised value |
| Value Stacking | Adds random examples | Secondary modes support primary value | Tips, steps, examples, questions, and story compound without clutter |

## Build Decision

Create `nicolas-cole-nonfiction-value-architecture` as a distinct skill. It overlaps with sentence craft, but it governs the earlier decision layer: what the piece gives the reader and how the outline proves it.
