See most of us need some underlying feeling of familiarity to have any
feeling of comfort. A completely new idea tends to make most people
suspicious and ill-at-ease, at first. What is worse, this discomfort can be a
huge obstacle for persuaders-because so often we are promoting ideas
or products that are in some way different from "the old standby."

And depending on how new and different what you're selling is, you
have a couple of options.
A quick solution is often a metaphor.
What's that? Maybe you're remembering some complicated or
convoluted definition from high school English class. Put that aside, and
understand that a metaphor is when you describe one thing to make it
easier to understand another thing. Anne Miller, the expert on metaphors
I always turn to (and author of a great book about using metaphors in
business, The Tall Lady with the Iceberg), says a metaphor is simply a
comparison.
Let's look at a metaphor in action, to create a story that eases the
acceptance of something new.
Suppose you are selling a business insurance policy where most people,
for decades, have been used to getting three types of protection-and
your insurance policy, innovatively, has five.
Using a metaphor, you could say,
You know how when you put on a glove to protect yourself from the
cold, it snuggly covers all four fingers and your thumb on each hand?
