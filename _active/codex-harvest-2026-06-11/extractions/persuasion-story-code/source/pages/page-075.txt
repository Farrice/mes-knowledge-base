We had to be shown. So we got samples. And we wore them. And

Then we washed them—with no sweat. They looked and felt like

forever.
The copywriter in this ad is playful, but purposeful. If you've ever
owned and worn a normal pair of socks, you know that they get holes in
them and they don't last forever!
So, think about how you would test a pair of socks that someone told you
were "indestructible." It seems that's what the copywriter did. And they
detailed out all the tests: wearing the socks, washing the socks, torturing
the socks.
That's the key to putting together a Story About A Hard-To-Believe
Claim Of Endurance. Think hard about how a reasonable person would
actually test the product to see how well it holds up. If you're honest—
and, for any number of reasons, you should be-actually perform those
tests.
Then, report the results in a story. It's OK if you have a little fun in the
way you report them.
STORIES ABOUT IF YOU WANT A REFUND
One of the biggest fears people have before they buy is: What if
something goes wrong?
Direct marketers always use guarantees to help reduce the feeling of risk
in a purchase. People in business who have never offered guarantees
before are typically horrified by this idea when they first hear it. Or at
least concerned. They believe that customers will take ruthless advantage
of merchants and rob them blind by asking for refunds.
