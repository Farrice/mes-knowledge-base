Think about that relatability transformation: Haughty people with big
titles morph into humble grieving parents you care about, all in the
timespan of about a minute. Helped, of course, by some very adept
acting and screenwriting.
Yes, indeed. Creating relatability is one of Hollywood's greatest illusions.
It's really quite amazing how hard entertainment pros work at this. And
how successful they are.
With the movie companies' special equipment and actors' special talents,
film crews spend hours upon hours on carefully crafted make-believe
sets in front of high-powered cameras and lights.
So many of the people you see on screen, somehow, seem like someone
you know—or could know. All this thanks to the skills of screenwriters,
directors, actors, lighting directors, costume designers, hair and makeup
artists, cinematographers and the rest of the crew.
Millions of dollars are at stake every time someone makes a major feature
film. A lot of deliberate effort goes into making these characters seem like
someone who could be your neighbor or relative. Ordinary people. Hey,
there even was a movie titled Ordinary People-directed by and starring,
of course, household-name multimillionaires.
Well, I guess, they were ordinary people—-if, that is, you live in Malibu or
Beverly Hills.
The extraordinary effort to create the illusion of relatability is "top of
mind" in Tinseltown. Hollywood has an entire sub-industry teaching
people in the film and TV industries how to make characters and stories
relatable.
