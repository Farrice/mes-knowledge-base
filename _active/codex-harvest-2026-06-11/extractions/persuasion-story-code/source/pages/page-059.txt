As an observer of human nature, I thought about this for a while and
decided the cop who made the sneering remark was actually pointing out
what goes on whenever two or more people get together. (Including, by
the way, cops.)
And actually, there was nothing particularly insightful about his
comment (even though it did add some great dramatic tension at that
point in the plot of this particular show).
Fact is, we all measure our status against that of others, all the time. Not
always consciously, either. Primarily because we are often taught by
teachers and religious leaders that we shouldn't. John Wooden, former
UCLA basketball coach, was called "the greatest coach of them all" by
Sports Illustrated magazine. Wooden emphasized in a TED Talk that the
best path to success was not to compare yourself to others. The path was
only to be the best that you could be.
Many other experts agree with Wooden. Only one problem: It's not what
people do. Regardless of the advice, we constantly compare ourselves to
others.
We have Nobel Prize Winners-and then we have all the lesser mortals.
