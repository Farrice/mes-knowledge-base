CHAPTER 6
REASSURANCE STORIES
People are funny. We rarely have just one emotion at a time. When we
are thrilled about something, there's also a part of us waiting to find out
if there's a catch. When we're excited about something happening now,
we're also wondering what will happen next. When we are at our most
decisive, our most timid and wishy-washy worries and doubts dance at
the edges of our minds.
We may not be completely aware of these multiple emotions as they
occur. But anyone in the business of persuasion is keenly aware that
prospects have more than one emotion at a time.
Take our whole legal system. As a juror, you have to be convinced beyond
a reasonable doubt that someone accused of a crime is guilty.
What does that mean? The way I see it, that means you can have a belief
that someone is guilty (first emotion), but if your doubt (second emotion)
is strong enough, then you cannot convict that person of the crime
they're charged with.
Well, if you can have belief and doubt at the same time, then you are
certainly experiencing two emotions simultaneously.
Most persuaders are not government prosecutors. But another version of
the same problem occurs for everyone else. No matter how much
rospects like what we are saying, they also have doubt... skepticism..
ear of the unknown... and similar feelings that stall or preven
persuasion. All these feelings at the same time.
