A persuasion story lie has a special quality. It moves like lightning.
Seemingly at the speed of light. And it may help you get what you want
quickly—in the short term. But the person who falls for your story, and
realizes they've been duped, reacts like they've been struck by lightning.
At first, they feel shocked... then, burned.
Of course, when you tell a persuasion story that's a lie, you may get
away with it.
But not once people realize they've been lied to.
If it's one person, and they mostly keep it to themself, then... you've got
an unhappy customer.
But in these days of social media, people don't usually keep things to
themselves.
And if you have lied to a large group of people... well, that chicken's
coming home to roost. Trouble ahead for you.
The Jeans That (Don't Really) Last Forever
Suppose you came up with a new kind of jeans. Your market research
tells you the biggest consumer complaint is that jeans wear out after a
few years. So you concoct this elaborate persuasion story about how your
jeans will last for 10 years.
You get tons of publicity, and tons of sales. Even though your own lab
simulations of wearing and washing have shown you your jeans will
actually start to wear out in three years.
But your attitude is a combination of "Don't worry, be happy" and "Que
será, será." You might even imagine you will go down in history
alongside the original jeans pioneer, Levi Strauss. His heirs owned a
major league baseball team. Maybe yours will, too. Visions of glory
dance in your head.

have forgotten your "optimistic" claim about how long the jeans last.
