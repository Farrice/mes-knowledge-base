The first thing to notice about this story is how it creates polarizing
identification among the members of its audience. The people who will get
drawn in: Readers and listeners who are following fitness routines and
unhappy with some of the results will identify quickly with this story
and get drawn in.
But this will probably not be very interesting to people who are not all
that interested in new techniques that solve stubborn problems. Because
people who are not fitness fanatics-or, those who are fitness fanatics
without belly-fat worries—will not really identify with the person in the
story.
And that's exactly the result you want. When you have a targeted
prospect, pull that person closer to you. But at the same time, be willing
to risk pushing everyone else away.
The other great thing about this story is it conveys a sense of relief at the
end of what must have been a long, frustrating search. Discovering this
one device (which has yet to be revealed in the example above) solved
the problem that nothing else could solve.
Once you get your prospect actively anticipating getting that sense of
relief themselves, you have a much more interested prospect than you
did just moments ago.
