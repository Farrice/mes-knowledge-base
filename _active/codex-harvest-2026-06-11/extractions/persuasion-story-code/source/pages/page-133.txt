The key to making these stories work is to provide enough information,
as simply as possible, to keep your explanation as exciting and
convincing at the same time. But don't get stuck in the weeds of intricate
detail, or drone on and on.
The best place to use this kind of story is after you have fully introduced
your idea, process, service or product. Imagine the question coming up
in your prospect's mind: "Well that sounds interesting, but how in the
world does it work?"
Your story that explains will answer that question-again, in the most
persuasive way possible.
Stories That Build Trust (Chapter 8)
When most people want something, they experience two things at once: a
sense of excitement (about getting what they want) and a sense of dread
(that something will go wrong).
This is a normal human reaction. You might even say that the seemingly
instantaneous sense of dread is hard-wired into us-emotional
protection against making a mistake that would lead to disappointment,
or, worse yet, disaster.
Persuasion stories can really help you here. This chapter covers five types
of stories that build trust (in other words, extinguish that sense of dread
in your prospect):
• Case Studies
• Certification Stories
• Expert Testimonials
