• Situational research. This is a little less directly related than the
first two kinds of research, but it's just as important. What's going
on in the world around you? Prospects and customers live in the
same world. If, for example, you're working in an industry that
fears new waves of government regulation coming on soon, it's
important for you to know that—and work it into your story, if it
fits.
All of this research is for the same end result. As a prospect hears or
reads your story, you would like to get one or both of the following
reactions from them (even if the reaction is only thought but not spoken):
"Yeah, that really could have happened."
or
"Yes! That's exactly what it's like!"
