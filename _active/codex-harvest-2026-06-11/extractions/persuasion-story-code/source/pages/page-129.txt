Forty years of study, writing, and coaching-starting with a New York
City playwriting class I took in 1981—led me to gradually discovering
the essential differences between Story and Story. I lay out those
differences in great detail in Chapter 2. One of my most important
discoveries was that hero's journey (dramatic) stories do not help most
persuaders most of the time, despite what many others claim —while persuasion
stories definitely help.
Here are the types of persuasion stories we've covered in detail:
Origin Stories (Chapter 3)
These stories answer your prospect's big question: Who are you, why are
you qualified or capable to deliver what you promise, and how did you
get to this point?
We covered four kinds of origin stories:
• Qualifications/ Track Record Story
• Discovery Story
• Failure/ Success Story
• I-Couldn't-Find-What-I-Was-Looking-For-So-I-Created-It-Myself
Story
Each one's tailored for the actual facts of the origin, whether of a product,
a process, or a person's expertise.
Notice that these stories are not especially long. Five paragraphs at most.
They don't cover all the details—in fact, they cover as few as possible to
still be solid and believable. This is important, because long, drawn-out
stories tend to lose your prospect's attention. But shorter stories
brimming with interest and unique, curiosity-prompting facts definitely
keep your prospect's attention.
