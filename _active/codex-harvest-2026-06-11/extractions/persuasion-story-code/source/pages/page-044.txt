Let's say you haven't experienced this pain yourself. What's the best way
to find out how others describe it?
Again, the best way is to have conversations with people who do. When
that's possible, find them and ask them. And listen closely to what they
say.
Now, that's not always possible. So, next best is reading what people
write — in online forums, and in customer comments on sites like
Amazon. People are remarkably willing to share, in granular detail, what
the pain is like for them.
Find the phrases they use, and be willing to use them. Copying short
phrases is OK, but don't lift more than one full sentence as written from
any single source. Combine material from several sources. See if you can
write a description that includes a sequence of events over time. Be sure
to include as much visual and physical description as you can.
Stories about your prospect's pain work well as a lead-in to the
presentation of a solution that promises to ease that pain.
STORIES ABOUT THE WORLD TODAY
The concept "top of mind" is very useful when it comes to writing this
kind of story.
"Top of mind" refers to thoughts so important that they
crowd out all (or most) of everything else someone is thinking about.
People sometimes think of these things because of topics that get
repeated over and over—in the news, on social media, and even in casual
conversations between people in a specific group or niche.
A story about the world today combines comments about a "top of
mind" topic with a particular worry—or opportunity—likely to be on the
mind of the prospect thinking about this topic. Of course, later on in your
pitch or sales copy, you'll want to present your solution to that worry, or
a specific offer to take advantage of the opportunity.
