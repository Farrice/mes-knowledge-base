When you analyze the Joe Karbo story, one thing to pay close attention to
is this repeated pattern:
I remember [embarrassing failure|==>Now I have [this success]
He does that four times in the excerpt you just read. This kind of
compare-and-contrast—past failure bumped up against current success
—is extremely effective, both at logical and emotional levels. In that way,
it is highly efficient, too.
It's effective logically because you can objectively compare, for example,
not being able to get a $200 loan from the bank with "loaning" the bank
$100,000, multiple times, to collect interest on certificates of deposit.
But it's also effective emotionally because anyone who has had money
problems can empathize. Possibly even feel the sense of embarrassment
and desperation Karbo himself felt when the bank said no to a $200 loan!
And it's hard not to imagine the pride of victory Karbo must have felt
when he turned the tables on the bank and started collecting interest on
money he loaned them!
