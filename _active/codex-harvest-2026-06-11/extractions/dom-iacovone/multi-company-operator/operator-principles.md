# Operator Principles

## 1. Four Initiatives Beat Infinite Priorities

The SGM move is not "make a plan." It is choosing a small set of annual initiatives, then forcing every weekly review to ladder back to them.

Use when: a founder has too many launches, too many channels, or too many personal priorities.

Output: four priority blocks, each with owner, metric, meeting cadence, leading signal, and stop/continue trigger.

## 2. Finance Sees The Truth First

The source repeatedly treats CM1, revenue mix, trade spend, and logistics as early truth surfaces. Marketing stories are subordinate to the financial signal.

Use when: the founder is explaining why a campaign "should" work while the numbers are saying something else.

Output: finance-led reality check with target, actual, variance, cause hypothesis, and operating decision.

## 3. Stage-Gate Every New Product

New products must clear strategic sense, real differentiation, margin creation, and channel fit. Novelty is not enough.

Use when: a team wants to launch because the idea is exciting, competitor-driven, or buyer-requested.

Output: go, revise, park, or kill decision with evidence.

## 4. The Founder Must Stop Suppressing The Room

Dom's leadership lesson is that founder involvement can create yes-men and weaken executive argument. The mature operator builds a room where strong people can disagree before the founder enters.

Use when: all decisions route back to the founder or meetings become performance theater.

Output: meeting attendance map, owner map, escalation rules, and founder intervention triggers.

## 5. Retail Is A Lagging-System Business

Retail and product decisions can be made this year and show up next year. A weak current period may conceal already-loaded distribution, while a strong current period may conceal future plateau risk.

Use when: forecasting retail, evaluating acquisition timing, or explaining why current revenue misses the full picture.

Output: lag map with active work, loaded distribution, expected timing, and evidence quality.

## 6. Channel Buyers Are Inputs, Not Product Owners

The Walmart lesson is the central anti-pattern: a channel buyer can pull a brand away from its own product truth.

Use when: a buyer, partner, platform, or client asks for changes that weaken the core product.

Output: buyer request audit with brand fit, margin fit, shelf logic, customer logic, and alternative proposal.

## 7. Trade Spend Is Enterprise Value

Gross-to-net leakage compounds into valuation. A point of trade spend or logistics waste is not merely monthly expense; it can become exit-value destruction.

Use when: a business scales retail, wholesale, or shipping-heavy operations.

Output: leakage waterfall with current cost, operating owner, fix path, and valuation impact estimate.

## 8. AI Makes Relationship Moats More Important

The source's AI point is not tool worship. As task execution becomes cheaper, trusted access, offline relationships, and partner credibility become more scarce.

Use when: designing AI-native business strategy, partnerships, sales, or sourcing.

Output: relationship moat map with who unlocks distribution, hiring, credibility, capital, or category entry.
