## Executive Answer

### Verified Evidence
Google's sole official statement on local ranking factors for Google Business Profile (GBP, formerly Google My Business) appears in its support documentation, which states: "Local results are mainly based on **relevance**, **distance**, and **popularity**. Together, these factors help Google find the best match for customers' searches."[7]  
No other official Google sources in the results provide additional details or lists of specific sub-factors.

### Directional Signals
- **Relevance**: Measures how well a business matches the search query, influenced by GBP elements like categories, keywords in business title/name, and alignment with search terms.[1][2][4][7][8]
- **Distance** (or Proximity): Prioritizes businesses closer to the searcher's location or query point, including address proximity to search center and map pin accuracy.[1][2][4][6][7]
- **Popularity** (or Prominence): Reflects prominence via reviews (quantity, recency, star ratings, text content), backlinks, citations, behavioral signals, and authority indicators like check-ins or directory listings.[1][3][4][6][7][8]

These align directly with Google's three pillars and are echoed across third-party analyses as primary GBP influencers (e.g., GBP signals weighted at 32% for Local Pack in surveys).[1][8]

### Inference
Third-party sources (e.g., industry surveys and SEO analyses) infer dozens of specific GBP fields as sub-factors under Google's pillars, such as primary category (top-ranked in 2026 surveys), additional categories, hours of operation, photos, posts, products/services, and NAP consistency.[1][2][3][5][6][8][9]  
GBP optimization is described as Google's "primary data source" for Local Pack/Maps, with signals comprising ~7-32% of rankings depending on result type (Pack vs. organic).[1][3][5]  
Negative signals like incorrect categories, low ratings, or "closed" status explicitly harm rankings.[1][2]

| GBP Field | Inferred Pillar | Source Support |

### Unverified Assumptions
- Specific weights (e.g., GBP at 32% for Local Pack, reviews at 20%) derive from unofficial 2026 surveys by BrightLocal/Whitespark, not Google-confirmed.[1][8]
- Claims like "consistent NAP boosts Local Pack appearance by 40%" or exact field rankings (e.g., primary category as #1) lack official validation and stem from industry studies.[1]
- No pricing, market, competitor, or social listening data in results; no source URLs beyond listed snippets qualify as such.

**Note**: Google does not publish exhaustive GBP field lists or algorithms, making third-party details interpretive rather than official.[4][7] For latest guidance, monitor Google's support pages directly.
