# Josh Shirt Launch - 02 Guided Creative Brief

## Role Of This Brief

This brief gives Josh a creative runway, not a cage.

Josh should stay the creative person. The goal is to give him stronger constraints, taste direction, and prompts so he can make shirts that feel loved by real dancers and nerds instead of generic joke merch.

## North Star

Make shirts from a real scene, not shirts about a scene.

The best version feels like:

- After-hours dance club.
- Indie software project.
- Community hall at 1:14 a.m.
- Warm, smart, a little tired, very inside-joke.
- A club tee people would wear even if they only partly get the joke.

The wrong version feels like:

- Startup swag.
- Etsy pun shirt.
- Swing-era costume merch.
- Fake hacker visuals.
- Clip-art dance apparel.

## Creative Principle

Someone should want the shirt before they fully understand the joke.

The joke is the reward. The design has to carry the purchase desire.

## Design System Direction

Build a micro-club system, not isolated slogans.

Useful recurring elements:

- Small left-chest club marks.
- Larger back-print moments.
- Tiny hem or sleeve marks, such as `SDDC // BUILD 01`.
- Dance count ticks.
- Floor-map arrows.
- Tempo stamps.
- Version numbers.
- Tiny debug notes.
- Abstract connection lines.
- Small club-label language: `FLOOR TESTED`, `COUNT VERIFIED`, `DEPLOYMENT FLOOR: LIVE`.

## Typography Direction

- Use one serious display type style and one restrained utility type style.
- Display type can feel condensed, athletic, archival, or dance-poster-like.
- Utility type can feel like small metadata, labels, or technical notes.
- Avoid making everything look like code.
- Avoid obvious default tech fonts.

## Garment Direction

- Heavyweight, boxy, garment-washed feel.
- Strong collar.
- Faded black, bone, moss, navy, graphite, washed charcoal, cream.
- One accent ink max: muted orange, washed red, pale yellow, or faded blue.
- Soft-hand screen print feel.

## Placement Ideas

For a premium first drop, do not make every shirt a giant front slogan.

Try:

- Small front chest mark, main back print.
- Tiny sleeve or hem detail.
- Front pocket-style label plus a stronger back system.
- Back print that looks like a club/event tee, not a novelty phrase.

## Concept Direction: Commit On Eight

Best role: flagship shirt.

Front idea:

- Tiny left chest mark: `COMMIT ON 8` inside a small dance-count grid.

Back idea:

```text
COMMIT
ON
8

SOCIAL DANCE DEBUG CLUB // BUILDING BETTER TIMING
[1 2 3&4 5 6 7&8]
```

Why this works: it can look like a dance club shirt first and a nerd joke second.

Avoid:

- Big centered code-font joke.
- Terminal-window graphics.
- Anything that looks like bootcamp swag.

## Concept Direction: No Drops In Prod

Best role: funniest community signal.

Front idea:

- Small chest text: `NO DROPS`.

Back idea:

```text
NO DROPS IN PROD
DEPLOYMENT FLOOR: LIVE
STATUS: PARTNER SAFE
```

Why this works: it turns dance-floor safety into deadpan club policy.

Avoid:

- Warning triangles.
- Falling-person icons.
- Construction sign colors.
- Overly aggressive safety signage.

## Concept Direction: Push / Pull Request

Best role: most nerd-pure concept.

Front idea:

- Small chest text: `PUSH / PULL`.

Back idea:

- Elegant partner-connection diagram.
- Two abstract arrows or frame lines.
- Count markers.
- Microcopy: `CONNECTION PROTOCOL // SOCIAL DANCE DEBUG CLUB`.

Why this works: it becomes a movement diagram instead of a dad-joke pun.

Avoid:

- GitHub-style icons.
- Code review UI.
- Too many arrows.
- Anything that feels like tech conference swag.

## Lifestyle Shot Direction

If Josh later shoots or creates imagery, the world should feel specific.

Environments:

- Community hall.
- College ballroom.
- Church basement.
- Converted arts space.
- Dance studio after class.
- DJ booth / water table.
- Folding chairs along the wall.
- Warm wood floor.

Human behavior:

- Switching shoes.
- Laughing after a missed move.
- Reviewing a practice clip on a phone.
- Sitting on the floor between dances.
- Asking someone to dance.
- Cooling off near the fan.
- Checking the next workshop schedule.

Details that make it real:

- Wristbands.
- Name tags.
- Water bottles.
- Dance shoes under chairs.
- Shoe bags.
- Handwritten setlists.
- Paper schedules.
- Cables and adapters at the DJ table.
- Notebooks with move notes.
- Slightly sweaty collars and lived-in fabric.

Avoid:

- Poodle skirts.
- Jukeboxes.
- Musical notes.
- Costume fedoras.
- Theatrical dips.
- Aerials on the social floor.
- Green code rain.
- Hooded hacker scenes.
- Cyberpunk lighting.
- Famous logos or recognizable franchise references.

## Creative Prompts For Josh

1. What would this shirt look like if it came from a tiny dance exchange that only 200 people know about?
2. What would make a dancer say, "I have been in that exact moment"?
3. What tiny detail would make the shirt feel authored, not generated?
4. Could the design still look good if the joke were blurred?
5. What is the club mark for Social Dance Debug Club?
6. What would be printed on the inside neck label?
7. What is the first back print that makes this feel like a real capsule?
