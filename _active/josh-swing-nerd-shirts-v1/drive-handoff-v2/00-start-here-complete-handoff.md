# Josh Shirt Launch - 00 START HERE

## What This Is

This is the complete first-drop handoff for Josh's swing dance x nerd culture shirt idea.

The goal is simple: help Josh decide whether this has real demand before building a full brand, store, or reusable agent team.

Working project name: **Social Dance Debug Club**

One-line idea: **dance merch that does not look like dance merch.**

Target reaction: **"That is painfully specific. I need that."**

## The Opportunity

There is a small but potent overlap between swing/social dance culture and nerd culture: software people, engineers, tabletop/gaming people, open-source types, data people, creative tech people, and couples or friend groups who already share hyper-specific inside jokes.

The shirt should not scream "funny swing dance shirt." It should look like a clean, wearable tee first, then become an inside joke when the right person reads it.

## The Launch Shape

Recommended V1 format: **Bonfire limited batch campaign**

Why Bonfire:

- No inventory.
- Buyer checkout is handled by Bonfire.
- Printing and shipping are handled by Bonfire.
- Batch campaigns create urgency and make the test cleaner.
- Josh can test demand before committing to a full shop.

Recommended test:

- 1 campaign.
- 1 to 3 shirt designs max.
- 10-day batch window.
- Unisex tee only for V1 unless Bonfire's product preview makes another product obvious.
- Let actual purchases, comments, DMs, and "I would wear that" reactions decide the next drop.

## The Three Best Starter Concepts

### 1. Commit On Eight

Text: **COMMIT ON 8**

Why it works: It bridges software "commit" language with 8-count dance timing. It is the cleanest, safest, most wearable first lead.

Best use: flagship shirt.

Design feel: premium club tee, small front mark, stronger back print, count-grid or rhythm-line detail.

### 2. No Drops In Prod

Text: **NO DROPS IN PROD**

Why it works: It is a social-floor safety joke and a software production joke. It has the strongest laugh if the audience gets "prod."

Best use: funniest community signal.

Design feel: deadpan club policy, not hazard-sign merch.

### 3. Push / Pull Request

Text: **PUSH / PULL REQUEST**

Why it works: It connects partner-dance mechanics with code review language. It is the nerdiest and most niche of the three.

Best use: connection diagram tee.

Design feel: abstract movement/protocol diagram, not software UI.

## Josh's Best First Move

Josh should start by sketching **Commit On Eight** as if it came from a tiny late-night dance exchange that only 200 people know about.

The question is not "is the phrase clever?"

The question is: **would Josh wear this outside a dance event and still feel proud when another dancer gets the joke?**

## Quality Bar

This is ready for Josh if he can:

- Understand the opportunity in under five minutes.
- Laugh at at least one concept.
- See where his own drawing/design instincts can improve the ideas.
- Pick one lead shirt to sketch.
- Know what has to happen before Bonfire publishing.

This is not ready if:

- It feels like generic programmer humor.
- It feels like costume swing merch.
- The phrase is doing all the work.
- The design needs a paragraph to explain.
- It borrows protected characters, logos, lyrics, or famous meme layouts.

## Final Recommendation

Lead with **Commit On Eight** if Josh wants the safest, most wearable first test.

Lead with **No Drops In Prod** if Josh wants the funniest community-specific signal.

Lead with **Push / Pull Request** if Josh wants to fully embrace the nerd overlap from day one.

My recommendation: **start with Commit On Eight as the flagship design, then keep No Drops In Prod as the sharper second option.**
