## Name Thesis
**Eightward:** A coined brand name with low trademark surface, clean domain registration, and cultural residue that honors Lindy Hop lineage without borrowing direct references. Positioned as a standalone company name for swing-inflected, literary, sci-fi adjacent apparel brand.

---

## Why Eightward Works

### Creative Fit
- **Surprising familiarity:** Original compound, immediately pronounceable, unexpected in category
- **Encoded lineage:** "Eight" references the 8-count fundamental to swing dancing, compass-like directionality  
- **Scalable world:** Supports three distinct brand worlds (The House Beyond The Count, Dance Past The Map, Social Floor Mythology)
- **Designer clarity:** Josh remains visible as founder/creative; brand does not require him as personality anchor

### Legal Defensibility
- **Clean trademark surface:** Eightward does not appear in USPTO Federal Trademark Register for apparel/Class 25
- **Coined status:** Brand is defensible as original lexicon, not borrowed from existing cultural property
- **Domain secured:** eightward.com registered and active through May 24, 2027 (GoDaddy, currently locked)

### Market Differentiation  
- **Not crowded:** Unlike "Airstep" (14+ USPTO records), "Eighth House" (jewelry marks, astrology phrase), or "Breakaway House" (sports/apparel saturation), Eightward has minimal competitive surface
- **Wearable:** Garage test score 23.5/25; ranks highest on "Wear It" and "Say Proudly" dimensions

---

## What Eightward Should Not Become

**DO NOT:**
- Use direct Lindy Hop song titles, artist names, or event marks (e.g., Savoy Ballroom logo, specific dancer names, "No One Enters On One" as a brand mark rather than a shirt concept)
- Borrow aesthetics from 1940s nostalgia tourism, jukebox art, green-code hacker visuals, or fantasy-costume parody
- Treat Lindy Hop history as a costume drawer instead of Black American cultural history
- Use the brand as a vehicle for Josh's personal identity; keep it centered on the work and the wearers

**DO:**
- Encode Lindy lineage through architecture (8-count, air step, social floor, late-night exchange, ballroom language, compass marks)
- Preserve respectful distance from direct appropriation while honoring the cultural source
- Let the shirt concepts (not the brand name) carry the inside-joke density and Harlem/Savoy flavor

## Top Legal Risks & Lawyer Questions

### Priority 1: Class 25 Apparel Clearance
**Risk Level:** Low-Medium  
**Question for Counsel:**
1. Does Eightward face any likelihood of confusion under Class 25 (apparel, shirts, merchandise)?  
2. Is there any prior or current apparel trademark on "Eightward" or similar compound that would block federal registration?
3. What is the likelihood of clearance for intent-to-use filing before public launch?

### Priority 2: Prior Music/Remix Use
**Risk Level:** Low  
**Background:** "Eightward" appears in some music/remix contexts (EDM, electronic producers). This is non-apparel use and unlikely to create apparel confusion, but counsel should verify.  
**Question for Counsel:**
4. Does prior non-apparel "Eightward" usage in music/remix create any conflict for apparel filing or registration?

### Priority 3: Cultural & Attribution Risk
**Risk Level:** Mitigated by design, but verify  
**Background:** Brand encodes Lindy Hop lineage via directional/architectural language (8-count, air step, social floor) rather than direct borrowing. Shirt concepts may reference songs/places respectfully.  
**Question for Counsel:**
5. What specific content from Lindy Hop history, song titles, event names, or venue marks should Josh avoid in shirt design and copy?  
6. Are there any trademark or publicity-rights risks from using historical references to Savoy, Harlem/Lenox, or named dancers/musicians?

### Priority 4: Intent-to-Use Timing
**Question for Counsel:**
7. Should Josh file an intent-to-use (ITU) application before public launch, or launch and file post-use? What are the advantages/risks of each?
