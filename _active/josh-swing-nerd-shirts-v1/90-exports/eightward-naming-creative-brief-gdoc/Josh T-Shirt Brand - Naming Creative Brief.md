## Start Here

This brief captures the naming and brand identity direction from the garage sprint.

The idea has moved beyond one-off swing-nerd shirt jokes. The stronger opportunity is a standalone apparel world with hidden swing DNA: Lindy Hop, 8-count movement, late-night social floors, old ballroom lineage, and speculative-fiction atmosphere.

The current recommendation is:

# EIGHTWARD

One-line read:

> Eightward is an apparel world for people who feel like motion opens a hidden door.

Plain-language version:

> A swing-rooted, sci-fi/fantasy-inflected t-shirt brand that can stand on its own without needing to explain the joke.

## Naming Owner And Standard

This naming pass was guided by the David Placek / Lexicon-style naming standard:

- A great name should be surprisingly familiar: easy to say, but not obvious.
- It should not feel like a pun, themed dance night, Etsy shop, or costume brand.
- It should carry a world Josh can draw into.
- It should survive being said out loud, printed on a neck label, shown as an Instagram handle, and used on a shirt tag.
- The best name should get better with repetition.

The key creative decision:

> Do not paste "Lindy Hop" onto a fantasy word. Encode the lineage instead.

## Strategic Shift

The original lane was:

> Swing dance x nerd/meme culture shirts.

Josh's updated instinct points to something stronger:

> A standalone brand with swing undertones and sci-fi/fantasy/literary world-building.

That means the name should not require a paragraph of context. It should feel cool first, then reveal the swing layer second.

### What To Preserve

- Swing culture as hidden lineage
- Late-night social floor energy
- Josh's humor and taste
- Apparel people would actually wear outside an event
- A world that can hold illustrations, symbols, shirt drops, and story

### What To Avoid

- Obvious dance puns
- "SwingQuest," "Dragon Groove," "Cosmic Lindy" style mashups
- Poodle skirts, jukeboxes, musical notes, or retro cosplay
- Famous fantasy/sci-fi franchise cues
- Direct song titles, lyrics, event names, or borrowed logos
- Names that only work if someone already knows the inside joke

## Legal Consult Questions

If Josh is doing a quick 15-minute legal consult, ask:

1. Can we pursue `EIGHTWARD` for apparel, shirts, and related goods in Class 25?
2. Does prior non-apparel use of Eightward create any meaningful risk?
3. Would `Shop Eightward`, `Eightward Studio`, or `Eightward Club` help or hurt defensibility?
4. Is `AIRSTEP` too close to existing footwear/apparel marks to use anywhere?
5. Is `THE EIGHTH HOUSE` too crowded as a brand, even if apparel-specific conflicts are limited?
6. Should Josh file an intent-to-use trademark application before a public test?
7. What exact goods/services language should be used if the first launch is t-shirts?
8. Are there any obvious cultural or historical references in the name that require extra care?

## Visual Taste Rules

### Do

- Make it feel wearable first.
- Let swing live in the details: count, floor, connection, late-night room, wristbands, shoes, water table.
- Use symbols Josh can own: paths, thresholds, maps, houses, orbit lines, floor geometry.
- Keep graphics simple enough to print cleanly.
- Make the brand feel like a real apparel label, not a themed event flyer.

### Do Not

- Use direct Lindy Hop event logos, song lyrics, or famous dance photos.
- Use fantasy clichés like dragons, wizards, swords, castles, or franchise-looking symbols.
- Use retro swing clichés like poodle skirts, jukeboxes, diner graphics, or music notes.
- Use green-code hacker visuals.
- Make the shirt depend on a joke that needs a paragraph.
