# Josh Preview Deck Talk Track

## Slide 01 - Cover

This is not a finished brand identity. It is a taste board for deciding what the clothes should feel like before we place Josh's designs on them.

## Slide 02 - The Reset

The biggest shift is product seriousness. The graphic tee can still exist, but the larger idea is apparel that feels native to the dance community.

## Slide 03 - Visual Center

The scene should feel current, joyful, and social. No costume shorthand. The clothes need to look right while people are actually dancing.

## Slide 04 - Tailored Social Floor

If Josh wants the brand to feel like a grown-up expression of Lindy Hop culture, this is the lead lane.

## Slide 05 - Practice Utility

This lane answers the dancer's real life. It is practical, repeatable, and easy to buy into first.

## Slide 06 - Music-Rooted Craft

This lane gives Josh a deeper product story: fabric, touch, repair, and music culture without turning the clothing into nostalgia.

## Slide 07 - Silhouette System

This slide is the argument for moving beyond graphic shirts. The tee gets stronger when it lives with the right trouser, jacket, towel, and shoe bag.

## Slide 08 - Graphic Placement

The designs should feel placed with care. A big back print, a quiet chest hit, a towel detail, or a bag mark all say different things.

## Slide 09 - First Capsule

This gives Josh a first offer that can sell as culture and utility, while still leaving room for the funny or insider graphics as a satellite.

## Slide 10 - Decision Questions

We do not need Josh to approve everything. We need him to pick the lead lane, the first pieces, and the first graphic placement strategy.

## The Three Decisions

1. Which lane leads: Tailored Social Floor, Practice Utility, or Music-Rooted Craft?
2. Which three pieces start: tee, camp shirt, chore jacket, wide trouser, practice pant, towel, or bag?
3. Where do the designs land first: chest, back, sleeve, towel, bag, or neck label?
