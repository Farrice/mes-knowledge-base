# Meg Heckman Buyer-Trigger Sprint: 12 New Josh Swing-Nerd Shirt Concepts

## Purpose

This sprint uses the Meg Heckman Buyer-Trigger OS to create twelve new swing-nerd shirt concepts for Josh's first-drop world. The goal is not just "funny shirt ideas." The goal is concepts that make the right buyer feel recognized fast enough to justify an impulse purchase.

Working rule: wearable first, funny second. Each concept should look plausible on a shirt from across the room, then reward the exact overlap buyer when they read it.

## Trigger Model

Each concept is judged against six buyer triggers:

- **Identity Signal:** Does the shirt let the buyer say "this is me" without explaining?
- **Recognition Speed:** Can the right person get it in one to two seconds?
- **Specificity:** Does it name a real insider behavior, tension, or ritual?
- **Social Currency:** Does it start the kind of comment the buyer wants to receive?
- **Familiar Twist:** Does it combine a known phrase or technical idea with a dance-world twist?
- **Emotion-First Reason:** Does the buyer feel seen, clever, nostalgic, or socially validated before they rationalize the buy?

## Best Three To Prototype First

### 1. Sync Before Send

**Why it wins:** It captures one of the deepest partner-dance principles in a phrase that technical people instantly understand. It also avoids looking like novelty merch. This can become a clean chest print, back print, or minimal streetwear mark.

**Source mechanics:** identity signal, recognition speed, social currency, familiar/twist.

**Domain extrapolation:** "sync before send" as a partner-dance rule is inferred from dance connection norms, not stated by Meg.

**Suggested listing angle:** For dancers who know the best moves start before the move.

**Visual direction:** Minimal technical diagram: two small nodes or foot-position marks align, then a subtle send arrow appears. Avoid literal dancer silhouettes.

### 2. Debug The Frame

**Why it wins:** It is highly specific, highly teachable, and emotionally familiar. Every serious dancer has had a frame correction. Every dev has debugged something that looked simple from the outside.

**Source mechanics:** specificity, familiar/twist, emotion-first purchase logic.

**Domain extrapolation:** frame correction as a common dancer pain is Josh/domain context, not a Meg claim.

**Suggested listing angle:** For anyone who has fixed the same "basic" problem twelve different ways.

**Visual direction:** Diagnostic grid, small anchor points, quiet annotation marks, strong typography. Make it clever, not remedial.

### 3. Frame Rate: 120 BPM

**Why it wins:** It is the most streetwear-adaptable. It can carry Josh's swing-nerd world while also opening toward MyBPM-style music/frequency aesthetics.

**Source mechanics:** fast recognition, familiar/twist, social currency.

**Domain extrapolation:** BPM/frame-rate crossover, MyBPM adjacency, and streetwear visual potential are domain applications, not source claims from Meg.

**Suggested listing angle:** For dancers whose timing has a refresh rate.

**Visual direction:** Kinetic type, small frame ticks, BPM label, one clean motion path. This one can look less "joke shirt" and more design object.

## One Revised Listing Angle

**Original direction:** Nerdy swing dance shirts for dancers who also code.

**Meg-trigger upgrade:** Shirts for dancers who want the right person on the floor to recognize the joke before they have to explain it.

**Why this is stronger:** It moves the purchase from "I like a pun" to "this gives me an identity signal and a social moment."
