## How To Use This Document

Three sections, one per ICP tier. Each section has six fields:

- **A. Scenes They Recognize Themselves In** — concrete, grounded, hookable
- **B. Phrases In Their Head But Never Said Out Loud** — the articulation gap
- **C. Cultural Moments Alive Right Now** — what jackpost surface this brand can plug into in May 2026
- **D. The Wince List** — what shuts them down
- **E. The Save Signal** — what gets screenshotted
- **F. The Recognition Sentence** — the one line that, if seen on IG, makes them stop scrolling and tap the handle

Hooks pull from A and B. Cultural moment alignment pulls from C. Voice guardrails pull from D. Content moves that produce saves pull from E. The crystallized articulation that opens the funnel pulls from F.

# Section 1 — Marcus

*Avatar A of Profile #3. 31, senior product designer at a health-tech firm in the Loop, Black, raised in Calumet Heights, lives near 35th and State. BJJ three times a week in Bridgeport. Records, Ocean Vuong, Baldwin. Therapy every other Tuesday. Reached via Channels 1 + 3 (founders' personal networks + Andrea's music network) and Channel 2 (friend-pair). Never via public copy — Marcus does not RSVP from a feed post.*

## A. Scenes Marcus Recognizes Himself In

1. **Saturday, 2:14pm at the record store on Milwaukee.** He's flipping through the soul section. A woman across the aisle pulls a Roy Ayers reissue and holds it like she knows what it is. Eye contact, half-smile. He thinks *oh.* Then he thinks *if I say something do I sound like I'm hitting on her or do I sound like a person who likes records.* The math runs in 2.4 seconds. He looks back down. She walks away. Eight minutes later he's outside kicking himself.

2. **Tuesday, 7:12pm at the BJJ gym in Bridgeport.** He's tying his belt before the warm-up. Two new white belts in the corner are trying too hard to talk to him about a podcast he doesn't listen to. He's friendly. He's also tired in a way that doesn't show in his face — the specific tiredness of being the only Black guy in a room for the seventh straight session this month, having to do the work of being legible *and* approachable *and* not-threatening *and* present, all at once. He rolls hard for two hours. Drives home with the music low.

3. **Sunday morning, 10:30am at his grandmother's kitchen.** She's still in her Sunday clothes from earlier service. She pours coffee and asks if he's met anyone. He says *not yet.* She doesn't push. She just nods and says *your grandfather and I met at a dance.* He's heard the story forty times. He still listens.

4. **Wednesday, 9:48pm at his kitchen counter.** He's reading Ocean Vuong. He's been on the same page for fifteen minutes because his mind keeps drifting to the woman at the record store. He puts the book down. He doesn't open Hinge. He hasn't had Hinge on the phone since he deleted it in March 2025.

5. **Thursday, 6:15pm walking out of therapy.** His therapist asked him what it would take for him to ask someone out. He said *a room where I don't have to do the calculation first.* He's still thinking about the sentence on the train ride home.

6. **Friday at a dinner party in Hyde Park.** Six people, friend of a friend. The host's roommate is single, late thirties, white, kind. She's interested. He can feel it. He's polite. He's not going to do it. He's tired of being someone's adventure.

7. **Saturday at 11pm, alone.** He's home from a friend's birthday at a bar in West Loop. He had two drinks, talked to no one new, came home before midnight. He's not sad. He's not lonely. He's *aware* — of the difference between the room he was just in and the room he wants to be in. He doesn't have a word for the second room yet.

2. *"I don't want to be an adventure."*
3. *"I'm tired of being the only Black guy at the thing."*

## C. Cultural Moments Alive For Marcus Right Now (May 2026)

1. **The "men's vulnerability" discourse cycle** — Darrell Jackson's *Practicing Vulnerability* essay (CounterPunch, Feb 2026) and the run of pieces it triggered. Marcus has read at least one of them. He is not interested in the discourse *about* him. He is interested in a room that already assumed the conclusion. Plug-in: Resonance signals it has done the safety work without making a content series about it.

2. **The third-place revival in Chicago** — run clubs, book clubs, "no swipes, just art" Fridays at the Cultural Center. Marcus knows three women in his life who have texted him *"come to the run club"* in the last six weeks. He hasn't gone. The plug-in: Resonance is the third place his sister-figures keep texting him about, but the one he actually shows up to because the friend who sends it knows him.

3. **The Ocean Vuong / James Baldwin / Mahogany L. Browne moment in Black literary culture** — Marcus is reading Vuong's new collection right now. The dominant register in that work is *soft Black masculinity that does not apologize for being soft.* Plug-in: Resonance's room reads the same way. Use the soft-without-apology aesthetic in any visual proof that reaches him via a trusted nominator.

4. **The TikTok "I deleted the apps and started showing up places" wave** — #OffTheApps, #MeetCutesOnly trending Jan-May 2026. Marcus has not made these videos. His sister has shown him one. Plug-in: he doesn't respond to the trend, he responds to the *one specific friend who participated in the trend and is now texting him about a real room.*

5. **The "I'm not doing date prep anymore" sentiment** in Black men's group chats — a quiet, undertheorized cultural moment, but it's real: men in their early 30s closing a chapter of curated-Hinge-photo-and-coffee-shop-first-date logistics and not knowing what replaces it. Plug-in: Resonance is the replacement, and it does not require date prep. The format does the work.

## D. Marcus's Wince List

1. **Any framing that reads as "we welcome diverse men!"** — explicit demographic-welcome flags signal that his demographic is the room's project, not its baseline. He closes the tab.

2. **"Conscious king" / "sacred masculine" / "embodied brotherhood"** — manosphere-adjacent wellness language. Instant out, never recoverable.

3. **"Bachelor energy" / "men's night" / "guys' lounge"** — any gendered-spotlight framing that asks him to show up as a representative of his gender.

4. **"Sober-curious community"** — codes recovery. Marcus is not in recovery and does not want to be cast in a recovery-adjacent room.

5. **"Find your queen" / "real men..." / any sentence that opens with the male-prescriptive voice** — out.

6. **Photos of men dancing performatively, arms raised, eyes closed, "vulnerable" poses** — reads as wellness theatre. Marcus is looking for proof the room is normal, not proof the room is therapeutic.

7. **Any caption that announces its own depth** — *"In a world where authentic connection is rare..."* He has read the sentence ten thousand times. He doesn't read the eleventh.

## E. Marcus's Save Signal

1. **A photo of a quiet daytime room with two men of color visibly relaxed in the frame, no caption, no announcement** — proves the room exists, lets him fill in the rest. Saved to a folder he never names.

2. **A short video, fifteen seconds, of Andrea cuing a record at golden hour — hand on the platter, no face, no voiceover** — proves the curator knows what she's doing. He sends it to his sister with no message.

3. **A line in serif font on cream that says something his grandmother would say** — *"Your grandfather and I met at a dance"* or its functional equivalent. Saved. Sent to one friend.

4. **A specific, small fact — "doors at 2pm" or "the room is 50 people" or "the application takes four minutes"** — operational specificity that proves competence. Screenshotted. Filed.

5. **A piece of writing — not a caption, an actual essay or letter — about why this room was built, in a voice that does not perform** — saved. Read three times.

6. **One sentence from a previous attendee, in their words, screenshotted from a Story** — *"I forgot people could meet each other this way"* or its equivalent. The sentence does the work of the testimonial without ever using the word testimonial.

7. **A Reel of the room *after* the event — empty floor, sun on the wood, a glass of water on the speaker** — proof of arrival. The room was real. He missed this one. He wants the next one.

## F. The Recognition Sentence For Marcus

> **A daytime room in Chicago where you don't have to do the math first.**

If Marcus reads this line in a sister's text or a trusted nominator's DM, he stops. He doesn't tap a handle (he won't be reached on a feed) — he replies to the sender: *"tell me more."* That's the conversion that matters.

# Section 3 — The Umbrella ICP

*30-42, Chicago, single and employed. Has tried the apps, the bars, the speed dating, the hookups, the friend-of-a-friend setups. Has stopped going out but has not stopped wanting to meet someone. Reads books, holds complicated jobs, has an inner life the city's nightlife wastes. Wants a committed partner. The umbrella holds everyone Resonance is for — Nora the stage manager, Imani the social worker, Marcus the designer, Daniel the architect, plus the dozens of cousin-shaped people each of them represents.*

## A. Scenes The Umbrella ICP Recognizes Themselves In

1. **7:42pm on a Sunday.** They've opened the apps three times today and closed them within ninety seconds each time. The phone is face-down on the kitchen counter. They are not depressed. They are tired in a specific way that doesn't have a name yet — the tired of having tried, the tired of having explained to two friends why they're not on the apps right now, the tired of being asked at brunch *"so are you seeing anyone?"* by the same person for the third year in a row.

2. **A Tuesday night around 11pm.** They are standing in their own kitchen. The overhead light is off. One lamp is on. They are holding a glass of something. The phone is face-down. They are not looking at it. They are looking at the lamp. The thought: *I am a person who used to go to things and met people and now I do not go to things and I am not meeting people, and I cannot tell which one of those came first.*

3. **A Saturday at the third-place run club they tried last month.** Twenty-five people, average age 34, half of them coupled. They ran the four miles, said *thanks, see you next time,* and didn't go back. The run was great. The meeting-someone math was not. They are not bitter. They are *recalibrating.*

4. **At a friend's wedding in late spring.** Two college friends are getting married. The friend group is now seven couples and them. They give a great toast. They dance with the bride's mother. They go to bed at 11:30 in the hotel room because the dance floor energy was wrong. On the drive home Sunday, they listen to the same album they listened to in 2014, which is the year they last felt like the world had rooms designed for them.

5. **At brunch with their best friend, Sunday at 11am.** The friend has a baby in a high chair. They are happy for the friend. They are also *aware* that the friend is going to ask them about dating in three minutes, and they have nothing new to say. They preempt: *"I'm not on the apps right now. I'm just doing my life."* The friend nods. The conversation moves. Both of them know the topic isn't closed.

6. **At 2:30pm on a Saturday at a museum, alone.** They had the afternoon free. They went to the Art Institute. They stood in front of the Hopper for nine minutes. They did not take a photo. They did not text anyone about it. They walked home along the lake. They felt good. They also noted: *I would have liked to have someone to text about the Hopper. Not a date. Just someone.*

7. **At 11:53pm scrolling Instagram in bed.** A post crosses their feed. It's a single line on cream: *"You've left a thousand rooms with a phone full of contacts and no one to call."* They stop. They read it three times. They screenshot it. They send it to no one. They put the phone down. They cannot sleep for the next twenty-three minutes.

You've left a thousand rooms with a phone full of contacts and no one to call. The room I'm building is different — and the music does the work."* The four-beat anaphora plus the landing line plus the mechanic. Save rate: high.

## F. The Recognition Sentence For The Umbrella ICP

> **You've stopped going out. You haven't stopped wanting to meet someone. The bar at 11pm was never going to be the answer.**

This works because it: (a) names the behavioral truth (stopped going out) without judgment; (b) preserves the desire (haven't stopped wanting) the ICP is protective of; (c) names the enemy (bar at 11pm) the brand bible identifies as Enemy 3; (d) does not promise the solution — it implies that one exists; and (e) uses none of the wince-list language. It is the line that, if seen on IG between a baby photo and a meal photo, stops the scroll for three seconds. Three seconds is enough.
