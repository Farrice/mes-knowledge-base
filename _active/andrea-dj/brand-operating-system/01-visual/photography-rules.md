# Resonance — Photography Rules

*Standalone brief for photographers, editors, AI-image generators, and anyone selecting an image for a Resonance surface.*
*Source spine: A1-reconciliation §3 conflict #4 + §6 cascade #1; Brand Bible §8; Manifesto v2.*
*Last updated: 2026-05-04.*

---

## 1. The Daytime-Truth Rule

There is one rule. Every other rule in this document follows from it.

> **If a photo could have been taken at 11pm in a club, it fails.**

This is not a stylistic preference. It is the mechanic of the brand rendered as a visual law. Resonance flagship events happen in daylight (Andrea's anchor §3.1, Line #1). The photography that documents the brand has to honor that — not in metaphor, but in physics.

When an image is in question, a designer or editor runs the test out loud:

*"Could this image have been pulled from a singles-event shoot at 11pm in a Chicago club?"*

If the answer is **yes**, the image is killed. No exceptions. Daytime is not a vibe we approximate. It is the literal light source we shoot in.

A second test runs alongside it:

*"Does this look like an AI render of a wellness retreat?"*

If yes, also kill. The brand is not corporate-wellness-coded. It is a real room with real people. The visuals must read as documentary photography, not as branded asset library.

## 2. What's Allowed

### Light
- **Natural daylight from real windows** — south-facing windows on a Chicago afternoon, indirect overcast light at 3pm, sun on a wood floor, dappled light through a curtain.
- **Honest weather variations** — Chicago is sometimes flat-grey-overcast. That light is on-brand. It is real and seasonal. We don't only shoot bluebird days.
- **Late afternoon when it is real** — actual 4pm light is welcome. *Faked* late-afternoon (warm filter applied to night photography) is not.

### Bodies
- **Real ages 28–40.** No teens, no twenty-somethings styled to look like the ICP. No older couples styled as the founder photographs. Real ages of the actual room.
- **Race-mixed, body-type-mixed, gender-presentation-mixed.** The actual demographic of Chicago's 30–40 single, partner-seeking population. Not stock-photo "diverse" tokenism. Real range.
- **Real movement.** People actually dancing, listening, standing. Slight motion blur from a real moment is welcome — it reads as authentic. People mid-laugh, mid-conversation, eyes on each other and not on the camera.
- **Phones not visible.** Phones are off the floor in the room (Andrea's anchor §3.8). Phones are not visible in the photography either.

### Composition
- **Body-centered, not screen-centered.** Crop tight to bodies and gestures. The architectural wide-shot is a supporting frame, not the hero shot.
- **Decentered framing.** People in the right third, gesture in the lower-left, negative space holding the rest. Editorial composition over symmetrical brochure layout.
- **Photo-as-foreground.** Type does not dominate the photograph. Headlines sit below or in the most negatively-spaced quadrant of the frame.
- **Real moments, not posed.** Candid over composed. The shot of someone listening — head tilted, eyes mid-thought — beats any posed portrait.

### Editing
- **Minimal color grading.** Preserve actual daylight color temperature. Do not warm it artificially. Do not cool it to feel "moody."
- **No skin-smoothing.** Real skin, real texture, real range.
- **No fake-blur, no Instagram filters.** Field-of-focus blur from a real lens is welcome. Faux-bokeh, vintage-filter overlays, lens-flare smears, scan-line textures — all forbidden.
- **Grain is acceptable when it is real.** Film grain or sensor grain on a high-ISO daylight shot reads as honest. Added grain to "feel analog" is forbidden.
- **Color correction, not color invention.** White-balance the image to match the actual room. Don't pull the whole frame toward terracotta in post — the terracotta in the brand palette is sourced from real wood-floor light, and forcing it onto every image makes everything look like a filter.

## 3. What's Banned

### Lighting bans
- **Flash photography of any kind** — direct, bounced, ring, on-camera. Flash erases the daylight that is the entire point.
- **Club lighting** — wash lights, gobos, color spots, lasers, strobes. Any light source that says *nightclub*.
- **Neon** — neon signs in frame, neon-lit subjects, neon overlay graphics. The brand's enemies live under neon.
- **Stylized golden-hour filters faking late-afternoon warmth** at 11pm. The most common failure mode. *If the light source isn't actually a window or actual sun, kill it.*
- **Tungsten room lighting alone** without daylight in frame. Reads as bar-interior, even if the bar is empty.
- **Black-and-white as a creative choice.** B&W in this system would erase the warmth that anchors the palette. Documentary work stays in color.

### Subject bans
- **Posed group shots** with everyone facing camera and smiling. Singles-event-promo grammar.
- **"Look-at-camera-and-smile" energy.** Eyes on each other, not on the lens.
- **Stock dancers and stock dancing.** The "everyone has their hands up at the same exact moment" frame is a stock-photography tell. Real dancing has variance — half the floor moving, half the floor watching, someone laughing on the side.
- **AI-generated portraits, anywhere, ever.** Front of house, back of house, tertiary-asset, AI faces are forbidden across the whole system. AI-generated landscapes or textures are also banned for primary surfaces — see §6 below for the narrow exception.
- **Couples or models recruited to act as singles.** Casting fakes the room. The room is the room.
- **Glassware, bottles, branded alcohol.** Sober is Line #2. Alcohol does not appear in any frame, ever — not in foreground, not in background, not as a styled prop.

### Vibe bans (the failure modes — kill on sight)
- **"Wellness retreat" energy.** Minimalist linen, all-white interiors, ambient candle, woman doing yoga at a window. Resonance is not corporate-wellness-coded.
- **"Sober rave" / "Daybreaker post-acquisition" energy.** Branded yoga mat, smoothie bar, "morning rave" hype graphics. Resonance is more disciplined than this and reads more grown.
- **"Singles night flyer" energy.** Photoshopped silhouettes, color-blocked text overlays, pink-and-purple gradient backgrounds, wineglass illustrations. Every element that smuggles the dating-event-flyer grammar is forbidden.
- **"Influencer brunch" energy.** Group of friends at a long table laughing at nothing. Reads as content, not as room.

## 4. Composition Principles (cheat sheet for the shooter)

1. **Lead with the gesture, not the face.** The way someone is standing, the angle of a hand, the direction of a glance — these carry more story than a smile.
2. **Crop into the body.** Tight crops on dancing torsos, hands, the side of a face listening — these read as intimate documentary. The wide-shot is supporting.
3. **Decenter the subject.** Subject in the right third or lower-left. Negative space carries weight.
4. **Photo as foreground.** Type later. Don't compose the frame "with copy in mind." Compose the frame as photography first.
5. **Capture transition, not climax.** The moment a song shifts and someone notices. The moment two people make eye contact across the floor. The pause before a turn. The transition is what the brand is selling — capture it.
6. **Andrea is not always in the frame.** When she is, she is doing her work — at the decks, in conversation, in the room. Andrea posing for camera is not the founder shot. Andrea mid-DJ-set, head-down, hand on the fader, is.

## 5. Specific Brief — Event #1 Photographer

### Pre-event walk-through (1 week prior)
Photographer scouts the venue at the same time of day as the event. Notes the light: which windows are direct sun, which are indirect, where the natural shadow falls, what the floor looks like in actual room light. Brings a camera and shoots reference frames of the empty space at the actual hour. These reference frames are the **light bible** for the event.

### During the event (the 3-hour arc)
The photographer is documentary, not stage-managed.
- **Phase 1 (arrival, ~25 min):** Faces and bodies entering the room. Coats coming off. The first scan of the room. Candid only.
- **Phase 2 (warm-up, ~30 min):** Bodies finding the floor. Slow start. Conversation in the corners. Andrea at the decks adjusting.
- **Phase 3 (witness circle / opening, ~15 min):** This is the only frame that may include people facing in one direction (toward Andrea / toward the center). Candid; do not direct.
- **Phase 4 (3 acts, ~90 min):** The dance. Tight crops on movement, gesture, listening. Mid-blur welcome. Capture transitions.
- **Phase 5 (slow song / arc-down, ~15 min):** Couples standing close. Faces in soft daylight. The room paying attention to itself.
- **Phase 6 (integration / closing, ~15 min):** Conversations on the way out. Numbers exchanged (if any). Andrea closing the floor.

### Hard rules during shoot
1. No flash. The photographer brought lenses fast enough to handle daylight at the actual hour. Confirmed at scout.
2. No directing. No "everyone look here." No "do that again, I missed it." We document what happened, not what we wished happened.
3. No phone shots from the photographer. The phones-off-the-floor rule applies to the documentation team too. Camera, not iPhone.
4. Andrea pre-clears any frame featuring an identifiable face that will be used publicly. Consent is the floor.
5. Frames featuring alcohol, branded merch, or anything the brand bans (§3) are deleted at the card, not at the edit. We do not "fix" a frame that broke the rules in capture.

### Deliverable
- 30–40 hero candidates, edited only for white balance and exposure.
- 8–12 hero photographs selected by Andrea + Farrice for use across the BOS (web hero, IG, press one-sheeter, post-event recap).
- 3 founder-portrait candidates of Andrea (DJ-set context, not posed).
- Source files retained; selects published with photographer credit in caption (label-caps in slate-500).

## 6. AI Image Generation — Daytime-Locked Prompting

The system **prefers real photography**. AI-generated imagery is allowed for non-front-of-house tertiary use only (texture references, mood-board internal use, IG story background blocks where no real photo exists). Every AI image generation prompt must obey:

### Prompt rules
1. **Always include daytime markers in the prompt.** *"2pm afternoon light,"* *"south-facing window light,"* *"daylight on a wood floor,"* *"overcast Chicago afternoon."*
2. **Never use night-coded language.** Banned tokens: *"glow,"* *"neon,"* *"night,"* *"club,"* *"bar,"* *"cinematic dark,"* *"moody,"* *"dramatic lighting,"* *"low-key."*
3. **Always specify real-room context.** *"Wood floor,"* *"warehouse window,"* *"converted-loft,"* *"real architecture."*
4. **Always specify real-body diversity.** *"Mixed-race group of adults aged 30 to 40, varied body types, varied gender presentation."*
5. **No faces on AI subjects** when the image will be public. Tight crops on hands, gestures, the side of a body. Identifiable faces in AI imagery are forbidden — full stop.
6. **No AI-generated Andrea.** Andrea is photographed by humans. Always.

### Sample compliant prompt
> *Photograph of a mixed-race group of adults aged 30 to 40 dancing in a converted Chicago loft at 2pm. South-facing window light, daylight on a hardwood floor, no club lighting, no flash. Tight crop on hands and torsos in mid-gesture. Documentary style, slight motion blur, minimal color grading, warm cream walls and natural wood. No phones in frame. No alcohol. The image reads as obviously daytime.*

### Sample non-compliant prompt
> *~~Cinematic dance party in a Chicago club at golden hour, moody lighting, glowing dance floor, intimate atmosphere, group of friends celebrating.~~* (Banned tokens: cinematic, golden-hour, moody, glow, intimate atmosphere. The brief produces 11pm-coded imagery.)

If the AI cannot produce a daytime-compliant image after 3 prompt iterations, the request goes to a human photographer or is shelved. We don't ship night-coded imagery and label it daytime.

## 6.5. Body Language Register — Connection vs Disconnection (added 2026-05-26)

*Why this section exists: 2026-05-25 generated 12 hero shots that passed the daytime test but failed the resonance test. The bodies were daytime, the room was correct, the palette honored — and Farrice rated them 0/10. Critique: "looks like a group fitness class for elderly people... every image with people showed a representation of disconnect versus a resonance with each other." Root cause: prompts pulled from the museum-coded `aesthetic-references.md` v1 with no party-energy or two-body-recognition reference culture. Daytime mechanic without connection mechanic = sober gathering, not Resonance.*

**Bind this section with `aesthetic-references-v2.md` (Group 6: Boiler Room + Cercle daytime archives; Group 10: Climax, Beats, In the Mood for Love, Y Tu Mamá También cinema references).**

### Connection cues (specific body language to capture)

These ten signals are what "resonance" looks like in a frame. At least 2-3 must be present in any hero shot.

1. One body leaning in to whisper to another, both mid-laugh
2. Two bodies facing each other on the dance floor, the DJ blurred in background (not facing the DJ — facing each other)
3. Hand on shoulder, gaze on a third person (active social attention — three-body geometry)
4. Hand on the small of a back, consensual partner-dance frame
5. Eye contact between two people across a small room, mid-recognition
6. Mid-laugh between strangers — teeth visible, eyes crinkled, head tilted toward each other
7. Multiple bodies blurred in motion, intermingled (not lined up)
8. A body in the foreground laughing, another body in the middle distance also reacting — proves the laugh is real
9. One person in conversation with two others — three-body social geometry
10. A still body in the frame's edge, watching, *also looking happy to be there* — wallflower as participant, not as exile

### Disconnection cues — kill on sight

These ten signals broke the v1 hero shots. ALL of them must be absent.

1. Backs of heads in a line (yoga-class lineup — the v1 failure mode)
2. All bodies facing the DJ in a stadium-pose (anonymous fan, not connected human)
3. Solo body isolated in the center of the frame, no other bodies in soft focus
4. Hands-in-air arena pose (festival grammar)
5. "Smile at the camera" group portraits
6. Posed in-line wedding-style shots where everyone is performing for the lens
7. Bodies in identical wellness uniforms (white linen lineup = retreat, not party)
8. AI-perfect lighting on every face (real rooms have shadows; perfect even light = AI tell)
9. Hands holding phones (kills the mechanic — Resonance is a phones-down room)
10. Glass-of-wine signaling (Resonance is sober; any cocktail visual is a kill)

### AI prompt language — pro-patterns to USE

For Resonance hero shots, prompt language MUST reference the v2 culture, not museum-coded fashion photography. Use these phrasings:

- *"In the energy of Boiler Room's Danny Tenaglia Loft session (NYC, 2013) — real loft, real daylight, real bodies a foot from the DJ"*
- *"Framing à la Christopher Doyle in 'In the Mood for Love' — two-body recognition, shallow depth of field, corridor-glance composition"*
- *"Handheld documentary-realist à la Emmanuel Lubezki in 'Y Tu Mamá También' (2001) — natural light, casual wardrobe, dance-as-secondary-to-being-there"*
- *"Embrace energy of 'Beats' (2019, Brian Welsh) — two friends hugging in a room of strangers"*
- *"Cercle Adana Twins at Palais Longchamp daytime crowd framing — earth-tone linen, sun on stone, audience composure"*
- *"Bodies leaning toward each other not facing the DJ"*
- *"Mid-laugh between strangers, teeth visible, eyes crinkled"*
- *"Documentary photography, slight natural motion blur from real movement, minimal color grading"*

### AI prompt language — BANNED for Resonance hero shots

These phrases caused the v1 0/10 failure. Forbidden in any prompt:

- *"documentary photography"* used WITHOUT a specific cinematic reference (produces Magnum-reportage solemnity)
- *"editorial broadsheet"* (matches creative_router Rule 2 → routes to fal-poster → wrong tool)
- *"photographed entirely from behind"* (produces yoga-class lineup framing)
- *"Magnum reportage"* (reverential observer-distance, kills connection)
- *"no posed"* used in isolation without pro-patterns (style overrides the negative)
- *"editorial fashion magazine cover"* (locks in posed-static-fashion energy)

### The pre-shoot reference deck

Before any human photographer shoots OR any AI image is generated, assemble a 5-frame scout deck:

1. **2 v1 frames** — Tyler Mitchell portrait (for body register) + Sandro Miller Chicago environmental (for room register)
2. **2 v2 Group 6 frames** — Boiler Room Tenaglia Loft session (audience density) + Cercle Adana Twins Palais Longchamp (architectural crowd composition)
3. **1 v2 Group 10 film still** — *In the Mood for Love* corridor frame OR *Beats* embrace beat

That's the pre-shoot scout deck. The discipline is to look at these five before pressing the shutter (or pressing generate).

## 6.6. Partnership Orientation + Age Band + Anti-AI-Slop (added 2026-05-26 — v2 failure response)

*Why this section exists: v2 hero shots (Higgsfield Nano Banana 2, 2026-05-26) scored 2/10. Three new failure modes: same-sex pairings in hero shots when the brief is heterosexual M/F partnership recognition; age drift to 25-32 when the brief is 30-38; AI-slop anatomy (fused fingers, duplicate limbs, glassy eyes). Full log at `evolution_store/failure_log_2026-05-26_v2-visual-regression.md`.*

### Partnership orientation — REQUIRED in every hero shot

Resonance exists for adults seeking committed partners. The ICP (`00-foundation/02-icp-master.md`) names heterosexual M/F archetypes: Maya (Latina, mid-30s) and LCSW (Black, Caribbean) on the women side; Marcus (Black, late 30s, designer) and Daniel (white, Polish-Italian, architect) on the men side.

- **Hero shots show heterosexual M/F partnership recognition.** This is not exclusionary copy — it is the founder's specific brand thesis.
- **Group shots may include mixed friendships and same-sex friend clusters.** But the romantic recognition beat is always M/F.
- **In prompts, name M/F explicitly.** "A Latina woman in her mid-30s and a Black man in his late 30s, facing each other..." Not "diverse couple," not "two adults dancing."

### Age band — TIGHTENED

- **Acceptable range: 30 to 38.** Early 30s to mid-late 30s.
- **Banned: under 30, over 45.** Resonance is not a 20-something party and not a midlife event.
- **In prompts**: "early-to-mid 30s, mature faces, eye crinkles when smiling, not in their 20s, no Gen Z styling."

### Specific multicultural cast — NAMED, NOT GENERIC

The ICP names specific ethnicities. Prompts must name specifics, not "diverse":

- Latina (Costa Rican / Mexican / Colombian — name one)
- Black (mixed-Caribbean or African-American — name one)
- White with Polish-Italian features (Daniel archetype)
- Filipino or Korean adult (honor Chicago's actual demographic mix)
- Mixed-East-Asian or South-Asian secondary

"Diverse cast" / "multicultural" without specifics → produces ambiguously-brown generic-diversity output. Always name.

### Anti-AI-slop paragraph — PASTE VERBATIM at the END of every AI image prompt

```
Anatomically correct human proportions. Five fingers per hand, no extra digits, no
fused fingers, no merged limbs, no duplicate body parts. Real photography fidelity.
Skin texture has pores, not airbrushed. Eyes focused, not glassy. No mutated faces.
No extra arms or legs in the background. Reject any AI-painting feel; this is a
photograph, not a render.
```

If the model produces anatomy errors, move this paragraph to the TOP of the prompt and repeat the slop-rejection clauses twice.

### Deployment routing — when to use MCP vs manual

| Use case | Route |
|---|---|
| Iteration speed needed, user has direct platform access (ChatGPT, Gemini) | **Manual deployment** — Claude produces high-density prompts; user deploys in ChatGPT GPT Image 2 or Gemini Nano Banana Pro. See `launch/03-visual-variants/prompt-set-for-manual-deployment.md`. |
| Bulk generation in pipeline | MCP (Higgsfield Soul or Fal photoreal) — verify response metadata names the requested tier, abort if substituted |
| Style-family / poster / typography | `fantastic-posters` (Fal) — confirmed-correct route |
| Stylized-but-real lifestyle | Higgsfield Soul (NOT Nano Banana 2; verify Pro tier explicitly) |

The 2026-05-26 v2 failure cause was tier substitution (Nano Banana Pro → 2) without surfacing. Always verify response metadata on MCP calls or route to manual deployment.

## 7. The Final Test Before Publish

Before any image lands on a Resonance surface, run all three:

1. **The 11pm test.** *Could this have been taken at 11pm in a club?* If yes — kill.
2. **The wellness test.** *Does this look like an AI render of a wellness retreat?* If yes — kill.
3. **The voice test.** *Could Andrea show this to a friend who lives the room and have them recognize it?* If no — kill.

Three tests, every image. The discipline is what the brand is selling.
