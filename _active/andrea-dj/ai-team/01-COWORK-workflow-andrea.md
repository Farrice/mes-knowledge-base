# How You Work With Cowork (and Stay Inside the Plan)

You have two places to work, and they do different jobs. Getting this right is what keeps
you fast *and* keeps you from running out of Cowork on the $20 plan.

---

## The rhythm: plan in chat, do it in Cowork

**1. Plan and draft in your Project chat.** This is where your team lives — Front of House,
The Crowd, the rest. They know the brand, your voice, the twelve lines. All the thinking,
the writing, the deciding, the figuring-out happens here. This is also the *cheaper* place
to go back and forth, so take your time and get it right.

**2. When it's time to actually *do* something, take the plan to Cowork.** Building a thing,
posting a batch, setting up a form, anything with hands-on steps — that's Cowork's job.

**3. Don't think out loud in Cowork.** Cowork is for executing a finished plan, not for
brainstorming. Every minute it spends figuring out what you want is a minute off your limit.

---

## The trick that saves your limit: bring a brief, not a conversation

When you're ready to move to Cowork, ask your Project for a **Cowork Brief**. Just say:

> *"Make me a Cowork brief for this."*

Your team will hand you a tight little plan — objective, the exact stuff Cowork needs,
numbered steps, and a "done" checklist. You paste *that whole thing* into Cowork as your
first message. Now Cowork doesn't have to ask questions or guess — it just runs the steps
and stops when it's done. One clean session instead of ten messages of back-and-forth.

**Why this matters on the $20 plan:** Cowork has usage limits that reset on a schedule. A
tight brief might take Cowork a handful of steps. A vague "can you help me with my content?"
can eat your whole window before anything ships. The brief is the difference.

---

## What goes where — a quick gut check

| Do this in the **Project chat** | Take this to **Cowork** |
|---|---|
| "What should I post this week?" | "Post these 3 captions to the drafts." |
| "Write the application email." | "Set up the application form with these questions." |
| "Is this person right for the room?" | "Send these 5 accepts and 2 declines." |
| "Draft the venue pitch." | "Email it to these 4 venues and track replies." |
| Any thinking, writing, deciding | Any building, sending, batch-doing |

If you're not sure — it's probably a chat job. When in doubt, plan in chat first, then ask
for the Cowork brief. You'll almost never lose by planning first.

---

## Three habits that keep Cowork happy

1. **One job per Cowork session.** Finish the batch, then close it. Don't let one session
   sprawl into five different tasks.
2. **Always start Cowork with a brief, never a blank "help me."** The brief is your seatbelt.
3. **If Cowork starts asking a lot of questions, stop.** That means the plan wasn't tight
   enough — go back to your Project chat, finish the thinking, get a cleaner brief, return.

You drive. The Project chat is where you think with your team. Cowork is where the plan gets
done. Keep them in their lanes and the $20 plan goes a long way.
