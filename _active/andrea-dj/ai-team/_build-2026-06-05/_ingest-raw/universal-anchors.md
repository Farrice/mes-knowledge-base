Resonance — Universal Anchors
Purpose: The calibration layer that sits between the Data Brief (quantitative ground truth) and the Recognition Map (qualitative deep-canvass). Collapses both into the universal pains, promises, and moments that work across every heterosexual 30–42 Chicago adult exhausted by current dating formats, regardless of which avatar tier they sit in.
Status: v1.0, 2026-05-20. Built from Data Brief v1.0 + Recognition Map (Marcus + Daniel + Umbrella tiers, locked 2026-05-19) + Brand Bible §1/§3/§5/§7 + Voice Document v1.0.
Operating principle (per Farrice, 2026-05-19): Specific in channel, universal in public. Marcus, Daniel, Nora, and Imani avatar profiles stay canonical for channel-targeted work. Public IG, bio, About page, Reels, landing page, and press one-sheeters need universal-but-resonant voice grounded in pains/promises shared by all ICP tiers.
The line that runs through every section: universal does not mean for everyone. Universal means the specific shared pains of heterosexual 30–42 Chicago adults exhausted by current dating formats. Tier-level specificity is preserved. Scene-level specificity is broadened.
Section A — The 8 Universal Pains
Each pain clears the 4-tier recognition test (Marcus + Daniel + Nora + Imani + umbrella each recognize themselves) and ties to a Data Brief finding. If a pain is not universal in this sense, it is not in this section — it belongs in channel-specific work.
A1. App fatigue: every interaction now feels like a job
Data anchor: Data Brief A.1 (Match Group Q1 2026 –5% paying users YoY). Data Brief A.4 (Pew: 48% of online daters report harassment). Data Brief A.8 (Forbes Health: 78% dating-app burnout, LIKELY).
Cross-tier recognition:
- Marcus — deleted Hinge in March 2025 and has not reinstalled.
- Daniel — deleted and reinstalled once, sends three specific messages, nobody responds before he closes the app.
- Nora — opens Hinge for the first time in two weeks, swipes nine minutes, three Ryans with the same gym selfie, texts the friend "i'm not built for this."
- Imani — has not opened Hinge in eleven days, has not deleted it, has left it open instead.
- Umbrella — opens the apps three times a day and closes them inside ninety seconds each time.
Wince test:
- Shuts them down: "App burnout is real" stated as a content claim. Reads as a Forbes headline a reader has seen 200 times.
- Opens them: name the specific behavior, not the category. "Three opens. Three closes. Ninety seconds each." The pain is in the count, not the diagnosis.
Sample line: "You've opened the app three times today and closed it inside ninety seconds each time. A body refuses jobs it was never supposed to take on."
A2. The bar at 11pm has stopped being the answer
Data anchor: Data Brief B.1 (Gallup 2025: 54% of Americans drink, 90-year low). Data Brief B.9 (alcohol share of total beverage 46% → 41% in three years). Brand Bible §3 names this directly as Enemy 3.
Cross-tier recognition:
- Marcus — does not go to bars anymore and has been to a friend's birthday at a West Loop bar where he had two drinks and talked to nobody new.
- Daniel — pours a finger of bourbon at 11:38pm on a Tuesday, decides he is not going to finish it.
- Nora — eats peanut butter off a spoon on the kitchen floor at midnight instead of going to the bar she used to close down at 27.
- Imani — drinks rarely and intentionally, has declined three "sober social" events because the room was emotionally heavy.
- Umbrella — has stopped going out and has not stopped wanting to meet someone.
Wince test:
- Shuts them down: a sobriety claim posed as a virtue or a lifestyle. "Sober living is freeing!" — reads as recovery-adjacent for Imani and as preaching for everyone else.
- Opens them: the bar at 11pm as a format, not a moral judgment. The bar broke its promise. The bar was the only permission slip the city gave you. That permission slip stopped working.
Sample line: "The bar at 11pm was a permission slip. It stopped working a long time ago. Nobody told you what came next."
A3. You have stopped going out and have not stopped wanting to meet someone
Data anchor: Data Brief A.5 (Eventbrite: 376,000 attended in-person singles events; 42% YoY growth in offline singles). Data Brief E.8 (the "30-something women have stopped going out" cultural surface, LIKELY).

Sample line: "You've stopped going out. You haven't stopped wanting to meet someone. The bar at 11pm was never going to be the answer."

Section D — The Public-Voice Register Definition
This section is load-bearing. It is the operating instruction for everyone who touches a public-facing Resonance surface.
The core rule
Public-voice surfaces speak from Universal Pains (Section A) and Universal Promises (Section B), not from avatar scenes. The pain "App fatigue: every interaction now feels like a job" (A1) is universal. The avatar scene "Bridgeport BJJ gym at 7:48pm, scrolling Hinge between rolls" is Marcus-specific. Public-voice work names the pain. Channel-voice work renders the scene.
What stays the same as the BOS Voice Document
	•	The six voice patterns (anaphora, frame-then-sharpen, crystallized-phrase stack, out-loud-asking, hell-yes filter, mechanic-as-sentence) all carry into public-voice work without modification.
	•	The two voice registers (polished manifesto + conversational dictation-energy) both carry. Public-voice copy is mostly polished register; the founder Reel and origin-pillar content carry the conversational register.
	•	The banned vocabulary list (vibes, community, intentional, container, sacred, manifest, energy, soul, embodied, authentic connection, like-minded, etc.) is fully active in public-voice work. Universal does not soften the bans — it tightens them, because the public surface has wider taste sensitivity.
	•	The banned structural moves (twin-sentence aphoristic endings, "It's not X. It's Y." reveals, italicized mid-paragraph aphorisms, "Here is what nobody..." framing) are fully active.
	•	Em-dashes capped at 2 per piece. Ellipses preferred for soft pauses. AI-tell openers banned.

3 voice anti-exemplars (universal flattening — what to never ship publicly)
Anti-exemplar 1 — Wellness-coded "everyone welcome" drift:
A welcoming space for conscious singles to authentically connect, find their person, and build community.
Why it fails: every word is wellness-coded. Welcoming space, conscious singles, authentically connect, find their person, build community — five banned phrases in one sentence. Universal-flattening becomes wellness-flattening becomes invisible-flattening. Four-tier test: zero avatars recognize themselves. Each avatar has explicit wince-list rejections of every phrase here.
Anti-exemplar 2 — Lululemon-grade inclusivity language:
For everyone tired of the apps. For everyone ready for something real. For everyone who believes in slowing down and showing up.
Why it fails: for everyone is the opposite of universal. It is generic. The anaphora is a hollow Pattern 1 — no sharp landing line, just a TED-circuit close. Something real, slowing down, showing up are abstractions the ICP has read 10,000 times. The hell-yes filter is missing. The room sounds like every other singles event that has tried to feel different.
Anti-exemplar 3 — Generic "find your person" copy nobody recognizes:
In a world where dating apps have failed us, Resonance offers a thoughtful, intentional alternative — where you can finally find your person in a curated, beautiful environment.
Why it fails: In a world where is a banned opener (voice doc Section 5). Thoughtful, intentional, curated, beautiful is adjective inflation. Find your person stated as promise activates the bullshit detector across all four avatars. The sentence does no work — no scene, no mechanic, no recognition, no filter, no spine. This is the slop the brand was built against.
