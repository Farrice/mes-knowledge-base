You do not need 30 agents, 12 consultants, and a production crew. You need two things:

1. **~25 people who trust Andrea enough to say yes** (the seed network)
2. **A vetting process that filters for hell-yes commitment** (not enthusiasm — commitment)

Everything else is optimization. The 400+ pages of intelligence your swarm produced is research debt you're paying off early — valuable for Event #3, #4, #5. For Event #1, you need about 10% of it.

**Event #1 is not a production. It is a proof of concept.** If 40-50 people show up, have a genuinely good time, and 10-12 couples form, you have everything you need for Event #2. The success metric is **25% couple-formation rate** — 10-12 couples from ~50 attendees. That is the number. Not impressions. Not followers. Couples.

The failure metric is **twelve events in a row without hitting that number.** Twelve events accounts for normal variance, market education, and the time it takes for a community to form. After twelve, the data is real. That is the patience window.

**What this is:** Daytime. Sober. Curated. Phones off the floor. Andrea at the booth, directing. JR playing. Six people running it tight. Out by 5:00.

**What this is not:** A production. A party. A hookup event in a singles-event costume. Speed dating dressed as something else. An algorithm. A scalable startup playbook.

**What this is:** A room. Fifty people. Music and movement. The meeting is the point.

## The Seed-and-Spread Model: How 25 People Become 50

Forget "ticket sales." You're not selling tickets to strangers. You're inviting people into a room that Andrea built. The math works like this:

### The Seed Layer (~25 People) — Weeks 5-6 Out

These are people Andrea personally reaches out to — DMs, texts, calls. Not a landing page. Not a post. Personal. One by one.

#### Tier 1: Andrea's Inner Circle (5-8 People)

- Close friends who would say yes to almost anything Andrea asks
- These are the people who will come even if they're skeptical, because they love Andrea
- Ask them first. Their "yes" gives you early momentum and social proof
- Some of these should be men — men who will move on the floor early and model participation
- Ask them 6 weeks out so they can save the date immediately

#### Tier 2: The Warm Network (10-15 People)

- Friends-of-friends, colleagues, people Andrea knows but isn't close to
- People who have said "I need to meet someone" within earshot of Andrea or mutual friends
- Music-scene people who would come for the music and stay for the connection
- The BJJ/training community — men who already trust their bodies
- Creative-class people in Andrea's Chicago circles
- Reach these 5 weeks out

#### Tier 3: The Extended Reach (5-10 People)

- People one degree removed — friends of Tier 1 and Tier 2
- These require a referral: "My friend Andrea is doing this thing — I think you'd actually like it"
- The critical group for gender balance: if Tier 1-2 skews female, Tier 3 should be male-targeted outreach (and vice versa)
- Reach these 4 weeks out, after you know your early gender split

### The Spread Layer (~25 More People) — Weeks 3-4 Out

Once the seed layer is committed (not "interested" — committed with calendar holds), you open two channels:

#### Channel A: Referral Engine (15-20 People)

- Every person in the seed layer gets asked: "Who's one person you think would actually like this?"
- This is not "invite your friends." This is: "Think of one person — man or woman — who is single, around our age, and would be open to something different."
- The referrer does a warm intro (text, DM, in-person) — Andrea follows up personally
- This is where Marcus comes from (the quiet man reached by a trusted woman)
- This is also where the "I Would Never Go to a Singles Event" woman comes from

#### Channel B: Content/Inbound (5-10 People)

- Andrea posts 2-3 times about the event in the 3 weeks before
- Not a sales post — a "here's what I'm making and why" post
- Authentic, personal, not polished
- The landing page is live by this point (friend's job) and captures interest
- People who find it interesting fill out a short form; Andrea calls them within 48 hours
- Expect a low conversion rate here for Event #1 — 5-10 people from content is realistic

### The Hold Layer — Weeks 2-3 Out

- 15-20% of your committed people will drop off. This is normal. Plan for it.
- If you have 60 confirmed with deposits by week 3, you'll land at 48-50 actual attendees
- If you have 55 confirmed, aim for a few more to buffer
- The 48-hour audit: confirm everyone 48 hours before. Expect 2-3 "something came up" messages. Have 2-3 people on a waitlist ready to fill those spots.

## Gender Balance: The Numbers That Actually Work

From the Room Chemist's research, the optimal ratio is approximately 24 men / 26 women (in a 50-person room). Here's how to think about it practically:

### The Realistic Starting Point

For Event #1, you're not going to perfectly engineer 24/26. Here's a more honest frame:

**The floor is 20 men.** Below 20 men, the room starts to feel unbalanced to both genders — women notice the scarcity and compete; men feel exposed and performative.

**The ceiling is 30 men.** Above 30 men in a 50-person room, the competitive energy rises and the room starts to feel like a target-rich environment rather than a shared experience.

**The target: 22-26 men, 26-30 women.** This gives you a slight female majority (which research shows women prefer) without creating a scarcity dynamic.

### How to Actually Hit This Ratio

**Week 6 (early outreach):** Track gender from your very first outreach. Use a simple spreadsheet:

| Name | Source | Gender | Status | Vetting Call | Deposit | Notes |
|------|--------|--------|--------|-------------|---------|-------|

**Week 5:** If your early yeses skew heavily female (they probably will — women are more open to this concept), deliberately target male outreach. Ask your committed women: "Who's one man you know who would actually come to this?" That's how you find Marcus.

**Week 4:** If you're still light on men, shift your entire referral engine to male-focused. Andrea's personal outreach should prioritize men she knows or is one degree from. The creative-class men (designers, engineers, trainers, teachers) are your targets — not the confident extroverts, the uncertain men who would come if someone they trusted asked.

**Week 3:** If you have fewer than 18 men confirmed, consider a targeted micro-campaign: Andrea posts specifically about the music aspect ("curating a daytime set of house, soul, Afrobeat, and disco for a small room — July 18, DM me if you want to hear it"). This attracts men who would come for the music and discover the connection.

**Week 2:** If you're at 20+ men, stop recruiting men. Close the male list. If you're at 15-18 men, make a final push through personal DMs. Below 15 men: consider whether Event #1 should be 40 people instead of 50. A smaller room with the right ratio beats a full room that's unbalanced.

### The Honest Fallback

If you cannot get 20 men committed by week 2, you have three options:

1. **Resize to 40 people** (16-18 men / 22-24 women) — still works, just smaller
2. **Brief 2-3 seed dancers** (see Seed Dancer Protocol below) — men who arrive on time, position at the edge, and dance naturally to give permission to the room
3. **Postpone Event #1 by 2-3 weeks** and keep recruiting. Better to delay than to launch with a broken room.

**Do not:** Compromise on vetting just to hit a number. 18 well-curated men is infinitely better than 25 uncurated men.

## Seed Dancer Protocol (Concrete)

This is not theoretical. This is what Andrea does.

**Who:** 2-3 men, pre-briefed by Andrea personally in a 5-minute conversation before the event.

**What they do:**

- Arrive on time (not early — on time, like regular attendees)
- Position themselves at the **edge of the floor**, not the center
- Dance naturally. Not performatively. Not to draw attention. Like regular guys who felt like dancing.
- **Do NOT approach women in the first 30 minutes.** Their job is not to approach. Their job is to be on the floor so other men feel permission to be on the floor.
- They are **NOT performers.** They are permission-givers.
- They should **NOT be identifiable as "staff" or "plants."** They are regular attendees who happen to be dancing early.

**When their job is done:**

- After 45 minutes, or
- When the floor is naturally full (whichever comes first)
- Then they become regular attendees. Dance, talk, meet people — like everyone else.

**What Andrea tells them (5-minute briefing):**

> "I need you to do one thing: be on the floor dancing in the first 30 minutes. That's it. Don't perform. Don't approach women. Just be a regular guy who's dancing. Other men will see you and feel like they can dance too. After 45 minutes or when the floor fills, you're done — just enjoy the event. Nobody should know I asked you. You're not staff. You're a guest who happened to dance early."

**What NOT to do:**

- Do not put them in the center. They are not entertainment.
- Do not give them a script. They are not actors.
- Do not make them identifiable in any way. No badges, no different dress, no special positioning.
- Do not have them approach women early. That signals "this is a pickup event" — the opposite of what we want.

## Confirmation Cadence: T-14, T-7, T-1, T-3h

Every attendee receives four touchpoints. Not random. Not excessive. A rhythm.

| Timing | Channel | Content |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **T-14 days** | Email | Preview + what to expect. Sets the emotional frame. "Here's what you're walking into." |
| **T-7 days** | Email | Logistics + arrival details. Address, transit, parking, what to bring, what not to bring. Practical. |
| **T-1 day** | Email | Final reminder + parking/transit specifics. "Tomorrow." Warm but brief. |
| **T-3 hours** | Text (separate from email) | Day-of reminder. Short: "Resonance today. Doors at 2:00pm. Movement on Montrose. See you in the room. — Andrea" |

**Why four?** T-14 builds anticipation. T-7 removes friction (where do I park?). T-1 is the nudge. T-3h is the pulse — a text feels personal, immediate. By the time they arrive, they feel held. They know what to expect. They're not anxious.

## Day-of Logistics: The 12 Items

1. **Restroom access management.** Restrooms are outside the studio. Know the capacity. Have a plan if queues form.

2. **Coat check / bag storage.** Designated corner, monitored. Not an afterthought. People need to feel their stuff is safe so they can be present.

3. **Decibel monitoring.** NIOSH SLM app. **>85dB triggers step-down. >90dB means DJ stops.** Protect the room's ears. Protect the conversation that happens between songs.

4. **Lighting as daylight shifts.** Warm lamps to layer in as natural light fades. **No fluorescents.** The room should feel like late afternoon golden hour, not a conference room.

5. **Emergency exits walkthrough.** Andrea + 2-3 staff know all exits. Done during setup. Not discussed — walked.

6. **First-aid kit.** Bandaids, pain reliever, electrolyte packets, tampons/pads. Positioned, not buried in a bag.

7. **Incident protocol.** Pre-decided script for who handles what. Someone behaves badly → who steps in? Someone has anxiety → who helps? The lull hits early → who signals Andrea? Decided before doors open.

8. **Trash + cleanup plan.** 30-minute cleanup window. Venue restoration. Movement on Montrose gets its space back exactly as found.

9. **DJ setup load-in/load-out timing.** Gear arrives ~12:00pm. Sound check by 1:00pm. Gear out by 6:00pm. No exceptions — the venue has a life after Resonance.

10. **Phone basket security.** Labeled baskets. One per slot. Monitored at all times. Tags with names. Check-in at arrival. Check-out at departure. The basket is sacred — if phones walk, trust walks.

11. **Check-in flow.** Pre-printed name list. QR scanner backup. 1-2 greeters. A-L/M-Z split if needed. Smooth, not frantic. The first 60 seconds sets the tone for the whole event.

12. **Water station.** 30% excess inventory pre-positioned. Water only. No food, no snacks. Replenished as needed. Not fancy. Present.

## Success Criteria for Event #1

Don't measure success by perfection. Measure it by these concrete signals:

**Must-have (the event fails without these):**

- [ ] 40+ people show up (out of 50-55 confirmed)
- [ ] The floor fills within 45 minutes of the first body moving
- [ ] Nobody leaves within the first 45 minutes
- [ ] At least 3-4 people say "when is the next one?" before they leave

**Strong signals (the event is working):**

- [ ] 6+ men say they want to come back
- [ ] 2+ men nominate another man for Event #2
- [ ] Men describe the event in specifics — "the song when I stopped thinking" — not "good vibe"
- [ ] At least one couple exchanges contact info and follows up

**Home run (Event #2 sells itself):**

- [ ] **10-12 couples form** (25% couple-formation rate — the hard success metric)
- [ ] 10+ people actively ask about Event #2 before leaving
- [ ] The referral engine is self-sustaining: confirmed attendees bring 1+ person each without being asked

**Not success criteria (ignore these):**

- [ ] Perfect gender ratio (close enough is fine for Event #1)
- [ ] Everyone dances (some people will stand at the edge all day — that's OK)
- [ ] Zero awkward moments (awkward is part of the format)
- [ ] Social media buzz or press (irrelevant for Event #1)
- [ ] A video or photos of the dancing (the phone basket means no floor photos — that's the point)

**The patience window:** Twelve events in a row. It accounts for normal variance, market education, and the time it takes for a community to form. After twelve, the data is real. If you haven't hit 25% couple-formation by Event #12, you stop. You look at the data. You decide what comes next.
