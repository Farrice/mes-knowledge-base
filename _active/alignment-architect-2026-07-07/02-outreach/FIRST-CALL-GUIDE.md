# FIRST-CALL-GUIDE.md — The 30-Minute Alignment Call

The gift this call runs on: you read the person, find the misalignment, and name it out loud. The named pattern is the demo. Nobody has to imagine what an Alignment Audit produces — they just watched you produce one, live, for free, in the first twenty minutes.

## Structure

**0-3 min — Open.** No pitch, no agenda-setting. "Tell me what's going on with the business right now — good and bad." Let them talk. You're not filling time, you're collecting their exact words for minute eighteen.

**3-20 min — Diagnose live.** Ask, don't tell. Investigative and connecting questions, in this order:

1. "Walk me through the last three people who paid you — what did they actually say right before they said yes?"
2. "If I asked you right now, one sentence, what do you do and who's it for — what would you say?" (Let the sentence be messy. The mess is the material.)
3. "When someone asks what you charge, what do you actually say?"
4. "What's it costing you, not having a clean answer to that?"
5. "How many of your clients right now came from someone telling someone else, versus you going out and getting them?"

Listen for the gap between what they're good at and what they can say out loud. Don't fix it yet. Let it widen.

**~18-20 min — Name the pattern.** This is the whole call. Reflect the gap back in their own words, not yours: "You just told me three people paid you for [X], but when I asked what you do, you gave me [Y]. You're not confused about the work — you're avoiding writing it down, because writing it down means charging for it." Say it plainly, once, and stop talking. Let it land. This is the demo. Everything after this is logistics.

**20-25 min — Bridge.** Because you already found the gap, the pitch is one sentence: "That's exactly what the Audit does — pulls the language out of your real conversations instead of you guessing at it, and hands you back one line and one price in 48 hours." If they're clearly ready for more than a diagnostic, name the Sprint too: "If you already know the offer needs rebuilding, not just named, the Sprint does that in three weeks." Don't oversell past what they just told you they need.

**25-30 min — Close and next step.** "Want me to send the details?" If yes: "I'll send the payment link and the request for three real client conversations or testimonials in the same message — the session's on the calendar once both are in, that's what the Audit runs on." Payment and material both land before the session books, stated up front, not chased after the diagnostic is already written.

---

## Verbatim Responses

**"Let me think about it."**
"Totally fair. What specifically are you weighing — is it the price, the timing, or whether this actually fixes it?" Wait for the real answer, then meet that one thing. Don't re-pitch the whole offer to fill silence.

**"I can't afford it."**
"I hear you. Can I ask — what's it costing you to stay exactly where you are for another six months?" Let that sit before you say anything else. Then: "The Audit's $300 to $500. If that's genuinely not there right now, no hard feelings, we can pick this back up later. But most people who say that aren't out of money — they're out of certainty it'll work. Which one is it for you?"

**"Can you just do it for me?"** — the Cooz trap. Say yes here and you're back to the unbounded engagement that burned you out.
"I could — and that's actually the exact thing that burned out the last coach I did this for. The Audit only works if it comes from your real conversations, which means you're the one pulling those three conversations, not me. You bring the raw material, I bring the pattern. That split isn't a preference, it's the only version of this that's ever actually worked." Then hold it. Don't soften the split to close the call faster.
