Draft:

A buyer wakes up with a sleep score they do not like.

They stand in the kitchen with a protein drink, a coffee, a supplement bottle, and a calendar they already resent.

The question in their head is not:

"Which health and wellness category should I research today?"

The question is:

"What should I do before the 9 AM meeting so I do not feel wrecked by noon?"

Most wellness brands are not built to answer that.

They are built around products:

energy drink, sleep supplement, protein bar, wearable, gut health capsule, coaching app.

That structure makes sense internally. It makes the website clean. It makes the content calendar easier to manage.

That clean internal structure forces the buyer to do the hardest part.

They have to translate the product claim into a decision inside their actual day.

PwC's 2026 consumer research describes health becoming less episodic and more continuous. Food, sleep, stress, weight, appearance, performance, and longevity are becoming daily buying and behavior moments.

NIQ points to the same pressure from another angle: the self-directed health consumer is using wearables, AI, and product data to choose, compare, and adjust.

That should change the way health brands write.

The more useful page might answer:

"What should I take when I slept badly but still have to perform?"

"How do I choose a focus drink if I am sensitive to caffeine?"

"What does this wearable score mean for today's training?"

"When is this product a bad fit?"

That last question matters.

In health, trust often comes from the brand being willing to say when the product is not the answer.

The audit I would run:

Take one product page.

Write down the three moments where the buyer is most likely to reach for it.

For each moment, ask:

What is the buyer trying to decide?

What can the brand safely say?

What proof or caveat needs to sit next to the answer?

What page, post, FAQ, founder note, or comparison should exist?

If the brand cannot answer those four questions, the content calendar is downstream of the real issue: a moment problem.

The moment is the brief.
