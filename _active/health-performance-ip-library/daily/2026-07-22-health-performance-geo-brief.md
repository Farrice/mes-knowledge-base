The other says "clean," "plant-based," "third-party tested," in that order, in a slightly larger font than the ingredient list. Nobody in the room has ever seen the lab report either tub is supposedly resting on.

Buyer tension: the buyer who switched to plant-based did it on purpose, to avoid dairy, hormones, or "chemicals" she associated with whey. She read "plant-based" and "clean" as safety information. The brand that printed "third-party tested" believed the phrase alone did the trust work a real, linked result would otherwise have to do. Belief shift: from "plant-based, organic, and clean label are safety signals I can read off the label" to "those are formulation and marketing categories, not test results, and the only way to know the actual number is if the brand shows it to me."

Plain-English version of the strategic phrase: a source-to-search trust layer, in this case, just means a brand publishing the real lab result behind its "tested" claim in one place, in plain language, so nobody has to take the phrase on faith, and so an AI engine answering "does this powder contain lead" has something honest to cite.
