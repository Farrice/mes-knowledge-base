- What happened: FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance states that health benefit or safety claims for foods, supplements, drugs, and related products generally require competent and reliable scientific evidence. # Why GLP-1-Adjacent Wellness Brands Need Citation-Fidelity Blocks

AI search changes the job of health content.

A brand no longer needs only a sourced page.

It needs a page that keeps the source's limits attached to the claim.

That distinction matters in GLP-1-adjacent wellness.

A protein brand might discuss strength.

A recovery brand might discuss fatigue.

A supplement brand might discuss energy, cravings, or metabolic support.

A founder might share a buyer story.

Each of those can be useful.

Each can also become bigger than the evidence if the page does not define the boundary.

Recent AI Overview measurement research points to the same concern from a search angle: being cited does not mean every claim in the answer is fully supported by the cited pages.

For health brands, that creates a new content requirement.

Every claim-heavy page should include a citation-fidelity block:

- What this claim says
- What the source supports
- What the source does not prove
- Who this language is for
- What wording the brand will avoid
- What a buyer should ask a clinician, pharmacist, or qualified professional

This is not legal decoration.

It is retrieval design.

If a search engine, AI answer, journalist, buyer, or internal copywriter pulls from the page, the limitation travels with the benefit.

That is how a wellness brand becomes easier to trust and harder to misquote.
