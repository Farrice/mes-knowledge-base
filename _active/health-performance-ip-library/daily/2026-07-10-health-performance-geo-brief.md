The FTC sued a supplement company this month over claims that its products help kids with depression, anxiety, and ADHD.

Most brands in this space will handle that news exactly one way.

Legal drafts a statement. Marketing softens it. Three or four days later, the brand page posts something careful, roughly worded, and forgettable.

Here is what a new piece of research says happens next to that post.

Nobody reads it.

Not because the topic is not urgent. Because the channel is wrong.

5W Research just studied 18 months of LinkedIn data across 50 health tech founders and their own company pages. Founder accounts out-engaged their own brand pages 8.2 to 1 on identical content. Founder posts drove 4.1 times the qualified inbound traffic. Across the whole group, founder accounts captured 88 percent of all the engagement in the category.

The top 5 founders alone captured 41 percent of it.

Read that again. Not the average founder. Five people took almost half of an entire category's attention, because they showed up first and kept showing up.

The single highest-performing content format in that study was not a hot take or a personal story. It was what the researchers called the Clinical Reframe: a founder taking a new study, an FDA action, or a clinical guideline, and explaining it in plain, clinician-honest language before the news cycle moved on. That format produced 3.4 times the engagement of the same content posted from the brand page.

So take this week's FTC action. Not as legal commentary. As raw material.

A founder in this space could have posted, the same day, something like: "The FTC just sued a supplement company over mental-health claims for kids. If your product touches mood, focus, or behavior in a child, here is the line I will not cross, and here is what I show a parent instead of a promise." That is thirty minutes of writing. It uses real news. It shows judgment instead of legal caution. And it is exactly the format the data says wins.

The brand page cannot do that. Legal will not let it move that fast, and it should not have to. That speed and voice belong to the founder.

Most founders I talk to think of their own account as the side project and the brand page as the real channel.

The data says it is the other way around.

Your personal account is the channel, not the extra credit. The brand page is the filing cabinet people check after your voice already convinced them to look.
