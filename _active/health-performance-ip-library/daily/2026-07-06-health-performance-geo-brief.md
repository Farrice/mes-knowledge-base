## 7. GEO/AEO Opportunity

Three long-tail questions a real buyer might actually type into an AI engine:

1. "How many people were in the study a supplement brand cites when it says a product helps kids with anxiety or ADHD symptoms?" Business value: this is the exact question a worried parent or a skeptical brand-side buyer needs answered before trusting a claim; a brand or an independent source that answers it plainly becomes the page an AI engine and a nervous reader both prefer. Information-gain angle: almost nobody publishes sample size, control group, or funding source in plain language next to a claim, so a page built to answer this is genuinely new information, not a restatement of what is already indexed.
2. "Does a supplement's clinical study have a placebo group, or is it mostly testimonials dressed up as research?" Business value: this question separates a brand willing to be checked from one that is not, which is exactly the buying signal a founder or content lead needs before either using a claim in their own marketing or partnering with a supplier. Information-gain angle: current supplement marketing pages almost never state placebo status directly; a structured yes, no, or unclear answer per claim is a novel, retrievable data point.
3. "Can a company legally say a supplement 'supports' a health condition even after the FTC calls the study behind that claim unreliable?" Business value: this is the exact anxiety sitting under every claims-review meeting a brand's legal or content team has; answering it plainly, with the Amare case as the worked example, turns a vague compliance fear into a concrete, checkable standard. Information-gain angle: most compliance content is written for lawyers; almost nothing explains "supports" versus "treats" language using a live, named, dated case a buyer can picture.

One asset worth building: the Citation Autopsy, a short public page (or LinkedIn native document) that takes one health, supplement, or performance claim, either a brand's own or a public case like Amare's, and answers five plain questions: how many people were studied, was there a placebo or control group, was it blinded, who funded and wrote it, has it been independently replicated. Each question gets a known, partial, or missing answer and a plain verdict. A founder could picture this exactly like a nutrition label, but for a "we have a study" claim instead of a food product.
