## Repeated Buyer Fears

- "I want the wellness product, but I do not know which claims are real."
- "This sounds medically adjacent. Is the brand allowed to say it?"
- "The page feels confident, but the evidence is hidden or unclear."
- "AI search may summarize the claim without the caveat."
- "I do not want another trend. I want someone safe to believe."

## Strongest Source-Backed Claims

- FTC expects health-related claims to be truthful, not misleading, and supported by science.
- CMS launched the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge on July 1, 2026 for eligible Part D beneficiaries.
- FDA has active concerns about unapproved GLP-1 drugs used for weight loss.
- Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use query fan-out and supporting sources.
- Protein, liver health, recovery, wellness-status products, and GLP-1 adjacency all create claim-boundary opportunities.

## Weak Claims To Avoid

- Any supplement improves GLP-1 outcomes.
- Any protein product prevents muscle loss or preserves muscle during medication use.
- Any detox or liver-support product cleanses, repairs, or treats the liver.
- Any recovery tool produces clinical recovery outcomes without product-specific evidence.
- Any AI-search citation claim without live observation, screenshots, or prompt logs.

## Highest-Value Long-Tail Questions

1. How can wellness brands talk to GLP-1 users without making medical claims?
2. What does a protein brand need to show before saying it supports lean muscle?
3. Which supplement claims are product evidence, ingredient evidence, or category logic?
4. Can AI search preserve a wellness brand's caveats when summarizing the page?
5. What health claims should stay in clinician context instead of brand marketing copy?
