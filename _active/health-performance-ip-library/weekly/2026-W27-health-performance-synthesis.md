## Repeated Buyer Fears

1. "We can talk about a hot category (liver health, recovery tech, peptides, GLP-1 adjacency) without the language quietly becoming a treatment claim."
2. "Our sources are real, but the caveat or limitation may not survive when AI search summarizes the page."
3. "We got cited by an AI engine and celebrated, without checking whether anyone actually believed what it pointed to."
4. "If we move slowly on a trending topic, competitors own the conversation. If we move fast, we may overclaim."
5. "Our proof, sources, and claims are scattered across pages, testimonials, and founder posts instead of living in one place a stranger could check."

## Weak Claims To Avoid

- "GLP-1 drugs cause inactivity."
- "Compounded GLP-1s are all unsafe."
- "AI search will cite this asset."
- "A testimonial is safe because it is a real customer story."
- "Studies prove AI citations are worthless."
- "Google confirmed GEO doesn't work."
- "Function Health bought SuppCo to control what supplements get recommended" (motive not sourced; acquisition itself is LIKELY, not VERIFIED).
- "FDA sent exactly 30 warning letters" (count disputed across sources; state the range, not a single number).

## Highest-Value Long-Tail Questions

1. What is the difference between an AI engine citing a supplement brand and a customer actually believing the brand's claim is true?
2. How can a wellness brand tell if the page an AI engine cites would survive a skeptical customer or a regulator reading it closely?
3. Why does an AI search engine name my supplement brand as an answer but buyers still check reviews before they trust the claim?
4. How can AI-search pages make a caveat or limitation survive being compressed into a one-sentence summary?
5. What is the difference between a lifestyle wellness claim and a mental-health treatment claim the FTC will act on?
6. What should wellness brands check before mentioning GLP-1 users in product pages, ads, or founder content?
7. How can health brands make claim discipline feel like premium restraint instead of legal drag?
