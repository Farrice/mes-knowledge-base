## Unsafe Or High-Risk Legacy Claims

| Legacy Claim Pattern | Risk | Safer Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| "Source Grade VERIFIED" based on broad references such as FDA records, patient interviews, and fitness app data | Too broad unless exact sources are linked and reviewed. | Label market signals as LIKELY unless direct primary support is attached. |
| "Very high" evidence for protein, electrolytes, and creatine in a GLP-1 context | General nutrition evidence can be strong, but GLP-1-specific outcome support is narrower. | Separate general nutrition evidence from GLP-1-specific evidence. |
| Supplement dosing examples such as sodium, creatine, or caffeine amounts | Can read as nutrition or medical guidance. | Remove dosing from public offer assets unless reviewed by qualified professionals and sourced. |
| "None relevant to supplement positioning" as a limitation note | Too absolute. Health-product context can create limits even for common fitness claims. | Say "limitations depend on product, claim, context, and review environment." |
| "Safe to ship: YES, fully" | Too absolute for health, supplement, and GLP-1-adjacent copy. | Say "lower-risk direction, still subject to brand/legal/medical review where applicable." |
| "This proof demo is safe to share" | Overstates readiness. | Say "suitable for internal review or warm-buyer demonstration with publish boundary visible." |
| "AI search can retrieve" without testing | Can sound like a performance promise. | Use "built for AI-search inspection" unless live testing is logged. |

## Medical-Risk Language To Avoid In Public Copy

Avoid using these as claims unless a qualified review process has approved the exact context:

- prevents muscle loss
- offsets appetite suppression
- works with GLP-1
- helps with GLP-1 side effects
- required if taking medication
- safe on appetite medication
- claiming clinical proof for GLP-1 users
- replaces food for workout energy
- promises strength, energy, or results
