## Top Weaknesses

### 1. Cold buyers may not know what a trust layer is

Impact: HIGH

Where: the offer uses the phrase "Source-to-Search Trust Layer" early.

Why it matters: warm buyers will understand the backend value. Cold buyers may hear "AI documentation project" instead of "safer content and clearer proof."

Fix: lead with the pain before the module name.

Recommended public line:

> Your best claims, sources, and founder judgment are scattered. I turn them into source-backed pages and posts buyers can trust and AI search can understand.

Use the module name after the buyer sees the gap.

### 2. Retrieval language can sound like a guarantee

Impact: HIGH

Where: "AI search can retrieve" is persuasive but could be read as a performance promise.

Why it matters: the offer explicitly avoids live citation claims, but strict public pages should keep promise language lower-friction.

Fix: use promise gradients.

Safe ladder:

- Warm sales call: "AI search can retrieve."
- Public landing page: "built for AI-search retrieval."
- Legal-sensitive page: "structured so search and AI systems can inspect the source, claim, and boundary more clearly."

### 3. GLP-1 proof is still curated, not proprietary

Impact: HIGH

Where: the GLP-1 Movement Receipt uses public reporting, FTC guidance, Google guidance, and the existing internal daily brief.

Why it matters: this proves the method, but not yet the market moat. The stronger proof is a mini-audit of real GLP-1-adjacent pages showing claim types, risky implication, and safe rewrites.

Fix: add an anonymized 5-page or 10-page audit sample before public launch.

Minimum public-proof upgrade:

- 5 real wellness pages reviewed.
- Claims tagged by type.
- 3 common risk patterns named.
- 3 safe rewrite examples.
- 1 answer-page outline.

### 4. Health risk language is well-contained, but brand review is still required

Impact: MEDIUM

Where: GLP-1 examples include "movement," "energy," "strength," "muscle loss," and "appetite-change medications."

Why it matters: those words are commercially useful and risk-sensitive. The demo handles them carefully, but a real client version must adapt to the product, label, evidence, and review environment.

Fix: keep the publish boundary visible and add this line in public versions:

> This rubric is a marketing and source-organization tool. Final public claims still require brand, legal, medical, or regulatory review where applicable.

### 5. The retainer needs a cleaner monthly rhythm

Impact: MEDIUM

Where: the offer insert lists retainer outputs but does not show the monthly cadence.

Why it matters: retainers sell better when buyers see the operating rhythm.

Fix: add a small cadence line when this becomes sales copy:

> Each month: signal brief, claim receipt update, source-backed posts/articles, one answer-page brief, and one measurement pass.

## Medical-Risk Review

The GLP-1 asset avoids medical advice, avoids product outcome promises, labels conference reporting as LIKELY, and keeps FTC/Google claims in VERIFIED lanes.

Risk remaining: a client might copy the safe examples into product claims without review. Keep the publish boundary in every public version.

## Proof-Gap Review

The method proof is strong enough for a demo. The market proof needs original audit data.

Required before calling this a flagship public proof:

- Primary-source check for the GLP-1 movement research.
- Anonymized page audit sample.
- One live AI-search observation sheet, if making any citation claim.
- Screenshot or table showing before/after claim clarity.
