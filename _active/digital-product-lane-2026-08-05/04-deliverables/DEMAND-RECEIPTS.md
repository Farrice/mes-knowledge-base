## Method + honesty note

Etsy hard-blocks automated browsing (403 on both search and listing pages, confirmed twice). All listing-level receipts below are pulled live from **Gumroad Discover**, where rating counts and prices are public. Etsy category demand is corroborated only by Etsy's own public market pages and secondary reporting — treat Etsy-specific numbers as directional, not measured.

**Rating counts are a sales floor, not sales.** Typical digital-product review rates run low single-digit percentages, so a product with 22 ratings has plausibly sold hundreds. Use them to rank comps against each other, not to forecast revenue.

| Fitness AI Prompt Pack / GRT AI Full Stack / One-Click PT Prompt | **$4–7** | negligible | The quality floor. Prompt lists, no system, no authority. |

### The finding that changes the product spec

**Trainer-facing systems sell at $44–79. AI-branded trainer products do not sell at all.** Every AI-flavored listing in the set has one rating or none, including one that is nearly identical to the concept as originally framed. Meanwhile the two products with real traction are a Notion system and an authority-backed program.

Two readings, and only one is supported:

- *Wrong reading:* "AI trainer products don't sell, kill the lane."
- *Right reading:* **AI is not a value proposition to a trainer. The finished client deliverable is.** Nobody with 81 ratings sold "prompts." They sold a system a coach hands to a client.

**Spec implication.** Lead with the deliverable — *a periodized, branded, client-ready 12-week program built from an intake form in under an hour*. The AI engine is the how, disclosed and documented, never the headline. Price at **$69–79**, anchored by the $79 Notion comp and the $89.99 failed attempt.

### Why this is defensible for Farrice specifically

The four cheap listings are written by people who have never programmed for a paying client. The two that work are backed by a name or a system. Farrice has both halves and nobody in this comp set has either combination: 18 years, 1,000+ clients, NASM CPT/CES/PES + Precision Nutrition L1 — *and* the systems architecture to make the engine actually run. That is the moat, and it belongs in the sales copy as fact, not flourish.

**Verdict: BUILD.** Strongest evidence of the two lanes. Demand for the category is proven; the specific execution is unoccupied.

### Two findings

**1. The fandom wedge is validated.** "Poké: New Gen" holds $19.99 with 33 ratings — a fandom-specific pack sustaining ~4× the price of the generic $5 mega-packs. Generic aesthetics ("futuristic," "space") compete on price; a named fandom competes on belonging. **This is the receipt for the gacha instinct.** The right products are Genshin/HSR/ZZZ/Wuthering-Waves-adjacent aesthetic kits, not another neon overlay set.

**2. The price band is brutal at the bottom.** A $0.99 pack with 100 ratings sits at the top of search. The healthy zone is $19.99 with a real theme; below that is a knife fight, above $30 belongs to OWN3D's brand.

### Honest math

At $19.99 (net ~$18), $150/wk needs **~35 sales/month**. Reachable with a catalog of 20–40 fandom-specific kits — but it is a volume game against entrenched sellers, and it is the slower of the two lanes to first dollar.

**Unresolved risk — flag, not blocker.** Etsy tightened AI-content rules in 2026 (disclosure required; enforcement has removed listings where art was AI-generated; an August 2026 originality tightening is reported). I could **not verify this at primary source** — Etsy blocked automated access. Before committing catalog production to Etsy, read Etsy's own policy page manually. Gumroad carries no equivalent restriction, which makes it the safer first venue even though Etsy has better buyer intent for this category.

**Verdict: BUILD SECOND.** Real, price-compressed, and only worth it through the fandom wedge. Run it after Lane 1 ships, not in parallel — parallel catalogs in month one is how both end up at 90%.

## LANE 3 — Productized AI services (added mid-session)

**The tension, stated once:** a productized service does not sell while you sleep. It trades hours for a much better rate. It belongs here anyway, because it monetizes *the exact same IP* as Lane 1 at roughly ten times the price, and it is the fastest cash in this document. Treat it as the engine that funds the catalog, not as the goal.

**Non-negotiable caps** — these are what keep it from becoming a job: fixed scope, async delivery only, **no calls**, one revision, no ongoing retainer, no subcontractors.

### 3A · "Program System Install" — the natural upsell off Lane 1
Take the $79 product and install it for one trainer: their branding, their exercise library, their client intake, delivered configured with a Loom walkthrough. **$300–500, async, one naptime block.** Zero new IP — the product *is* the deliverable, and every buyer of the $79 version is a warm lead for this. Six installs a month clears the entire $200/wk target on its own.

### 3B · "Brain Extraction" — his actual moat, sold directly
A coach or creator with a body of work (course, podcast back-catalogue, years of client notes) hands it over and receives a deployable AI assistant that answers in their method and their voice. **$500–1,500, async, no calls.**

This is the one thing in this document that almost nobody else can deliver at all. The 90+-agent system and the extraction formulas are not talking points here — they are the production line. Higher customer-service exposure than 3A, which is why it stays capped at one revision and why it should not be first.

**Sequence: 3A before 3B.** 3A has a built-in lead source. 3B needs a buyer found from cold, which is the same distribution problem this whole exercise exists to avoid.

## The order of operations

1. **Ship Lane 1 at $69–79.** One product, listed publicly within seven days, ugly and live over polished and private.

4. **Hold 3B** until there is a public receipt to point at.

**The structural warning, from his own file, once:** every path here dies at the same place — the moment the listing goes public. The seven-day constraint on step 1 is the whole plan; the rest is production.
