## Strategic Read

The old market story was "resell a course and make money." That angle now carries heat, buyer fatigue, and reputation risk. The stronger EdgeCase angle is the opposite: "I bought the thing, avoided the hype cycle, and made a sober reality map for people deciding whether it is worth touching in 2026."

The strongest buyer is not a dream-chaser. The buyer is someone who:

- has heard DWA or master resale rights are dead;
- still wants a structured course on digital marketing and digital products;
- is wary of becoming an income-claim account;
- wants a faceless or low-personality path;
- wants to know what is useful, what is stale, what is risky, and what to ignore.

## Campaign Thesis

Sell the free asset first, not DWA first.

The free asset should do three jobs:

1. Filter for higher-quality buyers who can handle a sober view.
2. Build trust by naming the problems with DWA/MRR hype before recommending anything.
3. Move only qualified people to a private conversation where the affiliate disclosure, no-refund rule, and buyer fit can be made clear.

## Required Campaign Boundaries

- No income claims.
- No "I made money with this" claims unless Farrice has proof and wants to use them.
- No "you can make your money back" framing.
- No implying DWA is new, secret, untouched, or low-competition.
- No hiding the affiliate relationship.
- No hiding the no-refund policy.
- No using DWA marks in account handles, domains, ads, or search keywords without checking the terms.
- No posting confidential course-backend details.
- No professional LinkedIn push until a separate reputation-safe version exists.

## Opportunity

The campaign can win by making anti-hype the filter:

- public post: "I avoided selling DWA because the market felt gross. I re-opened it, mapped what is still useful, and wrote down what I would ignore."
- comment trigger: `MAP`
- DM: free DWA Reality Map with affiliate disclosure before any link
- sale path: only after fit check, no pressure, no outcome promise
