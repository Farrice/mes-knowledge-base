## Category Tensions Identified

- **"Dosed for what" is starting to replace "clinically dosed" as the differentiator.** Tonic Health's cognition-specific creatine claim is a specific, falsifiable mechanism sentence in a category where "clinically dosed" has become as generic as "science-backed." The brands naming the exact outcome their dose targets are the ones still readable as proof.
- **A regulatory restriction can be rebuilt into the headline benefit, not just absorbed as a limitation.** Noctiva Reset didn't apologize for being melatonin-free in a market where melatonin is restricted — it made the absence the pitch. That's the claim-safe wedge working in the direction Farrice's Pillar 2 argues it should: constraint becomes growth lever when the language is built around it on purpose.
- **Category convention (bedtime, dosing floor, "clinically dosed") keeps getting built around an unstated average user** — and every brand that names who that average excludes finds real shelf space nobody was fighting over.
