## What Moved (This Run)

**1. Mondelez re-licenses the Sour Patch Kids / Swedish Fish flavor IP it pulled from Ghost to a competitor, Applied Nutrition — first products live on GNC's shelf now. VERIFIED. Not a Dream 100 prospect itself — GNC is the retail partner already on the hot list; content fodder + public-use caution (see Notes).**
Background, dated for context: Ghost Lifestyle built its Sour Patch Kids/Swedish Fish flavor collaboration with Mondelez (launched 2018, "Project X") into one of its most recognizable product lines. After Keurig Dr Pepper's $1.65B acquisition of Ghost closed 2024-12-31, Mondelez sued (filed 2025-04-24), alleging the change of ownership breached the license without written approval; the two companies settled on 2025-05-19 with a "soft land" agreement to phase the flavors out of Ghost's lineup over 12 months.
The new development, inside this window: Mondelez turned around and licensed the same two properties — Sour Patch Kids and Swedish Fish — to Applied Nutrition (LSE-listed, ticker APN) for its AN Supps/ABE line, announced 2026-06-01. The first SKUs to actually reach shelves — Sour Patch Kids Redberry and Blue Raspberry Hydration+ stick packs, $12.99 for a 7-count box, with a live buy-one-get-one deal for GNC Pro members — are listed now on GNC's dedicated Sour Patch Kids collection page, ahead of the originally-announced ABE energy drink and pre-workout flavors (per Stack3d's August coverage: "another performance supplement quietly made its way to market").
*Why it's worth naming, not just noting:* the flavor-license asset outlived the brand relationship that built it. Mondelez didn't retire "Sour Patch Kids Redberry" as a supplement flavor when it pulled the license from Ghost — it re-sold the exact same differentiation lever to a competitor within about a year, landing at the same retailer (GNC) either way. That's sharper evidence than the usual "borrowed nostalgia" observation: a specific licensing asset proved valuable enough to survive a $1.65B acquisition, a lawsuit, and a change of licensee.

**2. FTC finalizes $4M judgment against TruHeight (suspended to $750K), permanent bar on false claims and fake reviews. VERIFIED. Direct Dream 100 occasion — TruHeight is already on the hot list for its CVS mass-retail debut.**
Per FTC's own press release (2026-07-15), the Commission's final order against Vanilla Chip LLC (d/b/a TruHeight) and its two principals, Eden Stelmach and Justin Rapoport, imposes a $4 million judgment — suspended to $750,000 based on documented inability to pay in full — and permanently bars the company from making unsubstantiated height-enhancement claims for kids'/teens' supplements and from using fake or incentivized five-star reviews written by its own employees, vendors, or discount-motivated customers. The underlying complaint (filed April 2026) alleged the reviews were manufactured rather than earned. This resolves the standing watchpoint carried since run #3/#4 — no brand-side statement has surfaced since the order.
*Why it's a purchase occasion:* TruHeight sits on the Dream 100 hot list for its CVS mass-retail debut, flagged specifically for a "claim-safe angle for skeptical parent on shelf." This final order is the sharpest available proof point for that exact angle — a concrete, dated, public dollar figure in place of a hypothetical risk.
