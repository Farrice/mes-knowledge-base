## What Moved (This Run)

**1. Procter & Gamble agrees to acquire Thorne (supplements brand, ~$650M run-rate revenue) from L Catterton for $3.8 billion. VERIFIED. Not a Dream 100 prospect — Thorne is named in the OS doc as a beachhead-tier comp brand ("AG1, Momentous, Thorne tier"), now moving out of the founder-led buyer band into a P&G subsidiary.**
P&G CEO confirmed the deal to CNBC on 2026-08-04; the transaction remains subject to regulatory approval, with completion expected later in 2026. Thorne went public in late 2021 at a $525M valuation, crossed $500M in revenue in 2025, and is tracking toward $650M this year — consumers under 40 account for its largest sales share, with notable DTC growth. P&G's existing health portfolio already includes Metamucil, Align Probiotic, and New Chapter, sitting alongside Oral-B and Vicks in its healthcare division.
*Why it's a purchase occasion, category-wide:* it's the sharpest available proof that a legacy CPG giant will pay a premium multiple for a supplement brand's clinical/functional positioning, not just its manufacturing or shelf space — directly on-thesis for Pillar 1 (the message is the asset being bought) and a live test case for Pillar 5 (can a distinct clinical voice survive being folded into a four-brand supplement portfolio inside a much larger healthcare division).

**2. Proposed class action filed against Ryse Up Sports Nutrition alleging its Clear Protein Drinks contain 6–11% less whey protein than the 22g labeled per serving. VERIFIED. Ryse Up is not currently on the Dream 100 list — proposing addition below, with a verification caveat.**
Filed in the US District Court for the Central District of California by plaintiff Daniel Ruchman; per the complaint, independent lab testing (EMSL Analytical, AOAC Method 992.15) of three Clear Protein Drink flavors found 19.69g–20.69g of protein per serving against the 22g claim, alleging violation of federal labeling requirements and California consumer-protection law. Reported 2026-08-04.
*Why it's a purchase occasion:* protein content is one of the few supplement claims a lab can directly test against the label — a harder, more literal version of the claim-safe argument the TruHeight order (run #6) already made about marketing language. Same pillar, sharper proof: this isn't a soft claim in dispute, it's a printed number against a measured one.
Sources: [NutraIngredients, 2026-08-04](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/08/04/ryse-sued-over-alleged-protein-shortfalls-in-clear-protein-drinks/) · [BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2026/proposed-class-action-claims-ryse-clear-protein-comes-up-short-on-label-claims)
