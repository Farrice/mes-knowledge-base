## What Changed From The First Pass

The first pass was accurate but too official-source heavy. It answered: "What does DWA publicly claim and what must we avoid saying?"

The deeper pass answers: "What are buyers actually anxious about before they buy, and what public arguments already surround the product?"

The stronger campaign should be built around the live buyer questions:

- Is this still working now?
- Is this just a digital MLM?
- What can I reuse or repost?
- How do I sell without an audience?
- What costs extra after buying?
- Can this work outside the US?
- How do payouts and payment processors work?
- Is DWA the same as SWC or other courses?
- Is PLR better than MRR?
- What happens if I buy through the wrong affiliate?
- Will anyone answer me after I buy?
- I already bought and feel overwhelmed; what do I do first?

## Buyer Voice Themes From Positive/Review Comments

`REAL`: Public comments under a high-view review show buyers ask:

- whether DWA works in specific countries;

## Buyer Voice Themes From DWA Criticism Comments

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-4Yj6TIE8BA

`REAL`: Comments under a DWA criticism/scam-positioned video show a trust contradiction:

- people ask whether it still works after a year;
- some call the mechanism pyramid-like;
- some question how the founder earns if resellers keep most or all sale proceeds;
- some call out the confusion of selling a product while framing it as a scam;
- some see "scam" titles as attention mechanics;
- some compare DWA/UBC and similar products;
- some ask currency/location questions.

Campaign implication: do not use a "DWA is a scam?" public hook while linking to DWA. That may get attention, but it creates trust debt. Use "the questions I would answer before buying" instead.

## General MRR Skepticism Themes

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OB-HK43NjN4

`REAL`: Public comments under a widely viewed MRR criticism video show the broad anti-MRR frame buyers bring with them:

- "decentralized MLM" framing;
- "let me sell you the thing so you can sell the thing" skepticism;
- concern that people in hard financial situations are being targeted;
- confusion about how the original creator makes money;
- suspicion that the method is already used up once it is sold widely;
- suspicion of income screenshots and social-media urgency;
- pushback from defenders who argue MRR is different because there is no downline;
- debate over saturation, with defenders claiming there is no saturation and critics disagreeing.

Campaign implication: a clean DWA campaign must not be "anti-hype" only in tone. It must structurally prevent the MLM reflex by saying:

- no downline promise;
- no income promise;
- no "earn it back" promise;
- no resale-first advice for people without audience;
- no public link before fit check.

## Breakthrough Insights

### 1. The real buyer is mechanism-confused, not just skeptical.

The question is not "Is DWA good?" It is "What exactly am I buying: education, resale rights, affiliate link, PLR, community, bonuses, funnel, or support?"

Content move: publish a "DWA Mechanism Map" before publishing the Reality Map.

### 2. "Faceless" has a trust tax.

Faceless helps you avoid becoming the face of the hype, but it also removes personal trust. The substitute cannot be vibes. It has to be clarity: diagrams, checklists, refund/payment notes, what-to-ask prompts, and a no-link-until-fit-check rule.

Content move: be the faceless due-diligence account, not the faceless promo account.

### 3. Buyer questions cluster around after-checkout anxiety.

People want to know what happens after payment: access, support, payment processors, countries, content reuse, audience-building, costs, community, bonuses.

Content move: the free asset should be "Before You Ask For A DWA Link: 17 Questions To Answer."

### 4. "Scam?" hooks create contradiction if you sell the thing.

The criticism comments show people notice when someone calls it a scam and still sells it. That posture can get clicks but weakens the seller.

Content move: do not use "scam?" as a hook. Use "I checked the objections."

### 5. The strongest affiliate differentiator is support responsiveness.

In review comments, buyers notice when a seller answers questions. Some even praise responsiveness because many promoters do not answer.

Content move: build a public "DWA Questions I Will Answer Before Sending A Link" thread. The asset sells the right to a careful answer, not the dream.

### 6. The hidden competitor is not another DWA affiliate. It is a safer first step.

Some buyers should not buy. For those people, the better offer is a lower-cost "first product/funnel map" or a free 14-day content test. That protects trust and can later sell your own digital product.

Content move: every DM should offer a lower-cost/no-cost first step as an alternative to the affiliate link.

### 7. The old resale pitch is stale because it sounds like a saturated loop.

The repeated buyer concern sounds like: "If many people sell the same thing, why would I win?" The answer cannot be "no saturation." The honest answer is: you should not expect to win with the same angle.
Source cluster: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TnSkiNd9vRE and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OB-HK43NjN4

Content move: sell angle selection, audience selection, and buyer triage before selling DWA.

## Revised Hooks

Use these, not generic anti-hype:

- "The question people keep asking about DWA is not 'is it legit?' It is 'what actually happens after I buy?'"
- "I read the DWA comment sections so you do not have to. The same 11 questions keep coming up."
- "If your DWA plan starts with 'resell DWA,' you are already skipping the hard part."
- "Faceless DWA can work only if your account is clearer than your face would have been."
- "Do not ask me for a DWA link until you can answer these 5 questions."
- "The DWA resale pitch creates one huge buyer objection: why would anyone buy the same thing from me?"

## Revised Funnel

Public post -> comment `QUESTIONS` -> DM the Buyer Question Map -> ask which question they are trying to answer -> fit bucket -> optional lower-cost/no-cost next step -> affiliate link only by permission.

Fit buckets:

- `Skip`: needs fast money, cannot afford loss, no time, no audience, wants promise.
- `Study`: wants structured digital marketing education but should not sell yet.
- `Build`: better off making a smaller own product first.
- `Affiliate Test`: understands the risks, has audience/angle or will run a 14-day no-claim test.

## What The First Campaign Missed

- It did not weaponize actual buyer questions.
- It did not address the "how do I sell without an audience?" objection hard enough.
- It did not make content-reuse restrictions visible enough.
- It did not make payment/country/support questions a content pillar.
- It did not distinguish "skeptical buyer" from "operationally anxious buyer."
- It leaned too much on "I felt icky" instead of "I mapped the questions the market is already asking."
